Project Summary

The overall growth of the agriculture sector has been seriously weakened by the impact of the conflict in the North East states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) where crop production has been severely curtailed by insecurity. Without urgent support, the livelihoods of these predominantly farming communities are at heightened risk, leading to ever-increasing levels of food insecurity and malnutrition.
Support to crop production and the rebuilding of a robust crop value chain have been identified by FAO as key to the restoration of livelihoods and to improving the food and nutrition security of vulnerable populations in the BAY states.
The purpose of the intervention is to increase sustainable agricultural productivity and production of Male and Female farmer in the conflict sensitive region of the North-East of Nigeria( Adamawa, Borno, Gombe, Taraba and Yobe states through Climate Smart Agriculture technologies.
The target is to reach 17500 households within phase-3-5 of the CH through the following;
a. Community –based improved seed production and seed fair
b. Enhanced food and nutritional needs of IDPs, host communities and returnees for protection through integrated back yard/micro gardening and Homestead fish pond
c. Indigenous crop genetic diversification and Plant seed bank invigoration
d. Enhance extension support and capacity building for government extension workers to reduce food loss.

1 - Needs

Support to crop production and the rebuilding of a robust crop value chain have been identified by FAO as key to the restoration of livelihoods and to improving the food and nutrition security of vulnerable populations in the BAY states. In addition to insecurity, both production and productivity are severely constrained by biotic factors such as the lack of quality seeds,pest attacks and crop diseases as well as by drought, floods, soil degradation and the scarcity of arable land due to restrictions imposed by the military. Input-output markets as well as technical backstopping have also been negatively impacted by the conflict and currently lack the capacity needed to adequately support crop production. Farmers in the BAY states especially women and youth lack access to improved quality seeds as the now decade-long insurgency has also resulted in the widespread closure of input dealers who had previously supplied seeds for cultivation. Increasing the availability of quality seeds will significantly boost both production and productivity, particularly in light of the numerous constraints currently faced by farmers.

2 - Activities

FAO will collaborate with relevant government institutions, the state ministry of agriculture, research institutions and National Seed Council of Nigeria to support 122 500 people. The beneficiaries will include a minimum of 35% inclusion of women and at least a 60% inclusion of youth, throughout all activities. Their specific needs in terms of training, resources, communication with communities and all aspects related to their successful participation in the project will be considered. For seed distribution, FAO will prioritize women-headed HHs and developed specific kits of vegetable & cash crop seeds for their income generation & nutrition, taking into account their limited access to land. The activities are:• Training of existing seed producers on improved techniques of seed production, good crop production management practices, post-harvest handling, storage and record keeping •Establishment of community-based seed out-growers to promote the production of improved quality and climate-resilient seeds through seed multiplication •Support toward the certification of locally-produced seed by the NSC•Identification of suitable institutions as seed banks for the preservation of genetic pools for subsequent use by plant breeders•Organization of seed fairs to distribute improved seeds to beneficiaries through vouchers•Facilitate on-farm evaluations to demonstrate good management practices and mid-season evaluations and field days to promote farmer-to-farmer extension delivery systems•Create market linkages between seed producers and buyers (agricultural support projects, farmer cooperatives, government) Beneficiary identification and Targeting Criteria:Beneficiaries are identified through community social mobilization and targeted using the following criteria;a. Having secure control of land for plantingb. Household are food and nutrition secure to minimize risk of consuming seed as foodc. Willingness of HHs to accept improved agronomic practices d. Willingness of HHs to undergo seed quality control inspection by the National Seed Council of Nigeria FAO has developed a rigid protection sensitive strategy in the northeast, based on a do no harm, approach, boosting meaningful access and integrating principles of the accountability of affected populations and communications with communities to enhance participation. The goal of FAO’s approach is to boost participation in the project cycle, curb SEA and GBV. FAO implements feedback mechanisms including toll free numbers, suggestion boxes, focus group discussions. In the M&E of the project, FAO will undertake yield assessments & PDM which will collect beneficiary feedback concerning acceptability, timeliness, transparency of the selection process etc. Women, men and youth’s feedback will be deliberately targeted as part of the above processes, in consultation with local authorities and interest groups such as women and youth cooperatives. FAO undertook a broad scale protection risk analysis to understand the risks of specific groups across agricultural value chains in the northeast and is well placed to design projects which consider the specific risks facing at-risk groups. FAO has field experts including female field assistants and staff who speak the local languages and are frequently deployed during trainings, distributions, monitoring and planning missions.To greater understand the protection risks faced by HHs, FAO will undertake a protection risk assessment prior to the implementation of the activity. The Organization will also reinforce training to staff on GBV, PSEA, AAP and CwC. By adopting a CBA, ensuring beneficiary participation in decision-making, and involving local and traditional authorities, the project will promote accountability, capacity building & empowerment. Five percent of the budget will be allocated for the recruitment of a protection expert, training of stakeholders, communication materials & sensitization/advocacy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO will ensure that feedback mechanisms such as toll free numbers, complaint boxes, complaint desks are available throughout the life cycle of the project and that vulnerable community members have access to these mechanisms. The organization will also ensure that the views and experiences of the most vulnerable are integrated in post-activity monitoring and reporting. FAO will engage a wide cross section of stakeholders to identify the most-vulnerable and those least likely to utilize feedback mechanisms . These individuals will be identified and reached in person where possible and their specific requirements and needs will be taken into account in project design, implementation and appraisal.
