Project Summary

Despite a period of relative stability over the past year, the malnutrition and food insecurity situation across South Sudan remains critical. According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projections for January - April 2020, over 5.5 million people (47% of the population) are predicted to be severely food insecure (IPC 3, 4 & 5), even considering the presence of humanitarian assistance. Levels of acute malnutrition and food insecurity are expected to rise in early 2019 as post-harvest stocks become depleted. Recent SMART surveys conducted in Jonglei, Warrap and Upper Nile show GAM rates above the emergency threshold. Ongoing insecurity, poor hygiene and sanitation infrastructure, limited availability of diverse and nutritious food in local markets, low coverage of health and immunization services, and constrained and livelihood opportunities exacerbate these findings. To address these issues, the program will directly target 4,772 children under 5 and 6,106 pregnant and lactating women in the treatment of malnutrition at 13 nutrition sites (OTP/TSFP). 13,833 total caregivers will be sensitized on MIYCN topics. Treatment programs will prioritize the identification and treatment of children under five with SAM and MAM, as well as PLW with MAM, through outpatient therapeutic programs (OTP) and targeted supplementary feeding programs (TSFP). Caregivers will receive sensitization and counseling on MIYCN, health, and hygiene/sanitation practices, and critical gender- and age-sensitive hygiene items. To ensure early detection and referral of acute malnutrition, community volunteers will be trained to conduct mass screenings. During these screenings, volunteers will also provide sensitization on nutrition and hygiene/sanitation practices. Prevention activities will focus on community mobilization and sensitization as well as addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition. These activities include training community leaders, hosting mass awareness campaigns around MIYCN and hygiene/sanitation topics and promoting mother-to-mother support groups (MtMSG.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has experienced a protracted humanitarian crisis since 2013. As a result, over 4.2 million people have been displaced, with 2.2 million fleeing to neighboring countries. Despite a period of relative stability over the past year, the malnutrition and food insecurity situation across South Sudan remains critical. According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projections for January - April 2020, over 5.5 million people (47% of the population) are predicted to be severely food insecure (IPC 3, 4 & 5)(IPC Sept, 2019). The overall nutrition situation remains critical with a GAM rate at 11.6% and SAM rate at 2.3% and this is attributed to a combination of factors mainly, poor harvests, lack of access to a steady and diverse food supply as a result of disrupted markets, low health services coverage, limited WASH infrastructure, poor MIYCN practices, and post conflict (FSNMS round 23 report of August 2019).Further, the SMART surveys conducted in Jonglei counties show GAM rates above the emergency threshold of 15%: GAM prevalence in Fangak, 16.2%, respectively, while SAM prevalence was 3.3% (IMC and ACF June 2019), while this demonstrates some marginal improvement from previous years, continued interventions are necessary to encourage further progress and limit deterioration. Following the needs assessment conducted in Twic county, there were existing gaps in the nutrition service provision for example; people in Bolbel, Gerger kuedeng and Panyang bomas walk a distance of 2 to 6 hours to access nutrition services in Fangak county, in Panyikang, Nyilwak, Papoya, pakang and Uwachi boma walk a distance of 2 to 8 hours; In Twic, CAO is operating 5 OTP/TSFP mobile sites under 2019 SSHF SA2. To continue providing nutrition services, CAO is requesting for HRP fund for 2020 in order to address the nutrition needs of vulnerable people in hard to reach areas of Twic, Fangak and Panyikang Counties. CAO will treat SAM and MAM among children in mobile OTP & TSFP sites in the project locations, establish new OTP/TSFP sites while children with SAM with medical complications will be referred to Stabilization Centers. Additionally, CAO will initiate nutrition services in static health facilities that do not have nutrition partners while avoiding duplication. As part of enhanced nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information for early warning timely coordinated response decision making, CAO will also participate in SMART and FSNMS surveys.In partnership with UNDP and OCHA through the SSHF fund, CAO is currently implementing a 6 months nutrition project in Twic County with 5 OTP/TSFP mobile sites. Therefore, CAO has physical presence in Twic, Warrap state. Moreover, with the huge burden of the nutrition situation in Fangak and Panyikang counties where the IPC reports an AMN IPC phase 4, CAO is proposing a mobile nutrition response with the establishmen of 6 OTP/TSFP mobile or outreach sites (4 in Fangak and 2 in Panyikang). Additionally, CAO has physical presence in Fangak and Panyikang where we have been implementing WASH programs. For effective provision of nutrition services, CAO will effectively coordinate and collaborate with partners like WVI and WR implementing nutrition services in the already existing static health services to avoid duplication of nutrition interventions while ensuring complete referral linkages. CAO will coordinate with WFP and UNICEF to ensure continuous supply chain of nutrition commodities and supplies is provided and maintained. Through our nutrition specialist, CAO will recruit and train nutrition staff on CMAM and MIYCN to ensure quality provision of nutrition services to its beneficiaries. CAO will ensure that all caretakers of SAM children are provided with WASH NFIs, while Children 6-59months with MAM and PLWs shall be linked to BSFP and GFD programs to enhance their food security status. All nutrition staffs will be trained on key WASH messages.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes it does .The project will include sustainability through already existing local structures .CAO being a local non profit making community based organization it will maintain presence on ground even after project closes .To ensure smooth exit strategy and community sustainability of proposed activities, CAO proposes and promotes maximum participation by local key leaders throughout the project period as the project is community based. CAO staff will play a key role in knowledge sharing and skill transfer to the community members thereby encouraging more community participation towards sustainability of the project. The IDP, returnees and host community will be engaged and encouraged to take in the project on the understanding that CAO will only manage it to the end of the project period but that should not be the end of the activities. As long as there will still be needs at the community level, the activities will have to continue with minimum financial support and maximum community involvement since it is for the communities’ benefit. Through trainings conducted by CAO, community members will have the knowledge and also have seen the impact of the project on the lives of the beneficiaries, both direct and indirect. The different nutrition implementation strategies will eventually run systematically without much outside resources needed once the community has adopted the system. CAO will remain in the communities to provide any technical support or advice for the community and encourage them to continue activities after project closure.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

o The long distance traveled by some beneficiaries to receive services at nutrition sites exposes them to risks of violence and exploitation. Community Action Organization will be developing several mechanisms to mitigate these risks i.e advocating for security by spouses to the sites .o Many beneficiaries are illiterate which reduces the range of modalities available for them to use to provide feedback and could therefore affect feedback quality. For example, feedback presented in a face-to-face session may not be as honest or comprehensive as feedback offered anonymously due to the power dynamics at play. To address this, CAO will uses focus group discussions to garner feedback in a less individualized manner. Lastly , o Some nutrition sites have extensive catchment areas. In these cases, mothers, caregivers, and children must often travel or walk long distances to access the sites, sometimes staying overnight in the bush. This journey presents several safety risks, including harassment and sexual violence. In order to identify these risks, CAO will conduct regular GBV safety audits at its nutrition sites. Feedback from these safety audits will be used to understand the risks that beneficiaries face and therefore find and implement solutions to mitigate them.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CAO seeks to be accountable to the women, men, boys and girls whose lives it aims to improve, and places this responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. As a matter of human rights and meaningful programming. CAO as guided by the cluster defines Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) as “an active commitment by the organization to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to and being held to account by the people it seeks to assist”. By being more accountable to affected populations; Men, Women, Boys and Girls, CAO will do this by increasingTwic, Fangak and Panyikang Communities participation and feedback in programme identification, design, delivery and lesson learning . CAO seeks to achieve programmes of higher quality, with greater and more sustainable impact. The project will increase the space for community to shape their own recovery especially to better deliver against its commitments to stakeholders, including the people CAO assists and the resource partners who make assistance possible.CAO will ensure effective information sharing and communication channels by sharing information about CAO programmes in a timely, accessible and inclusive way .This will put community in a position to understand and shape decisions that impact their lives. CAO is committed to ensure that people receiving support participate in and influence all steps of the programme cycle, including initial assessment, project design, beneficiary selection, implementation, monitoring and evaluations. Systems of community representation must be fair and representative, enabling the most marginalized, vulnerable and affected to have a voice. CAO will use FGDs, using focus groups as a method of participation gives a voice to those in the community who are unable to speak up in a larger meeting or setting.
