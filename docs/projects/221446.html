Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan shows no sign of decreasing owing to high food prices, severe economic crisis, conflict, disease outbreak, flooding and cross border movements from Sudan owing to insecurity in Sudan. A multi agency assessment indicated that 1.4million are affected by the severe floods in entire country with 377,00 displacements resulting from the flooding seeking protection and other services like shelter and NFIs. This crisis has affected South Sudan significantly, with more than 855,000 people arriving from Sudan since April 2023 where 49% of the new arrivals are women and girls, 46% are below the age of 18 and 4% are over the age of 60 years; 89% of those arriving are South Sudanese. The core driver of movement of people, particularly women and girls, from Sudan to South Sudan is to seek protection services. The cumulative effect of these protection concerns called for scaling up of GBV services to improve access challenges. Alight’s GBV project will focus on preventive and response interventions in Ulang, Nasir, Longechuk and Maiwut. The response will primarily involve GBV case management services, cash assistance for case management, referral and provision of psychosocial support services while the prevention will involve GBV prevention and risk mitigation through awareness raising, dignity kit distribution, provision of energy serving stove, and training of frontline and community leaders on GBV integration. Alight has operational presence Ulang, Nasir, Longechuk and Maiwut Counties of South Sudan. Alight runs GBV response and prevention interventions using both mobile services and static services at WGFS in all the counties. The HNRP 2025 will be used to complement ongoing projects in; Ulang, Nasir Longechuk and Maiwut and integrate GBV into our joint partnership project with Relief International. This will allow Alight to only target areas not served by humanitarian interventions. In addition, Alight will work in coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Nutrition, Shelter, Education, FSL and Health to strengthen integration and linkages of services with the goal of preventing and responding to GBV cases in the targeted locations. The proposed interventions seek to address access issues, prevent and mitigate GBV occurrences with am of ensuring women, girls, boys and men live in peace and dignity. The proposed interventions are in line with the GBV Sub-Cluster objectives which seeks to:
(a) Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations.
(b) Promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities.
To ensure inclusivity of interventions especially for persons with disability, Alight normally conduct assessment prior to launch of project to ensure that disability information is captured for planning purposes. This information usually helps Alight to put in place deliberate measures that address access and inclusivity of special interest groups in our projects. The project aims to reach 36,214 persons i.e. 4,000 persons in Longechuk, 5,095 in Maiwut, 10,564 in Ulang and 16,555 persons in Nasir. Alight will make efforts to ensure the work that the organization does is sustainable through capacity building of the local authorities, community-based protection workers and frontline workers by working closely in every phase of the project design and implementation as well as working with the health facilities to strengthen CMR services.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation continued to deteriorate in South Sudan, driven by high food prices, a severe economic crisis, conflict, disease outbreaks, flooding and cross-border movements from Sudan. According to South Sudan Humanitarian snapshot published in October 2024, multi-agency assessments identified about 1.4 million flood-affected people across the country. Over 377,000 people have been displaced by floods, seeking shelter on higher ground. In Upper Nile State, in addition to flooding issues that affected all the counties, fighting between Government forces and local armed group on 16 and 17 October killed several people and displaced about 16,500 people in Nasir County alone. In Central Equatoria State, armed clashes erupted between armed groups in Morobo County of the Central Equatoria State in October, displacing an unconfirmed number of civilians towards the border with Uganda and creating severe protection risks for the population. The South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot further indicated that 855,000 people have arrived from Sudan since 16 April 2023. Of those arrivals, about 49% of the new arrivals are women and girls, 46% are below the age of 18 and 4% are over the age of 60 years; 89% of those arriving are South Sudanese. The Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security has declared quelea birds' infestation in Upper Nile and Unity states and the Ruweng Administrative Area, putting thousands of acres of grain fields under threat. On 28 October, the Ministry of Health declared a cholera outbreak in Renk, Upper Nile. As of 31 October, health authorities had reported 50 suspected cases of cholera, with six confirmed cases. The cumulative effect of the protracted crisis has disproportionately affected women and girls as it increases their risk to GBV including rape, sexual exploitation and abuse, intimate partner violence and physical violence. During displacements and economic meltdown, adolescent girls are even more vulnerable with child marriage increasing in many locations across South Sudan. Incidents of conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) have been documented in the Sudan crisis and the impacts of these are compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in South Sudan including clinical management of rape (CMR) and GBV case management and psychosocial support (PSS). Overall, GBV responses including health, PSS, and other services remain scarce in areas experiencing elevated levels of returnees and refugees in Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Jonglei and Central Equatoria. Ulang, Nasir, Longechuk and Maiwut Counties in Upper Nile State: Ulang, Longechuk, Maiwut and Nasir Counties are found in Upper Nile State along Sobat River Corridor. It is perennially affected by flooding and inter-communal violence (ICV) and now refugee returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia. This year, the four counties have experienced extreme climatic shocks from flooding, increased intercommunal violence causing displacements and thus protection risks for women and girls that place women and girls at disadvantaged position. According to the Spontaneous Refugee Return (SRR) bulletin released by UNHCR in August 2024, there are 36,887 spontaneous refugee returns from Ethiopia alone that entered Upper Nile State and most of these settled in Ulang, Nasir and neighbouring Counties of Maiwut, Longechuk and Jonglei areas. The IPC July-September analysis for 2024 has put Nasir in phase 5 under catastrophic category, Ulang, Longechuk and Maiwut in phase 4 under emergency category. IPC 4 and 5 comes with increased vulnerability and protection concerns. The HRP 2024 funding will be used to upscale services to these areas so that persons of concern in these areas are provided with GBV services and their access limitations to GBV services are addressed.

2. Project activities

Key activities planned for the project are structured under each objective as below. Objective 1: Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. Activity 1.1 Strengthening GBV awareness through house-to-house visit, focused group interventions, campaigns, and mass media. Alight will use the mentioned approaches to pass GBV key messages to the community. 22,095 women, girls, boys, and men will be reached with GBV messages through awareness raising. Activity 1.2: Strengthening the rollout and implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions. This will employ the use of EMAP approach to ensure 1,543 men, boys, women and girls will be meaningfully engaged in activities that help prevent and mitigate GBV incidences.Activity 1.3 Strengthening specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, legal aid, safe houses & WGFS programming) as well as capacity building, field monitoring, and linking GBV survivors through referral pathways. Survivor-centered and confidential case management services to GBV survivors will be undertaken as well as consent-based referrals. To this end, Alight will collaborate with partners to localize and update referral pathways regularly. Activity 1.4: Strengthening Psychosocial Support service provision through capacity building, field monitoring visit and collaboration with MHPSS working group. WGFS will be an entry for women and girls to access PSS and other services. Alight will identify a fully dedicated WGFS facilitator from the beneficiaries to coordinate skill building and PSS activities so that caseworkers can fully focus on case management. This will also foster sustainability and ownership of WGFS activities by the community. Activity 1.5: Strengthening the reach and coverage of dignity kits distribution and programming. Alight will request dignity kit from cluster pipeline, and these will be distributed to women and girls of reproductive age as a GBV risk mitigation measure. 5,571 dignity kits will be distributed to vulnerable women and girls in all the project areas. Activity 1.6: Integrating Cash and Voucher Assistance into GBV programming by working closely with Cash Working Group. Alight will introduce assistance to people affected by disasters as an effective, efficient, and transparent way of providing humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable. This ensures people have the freedom, dignity, and independence to decide on their own recovery and ensure that affected people are served in dignity through the cash-based programming. Activity 1.7: Scaling up the reach of fuel-efficient stoves, revamping the GBV AoR fuel-efficient stove Technical Reference Group (TRG), enhancing collaboration with FSL and WFP, adopting innovative approaches and best practices and Integrating Cash and Voucher Assistance, livelihood support into GBV programming, expanding availability of WGFS etc.Activity 1.8: Strengthening GBV referral pathways through conducting GBV service mapping and establishing / updating GBV SoP. This will help to ensure that survivors of GBV enjoy all the available services provided by partners operating in a particular location so that their multiple needs are addressed through complementary service provision. Objective 3: CO3: Promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities. Activity 3.1. Provision of need based capacity building trainings to non-GBV humanitarian actors on integration of GBV in their sectoral responses. 253 people will be trained across four counties in Upper Nile State.
