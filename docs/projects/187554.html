Project Summary

DRC intervention includes both lives–saving WASH assistance and more durable and equitable solutions such as maintenance and light rehabilitation of existing WASH systems. Interventions will particularly be focused on preventing the potential spread of covid-19 in all areas.
The conflict-affected population is assisted in compliance with the Sector and MoLWR standards. DRC aims to address overburdened communities with a high ratio of returnees and IDPs in the rehabilitation of sewage and water systems. Water supply will better resist the threats posed by conflicts and further impacts if utilities have stronger resilience capacities. Protecting a system against damage to social, environmental and economic components is a prerequisite for resilience. Supplying everyone equitably with water services also helps build a stronger service system.
A total of 103,470 beneficiaries ( 22764Women,18624 Men, 27937Boys, 34145Girls) will be reached out in different locations with a 3 and above severity scale during 2022 and will allow beneficiaries to have access to access to improved sanitation and drinking water in 2023.DRC will achieve this through the following outcome:
1. Newly displaced IDP s and returnees living in life-threatening emergency conditions and/or impacted by covid-19 have access to basic WASH assistance in Idleb, Aleppo, Dara'a, Rural Damascus, Homs, and Deir Ez Zor. DRC will prevent, mitigate, and respond to risks aggravated by the COVID- 19 pandemic, contributing to a strengthened protective environment for conflict and displacement affected individuals that are facing a sudden onset, a recent emergency that gives rise to an urgent WASH risk that they are unable to overcome.
DRC will respond to emergencies where sudden population movements cause conflict-affected populations to be living in conditions posing immediate life-threatening risks as well as address needs arising due to the covid-19 pandemic.
IDPs assistance following WASH Sector Standards will be provided in private unfinished/demolished buildings in both, urban and rural areas. Newly displaced populations get prioritized as an emergency response including COVID 19 response, according to the Sector’s directive. Prioritization is set on water supply, water storage, and provision of sanitary facilities also considering the additional needs of disabled populations. The Identified locations are in huge need of borehole rehab, pump stations, clean water networks, and sewage network rehab for the long term, and during emergencies, the response will be water tanks, water trucking HK distribution, repair of the partially damaged water system and hygiene promotion.
DRC will undertake the repair of conflict-damaged WASH infrastructure, enabling access of the conflict-affected population to safe water for drinking and domestic use, and safe excreta disposal.
Covid-19 prevention measures will be adopted, awareness on covid-19 will be mainstreamed through all interactive activities. Advocacy efforts will be mainstreamed through this action.

4 - Needs

The conflict in Syria is now entering its second decade, and the number of people relying on humanitarian aid for survival continues to grow, a decade of conflict in Syria has displaced a total of 6,845,888 individuals, averaging around 1.4 million displaced per year inside and outside of the country. People in Need are 12.2 million of WASH assistance, Out of Which 5,000,443 are in need (In Rural Damascus 1,740,081 M, In Daraa 680,951 K, In Der Ez Zour 622,431 K, In Idleb 2,637,931 M).According to the comprehensive Shelter and WASH assessments that were conducted in January 2022 by the DRC team, the results of the conducted needs assessment shows the following:- Residents in targeted areas are depending on water trucking, which turns out not only expensive but also unsafe due to the absence of water tests and water quality control, exposing households to the risk of disease. -The need to repair connections and the needed equipment to connect electricity networks to homes as well as street lighting to secure protection during night hours is evident.- WASH needs in schools, remain very high and could be linked with dysfunctional public water and sanitation systems the facilities are connected to.- For girls, appropriate WASH facilities are a particularly important part of ensuring their safety and healthy participation in school.-Waste management and availability of safe water in schools remain insufficient, especially with the COVID 19 ongoing pandemic.

5 - Implementation of activities

DRC will repair conflict-damaged WASH infrastructure, enabling access of the conflict-affected population to safe water for drinking and domestic use, and safe excreta disposal. Covid-19 prevention measures will be adopted across all the planned activities, awareness on covid-19 will be mainstreamed through all interactive activities. Advocacy efforts will be mainstreamed through this action.-Sanitation: Rehabilitation of sewage system: Encompasses repair of manhole and replacing damaged sections of piping in areas of intervention.-Water supply; Rehabilitation of water network Includes repair of water infrastructure, damaged sections of water piping, fixing leaking in the network pipes inside and outside buildings. Generators for the water networks that will be rehabilitated will be provided in coordination with local authorities and other actors in the area such as ICRC and UNICEF.-Infrastructure for agriculture: Repair of the partially damaged water system and infrastructure of the agriculture projects at the community levels. Implementation will be done by DRC directly through selected suppliers in line with DRC’s global procurement rules. DRC field staff involved in the monitoring and implementation will be equipped with PPE, including masks, gloves, and hand sanitizer. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria, or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Ensure beneficiary selection criteria are uniform/agreed upon among sector members and with the government and are communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. Establish complaint mechanisms at the community level- Available means of transporting water do not support equality and accessibility of water distribution for vulnerable groups. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases; Distribute smaller water containers more convenient for women and elderly people; Consider specific support to people with disabilities to ensure they access sufficient quantity of water;


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Water distribution not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities: LH: high, Impact: high, mitigation measures: Ensuring agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consulting separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design implementation phases; Establish complaint & feedback mechanism; Monitor safe and equitable access and use of water distribution points. Toilets present GBV and/or health, safety risk; Access to toilet is not available to all 24/7: LH: high, Impact: high - mitigation measures: Ensuring agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensuring consulting women and adolescent girls during the design, implementation, and monitoring phase of projects.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Non-availability of good performing contractors - Maintaining a good relationship to good performing contractors, contractor evaluation for procurement processes 2. Expanding sanctions of key construction material to Syria - Discussing the new development within the TWG of both Sectors 3. The long approval process for work execution - Considering the long approval process in the work plan, advocacy, and awareness-raising in the sector coordination mechanism, maintaining a good relationship with the ministries in the field 4. Increasing inflation and high depreciation of Syrian Pound with consequent increase of prices in the market. Contractors may be affected by the constant increase of price and withdraw from their contractual obligation, creating a delay in the implementation of the activities. 5. Non-acceptation of DRC delivered work by beneficiaries - Remaining in compliance with Sector’s guidelines, informing in advance local authorities and beneficiaries on the planned intervention, referring to MEAL findings of previous similar projects, feedback mechanism.
