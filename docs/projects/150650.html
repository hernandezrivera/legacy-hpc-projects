Project Summary

The project intends to provide an integrated response to better alleviate critical health and WASH needs of populations affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine. Life-saving health assistance and essential WASH services will be provided on EECPs. Population’s access to quality healthcare will be improved through the following:
- Decrease of associated costs (voucher for free access to drugs/medicines, voucher for transportation)
- Improve quality of care by provision of essential medical supplies
- Develop health staff education/skills through supportive supervision, capacity building (including MHPSS)
- Improvement of WASH-in-Health conditions in HFs
Morbidity/hygiene risks linked to poor water supply systems will also be decreased through repairs/rehabilitation of water supply networks.
The project intends to multiply the effects of this combined WASH and Health intervention by increasing the geographical consistency between all its components and trying to link the different activities together in similar locations, based on the needs. All project sites will be in the 0-5km zone, where needs are critical, but actual referral pathways will be taken into consideration when selecting SHCs to implement the voucher programs, as patients go to SHCs located outside of the 0-5km zone for essential, emergency healthcare services.
Lastly, the project seeks to both answer emergency needs (by supporting essential services at EECPs, primary healthcare package in health facilities and by restoring access to water supply) and look at middle/long term solutions to ensure communities and stakeholders have sufficient capacities to cope with the effects of the conflict (capacity building of health staff including when it comes to the health reform, MHPSS needs taken into consideration, WASH-in-Health, etc…). Though the proposed activities only cover 2019, their design will be constantly assessed during implementation to make sure they answer the needs adequately. Depending on the needs and the context development, PUI is willing to continue this approach in 2020, in line with the Ukraine HRP (with readjustment of the activities if necessary), in order to allow sufficient time to alleviate the consequences of the protracted conflict and allow communities to build their own resilience mechanisms.
In NGCA, PUI will work with Donbass Development Center, a local NGO that has been partnering with PUI for more than 3 years. DDC has excellent knowledge of the difficulties in the targeted areas and solid capacities to implement the e-voucher program. Besides, this will facilitate the acceptation of the project by the de-facto authorities, who are reluctant to let INGOs operate directly.

Needs

Conflict-affected areas of Eastern Ukraine suffer much from disruption of basic services. In terms of healthcare, access to services is limited because of real out-of-pocket costs (categories such as single mothers-headed HHs struggle to cover health expenditures) and security, distance to services/isolation of the communities. According to REACH (Humanitarian Trends, Aug 2018), 72% of HHs in the Donetska Oblast are not able to cover medical expenses at all and 63% of HHs in the Donetska Oblast are not satisfied with the quality of healthcare services (REACH). Issues are manifold: lack of essential medical supplies, disruption of pre-conflict referral pathways, work overload in HFs, lack of professional development of health staff. In PUI’s 2017 Hard-to-reach settlements assessment 20% of interviewed HHs lacked access to qualified health staff. It is worth mentioning that this leads to insufficient provision of MHPSS care while this is one of the most requested form of assistance for all gender and age categories. More specifically, children and adolescents show the greatest signs of distress due to the fact that they grow up in a conflict environment, while isolation of older people increases their need for PSS. The Health Cluster also reports a lack of appropriate MHPSS services for working-age categories. The PUI-IMC assessment shows that men and women express sign of distress differently. Other barriers to access healthcare cited by the HHs echo with the main issues already explained above. WASH conditions at health facilities are also impacted by the conflict – the 2017 PUI WASH/Health assessment showed that many HFs in the conflict affected areas have problems with pipes, rely on shallow wells for water supply, have sanitations infrastructures in bad conditions, etc. This affects hygiene conditions in HFs increases risks of waterborne diseases and infections. Poor conditions of the water supply infrastructure (due to the conflicts and lack of maintenance of aging network) in settlements located along the contact line also increases health risks. According to the WASH cluster and REACH, many settlements and municipalities continue to highlight water supply as the most pressing need for 2019. In 2018 the number of people living close to the contact line experiencing frequent water shortages, either daily or weekly was 27%. Quality of water is of high concern due to the lack of chemicals necessary to treat water and pollution risks, as well as is the capacity of the centralized heating systems to function normally in the winter. Lastly, WASH and health risks continue to exist at EECPs, though GoU started improvement works (GCA). However, given the increasing number of crossings (more than 1,3 million in August 2018, the highest number reached since the opening of the EECPs), waiting conditions still remain harsh and dangerous, leading to life-threatening risks. In these conditions, health risks affect all gender and age groups among the population, specifically affecting older adults and young children. During PUI’s hard-to-reach settlements assessment in 2017, 44% of HH interviewed mentioned that in the current year one or more HH members required healthcare, while 53% of all interviewed households were in at least one of the categories of vulnerability (IDPs, PLWs, Single-mother, PLWDs, war victims). Children under five, pregnant and lactating women and people with trauma/serious injuries or in need of emergency surgery (among all age and gender groups, but especially older people and PLWDs due to their additional difficulties to physically access infrastructures) are the ones who encounter the greatest health risks associated to the decrease of access to healthcare, quality of the services provided and their cost. On EECPs, stress and risks affect all people crossing the LoC, though older adults are particularly more exposed as they are the largest age group crossing.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: The access to emergency medical care and essential WASH services at EECPs is maintained.Activity 1.1 Provision of first aid services and essential WASH services/infrastructure at EECPs-GCA: Mayorsk, Mariinka, Novotroitske, Hnutove. Government’s current improvement of services at EECPs will be taken into consideration, so as to focus on critical gaps to complement the government or other stakeholders’ plans. The activities will benefit all categories of populations crossing at EECPs without any prioritization by age/gender, as needs are considered critical and affecting everyone, and the proposed activities provide life-saving assistance. Activity 1.2 Emergency intervention at Zolote EECP (GCA) if opening. Outcome 2: Access to and quality of healthcare are improved in conflict-affected areasActivity 2.1 Distribution of electronic needs-based vouchers-for-free medicines in 4 secondary health facilities and related primary health care centres (2 in GCA and 2 in NGCA) to decrease the costs associated to access to health care. The system will include a monitoring of the medicines distributed and the quality of prescriptions. The system will benefit the most vulnerable: children under 5, PLWs, people in need of emergency care among all age and gender groups (injuries/trauma, surgery) with focus on mother and child health. Individual feedback will be enabled through questionnaires administered to voucher recipients at health facilities plus a hotline for complaints. Activity 2.2 Distribution of vouchers for transportation to vulnerable patients in need of referral to SHC-level, upon recommendation of medical doctors of partner PHCs. The system will be implemented in 2 PHCs targeted under this intervention (in GCA), taking into consideration actual referral pathways, including referral to SHCs located beyond the 5km-zone in order to maintain the continuum of care and not disrupt access to essential medical care. Activity 2.3 Capacity building of basic health staff in PHCs (2 ambulatories and 4 FMAPS) and emergency health staff on first aid, quality health practices and healthcare provisions, medical protocols (in link with the health reform) and health education at community level, and MHPSS/mhGAP when applicable.. This activity will be provided through training of health staff and supportive supervision from PUI team. Attention will be put on addressing and taking into consideration the needs of different age/gender groups in provision of healthcare and health education. PUI will continue to liaise with other humanitarian actors whose mandate covers treatment of GBV cases; they are encouraged to place IEC materials at the PUI project sites in order to ensure confidential access to specialized assistance in cases of domestic or other violence for GBV survivors Activity 2.4 Provision of essential drugs, basic medical equipment and consumables to at least 2 ambulatories and 4 FMAPs (GCA and NGCA).Activity 2.5 Critical WASH-in-Health rehabilitations in 5 FMAPs (4 in GCA and 1 in NGCA). Standard gender & age requirements will be taken into consideration when improving sanitary infrastructures in health facilities. Outcome 3 – Immediate and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision, for conflict affected peopleActivity 3.1 – water supply needs assessment in villages along the contact line. PUI will engage with authorities at rayon and city council levels to ensure their commitment and sustainability of interventions. Assessment will reflect the needs of different age and gender groups among the assessed communities. Technical assessment to identify scope of works. If possible, villages to be assessed will be identified in the same geographical areas than the other proposed activities to ensure a better leverage effect of the intervention. Activity 3.2 - Repair/maintenance/replacement of pipes, tanks, pumps etc of existing decentralized piped water supply systems
