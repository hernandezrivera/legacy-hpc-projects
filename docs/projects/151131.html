Project Summary

This project seeks to address dire humanitarian needs in the IDPs and host communities in Banadir (Kahda, Daynile and Dharkaynley districts) districts), the activities will improve GBV and protection risk that is rampant in women and children.
Conflict and fraught resulted in a widespread of rural-urban dislodgment as households move in search of services and livelihood opportunities hence it increased displacement in Banadir region, hence presents significant protection challenges, particularly for women and children, as households lose traditional socio-economic safety nets and frequently experience extremely poor living conditions in Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) sites. There are significant information gaps on the specific protection vulnerabilities faced by IDPs and host communities in three regions and SCC outlined the need for strengthened system-wide data collection, analysis and data management across the protection sectors in banadir regions.
SCC plans to enhance community based protection of women and children against gender based violence in Banadir of Somalia through timely and life saving services
Strengthen capacity of targeted women, children and elderly and the persons with disabilities to increase their coping mechanism through GBV response and prevention.

Needs

Protection needs remain high for the most vulnerable groups especially women, children, and persons with disabilities, the elderly and the minority communities and Internal displacement continues to be a key driver of humanitarian and protection needs in Somalia.3.6 million People are estimated in need of protection. Conflict-affected populations are of particular concern, including those in hard-to-reach areas and displaced and marginalized populations. Protection issues include pervasive gender-based violence (GBV); child recruitment and early marriage; and forced evictions, where a concerning increase was recorded in the first five months of 2018, with over 171,000 people evicted.SCC will target the vulnerable population to protect them from persistent gender violence that most of the IDPs and the host communities are facing hence woman and girls remain the most vulnerable to gender based violence as they represent 88.6 per of the GBV cases registered last year. The trend requires more robust preventive as well as response interventions in IDP sites which are the majority of cases are reported from.The risk of eviction continues to rise for IDPs. Statistics by the Housing Land and Property (HLP) sub-cluster of the protection cluster show that over 204,000 people have been evicted in 2018, which equates to the number of people evicted in all of 2017. Due to the high population density of IDP settlements in towns, forced evictions are predominantly an urban phenomenon. Evictions are often carried out with little or no notice and at times with the complicity of uniformed, armed security personnel enlisted by land owners.Forced evictions expose displaced populations to a series of protection concerns, including the loss of shelter and access to essential services, and violence as a result of excessive force. Four people, including two children, were reportedly killed during evictions in Mogadishu between 16 and 18 July 2018.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

SCC shall involve stakeholders’ participation in the programming stage to ensure that the target locations are not prone to conflict, insecurity and are easily accessible. The response is planned to involve the government administrations and the community established structures/systems to ensure ownership, sustainability and resilience. During the intervention, SCC shall train and create awareness in protection mainstreaming through the participation of the key stakeholders SCC will ensure awareness of the planned GBV and protection and create acceptance in both the government and target communities. The participation is expected to improve coordination at all levels of the GBV and protection and prepare the stakeholders for handover of the project at the end of the interventions. SCC assumes that by the end of the project the stakeholders shall be well trained and prepared to take over and continue with the practices and maintenance on the awareness on GBV and protection at institutions (health centers, Ct Cs, schools,) and make sure vulnerable populations such as women, children and persons with disability is reached and attended to get rid of protection cases. By involving the government administrations it is to legalize the services and utilities by ensuring that the government representatives advise on all the legal requirements and facilitate the process in acquiring the same. Community participation shall create awareness of the ownership and the use of the utilities to the target beneficiaries. It is expected that by the end of the planned intervention, SCC shall ensure that protection environment is sufficiently safe/stable to enable durable solutions/resilience.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Rape incidents, domestic violence, men beating their wives and children, insecurity, lack of job creation and livelihood, lack of or functioning police and law enforcement institutions, lack of training and GBV engagement for law enforcement institutions, denial of resource for women and girls, lack of awareness raising for women and girls is a major challenge facing SCC target populations. The planned response shall target IDPs and vulnerable host communities that are most affected and discriminated due lack and limited access to basic services. Sharing the already inadequate resources with the host communities can trigger conflict and create insecurity amongst the IDPS, returnees and host communities. IDPs live in temporary shelters and these lead to protection risks. Single mothers and girls are exposed to GBV, lactating mothers, children under 5, the old and people living with disabilities cannot fight to access the limited basic services. The distances, locations, availability becomes a major challenge. It is due to this concerns SCC proposes to carry out interventions that will ensure protection for beneficiaries at all level of intervention. SCC will invade into the matter and discuss the issue with the local authorities to handle the case in hand to assist the vulnerable people out of risk of eviction. SCC will target the vulnerable population to protect them from persistent gender violence that most of the IDPs and the host communities are facing hence woman and girls remain the most vulnerable to gender based violence as they represent 88.6 per of the GBV cases registered every year. The trend requires more robust preventive as well as response interventions in IDP sites which the majority of cases are reported from..

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

IDPs live in temporary shelters without lockable doors that lead to protection risks. Single mothers and girls are exposed to GBV, lactating mothers, children under 5, the old and people living with disabilities cannot fight to access the limited basic services in WASH, nutrition and health. The distances, locations, availability becomes a major challenge. It is due to this concerns SCC proposes to carry out interventions that will ensure the following considerations are included in the project design. Locate water sources and sanitation facilities in visible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from settlement• Installation of lights near communal facilities. If lighting is not possible, consider coordinating with NFI sector for the provision of solar lamps / torches for each household. • No Facilities or distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points.• Introduce accompaniment arrangements to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls e.g. fetching water in groups• Identify central locations for distribution points - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.• Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. • Ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimizes exposure to safety threats that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their hygiene kits home.• Door-to-door distribution• Consider the frequency

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The Project activities will guarantee appropriate engagement of all the population groups with priority to vulnerable people. Different population groups will be targeted (returnees, IDPs and host communities) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion and to promote successful reintegration and long term solutions. This approach is paramount in displacement affected communities in order to enhance social cohesion and peaceful coexistence. The relevant local authorities will be properly engaged in activities design and implementation as well as informed of ongoing activities and developments through periodical meetings conducted by Project Manager. This will minimize the risk of misunderstandings and overlapping of activities with other partners and the government. A proper engagement also of village and community leaders will mitigate security risks thus allowing SCC Staff to safely implement activities in all the targeted locations. SCC staff has so far mitigated the risk by discussing SCC staff purpose in advance with community leaders and putting into place a security protocol consisting in monitoring and evaluating the security situation before movement and check in calls to Field monitors. Risks of hoax and dishonesty will be minimized by Segregation of duties and sharing of responsibilities, as well as monitoring visits and quality control of activities implemented. Beneficiaries will be provided with hotline numbers to refer complaints.
