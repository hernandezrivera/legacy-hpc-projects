Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve the access to safe water and sanitation in the most vulnerable communities in area C (78% of the proposed interventions), area A and B (16%), and H2 (6%) in the West Bank. The interventions will contribute to reducing the targeted communities’ WASH vulnerability with the focus on boys and girls and persons with disabilities (PWD), especially girls as they are considered the most vulnerable and more in need of these critical services. The interventions will target unserved and underserved communities with essential WASH services that will also protect the most vulnerable families under threat of forcible transfer. The interventions will focus on improving the communities’ access to 1) adequate and safely managed water services; 2) sanitation services and 3) WASH facilities in schools and health centres. All interventions will be implemented in parallel with the provision of hygiene materials and hygiene awareness sessions in schools, health centres and communities.
The project interventions were developed based on the WASH Cluster eligible actions for HRP 2023 and targeting the most vulnerable communities according to the Health Care Facilities (HCF) vulnerability assessment conducted by the WASH cluster for the West Bank. Therefore, UNICEF and its implementing partners are proposing to implement:
• Construction/rehabilitation of water infrastructures (pipes) (10 communities).
• Construction/rehabilitation of wastewater infrastructures (pipes) (1 community).
• Capacity building for two local councils in Hebron Governorate through developing a hydraulic model and supplying maintenance tools and equipment.
• Construction/rehabilitation of gender sensitive and disability friendly WASH facilities in 20 schools.
• Construction/rehabilitation of gender sensitive and disability friendly WASH facilities 16 health centres
• conducting hygiene awareness sessions in schools, health centres and communities. (20 schools, 16 health centres and 11 communities)
UNICEF and its partners will adopt several implementation methodologies that consider the targeted communities’ locations, proposed response technical standards, and UNICEF's accessibility and partners to these communities. Therefore, UNICEF and the partners will adopt and consider beneficiary/community implementation mechanisms for part of the water interventions in Area C. Implementation through suppliers/contractors will be adopted for WASH facilities in schools, health centers and community water and wastewater infrastructures activity in Area A & B.
Moreover, the proposed interventions will consider the targeted groups' socioeconomic, protection and COVID-19-related vulnerabilities. UNICEF will target the communities under the risk of demolition and forcible transfer in Area C, vulnerable communities in Area A & B, the families under the poverty line, and the communities highly affected by the COVID-19 outbreak. The proposed interventions will improve the targeted groups' capacities and resilience to deal with the challenges mentioned above.
UNICEF and its partners will carefully consider the special and persistent needs of women, boys and girls , elders and PWDs. Thus, these vulnerable groups will be consulted during the planning, design, and implementation of the interventions. Therefore, UNICEF and its partners will propose the sufficient response of these groups that consider the different social and traditional characteristics in the targeted communities.

1 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO and the recent joint (PWA-AAH) action plan, In the West Bank, 684,944 Palestinians are not connected to piped water services or are poorly supplied, while 92,400 people across the State of Palestine (SoP) suffer from an acute lack of solid waste collection services or are in the vicinity of informal and unregulated dumping sites. Israel’s occupation leading to difficult living conditions (social, economic and protection challenges) for many Palestinians are impacting an already fragile water, wastewater systems, school and healthcare system including the WASH services leading to a humanitarian need for WASH intervention at community level, in schools and HCFs in Palestine (HNO 2023). As a result of the fiscal and socioeconomic consequences stemming from the longstanding Israeli restrictions in the West Bank, the internal Palestinian divide, and the increased settler violence incidents, WASH vulnerabilities in the SoP have sharply increased in 2022. Approximately 1.37 million Palestinians across the SoP are affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and will be in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2023. In the SoP and according to the HNO 2023, over 237 schools and 197 healthcare units suffer from insufficient WASH facilities, primarily toilets, drinking and handwashing stations; overcrowding at existing facilities undermines hygiene practices, all of which raise particular concern following COVID-19 pandemic. WASH services in schools are considered a key component to provide a healthy and conducive learning environment. Although the Ministry of Education (MoE) in coordination with the WASH Cluster partners targeted around 250 schools as part of 2022 response plan, 237 schools are still lacking sufficient or adequate WASH facilities, affecting the access to water and sanitation for more than 116,000 students. This results in students overcrowding around available facilities and the associated protection, privacy, and dignity complications students, particularly girls, are suffering from during the use of these facilities. Children in these communities and schools are experiencing daily suffering due to their WASH vulnerability. T t The lack of sufficient sanitation facilities and access to safe water affects the students' daily hygiene practices and their willingness to attend the school. Women, girls and children with special needs/disability are also suffering in two different dimensions: 1) quality of life, due to restricted access to drinking water and subsequent obstacles to access to hygiene practices, and safe menstrual hygiene management; 2) sense of safety, due to the long distance covered to fetch water, they are exposed to settlers’ and Israeli Forces’ harassment and violence, a situation worsened by the frequent movement restrictions in response to the humanitarian crisis. Adequate WASH services in the hospitals in the West Bank are fundamental to reducing preventable causes of morbidity and mortality by minimizing risks of healthcare-associated infections, especially vital for immunocompromised patients, but also to improve staff and patient satisfaction and patient care-seeking behavior. In 2021 the WASH cluster performed a Vulnerability Assessment in HCFs, this study reflected the urgent need to address the humanitarian and development needs of Palestinian people. T According to this assessment, 16 HCFs in the West Bank are severely vulnerable and suffering from inadequate or unimproved WASH facilities (WASH Cluster HCF Vulnerability Assessment 2022) and 205 HCFs (40%) labeled as “under stress”. The main needs identified in this assessment for the West Bank were the insufficient water supply for almost 1 out of 4 HCFs, the lack of adequate sanitation services with only 37% of HCFs being connected to the sewer network but not necessarily a wastewater treatment, the absence of gender sensitive and PWD and children adapted toilets for patients (in respectively 68%, 80% and 91% of the HCFs in WB), the lack of regular hygiene promotion trainings of cleaning staff (66% of the HCFs) and the absence of water quality monitoring in almost 1 out of 3 HCFs. This project aims to improve the access to safe water and safe sanitation for the most vulnerable people at community, schools and health center levels, and improve the hygiene conditions in the targeted communities via the construction of sewer water networks with household connections. The targeted communities are not connected to any sewer water networks due to Israeli regulations in the targeted areas, as well as the limited capacities of the service providers being the most vulnerable in the West Bank. This intervention aims to support the councils to better manage their water resources and the overall operation and maintenance of the water networks. The interventions also aim to decrease targeted communities’ vulnerability and dependence on trucked water or unsafe sources of water by increasing their access to safe water and improve access of students and patients to safe water and safe sanitation in schools and health centers. The project will increase the capacity building of local councils with the development of hydraulic models and the provision of maintenance tools and equipment.

2 - Activities

2 - Activities (Narrative 6,000 character limit) The proposed project will deal with the source of WASH vulnerability in communities, such as the unavailability of water, lack of access to safe water and safe sanitation and the inadequate WASH facilities in schools and health care facilities. UNICEF will keep coordinating with relevant authorities at all phases of this project to ensure that the communities are targeted based on actual needs and vulnerabilities. UNICEF and its partners will organize meetings with stakeholders to coordinate the activities and the locations and avoid gaps and overlaps. Local councils in the targeted locations have been identified as partners for the collection of data related to the baseline and the end line. UNICEF will lead the advocacy efforts towards key stakeholders and the International Community, linked with the provision of the following WASH intervention implemented by partners in full coordination with relevant authorities. 1. Rehabilitation/construction of 29,550 m of water networks for communities: Construction of pipelines and filling points for vulnerable communities: the pipelines will be connected to the municipality systems. 10 communities in Area A, B and C will have improved their access to water through transmission pipelines and water networks; This will be implemented by contractors or Municipality/village council members and beneficiaries and will build upon their knowledge of the area and of the potential risks involved.2. Construction/rehabilitation of wastewater infrastructures for communities: Construction of 1500m of pipelines and filling points for vulnerable communities in H2 area: the pipelines will be connected to the municipality systems. One community in H2 area will have improved their access to wastewater through pipelines and wastewater networks; this will be implemented by contractors or Municipality/village council members and beneficiaries and will build upon their knowledge of the area and of the potential risks involved.3. Capacity building for two local councils in Hebron Governorate through developing a hydraulic model and supplying maintenance tools and equipment.4. Rehabilitation/construction of gender sensitive and disability friendly WASH facilities in 20 schools. This will include rehabilitation/construction of new sanitation facilities, latrines, and water fountains facilities in targeted schools for students based on the Ministry of Education needs assessment.5. Rehabilitation/construction of gender sensitive and disability friendly WASH facilities in 16 health care facilities. This will include upgrading water systems services including construction/rehabilitation or maintenance of water storage facilities, of water networks and connections inside the HCFs and rehabilitation of the stormwater drainage systems. Rehabilitation of sanitation services in the HCFs, including rehabilitation, construction or maintenance of already existing toilets and ensuring that the toilets meet the needs of staff and patients, men and women, care takers and special needs of PWDs and girls and boys , decreasing the risk of GBV cases and ensuring safety and dignity for l.6. Conducting hygiene awareness sessions in schools, health centres and communities. (20 schools, 16 health centres and 11 communities).

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

All feedback and complaints coming from beneficiaries will be given high consideration. UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in programmes by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. All UNICEF partners have been assessed on their risk to SEA and given access to a capacity-building program, which is continuous. UNICEF SoP also initiated a new project to digitalize AAP-PSEA mechanisms in the country office. UNICEF has expanded support to the SAWA hotline to be able to handle SEA claims at the interagency level, which includes referral of claims among agencies. Awareness and communication actions have started to progressively publicize the PSEA system and the right of affected populations to the complaint. During implementation all UNICEF implementing partners are required to have complaint mechanisms and policies for PSEA and integrate these into project proposals and results frameworks. Community-based and child-friendly participant satisfaction assessments using different tools including through focus group discussions with women, men, girls and boys will inform the project of what is working and what needs improvement and to be further tailored. A clause could be integrated into the contract with the engineering contractors, suppliers to allow for changes based on feedback collected during and after implementation. This will allow the project to make changes based on feedback collected and be responsive to beneficiaries’ actual needs.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Partners with UNICEF will arrange a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries and their communities throughout all the duration and up to 6 months after the end of the project (as such mechanism is being implemented by both organizations as part of the responsibilities of their departments). This will be in line with partners Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability, and commitment to guarantee transparency, fairness, and equity towards all our beneficiaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions, and inquiries in a secured way, and receives a response in a timely manner. All complaints/feedback are reported to the partner’s Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff, project managers, and/or focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to its sensitivity. If the complaintrequires follow-up, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call from partner’s Monitoring and Coordination manager. Upon registration of thecomplaint on the database, the admin officer analyses complaints and classifies each of them according to the internal classification system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by UNICEF and partners team and/or CRM team.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

This will be ensured through interviews, surveys, focus groups and field visits. Beneficiaries from different groups will be informed about the complaint mechanism and the different modalities of accessing it. The complaint mechanism will be face to face during the field visits or through the dedicated hotline. The latter will ensure an anonymous and discrete means to file a complaint, ensuring that all population groups may access the mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Yes. The project’s focus will be to provide the needed services in a safe and dignified manner. The project will instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measure to meet “Do No Harm” requirements. All staff involved in the projects will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in UNICEF’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The planned project considers protection as a key cross-cutting approach along the design, putting special emphasis in the community Participation and empowerment considering the needs of some groups of the communities that are in a vulnerable situation due to gender, age, disabilities or any different element. The “do no harm principle” will be respected and promoted in each action and the design of the project will implement the AAH approach that ensures community participation (promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community such as people with disabilities, women or children) in the identification of the needs and the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in an increased empowerment at the individual and community level. Aligning with non-discrimination policies, protection and vulnerability concerns and community participation, the identified activities are implemented through an approach that foresees the involvement of all community members.Implementation methodology is led by community members through a participatory approach. In addition, the action engages the targeted communities in assessing their vulnerability and in mapping appropriate protection responses, aiming to increase the capacity of the community to make informed decisions about their safety. This approach is built from a multisectoral point of view, which allows to understand deeply the needs and the possible responses of the community, considering the specific vulnerabilities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The planned proposed interventions will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children and PWD to safe water and sanitation through the provision of safe water, safe sanitation, and rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools and HCF. While most of public schools in the West Bank need extensive infrastructure work, yet, girls’ schools are the most vulnerable and in need of such services due to girls’ personal hygiene needs. The project will target these schools and provide the most needed WASH services. UNICEF and the partners respect a non-discrimination approach through ensuring the equality in the access of the outcomes and benefits of the project, some of the activities will directly promote the gender equality and the rights of children as a key element in the development, safety and dignity of the community itself. This approach will have a crucial role in the direct contribution to the beneficiaries’ coping strategiesdevelopment. In this sense, some of the activities will promote these coping strategies in which is expected that young people have a crucialrole. Coping strategies will not only be promoted through beneficiaries’ involvement with local councils and stakeholders, but also in a familyand individual level acting through the different trainings and awareness campaigns as direct agents of change spreading the importance ofthe different roles in the community and sharing the load of care-work needed in order to prevent the spread of the different diseases.
