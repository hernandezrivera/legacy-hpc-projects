Project Summary

This project intends to contribute to the delivery of integrated lifesaving and inclusive education service in Northern Bahr Ghazal (Aweil North, West and Centre Counties), Western Bahr El Ghazal (Raja County) and Central Equatoria (Kajokeji, Morobo, Yei and Juba Counties) States in South Sudan. The project will target overall beneficiary figures of 10,035 (6021G/4014B) of which 9,600(5760G/3840B) are conflict affected children (3-17 Years) in a protective learning environment, 240 teachers, 180 PTAs/SMCs and 15 caretakers in 20 schools with the following aims;
i) Saving lives by providing timely meals, enhance access to quality education and boosting of enrollment, attendance and retention
ii) Working to reduce gender disparities in the learning environment through awareness creation and training of teachers, PTAs/SMCs and learners on GBV and psycho-social services
iii) Promoting of safe and protective learning environment for children through improved sanitation and hygiene in schools
iv) Enhancing basic vocational and life skills training for youth and adolescents
The target group will include school children in ECD centers and in primary schools in the next 3 terms of 2020. Selection of the schools will be based on food security status, literacy statistics, enrollment, attendance data, environmental and economic aspects of the emergency to determine most affected persons regardless of their individual circumstances. Protection of the beneficiaries shall be put high on the agenda of the humanitarian assistance and protection. This will be in line with accepted international standard approaches of promoting community-based protection, meaningful engagement, offering of ideal leadership to crisis-affected communities, identification of protection risks, building of self-protection capacities, joint prevention and mitigation. Linkage will be made to protection actors so as to mainstream age, gender and diversity approaches aiming at equal enjoyment of rights by all crisis-affected people and integrate this into accountability frameworks
Activities envisaged in this project will include; payment of teachers’ incentives, school feeding, rehabilitation/construction of learning spaces, provision of teaching, learning and recreational materials, WASH in schools, capacity building of teachers, sensitization of community, SMCs/PTAs to actively participate in education activities, basic vocational and life skills training, training of Local Education Officials in school management, resource mobilization, monitoring and record keeping
A strong collaboration mechanism with other clusters like WASH, Protection, Nutrition and Health clusters will be enhanced to ensure cohesion, coordination and timely responses driven by the assessed educational needs of affected populations. The collaboration is meant to establish synergies among similar initiatives to avoid duplication and overlapping. The close liaison with nutrition partners on ground is meant to improve on the general nutrition status of learners by promoting balanced diet in every home. Synergies with health and nutrition cluster partners who manage the health facilities and OTPs Centres in the Counties will promote outreach services to supported schools as catchment areas for service provision on cases of acute malnutrition and health challenges to aid in referral and support services. On the other hand, SPEDP will ensure periodic reporting of FTS and 5Ws.
In the next two years, SPEDP is implementing basic vocational and life skills training for youth and adolescents in Aweil Centre and Juba through EU funds. It is also implementing WASH in schools in Aweil West through UKAID and Irish Aid funds, School feeding and WASH in schools in Yei and Kajokeji through SSHF funds and WASH in Schools in Old Fangak and Pigi Canal with funds from OFDA. Therefore, the funding under this project will greatly complement and fill the gaps in the on-going projects.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is currently undergoing numerous socio-economic and political challenges which has seriously affected all sectors of society; economy, infrastructure including the education system in the country. As a result, the country is now fragile thus requiring a lot of assistance for equitable access to education across the country. Since, the start of the conflict in 2013, over 7million people are said to be at risk of severe malnutrition hampering children from going to school as they along with their parents struggle to look for food for survival. Over 2.2million people have been forced out of the country to refugee settlements in neighboring countries, 2million are IDPs and over 2.2million children are out of school (UNESCO) where 75% are girls. The South Sudan Education Strategic plan affirm that the humanitarian crisis, displacement, protracted conflict emergency, poverty and livelihood insecurity are the main drivers leading to interruption of education and absence of protection for 3-17 years old children that are accounting to 3 million. Besides, UNICEF states that over 70% of the estimated 30,000 primary school teachers in the country are untrained. The gross attendance rate in primary schools in the country stands at 65% while net attendance is 40%. This means South Sudan is far from attaining the SDG goal for all children to be in school. And, according to the South Sudan IPC projection (August 2019–April 2020), an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified as being in Crisis (IPC Phase3) level, among whom an estimated 1.7million people are facing emergency (IPC Phase4) level and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase5) levels. Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity still persist in the country. In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start being depleted and an estimated 5.5million people (47% of the total population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase3) situation. Conflict remains the main driver of food insecurity and drop in school attendance and closure of schools. Current challenges affecting resumption of normalcy in education is the continuous violation of the right of children. A total of 2700 incidents of grave violations affecting 81,000 children and 2300 GBV cases have been reported to service providers by the UN since 2014. In a SPEDP June 2019 needs assessment in Kajokeji, Raja and Yei, the report indicates that the fighting between armed groups affected the ability of the people in the area to produce food due to constant displacements. Lack of school feeding and poor state of WASH in schools remains key factors causing high school dropout, more so among girls and PLWD because of their vulnerability. It also confirmed that 23% of the schools are not functional due to displacement, starvation and hunger at the household levels, making it difficult for the school children to attend classes regularly. The selection of these counties is due to their known myriad of challenges ranging from limited teachers, little and irregular salaries, limited scholastic materials, hunger and limited learning spaces. These mentioned needs have greatly affected the education performance and prevented children from attending classes. These counties are also earmarked to have poor harvest and access to humanitarian assistance may be limited too due to poor infrastructure and pockets of insecurity. They are also where possible returns of internally displaced persons and refugees may be great and further cause pressure on already scarce resources. Extreme levels of acute food insecurity are expected to persist in these counties. This project is therefore designed to reduce on the above factors and increase on enrollment, food security and reduce absenteeism

2 - Activities

A total of 20 schools will be selected by a joint team from SPEDP, the County Education Office and other partners operating on the ground. This will be based on food security status, literacy statistics and enrollment and attendance data. The activities in the project will include i). Setting up/rehabilitation of 10 temporary learning spaces (TLS) ii). Provision of psycho-social training and support to pupils iii). Provision of learning and teaching materials, supplies to pupils and teachers in selected schools iv). Provision of gender-sensitive WASH facilities in the schools having no such facilities v). PTA /SMCs members and teachers training on psycho-social support vi). Provision of ECD services to 3-5 year old children vii). Provision of school feeding to 3-17 year old children viii). Training of teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies ix). Training of community education committee members and local education officials in school management, monitoring and record keeping x). Training of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) in life-skills and basic vocational courses and xi). Payment of teachers’ incentives. To achieve these activities, SPEDP will ensure a very close collaboration with the education sector partners to establish synergies among the similar initiatives to avoid duplication and overlapping. SPEDP will also periodically report on FTS and 5Ws. Protection risks and benefits shall be greatly taken into consideration in this project. Community-based protection approach or meaningful engagement and ideal leadership in crisis-affected communities will be put into consideration to ensure identifying of protection risks, self-protection capacities and joint prevention and mitigation strategies

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the project will work to link the beneficiaries to safe, stable and protective environment. Community awareness will be carried out right from beginning on importance of safety, unity, protection of the vulnerable among the community and strengthening their resolve to speak up on any matter affecting them and their community without fear. SPEDP will also work through the community leaders to allow returnees in the IDP camps and refugee camps to be welcomed by the host community and settle in their former villages without being challenged. SPEDP will on the other hand adhere to follow the Sphere protection principles such as avoidance of exposing people to further harm as a result of the project actions (No Harm principle), ensuring of people’s access to impartial assistance in proportion to needs and without discrimination, protect people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion. Assist people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuse. SPEDP will ensure that protection risks and benefits are considered in this project. Community-based protection approach or meaningful engagement will be promoted. Ideal leadership in crisis-affected communities will be encouraged to be able to identify protection risks, self-protection capacities, joint prevention and mitigation abilities will be paramount in this project. The affected will be linked to the protection department and actors to seek and mainstream an age, gender and diversity approach that aims at equal enjoyment of rights by all crisis-affected people and to integrate this into accountability frameworks.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

SPEDP will seek to involved all stakeholders to this project right from the inception in the setting, planning, monitoring, implementation and evaluation of the project. PTAs/SMCs and County Education Officers will be trained on resource mobilization and monitoring skills.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

SPEDP will ensure that universal access to quality food through strengthening of civil engagement accountability to the beneficiaries will be a main focus. This is aimed at creating a strong accountability component to the policy framework, ensure reduction of inequality based on the principle of equal human rights for all citizens and to prevent the adoption of subjective notions of fairness, which might be the outcome of political negotiation in which most disadvantaged are often voiceless. The project will ensure non-discrimination and meaningful access to assistance and services. The implementing team will seek meaningful engagement with the local authorities, SSRC, Education cluster and UNOCHA to ensure that activities are only implemented in locations where the safety of beneficiaries and implementing team is guaranteed. All beneficiaries and other stakeholders will be central in decision making concerning their own protection and welfare. Special attention will be paid to ensure the participation of and consultation with the women and girls who are the most vulnerable members of society who may often be excluded from decision making processes. To maximize the linkages between social protection and access to education and to improve the effectiveness of both Education in Emergencies and protection, it is imperative that gender-sensitive measures are integrated into policy and programme design and implementation. The project will use Initial Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) that revealed a number of important lessons which can be used to inform policy dialogues on Education in Emergencies initiatives in project locations

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

A community committee, made up of diverse representatives such as student representative, PTA/SMCs, Teacher and a project staff will be formed to record and handle complaints and feedback especially regarding the quality of food prepared, hygiene of the cooks and in the kitchen and general status of the project. The committee will be briefed on their roles, how to keep confidentiality. Regular meeting with staff and student representative shall be conducted to review record, and deciding action needed. Making immediate changes to address the complaint will be taken. For example if a complaint was raised about poor disposal of food left over, immediately digging of rubbish pit will be done.
