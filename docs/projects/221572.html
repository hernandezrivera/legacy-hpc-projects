Project Summary

South Sudan is grappling with a severe malnutrition crisis, with an estimated 2.1 million children under five and 1.11 million pregnant or lactating women projected to need urgent nutrition support by mid-2025. Food insecurity affects 7.7 million people, with 31,000 at catastrophic IPC Phase 5 levels. Unity State is among the most affected regions, with all counties in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and thousands expected to deteriorate to Phase 5 due to severe flooding, displacement, and disease outbreaks. In Rubkona County, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are critically high at 27%, exceeding the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. Similarly, Koch County reports a GAM rate of 20%, with both areas facing compounding challenges from recurrent flooding, which displaces residents, disrupts livelihoods, and exacerbates food shortages. April 2024 MUAC screenings revealed a GAM rate of 22.87% among children aged 6 to 59 months in Rubkona. Additional threats, such as poor dietary diversity, open defecation (practiced by 74% of households), and high disease prevalence, further worsen the crisis. Nearly half of the children in affected areas suffer from diarrhea, malaria, or respiratory infections, with over 38% of morbidity attributed to malaria. Koch County, severely impacted by flooding, has seen over 36,285 people affected, with significant destruction in Pakur, Mirmir, Ngony, and other villages. Vulnerable groups, including children (49.4% of those affected) and the elderly (10.5%), face heightened risks. Meanwhile, Rubkona hosts 48,000 returnees from Sudan, straining limited resources and further stressing food supplies, health services, and shelter. World Relief South Sudan (WRSS) is addressing these challenges through humanitarian interventions focused on life-saving nutrition services, including CMAM outreach and treatment for children under five with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and tailored nutrition support for pregnant and lactating women. Programs also aim to improve Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices in areas like Bentiu IDP Camp and Koch County. Collaboration with partners, such as UNICEF and WFP, ensures integrated health and nutrition efforts and efficient screening and referral systems across Rubkona, Koch, and Fangak counties. To mitigate the crisis, WRSS emphasizes building resilience, addressing vulnerabilities, and enhancing local capacities for sustainable service delivery. Immediate and sustained support is critical to alleviating suffering and fostering long-term solutions.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces a dire malnutrition crisis, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6 - 59 months projected to experience acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025. Additionally, 1.11 million pregnant or lactating women are expected to require urgent nutrition support during the same period. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis estimates that 7.7 million people (57%) of South Sudan's population will face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between September 2024 and July 2025. Alarmingly, about 31,000 returnees across South Sudan are expected to experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Unity State remains one of the most affected regions, with all counties classified under IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). This includes 330,000 people in IPC Phase 4 and 10,000 projected to face IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) conditions from April to July 2025. Rubkona and Koch counties, both in Unity State, are experiencing severe challenges. Rubkona reports a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 27%, significantly exceeding the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. A mass MUAC screening conducted by WRSS in April 2024 revealed a proxy GAM rate of 22.87% in Rubkona. Koch County shows a similarly alarming GAM rate of 20%, with conditions expected to deteriorate due to worsening floods, displacement, disrupted farming, increased prevalence of waterborne diseases, and economic hardship. The malnutrition crisis in South Sudan is compounded by inadequate feeding practices, poor sanitation, and high disease prevalence. Only 16.5% of children meet minimum dietary diversity, while 74% of households practice open defecation, increasing the risk of disease outbreaks. Nearly half of children suffer from diarrhea, malaria, or respiratory infections, further exacerbating acute malnutrition. Access to healthcare is severely constrained by budget cuts, inflation, flooding, and cost-sharing, leaving vulnerable populations without critical services. Climate change, recurrent flooding, and conflict in states such as Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile have displaced thousands, destroyed infrastructure, and disrupted livelihoods. Flooding has worsened food insecurity and triggered outbreaks of measles, hepatitis E, cholera, and malaria. Rubkona County is also under strain from the influx of 48,000 returnees from Sudan, many sheltering in Rotriak, placing additional pressure on food supplies, health services, and shelter resources. Humanitarian actors and government authorities are struggling to address the critical needs in nutrition, water, health, and shelter sectors. Sustained and immediate support is essential to save lives, stabilize communities, and implement long-term interventions for Rubkona’s displaced and food-insecure populations.

2. Project activities

Strategic Objective 1 activities To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025. Strengthening capacity of 902 Community Nutrition Volunteers or CNVs, 530 lead mothers and 363 Nutrition staffs on MIYCN and GBV and hygiene promotion through 3 days training. Establish and train 150 Mother to Mother Support Groups to provide tailored counseling for pregnant and lactating women or PLW and caregivers of under-five children enrolled in the nutrition program. Promoting Food Security through Kitchen Gardens establishment. These demonstration gardens will enhance caregivers' skills in food production, enabling them to provide diverse and nutritious meals for their households Conduct quarterly cooking demonstrations at nutrition sites and within the community to teach caregivers practical skills in food preparation, hygiene, and age-appropriate complementary feeding. Supported child survival strategies with mass MUAC screenings, Vitamin A supplementation, and deworming campaigns for children aged 6-59 months in six areas. Promoted exclusive breastfeeding through events, IEC material distribution, and advocacy during public awareness campaigns like World Breastfeeding Week. Strategic Objective 2 activities Support for 27 Nutrition Facilities 26 OTP and TSFP Sites and 2 Stabilization Centers in Rubkona and Koch Counties. Support through active case screening and treatment for children under 5 years, pregnant and lactating women. Integration of nutrition sites in the health facility services delivery. Promote and improve program quality by adhering to nutrition Sphere standards for SC, OTP and TSFP Strengthen monthly monitoring and supportive supervision visits to nutrition facilities in coordination with respective County Health Departments. Strengthen the capacity of Community Nutrition Volunteers to conduct home visits. Establishment of functional community feedback mechanism: Provision of complaint and feedback desk, suggestions box, mobile hot spot line and conduct 19 quarterly community feedback meetings. Strengthen capacity building and training of pregnant and breastfeeding women groups on family MUAC approach. Supported 2,700 SAM and MAM cases with cash for transport to OTP, TSFP, and Stabilization Centers, ensuring timely treatment. Strategic Objective 3 activities To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2025. Conduct nutrition SMART survey in Koch, Fangak and Rubkona counties Prepare and submit monthly, and quarterly nutrition program progress report respective stakeholders Compile and submit monthly NIS and 5Ws reports with partners and donors. Strategic Objective 4 activities To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025. Strengthen the capacity of nutrition staff on IMNCI or Integrated Management of Child Illness. Training 363 health and Nutrition staff and 902 CNVs on IMAM and CMAM guidelines and GBV and PSEA for effective malnutrition management. Establish health and nutrition education programs in schools, training school health and nutrition clubs, 25 participants per school. Organize quarterly workshops at the state and county levels for nutrition stakeholders on prevention aid diversion. Establishment of 8 Early Childhood Development or ECD Facilities in Nutrition Centers. Rehabilitated 27 nutrition facilities to improve infrastructure, resources, and capacity for effective malnutrition case management.
