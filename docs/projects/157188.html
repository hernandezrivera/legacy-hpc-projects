Project Summary

ARC livelihood project aims to save the lives of disasters and conflicts affected population in neediest areas of South and East Darfur states which highlighted by the humanitarian work plan for 2018. The project will target a total of 10,000 people aggregated by 50% male (5000) and 50% females (5000) in two states of South (3000) beneficiaries and East Darfur (7000) beneficiaries , the intervention will be through food production, income generating activities such as vegetable gardens and poultry, livestock vaccinations and capacity building of the both farmers and agro- pastorals which will contribute to reduction of acute food and livelihood insecurity for most vulnerable people affected by conflicts in East and South Darfur. The project will target 7 localities which includes Sharyia, Yassin, Adilla and Abukarinka in East Darfur and Gereida, Elsalam and Beiliel in South Darfur.
The locations prioritized for this projects based on HNO severity ranking for the FSL sector. ARC Sudan strategy is committed to create a better future for Sudanese communities in where they live. Hence, in responding to the needs in South and East Darfur, our focus will remain on food security activities integrated with ARC other operation sectors include nutrition, health and WASH. The project fund will be used to support life-saving interventions within South and East Darfur communities through provisions of the required agricultural and livelihood skills needed for the survival of the IDPs and host communities in the targeted areas.
ARC recently has conducted community consultations assessment in East and South Darfur States in order to highlight the communities’ priorities for Livelihood program which are mostly agricultural driven activities, such as peanut productions, millet and sorghum as most top needs for communities in Adilla, Abukarinka, Alsafia and Yassin. As a result of the assessment, ARC will become in a better place to respond to the demands addressed by the upcoming livelihood interventions for the year 2019. while implementing the livelihood project ARC will consider national and international standard requirements for livelihood sector include sphere requirements , collective outcomes for Sudan and livelihood sector priorities as well in addition the project will stick on humanitarian aid principles ( DO NO HARM ) which will ensure the transparency of the service delivery in way that turn the dividers into linkages peacefully among the diverse communities of IDPs , Returnees and host communities .

Needs

According to Sudan humanitarian need overview 2018, the government Food Security Technical Secretariat (FSTS) estimates that in 2018, some 4.8 million people are living at crisis or emergency levels of food insecurity. Arable lands in some food-producing areas are difficult to access and, even when crops can be grown, inadequate transport infrastructure has prevented access to markets.In addition, famine early warning system for Sudan indicated that the harvesting period for 2018/2019 has been affected by fuel and cash shortages and the high cost of labor. The macroeconomic crisis is hindering food access for many poor households, and crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected between February and May in parts of Red Sea, Kassala, Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, Blue Nile, and among most IDPs in SPLM-N controlled areas of South Kordofan and Jebel Marra. Poor macroeconomic conditions persist, fueled by the scarcity of foreign currency, and the Sudanese Pound (SDG) stands at 61 SDG/USD on the parallel market. According to Sudan’s Central Bureau of Statistics, the December 2018 national inflation rate was 72.94 percent, compared to 68.93 percent in November 2018 and a five-year average of 27.16 percent.ARC Food security and livelihood project will response to food insecurity status caused by high inflation rate and impact of macroeconomic conditions that caused limitation of food access, hence the project will focus on life-saving emergency food production and livelihoods security assistance and strengthening the restoration of livelihoods options, and protecting, maintaining and building resilience of the targeted population. The project interventions aim to: (i) reduce acute food and livelihoods insecurity of newly displaced and affected by crises and natural disasters; (ii) restore and sustain the food and livelihood security of vulnerable people in protracted displacement; (iii) build the resilience of the food and livelihood insecure by reducing vulnerabilities to conflict and disaster risks; and (iv) strengthen the capacity of national partners to address food and livelihoods insecurity and improve coordination in FSL core functions at national and state levels. In addition, livelihood interventions will be designed and implemented to strengthen women’s and men’s productive capacity early on, when it matters most, and to promote longer-term self-sufficiencyThe intervention will mobilize communities (IDPs, returnees, host communities) to participate in programming interventions that address their needs. Communities will be sensitized on complementary services to their livelihoods such as animal health services and agricultural inputs from both Ministries of Animal Resources and Ministry of Agriculture and increase agricultural products through seeds productions and distribution. The intervention will equip and empower communities with skills to build on possible livelihood options that would secure food security at household level.

Activities

• Support house hold food productions for at least 500 families , ARC will conduct assessment prior to project implementation to see what is methods of food production appropriate in each project sites include the activities need to be carry out in order to contribute to food production such as peanuts planting, milk production, millet production etc. therefore it expected that the farmers will benefit from the increase in production and productivity through provision of improve seeds for peanuts, sorghum and millet etc. which enhance the management of small scale farmers at household level • Improved farming skills through Pre & post-harvest training for targets farmer’s. This will be through extension workers • Procurement of improved seeds such as peanuts, 500 metric ten • Support income generating activities for 200 women's mostly from SAM /MAM children’s families , as integration of ARC efforts and services ARC livelihood program will use the nutrition program database for 2018 /2019 in ARC nutrition centers as guidance to reach the most vulnerable families and assess their needs in order to provide the assistance, while do so the community leaders, nutrition and health volunteers in each sector of ARC health & nutrition facility are in better position to join ARC on vulnerability selections. • Support vegetables garden for most vulnerable women at house hold and ARC health facilities level. target (300) families ARC will support women with vegetables garden base on vulnerability criteria through provision of local seeds, water cans, extension trainings.• Minor Rehabilitation of the existing Haffirs in Alsafia for vegetable grows, there an opportunity for the community to invest more on the local dam water in Alsafia which last by mid term of dry season. ARC will support rehabilitation of local dam targeting of 200 household through FFA intervention to increase utilization of water for community benefit which is vital activity.
