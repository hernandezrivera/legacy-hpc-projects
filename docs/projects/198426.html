Project Summary

IOM through its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) aims to provide an overall picture of displacement context though timely tracking and monitoring of displacement and population mobility. DTM will continue to provide mobility tracking in the six conflict-affected states in Northeast Nigeria (Adamawa, Bauchi, Borno, Gombe, Taraba and Yobe) with the weekly mobility trends tool deployed mainly in Adamawa, Borno, and when necessary, in Yobe due to fluidity of population movement. Biometric Registration will remain a priority in selected camps and areas of return to assist partners for verification (e.g. WFP) and support other organizations and their assistance distribution. Finally, detailed assessment surveys for return locations will continue in selected areas. DTM will also strive to strengthen existing collaborations (internal and external) with emerging needs.

1 - Needs

Insecurity, triggered by the conflict between Nigerian forces and Non-State armed groups (NSAG), has further resulted in a massive displacement crisis in Northeast Nigeria, bringing the total number of displaced persons to just over 2 million. Population movements still present major humanitarian challenges to the already stretched resources. While camp management structures have been established under the lead of the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) and State Emergency Management Agencies (SEMA) in the main sites hosting IDPs, camp management staff presence and multi-sectorial response gaps remain in 2022. The latest DTM Displacement Report, Round 42 (July 2022) has accounted for 2,455,190 displaced populations and 1,983,130 returnees. In the last quarter of 2022, an average of 700 new arrivals have been recorded per month. Camp congestion is still an issue in Borno, i.e., at least 1,000 individuals above camp capacity/ camp, falling severely below the globally acceptable Sphere standards. Widespread displacement, new waves of displacement, or secondary displacement and a steadily increasing number of returnees have also highlighted the need to reinforce the data assessment mechanism through DTM and to expand the exibility of interventions conducted in camps, host communities, in areas of return and in areas expected to have humanitarian access within the next few months. Since 2014, when detailed widespread data collection began, over 60% of IDPs have been displaced more than once, confirming the continued, protracted and precarious plight of the displaced. Thus, continuous monitoring and analyses of the displacement patterns are essential. At the same time, since August 2015, more than 1.9 million persons have returned to their places of habitual residence, requiring the need for detailed assessments of the conditions of the locations of return, identification of livelihood opportunities as well as ways of both rebuilding and improving infrastructure across these locations.Moreover, as additional work continues to improve the conditions of the affected population in the crisis across the board, detailed information on the needs of the most vulnerable and deprived is needed in real-time. Given that the security situation continues to be highly fluid, there is a need for round-the-clock monitoring of new displacements or arrivals and capturing their needs for adequate humanitarian action. Timely and accurate information is needed not only for planning and response but also to avoid duplication of efforts by different humanitarian activities and to make better use of limited resources.

2 - Activities

IOM will continue conducting its comprehensive and periodic assessments in over 2,400 locations to bring to light movements caused by various factors, including communal clashes, escalating crime and natural disasters in Northeast Nigeria. DTM will provide a better understanding of the numbers and needs of the affected population and an analysis of sector-wide needs. The growing numbers and needs of returnees are similarly captured, analyzed and widely shared with humanitarian partners. Specifically, DTM will ensure improved information management mechanisms for displaced populations in Northeast Nigeria to facilitate planning, gap analysis and rapid response. More specifically, the following activities will be implemented: • Track and monitor displacement, including gender and age disaggregation, service delivery, and critical needs and gaps across communities, through weekly mobility trends and needs monitoring activities.• Biometric registration of IDPs and affected populations in the new humanitarian operational areas by registering new caseloads as well as update the previously registered caseloads • Reinforcement of monitoring and alert mechanisms for rapid referral in close coordination with sector partners and Government counterparts through rapid paper registrations to share data within 24 hours of displacement. • Use the IOM distribution application to track assistance provided to beneficiaries over time to prevent duplications of efforts by organizations and better enhance assistance provision. • Continued surveys on camp closures, returnees and case-by-case analysis to identify the return conditions in areas of origin and further advise government and developmental partners on areas of focus.• DTM proposes “Multi-Sector Needs Assessment” with sub-activities of Joint Intersectoral Analysis Framework (JIAF)/ Inter-sectorial Needs Assessment. The intersectoral components will include needs monitoring and ad hoc assessments. The proposed activities are directly linked with the OCHA/Nigeria Humanitarian Fund allocation strategy and the prioritization by Coordination and Support Services and DMS sectors which will provide a comprehensive understanding of the needs and vulnerabilities of crisis-affected people in Northeast Nigeria and inform the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan for 2023, Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) and Assessment and Analysis Working Group (AAWG) for a more coordinated humanitarian assistance.• Provision of data and sharing with respective local authorities and communities as this will touch on the commitment to data restitution/ dissemination and sharing with communities.. • IOM has established a Stability Index, which utilizes various statistical techniques to assess perceptions of community stability in return areas to enable partners to develop strategies better and plan resources and operations in vulnerable areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM mitigates protection risks and minimizes gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) risks in camps and camp-like settings through enhanced community participation and consultations with communities, such as identification of risks and hazards with representatives of the communities to plan site improvement works and install safety measures; inclusion of women and girls in decision-making fora in camps; training of entry points areas on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse PSEA; training of camp management staff and community volunteers on GBV and PSEA; and working with Protection, GBV, Child Protection and Health actors in sites ensuring referral pathways are functional and active. IOM’s commitment to preventing and mitigating risks of GBV in emergencies, including in camps and camp-like settings, is captured in its Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises (GBViC Framework). DTM will continue collecting protection data on the vulnerability and needs of IDPs, Gender-Based Violence risks referrals and access to health facilities. DTM data collectors trained on Protection will concentrate their effortson emphasizing, identifying and properly following the channels set about in referring Protection related cases. The team will refer all cases that fall into the category and refer them to specialized Protection agencies. DTM team, in close coordination with IOM's Psychosocial Support (PSS) teams, will refer all cases they may come across for referrals. Continuous engagement with other Protection actors, especially Child Protection Sector, where all unidentified minors captured during assessments will be referred to them immediately. Through its biometric registration and verification activities, profiles of each IDP or affected person will be created to include a small component on soft protection monitoring in newly accessible areas, promoting PSEA efforts and enlarging advocacy to provide further information to the Protection Sector.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population through all stages of the project life cycle. During the project's design and planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. Mapping community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, persons with disabillities, child-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, as well as other vulnerable groups, will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision-making process. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the project implementation. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through different methods such as information desks, complaints and feedback desks, suggestion boxes, audio recorders, and various community meetings. IOM has already established these mechanisms in all its managed sites across Adamawa and Borno, functioning as a two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors.
