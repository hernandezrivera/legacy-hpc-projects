Project Summary

Save the Children International (SCI) in South Sudan intends to increase access to education of boys and girls in conflict affected Jonglei, Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Lakes, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal by 2021. The proposed intervention through different funding sources funding will targets school going children between the ages of 3-18 to access and continue education services. Recent study on Out of School Children (UNESCO) reveals that at least 2.2 million children (75% of whom are girls) in South Sudan are not receiving an education, which represents one of the highest rates of out-of-school children in the world. For those enrolled, up to 86% of learners are at least dropping out if timely action is not taken. UNICEF (2017) report on the status of education and children in South Sudan, states that more girls are still not attending schools and the few who enroll are not able to complete a full cycle of basic education. In South Sudan, girls are 7 times more likely to die of child birth than complete basic education as 52% of girls are married before the age of 18. Girls must overcome many obstacles to enroll, stay and complete primary school education. Needs for school rehabilitation are urgent, only 25% of primary classrooms are in permanent structures, 30% in semi-permanent structures (mud, thatch, or grass), 10% under a roof, 2% in tents and 33% are held in the open air. (UNESCO Institute of Statistics. (2018). In general, classrooms are overcrowded and continuously used as temporary shelters by IDPs and frequently occupied by armed actors. Despite a 10% increase in national enrolment rates compared to 2017, children missing out on education, due to supply and demand barriers. SCI proposed to reach 59,023 children (3-18 years; 48 % girls) through pre-primary, primary, ALP and youth programming (soft skills), psychosocial first aid to address trauma, and distress; train 488 teachers (trainings on pedagogy, learner centered approaches, PSS) and 399 other education personnel i.e. SMCs/PTAs and local education authorities (training on school management, monitoring, school based protection, referrals pathways). SCI’s interventions aim to provide holistic quality and inclusive education to the conflict affected communities and where possible defining linkages with other actors supporting long-term education interventions

1 - Needs

The provision of education opportunities in the Republic of South Sudan takes place against a backdrop of political instability, a struggling economy, extreme poverty, food insecurity and armed conflict. According to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), there are still 1.47 million internally displaced in South Sudan (OCHA Humanitarian Situation Report 11, Oct, 2019). The nature of conflict in South Sudan is complex; not only political, it also comprises of layers of ethnic and inter-communal clashes, including an increasing number of cattle raiding events. Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in September 2018, formally putting an end to the conflict, the cumulative effects of years of fighting, displacement and economic collapse continue to deprive girls and boys of their childhood and of their right to education, harming the future of the country. The already fragile education system has deteriorated at both system and service delivery levels. It is characterized by poor educational outcomes and high numbers of out-of-school children and adolescents, particularly those affected by conflict and displacement. The South Sudanese Pound (SSP) has depreciated rapidly against the US dollar in recent years, which has impacted service delivery across all sectors, and notably the payment of teacher salaries. Lack or delays in salary payment is impacting teachers’ motivation, leading to absenteeism and, ultimately, to teachers leaving the profession in search for better economic prospects. Many schools have been destroyed during the conflict and/or have stopped functioning because children and teachers have fled or are too scared to attend, while some parents are no longer able to pay for uniforms and books as they deal with unemployment and the effects of the chronically devalued currency(Edwards, S. -2018). A recent study on Out of School Children (UNESCO Institute of Statistics ) reveals that at least 2.2 million children (75% of whom are girls) in South Sudan are not receiving an education, which represents one of the highest rates of out-of-school children in the world. For those enrolled, up to 86% of learners are at least five years over age for their grade and many are at risk of dropping out if timely action is not taken. The conflict and associated economic decline have eroded the Government’s ability to provide consistent basic services. While they revealed that an additional 6,000 schools need to be built or rehabilitated. Now, thousands of children who fled as refugees are starting to return to the country, increasing the burden on already over- stretched education services. Children make about half of South Sudan population and bear the brunt of conflict and economic pressures. At least one in two children in South Sudan is affected by the humanitarian crisis (UNICEF (2017), Childhood under Attack). 3 out of 5 school age children were not enrolled in primary school and the pupil-classroom ratio averaged 105 (Cluster assessment 2016). The conflict has prevented children across the country from growing up in a secure and peaceful environment that is conductive to their physical and emotional development. Social and cultural norms weigh most heavily against girls. Girls are raised to cater to the needs of their male family members. Such early childhood socialization inhibits women from viewing themselves as equal members of society.The conflict and associated poor access to services have increased the rate of disability in South Sudan. Children with disabilities suffer from stigma and inadequate services and infrastructure, notably in the field of education. 2.3% of children in South Sudan are estimated to be disabled. However, only 0.63% of school going girls and boys have a disability, which indicates many children with disabilities are not in schools. Over 54.7% of youths report no opportunities for employment or skills training.

2 - Activities

Access: SCI will establish or rehabilitate 100 classrooms (inclusive and with equitable access for children living with disabilities) in the targeted locations to ensure that learning spaces meet the minimum standards identified by INEE for education in emergency. 9,181 Boys and girls will be targeted through pre-primary (to support school readiness and to prevent drop-out at later stage); 82,632 learners through primary, ALP, and youth through soft skills to define linkages with future opportunities. These boys and girls will receive learning materials and textbooks to ensure enrollment, retention and ultimately continuation of the education. SCI will also focus on girl’s education through a) provision of dignity kits; b) safe and sex disaggregated latrines; MHM education and awareness). School premises (school fencing) as well as pathways to the schools will be protected or their safety ensured through community engagement, school safety bodies, or by establishing walk- buddy system. After signing of the revitalized peace agreement, access to many locations has opened up or will open; SCI will work with mine action groups through protection cluster to ensure that areas in and around schools are safe from UXOs or mines, community sensitization and awareness on such messaging will be done with illustrations to ensure that children and adults are able to recognize the danger signs and risks. To promote school resilience SCI will also distribute recreational materials and establish school clubs for peace, sports, art and crafts, child rights etc. Schools/learning spaces will have Child Protection Helpdesks to prevent and report any protection issues and concerns; these helpdesks will be staffed by trained personnel to be able to respond to sensitive needs. SCI will reach boys and girls through healthy and nutritious meals to prevent cognitive underdevelopment, absenteeism in highly food insecure context of South Sudan Education Quality: SCI will identify recruit and train 718 teachers to ensure that schools have adequate and trained teachers to support cognitive development of boys and girls (trainings on pedagogies child centered methods, PSS), understand special needs (managing differentiation of instruction, adapting teaching approaches), and are aware of managing and responding to the protection concerns (school based child protection mechanism). SCI training approach for teachers will be aligned with MoGEI so ensure transition and linkages with system, these teachers will also receive teaching materials. 662 members of school governance bodies i.e. SMCs and PTAs will also receive trainings on school management, record keeping, monitoring of the day to day education activities, and most importantly psychosocial support and wider community based protection mechanisms including referrals pathways on protection/GBV, health and nutrition. Systems: SCI will work with national and local education authorities, conduct trainings on education policies and their implementation, support joint visits with county and payam officials to monitor interventions, keep schools records at the payam and county level for better data management. SCI will coordinate and collaborate with other actors i.e. GESS, IMPACT, and GPE to identify transition opportunities for long terms support to education systems. In its targeting strategy SCI will monitor influx in the population movement, this will help to respond to the emerging needs i.e. IDPs returns to their home of record. And also adopt conflict sensitive approach towards programming to ensure its interventions do not contribute towards conflict, a critical element during reintegration. SCI has strong monitoring systems in place, it has fully staffed MEAL Unit at national and field level to monitor its interventions, ensure data accuracy and quality. Out of the total appeal requested, SCI has already secured funding of 3.3 million for 2020 to support EiE interventions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

- SCI is a neutral organization in humanitarian responses. SCI will ensure that all groups regardless of age, sex, ethnicity, disability are consulted by ensuring groups are representative of men, and women, boys and girls, community leaders. - Exclusion of women and girls from consultations could impact education indicators i.e. construction of learning spaces, latrines, teacher recruitment etc. SCI will ensure that PTA/SMC bodies have adequate representation of women, and they are trained on girl’s education rights, and understand importance of female participation for community development. - Youth make up half of the country’s population, SCI will ensure that in all of its education interventions, youth groups/their representatives are consulted, and their opinion factored in the decision making through, establishing youth focal points in consultations with local community leaders. SCI will also sensitize the local communities towards importance of meaningful youth engagement to prevent any conflict/violence in the community. - SCI will adopt two pronged towards child protection and safe guarding i.e. school based child protection helpdesks, and community led and driven protection mechanisms. SCI will train identified members to manage and respond to child safe-guarding and protection concerns i.e. referrals pathways and access to dignified and safe services. - SCI understands data protection concerns and challenges, especially in a complex context of South Sudan. SCI programme data is protected and follows organization’s strict data protection protocols. - Conflict sensitivity is a critical element of programming for SCI, it will ensure that its approaches towards implementation do not put any group or party at risk - Sensitization of the beneficiaries on the community feedback mechanism - SCI will ensure where possible to establish the schools/centres within the communities, in case of distance mobilize communities and agree on the safe and protected .

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Save the Children recognizes the right of beneciaries and community members to give feedback and seek response from projects affecting them. In this response, the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneciaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. Consultative meetings will held with the learners, communities and local leaders. The beneciaries and community members will be encouraged to give feedback and seek responses from the projects affecting them. They will be sensitized right from the start of the project through kick off meetings and community consultation workshops on key deliverable’s including community roles and responsibilities
