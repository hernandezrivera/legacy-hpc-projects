Project Summary

Since west Mosul was recaptured from ISIL in July of 2017, displaced families have begun to return to their areas of origin to rebuild their livelihoods and communities. Unfortunately due to the massive destruction left behind after the conflict, many do not have the resources needed to safely return in a dignified manner. This project focuses on improving the WASH conditions of vulnerable returnees and host communities in 5 neighbourhoods of West Mosul.
COOPI will work on the rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 11 schools providing supplies, knowhow and cash for work for the rehabilitation in order to restore basic water and sanitation services and facilitate takeover of care and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities by local authorities. Special attention will be paid to no discrimination, balancing the number of toilets by GENDER and adding new toilets for students with disabilities. To tackle the issue of water quality in public places of interest, COOPI will pilot cost-effective, innovative and durable solutions to ensure water safety at school level. Beneficiaries will be involved directly in the quality monitoring so as to facilitate the phasing-out of the assistance and foster the long-term overall sustainability of the intervention. To maximise the effects of the intervention COOPI will also organise: TOT for teachers on Life Skills-Based Hygiene Education; awareness campaign in schools on WASH and personal hygiene involving both students and parents and distributions of vouchers for NFI in order to encourage and sustain good hygiene practices but also to boost both the supply and demand side of local markets. There will be a special focus on adolescent girls (12-18) attending classes, so as to safeguard their rights and protect their dignity.

Needs

The protracted conflict in Iraq caused a massive damage of water and sanitation infrastructures. Available resource and capacities have been drained in supporting displacement populations but also in some cases sustaining strained existing functional infrastructures. This has resulted in insufficient access to potable water and inadequate sanitation services of the affected population in many districts, slowing down the reconstruction and the returning home of IDPs. Returnees and persons who have stayed in their locations during the conflict will continue to be in need of WASH assistance. Although most of the damaged water supply infrastructures in re-taken areas of Ninewa have been partially or fully rehabilitated in 2017-18, WASH services are still of major humanitarian concern in many areas of West Mosul. Due to heavy damage to infrastructure, decrease in municipality capacity, shortage in labor and supplies, many schools in West Mosul still have to face deep WASH gaps and challenges, which continue to have adverse impact on health and environmental situation and further exacerbating difficult living conditions for the affected population. In addition access to safe drinking water is limited as the basic infrastructure has not yet been rehabilitated in several areas. A majority of piped water is not safe for drinking and lack of filters and depuration system prevent access to safe and clear water. Due to loss of livelihoods, many do not have the financial resources to afford water services form the private sector in the form of bottled water. During the numerous school field visits conducted in West Mosul from February to November 2018, COOPI team collected evidences of outstanding WASH vulnerabilities which could be mitigated by complementing Government’s efforts in the areas of return. All proposed locations were selected considering the simultaneous presence of multiple needs insisting on the same areas, and pertaining to water supply, WASH services in public facilities of interest and liquid and solid waste management. According to the rapid assessment conducted by COOPI across several schools in 5 neighbourhoods in West Mosul (17 Tamooz, Mashirfa, Al Rifai, Al Najar, Al Jawsaq), almost all facilities showed evidences of inadequacy of the existing WASH infrastructure: damages to water tanks, WC siphons and stalls, basins and taps, lack of water pumps, drainage systems, as well as insufficient numbers of toilets, in particular for students with disabilities, or garbage containers were observed. For this it was decided that 11 schools would benefit from a tailored WASH intervention inside their premises aimed to provide emergency WASH assistance to the students who have resumed classes. The aim is to accelerate the early recovery of these sensitive structures – which are representing important aggregation points for the retuned population.

Activities

To cope with the needs assessed, COOPI will carry out the following activities: A1. Enhance water supply, services and knowhow in schools;A 1.1 Fully rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools;A 1.2 Cash for work for rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools and management sanitation issues and services;A 1.3 Installation of new toilets for students with disabilities;A 1.4 Installation of solid waste disposals and bins; A2. Introduction of sustainable and durable innovations, such as the 8 filtering system for water coolers;A 2.1 Introduction and piloting of innovative and cost-effective technologies for water treatments (8 filtering systems) ;A 2.2 Water quality monitoring ; A3. Hygiene education and promotionA3.1 TOT on Life Skills-Based Hygiene EducationA3.2 Organisation of awareness campaign in schools on WASH and personal hygieneA3.3 Distribution vouchers, to be used in local markets, for family hygiene NFIA3.4 Distribution of individual MHM kits to adolescent girls. A4. The first activities encompass the fully rehabilitation of wash facilities in the targeted schools, the cash for work program to integrated livelihood need of the communities and the sharing of knowhow and expertise in order to strength local actor capacity in managing water and sanitation issues, needs and services. The rehabilitation includes purchase and installation or repair and upgrade of water tanks (for drinkable and sanitation water), pumps, pipes, taps, basin, toilets (special attention on the balancing of toilets by gender and the inclusion on toilets for students with disabilities), drainages, septic tanks, solid waste disposals.The seconds foreseen the introduction and piloting of innovations technologies on WASH management, aiming to provide more cost effective, sustainable and durable solutions. In order to minimise risks related to the possible onset of water-borne diseases in urban settings and to relieve the targeted schools from the burden of the Water Trucking, COOPI will install and pilot 20 Eight Filtering systems, divided in the supported schools according to their need and student numbers. Depending on feasibility, COOPI will try to make use of the existing water infrastructure wherever possible (boreholes, water tanks, water pumps, existing plumbing and taps) in order to minimise costs and intervene surgically without wasting further resources. COOPI WASH Expert will train school personnel on the operation of the 8 filters system and on water quality monitoring, thus safeguarding the sustainability of this technology at the phasing-out of project’s activities. The last activities will focus and the education, promotion and dissemination of hygiene awareness and good practises. TOT to 220 teachers and headmasters on hygiene education will introduce more awareness and learning modules on WASH issues in schools and will be the first step to organise hygiene promotion campaigns in each of the targeted schools, involving both students and parents. Over the course of the project, a total of 8865 students and 209 teachers across 11 different schools will be targeted by two cycles of Hygiene Promotion Campaigns (at the beginning and at the end of the project) aimed to raise their awareness on hand-washing and good hygiene practices. Moreover sav-stickers for PSEA information will be attached on the awareness material for the 8 school-buildings and directly printed on poster and fliers. During the awareness campaigns COOPI will also distribute Vouchers to students’ parents for the purchase of hygiene related NFI for all the families (foreseen 53.399 beneficiaries, direct and indirect, based on a family household of six) in order to encourage and sustain good hygiene practices but also to boost both the supply and demand side of local markets. Before the distribution a market assessment will take place as well as Focus Groups sessions with PTA in each school to identify the NFI to insert in the vouchers list; after the distribution will be held PDM activities, through surveys and data collecting. Additionally, 2245 girls of Ebin Athir High School for girls, Sawda Bint El Zama’a Secondary School for girls and Aquila Ibn Ashim secondary school for girls will be the recipients of an Individual MHM Kit with enough consumables for 5 months, inclusive of Cluster-approved, specific IEC materials on Menstrual Hygiene Management and safe pads’ disposal practices. The distribution of MHM kits will be organized on 28th of May on the occasion of the international “menstrual hygiene day”.

Protection Risk Analysis

Increased insecurity in the area, new outbreaks of conflicts or severe tensions between returnees and host communities (discrimination, affiliation accusations) prevent humanitarian access to the select facilities.

Mitigation Measures

Security assessments and weekly check with INSO and local authorities will monitor constantly the security issues and provide eventual contingency plans.

How is your project accountable to affected population

During the assessment and project design phase, community engagement ensured that the affected population was involved in determining the beneficiary selection process and which WASH facilities and activities are the most relevant. Before the distribution of NFI vouchers a market assessment will take place as well as Focus Groups sessions with PTA in each school to identify the NFI to insert in the vouchers list; after the distribution PDM activities, surveys and data collecting, will be held in order to measure the benefits of the distributions and receive a direct feedback from the beneficiaries. Accountability to the population is ensured through intense monitoring and evaluation but also thorough coordination with local authorities, DoE and headmasters, teachers and PTAs. Besides being available in the field on a daily basis, COOPI staff will organise biweekly sessions with local stakeholders and beneficiaries’ representatives selecting random beneficiaries for every school where activities will take place, in order to collect beneficiaries’ impressions about the works in progress. Furthermore, COOPI staffs will periodically visit and interview beneficiaries with respect to on-going activities, in order to facilitate the collection and registration of any possible complaint. In addition, feedback boxes will be available at all of the schools as well as with the local mukhtars.
