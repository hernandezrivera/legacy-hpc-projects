Provision of equitable access to education in emergency for children aged 3-18 years in communities affected by flooding in Rubkona, Mayendit and Guit Counties of Unity State

Basic Info

Project Name

Provision of equitable access to education in emergency for children aged 3-18 years in communities affected by flooding in Rubkona, Mayendit and Guit Counties of Unity State

Start Date

01/01/2025

End Date

31/12/2025

Project Summary

The project will implemented in Rubkona, Mayendit and Guit Counties of Unity State. CASS already have active presence implementing various projects in the targeted locations.
The project will be directly targeting the enrollment of 11,852 children (6,045 boys and 5,807 girls) into primary school, train 113 teachers (70 men and 43 women). Additional 1,216 children (620 boys and 596 girls) will be enrolled into ECD. The project will establish 4 temporary learning spaces and classrooms and rehabilitate 4 temporary learning spaces and classrooms fitting them with 20 wooden desks/classroom including establishing 5 gender segregated latrines with water points for hand washing and distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers. The project will support the distribution of recreational materials from the cluster pipeline to the 19 target schools and provide teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education. Capacity strengthening will be provided by training of 186 teachers (112 men and 75 women) on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children and train and update teachers on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition including training of 7 SMCs/PTAs with 11 members each (40 men and 37 women) on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change. Other activities will include provision of life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents, support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets), conduct back to learning campaigns and Post Distribution Monitoring to document how supplies are used.
The project will also create a safe and protective spaces for children to play and learn, integrating psychosocial support with educational activities and advocating for school feeding programs to ensure children receive at least one nutritious meal daily, encouraging attendance and supporting children’s health and well-being. The project will be providing textbooks, exercise books, pens, and other educational supplies to children who have lost their belongings due to displacement and supplying teaching materials, such as chalkboards, posters, and lesson plans, to facilitate quality teaching in emergency settings.

1. Needs

South Sudan is experiencing severe concurrent climate-related shocks such as flooding, the economic crisis marked by currency depreciation and hyperinflation of food prices, ongoing conflict and insecurity, as well as the spillover effects of the conflict in neighboring Sudan leading to forced displacement, low agricultural productivity, and a decrease in humanitarian aid.According to IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Snapshot, September 2024 - July 2025, in the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4 and between April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analyzed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe).An Integrated Public Health Rapid Assessment of Internally Displaced Persons in Rubkona County was conducted by REACH in July 2024 and findings indicate that most households experiencing food consumption gaps indicative of IPC AFI Phase 4 (emergency), with some possible pockets of IPC AFI Phase 5 (catastrophe) conditions. An interagency Rapid Needs Assessment Report (IRNA) conducted in Mayendit County between 26th to 30th August 2024 reported that about 3,659 HHs (21,963 individuals were affected floods. Most schools in communities assessed are submerged and non-functional.Food insecurity has a profound impact on education in South Sudan, affecting school attendance, performance, and the overall educational development of children. The challenges posed by food scarcity intersect with the broader socioeconomic and conflict-related issues, leading to a cycle of poverty and limited opportunities. Children from food-insecure households are more likely to miss school or drop out entirely. Families often prioritize finding food and meeting basic needs over education, especially during periods of severe food shortages and disproportionately affects girls, who are often the first to be pulled out of school to support household tasks or care for younger siblings during food crises. Early marriage is sometimes seen as a strategy to cope with food insecurity, reducing girls' educational opportunities. In some cases, food insecurity can increase the risk of exploitation and abuse, including child marriage and sexual exploitation, which further limit girls' access to education.Majority of people especially girls in Rubkona, Mayendit and Guit Counties of Unity State drops out of school in early primary-level due to cultural related factors such as early marriages, early pregnancies and demand for bride wealth. Other factors are insufficient school feeding programs for hungry children, inadequate teaching and learning materials, lack of teacher’s incentive payment, inadequate teachers training, limited sanitation facilities at schools, low enrollment and attendance, and high drop-out rates, especially among girls. This project will be targeting children between the ages of 3-18 years for ECD, primary school and the ALP. It will involve provision of school materials, support to teachers for incentives, induction of teachers, Training and support to PTAs and SMCs, establishment and support to school clubs, renovation of classrooms and construction of TLSs, plus sanitation facilities (Latrines). The project will be directly targeting the enrollment of 11,852 children (6,045 boys and 5,807 girls) primary school, train 113 teachers and 7 School Management Committees of 11 members each (40 men and 37 women). Additional 1,216 children (620 boys and 596 girls) will be enrolled into ECD.
