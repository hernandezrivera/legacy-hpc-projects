Project Summary

To protect the most vulnerable households from falling into higher levels of food and nutrition insecurity, the project will include an integrated package of emergency agriculture-based livelihoods assistance tailored to the needs of farmer, fisherfolk and agropastoral communities as a means to restore agriculture-based livelihoods and food production capacities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The geographical scope of the project will be in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa provinces, targeting 666,680 people (133,336 households). While the project will target food-insecure internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities, priority will be given to households in resettlement camps that lack basic needs, female-headed households and elderly. Beneficiary needs based targeting will comprise of households facing IPC Phase 3 or worse levels of food and nutrition insecurity. By providing quality agricultural inputs (through vouchers and in-kind distribution) – such as cereal, pulses and vegetable seed, fertilizer, farming tools, products to control pests and diseases, irrigation equipment, and fisheries and aquaculture inputs – the project will prioritize quick-impact actions . To maximize the overall outcome of the response, a combination of direct support will be provided in agricultural production, smallstock rearing (chicken and goats), sustainable fisheries resumption and natural resource management for the affected IDP and host communities. The agricultural recovery kit will ensure communities can resume food production as soon as possible. The project will be accompanied by training and good practice demonstrations covering key areas – crops, livestock, fisheries, agroforestry and nutrition – through tailored extension approaches.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Agriculture-based livelihoods assistance will be in the form of increasing access to crop production inputs that include seed and planting materials of agro-ecologically appropriate varieties of early-maturing maize, bean, cowpea, horticultural crops and cassava cuttings, as well as agricultural hand tools, such as axes, alfaias, hoes and machetes. This will ensure households take advantage of the winter and main summer agricultural seasons, while also enhancing production and productivity through the application of extension-backed improved agricultural practices. The project will build on existing operational modalities and systems with beneficiaries using electronic vouchers to access agriculture inputs from local agro-dealers, where market assessments deem suitable. Fisherfolk communities will be re-equipped with appropriate fishing gear for sustainable catch, while forming associations and councils that coordinate, provide oversight and enforce sustainable fisheries management. Provision of smallstock (chicken and goats) will be facilitated to meet immediate food needs, while rebuilding herds and flocks. Livestock assets will be safeguarded.Provision of agricultural inputs and smallstock will be concentrated in: Cabo Delgado: Ancuabe, Balama, Chiure, Macomia, Mecufi, Meluco, Montepuez, Mueda, Namuno, Nangade, Quissanga. Nampula: Erati, Meconta, Malema.Niassa: Cuamba. Restoration of assets for fisher folks will focus in:Cabo Delgado: Cidade de Pemba, Ibo, Macomia, Mecufi, Metuge, Namuno. Nampula: Cidade De Nampula, Memba, Malema, Monapo.Niassa: Nacala. Smallstock will be provided throughout all the districts to ensure livelihoods diversification.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection is mainstreamed throughout the project, ensuring at-risk group’s specific needs are taken into consideration and risks mitigated, where and when possible. FAO will work closely with other UN agencies and organizations such IOM and UNHCR that undertake protection activities in the same communities where the project will be implemented.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The project will ensure there is partnership and coordination at all levels on complementary mechanisms and best practices, while promoting participation and inclusion of affected populations in decisions that impact their lives. For this to work appropriately, adequate communication is an integral part of the engagement with all relevant stakeholders at different levels during project planning, implementation and closure. Equal access to safe, appropriate life-saving information will be provided continuously through the different platforms and forums, taking into consideration the specific context and preferences of different groups of people namely the beneficiaries, village elders, government authorities (district, provincial and national), implementing partners (national and international NGOs), and the United Nations.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Targeted actions for persons with disabilities, will be based on assessed needs and consultations with disabled persons and their representatives during actual project implementation. Actions will be carefully considered so as not to do harm or disadvantage other segments of the community.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This project is classified as “Low” risk, namely – the project has no or minimal potential negative environmental and/or social impacts, either upstream or downstream. The project will not be controversial in terms of interests of stakeholders, and in case of minimal impacts, the risk remains low because there are widely known and readily available good practices that will be used to address those impacts, and track record that implementers of the project (e.g., farmers and fishermen) know how to apply and do engage in these good practices.
