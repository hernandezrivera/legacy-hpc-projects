Project Summary

According to HNO report 2022, Somalia remains on the frontline of climate change, which continues to induce crises resulting in widespread displacement, rapid urbanization, food insecurity, and increased poverty. Critically, climate change is also increasingly understood as a major driver of conflict in Somalia as the struggle for dwindling resources intensifies clan divisions and inter-clan conflict.
Lower Shabelle region has hosted high number of IDPs affected by droughts and conflicts and the areas mainly affected were included Marka, Qoryooley, Wanlaweyn, Afgoye and Kurtunwarey district, people are highly needed humanitarian aids and they remain IDPs to the said districts, due to the report of HN0 2022, The country is forecast to experience its third consecutive season of below-average rainfall, which has already resulted in a sharp increase in food insecurity, especially in rural areas. Without humanitarian assistance, nearly 3.5 million are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021.
This project will enhance the livelihood of the communities living in rural areas both host communities and IDP families encountered problems of living in terms of livelihood specifically areas impacted by floods, climate shocks and droughts, considering number of needs assessments conducted by AYUUB on September 2021 in Lower Shabelle, it was however noted that people are high in needs to be saved their lives through enhancing their livelihood opportunities and support of Cash for work programs to be sustain their living. Movements of IDPs became higher than the past years, due to different shocks happened in 2021.
At the same time, large parts of Somalia remain prone to severe riverine and flash flooding. Somalia was among the countries hardest hit by the worst desert locust upsurge in decades that started in 2019. Although infestation levels declined in 2021 as a result of intensive control operations and low rainfall, a risk to rural livelihoods across the country remains in accordance with humanitarian need overview in 2022.
According to the HNO report on 2022, a total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. With 2.9 million people estimated to be internally displaced throughout the country, Somalia has one of the highest numbers of IDPs in the world. Of these, 2.2 million require urgent humanitarian assistance. IDPs are chronically more food insecure and vulnerable than host communities. Displacements are typically rural-urban in nature, where displaced people moving into urban centres often lack the skills required for urban livelihoods, and IDPs and rural migrants are frequently confined to poor-paying unskilled jobs, if any at all.
According to the Protection and Monitoring Returns Network (PRMN), in 2020 over 1 million people were displaced in Somalia due to floods and drought, the highest figures since 2017 Between January and August 2021, drought displaced 90,000 people, while floods another 59,000
For this project, it will be targeted vulnerable groups living in the districts affected by droughts and heavy displacement, keenly disabled people, aged people, youth groups, women headed households who are not capable to get-by their daily lives to have assistance of livelihood support and to create opportunities that they could be sustain their lives.
The targeted beneficiaries of this project would be Wanlaweyn 2000hh, Qoryoley 2000hh and Kurtunwarey 2500hh which is a total of 6500 HH from the above mentioned districts, 39,000 individuals. AYUUB will set a concrete criteria to select the right beneficiaries who will directly benefit from the beneficiaries, below will be some of the criteria’s to be used for Household from Below Poverty Line, Disabled groups must be prioritized, male and female from poor background who do not have any other alternative livelihoods and female headed households with in the same locations.

1. - Projects activities

Through the proposed response, AYUUB will support 5500 HHs (33,000 people) with gender disaggregation of (2500 female, 500 male, 1500 boys and 1500 girls), among them 1200 disabled persons with the following service packages throughout the project period; Livelihood rehabilitation and/or restoration of household and community productive assets and capacity to build resilience to withstand future shocks and prevent further deterioration. Canal and water catchment rehabilitation to facilitate agriculture activities to fight against the floods impact- Income generation activities for vulnerable groups/ youth and improving immediate access to food through conducting agricultural trainings- Provision of farms seeds to the beneficiaries: Household directly benefitting the project will have support of quality seed to cultivate their farms and AYUUB will help them to practice proper farming system in terms of high production and sustainability- Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in the five districts under the project. - Registration of the beneficiaries, conduct assessment and identification of food and money vendors and conduct constant monitoring of the markets to ensure markets are not destabilized, targeting the poor women led households, elderly men and women, disabled men and women and pregnant and lactating mother- Cash for Work activities will be identified in consultation with participants to ensure nature of work is selected in consideration of specific gender needs & cultural practices for women in particular Local authorities will be consulted as well as the relevant parts and actors, bush clearing activities will under such CFW.
