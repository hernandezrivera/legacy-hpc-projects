Project Summary

The project Strengthening a community based approach to reach the protection needs of the most vulnerable especially women and children.
In 2019, continuous return dynamics from Chad and IDPs camp to villages in Darfur are likely to be observed. In parallel to this “normalization” process, localized and violent clashes are likely to occur particularly in Jebel Marra, causing massive population displacements.
The action aims at responding to both dynamics by providing emergency responses for 22647 to be implemented in protracted contexts to develop access to basic Protection services and build capacities of communities on basic services facilities management while including a timely and appropriate response to new emergencies.
Based on this structural and current humanitarian situation, an integrated approach is required in order to address the most basic humanitarian needs to ensure community have an access to protection services that will allow the beneficiaries to maintain or regain minimal dignity and mitigate the health, environmental and the other displacement/return related risks.
Return dynamic is expected to continue and to potentially increase in Central Darfur while new population displacements are expected considering the formal restart of the tripartite agreement between Chad, Sudan and UNHCR for Sudanese refugees repatriation and also the persistence of a relatively stable security context.
The combination of protracted and urgent needs in Darfur calls for the humanitarian development nexus, a core element of the project is therefore the development of sustainable approaches to support the process of returns started in 2016.
Involvement of communities in the design of the action as well as in the management of facilities is aprecondition for durability and a progressive phase out of humanitarian actors.
This Project will focus its planned activities in Central Darfur Jebel Marra , Nertiti (West Jebel Marra), Rokoro (North Jebel Marra), Golo, and Umddukun localities with HNO 2019 ranking 4 and 5.
SORC therefore proposes an integrated project in line with following Sub- sectors:
Protection: Community Based Protection Networks: Women centres (PSS, Referral, Medical treatment, Clinical management of Rape, vocational training site, information dissemination and sharing of protection risks, literacy classes). Community base structure, safe and loan associates, FES, birth certificates and livelihood options (IGA) women at risk.
Child protection: Identification of USM, Identification of children with protection concerns , PSS, community mobilization towards child protection issues ( FGM , Early forced marriage, child abuse , child associated with armed groups, Child BV, school dropouts children with disability Birth certification.
Clinical management of rape (CMR) SORR plan to increase capacity and support GBV actors to prevent and respond to GBV incidents knowledgeable active CBOs in regards to GBV, mainstreamed and engaged medical, protection, and social workers to strengthen the quality of GBV services. Established and trained GBV community base structures, established referral pathway, promote community self-driven GBV prevention, SGBV survivors has instant access to medical , social , and legal support.
Through the proposed project SORC will ensure both a timely emergency and sustainable intervention.
CBPN formed now need to offer more support to their communities at community levels and they need more training to do so including Psychological First Aid (PFA).
SORC will implement this project in close collaboration and partnership with key stakeholders like the community, HAC, locality leaders, partners on the ground like CRS, TGH, IMC, WFP, UNICEF,UNDP and field coordinator this is to ensure there is no overlap/ duplication of programs. SORC staffs shall directly lead implementation of all activities.
