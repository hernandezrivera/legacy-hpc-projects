Project Summary

In the context of the current multi-faceted emergencies (increased risks of flooding, protracted conflict, droughts, Covid 19, Cholera/AWD Outbreaks and the projected La Nina phenomena expected in Somalia, ARC proposes this emergency WASH project to target IDPs and most vulnerable host communities living in Kismayo, Benadir, lascanod,Xudun, Telex, Afmadow, Balcad, Badade , and El-Afweyne district with aim of saving lives and reducing morbidities associated with WASH-related diseases. ARC understands that internal displacements are the key driver of humanitarian needs in Somalia. Many IDPs and Poor vulnerable Host communities are currently residing in Urban and pre-urban informal crowded areas with insufficient access to water and Sanitation. According to JMCNA assessment done in August by REACH, 53% of the IDPs face challenges in terms of access to WASH services in Somalia. The situation of IDPs and non- IDPs vulnerable host community members are complicated by frequent forced evictions meted on them leading to disruptions of access to critical WASH services and loss of gains made on access to these essentials services. According to an assessment done by ARC in Mogadishu in September 2020, more than 7000 IDPs households were forcefully evicted from their homes in Kaxda district, Benadir in the past three months. Ongoing conflicts and riverine flooding along Juba and Shebelle will continue to displace more people to major towns. In Sanaag and Sool regions, persistent clan conflicts, insecurity and recurring droughts are the major cause of displacement of people in the region.
Cognizant of these contexts, ARC will provide emergency water trucking to 12000 (2000HH) Drought- affected IDPs and vulnerable pastoral communities living in drought-prone areas of lascanod, xudun, telex and Celf-Afweyn districts for a period of two months as the agency will find and rehabilitate alternative water sources for the affected people. 20 critical shallow wells will be rehabilitated and protected targeting IDPs and Non- IDPs vulnerable communities and settlements from Kismayo, Kaxda &Darkenley (Banadir), lascanod, Cel-Afweyne and Telex. ARC will also conduct Disinfections and chlorination of floods affected wells.
ARC will Provide 10 Strategic boreholes in drought-prone locations in lascanood, telex and Cel- Afweyen district with Generators, submersible pumps, cable wires and control panels to ensure drought affected peoples have access to safe, adequate and reliable water.ARC will establish 6 Pipeline water extensions to identified IDPs Camps and non- IDPs camps including construction of elevated water storage tanks, installation of solar powered systems and water kiosk with a minimum of 6 taps to ensure increased access to adequate water by the IDPs and Non-IDPs.
ARC will construct/rehabilitate 2000 gender-segregated, disability-friendly communal latrines for IDPs and vulnerable host community members who have no access to any sanitation facilities.
ARC will construct for the targeted sex-Disaggregated, Disability Inclusive 40 Institutional latrines with water and Handwashing facilities for 24 target schools and 15 health centers.
Hygiene promotion will remain a key component of the intervention to prevent the spread of Covid 19 and others WASH-related diseases through risk communications and community engagement promoting behavior changes. Hygiene kits and basic MHM kits will be provided to adolescent girls in schools and villages/camps. ARC will target approximately 71, 800 beneficiaries from North and south-central Somalia (11488 women, 7180 men, 22165 boys, 23069 girls, 5744 disabled, and 2154 elderly people).
ARC will consult men, boys, girls, elderly, and people with disabilities on their needs and preference regarding the type, Design and location of the water and sanitation facilities to address barriers relating to access. ARC will involve women, boys, girls and disabled people in the project monitoring and feedback sought.

Project Activities

ARC will provide emergency water trucking to 12000(2000HH) Drought- affected IDPs and vulnerable pastoral communities living in drought-prone areas of lascanod, xudun,telex and Celf-Afweyn districts for a period of two months. ARC will Provide 6 Strategic boreholes in drought-prone locations with Generators, submersible pumps, and control panels to ensure drought affected peoples have access to safe, adequate, and reliable water.ARC will conduct rehabilitation and /Extensions of water infrastructures in order to provide reliable and sustained access to safe water-based on identified strategic water points serving drought, conflict, and floods affected IDPs and Non- IDPs host communities. Pipeline water extensions to identified IDPs Camps and non- IDPs camps including construction of elevated water storage tanks and water kiosks with a minimum of 6 taps will be done. ARC will rehabilitate floods affected shallow wells for riverrine villages through installations of concrete rings, raising their well-heads, constructions of aprons, drainage systems, and installations of hand-pumps and solar-powered pumps depending on the needs of the water points. Following every Guu and Deyr rainy season, many water points get submerged by floods water from the Juba River, destroying water points, thus the need to rehabilitate these water points and to make them flood-proof and render them accessible to users at all times. ARC will also conduct shock chlorination of affected shallow wells. Point of use water treatment and distribution of Aquatabs and Water Makers will be distributed to targeted households to secure their drinking water and prevent AWDs outbreaks. ARC will establish Gender balanced water management committees for the targeted water points to ensure continuity of services, appropriate operation, and maintenance as well as strengthening women participation in the WASH project.On sanitation, Rehabilitation /Installation of Sanitation facilities that are lockable from the inside, gender-segregated where applicable, with handwashing facilities, disaster sensitive with appropriate disaster mitigation design features. ARC will construct sex-Disaggregated, Disability Inclusive Institutional latrines with water and Hand washing facilities in target schools and health centres. ARC will consult men, boys, girls, elderly and people with disabilities on their needs and preference regarding the type, Design and location of the water and sanitation facilities to address barriers relating to access. ARC will involve women, boys, girls and disabled in the monitoring activities and their feedback sought to improves future response. ARC will also conduct community mobilization on environmental sanitation and provide environmental Sanitation tools to support active sanitation groups in the camps and target villages. Hygiene promotion will remain a key component of the intervention to prevent the spread of Covid 19 and other WASH-related diseases through risk communications and community engagement promoting behaviour changes. Key messages focusing on Hand washing with soaps at critical times, menstrual hygiene management, safe water handling practices, and safe excreta disposals including those of infants and young children will be disseminated through a mix of methods. Hygiene kits and basic MHM kits will be provided to adolescent girls in schools and villages/camps. Child-child hygiene clubs will be established and training on Hygiene practices will be done. Best hygiene messages and practices will be disseminated through IEC materials, dramas in camps, and training of hygiene club members in schools who are agents of change. Hygiene supplies provisions will be implemented through the Market Based Programming (MBP) approach so as to strengthen the local market, where the right market conditions exist. ARC will give a cash voucher of 24 dollars to cover the needs of essential hygiene kits for 12 months period distributed quarterly.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project has proposed cash distributions to beneficiaries via their mobile phones to cover the essential needs of hygiene supplies according to the standards WASH cluster hygiene kit content. Each beneficiary will be given 24 dollars to purchase the items from their local markets which will last them for three months. There will be 4 cycle distributions in a year. ARC will do post-distribution monitoring after every cycle to seek feedback on the process, quality of the material accessed, and the satisfaction level of the beneficiaries. The justification for the use of the cash over in-kind is that cash will stimulate demand and supply at the local market level. By giving cash to the beneficiaries to solely purchase the hygiene supplies, beneficiaries will get the chance to link directly with the local traders, have choice of who to buy from, and encouraging the local traders to stock more of the demanded supplies. Furthermore, the use of cash directly delivered to the beneficiaries via their mobile phones is faster and logistically easier to do, less prone to diversion than in Kind distributions. For this project, ARC will be working in big towns (Banadir, Kismayo, lascanod, telex) and other smaller locations outside the big towns, where availability of the essential hygiene items and market functionality, inter-connectedness, and dependency of the markets are guaranteed.Hygiene supplies provisions will be implemented through the Market Based Programming (MBP) approach so as to strengthen the local market, where the right market conditions exist. ARC will give an unrestricted cash voucher of 24 dollars to 2000 most vulnerable community members to cover the needs of essential hygiene kits for 12 months period distributed on a quarterly basis (after every three months). ARC will conduct regular post distribution monitoring to seek feedback on the beneficiaries' satisfaction with the support. ARC will use its other ongoing interventions in the proposed areas (Health, Nutrition, and Food Security and Livelihood projects) to complement the proposed project by meeting other needs of the target beneficiaries of the cash voucher support. ARC will work closely with other actors on the ground and government agencies involved in the IDPS affairs supports. The proposed un-restricted cash voucher to beneficiaries to buy hygiene supplies was a model recently piloted by NRC in Somalia. Best practices and lessons learned from this model were shared with the WASH cluster partners in Somalia to replicate.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

ARC has conducted an initial market assessment at the proposed sites before deciding on the use of market-based programming. ARC has also reviewed the recent joint market assessment report done by REACH in Somalia and has found out that essential hygiene items and construction materials are locally available in major markets and beneficiaries can access them. ARC has also established that markets are integrated, inter-connected, and functional in the proposed target areas (Mogadishu, Kismayo, Sool, and Sanaag).

Mitigation Measures

ARC has conducted an analysis of the protection risks that potentially threaten ARC’s ability to provide assistance that is impartial, does not endanger or harm beneficiaries, allows for feedback to be incorporated into the program monitoring, plan and promote community empowerment. The first protection risk facing the target population is the risk of being targeted for attacks by armed groups or explosions due to association with organizations receiving a grant from donors or perceived their enemies. All beneficiaries are vulnerable to this risk. In order to minimize this risk, ARC will operate in a low profile with minimal or no visibility in such a context where such risk is imminent. ARC will ensure that the aid delivered is seen as ‘Somalis helping Somalia’ by supporting teams from the local community. The second main protection risk is the risk of beneficiaries being asked to provide sex in exchange for goods and services. The most vulnerable group to this risk is women, especially girls and young women. ARC believes the risk is low but recognizes the problem of sexual exploitation and abuse exists within humanitarian spaces, and must therefore take all measures to prevent its occurrence on the part of its staff, partners, vendors, and other associates. ARC has a fully adapted Policy on Sexual Harassment, Exploitation, and Abuse (PSEA) and has operationalized PSEA throughout its recruitment and procurement practices. ARC requires all partners and contractors, to sign its Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) policy at the time of agreement, and all staff and consultants at the time of offer letter so that the contracts are not valid without a signed code of conduct in place. The third main protection risk is exclusion, denial of assistance or services, or other factors that render accessing the services dangerous or impossible. Those who are most vulnerable to this threat are older persons, persons with disabilities, children, women and unaccompanied youth. ARC will ensure that the WASH assistance provided across all the project supported water facilities are accessible to these vulnerable populations by targeting these groups within needs assessments, monitoring patient data to ensure that all age and gender groups are represented and persons with disabilities are being served, providing the aforementioned complaints mechanism, and assessing the water points and their locations to ensure that physical and security challenges do not prevent access for any population group. The 4th risk is the exclusion of service due to gender affecting particularly the women. ARC is incorporating gender mainstreaming throughout. With previous institutional experience implementing WASH programming, ARC is acutely aware of how women and girls face different challenges than men and boys in the current context. To this end, ARC will worked tirelessly with project staff in all program locations to ensure the inclusion of female hygiene promoters in the assessment and in the implementation of WASH projects so that special interests and views of women are given special attention throughout the response period. 5th risk is a lack of feedback Mechanism or inability to report concerns. ARC uses the real-time beneficiary feedback system, Kuja Kuja. Kuja Kuja is a tool to allow ARC capture the ‘pulse’ of customer satisfaction with services and to allow ARC service teams, technical teams, and support functions both at the local and global levels to understand real-time issues being faced, see opportunities for immediate and strategic improvement to service delivery.
