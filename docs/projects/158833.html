Start Date

Project Summary

To improve the effectiveness and efficiency of humanitarian health interventions by strengthening coordination and emergency preparedness through better information management, leading the health humanitarian response and dissemination of relevant and quality information to sector partners. The goal is to ensure that the highest number of people have access to lifesaving healthcare across the oPt, either through direct service delivery and/or advocacy towards duty bearers.

1 - Needs

Increasing attacks against healthcare, violent trauma, especially in the context of the ongoing Great March of Return (GMR) demonstrations in Gaza and accumulating mental health disorders, alongside chronically depleting medical supplies and dwindling number of specialist health care staff, have left a deteriorating and fragmented health system. Lack of medicines is particularly severe in the Gaza Strip, where 42% of the items on the essential medicines list are completely out of stock on average in the first half of 2019 . As a symptom of the degrading health system, out-of-pocket expenditures for healthcare as a percentage of current health expenditure has increased to one of the highest in the region at 46 per cent , affecting welfare of households, disproportionally impacting the poorest. An estimated 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian health interventions across the oPt, of whom two out of every three are in Gaza. Within Gaza, almost 20 per cent of the population requires humanitarian interventions for reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health services including nutrition; one out of six of Gaza’s adult population requires treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs); ten per cent suffer from severe mental health disorders; and twenty per cent of pregnant women in the poorest communities are undernourished. Meanwhile, the health system is struggling to cope with the ongoing influx of conflict-related trauma casualties each week, as part of the Gaza Mass Demonstration. However, infrastructure, human resources, and the referral system remains only partially functional after 12 years of blockade, inadequate to cope with the growing needs. In the West Bank, 377,803 people are in need of humanitarian health interventions. Checkpoints, barriers, and settlement expansion have cut off communities from basic primary healthcare services, and even emergency care in certain cases. The continuing movement restrictions, particularly for ambulances and paramedics, the frontline healthcare system and support to those in need of timely healthcare assistance, remain under threat. In Area C, H2 and the ’Seam Zone’, 165 out of a total of 176 communities now need humanitarian assistance, a total of 162,663 people. In addition, in East Jerusalem, almost twenty per cent need some form of humanitarian assistance, with mental health one of the most pressing need.

2 - Activities

The Health Cluster will aim to enhance the response of over 100 partner agencies. At the heart of the Health Cluster response is the aim to enhance the resilience of the health sector and disaster risk reduction as a critical cross-cutting element in all activities. Key activities such as the monthly meetings, monitoring of health indicators, and setting of minimum standards for health, will continue to take place. In addition to this, the Health Cluster will focus its activities on key areas that help drive an operational response, particularly through inter-sectorial coordination and linkages. The activities are split into oPt, Gaza, West Bank. Across the oPt, the Health Cluster will focus its activities on: 1. Development of emergency preparedness capacity for health partners. This would include systematic mapping, training on emergency preparedness, in addition to joint emergency response interventions. 2. Situational Reports issued routinely. 3. Development of a Health Cluster Contingency Plan to ensure a timely response to emergencies, whether it is conflict-related or outbreak prevention. 4. Health Cluster will work closely with the partners and the MoH to hand-over health service to the local authority. One example of where this is already taking place is the mobile health clinics. 5. Strategic advisory group: The Health Cluster will establish a Strategic Advisory group consisting of health partners, in order to strengthen linkages with other clusters. 6. The Health Cluster will work jointly with the Protection Cluster, WHO and the ICRC to promote monitoring for attacks against healthcare, including barriers to accessing healthcare. This will enhance the protection of healthcare staff as well as the beneficiaries. 9. The Health Cluster will conduct health needs assessments, by mainstreaming geographical analysis using GIS, focusing on key areas, such as trauma needs, Area C and East Jerusalem. 10. Resource Mobilisation activities will take place, primarily through quarterly briefings targeting donors; alongside disseminating information to highlight the impact of shortages in funding. 11. Gender and disability mainstreaming will take place through a dedicated gender and disability focal point in order to support partners to improve gender/ disability reporting. 12. Enhance community engagement in health through the active participation of beneficiaries during monthly project visits. In addition to the above, the Health Cluster will focus its activities on the following areas: 1. The Gaza Trauma Working Group, Nutrition Working Group and the Mobile Clinics working group will continue efforts to establish standards and coordination. 2. The Health Cluster piloted the HeRAMS, an approach to monthly monitoring of health facilities and resources availability. The aim is to expand the HeRAMS as a monitoring tool that also encompasses NGO facilities.3. The Health Cluster will establish a multi-hazard early warning indicator dashboard 4. Finally, the Health Cluster will increase the surveillance of medicines and their shortages at the facility level to increase awareness on the shortages of medical supplies.

3 - Indicators

Selected indicators include: • Number of Health Cluster meeting facilitated in coordination with the MoH (12 WB; 12 Gaza) • Number of information management product shared with disaggregated figures by gender and age (12 Situational Reports, situation on medical shortages, 4 HeRAMS reports, 4 mobile clinics reports/infographics, EMTs reports, 3 GIS maps) • Development of a multi-hazard indicator dashboard to enhance preparedness • Number of partners actively engaged in emergency preparedness with the highest number of partners being local organisations (30) • Emergency preparedness plans updated and available in Arabic and English (1 oPt) • Number of joint advocacy initiatives undertaken (2) • Number of training events that achieve learning objectives, with local partners being the highest number of attendees (2) • Number of people that benefitted from training sessions (F: 30 M: 30) • Number of health and nutrition cluster partners report improvement in coordination and information through the CCPM (cluster coordination performance monitoring) (100)

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Do No Harm lies at the heart of this programme. The Health Cluster has insured the safety and dignity of beneficiaries through the following means: 1. Training on 'do no harm'for all medical practicioners 2. Assessing the sustainability of programming to avoid dependence on humanitarian aid 3. Improving doctor-patient communication through training 4. Ensuring that safety regulations are integrated in all projects that aim to provide a service

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Health Cluster promotes equitable distribution of resources based on the needs of the idenfitied beneficiaries. Examples of how the cluster promotes this include: 1. Provision of transportation costs for patients in need to ensure that distance to the health facility is not a barrier to access 2. Integrating an analysis of the impact of certain restriction on specific vulnerable groups i.e. impact of the Israeli permit regime on younger men 3. Provision of additional support to female headed households
