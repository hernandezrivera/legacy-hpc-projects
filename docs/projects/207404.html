Project Summary

IOM will be enhancing its GBV program, that was established in 2017, with a focus on refugees and host communities. The program centers around three key pillars: survivor-centered support, risk mitigation, and addressing the root causes that perpetuate GBV, all in alignment with IOM's GBV in Crises Institutional Framework targeting people of all genders and ages, including people with diverse sexual orientations, gender identities, gender expressions, and sex characteristics (SOGIESC).
IOM directly oversees case management, providing a comprehensive support package through nine Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) at eight different camps, abd uses appropriate camp facilities to support male and SOGEISC individuals who need case management. IOM also supports an emergency safe shelter for survivors of GBV. IOM’s WGSS offers various services, including information dissemination, age-appropriate psychosocial support, awareness and educational activities, life-skills training, and forums for information-sharing and learning. This includes peer-led programs on topics such as GBV, sexual and reproductive health, menstrual hygiene, and disaster preparedness. IOM, with its implementing partner PULSE Bangladesh Society, will roll out MBR (MaBoinor Rosom-Mother’s and Sister’s Way) edition 1 & 2 as well as EMAP and Girl Shine Curriculum in the locations IOM is operating. For sustainability and local involvement, IOM, with it’s partner, will expand capacity-building efforts to include community groups, community advocates, imams, majhis, GBV volunteers, and women's committee members. These activities aim to transition key WGSS awareness and outreach functions into regular meetings, capacity-building sessions, and supervision. This approach enhances community ownership and accountability, addressing the root causes of gender inequality. IOM will collaborate with the Protection Research Unit to develop peer-led, evidence-based curricula, involving men, women, and people with diverse gender identities and sexual orientations, ensuring inclusivity and safe entry points for GBV services.
IOM's program will prioritize GBV risk mitigation, placing a stronger emphasis on the increased participation of women, girls, SOGEISC, and persons with disabilities. IOM will identify GBV risks and vulnerable groups through participatory assessments and data collection, including regular safety audits, involving women and girls in assessing their access to and their safety around provision of services. Menstrual health education, based on IOM's summary report ""Ma Boinor Rosom"" and its related curriculum, will be a focus, along with life skills and male engagement programs. Empowerment and skills development activities for women and girls will be more structured and diversified including the production of reusable sanitary pads through CFW. Graduates from skills-building programs will serve as peer trainers and receive coaching and capacity-building support. IOM will continue to ensure the preposition and distribution of dignity items that specifically address women and girl’s needs. IOM will continue its capacity building effort to train IOM and partner staff and national authorities protection, GBV core concepts, principles, risk identification and mitigation measures, PSEA, AAP and Code of Conduct.
IOM will also support Rohingya women in identifying opportunities for enhanced participation and engagement, replicating its Women's Participation Project as a best practice model for fostering social cohesion and ensuring disability inclusion.
IOM’s GBV program actively participates in various subsectors and advisory groups, such as the GBV Subsector, GBV Strategic Advisory Group, GBV Case Management Task Team, GBV Prevention Working Group, Dignity Kit Task Teams, and PSEA Network. IOM's GBV staff serve as interagency GBV Camp Level Focal Points and are part of emergency and response structures like the Mobile Medical Teams and the PERUs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The decrease in humanitarian assistance and the concurrent security situation has worsened existing gender-based violence (GBV) trends for both Rohingya refugees and host communities. The 2023 JMSNA findings and various inter-agency assessments have confirmed this, underscoring the security challenges that have given rise to new protection concerns. This particularly affects individuals facing discrimination and additional barriers to accessing services, including single female-headed households, adolescent girls, persons with diverse gender identities and sexual orientations, and persons with disabilities.2023 GBVIMS reports, inter-agency gender analyses, IOM's GBV yearly outcome survey report, and the 2022 ACAPS thematic report on the protection implications of polygamy have all indicated an increase in incidents of reported intimate partner violence (79% of all GBV incidents reported in GBVIMS Q3), sexual violence (8.4% in GBVIMS Q3), domestic violence, and polygamy. According to 2023 quarterly reports from GBVIMS, the reported GBV incidents has continue to increase in Q1-Q3. The report indicates, most GBV incidents occur in survivors' residences, on streets/pathways, near water points, public toilets/latrines, and bathing facilities, highlighting the pressing need for GBV risk mitigation in other program areas such as WASH and Shelter/NFI. Building on the insights from the IOM and UN Women joint report ""Honour in Transition"" and recent inter-agency assessments “Such As Four Years On: Shifting Gendered Perceptions And Experiences” and reports, gender norms are evolving within Rohingya communities, impacting the mobility of women and girls and their ability to control resources, information, and access to services. These changes in gender norms are intersecting with new family structures and demographic shifts. Prioritizing safe and meaningful participation and empowerment of women is crucial for broader GBV prevention efforts (evident and recommended in GBVIMS reports) and ensuring safe access to services. Scaling up evidence based GBV prevention work is a top priority to challenge harmful norms and ensure access to services for vulnerable populations. IOM has also been testing a culturally appropriate Cash for Work initiative to further enhance access to safe and dignified services.Women and adolescent girls face a higher risk compounded by factors like poor living conditions in the camps, limited access to basic resources, a lack of income-generating activities, insufficient lighting, gender-segregated facilities, the distance of water points, and insecurity. Many of these risks have been observed to lead to harmful coping mechanisms, including child marriage, survival sex, and human trafficking.In-depth research conducted using participatory approaches with Rohingya women and girls has revealed that managing their menstrual periods is particularly challenging in this context due to social taboos. These taboos impact how women and girls learn about their periods, access necessary menstrual products, and manage their periods in the camps.Focus group discussions and reports have indicated that reporting barriers for survivors include pressure and fear from parents and community leaders, as well as the limited participation of women and girls in household decision-making. Risks of forced marriage with the perpetrator and a lack of protection against reprisals are commonly cited concerns. Social stigma, perceptions about survivors, interference by community leaders in handling rape cases, a lack of protection for survivors, insufficient awareness of the consequences of rape, the benefits of services, and service accessibility could significantly delay survivors' decision-making in reporting and accessing services in a timely manner. Specialized GBV services, including multi-sectoral services, will need further enhancement to consider the inclusion of male survivors and individuals with diverse gender and diverse needs.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IOM’s project proposal is aligned with the rationalization principles. IOM consistently coordinates and refers to other protection actors if needed. IOM co-chairs the Anti-Trafficking Working Group (ATWG) and is an active member of GBV Sub Sector as well as all relevant protection forums. In order to ensure continuity and that all our activities are in line with the Protection Sector and do not overlap or become redundant. The GBVSS gap analysis in 2023 reflects IOM continued to provide services following the GBV minimum standards of care and ensured that the services are in line with the approved Joint response plan. IOM has a General Protection, Child Protection, and Counter-Trafficking programming in the camps that complement the GBV work IOM is doing. We are able to provide a variety of services and offer a holistic package to support the beneficiaries we assist. IOM will maintain its collaboration with Deputy Comissioner, Upazilla Nirbahi Officer, Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner Office, and Camp-in-charge Offices at the camp level through regular coordination and capacity-building efforts in the GBV sector. We also developed good coordination with the CiCs and regular coordination with other stakeholders in the camps, that allow us to conduct our work without any issues.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

By placing protection at the center of its humanitarian response, IOM implements a comprehensive array of multi-sectoral and integrated activities in Cox's Bazar. These efforts are aimed at ensuring that individuals affected by the crisis can exercise their fundamental rights without hindrance, free from any form of violence, coercion, or deprivation. The goal is to empower them with the capacity and resources needed to build resilience against any future harm and ensuring that the safety and dignity of these individuals are front and center of our programming. IOM views protection as a shared responsibility, one that reduces risks and vulnerabilities by enhancing the dignity, safety, resilience, and meaningful participation of all those affected by the crisis. IOM assists individuals in need of specialized services through its integrated protection specialists, which may encompass general protection, counter-trafficking, or child protection. The GBV team operates with a survivor-centered approach, guided by IOM's GBViC institutional framework. IOM’s will continue to conduct protection monitoring, community consultation, safety audit and risk mapping sessions to understand the specific needs, safety and security concerns for people of all genders and ages, including people with disabilities. IOM’s community engagement and activities ensures a ‘Do No Harm’ approach. IOM will continue to conduct capacity sessions for its protection and non-protection community volunteers, community advocates, women support groups to ensure that the community have improve access to information about services and supports available to them, support them in informed decision making. IOM’s Cash for Work initiatives specifically target vulnerable women and EVIs. By empowering women economically, these initiatives promote women's empowerment, challenge negative social norms surrounding women, and fosters positive coping mechanisms for both women and their families.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Age considerations, especially child safeguarding mechanisms, are an integral part of all areas of IOM programming. These mechanisms are deeply embedded at every stage, from development to implementation and evaluation, with a continuous focus on ensuring the best interests of the child are upheld at all times.Within IOM's WGSS, Child-friendly corners are featured, providing a dedicated space for children that is managed by trained staff while their mothers participate in activities. IOM implements the MBR (Mother’s and Sister’s Ways) Edition 1-2, an age-appropropriate two-way exchange of culturally acceptable and accessible. IOM will also implement Girl Shine Curriculum to support, protect, and empower adolescent girls including the caregivers. IOM also implements EMAP which offers an evidence-based prevention model for engaging men. IOM’s WGSS and community outreach offers age-appropriate services including for elderly from the community. Since the inception of its GBV programme, IOM is working with people of all genders and ages including person with disabilities to ensure their meaningful participation in its intervention. IOM will ensure its inclusive approach in all areas of GBV risk mitigation, supporting survivors and addressing root causes by involving them in consultation, awareness raising, capacity sessions as well as risk mapping. Most of IOM’s facilities are persons with disability friendly and IOM will continue to work towards making all facilities inclusive. IOM collects Sex, Age, Disability and Diversity disaggregated data for all of its activities to ensure finding gaps, trends and inform the ongoing interventions to have adjusted according to the needs of the people of all genders and ages. IOM remains an advocate for Age and Disability inclusion. This commitment ensures that our efforts are inclusive and respectful of the unique needs and circumstances of all individuals, regardless of their background, age, gender, or abilities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM's GBV program prioritized its community-based and participatory approaches, which aim to cater to a diversity of needs and ensure inclusive participation. IOM is actively engaged in seeking feedback from the affected population to gain insights into emerging risks and threats and to shape how activities are carried out. This feedback process involves consultation on various aspects, such as who will be involved.IOM will continue to conduct gender and age responsive Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) surveys and other types of response monitoring through structured questionnaires, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) desks, case management feedback surveys, and regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and consultations within WGSS and at the community level, including individuals with disabilities and those with diverse gender identities, including those with diverse sexual orientations and sex characteristics, are utilized to maintain a community-driven and ""do-no-harm"" approach.IOM has a well-established PSEA hotline, online platform, feedback boxes within the WGSS and other facilities for the community for providing feedback. IOM also appointed PSEA champions from the staff in all IOM AoR camps who regularly provide capacity sessions to the volunteers, community-based support groups to ensure that are well informed about the reporting mechanism.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM is committed to ongoing efforts related to the PSEA. It will continue to roll out PSEA training packages and offer Training of Trainers (ToT) sessions for PSEA Champions in the coming year. This is intended to ensure that IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers adhere to the PSEA guiding principles and mandatory reporting systems. IOM has a designated and dedicated PSEA Officer to support and address the concerns of the beneficiaries. IOM has a robust reporting mechanism (hotline, online platform) in place and will continue to actively participate in the PSEA network while providing support to implementing partners (IPs) and the Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) in terms of capacity building and advocacy. IOM provides PSEA training to partner and ensure that partners has PSEA policy and established reporting mechanism in place. IOM regularly delivers adapted PSEA training packages for the community volunteers, women support groups and other community-based groups. IOM also disseminates IEC materials on SEA for community to ensure their understanding and accessibility on reporting is in place. IOM completed assessing 8 IP’s to understand the capacities on PSEA. IOM will continue to assess the remaining IPs in 2024. IOM and WFP – in partnership with Translators without Borders had launched PSEA at the Frontline, a campaign to further empower frontline humanitarian workers and partners on PSEA through a comprehensive package of communication and outreach materials.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM, along with its implementing partners, is an integral part of the Rapid Protection Assessment team, Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU), and Mobile Medical Team (MMT). This ensures an immediate response to the needs of people affected by crises and disasters. They maintain close coordination with Site Management and the Camp-in-Charge (CiC) offices and provide support to Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers, offering training on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and safe referrals for gender-based violence (GBV).IOM also remains committed to raising awareness about Protection Key Messages, with the aim of helping the community mitigate the impact of disasters before, during, and after they occur. This effort includes house-to-house visits conducted by trained volunteers to ensure that individuals with disabilities and other vulnerable populations receive the necessary support. Moreover, IOM and its implementing partner, PULSE Bangladesh Society, will continue to preposition clothing items and Dignity Kits as part of their emergency preparedness and response activities. IOM’s WGSS in camp 9 is reconstructed with treated bamboo and other environment friendly materials. IOM will replicate such environment friendly approach with other WGSS reconstruction where appropriate to ensure sustainability.
