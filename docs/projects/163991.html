Project Summary

North East Syria (NES) continues to experience conflict and displacement and access to health care has
been further eroded due to the on-going Turkish military operation started on Oct. 2019, particularly in
conflict areas and IDPs camps/settlements in Hassakeh, Deir-ez Zor, Raqqa and Aleppo Gov.
Through this Action, in continuation with previous phases (Jun.’17-Apr.'20), UPP aims to ameliorate living conditions and save lives of war-affected persons in NES. Action will improve safe and equal access to primary and secondary care and referral management system, together with increased health and protection prevention and outreach. The Action continues support for primary health care facilities in Raqqa and Areesha, Abu Khashab and Mahmoudli camps, expanding its support to Al Hol camp. Access to quality and timely emergency care and secondary health care will be further improved through the set-up of a NES-wide Emergency Management Coordination Center for an enhanced ambulance referrals management system.
Self-protection capacities of war-affected NES population will be enhanced through increased protection
mainstreaming, awareness-raising, and capacity building of local actors. Through community-based
approaches partners will provide prevention outreach in health and protection to support local resilience. It
will provide health and all other services to all free of charge.
The expected result is to reach 100.000 beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees, refugees, local population) over 12 months. 180 local staff (management, medical, non-medical, protection) are expected to be trained on combined health and protection capacity building programmes. In such a volatile context Action provides the capacity to quickly adapt to emerging health needs in other NES locations.

4 - Needs

RAQQA GOV.: 200.000 ind. had returned in Raqqa city since battle ended in Oct. 17. Health was the most commonly reported need (REACH, Dec. '19). HFs status remained critical: only 1 of the 2 public hospitals was re-opened and only 4 facilities offered free PHC, including UPP CPHCC in west Raqqa. The clinic, located in one of the most populated areas (catchment pop. est. at 50.000), serves an average of 200 patients/day (UPP, Need Assessment, Nov.’19). UPP ambulances provided the main emergency referrals service and outreach in rural Raqqa sub-district. In Mahmoudli camp, the population increased in the last month up to more than 8.000 ind., when families from Ain Issa camp were relocated there. UPP runs an Emergency Health Post since Sept. 2019, to cover the gaps of emergency care 24h and referral services as previously identified by the HWG and the local Health Committee. The need of scaling-up health services has been assessed by the HWG and the Health Committee. HASSAKEH GOV.: In Dec. 2018, Al Hol camp was exposed to a massive arrival of IDPs, increasing camp population from 10.000 to more than 70.000. Health situation was critical, with many cases of malnutrition and unaddressed war-related trauma. In Oct. 2019, the camp still hosted around 70.000 ind. (IDPs, refugees, TNCs), 96% of which women and children. Health services continued to operate at lower intensity than before 9th Oct. (WoS NES Health Sitrep, Nov.19). Since 2016, UPP supported health services in Al Hol camp, with a PHC in Phase I, assisting an average of 150 patients/day and supporting 55 natural deliveries/month. In Areesha camp, UPP clinic remained the only provider of emergency healthcare 24h, PHC, surveillance of epidemic-prone diseases and referrals to health and protection services. In Abu Khashab camp, where pop. continued to increase throughout 2019, UPP is currently setting up a static PHC that will be the only static HF in the camp, providing also ambulances' referral services.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will focus on ensuring continuity of services established by UPP between 2017-20 in project locations, including PHCs, mobile teams and ambulance network. Health services delivered are in line with the Essential Package of Primary Care for NES and include specialized services for chronic patients, nutrition package, maternal care, referral of cases in need of protection services. New activities include the set-up of an Emergency Management Coordination Centre (EMCC) for ambulance' referrals covering the whole NES. Capacity building in medical and protection modules will continue to be a key activity. Efforts towards strengthening protection mainstreaming and resilience in the affected population both in terms of health and protection through a community based approach will be maintained, through the deployment of outreach workers and awareness raising campaigns in health and protection. UPP will elaborate the MEAL plan at Action onset including updated main data collection tools used to monitor the Action with the support of the MEAL Specialist. UPP developed specific methods and tools for monitoring & evaluation for scenarios in which direct implementation is limited. In these settings, triangulation of information from the field and collection of beneficiary feedback is of utmost importance. Adapted MEAL strategies are associated to each key area: delivery of health care (static&mobile HFs), ambulances management and coordination, stock&warehousing, health&protection prevention awareness and outreach, capacity building. In the HFs, health consultations data is registered on electronic registers by health staff. Registers include specific fields to ensure that consultations are comprehensive as per WHO standards. UPP strategy of implementation for all actions is based on coordination with local, international actors, and stakeholders. This serves to avoid duplication of services, identify gaps, and find programmaticsynergies.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Limited access to services, especially for vulnerable groups identified including women, children, persons with specific needs, PWD, foreign families. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1) Carry out safety audits and community consultations for safe beneficiaries’ access to site locations, including appropriate gender separate areas, confidential spaces, lack of physical barriers; 2) Anti discriminationmeasures include staff training and sensitization, coordination with PwG and advocacy on a local level (camp management etc); 3) Staff recruitment/composition will be culturally and gender sensitive; 4) Staff training in outreach and prevention will increase access to services for women and girls; 5) Staff training and dedicated trained staff for referral to protection specialized staff for case management; 6) Outreach activities and awareness campaigns especially on free and available to all services, including outreachand consultation with most vulnerable groups. Protection risk 2: Risk of Sexual Exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers in activities including in clinics, mobile units and in the community Likelihood: Medium Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1) Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; 2) Train staff on code of conduct and PSEA mechanisms; 3) Increase awareness among communities about PSEA and the complaints mechanism; 4) Deploy Protection specialized staff to monitor protection standards including do-no-harm, with due consideration to working in pairs and allowing gender representation amongst staff; 5) Carry out safety audits for safe beneficiaries’ access to site locations, including appropriate gender separate areas and confidential spaces; 6) Carry out regular consultations through a community participatory approach with women, girls, boys, men and persons with specific needs to mitigate risks.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption behind the Action is that context situation allows supported HFs, ambulances and MUs tobe operative 24/7 to provide life-saving, primary health care and emergency referrals to the war-affectedpopulation, including the wounded. Main risks are the following: - Further escalation of violence between SDF and GoT, and increased waves of population displacement (high risk); - Regained full administrative and political control over NES by the GoS (high risk); - Violence break out between GoS and SDF (medium risk); - Failure of the joint patrolling agreement reached by GoT and GoR and conflict escalation (high risk); - ISIS resurgence and regained control over areas or infiltrates carrying out attacks (high risk); - Use of chemical weapons (medium risk); - Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies for implementing partner; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk); - Cross-border INGOs evacuate expat staff and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (high risk); - HFs, referral hospitals and project staff become a target in the conflict (high risk). Main mitigation strategies include flexible health structures to be relocated according to conflict scenarios. All equipment and staff may be deployed in other locations to respond to emerging priority needs. A second warehouse has been opened in Nov. ’19 in Hassakeh to mitigate the risk of stockloss/inaccessibility in case Amuda area is included in conflict area. Currently, UPP adopts a partial paper-less policy in order to protect staff and beneficiaries confidentiality. UPP has a back-up office in Rumeilan to safely store most valuable assets. IP, through its own Charter of Principles, adheres to ICRC CoC. A joint commission of UPP and KRC boards is established to ensure a common decision making process committed to humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence.
