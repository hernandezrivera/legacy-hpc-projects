Project Summary

Shelter needs across Syria have significant increases since 2016 as more large displacements have occurred, economic capacity has worsened, and the supply side of available housing has further decreased causing rent prices to be unaffordable for over half the country’s population. The UN rightly identify Shelter as “one of the most challenging sectors in a protracted and volatile conflict. Mass displacements have forced IDPs to find transitional shelter by sharing space with a host family, renting at high prices, squatting in unfinished or partially damaged buildings, or staying in a donated tent as part of a formal or informal IDP community. These haphazard shelter solutions increase protection risks to women, young girls and children because they lack privacy and utility services like water and electricity often do not function. Aleppo reports the second highest governorate for HHS reporting shelter inadequacy issues the most significant barrier to making shelter repairs was the expense; either that repairs required a professional, which households could not afford, or that households are able to make repairs but the materials are too expensive- this was more commonly reported than a general lack of shelter materials. Shelter/NFI cluster data clearly demonstrates that shelter units and more adequate shelter space are one of the most critical gaps with a need for increased support directed towards host communities to safeguard coping mechanisms and improve the standard and availability of shelter. Therefore, IYD proposes a more durable solution, focused on shelter rehabilitation, to reduce protection risks for the most vulnerable. In light of recent developments and shifting front lines across Northern West Syria, Albab and Azaz sub-districts continues to be the area most affected by the recent population displacement due to its proximity to the Turkish border. in addition, Idlib governorate received a huge wave of displacements after the agreements took place between the Syrian governmental and free Syrian army (areas of escalation reduction). IYD field assessments found a large majority of houses suffer from damages wherein most doors, windows, walls, and ceiling are broken or completely missing. While the needs are vast, the greatest shelter priorities in these areas are for materials, skills, and labor to repair houses. Utilities such as running water and electricity within the homes are also lacking. Protection is also a high need in the areas. These protection risks and security threats are further exacerbated by the vulnerability to health risks that these residents face from insects and rodents, leaking water, lack of protection offered to the weather and elements, lack of hygiene products and a general dissatisfaction due to the lack of state of WASH facilities at a household level. Through this project IYD is planning to intervene in four locations at the district level, Aleppo governorate, Azaz, and Albab district, Idlib governorate, Ariha, and Jisr Ash shugur district, those four locations have a large number of damaged houses. Under this project, IYD will rehabilitate 800 partially damaged houses between 40 % – 70 %, 300 houses in Aleppo governorate, and 500 houses in Idlib governorate, by reconstruction the destroyed walls, install new doors, and windows, and rehabilitate the water services, the estimated total number of targeted beneficiaries 5,000 individuals, also, there are 400 unfinished houses needs to rehabilitate to be suitable for human dignity, IYD under this project will construct wall to form separate rooms, and provide new materials such as doors, windows, and lighting systems, in addition, the rehabilitation of the water services will be done by providing water tanks, toilets, and baths, beneficiaries will be 1,500 individual. IYD finds that residents are willing to contribute to improvement projects; specifically, to provide labor to support the repair and rebuilding of the accommodations,

Needs

The number of people in need of shelter assistance has increased in overall Syria especially in Idleb, and Aleppo governorates. Overall, refer to the 2019 HNO report, the severity of need has increased by only 21 percent of sub-districts. Overall 300,000 more people now living in severity 4 and 5 areas over the last year with an overall increase in the number of people in need of 570,000. There has also been a significant worsening in overall shelter arrangements in Syria, with 36 percent of sub-districts reporting an increase in the proportion of communities living in damaged or temporary shelters. The acute humanitarian situation in the Northwest is driven by the high proportion of IDPs, overcrowding and the lack of shelter capacity, high rental prices and unaffordable items, and poor access to markets. A considerable number of people live in shelters with at least one adequacy issue, including lack of heat and insulation, lighting, necessary bathing or cooking facilities, inadequate space, lack of privacy and the inability to securely lock their home. The shelter adequacy issues mentioned by different age and gender groups were similar, with no notable difference observed. Approximately 680,000 households cannot carry out necessary repairs to their homes, most frequently because they cannot afford the materials and/or professional assistance required to carry out the rehabilitation, and due to a lack of skilled labor. As in previous years, the primary providers of shelter assistance and support are host households with an estimated 520,000 households hosting others. HLP issues remain a challenge on many fronts. Repair and rehabilitation of housing are only pursued following confirmation of HLP rights, however, many displaced people do not have sufficient documentation (many never had proper or formal documentation or lost them during displacement), and the processes and infrastructure to formalize or recuperate documents is not uniformly functional or accessible. There is a continued risk of eviction and/or exploitation with major protection implications (especially for particularly vulnerable groups), while there are also the risks of rights violation (either of owners or occupants) and future disputes due to the difficulties in demonstrating ownership. as a response to these humanitarian needs in Idlib and Aleppo governorates where the severity are 4 and 5 and due to a large number of IDPs in the two governorates (the IDPs number in Idleb is 1,125,234 and in Aleppo 990,494 as shelter PIN 2019 indicated).IYD’s field staff in Northern Syria worked with local councils, IDPs groups, and communities to identify the needs in each community, in Idlib governorate, Jisr Ash Shugur, and Ariha districts, and in Aleppo governorates, Albab and Azaz districts, data analysis was done to determine the essential and urgent needs in the shelter sector in the targeted locations, the survey results came as follow:70% of houses in Jisr Ash Shugur district and Ariha district, Idlib governorate, was exposed to air strikes caused damaged to the houses where 1200 house in Jisr Ash Shugur and 600 houses in Ariha need to rehabilitate, also in Albab district and Azaz district, Aleppo governorate, the houses were exposed to air strikes and bombing 60% of Albab city partially damaged, in Azaz city the reports mentioned 40% of houses need to rehabilitate, the majority of these needs in the proposed locations are to rehabilitate the houses partially damaged and unfinished houses to improve the shelter, by providing the necessary materials to reconstruction the destroyed walls, rehabilitate the doors, windows, and the water services in the houses, and shelter units ( baths, toilets, and sewerage lines were all out of services as the reports mentioned). The installation of a solar lighting system very acute need in case of lack of electricity to provide protection to women, girls, and children.

Implementation of activities

IYD planned to implement this project through the intervention in the following main activities to achieve the cluster objectives, Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support, by Rehabilitation of shelter units and transitional shelters (in-kind, physical repair, etc.), IYD determined 20 shelter unit and 400 unfinished houses to be rehabilitated in Ariha and Azaz districts, the beneficiaries from this activity will be 5,000 individuals. Objective 2: Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure, through Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/host families (can include: materials, cash-for-work, labour, etc.), 800 damaged houses will be rehabilitated in Jisr Ash Shugur and Albab districts, the total beneficiaries will be 5,000 individuals. Training of stakeholders on resilience and quality-oriented shelter / NFI skills and capacities individuals, the methodology of implementing these activities will be as following: IYD will hire the required staff to implement the project. IYD team in coordination with the local authorities will publish an announcement in the targeted locations to allow the beneficiaries register to benefit from the project, this registration will be through criteria after the registration process completed, the verification list will be checked by MEAL team, • Needs Assessment: The technical engineer will conduct a technical assessment in the proposed areas, 800 partially damaged houses in Jisr Ash Shugur and Albab, 400 unfinished houses in Azaz and Ariha, 20 shelter units in Albab and Ariha,• BOQ and Estimates: based on the technical assessment the engineers will prepare the BoQs of the project's activities needs, this BoQs will contain the unit number of doors, windows, walls, and all accessories need to rehabilitate the water services, and the unit price for each item. • Conduct the procurement/Bidding Process as per IYD’s Procurement Policy.• Review of agreements/Purchase order by project Coordinator or relevant staff and signing of agreements by supplier/IYD's Purchase order. IYD will contract with contractors who will undertake the work, this contract type will be a large volume of work.• Site Layout/commencement of work: project Officer and project assistant to perform site layout for the supplier/contractor.• The sample of Preparation and approval: Supplier to submit the sample of all materials and get it approved by technical engineers before any supplies to the site, project Officer to document the samples approval with the report describing the quality of material along with pictures for the record.• Conduct quality control/monitoring visits to the contractor's commodities stores.• Completion and Close-out: project Officer to check all the works completed at the site and ensure compliance with standard and specifications in BOQ and Service Agreement, then prepare the handover letters in presence of the formed committee to the local authorities.HLP: IYD will ask the beneficiaries of this project to sign paper contain a declaration of house property or rent, the beneficiary should provide evidence for the house property or rent, in case of rental the houses the house’s owners will ensure that they will not ask the family living in the houses to evacuate the houses after finishing the rehabilitation, and will not be asking for rental, this will be under the local authorities responsibility, the implementing of the project activities will be managed by the project officer in the field and project manager in Turkey, where field visits will be conducted by the project manager to the field in periodic visits. IYD will depend on the shelter repair and rehabilitation guidelines as base guidance in implementing this project.

Protection Risk Analysis

IYD has a security and safety team that conducts a security assessment for its projects by receiving information on security situations from its network of local councils and civil society activists, as well as by remaining continuously apprised of security situations through bulletins released by the international community. IYD has a good reputation as a non-profit organization and works side by side with the community to empower and encourage the community ownership of facilities. Accordingly. For emergency relief projects, when a security situation arises threatens the safety of staff, the staff is redirected to a nearby, safer location until the area is safe enough to conduct the aid or distribution project. IYD will coordinate together in the implementing process and both of the teams will work together to ensure the safety and security of the team in the field.
