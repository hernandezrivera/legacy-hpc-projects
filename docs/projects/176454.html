Project Summary

This project is designed to effectively contribute to the protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods of the most vulnerable and affected population in Rumbek East, Rumbek Center and Rumbek North Counties of Lakes State and Pariang, Mayendit and Leer of Unity State. The project is a one year humanitarian response plan targeting 64,196 HHs equivalent to 385,177 beneficiaries of vulnerable IDPs, returnees, refugees, and host communities; designed to make use of the windows of opportunity for targeted population facing acute food insecurity. It is very much consistent with the FSL allocation both in terms of the Priority, Geographical scope, and the seasonality of the activities. The major livelihood activities in the selected area for response is support for crop, vegetable and fish production with resilient light training.
A total of 385,177 vulnerable individuals will be targeted and selected from the targeted areas. These will include men, women boys and girls selected from the IDP, returnees, refugees, pastoralists and host population. The targeting will be done with gender lens and consideration of incorporation of Accountability to affected population. A total of 120CAHWs will be trained and 800,000 livestock will be targeted for treatment and vaccination. The program implementation will ensure that Do No Harm principle is adhered to as transparency and accountability to the affected population is placed right at the center of beneficiary targeting and provision of support. Humanitarian Aid for Development extensive presence and history in Lakes and Unity state coupled with an in-depth understanding of the community conflict dynamics and tailored approaches to specific communities will mitigate risk and reduce any potential negative impacts of the project and any protection issues.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity in South Sudan continues to cause untold suffering to the citizens. Out of the estimated 12Million people in the country, an estimated 5.82 million people are projected to be food insecure in the country between the period of Dec 2020 to March 2021. This is expected to get worse between April and July, 2021 pushing the number of food insecure people in the country to 7.24 million. In Unity and Lakes State, the food insecure population at IPC phase 3 and above between December 2020 and March 2021 is estimated to be 606,000 and 496,000 people respectively. This is so detrimental to the health and nutrition status of the community and generally very worrying for food security (IPC, Dec2020). The situation has been compounded by the fact that the protracted conflict and its effects are still rife in the community. The cost of living is too high thus the cost of food is almost unaffordable by most of the population. The consumption gap has been created and the food basket has reduced. People have resorted to negative coping strategies (FSNMS 26). The drivers of this persistent food insecurity include localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality (IPC, Dec2020). The frequent escalating conflict especially in our areas of southern and central Unity state and Lakes has negatively impacted and therefore disrupted markets and supply chains, compounded by a seriously general economic crisis and COVID 19 prevention measures affecting the whole country (IPC, Dec2020). According to the FSNMS 26, Food insecurity is expected to worsen due to poor harvests this year due to factors such as dryspells in some areas and flooding in other areas, compounded by diseases and pests, especially the Fall Army Worm and inadequate farming inputs and fishing equipment (FSNMS 26). All the counties of Unity and Lakes were cereal deficient in 2019 (CFSAM, May2020) and the situation in 2020 is expected to be worse due to floods (IPC, Dec2020). Widespread events of cattle raiding as well as altered marketing/migration routes occurred in most areas of major conflict/insecurity. Due to inadequate veterinary services, livestock diseases have threatened to erode livestock contribution to food security and coping strategies among agro-pastoralists (FSNMS24). This has been further exacerbated by conflict disrupted migratory pattern which end up creating new disease outbreaks, inadequate pasture and water, lootings among others (FSNMS 26). According to FEWSNET, despite the decline in conflict and anticipated improvement in food security, many households have lost key livelihood assets and coping strategies as a result of the protracted conflict. Consequently, high levels of acute food insecurity will persist. Based on past trends, deep food insecurity would be likely if conflict shifts and prevents populations from moving in search of food sources or restricts humanitarian access for a prolonged period of time. In order to sustain long-term food security improvements and a scale-up of assistance is needed. The affected communities do not want to continue depending on food aid and would also like to go back to their normal life and livelihoods. Thus, there is need for support for own production and livestock health services. Humanitarian Aid for Development is currently on ground in our field offices competent and experienced staff who can really deliver and implement this project.

2 - Project activities

Promote emergency food production through main season fishing, agriculture seeds & tool distribution through provision of livelihood kits that include fishing equipment, vegetable seeds and crop seeds.Reducing pest and crop disease through farmers training on integrated pest and disease management, FAW surveillance, detection, prevention and eradication. Livestock vaccination for disease prevention and treatment .Support value chain addition through milk, fish preservation and marketing.Training and capacity building through both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills) and vocational training.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Humanitarian Aid for Development will provide beneficiaries with information about protection against sexual exploitation and Abuse(PSEA) during community engagement forums. Through these forums we will strive to raise awareness on issues relating to PSEA by engaging women, girls, boys and men as well as the local leadership.Humanitarian Aid for Development staff will actively be involved in making sure that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements regarding the project being implemented in their region. For instance beneficiaries entitled to the livelihood assistance will be informed that they are not expected to provide any forms of sexual favors and that demand for favors in exchange of livelihood assistance is strictly prohibited.We will actively put mechanisms that will enable beneficiaries to know how and where to report PSEA incidences assuring them safety and confidentiality. We will go the extra mile in ensuring that information regarding PSEA issues are disseminated in community meetings, forums and trainings.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We will ensure that feedback mechanism put in place are sensitive to age gender and diversity and accessible to the most vulnerable members in the community. We will further consult with the vulnerable population representatives to gain a greater understanding of their views, needs and concerns.The beneficiaries will have the opportunity to be involved in project design, planning and decision-making concerning activities as well as opportunities to lead or support the implementation of the activities. The participation is expected to be meaningful and enable beneficiaries to help shape, be involved in, and – whenever possible – lead on some of the activities. We will make a priority during implementation to make the beneficiaries understand when and how they will participate and how decisions will be made. This will be discussed and agreed with beneficiaries. Persons with disabilities often experience heightened protection risks because of their disability, and being a woman or a child with disability further compounds these vulnerabilities. Girls and women with disabilities endure violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation twice as often, over a longer period of time, and suffer more serious injuries as a result of violence compared to women without disabilities. Women and girls with intellectual disabilities are at a particularly higher risk of violence, including sexual violence. As such, these special groups will also be identified, considered and encouraged to provide their feedback in a manner that they are comfortable in.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Humanitarian Aid for Development will work closely with community leaders representing minorities in the community such as the disabled persons, women, youth and the elderly. Through collaborative dialogues with these representatives we will be able to ensure the voices are heard and we will further explore ways in incorporating feedback and complain mechanism in project implementation activities.During program inception meetings, we actively involve the community representatives in discussing the program design and delivery. This ensures that HAD put in mechanisms that takes account the specific needs of the vulnerable populations .Through dialogues we are able to address concerns relating to constraints to accessing livelihood inputs and inclusion .Humanitarian Aid for Development actively conducts sensitization and awareness meetings across the payams informing the beneficiaries the importance of providing feedback and educating them on the various ways of offering feedback in the field setup. For instance, feedback can be presented directly to the local authorities, community representatives or directly to the HAD staff.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Humanitarian Aid for Development will use beneficiaries’ feedback to improve quality of activities implemented in the area of operations. From this feedback we will be able to make necessary adjustments to accommodate the beneficiaries’ needs especially when the information provided promotes accountability to the community. Beneficiary feedback will be used throughout the program.Humanitarian Aid for Development actively collects beneficiary feedback through specific data collections tools .We have further trained our staff to actively listen and solicit feedback when engaging with the beneficiaries throughout the project cycle. At all times we strive to offer immediate response by communicating to the relevant authorizes concerned. We actively negotiate with our donors and with the local authority to act on beneficiary feedback. We have also put in mechanisms that promote clear referrals pathways for feedback.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The major drivers of food insecurity that drives the populations to worse IPC phases include low cereal production, effects of climatic shocks such as floods and droughts and shortage of farming, fishing inputs and veterinary services. The intervention is geared towards addressing some of these issues through training of households on improved agronomic and fisheries practices, climate change adaptation and provision of farming and fishing inputs that include seeds, tools and fishing gear. Additionally, CAHWS will be trained to provide community animal health services. In the end, the amount of food produced will increase and the communities will be better adapted to changing climatic conditions. Therefore, more people will become food secure and reduce the populations in IPC phase 3 and above.Youth also are an important resource to the community, and are normally abused in conflicts and intercommunal clashes that include cattle rustling. They will be actively engaged in issues of livestock rearing, farming and fisheries that will ultimately benefit their families and the community at large.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Although cross-sectoral services are vast, HAD will provide services related to food security and livelihoods. We will also coordinate with other agencies responding in other humanitarian sectors and share information on where there are need gaps. We will also strive to share beneficiaries where possible in order for the affected communities to receive a holistic humanitarian assistance. We will also coordinate with the relevant government institutions in order to provide timely, coordinated and effective assistance to the affected communities.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This will be used as one of the vulnerability criteria for selection of beneficiaries. Households with malnourished children and pregnant and lactating mothers will be given priority for registration to receive livestock interventions, livelihood inputs and training especially on vegetable and fish production and nutritious utilization and livestock products value addition.
