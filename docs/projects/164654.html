Project Summary

Project aims to provide non-formal education services (Remedial, catch up, ALP programs) for 4,800 low-achieving and out-of-school children. While the intervention provides non-formal education activities, but it will feed into the formal education process by striving to reintegrate out of school children in the formal education as well as improving the performance of children who are not doing well or who skipped classes and thus, sustain their enrollement in school by eleminating the risk of drop out.
The program is tailored to be conducted through 3 cycles, each one last for 3 months. This non- formal education response will include 8 schools in the three targeted locations (Afrin, Azaz, Jebel Saman). The project includes capacity building program for teachers and educational staff to enhance their performance and increase of the quality of services provided; the training’ agenda include PSS, active learning, teaching on crisis, classroom management.etc and providing incentives for 96 of teachers and educational staff (64 teachers, 8 principals, 8 education supervisors, 8 PSS and life skills facilitators and 8 cleaners). To encourage children with disabilities and their parents to enroll in schools, transportation services will be provided on daily basis targeting 50 CWDs per cycle. Through the project light rehabilitation (of schools) will be undertaken to improve the learning and friendly environment; schools will be provided with IT equipment, solar systems and running materials on monthly basis. Rehabilitate, improve or construct gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities to make schools more hygienic spaces. At start of each cycle, BTL campaigns will be carried out to encourage children to enroll in schools in target communities through distribution of brochures conducting ceremonies and distributing gifts reaching 2000 children per cycle.
Children will receive classes for three main subjects which Arabi, English and Math in line with school-based psychosocial support and life skill programs as integration with education classes to enhance wellbeing of children.
Provide textbooks, school bags and stationery for children will be a tool to encourage the children enroll and alleviate the burden of expenses on parents. The teachers will receive teaching material to be used during lessons, this material will empower teachers to be out traditional methods in teaching and use illustrations tools.
Establish school-based governance and accountability mechanisms to support school operations through PTA and conduct regular meeting to discuss this mechanism.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2019, 5.9 million of children in need to essential education services. 2.1 million children in Syria are out of school, and a further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. Displaced communities remain significantly underserved, with most IDP camps having insufficient or non-existent education services. More than one in three schools are damaged or destroyed, while others are used for purposes not related to education such as shelter for displaced persons. Schools in IDP/returnee receiving communities have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students, causing overcrowding.Lack of school safety remains a critical barrier to children’s access to education. 762 attacks on education have been reported, 426 of which are verified, since the crisis started in 2011.JENA’s statistics (The Joint Education Needs Assessment), which was issued by ACU under supervision of Save the Children and cluster, targeted 112 communities across North of Syria within non-governmental areas in the governorates of Idleb, Aleppo and Hama to cover Out of School Children (OOSC), stated that 66% (1,130,229 children) are attending schools and 34% (582,239 children) are out of school. The study revealed that the rates of OOSC are rising in later schooling stages and always higher among females. Based on JENA’s assessment, male OOSC between 6-10 years of age – representing the basic stage (first cycle: from 1st to 4th grade) - constitute 16% of total male children, whereas female OOSC from the same age group constitute 18%.Male OOSC between 11-14 years of age – representing the basic stage (second cycle: from 5th to 9th grade) constitute 29% of total male children, whereas female OOSC from the same age group constitute 33%.Male OOSC between 15-18 years of age – upper-secondary school (from 10th to 12th grade) constitute 52% of total male children, whereas female OOSC from the same age group constitute 57%.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project includes; 1. Providing non-formal education program (catch-up, SLP, remedial, and literacy and numeracy classes) by targeting 4,800 children (6-18), of those who are with low attainment and dropped out, with a non-formal education program in order to enhance their educational level\refer them to formal education. The NFE will be implemented in 8 schools through 3 cycles, each lasts for 3 months. Besides the NFE classes, which mainly include Arabic, English and Maths, PSS sessions will be provided by Bahar's CP outreach teams, who in turn will refer CP cases to the CP caseworkers. BTL campaigns will be conducted before cycles targeting in total 6000 beneficiaries. As aforementioned, the NFE cycles will be implemented in 8 schools, note that these schools will be selected in coordination with the EDs and other NGOs providing educational services in the targeted locations to avoid overlapping\duplication. Large\light scale rehabilitation, including rehabilitation of wash facilities taking gender consideration into account, will be undertaken to these schools based on needs assessment; afterwards, the schools will be prepared with the needed logs equipment, including furniture, IT equipment, solar system..etc and will be supported by running costs covering stationery and interactive learning materials for teachers to enhance the learning quality, water, cleaning materials, and fuel for heating. As well as, children will be provided with bags/recreational items/textbooks/and curriculum to facilitate the learning process and increase their commitment to the NFE cycles. In addition, transportation will be provided for 120 CwDs to ease their access to schools. NFE teaching staff and education personnel will receive monthly incentives and training on PSS, PFA, identification and referral of CP cases to Bahar’s caseworkers. Also, Bahar will establish PTAs and will hold regular meetings with parents and teachers to discuss the challenges and areas of improvement.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Children are over-exposed to the risk of harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities.Mitigation; Learning facilities should have Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place and these should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed. Members of the community or local authorities try to over-influence or take actions that prevent, or try to change, the type of support provided to education services or who will benefit from the support provided.Mitigation;Projects should be designed to be context and conflict sensitive. Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling...etc,. Mitigation Preparedness training such as self-care, first aid, fire drills, evacuation drills and stay-and-shelter drills should be regularly practiced. Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Students participate in bullying of students. Mitigation,Teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. The application of learning should be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support. Students and their caregivers should be sensitized on the prevention, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying and the systems in place to present and response to it.5.Learning facilities are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.). Mitigation;Learning facilities should be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected.

Coordination with all authorized bodies to implement activities smoothly.4. children complete there education classes without drop-out.5. All education staff familiar with protection mainstreaming in education.6. Number of children with disabilities enroll in school.
