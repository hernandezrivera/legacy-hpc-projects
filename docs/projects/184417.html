Project Summary

According to UN OCHA 2021 Somalia Humanitarian Needs Overview Protection risks are increasing and the need to centralize protection throughout response activities to the most vulnerable is vital
Elevated protection risk exposure Protection concerns remain at the centre of the humanitarian crisis with a diverse range of protection issues in Somalia, including violations of international human rights and humanitarian law. At least 1.1million internally displaced persons (IDPs) and other vulnerable people, notably women, children, minorities, the disabled, child and female-headed households, survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation (particularly children), or older persons without support structures, are exposed to protection risks, such as forced evictions, discrimination based on status, child rights violations and child labour, family separations and gender-based violence (GBV), such as rape and sexual assault, due to conflict and protracted displacement as well as natural hazards. Settlements are often ungoverned, or governed by gatekeepers, overcrowded and displaced people have limited access to protective shelter, safe water and sanitation facilities as well as other basic needs.
Based on ongoing reports from UNHCR PRMN’s partners in the field, new displacements About
1,780households (approximately 10,700individuals) displaced by drought were recorded as arriving in various parts of the country High levels of displacement lack of social support systems and family breakdown mean children are at high risk of separation from their families which puts them at risk of trafficking, sexual exploitation. Separated and unaccompanied (UASC) girls are often “hidden” as a result of being absorbed into community structures often taking on the burden of domestic labour and child care. The current droughts in the target areas requires action to prevent family separation and respond when separation does occur through the provision of comprehensive IDTR and case management services for UASC.
JF will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism that will support the beneficiaries in accessing the services and recording their complaints and feedback regarding the project. The project will implement Protection monitoring activities and conduct service mapping, the organization will work with the local authorities to improve protection service provision in the target locations. The organization will conduct safety audits, this will include also child safety walks in 20 IDP sites and the reports will be shared with the relevant stakeholders. High risk IDP sites will be targeted with this activity.
The aim of this project is to respond to the drought by providing CP services in the target regions to protect child rights and safety, prevent critical deterioration of their physical and mental health and promote their short and longer-term well being. The activities to achieve the above includes: Monitoring of Child Recruitment in camps and host communities, Identification Documentation, Tracing and Reunification (IDTR),legal aid services, recreational activities and psycho social services, interim care services, reintegration services and referrals to other sectors as well as supporting expansion the Community-Based Child Protection (CBCP), Material assistance such as Clothing, dignity kits, solar lamps. The project will also focus on creation of awareness on GBV, Establishment of referral pathways, Distribution of Dignity kits to GBV victims and survivors. Support psycho social services among other activities.
The project will also focus on filling the information gaps on Housing land and property Juba foundation will work with the local authorities to prevent the risk of Eviction and especially for the most vulnerable IDPs, people living with disabilities and the most vulnerable women and child headed HHs in the project target area. JF will also provide HLP services in the project target areas.

1. - Projects activities

Juba Foundation will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism in all the target districts, this will be available for all the members of the community who will be able to record complaints and any feedback they may have during the project implementation. It is expected that the organization will record at least 500 complaints and feedbacks during the project duration. Juba foundation will conduct safety audits, this will include also child safety walks in 20 IDP sites and the reports will be shared with the relevant stakeholders. High risk IDP sites will be targeted with this activity. Juba foundation will conduct training on for at least 100 participants with 50% of the trainees being women on child protection. Training of Case Workers on, case management for GBV, clinical management of rape. Juba Foundation in coordination with the Child Protection sub cluster to conduct Case workers training on, case management for GBV, clinical management of rape in the target two regions. The training will be conducted in close collaboration with the Child Protection sub cluster CMR and their pool of trainers, line ministries and the GBV sub clusters. Participants will be selected in close collaboration with the Child Protection and GBV sub cluster. The goal of the training sessions is to educate the service providers on Child Protection, confidentiality, how to ensure correct treatment of GBV casesJuba Foundation will conduct a training that will involve stakeholder’s i.e. the local authorities, NGOs and the community leaders on the need to prevent GBV and PSEA. This will be mainly to give information and Knowledge on the subjectsJuba Foundation will give IEC materials with GBV information that will target at least 500 male and female, IEC materials will also consider people living with disabilities so that the messages can reach to all. Case management and referrals to other Sectors. For JF to be able to provide Child Protection service, the nurse shall first be trained on CMR and SCA approaches in caring for both adult and child survivors. The treatment and care will be offered by the nurses at JF Health centers children will benefit from medical treatment including post rape treatment within 72 hours.The organization will provide psychosocial support for FGM/GBV in existing GBV one stop centers and women and girls' safe spaces.JF will provide legal aid and information services to the victims and the Survivors of GBV, women and girls will have access to these services.JF will provide a complete 500 dignity kit, with menstrual hygiene kit and solar lanterns s to GBV survivors in the target location women and girls living with disability will be given priority.JF will provide psychosocial support to the caregivers, this will be mainly to give support and to improve child caregiver/parent relationship.JF will promote child Unification for the identified unaccompanied and separated children, these children will be united with their primary caregiver’s 500 boys and girls will be unified.JF will hold dialogues with the local authorities and line ministries to prevent eviction of at least 300 women and men.JF will reach out to 500 beneficiaries both men and women on HLP services, this will be mainly to cover the existing gaps on eviction. JF together with other NGO will conduct protection monitoring the organization will train the staff on SPMS, and reports will be shared with stakeholders.JF will provide MHPP services to the people living with disabilities and the elderly and especially those living in high risk IDP sites and those who have gone through trauma, the services will be available for both men and women. At least 200 members will be targeted
