Project Summary

The action contributes to the third strategic objective of the HRP 2022 as well as the Strategic outcome 1 National plan of the Ministry of Social Development "MoSD", by enhancing food security for highly vulnerable population affected by protracted crisis through improving access to diversified, sufficient and nutritious food. The overall objective is to contribute to reduced social and economic risks and vulnerabilities among crises-affected populations of all gender, age and abilities in the Gaza Strip. It will strongly participate to address the huge humanitarian needs in general and food insecurity levels, specifically under two main interventions with strong links between the Humanitarian Assistance and Social Protection. The main outcomes of the action are: (i) Crisis-affected persons of all gender, ages and abilities in the Gaza Strip have equitable and safe access to multi sectorial needs through Multi-Purpose Cash “MPC” and Cash for Work “CfW” modalities.; and (ii) Enhanced social protection for target population through effective coordination and activation of internal / external referral amongst actors in the Gaza Strip. This component will enhance the social protection of the target communities and individuals when having HHs profiles and protection/empowerment plans prepared and implemented. The projects components are: (i) Cash for Work "CfW" opportunities for 350 men and women who are members of the most vulnerable HHs directly benefiting (1,925 individuals including 514 women, 517 men, 435 girls and 459 boys, among them 48 PWDs 50%female and 50%male) for 6 months creating a total of 46200 paid working days. The project will target 50 graduates and 300 laborers (65% Men and 35% Women) who will be placed at CSOs, private sector companies and small-scale farms and food manufacturing factories. The participants will be paid average daily wages of 50 ILS (45 for laborers and 55 for graduates) for 22 working days according to the minimum monthly wages of the approved values within the CfW task force (Graduates 1127 ils and laborers 939 ils). (ii) Multi-Purpose cash assistance to 300 HHs directly benefitting (1,650 individuals including 441 women, 445 men, 372 girls and 392 boys, among them 41 PWDs 50% female and 50% male) for a period of 6 months that will improve their food intake and dietary diversity. The cash provided will be adjusted to the family size and based on the approved transfer value of the Cash Working Group “CWG” (1046 ILS/month). The HHs of the worst scores of socioeconomic vulnerabilities with high food insecurity levels and eroded coping capacities, will be selected and higher priority will be given to FHHs, HHs with protection concerns and HHs with members who are older people, chronically ill, or PWDs. Females will be encouraged to enroll to increase the women participation and engagement rates in the labor market. IRW adheres to protection principles and good practices as guided by the humanitarian standards with special focus on accountability to beneficiaries, applying complaint systems, access to assistance with dignity and gender equality. Also, IRW adheres to PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) among its staff, partners’ and hosting institutions through orientation before the start of the project implementation

1 - Needs

According to 2021 HNO, the overall Humanitarian situation in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains a protracted protection crisis, where more than 1.5 Million of Palestinians in Gaza Strip (more than 70% of population) are in huge need to immediate humanitarian assistance thus are struggle to live with dignity . The main drivers of the protracted crisis in the oPt / Gaza are: About 54 years of the Israeli Occupation “Continuous De-Development process”; Lack of respect to IHL and HRL; Internal Political Division (Since 2007); Recurrent Escalations (2008 - 2012 - 2014 - 2021); and Prolonged Electricity Blackouts. Between May 10th and 21st 2021, the conflict between Israel and armed groups in the Gaza Strip was the most serious escalation since the 2014 conflict. This escalation resulted in significant destruction of homes, businesses and infrastructure, damaging the economy and degrading public services. The impact has exacerbated an already dire humanitarian and development situation that resulted from various conflicts since the mid-2000s. The overall socioeconomic conditions in the oPt / Gaza deteriorated throughout the early months in 2021, driven by the COVID-19 pandemic (11,417 active, 1493 deaths, 158 critical, 462,020 vaccinated - 14.10.2021) the unemployment rate was 45% in the second quarter of 2021 ; poverty rates were above 50% and according to pre-conflict estimates, the World Bank foresees an increase in the poverty rate to 64 percent in Gaza due to the pandemic. An approximately of 80% of Palestinian households were receiving some form of social assistance. According to UNOCHA, more than two million Palestinians across the oPt are severely, or moderately, food insecure which represents a net increase of 300,000 newly food insecure compared to 2019, nearly 72% of the food insecure (About 1.4 million people) live in the Gaza Strip, where the pandemic and the Palestinian Authority’s financial crisis have compounded the socioeconomic impact. These people are estimated to suffer from severe, or moderate food insecurity and will require food assistance, and/or support to maintain their farming, herding or fishing-based livelihoods to generate at least minimal income and reduce their aid dependency. The absence of employment opportunities in the local markets and the poor salaries has led to the withdrawal of people from the labour market, as reflected in the fall in the participation rate, from 39 to 38% between the first and second quarters of 2021. Women’s participation in the labour market in Gaza is dramatically lower than among men and stood at less than 17% in the second quarter of 2021, and unemployment is particularly high among women with 60.4 per cent. In addition to lack of job opportunities, women face social barriers to employment in Gaza, particularly due to mobility restrictions and lack of technical skills in some fields While more than 66 thousand employees lost their jobs during 2020, the recent escalation of conflict is expected to worsen the already weak labour market. Also, in the first quarter of 2021, more than 80 percent of wage employees in the private sector in Gaza earned less than minimum wage according to PCBS. Young people are particularly affected, with unemployment rate of 65.5% at the end of 2020. Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. The households headed by female, PWDs and older persons are with high dependency ratio and they also need support to improve their food security and livelihoods.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Project Initiation and planning: IRW will hire the project team and contract with two implementing partners, then develop the detailed planning and start coordination with relevant actors including MoSD, FSS and CWG. Then, nominating the targeted HHs from the available databases of IRW, recent assessment surveys, MoSD database and local actors will be completed. The selection criteria will focus on the socioeconomic vulnerabilities of the households and the gender makeup of the households. The target beneficiaries include 650 food insecure, vulnerable households (3,575 direct beneficiaries considering the average family size of 5.5 (Food Cluster, 2020)) in Gaza Strip. A list of vulnerable households identified will be finalized and crosschecked with Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and FS cluster. Activity 2: Sensitization session with target group: To have high and two way participatory discussion on project scope, type of assistance, beneficiaries’ rights and entitlements, communication channels, protection and inclusivity principles, PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), complaints and feedback mechanism. Also, obligatory training and orientation are conducted for IRW and partners’ staff on protection and inclusivity principles as well as PSEA. Activity 3: Provision of CFW opportunities to 350 female/male adults, members from the selected households amounting to (1,925 individuals including 514 women, 517 men, 435 girls and 459 boys, among them 48 PWDs) for 6 months with 46,200 paid working days. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. The project will target 50 graduates in skilled/professional positions and 300 laborers in unskilled positions who will be placed at CSOs, private sector companies and small-scale farms and food manufacturing factories. The participants will be paid average daily wages of 50 ILS (45 for laborers and 55 for graduates) for 22 working days/month according to the minimum monthly wages of the approved values within the CfW task force (Graduates 1127 ils and laborers 939 ils). The activity will also include nominating and selecting the hosting institutions, and signing contracts with beneficiaries and hosting institutions. IRW and implementing prartners will ensure conducting ongoing follow-up and issuing the monthly financial payments for each lot in a timely manner. Activity 4: Provision of Multi-Purpose Cash assistance to the poor and food insecure households. IRW will provide (1,650 individuals including 441 women, 445 men, 372 girls and 392 boys, among them 41 PWDs) for a period of 6 months that will improve their food intake and dietary diversity. The cash provided will be adjusted to the family size and based on the approved transfer value of the Cash Working Group “CWG” (1046 ILS/month). The cash assistance provided under activity 3 and activity 4 will enhance the ability of the targeted HHs to purchase food and preparation equipment and other basic needs, repay debts and rent homes which will allow them not only to resume normal food consumption patterns but also providing stability and enhancing resilience capacities of the most affected groups, including children, women, older people and PWDs. Activity 5: Follow up and monitoring: include all forms of MEAL plan with focus on Base-line and End-line reporting, Post-distribution monitoring “PDM”, Results monitoring and satisfaction reporting.

3 - Indicators

IRW will measure progress against the following set of results indicators: (1) # (%) of highly vulnerable HHs (disaggregated into sex, age and ability) who have improved their food security level through equitable access to cash (Target for this indicator is 3,575 (955 women, 962 men, 807 girls and 851 boys, among them 89 PWDs); (2) % reduction in household Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS) for the targeted HHs by the end of the project; (3) % of the targeted HHs improved their Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS) by the end of the project; (4) % of the targeted households who have reduced their Coping Strategy Index (CSI) by the end of the project. The project deliverables will be tracked according to the following indicators: (1) # of F/M individuals (graduates and laborers), from poor and food insecure households who have access to monthly income through cash for work scheme (Target for this indicator is 1,925: 514 women, 517 men, 435 girls, 459 boys); (2) # of full-time working days created (Target 46,200 paid working days); (3) # of F/M individuals from poor and food insecure households who received multi-purpose cash assistance (Target for this indicator is 1,650 (441 women, 445 men, 372 girls, 392 boys). IR will use a robust monitoring system and collect detailed data to measure the achievements against project outcomes, measure impact and mitigate any challenge. Beneficiaries will be monitored after receiving assistance to ensure that they still meet the criteria. The project deliverables will be provided in a manner that reduces exposure to risk and ensures the safety and protection of all groups, including women, PWDs and elderly. A Complaint mechanism will be disseminated to project beneficiaries to collect any complaint, feedback or recommendations. To ensure the protection, quality and accountability mainstreaming, the following indicators will be measured: % of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner; % of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting satisfaction on the intervention; %of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting knowledge/access to CFM; % of the affected population (disaggregated by sex and age) reached through consultation in the establishment of community-based complaint mechanisms (CBCMs), awareness activities and community mobilization interventions on PSEA including how to report SEA-related complaints (disaggregated by type of PSEA communication materials developed for each population group identified). In addition to that, the indicator %of women reporting shared decision making on cash transfer use will be monitored to ensure women empowerment.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

IRW has an effective and reliable complaint and feedback mechanism that will be applied to provides a chance for all beneficiaries especially the most vulnerable to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities. Before the implementation of activities, IRW will organize an inception workshop and introduce the complaint and feedback mechanisms to all stakeholders and target group. The received feedback from beneficiaries will be used to inform programming and improve the project implementation. IRW will determine to what extent it is necessary to adapt the project and messages to the needs captured through feedback and complaints. Should any issue raised exceed the project’s scope, IRW will advocate this situation to the relevant Cluster, local authorities and other humanitarian partners to tackle the issue and ensure onset needs can be covered. For PSEA concerns, IRW has a built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA, in accordance with IASC guidance, and the staff and project beneficiaries are required to report directly to Safeguarding focal point at IRW, and an investigation committee is formulated under auspices of IRW headquarter, to investigate allegations and take appropriate decisions

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

IRW's primary data identified protection risks related to infection/C-19, theft, exploitation, social tension/disagreement inside HH. We will further analyze those risks adhering with the safeguarding policy standard operating procedures’ as the practical arm for DNH to avoid any form of intended or unintended harm of exploitation, abuse, violence, neglect or extortion. This will be done by ensure implementing a detailed participation plan that targets staff, volunteers, partners and participants and includes induction and empowerment activities and tools about CoC, safeguarding policies, rights and entitlements, CRM, as well as the dissemination of information, education and communication materials followed with real time monitoring and satisfaction survey. GBV and PSEA issues, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. The project includes effective use of referral pathways as main component for specialized support in different areas. IRW will also do a conflict analysis, understanding the context where we will be intervening and identifying the relations between the different groups that will be targeted. IRW will act upon understanding these interactions in order to avoid negative impacts, and IRW staff will perform interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to ensure that tensions are not rising in any way as an outcome of the project. Also, local cultural practices and gender sensitivity will be respected at all times by the project team and partners during implementation of activities. IRW will ensure that women, PwDs and older persons are represented in to ensure that their rights, needs, and views are well taken into consideration, and regular monitoring work is conducted to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on key items of the project. These feedbacks will help to continuously improve the intervention and for future learning. Also, beneficiaries and stakeholders will have a voice in the project through complaint mechanisms held in place. A satisfaction survey will be completed at the end of the intervention to assess the final valuation of the beneficiaries toward the project works. Data will be sex-and-age disaggregated to show possible trends and understand barriers that might need to be addressed. Regarding specific harm related to COVID-19, emphasis will be put on applying protective measures and behaviors to ensure the safety and protection of all people involved in the project (HHs, teams of IRW and partners, CfW beneficiaries, and communities).
