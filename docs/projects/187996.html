Project Summary

Syria’s protracted conflict has left 13.2 million people in need of WASH assistance (HNO 2022), undermining the collective objective of public health as a common good, and exacerbating humanitarian needs. In determining projects with the Sector Coordinator, NCA’s principle is to raise awareness and support the equitable distribution of WASH benefits and opportunities, with selected areas as ranked according to severity in the HNO 2022. The main purpose of NCA’s WASH program is to reduce health-related and protection risks among conflict-affected populations by providing sustainable access to safe water, adequate sanitation, and improved hygiene. This will address the need for long term prevention and control measures for maintaining public health, and for promoting socio-economic development among the target communities. The involvement of local stakeholders is important so as humanitarian crises are not used to reduce what needs to be maintained as a public service. The targeting adheres to sector needs and priorities, as identified and reported in the HRP and HNO 2022, this being the best guarantee of a truly good outcome. Activities include the use of renewable energy (solar power) for pumping and distributing water; monitoring and optimising groundwater extraction using remote sensing; operation and maintenance training and tools for pump operators; and the support of vulnerable groups with the distribution of essential hygiene items and awareness that meet family needs and are gender appropriate. In terms of geography, the targeting is informed by the needs as expressed by the severity scale for HNO 2022, with priority given to sub-districts with overall score of 4 or 5. Overall score of 3 is also considered under some conditions, e.g. if corresponding overall score for protection/GBV sectors is high. Relevance with recent, ongoing, and planned projects implemented through NCA directly, and through local partners is also considered.
The selected governorates and potential locations at the sub-district level are: (i) Rural Damascus (Nashabiyeh, Dhameer, Haran Al'awameed, Kafr Batna), (ii) Aleppo (As-Safira, Banan, Hajeb, Dayr Hafir, Tall Ed-daman), (iii) Hama (As-Saan, Suran, Hamra); and (iv) Idlib (Sanjar, Ma'arrat An Nu'man, Abul Thohur, Tamanaah, Khan Shaykun).
In summary, the project will address: a) Access to safe water (also related to public health and protection risks) b) Access to essential hygiene items to most vulnerable groups (women, girls, disabled, elderly, etc.) c) Resilience of public water systems (O&M and environmental protection).
NCA has established office in Damascus since 2021 and has a team consisting of program and support staff. Furthermore, NCA has secured core funding for its direct implementation of WASH activities in selected areas in 2022. This will allow NCA to coordinate with all stakeholders and mobilize financial and human resources for conducting detailed needs assessment in the locations included in this submission. This will be done in communication with relevant stakeholders and targeted communities and in close coordination with the WASH Cluster. Activities will be reported in the 4Ws by NCA's program/MEAL team.

4 - Needs

The drastic decrease in the efficiency of clean water systems, as picked up in the HNO 2022, is primarily due to the shortage of electricity, followed by low capacity for operation and maintenance, and due the destruction of critical infrastructure. The situation leaves an estimated 47% of the Syria population relying on unsafe water to meet their needs (HNO). The lack of safe water among communities and health institutions, in conjunction with COVID-19 pandemic, has also compromised public health, and has meant increased prevalence of waterborne diseases. HNO 2022 also reports that the depreciation of the SYP is pushing vulnerable groups into adopting negative coping mechanisms, e.g. in relation to safe water and basic hygiene items. Specifically, subdistricts that are targeted score high, 3 or above, on the overall WASH severity score, except Khan Shaykhun in Idlib, which will be targeted in 2023 and depending how the humanitarian situation will develop. NCA’s own needs assessment for GBV and WASH in Syria, conducted in 8 Governorates in Dec 2021, found clear links, highlighting that the lack of water supply, electricity, and increase in prices has placed households in a fragile situation which all may contribute to GBV exposure.

5 - Implementation of activities

NCA’s proposed intervention will target GoS areas in R. Damascus and Aleppo, covering 8 locations in 2022. 8 more locations will be targeted in R. Damascus, Aleppo, Hama and Idlib in 2023. For this submission, 0% funding has been secured, although approx. USD 750,000 has so far been secured from other donors in NCA’s total WASH portfolio for SyriaDuring the first quarter of 2022, prior to confirming project locations, NCA will conduct assessments that prioritise humanitarian and technical needs, and then draw up BoQs. Each solar powered water system will be designed and constructed based on the following data: energy demand for meeting minimum water supply needs; pumping tests, water system and network assessment, water equipment assessment, electrical assessment and evaluation of available area (also in terms of safety for the system). The capacity of operators will also be assessed, and training material developed accordingly, with focus on solar power principles and best practices in operating solar powered water systems. The intervention is complemented by the distribution of hygiene items and awareness activities, including for menstrual and incontinence management, determined through FGDs and KAP surveys, targeting 500 vulnerable HHs in each of the eight project locations. COVID-19 prevention and GBV/Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle. It is estimated that implementation will start in 1st half of 2022 to be completed by the end of the year.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risk for exclusion of vulnerable groups, e.g women and girls, people with disabilities, IDPS, etc. (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High): NCA will conduct specific FGDs with different types of vulnerable groups, in order to have all voices heard and inform the design of the intervention. In addition, beneficiary data that will be collected will be disaggregated also for vulnerability. Finally, NCA will assign focal point for inclusion. 2. Beneficiaries at risk of SEA. (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High): Mitigation measures: see below in PSEA section. In addition, frontline WASH staff will receive relevant training. 3. Delays in project implementation perpetuating negative impacts on target population and pushing vulnerable groups to adopting negative coping mechanisms. (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High.): NCA will start planning for the intervention well in advance and according to robust time plan. Specific bottlenecks will be identified in the design period and in communication with relevant stakeholders. All necessary permits will be cleared with relevant authorities in the preparation phase. 4. Distribution exposes vulnerable population (e.g. women, girls, PWD, elderly) to risk (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High): Communities and vulnerable groups in particular will be consulted about locations and timing of distributions. Safety audits for the distribution sites will be conducted. Relevant staff and volunteers will be trained in PSEA and core GBV concepts.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The capacity building on the Policy will take place to inform all project staff on the code of conduct (including UNSG bulletin on PSEA), reporting, and responding to cases of SEA; complaint report mechanisms will be set up with multiple entry points; PSHEA focal points for each project location will be appointed; awareness raising session and distribution of PSHEA related IEC will take place among affected communities (sensitive to age, gender, and cultural context). PSEA messaging and entry points for incident reporting will also be included in all other outreach and IEC materials as well.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:a) Delay in receiving permits from relevant authorities.b) Banking transactions are delayed as sending banks are reluctant to transmit funds to Syria.c) Access to target locations is restricted due security or other external issues.d) Import of relevant equipment and material is restricted or blocked.e) Fraud and/or diversion of aid.f) COVID-19 pandemic continues, with lockdown and staff getting sick. Mitigating measures:1) Selection of project locations based on needs according to HNO severity ranking, and as agreed with all stakeholders.2) Identify alternative banking routes, and stress the humanitarian nature of the funds (exempt from sanctions).3) Continuous monitoring of the security situation.4) Strategic procurement plans through market assessments and supply chain analysis.5) Financial and procurement monitoring and checking procedures in place.6) Comply with COVID-19 prevention measures, and encourage vaccinations among staff members.
