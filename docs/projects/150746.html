Needs

The water situation in Gaza is alarming in terms of both quality and quantity. Deterioration is caused by climate change, decreased rainfall, over pumping of the aquifer, micro-bacterial and chemical contamination of water, and water storage contamination. This situation poses significant health risks to its two million residents and constrains socioeconomic development. Groundwater from the coastal aquifer is the only water resource available in the Gaza Strip. However, abstraction from the aquifer stands at four times the aquifer‘s recharge rate at 200 MCM/year, and is expected to rise to 260 MCM/year by 2020. In addition, more than 96 per cent of abstracted water is polluted and not fit for human consumption due to high salinity levels from sea water intrusion and high nitrate levels from excessive use of agrochemicals and wastewater infiltration. In August 2017, a rapid assessment conducted by the WASH cluster has found that the energy crisis has reduced the available water supply from 84 liters to only about 54 liters per capita per day which is below acceptable water quantity standards of 100 l/c/d recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO).Furthermore, Water and wastewater networks have been damaged; raising the risk that sewage will enter water pipes and create stagnant pools of wastewater. Also the absence of sufficient wastewater treatment facilities results in approximately 35 MCM/year of untreated/partially treated wastewater discharged into the sea along the Gaza coast. During the winter season, intense storms could cause raw sewage trapped under the solid waste to mix with rainwater, increasing health risks because of a lack of adequate collectors and drainage infrastructure. Furthermore, Scarcity of waste disposal sites coupled with huge increase in waste generation increase cesspits overflow, discharging solid waste and producing toxic sludge, which eventually seeps into the groundwater and leads to serious environmental and human health impacts on the population. As a consequence, repairing and reinforcing WASH conditions at both the camp and household level is urgently required. Safe drinking water and good hygiene practices are essential for the survival and healthy development of children. Without them, the lives of children are at risk because for children under five, water- and sanitation-related diseases are one of the leading causes of death globally.

Activities

UNRWA will continue to provide critical emergency support for WASH interventions in Gaza, both directly, through solid waste management in camps, and indirectly, through public service providers. UNRWA will remove and safely dispose of solid waste from unofficial dumping sites in and near camps and in areas densely populated by refugees during emergencies. UNRWA will also undertake vector control activities through campaigns in mosquitoes breeding sites (particularly in Wadi Gaza, Nuseirat, Umm El Nasser, Beit Lahia, Khan Younis and Rafah), UNRWA will make emergency repairs to damaged and degraded water and waste water networks in Gaza’s eight refugee camps. Infrastructure in Jabalia and Rafah camps is of particular concern, and water processing plants, already over capacity and in need of maintenance and or upgrade, are particularly vulnerable. Flooding issues also need to be addressed. UNRWA will use its heavy duty storm water pumps to alleviate any flooding inside refugee camps as well. In each area of the Gaza , UNRWA sanitation offices are equipped with heavy storm water mobile pumps to alleviate any water flooding as well as assist outside the camps upon needs. UNRWA will coordinate with other agencies and public utilities (PWA) throughout implementation. Identification of emergency priorities will be carried out based on demonstrated needs as identified by local WASH committees, UNRWA, CMWU, PWA and solid waste actors in consultation with UNRWA’s Protection Unit and other relevant actors to ensure the most vulnerable areas are prioritized.
