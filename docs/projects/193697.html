Project Summary

From 15 April 2023, armed clashes erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in multiple cities across Sudan, IOM report estimates that approximately 1,210,214 individuals (242,666 households) have been displaced internally as a result of the conflict. In Blue Nile, the security situation remains stable but unpredictable, in terms of displacement in BN, IOM reported that 1,115 people arrived in Damazine, Rosaries and Tadamon localities added to that Blue Nile is one of the regions with protracted IDPs and returnees who didn’t adequately re-establish after the displacement because of the long-lasting conflict in the area, the proposed project is an integrated emergency project that will cover thematic areas of FSL, Education Health and WASH In order to provide timely multi cluster life-saving assistance to crises affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity and to mitigate protection risk and respond to protection needs through the humanitarian action for the newly vulnerable displaced, new returnees, vulnerable residents and vulnerable protracted displaced in Blue Nile state for 194,886 person (194,886 person (60,805 women, 56,127 men, 40,536 girl and 37,418 boy)), from Damazine, Kurmuk, Rosaries, Wad Almahi, Bau and Geissan localities in Blue Nile state. The project is aimed at providing the affected vulnerable communities with sustainable basic services to ensure well-being, improved food security at the HH level for 194,886 person (60,805 women, 56,127 men, 40,536 girl and 37,418 boy), Improved access to safe and protective learning environments for vulnerable children including children with disabilities in order to ensure vulnerable or marginalized children (ages 6-18) have access to safe and protective learning environments and improved structured quality of lifesaving education for crisis affected children to enhance structured lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being within a protective environment for 15,200 student (7,904 girl and 7296 boy), WASH activities will complement the other sector and will provide lifesaving activities and gender and culturally sensitive WASH and protection services while ensuring sustainable environmental practices for newly vulnerable displaced, new returnees, vulnerable residents and vulnerable protracted displaced in Blue Nile state for 45,780 person 55% of them are women. Support essential public health functions and strengthen emergency preparedness, response and emergency risk management plus Support the implementation of resilience solutions to reduce the drivers of the needs. The proposed project will complement and linked to existing IRW projects in the proposed project sites and will include of construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities, construction and rehabilitating school facilities and infrastructure including hygiene and sanitation facilities, providing small holder farmers with seeds and tools, capacity building of community members and healthcare staff on hygiene and sanitation. The proposed will thus contribute to improved and increased access to basic services by the affected vulnerable men, women, boys and girls while creating resilience and sustainability. IRW will coordinate the project activities with other partners and respective government departments in the project sites including UN agencies such as UNICEF, WFP, UNOCHA, WHO and UNHCR. Implementation strategy will be direct implementation strategy while IRW will also involve its implementation partners in community mobilization and engagement.

1 - Needs

Beginning on 15 April 2023, armed clashes erupted between the Sudanese Army Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in multiple cities across Sudan, including Khartoum, Al Fasher, Merowe, Nyala, Ag Geneina, Zalingi and El Obeid. IOM DTM report in May 2023 reported that as of 21 May, a total of 1,042,114 individuals (209,136 households) have been displaced across the country. Field teams report that IDPs were originally displaced from six states, including Khartoum (68.96%), West Darfur (24.22%), South Darfur (4.82%), North Darfur (1.52%), North Kordofan (0.29%) and Central Darfur(0.17%). IOM reported that 1,115 people arrived in Damazine, Rosaries and Tadamon localities but on 30 May, BN HAC reported that 5384 HH 1,446 individuals (unverified) arrived in Damazine and Rosaries localities from Khartoum due to the ongoing fighting, Damazine (HH 3,118, 18,713 individual), in Rosaries (1,527 9,162 HH & individual, in Tadamon (739 HH & 4,439 Individual). This situation will put more pressure on the basic services facilities, which were already suffering before this new situation . According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) platform 11.7 million people in Sudan likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3 or worse). This includes 3.1 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 8.5 million in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis).. BN state among the states with the highest anticipated percentage of residents in need of immediate assistance which is (37 per cent), in Baw locality 55 per cent of population suffering from severe food insecurity. Al Kurmuk 50 per cent of the population is food insecure. Additionally, in Geisan between 45 per cent of residents in these areas experience food insecurity. Education sector is currently riddles with dilapidated infrastructure as a result of the protracted armed conflict in the targeted areas. As a result of the ongoing conflict between the army and the Rapid Support Forces in several Sudanese states, it has caused the displacement of a large number of families to safer areas, including the Blue Nile. It is expected that if the conflict continues, more families will flee to the Blue Nile. The latest need assessment and Ministry of Education records revealed that the Blue Nile ranks very low among the states of Sudan when it comes to availability, accessibility and quality of education. Attendance rate among the school age children is only 55% (51% for boys and 49% for girls respectively). The literacy rate among the population 15 years and over is 38%. Children’s attendance to pre-school education is as low as 47.7%. There are around 44.8% which is 118,773 school age children outside the education system in the Blue Nile. The MSNA 2022 results indicate that 77 per cent of the population have access to improved water services from improved water sources without factoring the collection time. About 28 per cent of water sources do not have sufficient water, and 25 per cent of water sources are not functioning. Over 30 per cent of the population has access to basic sanitation with a 33.3 per cent prevalence of open defecation practices which is especially concerning for women and girls. Hand washing with soap and water (as the main indicator for hygienic practices) is estimated at 14.3 per cent.. The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues to impact the already fragile health system, reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, localized conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis affect the accessibility to health services. According to the 2022 MSNA, 62 per cent of the population had to pay in full for the medical consultation, 63 per cent paid in full for laboratory diagnostics, and 94 per cent paid for medicines. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project has been designed to respond to the different needs of groups at the activities, outputs, and outcomes levels. The project implementation team have vast experience on gender sensitive implementation and will practically implement project activities in a manner calculated to advance gender equality. The implementation teams will include both men and women. A significant proportion (52%) of the proposed beneficiaries are women. Girls and boys will be mobilized to benefit from hygiene sessions to reduce water borne diseases. Education, FSL and WASH Facilities will be constructed in safe locations that minimize risks and threats to beneficiaries and in particular women and girls for easy access. Fetching of water in most rural areas in Sudan is the responsibility of women, who spend a lot of time on this activity. However, this project will spare women an ample time and give them the opportunity to carry out other activities and participate in the development process of their communities, state and country through creating easy access and reducing the waiting time and distance when fetching water. School latrines will be gender and culturally sensitive and will be constructed separate for men and women, boys and girls thus promoting safety and privacy thus reducing dropout rates more so for the girl child. Hygiene promotion will be gender sensitive, with topics applicable for girls and for boys being delivered separately. Female hygiene will be sensitively addressed as per the norm of the community. Facilitators will ensure that issues addressed are gender balanced and engage both girls and boys> IRS has been working with schools in Blue Nile State for more than 20 years, frequently targeting IDPs, Returnees, Refugees and Host Community and has noted that girls tend to suffer disproportionally when there is a shortage of educational facilities. By providing safe learning spaces this project will encourage girls to enroll and attend school (and families to send their girls to school). Livelihood interventions will also ensure women participation and representation in community structures and capacity building opportunities. Some activities will directly target women and Female Headed Households in particular to enhance their selfreliance and empower them for sustainable livelihood. Nutrition education and awareness together with introduction of vegetables cultivation and fruits planting in the targeted villages will significantly improve the HHs diet with nutritious food items those are essential for children, pregnant women and lactating mothers’ health and wellbeing. Agro-processing training will equip women with the required skills and knowledge to access the local market and generate income for their families, jobs will be created as youths will benefited from the vocational training in electrical works and welding and will support them with tool kits to start their own Income Generating Activities (IGAs)
