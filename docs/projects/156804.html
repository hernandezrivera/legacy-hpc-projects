Project Summary

In order to help the most vulnerable families, Oxfam proposes to improve their food security status through access to daily subsidized bread, and to restart their farming activities. Oxfam identified urgent needs to rehabilitate and equip the bakeries with the essential machinery needed to produce subsidized bread bundles. Additionally, Oxfam proposes to rehabilitate critical agricultural systems in the most vulnerable communities and support farmers to restore their farming capacity.

Needs

The agriculture sector is a key contributor to national Gross Domestic Product (GDP), accounting for one-fifth of the pre-crisis economy, and represents the main source of income for the majority of people living in Syria. Since the beginning of the crisis, this sector has been significantly affected by a decline in crop production, the impact of drought and climate change, reduction in livestock numbers (mostly due to asset depletion), destruction of infrastructure and services (irrigation, storages, veterinary and roads), contamination by unexploded hazards, land degradation, migration and the loss of human resources for production. The population’s main source of income continues to come from agricultural activities such as crop production and sales as well as livestock breeding (although there is a significant change in cropping and breeding patterns that demonstrate negative coping mechanism). Salaries and remittances derived from these activities enable people to purchase food. Despite a slight increase in wheat production, hyperinflation, the lack of other sources of income and employment opportunities continues to heavily affect household purchasing power. In an assessment mission (27 August – 1st September 2018) performed by Oxfam to crisis-affected rural areas in Deir-ez-Zor, Oxfam found that the area has witnessed military action between different fighting parties including Government forces, Kurd fighters and ISIS. The subsidized bread production system has been impacted severely due to the fighting. The area is witnessing population returns despite the lack of the basic living conditions. Oxfam staff conducted field visits to up to 15 villages including Maadan, Shmeitiyeh, Hatlah, Eshara, Marrat, Jafra, Husainyah, Biqros, Saloo and Hamydyah and conducted several meetings with community members (representing up to 30 villages), local authorities and communities’ committees. Among other issues, in all the meetings conducted the participants prioritized the need to rehabilitate bakeries and the irrigation systems. Similar results were found through the assessments carried on in Aleppo.

Implementation of activities

• Thorough vulnerability, market and gender analysis• Rehabilitation of existing bakeries• Rehabilitation of critical agricultural systems through labour-intensive schemes when applicable• Provision of farming and livestock assets• Awareness sessions to farmer groups• Vocational training and provision of productive assets

Protection Risk Analysis

- Assessment is not done properly using right instrument and methodologies: in order to mitigate against this risk, Inductions will be conducted for consultancy research service provider’s staff to do the assessment according to Oxfam’s relevant standards. Oxfam will prepare detailed analytical framework with clear indicators and will share relevant references with the potential providers that will be asked to prepare their proposals accordingly.- The implementation may not be continued and will be stopped for an extended period due to high security concerns, like airstrikes, influence by non-state actors, etc: In case of any security risk, work will be suspended and security measures be adopted to resume the work where possible.- beneficiaries cannot receive their toolkits or participate in the CFW activities as 1) they do not have a valid Syrian ID card and because 2) in addition, without a valid ID card, they might be arrested at a checkpoint through which they need to pass to reach the FSP: Oxfam will transport the participants' due payments from the FSP office to the targeted community. The beneficiaries will be asked to submit alternative available legal documents (at least two documents).
