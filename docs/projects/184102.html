Project Summary

Through the established 25 health facilities in 7 states, the project intends to bring quality health services closer to the refugees in White Nile camps, East Darfur, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, Khartoum, Gedarif and Kassala to reduce inequities of access to health services. The focus will be on ensuring access to free of charge health services in camps where refugees are already settled and for the new arrivals. The health facilities supported by the project will be implemented by MOH and NGO partners. Malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea are major causes of illness of children below the age of five years, leading not only to suffering and death but also to social instability of refugee population. The project aims aprticularly to improve access for the most vulnerable populations in refugee and host community notably women and children under 5WHO intends also to support referral of emergency cases from the refugee camps to the secondary level health care facilities in Kosti, Jabaleen, Ed Daien, Gedarif and Kassala hospitals..
While WHO continuing the monitoring of the health situation, the project includes the expansion and management of emergency health information and public health early warning systems in the refugee camps. WHO will also procure international standardized Emergency Health Kits for curative, maternal and child health care as well as addressing the impending public health threats such as COVID-19. The supplies have been calculated on the basis of catchment population and a high expected utilization rate of the health services, and will be delivered immediately from the surge stock of the WHO.
The project will support implementation of case management trainings, support Rapid Response Teams in charge of assessment and initial response to public health events such as alert investigation and initial response, weekly EWARS (early warning) data will be collected and analyzed
Community health workers who have already been identified among the refugees in targeted states and trained earlier, will be continuously providing awareness sessions on healthy family practices.

1 - Needs

At present, Sudan hosts one of the largest refugee populations in Africa. South Sudanese make up the majority. Many others fled violence and persecution in neighboring countries, including Eritrea, the Central African Republic, Ethiopia, and Chad, but also the wars in Syria and Yemen pushed people to seek safety in Sudan. Most refugees live in out-of-camp settlements, host communities and urban areas, while others stay in 24 camps (10 at the East Sudan, 1 at Blue Nile State 10 at White Nile State, 2 at East Darfur State and 1 at the Central Darfur State). Sudan continues to generously host and receive additional asylum-seekers. A total of 787,000 South Sudanese refugees settled into Sudan, most of them in White Nile camps (35%), Khartoum (24%), East Darfur 12%, South Kordofan 5% and the remaining are scattered in greater Darfur and Blue Nile states. Beginning of November 2020, after military confrontations in the Tigray region in northern Ethiopia, UNHCR verified and registered up to 57,000 Ethiopian refugees coming throught border entry points between East Sudan and Ethiopia, 50,000 of them are settled in East Sudan (Gedarif and Kassala states). The majority of the refugees arrived after extended travel, carrying few personal belongings and are still in need of urgent humanitarian assistance shelter, water and sanitation, health, protection, food, etc, Women and children are the most vulnerable due to long distances of travel exposing them to gender exploitation or affecting the health care seeking behavior. This significant influx of the Ethiopian refugees over a relatively short period of time stretched the already weak basic services, space, and available resources across all the areas in East Sudan. High incidence of communicable diseases is the main contributor to high rates of morbidity and mortality especially amongst children below 5 years of age. The analysis of last six months’ epidemiological situation shows the main morbidities in the camps hosting South Sudanese and Ethiopian refugees as ARI, diarrheal diseases, and malaria. The poor living conditions, including access to safe water and proper sanitation, over-crowding, heightening the susceptibility to communicable diseases. More than 1800 Hepatitis E cases are reported in 6 states among which Gedarif, Kassala and White Nile are included which reflects the poor sanitation and condition. The needs include all aspects of identification of health threats and disease outbreak confirmation and control (surveillance, case management, health education, vector control, vaccination, and inter-sectorial coordination) became challenging with the steady increase of refugee’s caseload and decreased humanitarian funding for Sudan. The situation deepened the disparities of access to health services in States hosting refugees. Since beginning of the pandemic, 40,247 of COVID-19 were confirmed in Sudan but the number of cases is likely to be much higher as lack of access to health care and testing results in underreporting. No reliable data about confirmed Covid-19 cases among the refugee population are therefore available. COVID vaccination is scaling up and whilst by October 2021 only 3.5% of the population are vaccinated, the target is 20% by end 2021 including refugee populations. In the meantime, however, a sharp increase of reported Covid-19 cases has been reported and there are growing concerns of a new Wave of high transmission.

2 - Activities/Outputs

• Procurement and distribution of emergency medicines ( for most common diseases), medical supplies including TESK and cholera kits, and laboratory reagents (PHC level) enough to cover essential needs of 750,000 people in White Nile and South Kordofan, West Kordofan, East Darfur, Khartoum, Gedarif and Kassala states • Operational support for the running of emergency clinics to enable these clinics to provide free of charge emergency integrated PHC services. • Provision of medical equipment, clinic furniture, basic diagnostic tools and surgical supply instruments for the functioning of the clinics• Training of medical staff categories on case management, surveillance and infection prevention control.• Training of CHWs to deliver integrated health promotion messages at clinics including psychosocial counselling and support on mental health • Conduct alert investigation missions, including collection of samples and their transport for confirmation, identification of source, initiation of response through outreach and active case finding• Strengthening of EWARS for weekly collection of epidemiological data and enhanced detection of communicable diseases outbreaks• Monitoring and supervision• Implementation of public health screening at entry points including nutritional status, vaccination status, HIV/AIDS tests• Conduct water sampling for quality testing; Provision of the state laboratory personnel and portable water testing kits • Conduct regular vector surveillance and control, with a focus before and during rainy season when the campaigns will be conducted at 2 weeks interval (WHO guidelines) when health awareness and education messages and material will be disseminated to communities• Conduct refugees SAM training for the health staff , working in the targeted TFUs to provide quality services

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will promote strict infection prevention and control ( IPC) at HFs and during the mobile and outreach health activities to prevent the transmission of pathogens (microbes, Viruses, protozoa, fungus, etc) and wil support appropriate waste management of biological materials, medicines, sharp medical tools, disinfectants, lab reagents etc to preventcontamination of staff, other patients and environment outside the clinics and to prevent severe risks for population living in the areas. The training program of WHO includes modules (initial training and re-fresher) on Universal Precautions that includes infection prevention, injection safety,andsolid and liquid medical waste safe disposal for all levels of care. The national guidelines are distributed to all participants to the training, and to the SMOH so all HFs in targeted area are covered. All WHO medical kits include barrier protection equipment, and disinfectants that are biodegradable. In addition, the WHO/MOH monitoring and supervision check list includes indicators for Universal Precaution. Rehabilitation of any HF takes into account the standards for environmental protection through correct medical waste disposal (pits for sharps and placenta, incinerators, fence, septic tanks, etc. The disposal of any expired medicines is done according with WHO/National Guidelines by incineration in the presence of Health Authorities.
