Project Summary

According to the humanitarians needs overview for 2022, about 8.9 million people are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022, compared to 8.3 million in 2021. This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. The 8.9 million people in need include 8.54 million South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 328,000 refugees and asylum seekers. Two million people are internally displaced; of these 1.4 million require urgent support, and 1.8 million are returnees, of these 1.2 million have urgent needs. In addition, there are 6 million vulnerable host community/ non-displaced people, of whom some are older persons or persons with disabilities. According to the intersectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Fangak, Duk and Pibor counties in Jonglei and Cueibet and Rumbek East counties in Lakes, which were classified as in catastrophic need. People in 71counties face extreme needs, while two are in severe need. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. The other main challenge is lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout.
In 2022, world vision will join the humanitarian community to address the needs by implementing the nutrition programs which aligns with the Nutrition Cluster objectives which include: Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2022, Objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2022, Objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2022 and Objective 4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2022
The lifesaving nutrition interventions will be implemented in Warrap, Central Equatoria, Western Equatoria and Upper Nile. The proposed nutrition project will be integrated with health, WASH, protection and FSL activities. World Vision will prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. Additionally, the response will expand on nutrition with other clusters to address key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional child care and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. It will prioritise areas within World Vision program areas where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and displacement co-exist.

1 - Needs

As of December 2021, an estimated 5.3 million people were reached with some form of humanitarian assistance. However, to enable the response to people impacted by flooding, humanitarian organisations were compelled to the re-program in-country support, which resulted in a reduction of food assistance to internally displaced people, refugees and communities in acute food and livelihood crises. These unmet needs will increase vulnerability for populations in 2022 as more people are likely to experience severe food insecurity. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). Of those, an estimated 2 million people including 1.3 million children under the age 5 and another 675,548 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished. According to the FSNMS+ round 27 results, 26 counties in six states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Eastern Equatoria) have Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 percent (very high acute malnutrition level (WHO classification). The most affected counties are Pibor, Aweil South, Baliet with GAM of 21.6 per cent, 23.1 per cent and 20.5 per cent respectively. In 2021, the high level of food insecurity and malnutrition were compounded by new shocks including COVID-19, localized conflicts and floods that affected large parts of the country World Visions’ target areas in Warrap, Unity, Upper Nile and Western Equatoria states. Acute malnutrition has remained a protracted problem in these areas and does require sustained investment for life-saving nutrition service delivery. The key drivers are multifaceted, including severe acute food insecurity, the impact of COVID-19, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services driven by the elements noted above. Those who are malnourished are more vulnerable to common and infectious diseases such as cholera, malaria, diarrhea diseases, acute respiratory infection and measles. In South Sudan, 1 in 10 children die before their 5th birthday. Major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include the high prevalence of diseases which affects up to 36 per cent of children under five, and poor quality of food and limited food diversity. An elevated level of food insecurity contributes to acute malnutrition. Barriers to accessing nutrition services include insecurity, flooding, distance and poor road conditions. Another barrier to access reported is the lack of supplies at nutrition centers. Coping mechanisms reported for those unable to access nutrition centers to address their children's nutrition needs include reliance on locally available food and herbs, cow’s milk, borrowing money to pay for medicines and staying overnight in town centres with nutrition centres. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. In 2022, the precarious situation is expected to be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. Food assistance is expected to decrease by 10 to 20 per cent from the already reduced levels in 2021. The current economic crisis is impacting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services. An estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourished(HNO,PINS). World Vision through funding UNICEF,GAC,WFP and GAC ending in march, to provide lifesaving emergency nutrition interventions in the three state

2 - Project activities

In 2022, World Vision will continue to support: i) The outpatient therapeutic program (OTPs) which focuses on treating children identified as having severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications ii) The supplementary Therapeutic program (TSFPs) that focuses on treating children aged 6-59months with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) without medical complications iii) Stabilisation Centre (SC) which provides inpatient care for CU5 having SAM with medical complications iv) Community Mobilisation which focuses on awareness, mobilisation and involvement of the community to participate in prevention of acute malnutrition v) The Family MUAC program targets all House Holds in the catchment areas with children aged 6-59 months. It involves training mothers, family members or caregivers of CU5 to conduct screening of their children for malnutrition using the MUAC tape at home. This approach was adopted to prevent the spread of COVID-19 while continuing to identity and provide treatment to malnourished children. The activities will be conducted in line with each of the four outputs outlined by the Nutrition Clusters: Sectoral Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2022, World Vision will Prevent malnutrition through provision of blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP), conducting behavior change communication and through provision of micronutrient supplementation to prevent malnutrition. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, WVI will maintain its commitment to engage with affected communities at all phases of the project cycle, through focus group discussions with women, men, boys and girls on issues concerning their education, WASH, and food security concerns. Establishing/strengthening local structures such as Project Management Committee is one example of how WVI engages the community in a sustainable and accountable manner to determine and support appropriate need-based responses. WVI's Quality Assurance framework ensures that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with community needs and in line with other humanitarian frameworks.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The Nutrition project management will involve persons with special needs including the impaired and individuals who are not literate. The tools used during needs assessment, and distribution will specifically target these group of people, in order to gain their inputs. Data that is gathered will also be dis-segregated including during reporting. Community mobilisation for activities will also be done in a way that encourage them to actively participate in the project. Various local workers including interpreters will be recruited to disseminate information in local languages.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Complaints regarding any protection-related issue are handled by a Protection Officer to ensure confidentiality and to be able to determine an appropriate response to the feedback received, particularly in the event that such responses require CP or GBV intervention from the case management team. Any complaint involving sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be immediately escalated to the human resources team in Juba. All WVI staff are trained on SEA and all have signed WVI ’s code of conduct. Cases of SEA will be investigated by a team led by WVI South Sudan’s human resources staff in collaboration with the Safeguarding Manager in a safe and confidential manner. Strengthening the capacity of the stakeholders and staff.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

World Vision will ensure that all staff are involved in the implementation of this project will involve and inform the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders at all stages of the project management cycle starting from the initial assessment, design, implementation and close out stages. The project participants will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process, quantity and quality of services they are getting from the project. Communities are asked which complaints and feedback mechanisms they prefer, with efforts to ensure that unique needs of vulnerable populations are met (for example, considering child- and gender-friendly methods of collecting feedback, accounting for literacy or individuals’ safety, protection, and access to mechanisms, etc.) Project participant feedback is compiled and shared with the project teams on a weekly basis. The feedback is then verified and responded to by the relevant teams. To accurately respond to feedback and complaints, an investigation is conducted on each feedback item submitted. Thus, after the team verifies the concerns raised, corrective measures are taken and incorporated into project implementation.
