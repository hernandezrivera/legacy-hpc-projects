Project Summary

The project aims to support five primary health Care centres (Jisr Alshoghour, Qurqania, Afrin, Kafr naha, Albara) and one comprehensive primary health care centre (Ariha) in the northwest Syria. The health services which will be provided are according to the essential health services package (EHSP) which was developed by WHO and Syrian health NGOs and are the following:
• IMCI (integrated management of childhood illness)
• RH (reproductive health)
• NCD (non-communicable diseases management)
• CD (communicable diseases management)
• IYCF (infants and young children feeding)
• Mental health
• Dental services
• Provision of medicines and lab tests
• Community health programme
• Referral services
This project contributes towards the reduction of morbidity and mortality rates in the conflict affected areas and provides access to quality primary health care services. The cases which need higher level of care will be referred to a higher levels of health facilities, through developing referral pathways in collaboration with the health partners in the area.

Needs

Based on Health Sector Inputs HNO 2019, provision of health services continues to be disrupted by the ongoing conflict. Attacks on health have rendered 46 percent of hospitals and primary health facilities in Syria as either partially functional or not functional, while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained and predictable access to populations in need.Displaced populations and returnees, particularly those in the northeast and northwest Syria, are vulnerable to outbreaks of infectious diseases due to unsanitary living conditions, overstretched health services, and low coverage of routine immunization.Sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need across the country. Thirty-seven percent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services; an estimated 41 percent of Syria’s population requires treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs); and at least 15 percent of the population requires mental health and psycho social services (MHPSS), but the infrastructure and referral system that remains functional after seven years of conflict is inadequate to cope with existing needs.Nearly two-thirds of women reported delivering at home with a skilled attendant. Pregnant adolescent girls and their babies are at high risk of medical complications and death. Family planning is widely available, with free contraceptives were reportedly available in 192 out of 271 sub-districts surveyed in 2018, consistent with the fact that 28 percent of surveyed health workers reported the non-availability of organized family planning services in their community.During 2018 localized outbreaks of measles, acute bloody diarrhea and typhoid fever were detected and reported on, mainly affecting the governorates of Al--Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor, Aleppo, and Idleb, primarily due to limited access to clean water and sub optimal hygiene conditions.Non-communicable diseases remain among the most prevalent health issues in Syria. WHO estimates that approximately 12 percent of the population have diabetes and 20 percent hypertension.Mental health needs are extensive, with WHO estimating that at least 15 percent of the adult population is in need of treatment for moderate to severe mental health needs.The Syria crisis has impacted the health delivery system across Syria with some regions solely dependent on humanitarian assistance for the provision of health services. The number of people who are in need of health assistance in Syria is estimated to be 13.2 million. Females make up 72 percent of the PiN. Vulnerable groups include 5.2 million women of reproductive age, 2.2 million children under 5 years old, and 2.3 million people aged over 50, as at this age vulnerability to non-communicable diseases becomes highest. Syria is home to at least 3 million disabled people, and 7 million IDPs. Eight percent of people in need are currently residing in UN declared hard-to-reach areas.

Implementation of activities

Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referral, This activity will be implemented by: selecting the safe and accessible locations, recruiting medical staff , training staff on the EHSP package , equipping the PHCs with the necessary medical equipment , purchasing medical supplies ,providing the following services by the qualified staff : IMCI, NCD , RH, CD, IYCF, Community health, mental health, creating service mapping of the different humanitarian actors in the implementation area and coordinating with health partners in the area to create referral pathways linked with the MHPSS staff in the PHC clinics and to refer patients who need high level of health care.Mental health consultations will be provided by: the MH-GAP trained doctors as it is addressed in the MH-GAP program, including providing psychotropic medication, cases progress follow-up by the PSWs who are responsible for providing psycho education to the cases and their families/caregivers, outreach activities including awareness raising activities by conducting psycho education group sessions targeting the local community on MHPSS concerns (in camps, schools, home visits.), and referring the detected cases with signs and symptoms of one or more of mental health problems to the MHGAP doctor for further assessment and follow-up.Community health services which include (IYCF- IMCI- RH- NCD-CD) will be provided by community health workers at community level in coordination with health facilities staff to raise awareness of community and screening patients and referring cases to health facilities as needed and following up those cases during treatment.Provide health facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies, this activity will be implemented by: preparing the list of essential medicines, equipment, consumables according to the EHSP that meet the needs of the targeted beneficiaries, Purchasing the medical supplies, Delivering the supplies to facilities. (medicines will be provided in various types to suit all gender and age groups (tablets, syrup, vial, ointment, cream, ampoule, vaginal ovule, and spray)- Community health workers teams will distribute preventive Nutrition Supplies (micro nutrient tab and powder- plumpy doz- high energy biscuits) if UNICEF provide UOSSM by those supplies.Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services, This activity will be implemented by : conducting training need assessment according to EHSP, providing training packages in the first half of the project based on the need assessment results, providing refresh training in the second half of the project training to ensure the quality of the provided services.- Community health workers will be provided by WHO unified training package.Increase access to health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical units and supporting referral, this activity will be implemented by :rehabilitating the selected health facilities to be more suitable for all beneficiaries and creating areas that secure the privacy of patients and prevent the sexual exploitation ( Breastfeeding room ,RH clinic ,isolated WC), Equipping the health facilities according to EHSP , coordinating with Health Partners in the area to establish effective referral pathways.

Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: high, effect: highMitigating measures: Establishing separated toilets for men and women - Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for all patients – allocating Someone from the guards to regulate the flow of patients – establishing An easy-to-reach complaints mechanism( complaints box, WhatsApp number , exit interviews) - Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts - Activating the role of the monitoring and evaluation team.2- Neglecting the most vulnerable (children, women, the elderly and the disabled): possibility: moderate, effect: highMitigating measures: - Employing female health staff to ensure that services are being provided women –- Ensuring that the logistics are suitable for elderly and disabled people (e.g wheelchairs), activating the role of the monitoring and evaluation team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with the project problems.3- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: moderate, effect: highMitigating measures: - A comprehensive services mapping of the area - Activating the role of the CHWs to communicate with the population in the targeted communities and to identify the main needs - Selecting accessible and safe locations of health facilities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• Locations remain accessible for the team and for the beneficiaries.• Inflation and currency exchange rate remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned deliverable quality and quantity.Risk:• The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities.• The Close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities.• Local qualified staff required for project activities are not available and adequate.Measures:• Preparing an emergency plan according to the expected Scenarios in conflicted areas.• Capacity building plan for the staff.
