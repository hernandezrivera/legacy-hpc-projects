Project Summary

Based on community participation and AAP, the project is designed based on the following:
Increasing the food security and enhancing the livelihoods of IDPs and host communities in Idleb Governorate (Maaret Tamsrin sub-district) through supporting the wheat value chain as follows:
500 HHs (90% host communities,10%IDPs) in Hazano, Kelly, Kafr Kafrehmull, Zardana Mashehad, Ram Hamdan, and Haranbush will receive agriculture input vouchers to enable them to plant 2500 dunum of wheat in total. In addition, it will support the marketing of wheat through the purchase of 500 tons of wheat in harvest season from the project beneficiaries.
500 HHs of small livestock breeders (50% host communities, 50%IDPs) in the same locations will receive200kg each beneficiary of bran (wheat milling products) for use in feed mixtures to support livestock.
5000 families of IDPs will be supported through the distribution of free bread for two months in random and regular camps in Maaret Tamsrin sub-district *
Key interventions will include:
1- Provision vouchers to buy agricultural inputs, accompanied by related extension services**
2- Capacity building targeting farmers and local community members.
3- Support the marketing of wheat by purchasing wheat from beneficiaries to encourage farmers and the local community to cultivate wheat annually.
4- Support the production of bread through milling of wheat and providing free flour for bakeries to produce bread (400 tons of flour).
5- Support livestock breeders through the distribution of bran for use in feed mixtures.
6- Distribution of free bread to vulnerable IDPs in camps.
7- Monitoring the project during the cycle of the wheat crop.
ATAA is planning to implement the project by providing 1 type of fertilizer voucher,3 types of pesticide vouchers, 1fuel voucher, harvest vouchers, as well as wheat purchasing to:
500 HHs for wheat cultivation, in addition, ATAA will provide bran for 500 HHs of livestock breeders in the six target villages in Idleb district as follows: 2843 female and 2157 males, planed 790 men, 1145women, 1698 girls, and 1367 boys (the women and men farmers and their children).
ATAA is planning also to distribute 450,000 bread bundles for two months in camps in Maaret Tamsrin sub-district as follows: 14475 female and 10525 males, planed4300 men,6500 women,7975 girl, and 6225 boys.
ATAA will form specialized technical committees comprising experts to take receipt of wheat from farmers at the end of the harvest according to standard specifications approved by the grain institution.
ATAA will contract with a mill for the wheat milling then take receipt of flour and bran in bags.
(500 tons of wheat = 400 tons of flour + 100 tons of bran based on 80% extraction rate).
After receiving the flour, ATAA will contract with two bakeries for two months to bake bread, where ATAA staff will distribute it to the beneficiaries in coordination with the local authorities and camp management with daily monitoring by the MEAL department for bread quality and quantity.
(400 tons flour*1125 bread bundles=450,000 bread bundle÷52 a workday=8653 bread bundles daily).
ATAA will identify, verify and profile target beneficiaries through local councils according to the following eligibility selection criteria:
- Families who planted the wheat seed this season.
- Families which have 5 dunums wheat at least.
- Families which have (1-10 livestock).
-Families in the camps (1-5 person) one bread bundle (more 5 people) two bundles.
The shortlist will be identified depending on the vulnerability criteria
- Women headed households (widows, divorced, GBV survivors).
- Large families (more than 5).
- Families with disabled members.
- Families with low income (less than 50 USD)
- Families with elderly (men-women).

4 - Needs

The needs assessment in the project area shows that the target areas lacking the support of agricultural projects in conjunction with the massive displacement of civilians from the southern countryside of Idlib and northwest Hama countryside as a result of the recent military operations by GOS/GOR, which left huge economic burdens in the region, especially with the suspension of a number of humanitarian agencies their activities in northwestern Syria.Through key informants’ interviews (KIIs) including women farmers, it was proven that the targeted areas are ideal areas for planting wheat due to the availability of large agricultural areas and irrigation sources through the available wells in the area.However, the continuation of the Syrian crisis and the security threats in the region, in addition to the high prices of agricultural inputs (fertilizers, fuel, pesticides) in addition to high harvest costs and wheat marketing issues. All these reasons have led to the neglect of farmers to cultivate or care for their cultivated lands, which has led to the spread of many agricultural problems such as the spread of vole for example.Also, the interviewed key informants stated that the mills and bakeries with high production capacity are available. But they face difficulties related to the high operational cost of the mills and bakeries due to fluctuation in currency exchange rates and high fuel prices for example, Syrian north's dependence on imported flour has led to a decrease in job opportunities for mills workers and its owners.Moreover, the interviews with livestock breeders in the region showed that a large number of livestock breeders (Women & Men), including IDPs. where livestock breeders including IDPs are resorting to selling or slaughtering the herds as a means of adaptation, especially with high feed costs and poverty of the region's natural pastures.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will correspond to the OCHA allocation strategic objectives (ASO3): Improving livelihoods of vulnerable conflict-affected people both in camps and out of camps:1- Out of camps: Beneficiaries’ (women and men farmers) households will receive vouchers for fertilizers and pesticides, fuel for irrigation, harvesting vouchers in addition to supporting the wheat marketing by purchasing the wheat from the beneficiaries, to enable them to buy agricultural inputs for the next season while encouraging them to conserve part of the production to cultivate wheat for the next season and achieve sustainability. This makes the cycle of the inputs productive and effective.Beneficiaries’ (women and men farmers) households will receive bran to use in feed mixtures to support livestock on land out of camps.2-In camps: IDPs will receive free bread for two consecutive months to improve their food security.In addition, the activities in this project will:- Help beneficiaries to ensure access to food, enhanced their livelihoods, and protect their dignity. - Help beneficiaries to build their capacity. They will be trained to build on existing local strategies to preserve and store wheat seeds.- Help beneficiaries to follow new and scientific techniques in the cultivation of wheat crops in addition to providing the relevant training on the sustainability of production.All these needs have been identified in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2019.The project is also aligned with and contributes to the strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019FSA-SO2: Support the self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.The project will emphasise targeting marginalized groups of society, especially people with disabilities and also women and girls.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- When selecting the beneficiaries, some people and communities or dominant groups in the project area do not accept the idea of participation of women in project activities.This risk will be mitigated by external awareness-raising sessions in the community on women's rights and the importance of their participation in the project and signing MOUs with local councils which include the participation of women in the project.2- The inability to meet the needs of all women in the region that we work in.This risk will be mitigated by transparency with local authorities and community and by informing them very clearly of the selection criteria regarding women.3- During the distribution of agricultural inputs, the risks of GBV may emerge.This risk will be mitigated by the allocation of special days for women regarding the distribution of inputs, and ATAA will ensure that women &_men on the field staff have the necessary awareness of these risks. In addition, the distributing vouchers will help organize work and mitigate this risk, ATAA will make the traders aware of the procedures for women during the distribution.4- During the harvest risks may result from men and women working in the fields, the risks of GBV also emergeThis risk will be mitigated by a plan developed in coordination with the gender advisor from ATAA to reduce any risks related to GBV during the work of harvesters in the fields.All human resources will be mobilized to monitor the harvesting process and the women volunteers will be side by side with women to ensure that they are not exposed to any risks or abuse.ATAA will also coordinate with the GBV sub-cluster to receive technical advice that will provide more protection for women from any risk.5- Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid.This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Markets are functioning and all the inputs are available. The wheat varieties are staple and adapted to the environment. There is social acceptance for the project and the people are prepared to interact with it. ATAA will contract with many local markets to avoid fraud.An analysis of all risks related to the project was conducted (please refer wheat risk matrix in the documents). Where risk has been classified into protection, environmental and security risks.Environmental risks:1-Plant diseases such as insect diseases such as suna and fungal diseases such as yellow rust on wheat will be addressed through the distribution of pesticides in a timely manner in addition to strict observation by the competent agronomists through continuous tours of farmers' fields to investigate any diseases and advise farmers accordingly. 2- Fire during the harvest season: there is a high risk during the harvest due to factors such as high temperature or neglect, in addition to the Deliberate shelling during harvest.Raising the awareness of the local community about the dangers of fire and methods to mitigate it by:-Removing the weeds on the roadside-Work the lines of fire (firebreaks)between farmers' lands to prevent the spread of fire from one field to another.-Coordination with the Civil Defense especially during the harvest period.-Coordination with the Civil Defense-Distribute awareness-raising brochures to the community about the importance of cooperation to avoid fires.3- Drought: This risk will be mitigated by providing farmers with supplementary irrigation and advising them to choose suitable plant varieties for the area and a suitable planting date.4-To mitigate the risks of harvest, whether a delay in harvesting or fires, Due to the synchronization of harvest for all beneficiaries within one month a number of harvesters will be contracted to work in farmers' fields to accelerate the harvest.-Security risk:This will be processed with strict follow-up by the S&S
