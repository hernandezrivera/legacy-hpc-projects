Project Summary

Ensuring Food Security to IDPs /Host families: through on-farm training and the cultivation of short cycle crops/poultry farming and entrepreneurship in FAKO division of the south west region/MEZAM division in the noth west region of Cameroon
Project target:4300 with 1800 in the NW and 2500 IDPs in the SW Region
The present socio-political crisis in North West and South West has affected many people and also led to many that are now internally displaced, about 968,477(October 2019,) Cardre Harmonise assessment; 320,394 in the North West and 159,851 in the South West tageted by te Food Security In their new localities, these (IDPs)/host families are faced with the problem of inadequate food in terms of quantity and quality as well as not being able to have their most needed basic necessitie.,Women, youths, children,people with disbility are the most volnurable and affected. This project’s intention is to help provide solutions to this of problem of food insecuity by implementing activities that will help in livelihood recovery and livelihood enhancement/improvement of IDPs and host families.
This project would be carried out in two main sites; Fako division of the SW region with some 34,667 targeted IDPs out of 73 640(HRO PiN and target figures by division) IDPs (Buea Town area and Ekona Research Residential area). The rich volcanic soil makes it an agricultural community with oil palms, cocoa, plantains, cocoyam and vegetables as the main crops while pigs, goats and chicken are the main livestock. Most of the highly affected persons of the crisis, who could not move to bigger towns/cities relocated to a relatively safe area – the government’s IRAD Ekona residential area. It is worth noting that Ekona is one of the most it towns in this crises.
Buea, is the administrative headquarters of the South West Region with a population of about 200,000. The cental town is a safe heaven for IDPs.The rich volcanic soil and cold environment encourages the cultivation of plantains, yams, maize, beans, vegetables, cocoyam especially at the peripheries of the town. Pigs, goats and chicken are the main livestock. Buea municipality has about 185000 IDPs.
The project segment in the north west would be carried out in Mazam division, host to over to over 96,799 IDPs with a lot of pressure on food and basic needs from host community
This project will be focusing on women, youths, people with disability and the elderly who are the most vulnerable in the ongoing crises. It is worth noting that this project is an up-scaling of the UNDP small grant facility/ emergence response mechanism (July – December 2019) sponsored by the government of Japan. 100HHwere targeted while CEFORA reached 120 beneficiaries due pressure.
This project interns to bring livelihood support through food and nutrition security to some 2795 women and girls; 1505 men and boys ;and 120 people with disability and the elderly who are internally displaced and heads of households and some members of host families. Sensitization on the ills of gender based violence (GBV) is an integral part of all our interventions in humanitarian work, while meetings with beneficiaries, community leaders and local administration are ongoing to promote social cohesion, acceptance and protection. With creation of project management committee selected by beneficiaries and community leaders CEFORA is able to penetrate difficult areas like Ekona and work on safe corridors.
Core activities: On- farm training on climate smart agriclture technique on cultivation of short cycle crops -distribution of improved seeds, inputs and equipment
- training on Pest management and use of agrochemical
- training on Poultry management and production of manure for use on short cycle crops as fertelize -constuction of poultry houses and ditribution of 3week old chicks and input
-process to prevent post harvest loses
-post harvest management
-Training on entrepreneurship - provision of seed capital .

TARGETED FOCAL AREAS AND FSC STRATEGIC INITIATIVES AND JUSTIFICATIONThis project is demand driven after several town hall meetings in the project area in the NW/SW REGION. This project is going to provide sustainable food and nutrition security through the skills the would aquire from the different capacity buiding and on farm training they are going to receive. The cultivation of short cycle crops implies from 6 weeks to 3months beeficiaries would start harvesting their crops, which the wiil eat and take ecessto te market. Most of the elderly, PWD and youths have opted for poutry and petit trading, this would make te self reliant , attaining recovery in a very short time. This project would also lead to social cohesion reducing tension in these communities as both IDPs and host families are are participating in the project Also, most host families are now having the burden of taking care of extra persons and this is greatly affecting host communities. It is therefore necessary that the IDPs (especially women, youths and old people, and people wit disabilities) are empowered so they can have access to farm land, produce their own food and poultry, consume nutritive food and have the opportunities to sell extra products and even engage in petit trading so as to have some money to buy other basic necessities as well as afford to send their children to school. FOOD SECURITY (with focus on on-farm training, postharvest management, transformation and consumption of improved varieties of short cycle crops and poultry farming, and carry-out some economic activity) will help to provide a quick and sustainable response to the present crisis by ensuring food and nutrition security (good health) and alleviating poverty. All these align with the national priority areas (alleviate poverty and reinforce resilience during crisis), which in turn align to the sustainable development goals number 1(no poverty), number 2 (zero hunger, number 3 (good health and wellbeing), number 10 (reduce inequalities) and Number 13 (climate action). This thematic area of livelihood recovery also aligns with national agricultural and extension programmes that emphasize on building the capacities of farmers via trainings and supply of small machines for transformation and other agricultural activities. We focus on women who are the backbone of households and mostly involved in cultivation and marketing of short cycle crops, the thematic area of female participation is one of our priorities, for if we empower the women, we build sustainable communities.This project will go a long way to complement the UNDP early recovery mechanism small grant facility implemented by CEFORA (July-December 2019), the Youth entrepreurship program, Gender Climate Change Agriculture Support Programme (GCCASP). CEFORA participated in the implementation of the first phase of this project (January-June 2019). This project is “Capacity Building For women small holder farmers: Adaptation to climate smart agriculture practices for increase productivity and Agri-business. “This project was supported by AUD-NEPAD Agency, MINPOFF, The CENTER FOR RURAL ACTION carried different training workshops for women and youths, small holder farmers on group dynamics, leadership skills and gender sensitive approaches in agri-business. PROJECT OBJECTIVES.Main Objective: To enhance the capacity of IDPs/host families (women and youths, old and people with disabilities) on best practices for cultivation, transformation/post-harvest management, andconsumption of improved varieties of selected short cycle crops, poultry and small business initiatives (Maize, beans and traditional African leafy vegetables, and poultry, engaging in entrepreneurship) – ensuring fast relief from shocks resulting from the socio-political crises (livelihood recovery) Specific Objectives:1. To effectively monitor and evaluate project activities.2. To establish best practice farms for demonstration
