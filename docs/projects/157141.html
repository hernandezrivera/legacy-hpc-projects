Project Summary

The planned community based WASH interventions will help to create sustainable access to sufficient quantities of safe drinking water at institutions and community levels, and assist to promote best sanitation practices and hygiene behavior. This will be coordinated with line ministries at state and federal levels. This will also include implementation of CLTS, water quality and quantity in participatory fashion in coordination with targeted communities. In addition capacity building of targeted communities in sanitation and water infrastructure management To ensure sustainability and continuity of services.
Nutrition sensitive WASH interventions will be ensured through prioritizing areas with high malnutrition rates and providing sufficient quantities of clean water for households’ consumption as well as improving hygiene at community level in order to reduce the associated health hazards and avoid further deterioration in nutritional status especially among U5 Children and PLW.
The project will rehabilitate, upgrade and establish water infrastructure (water yards, miniwater yards and hand pumps) to improve people’ access to safe drinking water in the three targeted states. Locations of water points will consider women accessibility (selected in consultation with women). To improve the efficiency and reduce the negative environmental impact; the project will establish/ replace/upgrade diesel operated water yards by solar pumps.
Associated women and men WASH committees will be established and trained to manage and maintain rehabilitated/established water sources (resourced from user fees) in order to enhance the sustainability. Women consultation in site selection and participation in water management committees will be a condition to ensure women interests are reflected. Water monitoring systems those include water testing and treatment will be established for the different water points under the WASH committees’ management to ensure water safety. Water harvesting infrastructure will also be introduced to improve water supply systems (e.g. Simple Check dams). Hygiene campaigns will be organized to raise awareness about sanitation and hygiene at community and institution levels (e.g. school hygiene promotion clubs and teachers training on CHAST). WASH package will also include public and schools latrines and hand-washing facilities. These basic services are vital for keeping people healthy and productive.
Availability and accessibility of safe drinking water will reduce work load and risks of violence on women and girls. Number of gender-sensitive communal latrines will be constructed in schools, health facilities and markets; the multi-cubical latrines will be provided with hand washing facilities and solar lanterns for protection and safety.
Protection measures will be ensured through these solar lighted and separate latrines for women and girls.

Needs

According to MICS 2014; Percentage of Sudanese people who use improved sources of drinking water does not exceed 68% (71.3% in Blue Nile, 57.2% in Kassala & 50.6% in North Darfur). Moreover, percentage of populations depend on unfiltered surface water is 20%, 19.6% & 19% in Blue Nile, Kassala & North Darfur respectively. 96.7% of Kassala population do not treat water for human consumption compared to 85% in Blue Nile & North Darfur state. Percentage of household members using improved sanitation facilities which are not shared is only 32.9 at the national level. About 30% of the households in Sudan practice open defecation nationally including 44.9% in Kassala State, 11.4% in Blue Nile & 41.1% in North Darfur. Percentage of households with a specific place for hand washing is just 25.8%, and those use soap or other cleansing agent is about 55.4 % (4.6 % in Kassala, 26.6% in Blue Nile and 8.8 in North Darfur). UNCIEF June 2017 stated that Sudan is facing different evolving emergencies requiring immediate response, including the Acute Watery Diarrhoea outbreak in 12 affected states including Kassala & Blue Nile. Joint assessment conducted by Practical Action and its local partners in Telkok and Hamashkoreib localities in Kassala state (2018); FGD has reflected community urgent needs for safe drinking water sources as well as sanitation and hygiene promotion services. Practical Action led Assessment in Damazin, Geissan & Wedelmahi in Blue Nile Dec 2018 has shown that hand pumps represent the main source of water supply where women and children, both girls & boys, are the more responsible for water collection for the household. In addition to lack of spare parts and maintenance expertise locally, most become non-functional during dry season leaving population dependant on contaminated river streams and traditional unprotected shallow wells. A survey conducted covering Geissan and Kurmuk localities revealed that women are responsible for 75% of water collection business from an average distance of 1000 or double the minimum standard indicated by SPHERE.Through the last three years, Incidence of all waterborne diseases are increased with exception of intestine worms and causes losses in livestock as well as farmer – pastoral conflicts. Available data in North Darfur show that per capita consumption is only 57% (25% during summer) of the minimum level according to WHO; depending on Shallow unprotected hand dug wells and few hand pumps. Women & girls are traditionally responsible for water fetching added to the burden of domestic and cultivation work. Young boys & girls are responsible of carrying water using traditional carts for transporting heavy volumes of water what negatively reflect on their health & growth. A serious mix between drinking water and drainage water commonly appears too. Area is known for high Typhoid & Diarrhea infections especially among children. Moreover, access to sanitary facilities is very low & insignificant.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The project opts to replace diesel operated water yards by solar power which will reduce hazardous gases emission from burning fuel for water pumping, in addition communities' women and men committees will be trained to establish forest nurseries utilizing waste water to start community forests around water supply points. Conditioning user fees pay will -in addition- to providing for maintenance and sustainability encourage water use conservation. Increase in water supply amounts and points will reduce concentration of livestock at few points and cause environmental degradation by overgrazing.
