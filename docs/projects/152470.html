Project Summary

Provision of adequate WASH services is key to achieving sustainable solutions to communities in emergency. CMD, a national NGO working in the locations of Jonglei, Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria envisages a response aimed at reaching internally displaced persons and vulnerable host communities with water, sanitation and hygiene services. Spanning a period of 12 months, the project targets the locations of Ayod, Duk, Nyirol, Uror and Twic East (Jonglei), Ulang, Maiwut and Longechuk (Upper Nile) and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Budi (Eastern Equatoria) with WASH temporary and sustainable services, to address the vital needs, public health and protection issues.

The response aims at mitigating WASH related GBV by providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities and tackling issues surrounding gender, safety and WASH. Persistence of food insecurity, signalled by high GAM rates, with <5s and PLW mainly affected will be mitigated by the provision of WASH in Nutrition services, through targeted WASH minimum packages at OTP/SC mainly for mother/caretaker – malnourished children.
Water related disease outbreaks have been traced to health facilities and community infrastructures/settings without adequate WASH facilities. A WASH in health outbreak control response is planned especially in disease prone counties.
The project targets a total of 98,000 people; 44,100 of these in Jonglei (45%); (Ayod – 14,700; Duk – 2,940; Nyirol – 11,760; Uror - 10,780; Twic East – 3,920.); 24,500 in Upper Nile (25%); (Ulang – 9,800; Maiwut – 6,860; Longechuk – 7,840) and 29,400 in Eastern Equatoria (30%); (Kapoeta North – 5,880; Kapoeta East – 7,840; Kapoeta South – 11,760 and Budi – 3,920). 52% of those targeted are females, 31% of them girls. IDPs consist of 52% of those targeted.

Needs

Multiply displaced populations remain some of the most vulnerable, especially in locations of Greater Upper Nile (GUN), lacking essential WASH infrastructure and services. The counties targeted in Jonglei alone, have populations exceeding 778,153, Upper Nile with 214,730 and Eastern Equatoria with 428,124, more than half of these female. (Mid 2018 Population Est., OCHA). IDP figures in the counties targeted are in an excess of 357,900. A WASH cluster led vulnerability assessment estimated a total of 757,706 (54% female) persons to be in need of WASH services, 28% of these from Ayod and Nyirol counties. Safe access to water, hygiene and sanitation services has been a challenge in parts of Greater Upper Nile, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria; Less than 20% of the HHs assessed in the counties targeted reported their main source of drinking water is an improved source (borehole, tap stand & water-yard) and available in under 30 minutes, with over 70% of the proportion of assessed settlements reporting the main source of drinking water is an unimproved source (river, swamp, unprotected well), (REACH, August 2018). The insufficiency of water points was further exacerbated by increased concentration of IDPs in safe havens, deliberate destruction of water points by armed forces, breakdown due to over usage and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitations and repairs. Less than half of the populations use latrines, due to unavailability, safety concerns, knowledge and attitudes amongst other challenges. There’s been a destruction of WASH infrastructure, and a tendency of communities to settle in far to reach areas that usually lack adequate WASH services thereby increasing likelihood of resorting to unsafe excreta disposal and surface water consumption leading to water related diseases. Increased caseloads have further strained available sanitation and hygiene facilities, with majority of these requiring rehabilitation and/or upgrade. Unsafe sanitation and hygiene habits are a common practice with open defecation widely visible in areas where large groups of IDPs are concentrated. A cholera outbreak sustained throughout most of the last humanitarian calendar cycle, spanning into the dry season, thereby necessitating a series of preparedness mitigation measures especially in far to reach areas. Food Insecurity and lack of adequate WASH facilities play a role to high levels of GAM averaging about 15%, especially amongst under 5s, with AWDs seen as a contributory factor, resulting in mortality in areas with limited or no health facilities. The situation is expected to be compounded by the seasonality of these areas that lie along the Sobat and Eastern flood plains. Latrine and bathing shelter collapses have been inevitable in some areas, which have led to public health risks. Protection risks have been increased to girls and women who have to stray into nearby bushes due to lack of gender appropriate facilities. A significant number of persons interviewed indicated safety concerns prevented them from accessing the preferred water source and SGBV as the main protection concern for women and girls. Institutional WASH gaps have been identified in schools, health facilities, markets and nutrition centres, requiring response for outbreak control and mitigating public health risks. Significant disruptions of markets and high inflation rates in Jonglei, Eastern Equatorial and Upper Nile have further lessened opportunities of already vulnerable communities to access basic WASH supplies such as soap, PuR tablets, Hygiene Kits amongst others. Lack of Hygiene Kits and other WASH NFIs has reduced chances of enrolling girls into institutions of learning in an emergency setting making them more vulnerable to early marriages. Provision of emergency WASH services will reduce likelihood of deaths due to water related diseases, provide a platform for community settlement and provision of other lifesaving services.

Activities

The following activities are planned to achieve equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees. These are also designed to mitigate WASH related GBV, provision of WASH in nutrition for famine prevention packages and WASH in health for outbreak control at wider community level. WASH activities for IDPs/host communities and returnees Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations through; • Setup/rehabilitation of collective water points with secure location and access agreed after a GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staff, in the community or schools• Provision of water purification tablets (PuR/Aquatabs) on a case by case basis to vulnerable communities. Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services through; • Setup/ Rehabilitation of Emergency safe, gender appropriate latrines, bathing shelters and hand washing facilities (agreed upon by GBV analysis through focus groups conducted by female staff) at designated schools, communal and household settings with high GAM, GBV/SEA and cholera prone areas. Carry out WASH activities to mitigate WASH related GBV through; • Gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points and showers• Provide safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with/after appropriate risk analysis• Provide safe water sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with/after appropriate analysis• Distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women/girls of menstrual age to ensure dignity and mitigate risks of GBV.• Carry out post distribution monitoring of MHM kits to female adolescents and women WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention interventions • Provide safe drinking water at nutritional centres (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM• Provide WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC till the household level• Ensure small stock water requirement and promote appropriate hygiene practices to prevent small stock open defecation at household level WASH in Health for outbreak preparedness, response and risk reduction Provide a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an adhoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household levelIncrease knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through trainings, awareness campaigns and participatory learning.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

It's entailed in the organisational values to provide beneficiaries with information, as well as a donor requirement and demand from beneficiaries. Several methodologies have been adopted which include publication of progressive reports that are availed in hard and soft copies to stakeholders, focus group constitution as a means to pass on information, involvement of beneficiary representatives in project review exercises, project literacy campaigns, use of visibility items such as banners, posters, T - Shirts, flyers to create awareness of the project. CMD also runs a social media platform through which project achievements are detailed. A suggestion box is placed at major project locations, through which formal feedback is obtained from stakeholders and responses provided.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are designed to reach beneficiaries in the language they best understand. Design of messages involves a representative of the different gender groups including women, boys, girls, men and the elderly. Special needs persons are also included, with support from key actors such as Handicap International and HelpAge. Messages are mindful of beliefs, cultures, attitudes and practices of communities, and are aimed at delivering results based on project plans and goals.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The organisation has a PSEA policy in place, alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to children. These include PSEA procedures applies at staff recruitment, induction/training, performance management, whistle-blowing, discipline and grievance, programming guidelines, partnership arrangements and complaints/reporting. Specific responsibilities have been assigned for PSEA, and the organisation ensures staff, volunteers and associates understand and work to PSEA requirements. CMD engages communities to ensure that communities are fully aware of the expected behavior of the staff, how to complain if commitments are not met and ensuring appropriate awareness raising tools are available. This also applies to partners, suppliers and contractors, by assessment of capacity to implement PSEA measures and inclusion of clauses on PSEA in contract agreements. The project seeks to further identify potential risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, and has planned activities on PSEA awareness and sensitivity. CMD has developed a comprehensive complaints mechanism that aims to instill an organisational culture in which all complaints are taken seriously and acted upon.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CMD carried out several consultations across different strata, aiming at obtaining maximum response from all gender groups including women, girls, boys, men, elderly and persons with special needs.These included focus group discussions, house to house surveys, interviews with key informants, interviews with key beneficiaries at institutions, communities as well as village structures.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

As a commitment to IASC standards on accountability to affected populations, CMD has adopted and enforced clear feedback and complaints pathways, aimed at obtaining information on key programmatic aspects from the beneficiaries. These include provision of suggestion boxes at key project sites, that are periodically checked and feedback/complaints addressed, provision of feedback telephone/email addresses and focal persons with whom the community will be free with to provide information about the project. Review meetings are organised at field level with beneficiaries represented, aimed at getting feedback on project related matters.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A monitoring and evaluations framework is designed at the inception of the project, with participation of the community, different gender group representatives and people with special needs. These are orientated on the tools to be used for the exercise and then form part of the team that carries out the exercise with focus on particular areas of common interest.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project has identified specific areas of risk which include safety and dignity, humanitarian access to crisis affected populations, populations access to aid, beneficiary privacy, individuals with special needs/risks, social relations, fraud and diversion with protection implications and market impacts with protection implications. The project will include affected communities as participants in all phases of the programme cycle to ensure that affected populations identify their own protection risks, benefits and self protection mechanisms. CMD will consider whether intervention will create or exacerbate protection risks or benefits for individuals, households and communities, engage individuals with different and specific needs and protection risks, establish two way feedback mechanisms and focal points to ensure regular communication. CMD has done a gender, age and diversity analysis.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through feedback mechanisms instituted, barriers to access have been identified by the different groups constituted which range from physical, cultural and economic barriers. Mitigation measures have been instituted, in collaboration with communities affected.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Minimum packages for WASH in GBV settings have been designed, aimed at addressing protection issues arising from utilization of WASH facilities such as water points and sanitation infrastructure. Provision of safe, secure and gender appropriate WASH services will address protection threats especially faced by women and girls in their pursuit for dignity.

Project monitoring will be done based on plans and targets of project. Beneficiary feedback and complaints tools will be reviewed to assess access of beneficiaries to services as well as access of staff to beneficiaries.
