1 - Needs

Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 are having devastating consequences for civilians. The conflict also spread in other parts of the country (such as Darfur and North Kordofan). As of May 4th, at least 736,000 people have reportedly been displaced inside Sudan Prior to the conflict following a result of years of escalating inter-communal conflict, localized droughts, seasonal flooding and deteriorating economic conditions, Sudan has been facing a worsening hunger crisis. The number of populations facing acute food insecurity and requiring immediate food assistance. Over 2 million children under 5 suffer stunting annually, and 2 million wasting, which classified Sudan as critical with respect to spread of acute malnutrition. Latest HNO 2023 estimated that up to 11.7 million people are acutely food insecure during the lean season, an increase of 2 million from earlier estimates, according to the most recent IPC analysis (IPC Phase 3 or worse). There are 3.1 million people in IPC Phase 4 Emergency) and 8.6 million in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis). With GAM prevalence of 13.6 per cent Sudan is among the countries with the highest levels in the world and has remained at elevated levels for the last decade. Overall, 64 localities in nine states have GAM prevalence equal to and above WHO’s very high prevalence of 15 per cent. The malnutrition situation is exacerbated by multiple factors, including inadequate dietary intake, high prevalence of diseases and inadequate caring and feeding practices and sub-optimal health and WASH services. The deteriorating economy, inflation, high food prices (see food security section above), and displacement contributed to the worsening of the nutrition situation in 2022 and are likely to continue into 2023. Food prices in Sudan are approximately 3 times as in 2021; and rapid inflation continues. The political instability since 25 October 2021 has exacerbated the situation as donor governments and International Financial Institutions (IFIs) suspended developmental and budgetary aid. Furthermore, political instability drives intercommunal conflicts that damage agricultural/pastoral production and livelihoods (through loss of access to land, livelihood assets and typical food and income-earning activities); this reduces the resilience of communities to climate change driven natural disasters that include both devastating floods and localised droughts. This again exacerbates conflict over land that is valued both a source of livelihoods and a core part of local and national identities with interconnected rivalries that operate within a tapestry of political, social and economic grievances. Over 3 million people remain displaced across Sudan, and in mid-2022, mass conflict-induced displacement has rapidly escalated across Sudan, including Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile.In part due to localized droughts and dry spells, early 2022 harvests were 35% below average, leading to a cereal deficit of 2.5 million metric tonnes. About 64% of the households in the reported not having stocks to carry them over until the end of the lean season. In addition to internal factors, the ongoing war in Ukraine impacts Sudan heavily. Domestic production of wheat only covers 15% of Sudan’s wheat demand. In 2020, 60% of the total wheat imported to Sudan came from Russia (55%) and Ukraine (5%). Russia and Ukraine are also highly important for oil, gas and agricultural inputs. In the period between August and September 2022 food insecurity is projected to peak as 1-in-20-year floods damage cropland and kill livestock with flash flooding damaging infrastructure and limiting market access. From October, good harvests could likely to lead to eventual improvements in food security across the country into 2023, but this is contingent on sufficient access to land during planting season and agricultural inputs to take advantage of rains.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1: 78,000 people (15,288 men, 15,912 women, 23400 boys and 23400 girls) reached with unconditional food assistance (cash/voucher).To enable the most vulnerable households to access basic needs SC will provide 3 rounds of food assistance (cash assistance). Supported HHs will be provided with a cash payment that will cover the cost of minimum food expenditure basket defined by the cash working group, which covers food, water, cooking gas, and other essential non-food items. Activities:o Distribution of minimum three rounds of unconditional cash transfer for food to crisis affected population to meet immediate food and non-food needs. Output 2: 84,000 beneficiaries (16800 men, 16800 women, 25200 boys and 25200 girls) supported with livelihood assets recoveryWhile addressing the immediate needs of disasters and conflict affected population, Save the Children supports families to restore their livelihood assets and resume their livelihoods as early as possible. Activities under this output include provision of agriculture inputs, livestock health and restocking of small ruminants and cash grants for resorting off-farm livelihood assets that are lost or depleted because of the disasters/shocks. Market based approach is always our default modality to deliver livelihood assets unless it is not feasible in specific geographical areas. Activities:o Distribution of in-kind or cash for agriculture inputs (traditional rainfed and small-scale irrigated farming).o Restocking of small ruminants (Goats) through cash grants.o Provision of livelihood grants to affected small business to restore capital and market operations. Output 3: 24,000 beneficiaries (4800 men, 4800 women, 7200 boys and 7200 girls) benefited. Use Early Warming information to prepare for and undertake early actions that aim to prevent loss of lives and livelihoods and reduce humanitarian needs.Activities:o Livestock health services (vaccination, treatment, supplementary feeds, extension).o Provision of early mature/flood tolerance and/ or alternative crops seeds that are more adapted to the given climate forecast. o Short-term cash transfer to smoothen the consumption and maintain nutrition status of children during predicted crisis.o Crops pets and diseases outbreak control, application of integrated pest management at early signs or predicted outbreak. Output 4: Gender and protection mainstreamingThis project will mainstream the elements of protection, gender, disability, conflict sensitivity, and environmentally friendly approaches to ensure the project contributes to the lasting benefits to the targets and the local communities. Using lessons learned and best practices from previous interventions and building on SCI´s experience in the targeted area working on gender norms, the proposed action will continue to strengthen current strategies against gender discrimination, ensuring girls, boys, women and men are actively engaged throughout project implementation. SC will build the capacity of the government and local community on CP and SGBV and equip them to do day-to-day tasks and enhance their knowledge and skills.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project will adopt environmentally friendly approaches to ensure the project contributes to the lasting benefits to the targets and the local communities. SC will use environmentally friendly materials while conducting rehabilitation, cement and iron will be used and no wood related materials will be allowed. Solid waste will be disposed properly using incinerators to avoid soil contamination.Environment-friendly and sustainable ways of farming will be promoted through training on better land preparation systems, mainly on avoiding ploughing of hilly places, considering the farm slopes during land preparation (contour farming) to reduce the soil erosion from the farmlands. Farmers will be advised on recommended spacing and planting rates, for sustainable productions and avoid over farming of plots. Sheep/goat restocking will consider the availability of pasture and water in the areas, and the beneficiaries will be advised on controlled grazing and grazing land management to protect the pasture lands from erosion and avoid the risks of overgrazing.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

SC will ensure that protection is mainstreamed across the intervention. This means that the proposed activities will be operationalized with the consideration of avoiding harm and ensuring that the most vulnerable (women, the elderly, disability-affected, youth, etc.) can access assistance without absorbing additional risk resulting from their participation. Vulnerable groups will be considered in how assistance is designed and mobilized. Cash transfer programmes can create tensions in the local community and potentially exacerbate the risk of GBV. In addressing gender needs and reducing any potential negative risk, SC will ensure responding to the unique needs of all genders, recognize diversity within gender groups, and people with disability, avoid exposing recipients to harm and risk and build on social norms work. During implementation, SC will clearly outline responsibilities for integrating GBV prevention, mitigation, and response strategies into the project, highlight key GBV-related areas in cluster meetings and share GBV information with cluster members. Additionally, SC will work through the health and protection clusters to strengthen the referral systems for GBV survivors. Ensure that the entire community, particularly women, girls, and other at-risk groups, are meaningfully participating.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will establish an accountability system after communities’ consultation. The consultations will be done with focused community groups to identify the best means for them to participate and provide their feedback. Key considerations will be access, gender, culture and literacy level to ensure the equitable and meaningful participation of women and girls in particular. Our gender-balanced MEAL staff will regularly analyze and assess the level of participation of women, men, girls and boys and apply the necessary mitigation measures to adjust the imbalance. The project will establish a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) with multiple community-friendly channels. This will include both reactive (such as a hotline, suggestion box) and proactive (community satisfaction consultations, focus group discussions) channels. The complaints and feedback channels introduced in the project area will allow targeted communities and children to report their feedback and serious complaints safely and confidentially (including child safeguarding concerns, sexual abuse and exploitation concerns and breaches of the code of conduct). Any serious complaints will be immediately elevated to the designated focal point (e.g. child safeguarding focal point, HR Manager, Director for Program Development & Quality, Country Director etc.) SCI Sudan will manage complaints in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages with confidentiality. The MEAL Team will analyze and present the information from CFM to the Senior Management Team to inform management decision making. The MEAL team will work with the MEAL focal and Accountability Focal to close the feedback loop, communicating with communities and children about the actions that have been taken based on their feedback.
