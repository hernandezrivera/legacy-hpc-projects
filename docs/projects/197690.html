Project Summary

The project aims to improve IDPs' access to shelter and NFIs in temporary and resettlement centres in Cabo Delgado. 4,600 families will receive support with sphere standard shelters, and another 7490 families will benefit from capacity building, BBB training, and response and support shelter committees established in the communities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The objective is to ensure access to safe shelter for the most vulnerable people in the communities of Metuge and Ancuabe. Accordingly, three main activities are planned:1. Provision of emergency shelter for IDP population. Distribution of 1200 shelter kit of tarp, poles, bamboos and fixing materials, besides supporting with technical support in the construction of the shelters for new arrivals to IDPs resettlement and temporary sites. Priority will be placed on vulnerable families and new arrivals without alternative shelter in these sites due to the possible incidence of new attacks in other districts.2. Provision of sphere standards Transitional Shelters: Through the proposed intervention the organizations will provide 3400 early recovery Sphere Standard Shelter for households with emergency or provisional shelter solutions. In addition, many families have not received any support after 2 years since they receive the first shelter intervention after their first displacement and the state of the shelters is really poor, not assuring their security and their living conditions. 3. Establishment and capacity-building of a site maintenance committee in the resettlement and temporary. Build back better trainings will be held not only for the committes bu also with the communities (IDPs and host communities).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection mainstreaming is an integral part of AeA’s design strategy, this alongside the participatory methods that AeA uses to collect information will help identify the risks posed to each population demographic. Staff working in the field are trained on the do no harm principles that will help ensure the most suitable decisions for the populations in question. AeA teams will prioritize preserving safety and dignity during the design of shelter and NFI interventions such as during assessment, distribution, construction, and community engagement events. Community mobilization and engagement will be conducted to ensure equitable access and participation by all population groups. A multi-sector risk analysis for each component of the project will be made to ensure that all risks are considered and mitigated. As an example, early marriages (the families force minors to get married in order to create another HH and receive extra plot and shelter) are a frequent practice on the sites. In these cases, an analysis will be carried out to pre-determine the need to provide additional shelter support for such large families and/or having children under this risk. In addition, AeA will work alongside CCCM partner at the site to locate streetlights installation points where necessary and assure the proper secure conditions to access shelters.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

AeA designs projects through participatory methods, engaging affected communities, listening and recording their needs. AeA ensures that those in the most vulnerable groups are also included in the discussion, always taking into account the diversity of the population to allow the most at-risk to be equally considered. These considerations help AeA to design programmes tailored to the population. AeA ensures staff on the ground are capable of effectively transmiting important information to communiites, including sharing the organizational structures, process that may affect the population and methods of making complaints or finding information, such as Linha Verde or CCCM’s complaint channel, in Cabo Delgado. This project will continue to ensure that these practices are continued, most specifically keeping strong contact with CCCM’s complaint channel, setting up complaints desks at distributions and ensuring field staff involved with community leaders encourage an open line for contact via telephone for complaints which are to always be shared. Additionally, in order to ensure the participation of beneficiaries, consider special needs and include them in the design of the programme before starting the activity in a centre, different FGDs are carried out with the different existing population groups (differentiated by gender and age) and with the different committees and people with greater conditions of vulnerability identified within the community (pregnant women, elderly people, people with different degrees of disabilities, single female heads of household, victims of GBV...). Following these discussions, the intervention is tailored and the beneficiaries of the programme are identified. This process is followed by a more detailed analysis by specialised case of the particular needs of each family together with the construction team to adapt the shelter to meet their needs.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Specific committees of persons with disabilities and their relatives will be created, in order to ensure their needs are adequately considered during project implementation . This will take into consideration the different types of disability as relevant to the implementation accessible service structures. This refers to enhanced participation, as well as to sufficient physical access to all necessary services. In addition, during the implementation of the project, households containing people with disabilities will have priority for receiving assistance in terms of shelter/NFI and will receive extra support for the construction/rehabilitation of their shelters. Aspects such as functional diversity or other special needs will be taken into consideration when adapting the shelters to their users with measures such as enlarging the size of the doors for wheelchair access, direct access without obstacles, steps and other types of adjustments that are necessary to improve the comfort and life conditions of the people inhibiting the shelters.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Before starting project implementation, AeA will conduct an environmental risk analysis to inform its actions . Risk management, adaptation and mitigation measures will be the transversal axis of project implementation. This includes planning of the territory, analysis of ownership or use in this case, analysis of environmental risks by sector (shelter, agriculture, fishing, services, etc.), are actions that define our work. AeA will include environmental mitigation measures through the replanting of identified areas close to the beneficiary populations with bamboo (in riparian areas) and trees, following the relevant distance metrics and regulations. In addition, AeA will carry out and analysis and identify specific areas for the collection of earth and mud completion of the shelter. Through this activity AeA aims to reduce the risk of erosion, floodings and other associated risks that could later affect the shelters. In addition, this project will be implemented alongside WASH and livelihood components in Metuge and Ancuabe allowing AeA to optimize resources and reduce the environmental footprint of the project
