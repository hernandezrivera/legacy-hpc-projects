Project Summary

The proposed interventions aim at improving beneficiaries’ access to food through cash-based emergency support in the short term, supporting beneficiaries through the safety net programs, engaging youth in skill development program for livelihoods diversification and establishing small enterprises. These initiatives will enable beneficiaries to deal with the impending situation and mitigate the negative coping strategies on immediate basis and will help beneficiaries to recover from shocks. Livelihoods and Food Security will continue to engage with other like-minded agencies, institutions and respective public sector departments to promote social safety nets and social protection through cash programming, Skills development and establishing small businesses. The cash will be provided as Unconditional Cash Transfers (UCTs) for multiple needs, Conditional Cash Transfers (cash-for-work for small-scale infrastructure repairs) where appropriate, and companion programming for awareness raising around good nutrition through promoting diet diversity, BCC and safe food preparation, and hygiene practices. All cash transfer programs (CTPs) will take into account seasonality including agricultural calendar and fluctuating market prices.
NRC aims to support and strengthen resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks. This objective will contribute to protecting and conserving natural resources on which lives, and livelihoods depend, such as water, soil, land and forest resources. This will increase purchasing power of displacement-affected people to enable them meet their minimum needs for food and non-food items and assist in recovery of their livelihoods. NRC will continue to work with such entities to ensure that cash plus transfers are done using more efficient, stronger and locally accountable market-based approaches. NRC will support targeted populations especially in hard to reach areas to meet their basic food and non-food needs.
NRC is an active member of Somalia Food Security Cluster, the Somalia Cash Working Group, Somalia Cash Consortium and the Regional Food Security & Nutrition Working Group, Guidance from these fora will inform NRC program design and transfer values in each of the proposed regions.

Needs

As per September 2018 FSNAU report states that ‘Despite recent improvement in rain, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Furthermore, acute food insecurity remains prevalent among pastoral populations who lost most of their livestock and became destitute during the 2016/2017 drought and Cyclone Sagar; riverine populations affected by flooding in April and May 2018; and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards. As per the August 2018 joint rapid needs assessment report in South central region 'The protracted conflict in Somalia has led to complex humanitarian and livelihoods crises. This is characterized by increased socio-economic vulnerability and worsening poverty, with almost 50% of the population in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. The Somali communities, in particular those living in south central regions of Somalia, have been experiencing frequent and chronic conflict patterns characterized by violent clan tensions as well as the conflict between Al-Shabaab and the Somali government forces backed by AMISOM that led many communities to be uprooted from their homes in the attempt to escape from those tensions and sought refuge to move to the IDP settlements in Mogadishu corridor with the hope of accessing better Humanitarian assistance. The assessment report summarizes the main findings of the rapid needs assessment in terms of presenting the livelihood situation and the pressing needs of the new arrivals as well as recommending the typical lifesaving interventions required by the affected households. The study recommended 'Provision of unconditional cash transfers would address household food insecurity by the availability given that the affected community have access to functioning markets in the areas around IDP settlements'

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main risk identified in the disaster hit area included protection of basic right of vulnerable individuals (particularly that of Women and children), inclusion and exclusion error and peace. NRC will advocate to mitigate impact of the drought and contribute to improved protective environment and durable solutions for displacement affected populations across Somalia. This objective will be achieved through promotion and protection of HLP rights, expanding adequate access to legal documentations, fostering peaceful co-existence, strengthening tenure security, and addressing rights violations that are linked to forced eviction through prevention and response interventions. Beneficiaries, including those in hard-to-reach areas, will acquire knowledge, develop skills and be provided specialized counselling and legal assistance support to enable them participate and contribute to the protection and fulfillment of their rights. Specific emphasis will be placed on women and youth. The persistent humanitarian needs and continuing widespread insecurity across Somalia justifies the necessity to implement activities that are designed to mitigate the impact of the crisis and promote durable solutions in a way that accelerates recovery. NRC will therefore implement projects with customized assistance packages that crosscut the different phases of displacement. NRC’s implementation modalities will include structured information sessions, the operation of fixed and mobile legal clinics, formal training events, IECs and other visual aids, partnership with national organizations

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

It is critical to invest in life saving initiative which will not only help to save lives and mitigate a further deterioration of food security but will also help the communities to protect their livelihoods assets and minimize negative coping strategies. Over the period of time, NRC has been reaching the most vulnerable beneficiaries as per the set and defined criteria through communities targeting processes. In each of the selected site (Village and or IDPs camp) a community committee with male and female membership is established. This community committee is oriented about the program design and vulnerability criteria. The vulnerability criteria explicitly include women and women headed households, households with more children and families with SAM and MAM children and women to be included in the beneficiaries lists. Once the communities’ committees have registered the initial list of beneficiaries, then NRC Program and M&E staff randomly picks the identified beneficiaries and verifies the given particular through door to door assessment. As we understand that women and children suffer the most in any of the disaster, therefore a cognizant effort is made make sure that the vulnerable segment particularly women are included in the program and there is no compromise on selection criteria.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Over the period of time, this has been observed that forced child recruitment is on rise, especially in areas under the control of non-state armed actors in southern and central Somalia. It presents not only a protection concern but has also triggered displacement as families seek to shield youth from forced recruitment Exploitative relationships between IDPs and land owners or ‘gatekeepers’ are recurrent, affecting IDPs’ access to assistance and information, and increasing vulnerability and risk of forced eviction. thus effectively worsening an already dire humanitarian situation in which local capacities have been severely overstretched resulting to competition and conflict over resources in communities hosting IDPs. Majority of those displaced by the drought or conflict, mostly rural-urban, currently reside in unplanned camps in worse affected communities without any immediate prospect of returning due to persistent chronic conditions in both their places of origin and current places of displacement. NRC will continue monitoring, documentation and reporting to prevent forced eviction and/or facilitate lawful and dignified relocation processes; and mitigate post-eviction shocks by providing emergency appropriate lifesaving assistance packages. Program team will coordinate with other thematic areas (ICLA) for advocating the challenge at right forum.
