Project Summary

The project is specifically designed to address the educational needs of two vulnerable demographics: children, both girls and boys, returning from the Al-Hol camp to Ar-Raqqa hosting communities, and children who have dropped out of formal schools. These groups often find themselves marginalized due to circumstances beyond their control, such as displacement and disruptions in their educational journey. Our approach is grounded in the fundamental principle of equity, ensuring that all children, regardless of their background or past educational experiences, have access to high-quality learning opportunities. According to the HNO 2024, 7.2 million people in Syria are in need of education services. Of this number, 6.9 million are children aged 5-17 years. Specifically, Ar-Raqqa accounts for 300,644 of these children. Additionally, there are 2.45 million children out of school nationwide, with over a million at risk of dropping out. Notably, 48% of the children at risk are located in Ar-Raqqa. Moreover, RNA conducted by Ashna revealed that 88% of the children returning from the Al-Hol camp, especially those from ex-ISIS families lack access to education services, and they face significant barriers due to social stigma, economic challenges and lack of non-formal education centers. Additionally, 78% of HHs were forced to withdraw their children from school due to financial hardship and the inability to cover educational expenses. Our proposed project tackles various dimensions of educational disparity. This includes setting up two non-formal education centers in Ar-Raqqa governate, located to ensure accessibility for all children in need. These centers will primarily offer three programs: Basic Literacy and Numeracy (BLN), Accelerated Learning (ALP), and Self-Learning Programs (SLP), which will be equipped with trained educators who are sensitive to the diverse learning needs of the students. Additionally, the education services will be complemented with recreational and protection activities, more specifically with PSS and child protection referral services to provide holistic support to children, addressing not only their academic needs but also their emotional well-being and safety. Furthermore, a digitalization approach will be adopted to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of education delivery. The educational software and applications will be pre-installed on the tablets to facilitate interactive learning experiences. Moreover, students will receive educational materials like stationery kits. A thorough protection risk analysis will be conducted to identify potential vulnerabilities, including risks of exploitation, discrimination, and violence against children in educational settings. Measures will be implemented to ensure the safety and well-being of learners, including providing child protection training for staff and community members, as well as establishing monitoring mechanisms. A robust PSEA policy will be integrated into the project to prevent and respond to incidents of abuse or exploitation. This will include implementing clear reporting mechanisms, conducting awareness campaigns, and enforcing stringent vetting procedures for staff and volunteers. Some risks may arise. The local conflict poses a threat to the safety and security of education centers, potentially disrupting learning activities and endangering students and staff. Insufficient training opportunities for staff and educators may also hinder their ability to provide quality education to students. Additionally, some households may be resistant to change or skeptical about the benefits of education services for their children, leading to reduced participation and engagement. To address these risks, regular security assessments will be conducted, training for staff and educators, back to learning campaigns and awareness raising for communities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project is tailored to address educational needs via non-formal education (NFE) services, coupled with child protection services, catering to two distinct demographics: children returning from Al-Hol camp to host communities in Ar-Raqqa City, impacted by the radical environment within the camp, and children who discontinued schooling due to economic obstacles. Targeting 6,000 children aged 6 to 17, with 50% being girls, services will be facilitated through two NFE centers to be established in two separate neighborhoods within Ar-Raqqa City. The digitalization approach will be integrated into the education activities to ensure modernized and interactive learning experiences, leveraging technology to facilitate curriculum delivery, assessment, and communication among students and educators. Additionally, recreational services will be conducted, such as sports activities, arts, and music sessions to promote physical well-being, creativity, and social interaction among the children. Moreover, child protection services will be implemented at the NFE centers, offering activities such as PSS and child safe referrals to connect children with other service providers such as healthcare professionals, mental health aid organizations ensuring they receive comprehensive support and assistance as needed. Furthermore, to mitigate the financial obstacles faced by students' households, kits will be distributed to the students, such as stationery kits, to ensure they have essential resources for their educational pursuits. A total of 50 teachers will be supported with specialized trainings and teaching materials to enhance their skills and effectiveness in delivering quality education to students. Ashna will closely coordinate with the NES education working group to prevent duplication of efforts, streamline activities, share best practices, and ensure efficient use of resources in enhancing educational opportunities and outcomes for vulnerable children in the target areas. A robust M&E process will be conducted to gather information, assess progress, and ensure the effectiveness of the implemented interventions, enabling data-driven decision-making and continuous improvement of the project. Ashna will also ensure regular engagement of stakeholders, specifically the students' parents and caregivers, in adjusting project interventions and seek their perceptions and input to foster collaboration and ensures that the project remains responsive to the evolving needs and priorities of the students involved. A robust PSEA procedures will also be adopted to prevent and respond to incidents of abuse or exploitation, ensuring the safety and well-being of all beneficiaries, particularly vulnerable and at-risk children. Back to learning and outreach campaigns will be executed to raise awareness within the communities, encouraging households to send their children to participate and engage in educational services within the NFE centers.
