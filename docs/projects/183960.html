Project Summary

The plan is designed to respond to conflict affected children with protection needs as per the Humanitarian Needs Overview in targeted localities across 7 states including some locked areas of Jebal Marra, Nouba Mountains, and BN state, with a particular focus on children affected by conflict and displacement. the intervention will majorly contribute to SO3: mitigate protection risks and respond to protection needs through humanitarian action.
This project aims at addressing the most pressing CP needs in BN, CD, ND, NK, RS, SK, and WD states that are all with highly ranked severity of 2,876,116 PIN. 144,025 beneficiaries will be reached by the project's proposed services, desegregated as follows (14,402 men, 28,805 women, 47,528 Boys, and 53,289 are Girls). 9% of the total target will be reached in BN, 24% in CD, 14% in ND, 6% NK, 4% in RS, 19% in SK, and 24% in WD state.
Quality, specialized CP services will be provided to the most vulnerable, at-risk children, by provision of case management and referral services for UASC, and other vulnerable children. alternative care will be arranged considering the child's best interest, while providing family tracing and reunification services. also attention will be payed to assisting children with disability by direct, or indirect protection support. furthermore, SCI will promote capacities, and resilience of children families, and communities, through child protection group activities including establishing MPCs, and community based PSS. community-based structures, along with government staff will be built capacity on how to prevent, identify, and respond to existing protection risks and concerns, contribute to advocacy, and referral of child protection cases, and promote a safer environments for children.

1 - Needs

As per the 2022 (HNO), 14.6 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance across Sudan in 2022. Needs are driven by an extended economic cirisis characterized by sustained high inflation, weak public service provision and a volatile political context; with repeated shocks including intercommunal conflicts, mass displacement, flooding, and disease outbreaks including the COVID-19 pandemic. The needs of children are likely to remain high in 2022, given the ongoing economic deterioration across the country, and with an expected increase in intercommunal conflict in several parts of the country including Jabal Marrah in Darfur. Needs of adolescents are likely to increase as their specific concerns including safety, property rights and livelihoods remain unaddressed in conflict settings. The 2022 HNO indicates that a total of 3.1 million children are in need of child protection assistance out of which 1.3 million are IDPs, 474,406 are returnees, and 1.4 million are vulnerable residents. There are igh rates of children who are separated from their families/typical caregivers, children engaged on hazardous labor, children that have experienced violence, abuse, and neglect, and children who are not accessing core child protection services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

A- Activities to provide Quality, specialized child protection services are available and accessible to the most vulnerable and at-risk girls and boys :-the activities will mainly include Identification and registration of unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs) and family tracing and reunification (FTR) services; case management and referrals for at-risk children and child SGBV survivors to medical, legal support and other services and provision of material assistance; support to children associated with armed groups (e.g. life skills, prevention awareness, vocational training).B- Activities to improve well-being, capacity, and resilience of girls and boys through age- and gender-appropriate community-based protection services:-this will be achieved through Provision of psychosocial support to girls and boys of all ages, including through establishment and operation of Child Friendly Spaces for provision of structured MHPSS and recreational activities. Referral to specialized support and formal counselling services, establishment, training and guidance to Community-based Child Protection Networks (CBCPNs), and other community-based protection activities, construction / rehabilitation of community infrastructure which supports improved security and protection (community support protection mechanism), and carrying out activities for youth and adolescents to build resilience (including life-skills, creative activities, building social skills, addressing risks etc).C-Activities to enhance protective environments for girls and boys through advocacy and mobilization of caregivers, communities, local service providers, and duty bearers:this will involve strengthening of reporting and referral mechanisms, and training government and key stakeholders on case management, CP information management system (CPIMS), child rights, safeguarding and protection. Policy advocacy and support to SOPs, including for children on the move. child Protection and SGBV community outreach activities including (a) preventative activities on child marriage working with community decision-makers including religious leaders and midwives; (b) training parents and communities on positive parenting (and positive discipline for parents and teachers) to tackle physical and humiliating practices (PHP); (c) contribution to international campaigns (such as 16 Days of Activism, Day of Girl Child, Day of African Child); (d) awareness-raising on migration risks for children on the move (e) registration campaigns (birth registration and death for mothers and children under 5).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate crisis is an intergenerational child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure, will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics (e.g. deforestation, brick manufacturing processes) and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. All construction works will be done to limit negative environmental impact on the soil and its surroundings. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. SC Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation in Sudan.The Community Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPNs) will be supported to monitor, and mitigate some environmental concerns that could possibly expose children to further harm including, improper waste management, unsafe drinking water, etc through positive environmental messaging. moreover, the project will consider including DRR awareness raising in locations where natural disasters are likely to happen.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Programme design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. Save the Children will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. SC Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. A Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions. disability sensitivity will also be considered in the design/Accessibility of MPCs, latrines, selection of recreational activities within the child friendly spaces, and especial arrangements will be carried out when needed, in counseling (mobile, home visits approach), and referrals to required services. CBCPNs will be capacitated to report, and monitor protection concerns, and rights violations, and they will be shared with cluster lead, and relevant CP agencies.SCI will ensure that confidentiality and privacy are respected in all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing, following the data protection and information sharing protocols especially during the implementation of case management.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Save the Children will ensure that girls and boys, women and men (of all ages, abilities and backgrounds) are consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle, and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in local languages and culturally appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. SC Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities which provide affected populations opportunities to give feedback anonymously and/or privately, and options for those literate and illiterate. Field level MEAL staff are responsible for receiving complaints, managing responses, and reporting them to Country Office MEAL staff who report to Senior Management. Data is anonymised, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. There are clear processes for responding to PSEA and other safeguarding concerns raised through any feedback channel. Save the Children has safeguarding specialist staff at Country Office level, who will provide additional support and conduct monitoring visits.
