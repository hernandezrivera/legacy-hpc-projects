Project Summary

The project seeks to provide a regular program of Food Baskets distribution for most vulnerable people in Aleppo governorate, UFUK Int in coordination with LCs will select about 1200 HHs as beneficiaries in four communities in A’zaz, Al-Bab and Afrin district.
The beneficiaries will be selected according to many vulnerable standards, four distribution center will be provided in each targeted communities to facilitate distribution process
UFUK will take care to avoid overlap or duplicating process through good coordination with FSL cluster and other stakeholders among the region, as well as verifying the names of beneficiaries.
Who can benefit of this project?
Vulnerable people affected by the conflict will be the benefit of this project, in Syria situation, particularly in NW Syria. We set many vulnerable criteria to select the beneficiaries of this project as the following:
1 Female Headed Household/child-headed household
2 Injured/Disabled-headed households
3 Households with at least one pregnant women
4 Households with at least one elderly, chronically ill or disabled person
5 displaced families
6 Households living in collective shelter/abandoned building or equivalent poor living conditions
7 Households caring for a separated or unaccompanied child
Regularly distribution program:
This project will provide monthly Food Baskets for selected beneficiaries, that’s will be necessary for achieving not just food security, but also the stability and empowerment for these vulnerable people that will help them to revive and recover again as well avoid them to resorting to negative coping mechanisms such as early marriage, child labor and recruit in armed groups as sources of livelihoods.
Replace or exclude names of beneficiaries will be available if some of them didn't meet the criteria or provided wrong information or moved to other areas, anywise the aid will provide based need.
Regularly distribution will be in coordination with LCs and IDPs committees in the targeted communities, Through distribution center will take the place in each targeted community;
UFUK Int will take in consideration the safety and security of distribution place, UFUK team commit to apply the humanitarian and protection principles in all projects as well as set a complaint system including complaint box, Whats’Up number and monitoring officers to ensure the services are delivered to beneficiaries as dignity and transparency.
For elder people and women or people with special needs; distribution team will deliver the baskets food door to door as well specialising corner for them in the distribution centre.
Procurement and Distribution:
UFUK have developed its financial and procurement policies as appropriate with Syrian context, to contribute in reviving the economic NW Syria, UFUK prefer to purchase the required procurement from local markets, if the goods are not available in local markets
UFUK have ability to purchase from Turkey and shipped to Syria by Turkey-Syria cross borders. The procurement process including tenders, quotations, conflicting interest will be under transparency ways, sharing all document with donors.
The beneficiaries are being registered and verifications have been finalized prior to the distribution.
Distribution team have already lists of beneficiaries who have to provide the required documents such as ID and family card to get the baskets and sign, as well UFUK will invite the represent of communities (LCs / IDPs committees / Women associations) to monitoring distribution process
What is Food Basket content?
The food basket consists of a number of items (the minimum three are cereal, pulses and oil, but items such as salt, sugar, canned meat can be added). The general basket is normally delivered as a package of dry items. For families have children 1-5 years; will get additional items that considered nutrients are essential for children.

4 - Needs

According to Situation Report 29 January 2020 (OCHA)Some 2.8 million of the four million people living in northwest Syria are estimated to require humanitarian assistance, with women and children estimated to comprise 76% of this population. The main reported needs of the recently displaced people are shelter, food, non-food items, and cash assistance. Winter conditions are adding an additional layer of urgency to the situation, with cold weather and flooding exacerbating vulnerabilities. Civilians living in northwest Syria have been enduring a drastically worsening humanitarian situation as hostilities continue to intensify since November 2019. From 1 December 2019 to 27 January 2020, nearly 390,000 people – around 80% of whom are women and children – were displaced in northwest Syria, mostly from large population centers such as Saraqab and Ma’arrat An Nu’man, as well as smaller towns and villages in other parts of southern Idleb governorate. Many have been displaced multiple times, with each new displacement compounding experienced risks and vulnerabilities. With further mass displacement taking place since 27 January, this number is likely to continue increasing significantly.Most people displaced since December 2019 have been moving towards northern Idleb as well as Afrin, A’zaz and Al-Bab areas of northern Aleppo governorate. With an estimated 400,000 people displaced in northwest Syria between May and August 2019 and nearly 390,000 people displaced in northern Syria since December 2019, increasingly large numbers of people are being squeezed into an increasingly small pocket of the northwest. The large numbers of displacements are severely exacerbating shelter and accommodation shortages across northwest Syria; thousands of families are reportedly temporarily accommodated in public buildings, schools and mosques.
