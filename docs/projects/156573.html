Needs

According to HNO, sustaining basic health services remains a key need across the country. Thirty-seven percent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services; 41 percent of the population requires treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs), but the infrastructure and referral system that remains functional after seven years of conflict is inadequate to cope with these needs.Access and referral to specialist health services are similarly affected. This includes dialysis, and cancer and thalassemia treatment, as well as trauma care and rehabilitation. UNICEF/WHO estimates that in 2019, about 577,219 newborns will require routine immunization. In addition to this, an estimated 320,000 children between 13 and 59 months of age are estimated not to have been vaccinated during their first years of life.Due to limited access to clean water, and suboptimal hygiene conditions, Localized outbreaks of measles, acute bloody diarrhea and typhoid fever were detected and reported on, mainly affecting the governorates of Al--Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor, Aleppo, and Idleb, an average of 6 percent of all patients seen were treated for diarrhea, while 8 percent were treated for influenza-like illnesses.The limited availability of diagnosis and treatment for cases of tuberculosis is an issue highlighted by over 50 percent of health care workers interviewed for the 2018 WoS health sector assessment. Non-communicable diseases remain among the most prevalent health issues in Syria. WHO estimates that approximately 12 percent of the population has diabetes and 20 percent hypertension. Similar data for the prevalence of chronic respiratory diseases and cancers were not available. Untreated non-communicable diseases lead to serious complications such as renal failure and disability due to stroke and amputations. In northwest Syria, a total of 837 renal failure patients are receiving dialysis services through 20 facilities (6,575 sessions on a monthly basis). Although the availability of such services is limited, it is essential that this treatment can continue. the health sector’s current capacity to provide emergency and comprehensive health services for trauma and burns, as well as surgery and postoperative rehabilitation care, is insufficient to meet the existing need across the country. Referral of these cases to the small number of specialized hospitals is a significant burden on the health system. An average of 52,000 trauma cases per month was treated in health facilities supported by humanitarian partners in NSAG-controlled areas. During the first seven months of 2018, the already overstretched health service providers treated over 30,000 war-related trauma cases.

Implementation of activities

Bahar will start the implementation by communicating with the staff, local health authorities and Turkish Health Directorates to inform them about the project and the changes that will take place on the targeted facilities.A Kick-off meeting will take place in Bahar Organization to inform the departments about their tasks and duties through this project.Bahar will renew the contracts with the health staff of the ongoing facilities, and start the recruitment process to hire the staff of the new ones.The logistics department of Bahar will conduct the procurement process for the items mentioned in the budget according to the Standards Operating Procedures SOPs of the logistics and Finance departments.Bahar will conduct an Information, Education, and Communication IEC campaign in the target communities to inform them about the health services provided through the targeted facilitiesBahar will communicate with WHO to allocate pharmaceutical kits to these centers according to the number of beneficiaries and type of services.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Bahar will deconflict all the targeted health facilities, report any attack on them, and support any efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. - Gender Based Violence GBV: Hiring of female staff to increase access to health services for women and girls, training gynecologists, midwifes and nurses on the clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals, and link the health facilities with the social services to ensure safe referral pathways between both of them.- Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: Bahar will conduct orientation session on PSEA on all health facilities to ensure all the staff is aware of PSEA, all the recruitment process will include PSEA in the criteria of selection, and all the staff should sign Bahar code of conduct, and PSEA policy.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Cooperation and coordination with authorities: Bahar holds an official permission from Hatay and kills Health directorates to operate its health facilities in Afrin and Azaz, and have good coordination with the local health authorities in Aleppo and Idleb, in addition to good relation and coordination with the local councils and all the active partners on the targeted areas.- Access to implementation areas: Bahar has good experience in implementing projects in the targeted areas, and to decrease the risk of the lack of accessibility, all the project materials will be procured as one shot.- changing frontlines, changes in the parties controlling the implementation areas and significant population movements as they flee shifting conflict areas: Bahar has an evacuation plan and can ensure the security and the evacuation of the staff in case of emergency.- Available, qualified staff: Bahar has good experience in implementing health projects, and have much high qualified staff, also, we will build the capacity of the staff to increase the quality of services.
