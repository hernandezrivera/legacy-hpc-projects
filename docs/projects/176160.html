Project Summary

The programmatic activities are designed to align with the 4 Health Cluster’s Sectoral Outcome Objectives and the direct project beneficiaries targeted are 10,732 people with an average budget of $46/beneficiary. The indirect project beneficiaries 35,113 people in Akobo County (Akobo East Area) of Jonglei State and Rubkona and Guit Counties of Unity State. The total budget of the project is $495,000. The project will support about 7,200 OPD consultations in static facilities/mobile clinics, facilitate 200 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants, provide 52 weekly EWARS/IDSR reports from each of the 3 focus Counties, facilitate 3,600 children with 3 doses of PENTA-3 vaccine, provide maternal healthcare services to 4,506 women of child bearing age of IDPs, returnees and host community including FP, ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming and TT immunization and also provide child healthcare services (routine immunization, Vit A supplementation and child growth monitoring) to 1,300 girls and 1,300 boys, including accelerated mass measles and polio campaigns.
The project will provide psychiatric treatment and psychosocial support to 75 mentally ill persons and provide community-based level 2 and level 3 (non-specialized) MHPSS to 2,700 persons living with disabilities (PLWD's), including referrals for specialized services from other partners.
About 3,000 uncomplicated diarrhea cases will be treated with ORS and Zinc, 4,200 uncomplicated malaria cases will be treated with ACT including 2,700 uncomplicated respiratory infection (pneumonia) treated with Amoxicillin.
Through active community engagement, about 22,500 people with health education and promotion to prevent disease outbreaks and another 27,500 people will be reached through awareness raising sessions to prevent GBV and child abuse, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, drug abuse, peacebuilding and promotion of peaceful co-existence including ensuring that 3,000 pregnant women, lactating mother and children under-5s are screen for malnutrition and refer for treatment/management
The project will facilitate 12 community meetings in each county to provide accountability to the communities, facilitate 4 joint supportive supervision visits to increase quality of services quarterly and strengthen linkages with Boma Health Initiatives (Boma Health Workers) for community-based disease surveillance and referral and also ensuring provision of care to physical injuries and CMR including level 2 and level 3 MHPSS to survivors of SGBV.
With funding from HPF in a consortium with CORDAID and CARE International, CHADO has presence in Guit County moving into the 3rd consecutive year. Similarly, with funding from ECHO/-WF/BUD/2020/91035, Concern World Wide sub-granted CHADO part of the funding to implement health components of the funding in Guit and Rubkona Counties of Unity State, moving into its 2nd consecutive year. In Akobo, CHADO will be operating in Walgak area. The PHCC+ and 4 PHCUs has been non-functional for over 9 months. CHADO will reopen 1 PHCC+ and 4 PHCUs and establish 2 mobile clinics in Akobo East (Walgak area) while in Rubkona and Guit counties, CHADO will establish 1 PHCC, 2 PHCUs and 2 mobile clinics in each of the counties respectively so as to increase access and utilization of health services by people in hard-to-reach communities including strengthening linkages with Boma Health Workers to establish very strong community-based surveillance and referral pathways.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO, 2021), estimated that some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the nearby countries. According to South Sudan Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) projections between December 2020 to July 2021, it is estimated that about 5.8 million people or 48% of the people analyzed will likely face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between December 2020 and March 2021 and the 7.2 million people or 60% of the people analyzed will likely face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) from April to July 2021. Akobo County of Jonglei State, Rubkona County of Unity State and Guit County of Unity State are all in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) within the projected period. The key population in all the 3 targeted Counties are children, women, men, the aged and persons with disabilities including survivors of GBV among returnees, IDPs and host communities. The host community’s resources has already been strained due the increasing number of returnees and presence of IDPs thereby increasing their vulnerability. Key informant interviews, focus groups discussions (FGDs) and desk reviews were conducted as part of to inform this proposal in Guit, Rubkona and Akobo Counties. A number of health risks and emergency health needs identified based on facts. First, there will be an increased malnutrition, heightened risk illness and death for neonatal and children due to pneumonia, malaria, diarrhoea, and measles related cases. Second, pregnant women are at greater risk of complications from obstetric emergencies. Third, there will be risk of increased incidents of sexual violence and an increase in the number of people requiring help for addictions, substance abuse, mental health illnesses and psychosocial support. Most facilities are mainly run by unskilled staff and deliveries are consequently mainly assisted by unskilled staff compounded by lack of physical access to health facilities especially during the rainy season. There is also a heightened risk of outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases, including meningitis, measles and cholera. In Akobo County with an estimated population of 221,022, the PIN are 77,358 people and HC targets 61,886 people. CHADO will be targeting 25% of the HC target which is 15,471 people (4,868 women, 2,750 men, 3,986 girls and 2,750 boys). Among them are 2,320 people living with disability and 4,378 people are returnees. Similarly Rubkona County, with an estimated population of 333,412 people, the PIN are 142,818 people and HC targets 142,818 people. CHADO will be targeting 10% of the HC target which is 14,282 people (3,400 women, 3,300 men, 3,857 girls and 3,756 boys). Among them are 3,213 people living with disability. Likewise Guit County, with an estimated population of 68,179, the PIN are 68,179 people and HC targets 36,362 people. CHADO will be targeting 100% of the HC target which is 36,362 people (8,105 women, 6,730 men, 10,505 girls and 11,021 boys). Among them are 5,454 people living with disability. With funding from HPF in a consortium with CORDAID and CARE International, CHADO has presence in Guit County moving into the 3rd consecutive year. Similarly, with funding from ECHO/-WF/BUD/2020/91035, Concern World Wide sub-granted CHADO part of the funding to implement health components of the funding in Guit and Rubkona Counties of Unity State, moving into its 2nd consecutive year. In Akobo, CHADO will be operating in Walgak area. The PHCC+ and 4 PHCUs has been non-functional for over 9 months. CHADO will reopen 1 PHCC+ and 4 PHCUs and establish 2 mobile clinics in Akobo East (Walgak area) while in Rubkona and Guit counties, CHADO will establish 1 PHCC, 2 PHCUs and 2 mobile clinics in each of the counties respectively so as to increase access and utilization of health services by people in hard-to-reach communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Through establishment community-based complaints mechanisms (CBCMs) as a key component in the prevention and response to SEA, the local communities will be informed that humanitarian assistance is free and never conditioned on sexual favors. CHADO will set-up a specialized approach, including confidential complaints and investigations procedures if there are breaches.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CHADO will ensure the representation of all segments of a community, including women, men, girls, boys, and people living with disabilities, young people and the elderly, individuals who are not literate, will have access to feedback session or community engagement.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CHADO will actively and routinely seek the feedback of the people in the community at every stage of the project cycle. All segments of a community, including women, men, girls, boys, and people living with disabilities, young people and the elderly will have access to feedback mechanisms, with particular attention to access by the most vulnerable. Feedback will be used to adjust and improve practice in programming at a local and timely way and communities will be informed as to how their feedback will be used, or who it will be referred to. The feedback sessions will be through community meetings and integrated FGDs. CHADO will establish joint mechanisms with communities that will offer coherent, unified, entry points for communities and joint approaches to processing, managing and responding to feedback and complaints.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback mechanism will foster a sense of mutual trust and encourage teamwork from planning, implementation and project review. Context specific and culturally sensitive feedback plan or social accountability guidelines will comprise a number of sub-activities such as beneficiary scorecards about the services and periodic joint review sessions of beneficiaries and the local authorities. Other feedback plans will include: Joint stakeholder field monitoring visits, freedom of information petitions, community monitoring of service delivery and participatory budgeting.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CHADO at the planning stage of project, CHADO has strongly incorporate protection awareness from onset to the closing of the project to ensure beneficiary safety. Experience from South Sudan highlights key risks that require careful and measured approaches. These include: Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. CHADO will conduct risks assessment and analysis before providing services in any particular location. Services will ensure to provide services in locations that are easily accessible to all including persons with disabilities and elderly. Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse lie at the core of CHADO strategy to combat Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. As such, CHADO has a strict PSEA policy that is signed by all staff and contractors before engaging in any business with CHADO. Its implementation and adherence are undertaken in a comprehensive and proactive manner in collaboration with all project beneficiaries and CHADO partner agencies. CHADO screens and vets all hired staff for any misconduct in any former humanitarian organization, when a person is found to be in violations, this person is not hired by CHADO. Any person that is found to have committed an act of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse; such a person is immediately terminated. CHADO will conduct training to staff and partner agencies on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. This training is mandatory before a staff starts to work with CHADO and will continue regularly on refresher sessions. CHADO will also conduct awareness and outreach activities on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse including how to report, where to report and why it’s important to report. CHADO will regularly conduct risk assessment on PSEA including safety audits and taking all measures in preventing Sexual Exploitation and AbuseCHADO will use local content human resources especially persons from targeted communities to build trust while also being transparent to all project stakeholders in providing project objectives, targets, outcomes, period of project, who are being targeted as primary beneficiaries, how the project will benefit the community, CHADO will be transparent with the local authority, all beneficiaries through thorough consultations and involvement of women, men, boys and girls including PWDs and elderly in decision making and representations in the entire project life cycle. Apart from security risk which is significant enough and the likelihood of occurrence is high, CHADO do not perceive other risk significant enough to have negative impact on the project. The measures below will be put in place to reduce the risk that may occur in the field.i. Ensure CHADO presence at humanitarian coordination meetings ii. CHADO will ensure strong and extensive on-ground monitoring of activities.iii. CHADO will maintain daily assessment of security situation. iv. CHADO will meet with key stakeholders regularly as part of AAP.v. Consult staff on ground as they are from within the communities, formally and informally on a regular basis.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

CHADO will contribute by delivering survivor-focused interventions to survivors of sexual/gender based violence through provision community psychosocial support, psychological and specialized mental health care to Key Population (KPs)Accessibility: Given the high levels of GBV against KPs and the significant barriers KPs face in accessing health services, especially in relation to GBV, integrating GBV services into health services would be the best way to ensure that more GBV survivors access appropriate and timely care. Utilization of existing services: Of the five essential GBV services i.e. psychological first aid, emergency contraception (EC), post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP), prophylaxis for sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and vaccination against tetanus and hepatitis B, most are already provided by trained midwifes at the health facilities making the integration of GBV services cost-effective and logistically easier. Time-sensitive services delivery: All screened GBV survivors and people who are referred for GBV can access all GBV services in the same clinic and receive time-sensitive EC and PEP within the critical time frame.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The project will generally reduce the vulnerability of the affected population and with a resultant reduction in severity of needs and decreasing populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5. The project will promote sustainable solutions to the food security challenge that will improve household income and dietary diversiﬁcation. The project will utilize local initiatives, such as enhancing traditional livestock-crop systems, including supporting the key role that women play in improving poultry and crop production integration.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

All vulnerable population will have safe and equitable access to CHADO services including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons. CHADO will adopt systematic approaches to ensure the inclusion of older people and people with disabilities. Both (overlapping) groups are affected by many of the same or very similar barriers to access and participation. CHADO will ensure that women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons are recognized to ensure they receive assistance that is appropriate and relevant to their needs and have access to the health services they need. CHADO will also ensure that they are not negatively affected, and are more prepared, resilient and less at-risk as a result of CHADO action in the provision of health services including ensuring that they have access to information, and participate in decisions that affect them on an equal basis with others. CHADO will also ensure that they receive assistance from competent and well-managed staff and volunteers who are skilled and equipped to include them in humanitarian responses, and they have equal opportunities for employment and volunteering in humanitarian organizations.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will coordinate with Nutrition cluster partners in the micronutrient supplementation for pregnant women, nutrition education during ANC, promotion of breastfeeding and appropriate complementary feeding, child growth monitoring and promotion (GMP), management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), nutrition education within integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI) packages, vitamin A supplementation for children; and treatment of diarrhoea with zinc.
