Project Summary

The objective of the project is to strengthen the coordination of the humanitarian response in Cameroon to save lives, protect the most vulnerable and improve the resilience of populations. With the worsening crisis in the North-West and South-West regions, Cameroon now faces three complex humanitarian crises affecting eight of Cameroon's ten regions. The impact of these three crises on the living conditions of the affected populations is reinforced by pre-existing structural vulnerabilities that hamper their recovery, particularly in the North-West and South-West regions. In the North-West and South-West regions, on-going violence has had a major impact on the regional economy. Violence and insecurity have resulted in major displacement, affecting agricultural yields in the North-West and the abandonment of large parts of fruit, palm and rubber plantations in the South-West. However, unlike the North-West, the South-West has maintained a certain level of economic activities. Ethnic and political tensions between farmers and herders persist. The border with Nigeria, Africa’s greatest economy, is officially closed, making trade increasingly difficult. In the North-West and South-West regions, high levels of insecurity continue. The presence of national security structures - police, gendarmerie, army - is concentrated along the main roads and cities, leaving most rural areas under the influence of NSAGs.
The complexity and the scope of these crises therefore require more effective coordination, adapted to the different intervention areas and based on humanitarian principles. OCHA Cameroon's coordination project will thus make it possible to achieve a humanitarian response that is better funded, more strategic and that centralizes human rights and the protection of people in the North-West and South-West regions. It will also enable a better complementarity between emergency actions and development programs, in order to fight together the humanitarian needs and their underlying causes. To this end, OCHA will provide the essential services of disseminating key information products, coordinating the Humanitarian Programming Cycle and facilitating joint multisectoral needs assessments. Its presence in Yaoundé, Bamenda and Buea will enable it to provide decisive support to coordination mechanisms and promote coherence between operational issues in the field and strategic decision-making bodies. This key position will also enable it to advance the overriding considerations of the Centrality of Protection and Cross-cutting Protection, Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and nexus humanitarian development. From its sub-offices in the North-West and South-West regions, OCHA will also support the scaling up of the response in these regions, including through the management of CMCoord and humanitarian access responsibilities, as well as capacity building of local responders.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the North-West and South-West, the socio-political crisis, now entering its fourth year, has led to massive population displacements. As of August 2020, the North-West and South-West crisis has displaced over 1 million people, an increase over almost 200,000 people in comparison to December 2019, when 930,000 were displaced. As the crisis deepens, more people are leaving the insecure rural and bush areas and are moving to urban areas in the two regions, elsewhere in the country or to Nigeria. Meanwhile, a remarkable increase of returnees to the North-West and South-West regions is observed: while 204,000 returnees were reported in August 2019, 361,000 returnees were reported in August 2020. These returnees are typically those who displace into the bush after localized conflict and return soon after, constituting a regular pendular migration pattern. Over 100,000 returns were observed between January and August 2020. 10 percent returned from neighboring Nigeria, where the majority had never been registered. 42 percent of the returnees cite the safety in the village of origin as reason for their return, while almost 50 percent of the return movement is motivated by reasons related to livelihood. The number of IDPs from the North-West and South-West in the Littoral, West and Center regions has also increased: from 224,000 in 2019 to 297,000 in 2020. A majority of the displaced are women and children amongst whom are unaccompanied and separated children. The ban on education by NSAGs and attacks on education has forced many families to send their children to the Littoral, West and Center regions where they can pursue their education. Based on Government statistics, 35,000 IDP children are enrolled in primary schools of the Littoral and West regions. Despite this slight increase (+ 14 percent) from last year, an estimated 50,000 displaced children are not yet going to school, especially in the West region, which poses serious protection concerns. In the Center region, 2,284 displaced children are attending education in primary schools. The coordination needs are related to:• The definition of an overall coherence between the intersectoral coordination system in force at the national level and in the inter cluster system active in the North-West and South-West regions; and • The adaptation of access and protection strategies. However, the need for civil-military coordination remains. There also remains a need to improve efforts related to the collection and analysis of data on the displacement, needs and vulnerabilities of affected people, in order to propose the most appropriate interventions possible. Finally, it is still essential to strengthen the links between the humanitarian and development spheres through harmonized programming and common coordination mechanisms. The growing trend towards the return of IDPs to the Far North has served as a reminder that strategic collaboration between the authorities, development partners and humanitarians will be essential to enable IDPs to make progress towards durable solutions.
