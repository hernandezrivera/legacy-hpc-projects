Project Summary

The project objective is to provide of live saving nutrition services to the most drought and Conflict affected population (IDPs and host community) in Banadir,Bay, Lower Shebelle ,Mudug and Nugal regions
The project responds to the gender and age specific needs such as through lifesaving nutrition services to vulnerable drought and conflict affected communities in the IDPs and host community in target areas.
The proposed project areas are well known by HIJRA through the past and ongoing operation. The proposed project is intended to complement HIJRA’s existing emergency and resilience intervention. HIJRA will coordinate other actors in the area to avoid overlapping and will also be used referral for cases so as to be complementary each other and to maximize impact. Project will be monitored and report will be shared to cluster,unicefe,MOH and donor as per needed HIJRA engages the community in a sustainable and accountable manner to determine context and culturally appropriate need¬ based responses. The principle of Do-No-Harm approach will be use the entire project cycle. Community (men and women and youth) will be involved and will be fully participate in all project cycle and decision making processes . Hijra has Beneficiary protection desk aimed at increasing our accountability to our target beneficiaries. The beneficiary Protection desk will handle complaint and feedback mechanisms, the desk will receive complaints from beneficiaries, local authorities, internal displaced person (IDP) Leaders, other agencies and any other project stakeholder. Complaints will be channeled either through phone call or direct visit our offices located at KM5 Zobe In Mogadishu.
Therefore, under this HRP, HIJRA Will scale basic nutrition services in priority to IDPs and their host communities and other socially marginalized groups in target areas. The project will continue the ongoing OTPs in Banadir and Lower Shable region and expanding to Nugal,Mudug Bay region through fixed and be outreach base. HIJRA will focus on ensuring access to nutrition service for the women, men, boys and girls along with the most vulnerable among these groups such as the youth, elderly, persons with disabilities and pregnant and lactating women.
The project will use existing staff and recruit if need .Community health nutrition workers will detect active case of acute malnutrition and refer to facilities or mobile teams if without medical complication and to stabilization center if with medical complication .they will follow up progress of case. Staff will be trained on infancy young child feeding practice and Integrated management acute malnutrition (IMAM). IYF Promoter will perform breast feeding promotion and infancy young child feeding support through consulting one to one and workshop season, the OTPs treat acute malnutrition case and referral with medical complication MAM to stabilization centers and TSFPs respectively . The break in the provision of essential nutritional supply will have humanitarian consequences throughout the country. So HIJRA will procure essential drugs and nutrition supply and this will distribute to integrated facilities and outreach team in order to maintain running activities and to avoid stock out. This supply will be complement from supply receiving from MOH, WHO and UNICEF. The project will reach and treat acute malnutrition , to 10977 children 6 to 59 months (5379 boys and 5898 girls) and 3223 pregnant and lactating women as well as promoting IYCF practices to care givers through static OTP and mobile/outreach OTP in hard to reach areas

Needs

In spite of improvements, there are persistent humanitarian needs in Somalia; an estimated 1.5 million are in (IPC 3&4), of which 57% are IDPs. About 500,000 0ut of 1.5 million (IPC 3&4) and 1,172,000 out of 3.1 million people (IPC 2) are in Bay,Banadir,Lower shabelle,Mudug and Nugal regions. Somalia is 3rd highest acute malnutrition in eastern and southern Africa .An estimated 588 400 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including 11500 who are likely to be severely malnourished for Aug 2018 -Jul 2019. Severely malnourished children are nine times more likely to die of killer diseases. The GAM rate was critical in The IDPs of Garawo,Galkacyo,Baidoa and Banadir (16.6-17.7 percentage GAM) while SAM rate was crisis (2.5-3.6 percentage SAM) in same areas except Banadir which was critical SAM 4.1%, CDR 1.06 and U5DR 2, 56. In lower Shablle is crisis GAM 15.5% and SAM 2.9%. The target areas needs urgent nutrition and health support intervention. The high morbidity, low humanitarian support, poor child feeding and caring practices, food insecurity, limited health service availability, like poor EPI coverage, and poor health seeking behavior, and difficulty of accessing clean water supply are driving factors for the current acute malnutrition. The exclusive breast feeding is 33%, breast feeding 1st hour after delivery is 80%,by the 6 to 8 months infants introduced, solid, semisolid and soft was 80%(FSNAUand UNICEF,2016). The last 4 months HIJRAs facilities treated 1,230 6-59 months of boys and girls with SAM without complicated, 921 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) provided micronutrients and vitamin A ,as well provided IYCF practice consultation and awareness to 890 of care givers ,80% were women 20%men. Scale up of humanitarian assistant must be sustained to prevent further deterioration of nutrition to the affected population. The project will provide nutrition services to 5379 boys, 5898 girls and 3223 PLW in target areas.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project has seriously taken a note the risk facing displaced and vulnerable groups are excluded from the resources including humanitarian aid by the host communities. Marginalized groups including minority clans such as Somali Bantu, and other marginalized tribes at risk of being neglected will be given equal opportunity to participate as well as the disabled. During the selection of nutrition sites and training access to these groups will be considered. HIJRA will recruit some of its project staff from displaced populations especially for positions such as community nutrition workers in order to ensure IDPs get inclusion in accessing and benefiting from all our projects and will also ask for their participation in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.The project will note that making particular provision for persons with specific vulnerabilities – e.g., women headed-households, pregnant or lactating women, persons with disabilities (physical or mental), persons with HIV/AIDS or other chronic illnesses -- to ensure that everyone has equal opportunities, is not discrimination. this project for IDPs seeking for services must not be subject to discrimination for reasons related to their displacement

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

HIJRA Somalia therefore has first taken account of the different needs, recognize the potential barriers faced and ensure women and men access health services equally. All data will be disaggregated by sex and age and apply a gender analysis. HIJRA will involve from the outset women, girls, boys and men, including those that belong to the vulnerable groups, in health assessments, priority setting, programme design, interventions and evaluation. All nutrition consultations, examination and care will be in privacy. Nutrition personnel (women and men) will be adequately represented in gender theme groups, Communication strategies will highlight specific nutrtion risks affecting women and men, as well as targeting adolescent girls and boys in local Somali language and include physical and mental health services and their locations and ensure all hard to reach vulnerable members are fully aware of the existing services

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

This project has seriously taken a note that women and girls are at increased risk of violence due to the on-going civil unrest in this project area and may be unable to access assistance or make their needs known. On the other side the men?s roles as protectors in this otherwise conflict affected area has placed them on greater responsibility for risk takingHIJRA works very closely with communities and local authorities, ensuring that local laws, customs and culture are adhered to, and avoiding action and advocacy that may be perceived as offensiveConflict sensitivity in the program; do-no-harm approachProtection of women against Gender Based Violence (GBV); The program provides a provision to mainstream GBV monitoring and prevention mechanisms including; provision of referral notes to victims of GBV, gathering data of GBV cases in the program target areas and providing regular reports to relevant stakeholders including protection cluster as well as specific agencies that thematic focus is to prevent GBV in Somalia.
