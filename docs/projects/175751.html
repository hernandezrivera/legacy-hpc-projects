Project Summary

South Sudan has had serious internal conflict which started in 2013 and caused massive displacement of communities and protection needs. Gender based violence, sexual abuse, early marriages are just but some of the protection issues facing the communities due to the conflict.
In 2020 flooding further caused massive displacements of communities further causing serious protection needs to women and girls and children. Continued inter clan and intercommunal conflict further raises the protection needs in the country. This project will therefore address these protection needs by ensuring that activities are line with the protection cluster objectives of providing protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to the vulnerable and affected populations by offering protection services to the affected populations.
TADO will strive to prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and building resilience of the vulnerable and affected populations. This will be achieved through offering psychological support to the vulnerable and affected populations by establishment of psychosocial support desks and centers and establishment of referral pathways for SGBV survivors to access essential services leading to healing and recovery.
TADO will also build resilience among the vulnerable and affected populations by establishment of women and girls friendly centers where the beneficiaries will be given services and trainings which include recreational activities-Games , livelihood services (Making door mats, designing bed sheets and establishing Kitchen Gardens) parenting skills on child care, children upbringing and sensitivity on child stress.
Referral systems to other actors to access services by victims of SGBV will be done to ensure that the affected persons are offered comprehensive care that includes health care services and legal aid where possible and psychosocial support services.
In order to prevent and mitigate protection risks and build resilience, TADO will create awareness on GBV among the communities by ensuring that the communities are aware of the disadvantages of GBV and other protection threats in the communities. TADO will also support women and girls by training them on fuel-efficient stoves thus reducing protection risk raised as they walk to in search of firewood.
TADO will also mainstream protection in all her Health, Nutrition, WASH, Emergency Shelter and Nonfood items, Education and Food Security programming to ensure that there are linkages and synergies in their programming and offer integrated services to the vulnerable and affected populations. TADO will Provide capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to frontline service providers like basic advocacy tips and strategies, Gender/GBV referral pathways and how to establish women and girls friendly spaces, COVID19 awareness and protection measures/other related challenges it has on women and girls.
The response will have an overall target of 5,900 beneficiaries with a disaggregated data of (Girls=2000, Women=2000, Boys=1000, men-900) in Tonj North, and Tonj South. The project will target 1500 people with disabilities who are also highly vulnerable to protection issues to ensure disability inclusive protection programming.
A community centered-approach will be the focus, and TADO will work closely with the community stakeholders in mobilization, coordination and accountability to ensure that they receive appropriate services

1 - Needs

People in South Sudan continue to reef from the impacts of years of conflict, violence and limited in development and face a convergence of high needs related to food insecurity, protection, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). According to (HNO -OCHA report 2021), 80% people affected by multiple risks of protracted violent conflicts, climate change causing flooding which resulted in adverse food insecurity worsened by COVID-19 pandemic restrictions limiting movement of goods and people. Tonj East, Tonj South are in IPC level 5 according to IPC analysis in October 2020 IPC report. This severe food insecurity has caused high protection needs as women girls and boys are highly stressed due to lack of food and therefore exposes them to gender based violence and other serious protection concerns as they move in search of food and other essential commodities. A percentage of the affected are in need of general protection needs while 916,840 of this population are in urgent need of GBV specialized services, in such a situation, persons with disability are more vulnerable as the study reveals that they are 3 times more at risk and exposed to discrimination, exclusion and majorly suffer the impacts of gender-based violence (Oxfam, care international, HI 2012). According UN-OCHA led IRNA report 11th -14th August 2020, displaced, food insecure and conflict-affected women and girls in Tonj North county experience elevated levels of psychological distress causing mental health conditions which is widespread and largely untreated, a percentage of women and girls, boys reported signs of distress including depressive symptoms. The women and girls due to the conflict were exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse, domestic violence and are traumatized by the displacements. Coupled by these, displaced women and girls continue to bear the brunt of GBV that includes early marriages and unwanted pregnancies. A third of people displaced live in communities where women and girls avoid certain areas due to fear for their safety. Sadly, basic GBV risk mitigation measures around sanitation facilities remain extremely rare. According (UNMISS report 2021), Sexual violence including rape and sexual slavery continue to be rampant in Tonj North and South counties trend as major hotspot of conflict-related sexual violence referral pathways to the GBV services are lacking and communities are not aware of the available SGBV services. COVID-19 pandemic has worsened the mental health situation in South Sudan. In Tonj North and Tonj South counties are among the counties devastating leading to distressed Women, Boys, girls and men due restricted movement confining men and women at home for prolonged periods of time leading to increased domestic based violence.According to OCHA led multi agency IRNA assessment conducted in Tonj South, serious protection issues were identified after flooding and intercommunal fighting caused displacements in the communities. There were reported cases of gender-based violence, rape and communities were lacking shelter exposing women and girls to rape and other protection concerns including violence to women, girls and boys. The communities lacked awareness on GBV and there were no clear referral pathways to victims of GBV and other protection concerns. (Tonj East and South IRNA report June 2020). These GBV gaps needs emergency humanitarian response to ensure that women, girls, men and boys are able to access GBV services, build resilience and recover from multiple shocks of displacements, conflict, flooding and severe food insecurity.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

As TADO routinely Undertake PSEA orientation and briefing of its staffs and contactors, will be trained and oriented on the dangers of any related act of sexual exploitation and abuse to either the survivor’s and any other project beneficiary and cautioned to keep in mind a do no harm principle Confidentiality, Respect, keep safe and security and non discrimination of the beneficiaries, This will be held at all levels during project life. a) Confidentiality – information will be shared only with others who need to know in order to provide assistance and intervention, or as requested and agreed by the survivor. All actors will adhere to agreed protocols for sharing GBV-related information.b) Respect – actions and response of all project actors will be guided by respect for the wishes, the rights, and the dignity of the survivor, including treating the survivor in a non-judgmental way. c) Security and Safety – All project actors should be sensitive to the survivor’s needs for immediate care, while aiming to prevent further harm and/or distress. Any options should only be implemented with the consent of the survivor. d) Non-discrimination – All project actors shall always commit to providing services without discrimination based on ethnicity, clan, age, religion, gender, marital status, wealth, language, nationality, HIV status, political opinion, or other basis. The project will further be guided by the Humanitarian Charter, the Core Humanitarian Standards and Sphere Protection Standards, which require accountability of humanitarian actors to affected populations for the services they provide

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The proposed project will employ a mix methodological framework in realization of the desired change. This will include use of already existing community and local government structures such as; women and youth groups, local leaders as well as county and state officials. These various stakeholders will contribute towards mobilizing the target beneficiaries, influencing attitudes and behaviour change amongst targeted audience. local authority, community leaders, young women and men, informal and formal youth associations, women associations, faith based groups, young entrepreneurs, youth in leadership, organised drama groups, local artists and community leaders are the Primary target beneficiaries for this program. Traditions, beliefs, perceptions and lack of political will have sidelined young women and men in decision making processes including peace building and reconciliation process over the years. In most formal and informal structures, young men and young women are always neglected and at times few are involved for the sake of it but not meaningfully yet they form a greater portion of the country’s population. Therefore, this project has put a special attention to empower youth to demand for participation and to meaningfully contribute to the peace building, reconciliation, reforms and decision making processes and to take meaningful participation in the peace building practices. This project will especially pay attention to creation of mechanisms for the youth to actively participate in the implementation of the ARCISS. In terms of distributions activities, Safety audits will be done prior to the distribution days to ensure safe and convenient distribution points and timely distributions to ensure that women and girls go home early enough. They will in addition act as key pillars for future project sustainability.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

TADO will ensure Setting-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Mechanisms for collecting feedback will use a combination of channels. Channels include active channels, where client perspectives are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups); reactive channels, where clients have more control over when and how they share perspectives (such as complaint lines); and finally, ‘open channels’ where feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during implementation. TADO will strive and put affected populations at the center of decision-making processes that affect their lives so that services are choice-driven and client- focused. Special attention will also be paid to ensure the participation of, and consultation with, the most at- risk and/or marginalized members of society whose voices may not otherwise be heard. The project will also seek to support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including (not exclusively) the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education, TADO will ensure that women and girls are an integral part of the design in terms of consultation of activities, older persons and persons with a disability (PWD) part of the design in terms of consultation of activities.

The marginal decrease in the prevalence of food insecurity by nearly 6 percent from 75 percent in August 2019 is the result of slight improvement in access to food from the main harvest in october and november while modest improvements in access to markets and livelihoods have also helped to sustain the current level of food security, Malnutrition rates estimates at serious levels in the post -harvest season since 2017, however the increase of one percentage point from 2018 assessmet to December 2019 is not significant, Several aggravating factors,explain the high malnutrition rates and these include high morbidity rates in almost all states, compounded by poor quality of diet with only 4 percent of children achieving the required MAD (minimum Acceptable diet) and low WDD, the other aggravating factor is flooding that contributed to internal displacement in Tonj North, East and South , disrupting households and water.Among the displaced majority of the vulnerable population are children and mothers who are acutely malnourished. For example; out of 50 children fewer than five screened 14 were moderately malnourished and 26 mothers acutely malnourished out of a 100 population. Loss of follow-up for the beneficiaries in the programme due to conflict, most was displaced to far locations from their sites. Inadequate dietary intake due to lack of food hence increasing the risk of malnutrition.
