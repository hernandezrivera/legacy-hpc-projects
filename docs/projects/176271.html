Project Summary


The nutrition project will be implemented in Kapoeta South, Terekeka and Aweil South for a period of twelve months. MaCDA will seek to boost OTP & TSFP services to treat malnutrition in both children 6-59 months and PLWs (Pregnant & Lactating Women). Cases shall be identified through community screenings & family MUAC approach at the household level. Additionally, the MIYCN package shall be implemented through the community component by establishing 20 MtMSGs in Terekeka, Kapoeta South & Aweil South Counties. This package shall support establishing of kitchen gardens and aid cooking demonstrations at community & facility level to tackle dietary diversity & adequacy. MaCDA has been in partnership with WFP implementing TSFP, BSFP and IFP in Kapoeta South County since 2016 and has been addressing and preventing MAM among children 6-59months, PLWs and improving the nutritional status of people living with HIV/AIDs, TB and Kalazaar. 1 - Needs

About 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 based on same season historical data of food security and nutrition monitoring system, SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020. Based on the FSNMS round 26 and the consequent IPC analysis in December 2020; in the projection period from November 2020 to March 2021, Aweil South was expected to be in AMN Phase 4 (Critical), while Kapoeta South & Terekeka were expected to be in AMN Phase 3 (Serious) & AMN Phase 2 (Alert) respectively. The April to July projections placed Aweil South in the same classification of AMN Phase 4 (Critical) with deterioration of the situation seen in Kapoeta South where the area was classified as AMN Phase 4 (Critical) while Terekeka remained in AMN Phase 2 (Alert) classification. However, the proxy GAM rates for the three counties are 23.40%, 15.20% & 22.00% for Kapoeta South, Aweil South & Terekeka Counties respectively. The SAM & MAM Burdens for the three counties of Terekeka, Kapoeta South & Aweil South are 16,989, 9,182 &12,786 respectively.The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases, poor quality and diversity of food. Elevated level of food insecurity (IPC AFI phase 3 and above) in most counties, poor access to health and nutrition services due to heightened, sub-national conflict, COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates, as well as changes in SAM and MAM referral protocol for children have exacerbated access to services. These locations urgently need humanitarian assistance to curb malnutrition and it's associated short term and long term effects. This requires both Nutrition Specific & Nutrition Sensitive intervention packages to holistically address the malnutrition burdens.

2 - Project activities

MaCDA shall form 20 MtMSGs each in Terekeka, Aweil South & Kapoeta South counties. Each group shall consist of PLWs, two adolescents and one man. The MtMSG’s main aim is to promote behavior change through biweekly meetings of not more than two hours per session where the members discuss the recommended MIYCN practices and good health seeking behaviors. Meeting venues shall be rotational i.e. household to household in a bid to promote practice of what was discussed by all members. Through the MtMSGs, pregnant mothers shall be encouraged to attend ANCs as part of WHO’s recommendations for optimum outcomes. The MtMSG shall be supported with a seed capital to form VSLA which they can use to better their livelihoods through the little savings they’ll be making. There shall also be a training for FES (Fuel Economy Stoves) to promote a green planet; an initiative they can use to make money as they sell them to the community. The MtMSGs, the CNVs & Nutrition Assistants shall utilize the vegetables grown in the health facility kitchen gardens to conduct cooking demonstrations to primary caretakers of children in OTP. The caretakers shall be taught on good cooking methods for food preparation techniques for nutrient conservation. This demonstration is expected to help boost the community’s dietary diversity & adequacy. MaCDA shall also support the CHD with logistical & technical support during VASD Campaigns through provision of trainers for training, recruitment of Vitamin A and Dewormers administrators and their payment. MaCDA shall advocate for all children who missed immunization to be brought with their immunization cards for while airing out the key messages for this very activity. Social mobilizations shall be done by the MtMSGs members and CNVs at household level and at the health facility. The CNVs & MtMSGs shall work together to ensure every child is reached during community & active case findings to ensure maximum coverage of all locations. Each CNV shall be allocated 150 households to screen and he/she shall be directly responsible for monitoring the nutrition situation in those allocated households. Additionally, mothers and caretakers shall be trained on Family MUAC & encouraged to routinely measure their children for early detection and referral before conditions worsen. Children 6-59months with a MUAC<11.5cm without medical complications shall be admitted in OTP while those with medical complications shall be referred to the SC to receive appropriate care. Children 6-59months with a MUAC ≥ 11.5 cm and <12.5 cm shall be admitted into TSFP program. Meanwhile, Pregnant & Lactating Mothers with a MUAC < 23.0 cm shall be admitted in TSFP for treatment. All admitted cases shall be monitored and provided with care for as long as it is needed and only discharged based on the right criteria. Rapid RDT tests shall be conducted to children with SAM before admission to rule out any presence of malaria. Children found with malaria shall be referred for treatment while they concurrently continue with the nutrition program. MaCDA shall liaise with Aweil South, Kapoeta South and Terekeka CHDs to acquire some nets which shall be given to mothers in OTP & Pregnant women to protect them from malaria. The mothers shall be routinely advised and urged to correctly & appropriately use the mosquito nets for protection from malaria. MAM or SAM cases found with complications such as hypothermia, diarrhea, shock, convulsion will be referred to the SC. In absence of SC, the case will be referred to the existing health facilities. MaCDA shall set up hand washing facilities at key points i.e. entrance to the facility & close to the latrines for use by the community. Every two months, MaCDA will distribute Treatment (food) Rations at health facilities. The activity will be administered by Nutrition assistants, CNVs and Community mobilisers. It is intended to treat both children 6-59months and PLWs registered in the program.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

MaCDA staff work on principles of humanity, human rights, respect and do no harm. These principles are articulated in MaCDA’s PSEA Policy and the Code of Conduct. All forms of exploitation, abuse or harassment (including sexual, physical, and verbal) by MaCDA staff or representatives are prohibited. MaCDA will provide information about its PSEA policy during inception training with community leaders and local authorities, during the training of Accountability to affected population and project enumerators, during meetings with community members, leaders and beneficiaries and by use of brochures distributed in the community and banners which will be prominently displayed during meetings, trainings and at all project sites. MaCDA is a protection as well as GBV partner and is a member of referral pathway in the project locations. As such, MaCDA will also provide information and contact details of existing referral pathway at respective project locations to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to enable them seek assistance of need on need to need basis. The Gender & Protection Officer's main role is to act as the PSEA Focal Point for the project. The Officer is to ensure that staff are trained on PSEA. Posters & visual aids (IEC) materials shall be pinned at the Nutrition sites for use. The Annual 16 Days of Activism shall be used as an opportunity to pass out PSEA messages to the community through role plays, dramas & songs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

To ensure feedback from people with disability, MaCDA has tailored its feedback mechanism to allow for personalized approach when collecting feedback from disabled persons. This include home visits, specially organized meetings with disabled persons and by contacting either the caregivers who may be family members or friends to gather information and views about the kind of assistance provided to people under their care. To ensure feedback from women and girls, MaCDA organizes small and separate focus group discussions with women and girls in order to obtain feedback on type, timeliness and quality of assistance provided. Focus Group Discussion are often facilitated by females in order to give confidence to women and girls to come forward with their feedback. In order to allow feedback from illiterate members of the community, MaCDA has pictorials of its Feedback Mechanism showing step by step approaches in submitting Feedback. MaCDA also trains locally hired staff on the Feedback Mechanism who can be able to communicate using local dialect to their communities while receiving feedback.Due to the numerous years we have been implementing all the four components of CMAM in other locations in South Sudan, we have accumulated and learned from experience so that the support we give to the beneficiaries improves their lives over time. We as an organization know that resources for project implementation can never be enough and not everyone can be served. It is therefore due to this that we try to efficiently utilize the limited available resources to ensure that the beneficiaries get the most out of it by targeting the most vulnerable. The organization shall liaise with other IPs in the area in order to combine our knowledge and resources to provide a holistic package to the beneficiaries. We shall provide support that helps the community recover and makes them resilient. This is why the project designed activities around preventive packages such as MIYCN, VSLA, Kitchen gardens etc. These help to address the needs and strengthen the community in terms of knowledge, attitude, practice & livelihood aspects.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In this project, MaCDA through its Gender & Protection Officer will conduct regular sensitization and awareness-raising on the feedback mechanism. During sensitization and awareness raising on Feedback mechanism, MaCDA’s Gender & Protection Officer will provide Information about MaCDA ( who we are, what activities we are doing in that community, and the behaviour they can expect from our project team); Contact details for the MaCDA head office, why feedback is important for MaCDA; Who can provide feedback and report concerns; What type of feedback and concerns can be reported using the Feedback Mechanism, the principle of non-retaliation, privacy and impartiality. The Gender & Protection Officer will also provide specific details on how various Feedback Mechanisms such as feedback box will be checked, frequency of community meetings or women, child and disabled friendly feedback discussions will take place; What happens when someone has given feedback (Privacy of information received, how the information will be stored, who Is authorized to receive the information, an estimation of how long feedback will be provided and avenues MaCDA will use to provide feedback).During admission into the nutrition program and on distribution days, beneficiaries shall be informed of their entitlement in terms of what they are getting, duration, quantity & usage. The MtMSGs are key in disseminating messages to their fellow women and community members; it is such women who shall be actively used. We owe it to the community we serve to ensure the people we employ to serve them are professionals with enough skills and experience so as to provide the best services possible.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

MacDA humanitarian assistance adheres to principles of safety, access, dignity, empowerment, and inclusion. In order to meet the expectations of donors and affected population, MaCDA has a robust, proactive, women and child friendly feedback mechanism embedded in all of its projects. MaCDA feedback mechanism is meant to serve four purposes including: improving planning, implementation and evaluation of activities; is a means for the affected populations (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to hold MaCDA accountable; to make adjustments and adaptation to activities in order to meet the specific needs of the beneficiaries and to ensure accountability between MaCDA and its donors. MaCDA feedback mechanism is used throughout project implementation. MaCDA has a Gender & Protection Officer hired for each project whose role is to ensure functionality of the Feedback Mechanism. All complaints or feedback are received through several complaints channels such as a free toll number, feedback and complaint desks, and suggestion boxes and are maintained and responded to by the Gender & Protection Officer. To ensure increased participation of affected population in addressing complaints arising during implementation, MaCDA will train a minimum of 15 individuals (8 Females) from the host community and affected persons on procedures and avenues they can use to submit and receive complaints. Including local community in the feedback mechanism is meant to increase the level of reporting any complaints that may arise during implementation process. All complaints or feedback received are processed appropriately, timely and fairly while ensuring utmost confidentiality, privacy and safety of complainants and those affected. A log is used to feed and track all complaints and is securely stored in soft and hard copies. Soft copies are password protected while all hard copies are kept in a secure folder and a lockable cabinet. Feedback Register is reviewed on weekly basis to ensure any open complaints or feedback are being addressed, processed and closed appropriately and the log is being kept updated. Key learnings arising from received complaints or feedback are extracted monthly and shared with Project Team for wider organizational learning. Beneficiaries and communities, especially the most marginalized and vulnerable are periodically consulted to ensure they are satisfied the complaint and feedback mechanism is accessible, effective, confidential and safe.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Children and women are the most vulnerable people in the community. They face a lot of protection risks and concerns. MaCDA shall recruit a Gender & Protection Officer to carryout child protection and GBV risk mitigations at the Nutrition sites. The Officer shall also ensure that GBV & Child Protection messages are developed and awareness created during distributions days, campaigns, cooking demonstrations. So as to protect beneficiaries from harsh weather, a waiting shade shall be constructed and distributions shall be done early so that the women don’t face any risks due to traveling late from distribution sites. A complaint and feedback mechanism shall be established with a complaint desk where issues are raised and handled. A clear referral pathway for more sensitive cases of GBV & Child protection shall be established to ensure survivors get justice. Child stimulation & ECD go hand in hand with play. However, children face a lot of infection risks due to crawling, playing and mouthing which leads to diarrhea- one of the leading causes of death among children under five globally. More than one in ten child deaths with about 800 000 each year. 88% of diarrhea-associated deaths are attributable to unsafe water, inadequate sanitation, and insufficient hygiene. Therefore, this project shall establish a simple Household Play Space; an area which puts a barrier between the infant & infection. Household Play Spaces have been proven to reduce faecal contamination and exposure to children. The WHO deems a Household Play Space a ‘critical’ intervention component which might help reduce infection from contaminated floors and objects that become contaminated from faeces and was a very strong recommendation from the Lancet Series. Research shows associations between animal proximity, early infant infection and undernutrition. The project shall engage the MtMSGs to locally create child toys for the various nutrition sites. These toys shall later be bought by the organization as a means of boosting the income of these mothers.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Kitchen gardens are easy to establish and maintain. They provide the household with quick vitamin sources which in turn boosts immunity thus increased productivity. Due to the above, kitchen gardens shall be established at each of the nutrition sites for demonstration purposes. The seeds and implements shall be acquired either directly from FAO or the FSL IP in the area. Through the direct supervision of the Nutrition Assistants, the MtMSGs shall establish, till and look after the gardens at the facilities. Upon vegetable maturity, the harvests shall be given to the OTP Cured cases and some used to conduct cooking demonstrations at the facilities. Sack mount gardening shall also be introduced first at the facility for demonstration then later scaled at a household level.
