Project Summary

The conflict, which has been going on in the east of Ukraine for more than 5 years, negatively affects the socio-political and economic situation of the country, has a negative impact on community security and social cohesion. People living along the demarcation line are the most vulnerable to overcoming the main consequences of the conflict. The situation at NGCA in Donetsk region remains unstable. The periods of relative ceasefire alternate with the aggravation of the situation, which leads to further damage to civil and social infrastructure. Every day people are faced with a violation of human rights in the field of protection, and especially the right to life.
Residents of remote locations live in difficult conditions because they do not have access to assistance in the repair of their homes, access to healthcare and education, and also experience additional economic difficulties due to loss of work due to the closure of enterprises, and the inability to cultivate land due to landmine pollution , isolation due to the suspension of pensions and social benefits and high inflation. This leads to a subsequent reduction in the cost of food, medicine and heating. A significant proportion of people in these settlements (about 30%) are over 60 years old, and the majority are women. Also, a large number of households with the most vulnerable members (including 26% of households with at least one disabled member) increase the burden on social service providers. According to statistics, the vast majority of households with disabled members in the NGCA do not receive any care services (95%).
The lack of infrastructure and access to public transport for people limited the possibility of freedom of movement, and made it difficult to purchase food, drugs, and essential goods. As a result, the degree of protection at the individual and community levels is insufficient to counter the risks posed by constantly emerging new threats and a significant part of people at the NGCA are forced to rely on the support of humanitarian organizations.
Also limited is not safe freedom of movement through the demarcation line. Huge lines in which people are forced to stand in the midst of a mined area, sometimes for days, in any weather, adversely affect the psycho-emotional and physical health of citizens. First of all, at risk are pensioners who regularly travel to the territory controlled by Ukraine to receive pensions. According to UNHCR, an average of 25 thousand people cross the contact line daily
The aim of the project is to promote the protection and promotion of human rights, develop and achieve long-term solutions for people living at the NGCA, increase awareness, activate community members and implement developmental initiatives to increase cohesion, strengthen the ability to cre-ate and equip public spaces. The project aims to provide direct support to the most vulnerable dis-placed and affected by the conflict, returnees, host families and communities, as well as to enhance the capacity of civil society.

1 - Needs

In accordance with the Humanitarian Response Plan for 2019, 2.2 million people living at the NGCA need various types of humanitarian assistance. All these people to one degree or another felt the consequences of a protracted military conflict. Along with basic needs such as food, access to water and medical services, security and protection are a key need for such citizens. The freedom of movement of millions of people in eastern Ukraine is severely limited. Civilians who need to cross the “demarcation line” to gain access to pensions, hospitals, shops, social services, or just to visit friends or relatives, are forced to spend several hours in long lines at the checkpoint, and sometimes stay there overnight, a very unstable environment under the threat of shelling and with extremely high levels of mine pollution. The ongoing hostilities and mine pollution led to the isolation of thousands of people living near the “demarcation line”. In many villages along the demarcation line, men, women and children who have been isolated are experiencing enormous difficulties in accessing basic services: receiving medical care and education, using transport, and electricity and gas. Often these services are intermittent, and sometimes completely unavailable. The conflict in eastern Ukraine has a special impact on older people. Of 2.2 million citizens who need help, 30% are people over 60 years old. These people face serious difficulties in accessing basic services due to their disability and limited mobility, and they are often separated from their families. They are also more vulnerable to abuse and lack of custody, have special medical and nutritional needs, and are most economically vulnerable. The reduction and lack of livelihoods and income is another cause of concern for people affected by the conflict, as they often have to make an impossible choice between buying groceries, paying for medical services and sending their children to school. Due to insufficient or lack of income, people's stocks are completely depleted, so families are forced to resort to negative survival practices, such as selling the things they need or cutting significant, but necessary expenses, for example, in this way, by joint humanitarian actions, primarily vital assistance and protection, it is necessary to provide those who needed it most.

2 - Activities & outcomes

As a result of the project activity, it is planned to implement the following activities and achieve the following results:1. Monitoring the situation in the field of protection through monitoring visits and interviewing people in the framework of monitoring in the field of pro-tection2. Providing primary legal assistance.3. Individual non-monetary assistance to the elder-ly in the format of organizing the work of a free minibus, designed for 8-10 passenger seats on the route: Donetsk - KVPP “Mayorsk” - Bakhmut-KVPP “Mayorsk” - Donetsk. Flight frequency - once a week in both directions.4. Individual targeted non-monetary assistance (priority - persons with special needs)5. Dissemination of information about mine danger through billboards, information stands, leaflets and booklets6. Dissemination of information through infor-mation sessions in order to support an open dia-logue on the prevention of GBV, the empower-ment of persons affected by gender-based vio-lence.7. Individual socio-psychological assistance, includ-ing social support and the provision of transport services to specialized social and medical institu-tions for persons who cannot access these services on their own.8. Support for peaceful coexistence and protection at the community level through the implementa-tion of projects aimed at strengthening peace and social cohesion with the active involvement of community members in order to increase commu-nity sustainability. Community mobilization efforts will ensure the full participation of women, men, girls and boys of all ages and backgrounds.9. Building capacity in communities affected by the conflict in order to minimize risks and empower communities, create conditions conducive to re-specting human rights, humanitarian principles and standards.Through the implementation of project activities, it is planned to increase the economic, physical and psychological well-being of both individuals and their families and communities, reduce their long-term vulnerability and work out long-term solu-tions. Targeting the most vulnerable people in hard-to-reach communities affected by the conflict will have a major impact in terms of reducing nega-tive survival mechanisms and supporting benefi-ciaries.Planned results of the project activity:- The most vulnerable people affected by the con-flict, in hard-to-reach areas, gained access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, reduced their long-term vulnerability and increased resilience;- IDPs and returnees are given equal access and the right to an adequate standard of living without any discrimination, access to personal and other docu-ments and freedom of movement, promotion of the adoption of policies and practices of long-term decisions in accordance with international stand-ards for the most successful integration into host communities -states;- conditions have been created for developing ca-pacity by involving communities in meaningful and substantial participation in all aspects of programs and strengthening their ability to respond at the community level to emerging protection risks;- The level of awareness and support of the hu-manitarian community has been increased in order to increase operational capacity and ensure unhin-dered humanitarian access;-the necessary assistance was provided in resolving the issue of restoring critical infrastructure with the direct involvement of local communities at all stages of the program.

3 - Indicators & targets

As a result of project activities:1. 200 monitoring visits were conducted, 1,250 beneficiaries (750 women, 500 men) will be cov-ered through interviews as part of monitoring the state of human rights and protection2. 1,500 beneficiaries (900 women, 600 male em-ployees) primary legal assistance3. 800 beneficiaries (65+) (480 women, 320 men) will be provided with the opportunity to freely cross the demarcation line4. 300 beneficiaries (180 women, 120 men) with special needs will receive targeted non-monetary assistance5. 8,000 beneficiaries (4,800 women, 3,200 men) will increase awareness through the dissemination of information on mine risk;6. 400 beneficiaries (240 women, 60 men) (20 ses-sions) will increase awareness as a result of dissem-inating information through information sessions on GBV issues.7. 500 beneficiaries (300 women, 200 male employ-ees) living in the immediate vicinity of the contact line will receive individual psychosocial support, including social support;8. 30 social institutions (15,000 people (9,000 wom-en, 6,000 men) served by targeted social institu-tions and benefited) carried out small and medium-sized repairs and facilitated peaceful coexistence between IDPs and host communities, a platform form was created for the most successful integra-tion into host communities9. Support will be provided to 20 communities (20 sessions, 500 beneficiaries (300 women, 200 men) in building capacities to address risks and empower communities, creating conditions conducive to re-specting human rights, humanitarian principles and standards.The number of direct project beneficiaries will be 28250 people.During the implementation of project activities, all activities will be carried out in compliance with basic humanitarian quality standards and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decisions at all stages of the programme: assessment, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and review. For this purpose, communicative approaches will be used, involving transparent and constant two-way feedback, through which information on possible risks, needs and opportunities of communities will be collected.The implementation of mine awareness activities will be coordinated with the mine action sub-Cluster to ensure compliance with international mine action standards. The team of CF «HADC» has experience in implementing such projects. In December 2018, a project was implemented in partnership and under the patronage of the Danish refugee Council - the Danish demining group in Ukraine, "Mine awareness aimed at young people who are most threatened by landmines and other explosive remnants of war on the territory of Ukraine". 80 sessions were held in 20 secondary schools of Gorlovka. The total number of informed children from 6 to 17 years was 2,049. The project was implemented thanks to the financial support of the UN emergency children's Fund UNICEF.At the moment, our organization is at the stage of signing a new contract with the organization DRC to continue the implementation of this project. Financial support for the project will be provided by ECO. NGCA accreditation for the project has already been obtained.
