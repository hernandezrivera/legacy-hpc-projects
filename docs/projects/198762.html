Project Summary

UNICEF planned nutrition response aims at scaling up critical lifesaving interventions for treatment of severe forms of wasting and prevention of under nutrition in North West and South West Regions. The planned response is in tandem with the nutrition cluster HRP 2022 strategy and response framework.
UNICEF will support the screening of children under-5 for acute malnutrition and appropriate treatment. UNICEF will advocate and explore adoption of simplified approaches to management of acute malnutrition to improve coverage to care for children suffering from wasting. Family MUAC/Mother MUAC approach will be scaled up to enhance early identification of SAM . Preventative actions that will include promotion of infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E), deworming prophylaxis and micronutrient supplementation -Vitamin A supplementation (VAS) and iron folate supplementation with the aim of addressing micronutrient deficiencies. For 2021, UNICEF will also focus on ensuring equitable access of iron/folic acid supplementation and deworming prophylaxis by pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers - with special attention to pregnant adolescent girls.
To reinforce partners and the community’s capacity to deliver basic nutrition services to affected communities, UNICEF will support training of additional Trainers of Trainers(ToT) at regional level who will later cascade the training at the health district levels. Furthermore, alternative approaches of capacity development to complement the conventional classroom training will be explored and tested e.g use of short videos, On Job Training. To inform evidence-based programming, the nutrition information system will be strengthened. UNICEF will strengthen the supply chain management with focus on prepositioning of nutrition inputs (RUTF,F75/F100), IEC Materials, drugs and anthropometric equipment at health districts level to ensure continuity of services in the event of movement restrictions. Furthermore, UNICEF will continue strengthening nutrition cluster coordination and enhance multisectoral collaboration with other nutrition sensitive clusters (Health, Food Security, WASH and protection) to maximize optimal nutritional outcomes.
UNICEF nutrition response will apply the principle of “do no harm” and mainstream aspects related to gender, protection centrality, age, gender, disability and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP). Nutrition trainings will incorporate sessions these cross-cutting issues. Interventions will be also be adapted in the context of the COVID-19pandemic and enhanced with integrated Communication for Development (C4D)capacities.
UNICEF will partner with organizations that have a better level of operational capacity, access to and acceptance by populations in need. Intervention will use of static health structures, mobile clinics, rapid response mechanism (RRM) and comprehensive child response (CCR) approaches to access the affected population both in accessible and those in hard to reach remote areas.
The target groups will include boys and girls under-five for screening, referral, children with SAM, Vitamin A supplementation and deworming prophylaxis. Adolescent, pregnant and breastfeeding will be targeted for the iron and folic acid and deworming prophylaxis. Health workers will be targeted for trainings on SAM management (mobilize, screen and refer acutely malnourished), information Management (DHIS II) and IYCF-E.

1 - Besoins / Needs

It is estimated that in 2023, more than 230,700 people including children under five and pregnant and lactating women will need nutrition services in the North-West, South-West, West and Littoral regions. Up to 48,800 boys and girls under 5 years are likely to develop acute malnutrition in these regions including 11,600 with severe acute malnutrition. Populations living in remote areas, IDPs in urban/peri-urban areas, and households/communities hosting IDPs are vulnerable to malnutrition since they are exposed to aggravating factors (food insecurity, limited access to water, poor sanitary conditions, sanitation and hygiene services, and morbidity due to overstretching of basic social services). The nutrition situation in the North-West and South-West regions remains unclear due to the absence of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey using the SMART methodology. Proxy data from rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1 per cent and 7.1 per cent in the North-West and South-West regions respectively. Analysis from Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data conducted by nutrition partners from July to December 2021, showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 1.5 and SAM prevalence of 0.4 per cent in the North-West and the South-West regions. However, these findings are not conclusive of the nutrition status in the two regions as there could be pockets of high nutrition vulnerability that are not yet identified. Prior to the crisis, the North-West and South-West regions presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with high rates of stunting of 36 per cent and 28 per cent in the North-West and the South-West respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on infant and young child feeding practices were reported with 42.5 per cent for the North-West and 58.3 per cent for South-West, of exclusive breast-feeding rates. Proportions of children 6 to 23 months who met the Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) was reported to be low at 31.8 per cent in the North-West and 20.7 per cent in the South-West. Approximately 40 per cent (44.3 per cent in the North-West and 41.9 per cent in the South-West) of children aged 6 to 59 months were reported to have anemia and with the ongoing humanitarian crisis, it is very likely that the poor situation of MDD has worsened. Poor host households and displaced populations will continue to face acute food insecurity as a result of the crisis. According to the results of the Cadre Harmonisé analysis of March 2022, 612,453 people in the North-West and 365,272 in the South-West (25.9 per cent and 25.7 per cent of the population respectively) are in acute food insecurity (phases 3 to 5). Access to health care remains a challenge due to insecurity and persistent attacks on health facilities. While around 71 per cent of the facilities are found to be functional, the majority (58 per cent) of IDPs have limited access to health care due to distance (45 per cent) and financial constraints (17 per cent). Furthermore, the weak disease surveillance system has put the population at risk of epidemics with late detection and limited response capacity resulting in repeated pockets of cholera outbreaks reported in the South-West. Acute malnutrition of children aged 6 to 59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health and WASH situations. Despite low programme coverage in 2022, over 1,300 children 6-59 months were admitted for SAM treatment in the North-West and South-West regions.With the major drivers of malnutrition (food insecurity, morbidity, poor sanitary conditions and repeated internal displacement) expected to follow the same trend in 2023, the nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate.
