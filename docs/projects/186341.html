Project Summary

• This project aims to promote and improve psychosocial support & well-being for children affected by the conflict through appropriate education services. The component will integrate gender issues and sensitization to gender-based violence, through teacher training, promotion of equal and safe education for girls and boys from IDP and host communities. To achieve this, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Cameroon, adopting a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The project results will be achieved through activity packages aimed at strengthening the capacity of education actors at the community level and ensuring the protection and development (cognitive, physical, emotional and psychological wellbeing) of children and adolescents, boys and girls. The activity packages include, learning and teaching kits distribution, teacher training on psycho-social support and NRC better learning program, revamping school governance structures, set of recreational, breathing, relaxing and calming exercice activities with children and care givers. Additionally, awareness and sensitization activities will be carry on within targeted communities for protection of education from attacks and safe school declaration per on the South West and North West security context. NRC will also help to meet school infrastructure needs by supporting MoE in construction of clasrooms and rehabilitation of existing structures, ensuring meaningful access and equal participation of people with disabilities and special needs. Theses classrooms will be equipped with classroom furniture (including desks with modesty screens) and blackboards to enable their use. To make these schools safer, sensitive to specific needs and inclusive, there will be a particular focus on wash facilities in school (gender sensitive latrines, access to water…), ensuring access for people with disabilities;
However, At the beginning of the project a baseline will be set up to have measurable and qualitative data to be analyze at the end of the action. This aim to have a better understanding of the impact of the project within the targeted community. In addition, NRC M&E staff will carry on outcome and output monitoring field activities according to the project period. They will also set up feedback and complain mechanism in targeted communities to ensure our accountability to donors and beneficiaries as well and to ensure the compliance of our action with NRC program and operation policies.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Access to education opportunities: Cameroon is under a lot of pressure due to the devastating effects of three ongoing humanitarian crises. The mutant and rampant insecurity in the Lake Chad Basin region affecting Far North region of Cameroon, the socio-political instability in the Central African Republic that maintains refugees in Cameroon, and the violent conflict in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon. Violence, attacks and threats against education, population displacement, and limited capacity of the school system and of communities to absorb extra student populations has placed almost 1.9 million school aged children in need of some form of assistance in education in Cameroon.The crisis in the North-West and South-West has had a major impact on the Education Sector, leaving around 700,000 children out of school in late 2020. Children forced to flee to bush areas have been left without access to any form of education. Need in school infrastructure: More than 4,482 schools (80%) in NW/SW Cameroon have been forced to Close. The 20% remaining are mostly found in urban area were classroom are overcrowded due to lack of classrooms and teachers. The barriers to access to education and reason for increasing out of school children, primarily, include (a) the presence of armed groups and military personal with security threat, (b) closure of or non-functional schools and learning spaces mostly in rural area (c) lack of teachers due to insecurity, and (d) paying school fees . Need of Psychological support for affected children : An estimated 146,700 school-aged children have been internally displaced and an additional 60,000 have fled to neighbouring regions or countries. Out-of-school adolescents and youths are at particular risk of being recruited into armed groups, facing harassment or arbitrary arrest by military forces or adopting negative coping behaviours, including early marriage, commercial sexual exploitation or child labour. Nearly all children in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon have been exposed to traumatic incidents such as witnessing violence from military and/or NSAGs, destruction of homes and villages, torture and killings, and mass displacement. More than 90% of children are now out of school and are therefore completely exposed to a variety of severe crisis-related protection risks including sexual exploitation and abuse, gender-based violence, harassment and recruitment by armed forces or NSAGs, arbitrary arrest, early marriage, pregnancy and child labour. Children require urgent support to manage their emotions, understand the normal reactions they are having to an abnormal situation, and improve their psychosocial well-being through play-based learning and positive social interactions with peers and adult role models. A sense of normalcy and structure needs to be established before children will be emotionally and socially ready to re-start their learning. In addition, it’s also important to strengthen linkages with GBV actors by increasing teacher’s capacity on safe and ethical referral.
