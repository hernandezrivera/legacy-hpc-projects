Project Summary

This project aims to address the additional needs of the IDPs who live in camps and camp-like settings, and will also target the host community through the out-of-camp approach in order to address issues around preparedness, prevention and response on COVID-19 in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY states). It’s an addendum to the 2020 HRP and only highlights the humanitarian component of the preparedness and response that complements the efforts the Government of Nigeria in the BAY states. It seeks to fill the gaps of CCCM Sector in line with the centrality of protection strategy and will target 120,000 women, men, boys and girls IDPs living in the camps, camp-like settings, IDPs out of camps and host community in the context of the COVID-19. The project will cover 4 LGAs in Borno (Ngala, Munguno, Banki and Damasak), 4 LGAs in Adamawa (Yola South, Yola North, Mubi and Madagali) and 2 LGAs in Yobe state (Damaturu and Gujba). The breakdown of the target for this CCCM project is 97,298 individuals (19,460HH) for IDPs and 22,702 individuals (4,540 HH) for returnees distributed as follow: 46% (male), 54% (female), 60% (children), 36% (adult) and 4% (elders). People affected by humanitarian crises, particularly those displaced and living in camps and camp-like settings, are often faced with specific challenges and vulnerabilities and CCCM will take this into consideration when planning for readiness and response operations for the COVID-19 outbreak. UNHCR recognizes that they are frequently neglected, stigmatized, and may face difficulties in accessing health and other critical services that are otherwise available to the general population, hence they will be consulted and will form part of the COVID-19 planning process by assessing needs of the women, men, boys, girls, adolescents and older displaced persons in a participatory manner and based on age, gender and diversity mainstreaming, supporting the coordination of response of service providers, capacitating the communities through their effective participation and ownership of activities and developing the resilience and self-reliance when they are still in displacement. Although all IDPs are categorized as a most affected population group, there are more significant vulnerabilities even among the IDPs such as the elderly, women, and girls with specific needs, children and persons with disabilities, and those with underlying illnesses. It is also important to highlight that IDPs living in host communities and host communities without CCCM site facilitation or out of camp are equally under the category of the most affected population group due to limited COVID-19 preventive and response measures. Furthermore, a higher percentage of IDPs are living in heavily congested camps and deplorable conditions. Many are sharing the shelters with other households, while others live in damaged makeshift shelters, and others live in the open without any shelter and core relief items. UNHCR through this submission will continue with its decongestion plan that will help address issues related with social distancing and dignified living standards.
CCCM remains a key sector that stays at the community level daily monitoring of the protection and humanitarian assistance by all service providers in the camp. CCCM in its coordination role at the camp level will work closely with Health, WASH and FSS sectors in COVID-19 preparedness, prevention and response. CCCM is also involved in Point of Entry monitoring and screening, monitoring of COVID-19 related hygiene promotion with guidelines from WHO and WASH sectors, Risk Communication and Community Mobilization, support with food distribution monitoring to ensure adherence to physical distancing and community surveillance.

1 - Needs

There are total of 272 camps in BAY states with Borno having highest number of camps and IDPS. The total population residing in these camps is around 826,724. There are still 126 camps without site facilitation in place. Even for the camps with site facilitation support, the number of site facilitator required for the camp is far below the SPHERE standard making it really challenging to have proper camp coordination and camp management in place. This often leads to huge gaps in supporting beneficiaries thereby leading beneficiaries to suffer heavily. There are around 1 million people in hard-to-reach areas who are of concern to CCCM. Based on past trend, around 40% of people from those areas come to stay in camps which even increases the number of CCCM needs. Additionally, some of the refugee returnees coming from Niger, Chad and Cameroon also come to stay in camp as a secondary displaced people as they can’t go back to their area of origin. There is need of proper reception centre in different places as new arrivals needs to be received and taken care of before being sent to camp. Return areas like Damasak and Banki still do not have proper reception centre in place whereas the existing ones are also not able to cater to the needs of new arrivals. A good number of camps and camp like settings have inadequate living conditions with insufficient water supply, poor sanitation, lack of fuel/coal for cooking, overcrowding, no appropriate land for expansion, and self- settlement with make-shift shelters. Conditions do not provide for proper site planning, thus leading to congestion, flooding during rainy seasons and enabling environments for fire outbreak. All these conditions and their aftereffects are favorable grounds for high and risky human to human transmission of COVID. Currently CCCM partners are managing 140 of 247 camps. Only 864,844 IDPs are receiving CCCM site facilitation and the majority of whom live with relatives and friends in the host communities are not reached with CCCM services, while a few are in inaccessible locations. The level of congestion of most of the IDPs camp in Borno is high. This is mainly due to challenge of accessing secured land and inability to provide functional security systems to large camps. This require therefore a more structured site in order to ensure that internal security mechanism and various camp committee can function effectively in a more comprehensive way. The care and maintenance mechanisms interventions in the various camp also require review. The current environment clearly shows that IDPs are leaving various camps to either stay in new surroundings or return to their home of origin. The alarming rate at which new arrivals continue to access safer LGAs and camps/camp-like settings, even in this period of COVID19, has significantly overwhelmed the humanitarian capacity to meet the IDPs basic needs. This result is congestion of camps/sites and a pile of unmet needs registered by most settlement sites, as well as the stretching to breaking point of the national authorities’ capacity to facilitate the CCCM component. As such, duplication of services, insufficient advocacy for services, lack of proper coordination among implementing and operating partners to provide appropriate and timely assistance, and lack of quality information to help in joint planning of beneficiaries’ services was registered. Additionally to this, it has been detected a poor site planning and high congestion of beneficiaries in the camp sites, untimely response to natural hazard occurrences such as fires, flooding and storms that destroy camp residents property, unmaintained, abandoned, damaged or dilapidated camp structures and infrastructures that need urgent decommissioning (for example filled up latrines, blocked water drainage system or the lack of drainage channels).

2 - Activities

• Establish Community Centers: - UNHCR will establish community centers targeting IDPs who are out of camp which will also include those settling in urban centers at the host community. The centers will act as community mobilization centers where they can get information, share information and as well do referrals and get feedback. To complement the information centres will be establishment of mobile monitoring teams through area-based approach. The information centers will be managed by Counsellor for psychosocial support, CCCM Information Center Manager, Protection Officer and information management assistants and will need to be constructed and furnished. Abandoned structures could also be rehabilitated to serve the purpose. It will also be equipped with necessary IEC materials. • Community mobilization, awareness raising and sensitization: - UNHCR will collaborate with Ministry of Information and National Orientation Agency, WHO and UNICEF and partners, and through community volunteers, community leaders and other community networks, religious leaders, sector committees, members and other community health workers to conduct awareness raising and sensitization to the communities. Message dissemination media will include jingles on government and private owned radio and TV stations (where applicable), live radio programmes allowing listeners to call in to ask questions, mobile messaging campaigns, public service announcements, pre-recorded messages and jingles played through communities and villages. Additionally, for the youth, digital platforms such as WhatsApp, Twitter and Facebook continue to promote messages through short videos, drama skits and infographics. Key messages will be disseminated include essential practices such as stigma reduction, physical and social distancing, staying at home and handwashing with soap. New volunteers and community engagement teams will undergo an orientation to ensure they align their activities to COVID-19 guidelines. This program falls under Risk Communication and Community Engagement which is one of the 8 pillars in response towards COVID-19. • Strengthen Camp Management essential activities: - UNHCR will further strengthen its camp management essential activities to reinforce referral mechanisms, especially for health partners, strengthen coordination with community groups, especially WASH and Health committees, and implement COVID19 guidance on activities such as distributions, management of the quarantine/reception centers, protocols on having symptoms, hygiene promotions; and will work closely with the Rapid Response Teams whose responsibility is case management, tracing, isolation and infection prevention control. UNHCR will also support with surveillance and screening at the port of entry. • Decongestion and re-organizing of camps that are overcrowded. With land advocacy ongoing, the sector will support shelter and NFI colleagues to draw site plans, prepare new land for construction, identify beneficiaries for relocation and then carry out the actual re-location of beneficiaries once new camp sites are established. In addition to the above, CCCM partners together with community leaders and members will support IDPs who have been relocated to dismantle their previous shelters, then arrange for camp re-organization of vacated camps. However, this can only happen if land has been acquired and with funds availability. • Management of Reception/Transit Centres: UNHCR will continue to provide management of transit and reception centres in covered camps and settlements in order to help arriving IDP households with normal life, as they transit to their permanent living spaces in the camps/settlements. • UNHCR will support actors in tracking returns that will contribute to the development and implementation of a durable solutions strategy that will ensure that the humanitarian community address the return and the relocation process in more comprehensive and effective way.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to affected populations will be strengthened through the development of a community-based feedback mechanism; supporting partners to the inclusion of functional and accessible complaints and reporting mechanisms, convening frequent meetings with the community leaders, and beneficiaries and focus group discussions. UNHCR is an active member of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and has community-based structures known as Protection Action Groups (PAG) within Maiduguri and LGAs where UNHCR is present such as Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Damaturu, Dikwa, Gudjba, Gwoza, Jere, Gwoza, Madagali, Michika, MMC, Monguno, Ngala, Rann, Yola North, Yola South. Through these structures, UNHCR ensures that issues of concern to the community are brought to the attention of UNHCR and its partners for intervention. UNHCR shall also establish a feedback mechanism through its Protection Desk. In previous projects, some key concerns raised include beneficiary selection, the support provided, community representatives and payment process. All issues reported will be systematically investigated and followed up in a timely manner. Received complaints shall be categorized according to the level of sensitivity and severity and responded in accordance with protection principles.
