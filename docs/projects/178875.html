Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, TGH will contribute to achieving objectives 1, 3, 4 and 5 of the 2021 Protection Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria through providing psychosocial support (PSS) to children, including the identification of cases facing protection risks, their access to specialized services and case-management. The project will also include awareness raising on a high range of protection concerns such as GBV, child protection (CP) and risk education (RE) toward explosive hazards. The proposed intervention will be part of an integrated programme which will consist in providing both CP and Education assistance in public schools and in the communities given that both sectors’ needs are intertwined.
TGH is already implementing CP programs in and out of schools in Hama, Dar’a and Rural Damascus and aims at scaling up its intervention through providing assistance to a total of 5,180 conflict-affected children in Sinjar (Idleb), Karnaz and Ziyara (Hama), Dar’a and Sheikh Miskine (Dar’a), Duma and Nashabiyeh (Rural Damascus). The intervention will provide approximatively 4,900 children with CP in public schools and 280 adolescents in safe spaces referrals and case management at home and protection awareness campaigns among the parents and the community. These sub-districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and TGH field assessments. It is important to note that all locations are rated level 3 and 4 as per Protection Sector’s severity scale.
The proposed intervention will consist in:
- Providing PSS including risk education and recreational activities to children, including parenting programs;
- Conducting community GBV prevention activities;
- Conducting community-based risk education regarding explosive hazards;
- Conducting COVID-19 awareness campaign in and out of schools;
- Providing specialized services through referrals, case management and counselling;
- Building the capacities of facilitators, counsellors and case managers to prevent and respond to specific CP issues;
- Building the capacities of outreach volunteers (ORVs) to conduct data collection, awareness sessions and case identification and management, under the supervision of TGH case workers, in the surrounding communities;
- Establishing and building the capacities of community based volunteers with the objective increasing awareness about child protection risks and ensure sustainability of the project.
TGH will consider and ensure gender-and-age aspects across all sub-sectors of focus – CP, GBV, and general protection. Cultural preferences and sensitivities will be considered through consultations with the staff – the majority of whom will be from local community – and the affected populations. Specific needs of children, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups have been considered.

4 - Needs

TGH’s proposed intervention is built on extensive protection risks identified through TGH field assessment conducted in January 2021 and by 2021 HNO. It has been reported mainly in Hama and Rural Damascus that the conflict has had a profound impact on children children’s well-being and development, and putting them in a state of psychological distress. Boys and girls are subject to violence including intimate partner violence, sexual violence, early marriage, forced marriage, deprivation of freedom of movement and forced pregnancy. Victims are thus more likely to drop out from school than other children because of psychological traumas. It has been reported that around 20% of the children in all targeted areas are either orphans or unaccompanied due to the death or imprisonment of their parents. Child labour is also very common due to the poor economic situation worsened by the Lebanon crisis, COVID-19 and endless inflation in the country. It is preventing school attendance and exposing children to a high range of risks, including injuries, work exploitation and harassment. Children’s access to specialized service providers including specialized medical care, MHPSS, legal service and civil documentation and alternative care services is very limited due to lack of capacity and resources among service providers. TGH team reported that around 75% of the children assessed are still exposed to mine risks as a consequence of the hostilities. In addition of causing serious injuries, explosive hazards also prevent access to properties, basic services and livelihood, thus limiting the scope for recovery and reconstruction efforts.

5 - Implementation of activities

Child protection assistance will be complementary to TGH education programs and will be provided through PSS sessions in schools and safe spaces along with NFE activities five days per week and in the community through community based approach and home visits.. Qualified PSS volunteers will be hired and trained by TGH to ensure the implementation of these activities. Besides, TGH will establish and train community based volunteers in each location who will support TGH team in PSS, identifying the protection risks and , organizing the campaign awareness toward parents and the community. PSS sessions in public schools will be used as an entry point to identify children with protection risks. Formal and non-formal teachers and community based volunteers will be trained to identify child protection cases and will be encouraged to refer the cases to TGH case workers who will be able to take over. Case workers will be able to provide direct support or external referrals such as medical care, MHPSS, legal service and civil documentation and alternative care services, etc. Where decision by mandated government agency is warranted, cases will be referred to the Directorate of Social Affairs and Labour (DoSAL) for appropriate action and legal decisions.TGH will ensure daily monitoring on the project progress to ensure that standards of project implementation and fund resources are being effectively implemented. Participatory approach, including through a complaints and feedback mechanism, will be undertaken to identify needs, implement and evaluate programs to ensure the views of all stakeholders are considered. Discussions will be gender-disaggregated and conducted by women facilitators when necessary.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Protection risk faced by girls and boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services: linking protection intervention with existing services within targeted communities to enhance the system approach; support national actors and community based groups to ensure sustainability of protection intervention; established community based committees to enhance community based protection mechanisms.- Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed groups: training on RE; collaboration with relevant authorities to identify safe locations and ensure their support in the safety of the project implementation.- Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality: ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards; services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules or adequate academic and professional background.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

- Children victim of abuse or violence from TGH staff or from other children. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: application of Child Safeguarding and PSEA measures, transparency of recruitment process; training of all staff and associates who engage with children on PSEA, signature of code of conduct also posted on walls; establishment of effective child-friendly complaint mechanism; awareness sessions on children's rights and GBV; PSS sessions on child protection risks, similarities and differences/accepting others; gender-balanced staffing.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- The security situation puts TGH staff and beneficiaries at risk. Mitigation: daily follow-up of the security situation in the targeted areas in coordination with SARC and activities on hold until the security situation is stabilized. - TGH is not granted the necessary approvals to implement activities in public schools. Mitigation: TGH has a good relationship with MoE and ensure good coordination and communicate in order to get approval on time. In case PSS and recreational activities would not be approved even after negotiation with MoE, TGH will propose mobile unit and reinforce its work at home through the approval of SARC for case management. - Perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner leading to tensions. Mitigation: selection of localities based on the Protection sector severity scale analysis and consultation before starting the project with Community and community based volunteers; coordination with other service providers; prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable; monitoring and feedback during and after service provision; develop schedules, in collaboration with children and caregivers, and with consideration for religious and other activities. - Inflation rate increases. Mitigation: TGH has already included the inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inflation TGH will adjust the incentives and BoQs accordingly in coordination with the donor.- Parents/caregivers discourage their children from attending TGH activities. Mitigation: door-to-door campaigns and communication with support of Community based volunteers towards parents, case management by TGH case workers.
