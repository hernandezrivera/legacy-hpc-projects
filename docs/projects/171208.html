Project Summary

In relevance with 2021 FNO identified needs, FSS Strategic Objectives as well as with Palestinian National Priorities more specifically with the Ministry of Agricultural & Social Development Objectives, this project aims at increasing the food security of the most vulnerable Palestinian community and increasing their steadfastness in their lands, through different intervention including rehabilitation of 449 dunum of agricultural lands, opening 31 km of agricultural roads, rehabilitating 271 animal shelters and finally raising the targeted group in legal issues and sharing experience in lands follow-up.
The project target 37 location in Area C and East Jerusalem, based in 4 Palestinian governorates: East Jerusalem, Hebron, Bethlehem and Ramallah.
In order to provide the most complimentary assistance to these households, UAWC and ACTED will conduct the following activities:
- land rehabilitation and provision of inputs for the most vulnerable 190 farmers and their households;
- animal shelter rehabilitation/winterization and provision of inputs for the most vulnerable 120 herders and their households;
- Rehabilitation of 6 kilometres of agricultural roads, expected to benefit at least 300 households directly; Creation and training of 6 Community-based Protection Committees in target areas, to ensure protection mainstreaming within communities, communications with communities, and accountability to affected populations throughout the project duration.
The project will be implemented in partnership with ACTED for 1 year, with a total budget
of USD 1,300,000

1 - Needs

According to the FSS HNO 2021, and within a dramatic expected increase of food insecurity in Palestinian by 18% in 2021, around 45% of Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza are expected to be food insecure in 2021. This project will target Area C & East Jerusalem, where 189,756 of people are food insecure according to the report above. Many reasons contributed to reaching this high rate of food insecurity in the targeted areas, including but not limited to: 60% of agricultural lands are inaccessible by Palestinians, the Israeli occupation confiscates 82% of the aquifer water, 65% of the agricultural lands are without access to water to the occupation’s control over water, following to other restrictions imposed by COVID 19 such as losses of jobs for both M&F, as well as the inability of the Palestinian government to support vulnerable people due to the current financial crises. According to the recent World Bank statistics, the low rate of Palestinian households is expected to be doubled, reaching around 30% by the end of 2020. Also, the current restriction to have access to Area C lands by Palestinian farmers, and the Area C annexation plan by the Israeli government, if occurred, will compound the suffering of Palestinian people, particularly the women, and make them more and more food insecure, specifically that annexation may include: confiscation of unused & unreachable lands for the interest of Israeli settlement in the Palestinian territories. Besides and as a result of the above dilemma, Palestinian Bedouin and herding communities, where the female have a significant role, are also suffering and facing unprecedented challenges such as forced displacement and their existence in a highly sensitive area, make them more food insecure and vulnerable as animal breeding is their main source of income. In addition to this, the COVID-19 crisis is disproportionately affecting vulnerable groups and deepening pre-existing vulnerabilities. Women, who already had an unequal representation in the workforce and less employment opportunities, are further affected by the crisis. Informal economy jobs have quickly disappeared because of the crisis, leaving numbers of women without a source of income. Due to their traditional child-rearing roles, women have also been expected to quit their jobs to take care of children after school closures. Women also represent a larger share of care roles i.e. nurses, and are thus more exposed to the virus itself, representing almost 54% of confirmed positive cases in oPt. Elderly people are also more affected by the crisis, due to their more critical exposure and death ratio to the virus. The elderly and people with disabilities are further isolated from society due to movement restrictions and closures, which prevent their carers from reaching them and limits their access to markets to cover essential needs.Therefore, we see that opening agricultural roads rehabilitation of lands as well as rehabilitation of animal shelters of the targeted location in Area C and East Jerusalem are urgent interventions that help the targeted communities to get out of food insecurity situation. The needs of the targeted communites for open agrocultrual roads, lands to be rehabilitated as well as shelters for animanl are the common and around similar in all targeted location. Agricultural Roads will help targeted communities to have efficient and safe access to their used and unused lands so that making them more productive and protect their lands from sudden confiscation action by Israeli authorities and settlers.. Land Rehabilitation will also contribute to agricultural productivity. We see that the open roads will reduce the suffering of female and children specifically as they have different roles in following up their land and reaching it. . Besides, protecting the animals, one of the primary source of the Bedouin community will also contribute to their food security level. Based on close consultation with the Directorate of Agricultural and the Food Security Cluster Coordinator, most vulnerable communities in the 37 Area C and Eat Jerusalem locations in Ramallah, Hebron, Bethlehem and Jerusalem governorate are selected for the following interventions: the land rehabilitation, opening agricultural roads, as well as animal shelters, are of high priorities. The main findings of this consultation are as follows: 449 dunums, owned by 225 HH, need urgent land rehabilitation, 31 KM of agricultural roads, serving around 2,790 donums of agricultural lands and 930 Households (an average of 90 dunum and 30 families / KM), are needed urgently to be opened, as well as 271 animal shelters, owned by 271 HH and feeding 1327 individuals, are highly urgent to be rehabilitated. It should be noted that consulted persons and official institutions have already conducted their studies and meeting with targeted 37 communities.

2 - Activities

Preparatory activity:UAWC and ACTED will hold meetings with local authorities of each community to present the project and ensure the local authorities' support. UAWC and ACTED will then conduct information sessions in target communities, to explain the project's aim and activities to community members. Ahead of each selection process and activity implementation, UAWC and ACTED will hold gender- and age-sensitive focus group discussions (with men, women, children, elderly people, and people with disabilities) to ensure differentiated needs are taken into account while designing activities.- Standard Activity: Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for farmers, herders, breeders, and fishers, including the promotion of technical capacities and small scale production units, and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience. - Intervention 1: Preparatory Phase Following the signature of the Grant Agreement, UAWC will contact the main stakeholders, including MoA representatives, FSS Coordinator, the funder, and targeted communities representatives, to notify them about the project plan and to finally sign Contract Agreement with the concerned parties. Once signed, the bidding processes start. - Intervention 2: Land Rehabilitation, 449 dunums, based in 29 locations out of targeted 37 locations in the four targeted governorates, will be rehabilitated. It mainly includes land levelling, building water cisterns & retaining walls, land ploughing, clearing, fencing, and planting them with seeds and seedlings. A priority of selection will be given for the most vulnerable HH, including mainly Female-Headed Families, and women landowners. Also, a priority will be given for Area C. lands that are exposed to the risk of confiscation by Israeli authorities or settlers and those threatened with planned annexation action by the Israeli government. Intervention 3: Opening Agricultural Roads Based on the tendering process, 31.2 KM of agricultural roads will be open in 19 locations out of targeted 37 locations in the four targeted governorates. A priority of selection will be given for lands owned by the most vulnerable HH, including mainly Female-Headed families, and women landowners., and for Area C lands that are exposed to the risk of confiscation by Israeli authorities or settlers and those threatened with planned annexation action by the Israeli government. Intervention 5: Animal Shelter Rehabilitation and distribution of assets, 271 animal shelters will be rehabilitated, and assets will be distributed over 271 HH in 32 locations out of targeted 37 locations in the four targeted governorates. A priority of selection will be given the most vulnerable HH, including mainly Female-Headed families. - Standard Activity: Awareness sessions (advocacy/policy influencing sessions /dialogue / trainings) Intervention 6: Advocacy Sessions 15 advocacy sessions will be delivered for targeted farmers and community in related topics, including mainly how to react with and follow-up the confiscation orders legally. Intervention 7: Sharing Experience Session in Land Following-up 15 sharing experience sessions will be conducted among the beneficiaries from the 37 location to share experience on the lessons learned and best practices of lands following up. Finally, in each cluster of target communities, ACTED will establish CBPCs to ensure solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee better respect of the safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female headed HHs, disabled, elderly). CBPCs will be composed of at least 7 members (including at least 40% of women), and will be trained on topics including protection mainstreaming; agriculture principels and minimum standards, ACTED's Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM). These CBPCs will participate in the selection process for all activities to ensure transparency in the process and community engagement. They will monitor the implementation and act as a liaison between Implementing partners and community members in case of any feedback, complaint or challenge.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. UAWC M&E Office is an essential part of UAWC’s project management structure, providing a mechanism to crosscheck and give recommendations on the work of the project team and partners. UAWC M&E Office leads the assessment of baseline situations of beneficiaries through data collection, monitors ongoing implementation through regular field visits and consultations with beneficiaries of both sexes, and evaluates the outcome of the activities. This methodology ensures the UAWC M&E Office uses an outcome evaluation approach to analyse the effect of the action on target groups. Each monitoring team will have 1 man 1 woman to make sure that women and children feel safe to report their concerns during monitoring activities. Also CBPCs will participate in selection and monitoring as impartial actors to ensure that all concerns (including those against partners) are taken into account and responded to.It should be noted the following components are highly considered while conducting the monitoring actions: Beneficiaries feedback are highly considered, the beneficiaries safety & dignity are respected, active participation of beneficiaries including women in the monitoring processes including the discussion on the project progress, lessons learned & best practices, how to improve the implementation quality so that achieving the project objectives, challenges & obstacle they face and how to overcome it. Etc.. On the other hand, financial monitoring will be also undertaken by all project partners and then consolidated by UAWC, with external audits of the action taking place on an annual basis. UAWC staff will request from ACTED monthly reporting on activities on top of contractual obligations to ensure all indicators are on track and register the beneficiaries’ satisfaction and any difficulties encountered. This monthly reporting will minimise the risk of delays in the activities’ implementation, that any change in the implementation of the project is notified and approved by OCHA and finally that all indicators are on track and if needed corrective measures are taken to reach the target. Finally, it will enable UAWC to provide comprehensive and transparent interim and final reports to OCHA. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

UAWC adopts different mechanisms of stakeholders’ feedback and their participation. Verbal feedback to the project team in the field or over the phone, filling out the Complaint Cards, and through the UAWC field monitoring visits by UAWC MEAL Officer. The feedback processes consider the fully participation of all concerned parties including specifically women dirct beneficires. The feedback is treated seriously and confidentially, and then taking the proper action, either preventive or corrective actions. The feedback may be received by direct beneficiaries, official entities, or from the out-sourced service providers such as contractors, or other stakeholders. By UAWC experience, the complaining parties contact the coordinator of the field team directly, either by face to face while being in the field or over the phone. In this case, the coordinator takes the right action and may inform/consult with the UAWC higher decision-making level if necessary. Even the stakeholders are informed about the possibility to share their feedback via Complain Card - CC or over the official phone of the institution; complaining parties use the CC in a few cases. On a regular monthly basis, and with attendance of the head of the UAWC Operational Head, MEAL Officer and Program Head, UAWC Project Coordinators meet and discuss the project progress and issues including mainly the received complains. The main purpose of this monthly meeting is to share experience on how to react with all project issues and discussing the lessons learned & best practices. Besides, and to ensure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with the project deliverable and team, the MEAL officer conducts desk review of the project reports and field visits to meet with a random sample of direct beneficiaries as well as with official institution representatives. Before each field visit, the MEAL Officer customized the M&E form, considering the type of interventions, provided service/product and direct beneficiaries.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Based on Human Righ Approach (international human rights law and international humanitarian law), and the adopted Code of Conducts principles by UAWC & ACTED, including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, UAWC ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals (including specificallt female abd children) and communities involved. In this project, strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities are taken, particularly concerning designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, UAWC analyzes protection risks and possible consequences that may arise and finally are reported to the right concerned agencies. In the field, UAWC conducts regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are considered throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for vulnerable groups including mainly women, children. UAWC is highly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For that, UAWC staff are made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in UAWC’S Code of Conduct and related to other policies including Child Protection; and others. On the other hand and to avoid negative impacts and maximize the positive impact of the project interventions, UAWC adopts different principles to avoid any attempts of any individuals/groups/political parties to control and use aid resources to support their side. These principles are mainly the transparency, professionalism and considering some cross-cutting issues, mainly gender equality and giving priority for the most vulnerable groups, including small farmers, women-headed families, people with disability. To ensure Do-No-Harm, UAWC involves and consult with different stakeholders in the project design, beneficiary selection criteria, project implementation mechanisms. It should be noted that the UAWC Team is aware of Palestinian context, and interaction between the different intervention and this context, so that avoid negative impacts and maximize positive impacts on the conflict.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Since its establishment, Union of Agricultural Work Committees - UAWC has credible local agricultural committees in all mostly Palestinian locations, through them, the information on assistance are disseminated. To ensure all targeted beneficiaries, specifically the most vulnerable group such as small farmers, women-headed families, and people with disabilities, are fully informed of the assistance available under the project, UAWC field team ensures that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages. In general, UAWC adopts a different way of dissemination about the (Direct beneficiary groups, list of the beneficiary of high priority, and beneficiary selection criteria, etc..). These ways are mainly through UAWC local agricultural committees and their representatives in all Palestinian locations, using announcement board in the local official entities such as the local municipality, the cooperative, local CBO. For some intervention such as land rehabilitation or agricultural road, beneficiaries are requested to fill-out applications form that is studied with high transparency by a committee formed by the UAWC team and a representative from the local official entity and local agricultural committee focal point. To ensure equal and impartial access to aid, UWAC M&E Officer carry out a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process, and all applicants are notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process.
