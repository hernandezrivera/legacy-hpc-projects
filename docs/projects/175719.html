Project Name

Start Date

01/01/2021

End Date

31/12/2021

Project Summary

This project aims at providing life-saving frontline for most vulnerable population including IDPs and returnees with sufficient attention given to women, girls and children, by improving the living environment with provision of basic WASH services, reducing malnutrition cases and mitigating GBV risks among IDPs, returnees and vulnerable hosting community residents in targeting Bor South, Pigi and Duk, Juba, Lainya, Yei, Terekeka, Magwi, Torit and Ikotos Counties in the 3 states of Jonglei, Central and Eastern Equatoria States.

1 - Needs

Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2020. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding. Amidst efforts to resolve its ongoing conflict situation, South Sudan finds itself having to confront the global outbreak of the Novel Coronavirus (COVID-19). More children were acutely malnourished than in the past three years. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labor and child marriage. People’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. A total of 72 counties face extreme needs while five are in severe need.Large-scale inter-communal and sub-national violence in Jonglei and Greater Pibor Administrative Area displaced hundreds of thousands of people.Ongoing sub-national violence escalated in May 2020 with an estimated 80,000 people displaced in Central Equatoria, Jonglei, Unity, Warrap and Western Bahr el Ghazal and another more than 60,000 people were displaced by sub-national violence in Jonglei State in June 2020 and the violence led to the disruption of humanitarian services.The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Towards the end of 2020, PWJ held several meetings with the community leaders, local authorities in some of the counties/payams/boma in Juba, Bor South, Magwi and Torit counties to find out the WASH situations in the areas and the findings were as follows;The State Director for water and sanitation in Jubek State expressed that the capacity of the staff in his department is low and that of public health system is inadequate. He also said that there are no spare parts and tools for rehabilitation of water points and they have not managed to meet the standard of training water committees per borehole for sustainability of existing water points. The county’s plan to monitor water points and identify risk factors is strained by funds. Poor access to basic WASH facilities especially safe water supply, improved sanitation facilities and access to good hygiene practice knowledge in both the institutions and at the community levels are key concerns raised in the County. Most populations in Juba County rely on river Nile water through expensive water trucking systems. The few existing functional hand pumps are not enough to serve the bigger populations hence a bigger portion of the populations still collect surface water from swamps, rivers, hand dug wellsThe sanitation coverage in most Payams in Juba county are generally very poor among both the IDPs, host communities and the returnees. The main IDP site is the former UN-House POCs, Mahad and Don-Bosco. There are also IDPs living with host communities in different parts of Juba County. Where more than 90% of the population practice open defecation. The Payam authorities has raised a concern of putting in place sanitation facilities in the markets and community centers places and targeting cattle keepers with hygiene messagesSince large portion of the temporary latrine and bathing facilities were constructed back in the year 2018-2019, most of them have poor conditions, are deeply dilapidatedPWJ is operating and implementing basic WASH services in CES, EES and Jonglei since 2014.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During the project inception meetings PWJ will engage the community representatives, where information PSEA will be passed to them. The community representatives will be given the responsibilities of reporting any act of PSEA to PWJ and local authorities. Any contractors that will be hired by PWJ will sign code of conduct which will include avoiding PSEA and child abuse policy among the target project beneficiaries. During the project implementation, PWJ will conduct monthly community representative meetings during which project progress updates are shared and feedback from the community about any act PSEA are also heard and appropriate actions taken to address any concerns that will be raised by the community and beneficiaries representatives.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

PWJ will engage the representatives of people with disabilities during the project designs especially regarding the locations and the designs of the WASH facilities so that people with disabilities and persons with special needs like old age are able to easily access and use the WASH facilities in secured and safe conditions.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

PWJ will encourage equal participation of girls, boys, men and women in project sites assessment and project designs while respecting the views and concerns from women especially in the siting new water points and prioritizing the rehabilitations of existing WASH facilities in the community.PWJ will conduct project inception meetings with the community representatives (women leaders, youth leaders and community leaders) in each project sites to give information about the project.During the project implementation, PWJ will conduct monthly community representative meetings during which project progress updates are shared and feedback from the community are also heard and appropriate actions taken to address any concerns that will be raised by the community and beneficiaries representatives. PWJ will also avail the contacts for Field Project Assistants to beneficiaries for follow up and sharing information during the project implementation period.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

PWJ will take any feedback from project beneficiaries seriously by investigating and analyzing the concerns raised regarding the projects and taking appropriate actions to adjust project designs where necessary.
