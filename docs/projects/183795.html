Project Summary

ADRA Sudan will continue to Provide life-saving integrated interventions in Sudan to address the prolonged food security, livelihood challenges and Protection conditions faced by the population and alleviate the underlying food security issues by restoring livelihoods and agriculture cultivation. A total of 200,000 will be reached by the proposed initiative. The project activities are grounded in this development hypothesis and are focused squarely on the following five parallel “If” statements: IF Project trains smallholder farmers on best practices aim to increase yield, provide agriculture inputs, in addition to strengthening their production capacity through farmer field schools; THEN, the program participants will be able to decrease their crop yield losses and enhance household level food security and income.
• If Project provides access to cash assistance for small businesses interrupted by conflict; THEN, vulnerable households will be able to increase their income resulting in improving their food security. Following nonproductive agricultural methods that lead to land degradation and environmental damage. Decrease in agricultural yields leading to food insecurity as communities are dependent on agricultural production to meet their food needs. Lack of access to improved inputs especially for female farmers and female led households. Project proposed intervention will enhance farming productivity of farmers by introducing improved agricultural methods and organising farmers to farmer field schools. Provision of improved tools and seeds to meet food needs. Especially female farmers.Restarting small business to help in restoring income generation activities for conflict affected households and especially women. ADRA will take steps to ensure that IDPs, women, and youth are given priority during beneficiary targeting to participate in Farm Field Schools (FFS), where demo plots will be used to give beneficiaries hands-on learning opportunities and produce coming from those demo plots goes to beneficiaries. During the implementation of agricultural programs, ADRA will hold meetings with the community leadership to ensure that the land will be set aside for the FFS demo plots. ADRA will continue to promote access to land for those most vulnerable in communities by creating formal agreements with those communities’ leaders, arranging for a percentage of the beneficiaries’ crops to be returned to landowners as compensation after the program has finished. This will be based on the assumption that beneficiaries are willing to work within the framework of the program in agriculture activities,Excessive rainfall will not cause targeted localities to be inaccessible and will allow program activities to proceed without significant delay, Program staff are able to access the program area regularly to work in implementing the components and follow up with installations and progress. Beneficiaries for the FFS will be selected through a participatory approach with the communities in the area. ADRA will work with its national partner on compiling the list of smallholder farmers to be enrolled within the project, especially for those farmers involved in the first trainings who will be doing subsequent cascade trainings. Working with community committees and local stakeholders, ADRA and its local partner will identify small-scale farmers—mainly displaced and returnee HHs—with limited access to seeds and tools for agricultural production. Farmers must also have demonstrated access to land for agriculture (average of 2–5 feddan [.84–2.1 hectares]). Access to land does not necessarily need to be ownership; many farmers farm on a seasonal basis on rented land (share cropping).The FFS group will form the entry point around which ADRA will introduce various awareness messages, especially regarding food security and nutrition.Using a Training of Trainers (ToT) model, ADRA will identify and train 2500 lead farmers .

1 - Needs

African countries, such as the Sudan, continue to see high levels of currency depreciation and food inflation, which is reducing people’s purchasing power. Considering that some of these countries import substantial amounts of food to meet domestic needs, elevated international food prices will likely reduce food consumption of poor segments of the population in the coming months. In Sudan, a volatile security situation is likely to cause further displacement, and ethnic violence, particularly across Darfur, is likely to continue. According too the FAO-WFP hunger hotspots Sudan published in 2021, 4.6 M people are classified IPC/CH phase 3 and 1.3M IPC/CH Phase 4.Around 9.8 million people are projected to be in Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above) between June and September, during the peak lean season. That includes 2.7 million people in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). The total population in Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above) has remained similar to the same period last year, but the severity has increased.181 Looking ahead, food availability is likely to improve in the harvest and post-harvest period. As a result, about six million people are expected to be in Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above) between October 2021 and February 2022. According to the latest IPC results over 50,000 individuals in Bau and Kurmuk in BNS are at fall into IPC 3 (crisis) and 12,000 into IPC 4 (emergency). with some areas falling into IPC 4. The majority of the HHs in BNS, and especially in Kurmuk fall into either poor or borderline FCS (WFP FCS index). The COVID-19 pandemic had also wreaked damage on the overall economy in Sudan, and this has in turn gravely affected vulnerable communities, who have resorted to negative coping mechanisms, especially female led HHs, such as borrowing and collection of firewood to sell. The majority of HHs are agro-pastoralists (64 percent), making farming the main source of both income and food supplies in the communities, especially during the rainy seasons, where roads are cut off . Other sources of income include manual daily labor (18 percent) and small trades (1 percent). HH income has not increased in proportion to the overall increase in prices in the country, in fact the current economic situation has adversely affected those most vulnerable especially IDPs and returnees. An assessment ADRA conducted in December 2020, shows that 51 percent of the HHs received less that 10,000 SDG (equiv. $30 USD) per month, while 31 percent received between 10,000 – 30,000 (equiv. $30 - $100 USD). The current baseline report also shows the extreme vulnerabilities of female led HHs, who make up a little over half of the number of HHs in the community. Female led HHs are often as a result of the conflict whereby many are either widowed or have husbands currently enrolled with the different military factions. High percentage of those who are not educated (illiterate) is found remarkable requiring consideration in awareness raising and behavior change action. The highest percentage of populations in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse is found in North Darfur (25%), West Darfur (22%), North Kordofan (20%), South Kordofan (20%), Red Sea (20%), Gedaref (19%), Blue Nile and central Darfur (18%); South, East and South Darfur states (17%). The highest percentage of populations in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) are in the following four states: West Darfur (9%), Red Sea (7%), Central Darfur (6%) and Kassala (6%)

2 - Activities/Outputs

To improve access to food supplies through increased access to agricultural inputs and resilient practices while also increasing household income from new and restored livelihoods through developing strategies to improve food systems and reduce food losses and waste, Strengthen and support initiatives addressing cross-cutting issues of food security and nutrition of vulnerable groups. Subsidise agriculture inputs such as seeds and fuel, and improve investments in extension services and support to protect productive assets, especially areas impacted by flooding, conflict or other shocks, Ensuring Land Access for Vulnerable Beneficiaries. Establishment of the FFS will follow a “foci model” (in which groups grow outwards from a core nucleus), where successive FFSs are established in the immediate neighbourhood to form a cluster. Provide improved seeds and tools to 15 000 farmers—20-25 farmers in each of the targeted villages. The seeds will be selected through a technical committee to ensure that they do not pose a threat to the crops in the area and do not damage the ecosystem. ADRA will also further enhance integration, by referring farmers with production capacity to market development programs ADRA is currently implementing in the area with other donors. This will include ADRA Farmer Market School trainings to help strengthening linkages between farmers and goods and service providers. Activities will also include Non-conditional Cash assistance, Farmer Field School Training, Provision of training, and livelihood assets, Permaculture and Cash for work in targeted areas to support 200,000 population and improve their food security and nutrition. This will provide opportunity of food and cash support to targeted communities, the activities will ensure equipping farmers groups with necessary skills to provide for themselves and their families with to plant and enhance their agriculture production through training and farmers farming schools, the project will enhance and strengthen the connection between communities and markets to ensure their accessibility to markets and buy their farming needs as well as sell their products with fair price and are able to sell what they produce.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Some of the agricultural methods used by small-scale farmers that lead to land degradation and environmental damage include: clear cutting of land to make way for cultivation, the lack of crop rotation, and the practice of monocultures. In addition, due to the low productivity of their farmland, small scale farmers often resort to cutting trees all around to make charcoal or sell as fuel wood to earn income for buying food crops. To remedy this situation, ADRA will distribute high yielding crop varieties in addition to training using the FFS curriculum in which productive farming using conservation methods is one of the main premises of the trainings. Farmers will be encouraged to adopt agroforestry instead of clear cutting, and the establishment of shelter belts through growing of gum Arabic trees to encourage intercropping and reduce reliance of monocultures.Through the FFSs, the project will provide technical support to beneficiaries to ensure they have the information and hands-on knowledge for how to reduce the risk of pest and weed damage to their crops. The Project will focus on technical—rather than material—support, using FFSs as a vehicle to train beneficiaries on implementing integrated pest management (IPM). The IPM component of the training will have modules focusing on:• Balancing crop productivity with the height of the annual “pest” infestations when planning planting times.• Intercropping—the use of intentional planting of different crops together to mitigate pest infestation problems associated monoculture planting.• Crop rotation, to mitigate planting crops that certain pests prefer and where they may have left eggs/larva for the following year.• Natural pest deterrents utilising plants such as neem and hagel.a. Project locations and surrounding area will not drain into any bodies of water. All project locations are far from the Nile and other river sources.• Ensuring optimal environments for natural pest predators.• Enhancing or creating linkages between farmers and agriculture extension offices in the target localities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection is a concept that encompasses humanitarian operations in response to complex emergencies or natural disasters aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual in accordance with the letter and spirit of human rights, refugee, and humanitarian law. The project design is sensitive to the heightened vulnerability of women and girls in the current crisis and ADRA’s transparent working style will mitigate the risks for harm, exploitation and abuse and builds trust. ADRA will build on our existing relationships with communities and build rapport with new communities, to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable in reaching out to them if and when issues arise to provide support to those who may not have any way to report issues; particularly surrounding GBV. In program design and set up, ADRA will work closely with women and children to ensure that program designs, including proposed training sessions and FFSs, are implemented in a way to not expose vulnerable individuals to situations that would expose them to potential GBV. Community engagement is at the core of the program, especially the protection component. In order to successfully deliver the services in the area, ADRA will ensure that channels of communication continue to be open, regular, and accessible throughout the program cycle. At the outset of the program, the program team will conduct meetings with the different communities, including the tribal leadership, and those from specific vulnerable groups (women, girls, elderly, youth, PWDs, PLW) to inform them that the program, to which they contributed to the planning process, has been approved. Together the program objectives, expected outputs, methodology of activities and timelines will be reviewed and agreed on. In order to foster inclusivity in decision making, ADRA will actively aim in engaging different age groups . Youth are usually the main community groups that volunteer to undertake such responsibility due to their physical abilities. However, ADRA will also provide youth with the knowledge to participate in decision making and allow them to follow-up on the program activities.
