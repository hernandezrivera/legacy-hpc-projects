Project Summary

Global surgery encompasses anaesthesia, all surgical specialties including trauma surgery, general surgery, Obstetric & Gynaecology, peri-operative medicines, critical emergency medicine, pain management, palliative care, and rehabilitation.
The main goal of this project is to strengthen the surgical, obstetric, and anesthesia (SOA) cares of ten hospitals in five geographical areas. The project will equip the selected secondary facilities with the critical emergency surgical specialties, perioperative medicines, surgical supplies, anaesthesia medicines, and advanced training of health professionals involved in care of the surgical patient on life support, patient safety, pain management, and palliative care. Furthermore, the project will support expansion of trauma care and life support through establishment of efficient referral system from primary health care facilities in the five geographical areas to the targeted hospitals to ensure maximum coverage of vulnerable people including migrants, displaced families, returnees and people with special needs.

1 - Needs

The Libyan health system continues to be challenged by limited capacities, shortages of medicines and staff. The most requested needs are for specialized surgeons, anaesthesia, paediatricians, and gynaecology and obstetrics specialists. Service availability and readiness assessments of Libyan hospitals were done in 2017 and 2019 revealed that surgery is offered in more than 50% of all hospitals but the overall readiness score for major surgery is 52% and 32% for minor surgery. Onsite 24-hour anesthesia staff is available only in 57% of the hospitals offering major surgery, and the mean overall availability of anesthesia medicines is 43%. In addition, standard patient care guidelines for routine post-surgical care are available in 25% of the hospitals, and there is a significant need to address general quality control measures in the hospitals especially in laboratories.In 2019, continued and expanded conflict and displacement added more strain on surgical services and called for an urgent response to strengthen the availability of accessible, equitable, available and sustainable quality surgical care services across the country. Many people (Libyans and non-Libyans) lacked sustained access to secondary health care services. In addition, hospital staff is used inefficiently for non-emergency consultations as the first point of contact, rather than the PHC system. In October 2019 hospital assessment revealed that the six essential services are offered in only 6.4% of 78 public hospitals allover Libya. Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC) is available in 49%, emergency in 63%, and major surgery in 52% of the 78 assessed hospitals in the different four regions of the country. The targeted hospitals were selected based on the shortages of qualified health professionals in the different surgical fields.WHO member states declared surgical, obstetric, and anesthesia (SOA) care as both ‘‘critical’’ and ‘‘integral’’ components to achieve global health goals and emergency care (World Health Assembly Resolution 68.15). Therefore ten hospitals in five different geographical areas will be targeted to provide sustainable and quality global surgery and referral services across Libya.

2 - Activities

The following activities will be implemented to enhance access to surgical, obstetric, and anesthesia (SOA) care and referral services in order to achieve the overall objective of the project:1. Establish two Emergency Medical Team (EMT) according to EMT standards of WHO with the needed specialties2. Sub-contracting the Libyan Red Crescent to strengthen referral services including emergency3. Provide technical and financial support to the first line trauma care respondents4 Provision of trauma kits, emergency surgical (TESK) kits , anaesthesia medicines kits, and Reproductive Health kits to ten hospitals 5. Provide maintenance services to laboratories in ten hospitals6. Increasing capacity of ICU beds in ten hospitals7. Conduct five training workshops for health workers on health service quality, patient safety, and infection prevention and control8. Conduct five training workshops for health workers on Basic and Advanced Life support courses (BLS & ALS), neonatal resuscitation and new borne care at birth and dissemination of different related guidelines9. Develop surgical indicators to identify the most pressing needs, plan appropriate service delivery, and evaluate ongoing interventions.10. Integrate SOA data with the National DHIS-2 platform to create SOA data systems to measure impact at national level 11. Support SOA planning processes by engaging and informing key national stakeholders while leveraging external support for developing National Surgical, Obstetric, and Anesthesia Plans (NSOAPs) and national emergency plan

4 - Protection risk analysis

The main protection risks are:Health care staff are subject to violence Hard-to-reach areas and areas of higher severity scale Inadequate information is available to communities and people affected by crisis about the activities of humanitarian partners Limited collaboration with grass-root organizations in implementation of projects

5 - Mitigation measures

This project is based on identified protection risks and needs. Systematic and meaningful tools were used during health needs assessments to conduct protection risk analysis.Lessons learnt from the 15 medical mobile teams deployed by WHO through the whole year of 2019 will guide us to mitigate the risk for 2020, and weekly review of security situation through WHO focal points will be produced with development of necessary recommendations. Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure connecting health services in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the patients.WHO will organize a full orientation session on the accountability to affected people and protection for the service providers.
