Project Summary

IOM will continue supporting refugees and host communities aiming at reaching 96,946 households living in 18 IOM Areas of Responsibility (AoR) camps with Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) including Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) interventions and 300 host community households with shelter upgrades.
Under Objective 1, IOM will implement projects on Transitional Shelter Assistance Phase 3 (TSA 3), new Mid-Term Shelters (MTS) and their maintenance, trainings on shelter reinforcement/disaster risk reduction (DRR) with the inclusion of youth and women, provision of treated borak bamboo poles in all shelter-related activities, replacement of cooking stoves, provision of LPG refills including related trainings, solar lamp distributions, NFI voucher assistance in camps and shelter upgrades through conditional and unrestricted cash transfers combined with Shelter/DRR training in host communities. For TSA 3, Pipeline Partners will be involved, including Caritas (Camp 13 and 19, only borak), NRC (Camp 8E), Save the Children (Camp 8W) and HEKS (Camp 15 and 16), with HEKS also covering NFI voucher assistance.
Activities under Objective 2 include provision of emergency shelter support and NFIs based on the Rapid Damage Verification (RDV), prepositioning of emergency shelter kits (ESK), assisting 30 per cent of households in camps in Ukhia and 100 per cent in camps in Teknaf, provision of tie down kits (TDK) and awareness as part of monsoon preparedness, as well as provision of new LPG packages for those with damaged and new households. IOM will also cover respective camps as camp-level focal points, ensuring camp-level coordination as well as receiving and addressing referred cases from other stakeholders such as Site Management and Protection actors. For Emergency shelter, NFI support and TDK, IOM will work with Caritas (Camp 13 and 19) and HEKS (Camp 15 and 16) as Pipeline Partners.
Under Objective 3, IOM will ensure shelter activities are carried out with the involvement of women, older people, persons with disabilities and gender-diverse populations through cash-for-work activities. IOM will also conduct multiple consultations with communities before and after the implementation to capture the beneficiaries’ feedback in the planning and implementation. Feedback and complaints received will be also utilized for modifying future interventions.
IOM will maximize the use of voucher and cash for work as implementation modality in all programmes to ensure beneficiaries have freedom of choice to provide the most needed materials and items. All borak bamboo poles used in shelter-related activities will be treated at IOM’s Bamboo Treatment Facility to provide a longer lifespan and strengthen structures in combination with metal footings. The pilot initiative for care and maintenance of solar lamps will be implemented with trained youth, and basket making initiatives with female cash-for-work beneficiaries.
In all Shelter/NFI and LPG activities, IOM will ensure protection mainstreaming in line with IOM’s Gender-Based Violence (GBV) in Crisis Framework and its concurrent Action. Those identified as households with Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVI) will receive porter support. Construction support for shelter-related activities will also be provided to all EVI cases. Distribution points and team composition for the implementations will be arranged to mitigate the risk of GBV. Community feedback mechanisms with complaints desks, community mobilizations, regular project monitoring and post distribution monitoring will be deployed. All data collected during assessment and monitoring activities will be sex and age disaggregated and particular information regarding vulnerabilities will be collected to provide additional support during the implementation. Continuous efforts for the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) including (refresher) training for all staff and awareness on PSEA for beneficiaries will also be continued.

1 - Identified Needs

The Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) 2021 results indicate that with the limited access to self-reliance activities, the refugee population in camps remain highly reliant on humanitarian assistance. Almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs, most commonly related to Shelter/NFI among others. Roughly three quarters of households reported issues with their shelters and large proportions reported having insufficient NFIs. More specifically, according to J-MSNA 2021 results, 74 per cent of the assessed population have indicated Shelter/NFI as priority needs which is the highest among other needs. The reported Shelter/NFI needs include shelter materials/upgrades, solar lamps and household/cooking items. Many shelter materials such as bamboos, tarpaulins and ropes require replacement, considering their limited lifespans and difficult weather conditions with monsoon and cyclones. Improvement and maintenance of existing shelters are also required to increase privacy, security and ventilation. In addition to the robust shelter intervention with TSA 2 covering IOM AoR camps, the emergency shelter response needs to continue to address the life-saving shelter needs of those affected by monsoon, cyclones and other shocks within a short timeframe, considering the vulnerable shelter conditions across camps. Need for repair and maintenance of community shelters such as mosques, madrasas, women-friendly spaces and child friendly spaces continues to ensure that these can serve as evacuation centres for families in case their shelters are damaged due to disasters. With 3.4 per cent population growth in camps every year, new shelter construction (either double-storey shelters and/or new MTS) on properly developed land with site development and site plans will continue to be a need in 2022. The distribution of LPG has had a significant environmental benefit by reducing deforestation and preventing the extraction of over 533,000 tonnes of firewood per year from the forest area in Cox’s Bazar district. The continuation of LPG distribution will also be required to mitigate protection risks such as GBV and reduce negative health impacts due to smoke inhalation. It is critical that sustained access to LPG cooking fuel continues without interruption to meet the essential needs of refugees and avoid renewed degradation of recently restored landscapes, especially given current restrictions on refugees’ access to stable income to afford the cost of LPG themselves. On top of existing difficulties across the Rohingya refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar, the COVID-19 outbreak further impacts the lives of vulnerable groups including women and girls . Lack of space and privacy in shelters of Rohingya refugees may lead to increasing protection-related risks, such as GBV and child abuse. Specific actions to mitigate the risk of GBV is needed and incorporated in all Shelter/NFI and LPG activities, including female and protection staff presence in all activities, prioritization of vulnerable individuals in distributions, staff (refresher) trainings on good distribution practices, and clarifying referral pathways when encountered with survivors. The refresher training on PSEA and training for new staff, as well as awareness on PSEA for beneficiaries will be required to continue throughout 2022. For host communities, according to the 2021 J-MSNA, the proportion of households reporting shelter materials among their top three priority needs was notably higher this year compared to previous years. A lack of money to pay for materials or labour was the most commonly reported reason for not having made shelter repairs and 71 per cent of the surveyed population had reported shelters issues.
