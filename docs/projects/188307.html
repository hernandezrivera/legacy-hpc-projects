Project Summary

Syria is one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities, triggering one of the worst displacement crises in modern history, resulting in widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, including health and agricultural facilities leading to food, nutrition and related insecurity.
As reported by HNO, there are 4.9 Million Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) in dire need for nutrition assistance at the end of 2021. In Afrin sub district alone, the rate of malnutritioned PLWs and CU5 is a staggering over 80%. The access to health facilities is limited by PLWs unaffordability to reach and / or follow nutritional advise. Another major factor playing a vital part in infant health is lack of awareness of young child feeding practices. This coupled with lack of mother support groups puts a vast majority of PLWs in severity scale of 4 and 5. Amongst all PLWs, a vast majority (90%) reported unaffordability to cope with nutritional needs. This is complexed by social issues such as underage marriages, resulting in malnutritioned PLWs in age group of 15 – 17 years. This age groups has no coping mechanism except begging for food or picking food from garbage. This was observed during implementation of a program by us in last part of 2021 in Afrin sub district.
Building upon the previous program, the proposed action will provide response to 2000 PLWs, mostly IDPs. We plan to focus on 1 sub-district (Afrin). The planned programming intends to be achieved through an integrated program of assistance focused on providing targeted, CVA, coupled with awareness and creation of Mother Support Groups.
Nutrition Response (2000 PLWs age range 18 - 40) – We have already proposed an action in consultation with BHA to addressed the needs of 1200 PLWs in Afrin Sub District. This is an integrated proposal, integrating FSL, Emergency, Livelihood and PLW support. This is a holistic approach to ensure best use of assistance provided.
Modality – The planned activities will have a repeated support for a period 10 months through our voucher program. The voucher program will be complimented by 4 awareness sessions each month for a period of 12 months. As implemented previously, 150 Mother Support Groups will be created to support beneficiaries as well as non-beneficiaries. There will be soft interventions on healthy cooking and hygiene for PLWs. Linkages will be formed with health centers and other health actors for provision of micro nutrients. The selection of beneficiaries will be based on our already developed vulnerability criteria taking into consideration protection issues as well.
Data Collection and Monitoring - The monitoring will be through our well defined M&E systems. Monitoring will be diligently executed starting from Verification, Distribution Monitoring, Post Distribution Monitoring and Satisfaction Surveys. The data will be collected online, primarily using tablets and Kobo tools. We deploy several tools to include Excel and SPSS to analyze and report. A robust system is in place for complaints receipt and redressal, with dedicated teams available for handling and resolving complaints. A robust system of lodging complaints is already in place through communications tools such as WhatsApp, SMS and email. Further the complainant can also lodge a complaint via hard copy and drop it in the drop box placed at our office locations.
Reporting – We will ensure prompt reporting on all of our projects through regular filing of donor as well as 4W reports and ad hoc reporting requirements.
CVA Support Systems – A fully automated and online Cash Voucher System developed internally and utilized for last 6 years will be deployed. The system is flexible to use paper vouchers or cards and can be of single or multiple use. To complement our voucher programs, we have framework agreements with Fund Service Providers that provide cash distributions through formal arrangements. Due to this, our response time is short

4 - Needs

As indicated in the summary, HNO 2022 reported 5.5 Million People in Need (PiN) in need for nutrition assistance during 2022-23, out of which 2.4 million classified as extreme and 2.1 million catastrophicneed. Out of the total 5.5 million, 4.6 are in NES and NWS. PiN’s access to health centers is limited by inability to afford transport, complexed by inability to follow nutritional needs and limited availability of micro nutrients. Majority of PiNs (90%) reported unaffordability to cope with nutritional needs. This is complexed by social issues such as underage marriages, resulting in malnutritioned PiNs in age group of 15 – 17 years. This age group has no coping mechanism at all. A vast majority of PiNs are in severity scale of 4 - 5. As informed by our own experience as well as HNO 2022, 25% of the women are not feeding their infants and young children optimally. Coupled with conflict and displacements, 60% of IYCs (6-24 months) are not receiving optimal diets, either in quantity or diversity. This has further aggravated due to failing economy and COVID. Access to diverse food is out of reach for most PLWs, both in availability and affordability. Poor hygiene practices, results in IYC disorders, including malnutrition. Pre displacement, there was a family / social support available to guide PiNs. This has diminished with displacements, leaving a wide gap in awareness. Lack of multi-sectoral assistance further aggravates the situation and complexes the issue further.

5 - Implementation of activities

The planned activities will build upon lessons learned on previous program, where a total of 1085 PLWs were supported. The planned activities will be in Afrin Sub Districts, where needs have already been identified and interventions were conducted. Coordination with other NGOs has already initiated through MoUs, along with MoUs with the health centers. As indicated previously, currently there are 6000 PLWs in need in this sub district. The planned activities will reach out to 2000 PLWs in 2022, increasing to 3000 in 2023. The primary modality of support will be providing voucher support in the amount of $ 75 each month for 10 months. The modality will allow PLWs to support nutritional needs as well as visits to the health centers. To Strengthen health centers, the planned activities will support the health workers with monthly stipends for a period of 12 months. Complementing the CVA program, there will be 4 awareness sessions each month for 12 months, comprehensive material has been developed in nutritional needs, breast feeding and hygiene practices. The awareness sessions will be enhanced by creation of 150 Mother Support Groups as well as 200 beneficiaries’ groups. In collaboration with health centers, the program intends to identify the micro nutrient needs and will be sourced from other health actors. Based on previous programs this approach will yield optimal results.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible. Accessibility measures will be built into assistance delivery, including the option for home delivery to beneficiaries unable to safely access distribution points due to mobility or other restrictions. To mitigate against protection risks, we work closely with LCs and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries served. Refresher training on NGO PSEA and Child Protection policies were delivered during 2020 to ensure all staff understand how to identify and correctly report any SEA or CP concerns


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Syria faces the most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities which resulted in massive numbers requiring assistance. With increased deteriorating situation, the coping mechanisms are failing leading to serious protection issues. This is further complexed by change of roles where females are heading the households, a role traditionally held by the male population. These insecurities comes with protection issues, particularly GBV and child labor. Coupled with increased hostilities and COVID, resulted in negative coping mechanism such as early marriages and child labor. A safe estimates informs that 42% of the affected population faces one or more protection issues. No significant risks were found during implementation of current programming. To counter any risks, we have designed and put in place a strong Protection mechanism, where protection issues can be reported and complaints handled speedily both at field and HQ level. Our staff is continuously trained in PSEA. Variety of mediums have been deployed for complaints including telephone, email and physical receipts.To counter risks, a strong awareness campaigns are designed along with a robust Complaint Redressal Mechanism to handle complaints. A strong team is in place with appropriate trainings and refreshers to handle complaints. All complaints will be duly reported to the funding agencies.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

We monitor risks real‑time and will continue to follow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. The following potential risks exists and appropriate measures are applied to program design: Risk 1 Authorities: LCs and local authorities (including AFAD) remain supportive of the NGOs activities and continue to cooperate with established Standard Operating Procedures designed to ensure targeted and transparent delivery of assistance on the basis of need alone. We continually meet the local authorities to ensure optimal cooperation. Risk 2 Verifications: 100% house-to-house beneficiary verification reduces the risk of inclusion or exclusion criteria while close coordination with other FSL actors reduces the risk of duplicationRisk 3 COVID 19: Established guidelines for COVID-19 prevention measures for all field operations continue to mitigate against the risk of transmission Risk 4 Security: Security is continuously monitored and context in targeted areas allows ongoing access by NGO staff. Risk 5 Protection: Protection risk will be identified earlier and mitigated through established systems. We have an established Risk Advisory Department managed by expatriate staff. We are continually update our contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in Syria, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Risk Mitigation is updated on a monthly basis both at HQ and field.
