Project Summary

Refugees and asylum seekers lack the clan affiliation enjoyed by host community members and this exacerbates the protection risks they face. In theory, they have access to the justice system, but many disputes in Somalia are settled through the clan system and refugees and asylum-seekers accessing the formal legal system often face retaliation. Lack of education and marketable skills for many refugees and asylum-seekers reduces the economic opportunities available to them, particularly Ethiopians. As a result, they engage in informal manual labour that further exposes them to abuse from the host community and violations of their labour rights.
Joint engagement and working in partnership with national and international actors will be pursued to support the Government in working towards improved protection of refugees and asylum seekers while laying the groundwork for the future attainment of durable solutions. This will entail an integrated approach that aims at mainstreaming protection and solutions, underpinned by a strengthened evidence based interventions through improvements in Information Management initiatives. Engagement will be guided by a focus on inclusion, national capacity building, and sustainable, long-term and multi-partner planning; and principles and objectives of the CRRF and other applicable frameworks such as the NDP and relevant national and FMS legislation and policies.
Inclusion will be key, and partners will be encouraged to take rights rather than status based approach, and to -include refugees and asylum seekers in their programmes and strategies.

Project Indicators

1. Enhanced multi-sectoral, rights-based, life-saving protection assistance including legal interventions to address legal and physical protection risks.2. Improved registration, identity, and civil status documentation, and targeted Refugee Status Determination (RSD)3. Promotion of refugees’ access to and their integration into national systems, in particular basic services available to nationals such as health and education, (iv) enhancing self-reliance through livelihoods and Vocational Skills Training (VST).4. Support for the development of updated refugee protection legislation, policy and procedures adhering to international standards, and the capacity-building of federal, state and regional institutions to prepare for transition of registration and RSD5. For Somali refugees who have made the informed decision to voluntarily return to Somalia, UNHCR will continue to manage way stations/reception centers in Somalia that act as points of entry for the returnee population, and where counselling and basic services are provided. UNHCR will continue to use cash as the assistance modality of choice, while assistance will also be delivered through in-kind support and, indirectly, through capacity-building activities with the Government, local actors and partners.Maintain way station and reception facilities in key entry ports
