Project Summary

The proposed intervention will prioritize women-headed household and other vulnerable populations IDPs, returnees, and female headed household in the thre target areas At this stage, ACF is requesting flexibility on target areas given the volatile security context within the areas especially Central Darfur,. The intervention will aim to cover a total of 1200 households. At least 50% of the target beneficiaries, should be women. Other vulnerable families will likely be categorized as among the ‘very poor’ wealth group within the community (having a limited asset base or high dependency ratio, limited or no other income sources etc.). Among this, households headed by, or having members of, persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and multiple children under the age of 5 years will be prioritized. Given the holistic nature of the intervention, it will be an important criteria for the selected beneficiaries to have access to land (owned, rented, or free use) to establish the kitchen gardens or to receive seeds and also have at least 12 small ruminants (sheep and/or goats) for the livestock support.

1 - Needs

he recent social unrest in main towns of have resulted in heightened restricted security measurements set by government, declaration of state of emergency in many areas which resulted in further disruption of people access to their normal livelihoods activities, disrupted access to main markets and free trade flows across the country in addition to further deterioration on overall macroeconomic of the country, all these have contributed to high price of essential food and non-food items and have weekend majority of poor households purchasing capacity to levels beyond their survival threshold. It is estimated that 14% of the total population (5.8 Million) are experiencing Crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and are in need of urgent action (IPC June 2019). This figure is the highest on record since the introduction of the IPC. One million individuals are facing Emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4), and around 4.8 million individuals are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), while nearly 11.8 million are estimated to be in Stress Phase (IPC Phase2). In White Nile State, the food insecurity rate is 11.6 (Phase 3) and 6.2% (phase 4). While, in Central Darfur, the food insecurity rate is 14.9 (Phase 3) and 6.8% (Phase 4). Since the separation of South Sudan, the economic situation was in decline phase, which worsened since Dec2018 has resulted a limited livelihoods source coupled with extremely high inflation, lack of cash liquidity, high prices, and scarcity of bread and fuel. According to the comprehensive Food Security Assessment (CFSA) 2018, in White Nile had the highest per capita expenditure increased by 140% in 2018, while in Central Darfur it was increased by 106%, Such a dramatic rise is attributed to rising inflation rates throughout 2018 across Sudan and most probably it is worse in 2019. Although crops benefited from favourable precipitation and harvest in 2018, the high cost of production a result of high agricultural input prices as well as shortages of fuel and cash have affected farming production. Subsequently, during the past year, the national average cost of crops and livestock on the market has increased significantly compared to historical averages. Rising prices, compounded by limited access to livelihoods opportunities, have been identified as the main drivers of food insecurity and inadequate food consumption, which have led to a severe decline in the purchasing power of the most vulnerable communities in Sudan.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1) Conduct market assessment for cash transfer programming (CTP) feasibility ACF will assess the availability and quality of the needed inputs in local markets, as well as the accessibility of these markets for beneficiaries, and the capacity of traders to meet the increased demand as a result of the project. 2) Carry out baseline and endline survey in target location for our beneficiaries ACF and its partners will collect basic information on the food security, nutrition and livelihoods situation of a sample of beneficiaries in the target areas prior to any support being provided. This will form the basis against which ACF will measure the impact of the project. This will include measures of dietary diversity, food consumption, income sources and asset holdings.3)Identify suppliers, procure all livelihood inputs and ensure quality of all inputs (with a CTP option) Depending on the results of the market assessment and feasibility study, ACF will determine the most appropriate modality (including potentially a combination of modalities). 4 Build capacities of beneficiaries on cultivation/kitchen gardens, dairy production and livestock management with promotion of key nutrition and hygiene messages In order to instil beneficiaries with the skills and knowledge to maximize the benefits of the assistance provided, ACF will conduct trainings in vegetable cultivation, including basic water catchment and using household run-off water, livestock management and basic care practices, and dairy production.5) Training of community Animal Health workersACF, state ministries and the beneficiary committees will identify 25 animal health workers based on agreed criteria. 6) Distribute agriculture and livelihood inputs (in-kind and/or equivalent CTP modality) ACF will provide three packages of livelihoods support to the 1200 H.H (vulnerable) households.7) Formation and capacity strengthening of VS&LAs: ACF is planning through this intervention to establish and train a further 30 groups, also with an average membership of 20 persons per group, totaling 600 individuals. 8) Rehabilitation of community agriculture extension centers and training workers (CAEW) and conduct agricultural extension outreach: Through this intervention, ACF with partnership with the ACSAD and SMoA to provide extension services to rural farmers, and propose to identify more CAEWs, including available female CAEWs, to be trained by the SMoA to support communities. In order to empower the women farmers in the target communities, there is a need to train more female CAEWs. 9) Training of nationals COS:ACF has mapped local organization working on agriculture and community development in White Nile and Central Darfur, out of six organizations identified ACF two organization will be selected to work with ACF under this project and all of the national organizations mapped by ACF exercise will be trained on agricultural support, adaptation to climate change and VSLA.
