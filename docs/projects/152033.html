Project Summary

The insurgency in Nigeria continues to affect the population living in the North-East. According to the Needs Overview by the Early Recovery & Livelihoods sector(August 2018) an estimated 5.5 million people, in the most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, are in acute need of early recovery assistance.
ER&L sector need overview reveals 5.5 million people in need, 1 million are IDPs in host communities, 1.5 million IDP returnees, and 2.9 million people in host communities . Several LGAs have been prioritised based on the levels of damages and service delivery burden associated with high numbers of returnees and IDPs in host communities: Damboa, Konduga, Monguno (Borno State); Madagali, Mubi North, Hong (Adamawa State); and Gujba, Potiskum and Fika (Yobe State).
The XXIV round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM - August 2018), estimated the number of IDPs and Returnees to be 3,506,841 (1,926,748 IDPS and 1,580,093 Returnees), 94% of whom have been displaced due to the Boko Haram insurgency in the North East. Out of the total number of displaced people, 137,588 IDPs and 165,771 Returnees are currently in Yobe State.
Ongoing crisis has worsen the situation including disruption of market supplies and livelihoods, and significantly limiting the populations having access to livelihoods. IOM estimates that 60% of the IDPs reside with host communities.
According to FEWSNET ’s Market Monitoring Bulletin of May 2018, prices in NE Nigeria are generally higher than other regions as result of insecurity and are expected to remain above average until the revival of main harvest, moreover, populations depend heavily on market purchase, currently since significant improvement in security in Yobe state, many markets are functional with enough supplies. The persisting conflict continues to substantially impact households’ livelihoods in the NE Nigeria. Despite the main seasons harvest coming soon, due to restricted access to land for most households in the NE, harvest will be substantially below average for most of the households. As situation in Yobe state is stabilizing, COOPI proposes livelihood activities in Yusufari,Geidam, Potiskum, Fika and Gulani LGAs. The project will target 5,865 households (approximatively 24,173 beneficiaries) in the project. Beneficiaries will be supported in order to foster the transition from emergency to resilience as COOPI is committed to apply the humanitarian-development nexus approach and ensure coherence between the actors. COOPI intends to protect and rehabilitate livelihoods of most vulnerable groups, pre-identified through COOPI’s ongoing food assistance and CBT activities funded by other donors, through technical trainings on adapted livelihood practices where possible in coordination and complementarity with ongoing line ministries and sector.
COOPI, in order to ensure sustainable and longer term impact and contribute to Yobe state government’s early recovery strategy (YOSERA) will consider beneficiaries from its food assistance programs to be trained and supported for livelihood assistance as transition. COOPI is suitably placed (technically and operationally) to implement livelihoods support projects in Yobe state due to current presence, access, and ongoing partnership with WFP. COOPI forecast strong potential for humanitarian development nexus through its presence in Yobe and particularly in proposed LGA. COOPI has already developed liaison and MOUs with state government’s line departments as well as providing capacity building opportunities to government institutes. COOPI will also consider the livelihood action to be linked with other sectors which are planned to be executed as multiyear response mainly to ensure transition from emergency to recovery and rehabilitation including conflict management. COOPI will consider to work through local NGOs. Implementation of current activities in the area have revealed a deterioration in livelihoods sources primarily due to frequent conflicts and inherent hardships in the state as well as climactic conditions and increased market demands. COOPI, following Early Recovery and Livelihood sector's objectives, intends to protect and rehabilitate livelihoods of most vulnerable groups, identified through technical trainings on adapted practices and formation and training of livelihood beneficiaries focusing on women groups who are already engaged with various small enterprises. COOPI will ensure that composition of sectors is defined based on COOPI Community Based Participatory Planning sessions and standard as well as aligned and coordinated with the Early Recovery and Livelihoods working groups. COOPI is implementing protection project in proposed LGAs, will ensure all the livelihood action is linked to protection support to the beneficiaries, particularly women and children.
Monitoring and reporting
COOPI is committed to monitor and report the proposed project as per ER&L sector monitoring framework and guideline and will ensure that results and indicators are achieved within the designated time frame. Programme Coordinator based as well as the Country Admin will ensure a constant and close monitoring of activities from program and administrative/financial point of view, while Child Protection team will support Protection Mainstreaming of livelihood response and also provide advice to ensure that all protection activities are responding to COOPI and global protection standard. Logistic Coordinator will also support Project Manager to ensure all procurement will be in line with donors and COOPI’s regulation and requirements.
COOPI’s monitoring system will involve all program staff and beneficiaries aiming at: a) assess impact of activities on the lives of beneficiaries, through interviews, focus group discussions, surveys, market surveys and observations; b) assess actual program achievements against its planned objectives and indicators; c) identify strengths and weaknesses of individual program activities; d) make changes to programming as needed to increase the quality of services; and e) advocate for unmet needs and gaps. The project M&E system will include: Monthly outputs and outcomes tracking against defined indicators as well as 5Ws. Project Manager will submit with timely updates as dynamic way of sharing challenges and progress. PM will also keep relevant donors and sector update on regular basis for any project related issues as required. COOPI will submit interim report as per guidelines as well as final report within timeframe of the project completion as per agreement.

Needs

In August 2018, COOPI made rapid assessment in Northern Yobe to examine the impact of the conflict on livelihoods issue including the effect of any shock at the household level. Finding from the KIIs and FGDs show their livelihood base is deteriorating from time to time and most vulnerable coping mechanism (like borrowing, social security) are affected. Most of the population in Yobe State relies on daily labour and livestock production for subsistence and income, with more than 80% of citizens reportedly engaged in small-scale subsistence farming. Markets status varies from town to town and across LGAs: the main urban towns such as Damaturu, Potiskum, Nguru, Gashua and Geidam currently have functional markets, and Potiskum Local Government Area (LGA) is home to one of the largest cattle markets in West Africa. The insurgency hit market infrastructure, with fuel stations, water pumps, and storage facilities destroyed, damaged, or closed. Half of all agricultural households in Yobe do not have access to agricultural storage infrastructure, increasing the risk of post-harvest losses. Deforestation, due to burning by insurgents and displaced populations’ need for cooking fuel, will have lasting economic and environmental impacts if not effectively managed in the short-term. Crop pests such as Fall Armyworm have also impacted yields in Northeast Nigeria. The supply of fertilizer, pesticide, and herbicide dropped to about half of pre-conflict levels, and have not fully recovered. Input suppliers lack rural outreach and marketing strategies to distribute their produces ‘over the last mile’ and into the hands of rural farmers, and sometimes only sell one product, forcing farmers to visit multiple input dealers to access all demanded inputs.COOPI is proposing a project that is aligned with ER sector’s Strategic Objective 1“Improve access to infrastructure and basic social services through rehabilitation and reconstruction of public service” and SO 2 “Support livelihood stabilization and income recovery” will roll out livelihoods recovery and promotion.,5865 households will be targeted, for approximatively 24,173 beneficiaries in the project. Proposed Livelihoods interventions reflects the recommendations of the Yobe State Socio-Economic Reform Agenda (YOSERA), produced by the State Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning.The WFP livelihoods assessment in Damaturu, Bade and Jakusko (Rapid Livelihood Assessment, April 2018) and result from COOPI’s recent community consultation, FGDs and KIIs reveal the needs of livelihood support to households and recommends the implementation of livelihoods and other conditional assistance through engaging communities. Based on COOPI’s past experience through its presence in proposed LGAs and implementing FS and IGA since 2015, will carry out Cash for Work, Income Generation Activities and conditional cash transfers as well provide capacity building to beneficiaries and government line department staff. COOPIs field observation is showed Yobe is appropriate for cash transfers and voucher modality. Beneficiaries will be selected among current in order to foster the transition from emergency to resilience as COOPI is committed to apply the humanitarian-development nexus approach and ensure coherence between the actors. CBT and food assistance beneficiaries from COOPI’s existing programs, will be selected as per their skills and links to various livelihood activities finalised through community consultation, CBPP and will be registered as livelihood beneficiaries. All asset creation or restoration initiatives will be designed in a way to maximize the potential for cash for work opportunities for local community members, however contractors will be engaged for technical expertise and to ensure quality service delivery when required. COOPI intends to protect and rehabilitate livelihoods of most vulnerable groups through several opportunities .

Activities

Strategic Objective 1: Improve access to infrastructure and basic social services through rehabilitation and reconstruction of public service facilities Activity 1.1: Vulnerable households receive conditional cash transfers – cash for work, providing CFW to 1000 households over the course of 9 months. Activity 1.2: Rehabilitation/construction of agri farms, infrastructure, water access points in markets, schools, and/or health centres through Cash for Work. During COOPI’s focus groups and KIIs, all FGDs and a majority of KIIs indicated that access to water is a key constraint to productive livelihoods, health, and is a potential driver of conflict. Activity 1.3 male and female youth aged 18-24 receive skills-training for in-demand sectors and/or are referred to market-aligned vocational training programs1.4: Support environment clean-up, and rehabilitation of local infrastructure1.5: Restoration of critical local infrastructure at community level which can support generating employment Strategic Objective 2:Support livelihood stabilization and income recovery Activity 2.1: Conduction of baseline and end-lineActivity 2.2: Beneficiaries identification/ CBPP verificationActivity 2.3: Community mobilisation and sensitisationActivity 2.4: Defining modalities of cash transfers Activity 2.5: Identify improved seed vendors in target LGAsActivity 2.6: 1,000 male and female farmers (50% female, 20% youth aged 18 to 24) have skills in climate-resilient agricultural techniques and effective post-harvest storage. COOPI will adapt the approach to the specific agro-ecosystems of Yobe State Strategic Objective 4: Support Restoration of Local Governance Administration and Services. Activity 3.1: Consultation meetings and sensitization sessions with line ministry staff Activity 3.2: Conduction of capacity building training for local authorities staffIdentify farmer and livestock associations/cooperatives for strengthening; or create new associations/cooperatives Activity 3.3: 20 Farmer and livestock associations created or strengthened. Farmer and livestock cooperatives and associations are able to negotiate a number of benefits for members, such as access to inputs and training, market information, higher prices for goods and services through bulk purchasing, and reduced transaction and storage costsActivity 3.4 30 Establishment of Community resilience groups, with diverse representation, to develop community resilience plans and conflict prevention forums.Activity 3.5: Conduct conflict prevention and conflict management dialogue sessions
