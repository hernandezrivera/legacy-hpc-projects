Project Summary

The project aims at contributing towards protection, physical and psychosocial well-being of 60,000 refugee and non-refugee children and adolescents in the Gaza Strip.
This is a multisectoral project linked with and aligned to the 2020 Protection and Education Cluster Response Frameworks. More specifically, the project addresses Protection Objective 2 “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence” and Education Objective 2 “Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to potential emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school-based psychosocial services”.
Analysis of UNICEF reports from 2019 reveals that of children injured in the “Great March of Return” demonstrations 95% were boys, and 63% were aged between 14-17 years old. The participation of adolescents in the demonstrations reflects their sense of hopelessness and despair. This cohort of children are particularly vulnerable to risky behaviours, particularly during the school holidays.
The project will enable boys and girls from vulnerable communities to benefit from recreational and life-skills activities given the absence of safe spaces and the deteriorating socio-economic situation in Gaza. Provision of psychosocial support activities and referrals to case management services will be ensured, where needed, to address distress caused by the conflict and violence to support a normal back to school.
Through targeting school children and staff, the project will contribute to the improvement of the student’s wellbeing, preventing school drop-out due to injuries by providing children with safe spaces and will support the reintegration of the drop-out children through the referral mechanism. The project will focus on reaching children with low learning achievement and those injured. The project activities will be carried out on a daily basis during the school summer-break.
At its core are the long-standing summer activities for elementary school refugee children in Gaza undertaken by UNRWA and civil society organisations. The project builds on experience from partnerships between UNICEF-UNRWA joint initiatives named “Keeping Kids Cool”, and “Keeping Kids Active” undertaken in the summer months of 2018 and 2019. These activities highlighted the positive impact of such a partnership in improving the well-being of vulnerable children through structured psychosocial support in addition to sport and after-school activities. The project will support capacity building for staff including psychosocial counsellors and facilitators to enable children acquire positive coping mechanisms, and enhanced access to referral pathways for targeted responses to particularly vulnerable children.
The majority of the activities is school-based and planned to take place inside 50 UNRWA schools with engagement from trained staff. Such activities will contribute to building schools’ capacities in detection, and referral to case management to respond to identified child protection needs. In addition, UNICEF with implementing partners will focus on providing adolescents with life-saving skills such as awareness on explosive remnants of war (ERW), conflict resolution, emergency preparedness, disaster risk reduction and first aid, psychosocial support and referrals to case management in around 30 child friendly spaces across the five Gaza governorates, reaching around 15,000 adolescents.

1 - Needs

Children and adolescents make up a half of the total population in Gaza. For them, life is overwhelmingly characterized by conflict, poverty and despair. Across Gaza, psychological distress, poverty and environmental problems have had a negative impact on children’s physical and mental health. More than 11 years of blockade have caused humanitarian and development crisis. People are suffering from chronic electricity deficit that has undermined their already fragile living conditions and negatively impacted their lives. It is estimated that 258,071 children in Gaza (50% boys) need structured psychosocial support and child protection services (HNO 2020). During the school year, children spend their day in overcrowded classrooms with schools working on double-shift and even a triple-shift basis. Hence, provision of extracurricular activities is rare due to the necessity to run schools in multiple shifts to accommodate a continuously increasing number of students. Outside schools, years of destruction and deprivation have left few or no spaces where children may participate in recreational activities, particularly during hot summer months. Girls have less opportunities to spend free time outside their home to participate in social activities due to entrenched discriminatory social norms and traditions. Children and adolescents are already experiencing loss of hope with the lack of opportunities and ensuing psychological distress, participate in demonstrations near the fence between Israel and Gaza. On these occasions, adolescents, especially boys, are more exposed to protection threats including death or serious injuries with long-term effects on their well-being. In this regard, boys who are maimed or wounded face major challenges to attend school as they need rehabilitation from injuries, which in turn puts them at risk of school drop-out and of child labour. Children (6-14 years) constitute 39% of the total population and are the most affected of the escalations and continuous deteriorating security situation. Between 1 January and 24 August 2019, a total of 8,901 Palestinians (5,009 men, 487 women, 2,706 boys, 137 girls and 562 not specified) were injured by Israeli forces during incidents related to demonstrations in Gaza. Before the summer months (01 January – 14 June 2019), the total child injuries were 2,209 (2,098 boys and 111 girls) in comparison to 4,502 adult injuries. During the school summer break (15 June – 24 August 2019) number of child casualties was nearly 40 % with 700 boys and 27 girls injured out of 1,175 Palestinians injured (OCHA database). July recorded the highest number of child casualties, in which children are not attending school and more likely to participate in demonstrations. The data on children hospitalized for serious injuries in the context of mass demonstrations gathered by the World Health Organizations and the Ministry of Health in 2019 also confirms the above. Between 1 January and 31 May 2019, 979 children (or 29%) were hospitalized out of 3,431 patients as result of serious injuries. During the summer months between 1 June and 31 August 2019, the proportion of children hospitalized was on average 39% with its 43% peak in July 2019 (144 children/338 people hospitalized). The proportion of children hospitalized could thus be another indicator of extent of children’s participation and serious injury. Based on the above, children and young people in Gaza have a need for recreational activities particularly during the summer months. The proposed project will start before summertime with sensitisation and provision of capacity building for staff. In the summertime the project will provide children with safe spaces with the aim to prevent or diminish protection threats faced by children and adolescents and to promote psychosocial well-being of the target groups and will support the drop-out/injured children to be reintegrated in the education system through the referral mechanism.

2 - Activities

The project will support the resilience and well-being of children and adolescents through school and community-based structured activities conducted in safe and child friendly environments. It aims at targeting low academic achievement students and drop-out children, and refer them to the case management to reintegrate in the education system UNICEF will partner with UNRWA and CSOs to implement activities for both refugee and non-refugee children and adolescents. UNRWA has long-standing experience to provide school-based psychosocial support to children and adolescents from Grade 1 to 9 through the summer activities in UNRWA schools across Gaza. UNICEF and UNRWA will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to register and identify the non-refugee children and adolescents (6-13 years) across the Gaza strip. UNICEF has a well-established network of implementing partners. To ensure an impactful intervention, UNICEF will select partners to implement the project in the target areas in a transparent and accountable manner in line with internal guidelines. Activities for UNRWA will take place in UNRWA schools where children can feel safe and secure. Children will receive 18 sessions focused on key life skills based on their age groups. As school staff including animators, counsellors and guards are not trained to detect children and adolescents in need of protection, individual case management and psychosocial support (PSS), the project will provide training for animators to implement the UNICEF-UNRWA PSS manual and detection, counsellors on the referral to case management, guards on the basic child protection principles, safeguarding children and child protection, and a training for all on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. These trainings are proven to be critical—lessons learned from previous projects—to detect children and adolescents in need of PSS and case management as well as referral to existing specialized service providers in line with Gaza referral pathways during and after the summer activities. The project will also target refugee and non-refugee adolescents (14-17 years) inside CSOs across the five governorates. UNICEF will identify the non-refugee children and all refugee and non-refugee adolescents through coordination with partners in the target areas. The adolescent’s activities will be implemented daily including every Friday’s afternoon when UNICEF and NGO partner will be running sport tournaments and other activities in the target localities. The project activities will focus on reaching all children and adolescents in the five governorates with focus on “East Rafah, East Khanyounis, Al Buraj, East Gaza and Jabalia”. • Activity 1: 45,000 children aged 6-13 years old (60% boys) both refugee and non-refugee including injured and drop-out in Gaza will benefit from at least 18 sessions of structured school-based psychosocial support activities, and referrals to case management services inside UNRWA schools and other designated locations during the summer weeks.• Activity 2: 15,000 adolescents (70% boys aged 14-17) both refugee and non-refugee from Gaza will benefit from emergency child protection responses, including referrals to case management, psychosocial support and programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children during summer weeks including Fridays in the most marginalized locations.• Activity 3: Approximately 250 (220 UNRWA, 30 CSOs) counsellors will be trained and mobilized to provide life-skills and referrals to case management • Activity 4: at least 300 (200 UNRWA, 100 CSOs) staff (facilitators, location managers, nurses, clerks, guards) will be trained and deployed on management of activities, participation tracking, child protection, awareness rising on addressing violence against children (VAC).• Activity 5: 60,000 children and adolescents in Gaza will benefit from ERW risk education sessions inside UNRWA schools and other locations during the summer months.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In line with the principle of do no harm, school-based psychosocial activities will adhere to the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings and the INEE Minimum Standards for Education: Preparedness, Response, Recovery. Notably, the intervention will ensure that activities are highly inclusive and non-discriminatory; child friendly spaces are safe and secure promoting a participatory environment. Activities will encourage school counsellors to listen to children and value their views. In addition, safe spaces will be crucial to identify children and adolescents in need of child protection and psychosocial support. When child protection cases are detected, only trained professionals will provide specialised assistance to prevent unintended harm. Cases will be referred to case management in respect of the principles of confidentiality and in line with the existing Gaza referral pathways. UNICEF and the NGO Implementing partner will identify refugee and non-refugee adolescents in intervention areas through coordination with relevant stakeholders, local communities, and CBOs.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The intervention will ensure that activities are highly inclusive and non-discriminatory, as it targets both refugee and non-refugee students.
