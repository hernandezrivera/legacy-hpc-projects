Project Summary

PUI seeks to improve the access to free comprehensive essential primary health care, including SHR services in Monguno. To achieve this purpose, PUI proposes to implement this health projects in 4 facilities (ALGON/Kuya PHCC, Gana Ali PHCC, Waterboard clinic and Chasala PHCC) in Monguno targeting 82,271 beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 (HNO), conflict-affected and internally displaced people (IDPs) in camps and camp-like settings have limited or no access to integrated primary health care services, especially those in Borno Sate Local Government Areas of Damboa, Dikwa, Ngala, Bama, Gwoza and Monguno. Across these 6 LGAs, over 842,000 IDPs rely on only 45 functioning health facilities, or 18,700 people per facility– far short of the standard of one health facility per 10,000 people. According to Borno State 25-year Development Plan, 48% of the facilities are not functional. According to this same document, there are only 8,315 health personnel across the state, meaning a 5.8/10,000 inhabitants skilled health care worker ratio, a 5/100,000 inhabitants doctor to patient ratio, a 6.5/100,000 inhabitants nurses and midwives/patient ratioIn Monguno, according to the Health sector’s Health Resources and Services Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) online dashboard, out of the 32 existing and accessible health facilities, 6 (19%) are partially damaged, more than half of the remaining 26 health facilities have partially or not available services due to a lack of health staff and properly trained staff when present, of medical supplies, medical equipment, and financial resources to run the health facilities. This lack of staff or trained staff is also highlighted in the HNO 2023, particularly regarding Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) staff, with the limited number of trained midwives and skilled birth attendants and obstetricians leaving women facing huge barriers to access SRHR services. Access to health services is also hampered by high medical costs that are unaffordable to vulnerable groups. PUI-supported health facilities are located in areas where the water infrastructure is very often vandalized, resulting in an erratic access of the facilities to clean water.In Nigeria, it is estimated that 10 to 25% of the waste generated by medical structures are hazardous. Healthcare waste management remains unsatisfactory because of non-systematic practices in collection, storage, transportation, treatment and final disposal of healthcare waste. Healthcare solid waste are either indiscriminately dumped into public dumpsites, buried, or burnt at low temperature in open pits, with high risks of injury or infection for healthcare waste handlers, who often lack Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), and waste scavengers. Those armed by needle stick for instance are not provided with PEP and left facing risk of infections. This also has a negative impact on Nigeria’s natural resources, with soil, air, water contamination, impacting in turn people’s health.

2 - Activities

A1. Support of health facilities For this project, PUI will continue to support 3 of the PHCCs, namely ALGON PHCC, Gana Ali PHCC and Waterboard Health Clinics, with the provision of free of charge comprehensive primary healthcare services, and Chasala Dispensary, with the provision of Outpatient consultations, and referrals of critical cases to Gana Ali PHCC. A2. Rehabilitation of health facilities (WaSH in health facilities, Structures) Gaps in the WaSH sector in Monguno have been adversely affecting activities in Gana Ali and Algon PHCCs, both facilities seeing their water supply affected after the water infrastructures at the water points which supply them were vandalized. A3. Training of healthcare staff Training and capacity building of healthcare staff will be conducted either by PUI’s team or through an external facilitator from the MoH and/or other NGOs. PUI currently has an MOU with the State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) for a capacity building scheme where the agency provides upon request competent facilitators for staff capacity building. A4. Provide OPD consultations including referral support for cases needing specialized care PUI intends to provide 102,480 OPD consultations for communicable diseases, non-communicable diseases, and trauma-related cases to all individuals, irrespective of their age, gender, socio-economic background, residential status (IDPs, returnees, HC), religion, or political affiliation. A5. Support to Community Health Workers (CHWs) to implement community public health surveillance and key messaging. PUI will continue to train and supervise 20 CHWs across the 3 camps to spread key health messages, implement public health surveillance, and carry out community mobilization, sensitization, and awareness of communicable and non-communicable diseases. A6. Support safe delivery services via skilled birth attendants. PUI’s reproductive health intervention model in Monguno is aligned with Borno state Ministry of Health and the Inter-Agency Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) PUI started implementing 24/7 BEmONC services (Basic Emergency and Maternal Obstetric and Newborn Care) in Monguno in February 2022, in Gana Ali, the only facility to date to be providing such services in the city and the LGA. A7. Provision of clinical management of rape (CMR) and medical support for Gender-Based Violence (GBV) survivors In Monguno, those two services are and will be provided in two health facilities, namely Algon and Gana Ali PHCC. For survivors who present to Waterboard outpost clinic, they will be carefully referred to Gana Ali PHCC by the receiving clinician in an anonymous manner to avoid tagging. The entry point to PUI GBV service provision system for patients can be through self-referral, internal or external referral. A8. Provision of safe maternity care, including postnatal and newborn care services PUI midwifery staff, assisted by MOH midwives will conduct ante and post-natal care consultations for all women seeking these services at PUI-supported health facilities. Pregnant women will have access to ANC services in all supported facilities to ensure a safe pregnancy experience. A9. Supporting referrals of obstetric and neonatal emergency cases As part of the effort in ensuring that mothers and newborns receive the necessary care they need during emergencies, thereby contributing to the reduction of maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality, PUI has included funds for reimbursement of in-patient treatment at referral facilities for obstetric and neonatal emergencies who first come to Gana Ali PHCC and Algon PHCCA10. Training health facility staff in medical commodity supply chain management A11. Management of pharmaceuticals including, distribution, dispensing and data management All pharmaceutical commodities including drugs, medical supplies, and medical equipment, will be provided to all patients free-of-charge.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

According to PUI’s Gender and GBV analysis report (2020) mentioned above, Nigerian society is ruled by a pervasive patriarchal system, which supports male supremacy, leading to unequal gender role relations, and grants men power and control over women in both the domestic and the public spheres. A variety of gender-based violence have been reported including domestic violence (such as wife battering, rape, and assaults), sexual violence and harassment, violence against women and girls in conflict and post conflict situations, and harmful traditional practices against women and children (i.e. obnoxious widowhood rites, early marriages, and female genital mutilations among others). Because of the immediate and potentially life-threatening health consequences, coupled with the feasibility of preventing these consequences through medical care, addressing sexual violence is a priority in PUI’s areas of interventions. PUI ensures that survivors receive support and assistance across sectors in a safe, and accessible way and in accordance with IASC’s GBV Guidelines.PUI in this regard has taken some measure i to mitigate the SGBVs issues during humanitarian response:- PUI recognizes that every intervention aimed at tackling GBV can provoke harmful and potentially fatal consequences, and therefore aims at prioritizing first and foremost the survival of female beneficiaries through safety planning followed by adequate psychosocial support by trained PSS providers. Specifically, midwives (who are likely to be the first point of contact with GBV survivors) are trained in the Psychological First Aid (PFA) approach strengthening referral linkages with internal PSS service providers who in turn provide trauma-informed psychosocial support in the management of acute and on-going stress emanating out of survivor experiences, to provide some beneficial initial response to vulnerable cases. Considering the complexity of identification and referral to external organizations, PUI staff are advised against actively identifying GBV survivors, responding with the possible psychological support (including PSS sessions) only to self-identified cases. PUI also plans to conduct GBV training, protection mainstreaming training and training on CMR, for frontline health workers, to ensure better knowledge on medical treatment and referral (covering GBV basics, including among other topics safe identification and referral and handling disclosure).- PUI ensures increased accessibility and quality of SGBV multi sectoral response services and prevention activities through key messages on prevention of SGBVs and management of SGBV- PUI health and protection teams ensure the effective coordination with partners and national authorities to establish standard operating procedures and referral pathways- PUI has effective protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) networks and systems in place beside a robust child protection policy. Both policies are explained, read, accepted and signed by all the PUI staff, vendors, suppliers and service providers.- PUI ensures the engagement of communities on SGBV prevention, risk mitigation and response through the continuity of women- and girl-specific services.PUI has some integrated activities to mitigate GBVs for women at household through empowering them to:- Access to integrated response including obstetrics care, SGBV services, psychosocial support services and existing SGBV survivors’ referral pathways to other basic needs.- strengthen multi-sectoral response services, with a focus on psychosocial well-being- Access the distribution of Dignity Kits and reproductive health kits to individuals and facilities.

PUI will ensure that women, girls, men, boys, older persons, people with disabilities, people living with HIV and affected by other vulnerabilities are regularly consulted to provide relevant and accessible information about their unmet needs and services.The monthly Community Committee Meeting (CCM) gave us information about where is the gap of which services that we could use to design a new project and for collective advocacy.Furthermore, their voices will be captured, engaged, and integrated through informed advocacy in cluster/ sectoral and coordination mechanisms within the humanitarian sector. The information gathering will be conducted through community meetings, focus group discussions and interviews with the community members. Affected populations will be consulted on project design, including the design of feedback and complaints mechanisms to enable PUI to provide efficient and effective programming that meets the needs of the vulnerable population and allows them to express feedback that creates sustainable implementation through community ownership of the program.
