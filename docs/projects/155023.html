Project Summary

The project will help to increase the quality and quantity of community engagement and accountability outcomes within the Rohingya response in Bangladesh, both by providing specialist, technical support to all agencies and sectors; and by delivering direct community engagement and accountability activities with refugees and host communities.
The project comprises the continuation and expansion of the existing Common Service for Community Engagement and Accountability; community engagement activities focused on GBV and other protection-related issues; and a new co-created, cross-community initiative focused on building social cohesion and peaceful coexistence.
Together, these activities will seek to reach the entire affected population, ensuring comprehensive accessibility to life-saving information and knowledge; providing actionable, two-way and culturally appropriate multi-sectoral communication resources; and strengthening participation of, and accountability to affected populations.

Objectives

1 - Strengthen coordination and advocacy towards the integration and reinforcement of Communication with Communities capacities and community engagement to ensure comprehensive accessibility to life-saving information and knowledge on rights and available resources by affected populations, across sectors. 2 - Establish and reinforce needs-based, actionable, two-way and culturally appropriate multi-sectoral communication resources and strengthen dissemination and access to information. 3 - Strengthen participation of, and accountability to affected populations through effective community engagement, participatory approaches and functional feedback and complaint response mechanisms.

Needs

At the outset of the response, assessments showed that the affected community were overwhelmingly unable to access adequate information in the right language, or effectively provide feedback to aid providers and authorities. Assessments have also found that accountability systems were largely not well understood, not linked up to each other and often lacked a structural approach to close cases. Activities led by BBC Media Action – including the common service – have already started to help fill some of these gaps. A recent evaluation of the CwC Common Service found that 75% of the Rohingya community feel that it has become easier to access information over the past six months. It is clear, then, that both the channels and the will are present, if they can be productively mobilised. In spite of these figures, the evaluation showed that, while the Rohingya community feel better informed than when they first arrived, they still have many questions, particularly around their future - only 41% of the community said they felt informed about their options for the future and only 31% of respondents said that aid workers spoke their language. It is therefore clear that more needs to be done for the humanitarian community to gain and sustain trust by providing effective, two-way communication channels in the languages and formats (verbal, visual, and pictorial) relevant to and preferred by the communities they serve. Agency demand for the common service remains high and there is a need for it to continue, to fully capitalise on the appetite within the response for fully embedded community engagement and accountability systems. This continuation of specialist communication with communities (CwC) inputs is required, in order to support all sectors to maximize the impact of the overall humanitarian response. There are also stark CwC-related needs in specific thematic areas, which BBC Media Action’s project also seeks to address directly, outside the common service. Rohingya women and children in particular face a number of personal security and protection-related risks. Evidence – including our own formative research – points to gender-based violence as a critical issue. Lack of access to information, basic services and self-reliance opportunities exposes Rohingya – especially women and adolescent girls – to protection and potentially harmful coping mechanisms such as trafficking, exploitation, child marriage and drug abuse. Agencies have also identified other gaps, particularly in terms of information on longer-term or more intangible topics such as rights, repatriation, governance and access to legal services. There are some areas within the response where deteriorating social cohesion is causing significant tensions. These specific needs – in GBV, rights and social cohesion – will be addressed by this project, in the context of overall information provision and two-way communication and engagement. There remains a clear preference for face-to-face and verbal mechanisms and BBC Media Action’s work in the response to date has focused on providing tools and services which can enhance the effectiveness and consistency of these mechanisms. This project will build on that approach and enhance the range of materials and tools available to agencies across the response as they seek to fill these gaps.

Activities or outputs

The Common Service for Community Engagement and Accountability, delivered in partnership with Internews and Translators without Borders, will be extended into 2019, providing the following services to agencies and sectors across the whole response:- Further strengthening of feedback mechanisms, operationalising the CwC Accountability Manifesto, including through provision of linguistic support for accuracy and consistency in data collection - Collective feedback analysis service, including scale-up of existing foresight service pilot- On-demand support (training, mentoring, consultancy, technical advice, translation services, data collection) for agencies and sectors across all CwC activities, and for cross-cutting priorities (such as PSEA) - Creation of tailored, gender-inclusive information and communication materials for use by agencies across the response, in relevant languages and formats- Rumour tracking service- Narrowcast audio service and listening posts- Increased number of listeners’ groups, with training and mentoring for facilitators- Socio-linguistic research and sectoral guidance production Outside of the common service, the following activities will be directly implemented: Audio drama and face-to-face community engagement activities, focused on GBV and other protection-related issues. In partnership with NCA, and building on the current pilot, 50 weekly episodes will be produced and broadcast through a local radio partner and growing network of 300+ listening groups. Characters and storylines in the drama will seek to shift the audience’s attitudes and social norms that drive behaviours in key protection-related areas. Common underlying drivers – particularly gender norms – will be threaded through the output. The audio outputs will be supplemented with face-to-face activities, including interactive forum theatre, community dialogue and engagement events, delivered in partnership with Arts for Action. Specifically focused on community cohesion and peaceful coexistence outcomes, a co-created, cross-community media initiative will bring together refugees and host communities to create media-based outputs which explore and celebrate the culture and stories of the two communities. Participants from both communities will create short fictional or factual films together, which will be played in both communities and used as the basis for a series of community discussions. Through both the process of creation and the outputs themselves, this project will start to build social cohesion and understanding and contribute towards an overall purpose of peaceful coexistence; as well as promoting mutual respect and trust between host and Rohingya communities and promoting shared values and social participation of Bangladeshi and Rohingya members. Finally, in partnership with NRC, this project will support the provision of accurate, timely, relevant and appropriate information for the refugee and host communities related to their rights to civil and other documentation, housing, land and property and/or access to justice.
