Project Summary

The project is designed to positively increase access to identity/civil documentation (mainly birth, death, marriage, and divorce certificates), documents required for accessing essential services, and HLP including tenancy rights in the refugee camps and host communities, whilst also mitigating and addressing tensions between the Rohingya and local communities generated by HLP disputes. This objective will be achieved through the increased capacity of refugees and host communities to exercise/claim their identity and HLP rights and improved access to and use of collaborative dispute resolution mechanisms. NRC will provide legal counselling and assistance based on individual needs to enable target beneficiaries to overcome legal, procedural, and documentation challenges, including referral to specialized case management actors as it may be required; combined with information dissemination and awareness-raising initiatives to empower target beneficiaries to claim their rights and access available justice mechanisms. Improvement of the relationships between refugees and host communities, and within the Rohingya community itself, will be achieved through the local leaders` capacity building in HLP dispute resolution. Key staff, volunteers, and other stakeholders, including target communities whenever possible, will be involved to identify existing and emerging threats to safety, dignity, and rights faced by different sex and age groups among the refugee and the host populations. Special attention will be paid to threats and vulnerabilities generated or exacerbated by COVID-19 and the associated containment measures. This information will be analyzed and used for evidence-based advocacy and will also be shared externally with other protection actors and stakeholders.

1 - Identified Needs

The proposed response is informed by an analysis of needs and gaps identified in the J-MSNAs, 2021 JRP PWG`s Summary Overview, 2020 JRP, Situational, and the Response Analyses elaborated by NRC in June 2020, as well as NRC`s ICLA Programme`s field experience. Overall findings on identity documentation and HLP in the Refugee camps and host communities: Both refugee and host communities, women in particular, are not aware of the application procedures for identity documents and the potential benefits of each document; Vital events registration practices vary between camps, with CiCs having varying capacities and understandings of this aspect of their roles; There is a lack of knowledge about how to obtain/amend the “Smart”, Family Attestation, WFP Scope cards, and register vital events; Due to the low literacy level in general, and in Bangla language in particular, especially amongst women and elderly persons, most Rohingya are unable to complete the applications to CiC required to register marriage or divorce, or to amend the “Smart”, Family Attestation and WFP Scope Card. Individuals who are unable to complete the application forms pay Mahjis or other camp leaders to complete the application for them; Forgery, bribing of officials, and marriages to Bangladeshi nationals are widespread strategies adopted by Rohingya trying to obtain Bangladeshi NID; Only 61% of host community households report that all adult household members have a valid ID card. Local people living in rural/remote areas are having limited access to citizen services, including availing NID, marriage registration, and inclusion in the updated voter list; With the resumption of birth registration for Bangladeshis living in Cox`s Bazar District, there is a lack of detailed information about how to apply for the birth certificate and/or how to check the birth certificate in the online system (BRIS), as well as on how to correct errors in the birth certificate; There is an overall high level of reliance on informal justice. At the same time the quality of informal justice not aligning with basic human rights and principles, women’s interests not represented, lack of female representation in informal justice bodies; there are risks of corruption and power abuse; Many refugees, particularly those residing in the Teknaf camps, are engaged in tenancy relationships with host community landlords and face rent extortion and threats of forced eviction; On many occasions, the land rented out to refugees and I/NGOs by the local population is classified as forest land, which is prohibited under the national legal framework for any form of settlement/development; Already existing tensions between Rohingya refugees and the host community have further increased because of the reduced means of livelihoods. This has been seriously exacerbated by the impact of COVID-19 due to the reduced availability of assistance and services classified as non-essential – including legal assistance activities, and show the needs in the provision of information and legal advice (and possibly assistance) on (a) identity documentation; and (b) housing, land and property (HLP) issues; While 98% of refugee households reporting that disaster preparedness assistance/services went well (97% for cyclone response respectively), level of satisfaction with emergency situations assistance amongst host communities is significantly lower, calling for paying equal attention to the strengthening of existing disaster management systems and climate change adaptation processes in both refugee and host communities.To complement the progress and work already initiated by Protection actors to mainstream gender into the activities implemented in the camps and host communities, NRC plans to conduct gender-learning needs assessment aiming at assessing the unique protection needs of women-headed households and what impacts do they have on women`s personal safety, livelihoods, and security of tenure.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1. Information sessions for refugees and host community members explaining the importance of registering births, deaths, marriages and divorces, and respective application procedures; COVID-19-related health messages; available essential services; legal consequences of child marriage and human trafficking; ownership rights, lease agreements, other HLP rights. By receiving updated and accurate information, beneficiaries should be able to understand their situation and what services/assistance are available. The activity includes adapting information materials, location and timing of services to be accessible for women and people with disabilities. IEC materials on specific topics will be developed and will include visual messaging. Diversified entry points will be offered to target populations to reduce access challenges, including the challenges due to the COVID-19 containment measures and the humanitarian access restrictions at large. Activity 2. Provision of counselling – one-to-one advice to a beneficiary or beneficiary group on what is the best possible course of action to resolve issues related to all aspects of identity documentation, documents required for accessing essential services, and HLP rights. NRC counselling sessions are designed to enable beneficiaries to claim and exercise their rights by themselves and to pass obtained knowledge onto family and community members. Counselling services will be available both in-person (during field visits) and through the hotline.Activity 3. Provision of legal assistance, which involves different types of interventions by NRC on behalf of beneficiary facing an obstacle in obtaining his/her rights (as long as these rights are within the ICLA mandate). Legal assistance activities could include assistance with reviewing or filling out applications for birth, death, marriage, divorce, etc., accompaniment to administrative bodies, HLP case management, HLP due diligence or CDR activities. Legal assistance beneficiaries are both individuals who receive direct legal assistance and those individuals who receive legal assistance as part of a group (could include indirect beneficiaries). Special attention will be given to cases extremely of vulnerable and/or at high risk such as female-headed households, households composed predominantly of women and/or girls, older persons, persons with disabilities, persons who are a survivor or at risk of exploitation and/or eviction by the landlord; while internal and external referral and coordination mechanisms will be continuously developed, especially outside of camps. Activity 4. Capacity building (training) of key local actors from both refugees and host communities in Identity / Civil Documentation Procedures and Related Human Rights, and in Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR) techniques. Key local actors might include but are not limited to CiCs, Majhis, Imams and Masjid Committee members, Elites/Community leaders in camps; and DFO, AC Land (Ukhiya and Teknaf), UNO (Ukhiya and Teknaf), DC, and the UNP outside of camps.The CDR trainings will be delivered in a training of trainers (ToT) format. NRC will create a “capacity-building plan” for specific locations and a contextualised CDR training manual. Engagement of women and other diversity groups in the informal justice system will also be encouraged to the best extent possible.In addition, information collected by NRC during the provision of services will be used for evidence-based advocacy and further programme development and will also be shared externally with other protection actors and stakeholders within relevant coordination mechanisms. Moreover, NRC will make available its global expertise in ICLA and the experience gained in Bangladesh in LCD and HLP to technically support other service providers and relevant stakeholders on these thematic areas. All NRC`s reports and other materials will include sex, age and disability disaggregated data.
