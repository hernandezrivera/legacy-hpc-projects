Project Summary

In 2020, OCHA will continue to focus on the humanitarian crisis in the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states, with the centre of the operational response in Maiduguri, while maintaining a commitment to support the HC, humanitarian partners and the Government in Abuja, including for emergency response and preparedness as dictated by events and circumstances.

1 - Needs

Nigeria has a complex humanitarian coordination structure, with federal level mechanisms managing the national interface with the humanitarian community on all strategic issues. The newly created Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs, Disaster Management and Social Development (FMHDS) will be the main interlocutor for the international humanitarian community. At the operational level, the humanitarian actors interact with autonomous state structures with devolved functions whose mechanisms and governance structures do not necessarily mirror the federal level mechanisms. However, the mechanisms are closely interlinked and constant engagement with government oﬃcials at both federal and state levels is crucial for the success of the humanitarian response. OCHA plays a strategic role within the overall humanitarian architecture in discharging its core functions (coordination, advocacy, information management and resource mobilization) in support of the Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) and the Deputy Humanitarian Coordinator. OCHA services are required to support coordination of over 95 humanitarian partners currently operating in the BAY (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) states. OCHA will continue to consolidate its expanded presence in the BAY states, especially given the number of humanitarian workers deployed to the area which has more than doubled since the end of 2016. OCHA will continue to support both the Operational HCT and the ISWG to become more effective in the BAY states. For better coordination at the sub-state level, OCHA will continue providing the necessary support to coordination at the Local Government Area (LGA) level. OCHA will continue to explore ways to promote and sustain humanitarian access, where feasible, and reinforce CMCoord mechanisms at federal, state and LGA levels. OCHA has three operational sub oﬃces providing services in the BAY states. The oﬃce in Maiduguri supports the sub oﬃces in Yola and Damaturu and consolidates operations as one BAY response. The Deputy Head of Oﬃce based in Maiduguri oversees all three oﬃces and acts as the head of OCHA operations in the BAY states. OCHA’s presence in Abuja under the leadership of the Head of oﬃce continues to support ﬁeld operations—but its emphasis is also on supporting the Humanitarian Coordinator, UN agencies, donors and NGO partners located in the capital. This includes the HCT and other inter-agency coordination mechanisms at the Abuja level. The OCHA Abuja oﬃce is further responsible for ensuring strategic engagement with relevant federal government ministries and key interlocutors, including the FMHDS, Ministry of Budget and Planning, and the North East Development Commission (NEDC). The OCHA Humanitarian Financing Unit (HFU) based in Maiduguri manages the Nigerian Humanitarian Fund (NHF) while a Humanitarian Financing Advisor based in Abuja supports on resource mobilization for the NHF and the HRP through strengthening donor relations and building private-sector partnerships.

2 - Activities

1. Advice and support the HC and DHC on principled, timely and effective humanitarian action, thereby helping them to ensure strong and decisive leadership.2. Regular HC/DHC and HCT interaction with the relevant ministries and government humanitarian coordination mechanisms at national and state levels on operational issues.3. Public and private advocacy to raise awareness on the crisis and needs, promote respect for international humanitarian law, and bring voices of crisis-affected people to the forefront.4. Advocacy and fundraising campaigns or events in support of HCT advocacy strategy, and HCT resource mobilisation strategy including NHF fundraising initiatives.5. Conduct timely analysis on population data, including information on data disaggregated by sex and age, and develop specific analytical products.6. Establish and maintain a high quality of information and analysis from OCHA sub oﬃces on needs and response, recognizing timeliness, quality and relevance.7. Produce HNO and HRP and revise as needed. 8. Strengthen CMCoord mechanisms at federal, state and LGA levels.9. Strengthen the contextual application of the Access Monitoring and Reporting Framework (AMRF) for the BAY states.10. Facilitate operational coordination meetings and follow up on the implementation of action points in all priority LGAs.11. Support the creation of platforms to promote partnerships, coordination and leadership for collective outcomes across the humanitarian and development community.
