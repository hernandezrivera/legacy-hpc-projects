Project Summary

This project is proposing to provide emergency lifesaving integrated primary health care services to conflict-affected populations in Jere, Mafa Monguno, Damboa and Gubio LGA of Borno State and Madagali, Mubi North and South, Hong and Michika LGA of Adamawa State toward reducing mortality and morbidity of the population of concern.
Through this project, the urgent and critical needs of conflict-affected populations shall be prioritized. Rise to Inspire Africa Initiative shall be working in collaboration with the Borno State Primary Health Care Development Agency to deliver these services. The intention of this project is to prioritize maternal and child health against killer diseases while providing OPD consultations, rapid diagnosis, and treatment of common morbidities such as malaria, SRH services, and referral of patients in need of secondary/specialized health care services, mobile outreaches, etc.
Through this project, RIA will work towards strengthening the existing health system by providing support to the MCH health facilities in the targeted location. This project will also include the deployment of Mobile clinics in the remote and hard-to-reach (H2R) parts of Jere, Mafa, Monguno, Damboa and Gubio LGA of Borno State and Madagali, Mubi North and South, Hong and Michika LGA of Adamawa State communities in the respective states RIA will support MoH to ensure the continuation of health services by the deployment of health staff, capacity building/training, procurement and distribution of cholera, and malaria essential medicines and supplies. The project will also support reproductive health services, Malaria, RTI, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), ARI, Basic Emergency Obstetrics, and Neonatal Care (BEmONC). The project will facilitate the referral of patients in need of secondary/specialized healthcare facilities to Maiduguri where necessary. The funding shall also be used in strengthening cholera surveillance, risk communication, community mobilization, and the prepositioning of cholera treatment supplies. There will be a training of the CHEWs, JCHEWs and CHVs, which will be deployed into facilities with lesser human resources and form two mobile teams per LGAs that will comprise 2 CHEWs and 1 JCHEW in a team. The CHVs would be deployed in conducting Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE).

1 - Needs

In northeast Nigeria, a region already enduring a humanitarian crisis with 8.4 million people in need, over 210,000 people have been affected by the flooding, according to NEMA. Across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States, the flooding has exacerbated existing vulnerabilities for people already impacted by conflict, displacement and severe food insecurity and malnutrition. The contamination of water sources has increased the risk of waterborne disease during a severe cholera outbreak. The northeast has recorded 14,000 cases of cholera and 44 fatalities, as of 3 November 2022. An IOM flash report for the month of August 2022 on rainstorms and flooding in the region, a total of 37,475 individuals (16,393 in Borno State, 5,979 in Adamawa State and 15,042 in Yobe State) have been affected by heavy rainfalls that have led to flooding since the inception of the rainy season. Of the total individuals, Borno State accounts for 44%, Yobe State 40% and Adamawa State 16%. 14 LGAs were affected in Borno State, affecting farmlands, and shelters across several locations of the state thus exposing the Population of Concern to further risk of water-borne diseases such as Cholera, RTI, AWD, and the prevalence of Malaria. More specifically, Jere LGA, 2,562, Monguno, 2,859, and Kala Balge 218, were affected by the flooding.The crisis in the northeast region continues to become one of the world’s most severe humanitarian crises leaving over 8.0 million people in dire need of humanitarian support according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022. Of 5.0 million people across the BAY states who willneed humanitarian health care services, 1.4 million are IDPs, 1.3 million returnees, and 3 million people living in host communities. It is estimated that one in four is under 5 years of age. Women and children are the faces of the protracted crisis, as they constitute up to 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of new displacements. The gender and age dimensions of the conflict-triggered displacements are having a significant impact on the health and well-being of women and girls, especially with respect to crude mortality, morbidity, and psychosocial trauma from the fragmentation of households.Approximately 28% of IDPs (616,000), 34% of people in host communities, and 32% of returnees do not have sufficient access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene, thus exposing them to the health-related risks of Cholera and other water-borne diseases during therainy season. From 2018 to date, the region has also been plighted with a lot of life-threatening diseases and outbreaks such as Cholera (in which the state had witnessed several of these with high mortality rates and case fatality ratio), Lassa Fever, measles, and Malaria. As of 23rd October 2022, the total number of confirmed cases of Cholera was 10,196 while 9,723 have recovered and discharged. The state has recorded 328 (190 confirmed and 138 suspected) deaths. 145 cases are on admission with current bed occupancy of 611. Malaria (confirmed 8,052 and suspected 12,127) remains one of the leading causes of morbidity in the state accounting for over 38% of the reported cases, while Acute Watery Diarrhea deaths (11) were the leading cause of mortality accounting for 47.9% of the reported deaths through EWARS.The recent flooding has further increased displacement, including the influx of IDPs, refugees and returnees, into overcrowded and underserviced camps and settlements in some LGAs, mainly in Borno State, exacerbating the risk of disease outbreaks including cholera,hepatitis, meningitis, and measles.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

As a core value in planning and implementation of all programs and activities, RIA, advocate, and employ a “do no harm” approach, which implies taking cognizance of protection risks or potential violations of rights of members of target communities or simply put, DOING HARM PRINCIPLE to intended solace to community members or target beneficiaries. In addition, all staff are bound to the Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties, and responsibilities of all RIA operators, collaborators, partners, advisers, and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity, and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, and transparency. In addition, RIA shall be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero sexual exploitation and abuse. All staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project shall be trained in PSEA. They shall be familiarized with RIA‘s policy on PSEA, and they must express their unwavering commitment by signing the RIA’s PSEA code of conduct. In addition, RIA shall also lay down reporting protocols for reporting PSEA cases if, unfortunately, they occur. RIA shall also ensure that the identity of the reporters is kept very confidential. When convenient, RIA should encourage reports to be made on anonymous grounds.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

RIA will ensure accountability to affected populations through various activities. Firstly, in the present project, an accountability and quality improvement committee (AQIC) will be set up composed of community leaders, religious leaders, representatives of women, youth, and other social groups and a representative of all ethnic groups present in the community. The AQIC plans community actions, implements and acts as a feedback mechanism between RIA and the community. This committee has had monthly meetings since its inception and has discussed several issues affecting the community. During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to health, were also aired and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners. The AQIC committee is currently acting as a community sensitization agent for behavioural change on the need for visibly pregnant women to keep their clean delivery kits in a safe and hygienic environment. The RIA team is gender-balanced and speaks the local languages to ensure the information about the project is disseminated through community leaders. By doing so the target population will receive clear, relevant, and timely information regarding the project. The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback mechanism that can be received using various means, such as FGD, interviews and feedback boxes. Information on relevant services including referral pathways if available will be shared in coordination with other sectors implementing in the same location. Furthermore, accountability towards beneficiaries will be guaranteed through the application of internal tools already available, such as the RIA Safeguarding Policy which is all-encompassing of the PSEA Policy, Child Protection Policy, and Code of conduct ethics.
