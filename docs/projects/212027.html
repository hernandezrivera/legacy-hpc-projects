Project Summary

This project supports Mali Ten-year programme for the development of second-generation vocational education and training (PRODEC 2) long-term objectives of: (i) improving access to education for vulnerable people and (ii) reducing gender disparities in school. It contributes to Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 2 (end hunger) target 2.1 (Everyone has access to food). It also contributes to SDGs 1 (end poverty), 4 (inclusive and qualitative education and lifelonTng learning), 5 (gender equality) and 17 (Partnership for implementing the SDGs). WFP Mali is planning to implement a school feeding programme to relieve hunger and incentivise learning as well as children protection in some of the most vulnerable crisis affected localities in North (Menaka, Gao, Kidal, Timbuktu and Taoudenit) and Centre (Mopti, Bandiagara and Douentza). WFP plan to support total of 192,000 schoolchildren under these affected localities (among 48% of girls). Beneficiaries of the school feeding Programme are children enrolled in the basic and pre-school cycle. The assisted population under SFIE is composed of IDP, refugees and host populations. To promote social cohesion between meals will be distributed to IDP, refugees as well as host community students attended schools without any distinction of origin. In addition, during the implementation of activities, WFP will ensure that the canteen management committees will be made up of both indigenous and refugee populations at the level of all decision-making and participation bodies.

1 - Contexte

There are more than 1,500 schools closed in Mali in 2023 due to insecurity across the country. Even though some are opening due to apparent calm in several communities, most are still closed, and schools could always close again if insecurity arises. T Children in conflict-affected countries are more than twice as likely to be out of school, and specifically girls in conflict-affected countries are 2.5 times more likely to be out of school than girls in stable contexts. Harmful social norms combined with the disruption in their education expose girls to the risk of not returning to school. Parents tend to engage them in domestic work or marry them early to offset economic pressures on the family. This has increased the vulnerability of girls to various types of violence, discouraging them from continuing with their education. The school environment, where children spend 60 percent of their daytime protects them from many forms of existing and potential harm. It is, therefore, imperative that children be kept in school despite the crisis. School feeding helps keep children in school- to learn and stay away from harmful practices. Known to be high food insecure area, North and Centre regions of Mali which are hosting most of the crisis affected populations (IDPS, refugees and host populations). Indeed, the latest ENSA pointed out that at least one third of the population living in these areas are food insecure and in need of food assistance support. Therefore, the refugee’s presence is adding an additional pressure on an already difficult situation, with its impact on the nutritional status of children who may not benefit from quantitative proper feeding as well as qualitative intake to ensure their development at this age.Based on the Educational Clusters member’s field need assessment and WFP statistics linked to structural malnutrition and food insecurity leaded by conflict and population movements, it has been recommended to implement a school feeding assistance in favor of crisis affected children with specific objectives. Mali WFP Country Strategic Plan (2020-2024) aims to ensure that food-insecure populations, including school-age girls and boys, in targeted areas have access to adequate and nutritious food all year-round. Activity 3 of the Mali CSP seeks to provide nutritious school meals to girls and boys during the school year in targeted areas, in ways that support local markets and promote girls' education. Regarding the increasing level of insecurity and population movements in the Northern and Center regions of Mali because of insecurity It is important that WFP implement school feeding in emergency for affected populations and host communities in crisis-affected areas. Underpinning WFP targeting approach is the priority of equitable coverage of everyone in need of support are of special concern in emergencies. Integrating School Feeding in Emergencies (SFiE) into a food security safety net and comprehensive social protection response in crisis settings requires addressing issues of scale related to the “Leave no one behind” principle. The focus will be a complementary approach that provides the on-going targeted food assistance combined with SFiE and nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive support to enable vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls to meet their basic food and nutrition needs during crises. The expected output of SFiE is that “Children enrolled in fundamental schools in crisis-affected areas receive a ration that helps to meet their food and nutrition needs and promotes attendance in school”. As part of the nutrition-sensitive efforts, WFP will leverage the awareness sessions provided under the general food assistance on good nutritional and hygiene practices at schools as well as display the toll-free number for information and complaints (ligne verte).
