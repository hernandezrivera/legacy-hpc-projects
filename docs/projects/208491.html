Project Summary

The situation in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States continues to deteriorate as the effects of shrinking humanitarian space and increase in needs because of other natural disasters including flooding, further displacements and disease outbreaks . There are significant and severe humanitarian needs across Nigeria, caused by poverty – further deepened by high rates of inflation – lack of access to basic services, weak rule of law, widespread insecurity and criminality, and the impact of climate change. Four population groups are identified as in need of humanitarian and protection assistance across the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states: (i) internally displaced persons (IDPs) living in camps or camp-like-settings; (ii) out-of-camp IDPs living in host communities; (iii) returnees; and (iv) host communities. The latter also comprises communities affected by conflict that may not necessarily host IDPs or returnees.
Year 2023 had five themes endorsed as the key drivers of the humanitarian crisis: conflict and displacement, food insecurity and malnutrition, disease outbreaks (including cholera), floods, and camp closures and involuntary relocations and resettlement . As part of the 2023 Humanitarian response plan (HRP) for Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states, the funding appeal by United Nations and partners for 2023 is US$ 1.31 billion to respond to the critical needs of 8.3 million people needing life-saving humanitarian assistance . As at July 2023, only $379.5 million, which represents 28.9 per cent of the total requirement had been received with Food Security and Health sectors being the highest funded sectors. The humanitarian funding overview on WASH as at 31 July 2023 had WASH funded at $18.9m which represents 17% of the total requirement of $18.9 m. The shrinking humanitarian space according to European Commission is largely due to deteriorating security and restrictions imposed by the government and mistrust towards humanitarian actors. The Covid-19 pandemic has made WASH needs and response more complex because of existing vulnerabilities in BAY States. Self-organized and government-led movement of people, (new displacements and returns) have constrained development of durable WASH solutions as the outlook constantly changes. On the other hand, protracted displacement is straining host communities because of the high pressure on the existing, already-overburdened water points and sanitation facilities.
Access to sanitation is fragile from wear and tear of emergency-type construction, and seasonal flooding. WASH needs in the Yobe and Borno states entail water supply in form of deep boreholes, sanitation and hygiene sensitization. WASH NFIs are also part of the humanitarian needs especially for the population affected and displaced by floods in Yobe states. Low sanitation access raises rates of open defecation and reduced handwashing elevates the risk of a major outbreak within the susceptible community. A critical gap for the sector is sanitation for persons with special needs, women and children to ensure safety and equitable access; solving these issues of proximity and design of the facilities is likely to require additional resources OCHA, 2021. Effects of extreme weather conditions have worsened the situation.

1 - Needs

Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Majority of the project location has the majority of thedisplaced population source their water from unprotected/open water sources which areprone to contamination. Poor water quality at water sources is due to multiple factorsincluding open defection around the water points and limited community management ofexisting water points, leaving them prone to algae contaminations and pollution fromwaste debris in unprotected water sources. Recent WASH assessments findings within NRC target populations indicate that current volumes of water supplied to beneficiaries is not far above ‘survival levels’ as defined in the Sphere Standards. Whereas rainfall data, perennial flooding and static rivers levels would indicate that far larger quantities of water are available for use, not just in drinking water but for washing cleaning and cultivation. Bringing safe clean water closer to the point of use can have profound implications for women and girls in many ways and their safety and dignity can be better assured. Existing sanitation facilities are either full and overflowing, some latrines are without privacy and withoutdoors, abandoned or in disrepair, and often there is a high user to latrine facility ratio especially as a result of new influx brought about by the needs displacement from ongoing displacements. Drawing upon numerous internal assessments , frequent updates on the emergency WASH needs from the past and recent displacement, meetings held with partners in the field and through consultation at country level, certain recurrent themes quickly become apparent. A quick survey of schools across Adamawa and Borno States and in particular those visited by NRC in its areas of operation usually indicate inadequate levels of hygiene awareness amongst pupils. This reflects what is happening in the adult population and why open defaecation is so prevalent. Consequently, inside and outside of schools, NRC needs to be more inclusive and participatory in its approach to hygiene promotion. To this end, the WASH strategy prioritises the introduction of Community-Led Total Sanitation, WASH ‘Em Approach and Children’s Hygiene and Sanitation Training - CHAST. In consideration of the volatile context, NRC needs to retain enough capacity to respond to new emergencies and to work innovatively with the WASH team to respond quickly and effectively. Each of the abovementioned interventions in the response plan address the necessary transition from emergency to durable solutions. This approach to hygiene awareness raising amongst beneficiaries adds weight to the message. Then, by increasing quantities of available water, NRC’s overall approach is linked to livelihoods and food security, allowing people to cultivate their own kitchen gardens or larger areas of land. And finally, through capacity building at all levels and within NRC we draw on the experience of the whole WASH team to deliver significant benefits to all.

2 - Activities

The response strategy plans to facilitate and increase the access of WASH facilities to affected people, including those with special needs and the elderly. WASH response will be done in full package as Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene. Provision of safe drinking water: Sustainable and equitable access to drinking water will be ensured through the construction of dignified and secure hydraulic WASH infrastructures. NRC will construct new water points and rehabilitate dilapidated water points to address the gaps associated with access to adequate water supply. The water points in this case include shallow wells, deep wells alongside improvements of surface water supplies, tap stands and shallow wells in the Adamawa, Borno and Plateau state. Additional water supply works will include rehabilitation/construction of new water points in form of tapstands in the schools. Water quality, delivery and monitoring: NRC will also conduct periodic water quality analysis on existing water sources points for bacteriological, physical and chemical parameters from a competent or certified laboratory. The water quality tests will also be conducted before handing over of any new or rehabilitated water point. NRC will continuously sensitize the community on other traditional water quality management options notably boiling of drinking water to ensure that the community is able to continue with water quality management at household level even in the absence of aqua tabs. Provision of appropriate sanitation and handwashing facilities: In order to ensure that interventions are gender responsive, latrines will be located in safe locations within reach of the households, while ensuring that adequate privacy is provided. NRC will consult with women and girls before agreeing on appropriate latrine locations. NRC will also guide on the design, building materials, size, while also ensuring that the target beneficiaries will feel safe while using those sanitation facilities. In addition, NRC will promote construction of family/household latrines as opposed to communal latrines that have proven difficult to clean and maintain from the lessons learnt on previous assessments especially in the implementation of the current projects in the three states. Menstrual hygiene management: NRC will combine hardware interventions designed to address material deprivations such as the provision of absorbents (menstrual hygiene kits), and ensuring provision of improved Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. The software interventions will address deficits in knowledge of menstruation and management by providing education. Other interventions will include: • Focused group discussion and gender analysis to collect feedback on appropriate and preferred culturally sensitive approaches on safe disposal and management of menstrual waste to avert environmental health risks. • Partnership with local organizations demonstrating Menstrual Hygiene Management capacities will be explored for consideration as part of localization agenda. Solid waste management and vector control: Solid waste management systems shall be introduced gradually and supported through awareness-raising to promote community-led waste management using simple waste collection pits, soak away pits for wastewater and water collection point for re-use. Dump sites will be constructed in schools in line with SPHERE standards and in consultation with local solid waste service providers and ministry of health departments aligned to solid waste management. Markets based programming (MBA) – Markets: NRC will deliver on this through the use of cash & vouchers and use of the service providers notably contractors, subject to proper markets assessments. The use of vouchers will be preferred over in-kind as it gives the beneficiaries a variety of choices to purchase the actual items needed and in the desired brands.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Within this project, men, women, girls and boys in displaced and host communities willbe involved in the project, especially women who are excluded from decision-makingcircles, while ensuring it will not cause any harm. NRC will sensitize men during projectactivities, on the importance of women’s participation and explain the rationale ofNRC’s focusing on female and children headed households. Females will participate inthe same way as males in the identification of beneficiaries. Girls and women will beconsulted about the location of latrines to ensure their protection and privacy. Men willalso be sensitized on the importance of the participation of women, girls and boys indecision making in IDP settlements and host communities. NRC will conduct priorsecurity checks and organize distributions of assistance in safe places wherebeneficiaries can access without fear. NRC will also continue to work on communityacceptance making a clear difference between NRC’s assistance and the government’sone. The Do No Harm principle will guide all activities to ensure safe and effectiveparticipation of women, men, girls and boys. The selection of beneficiaries will bebased on well-defined vulnerability criteria, exposure to protection risks, people at riskof eviction and people with less access to humanitarian assistance. NRC will target theareas most affected by displacement in accordance with the HRP and data collectedfrom local NGOs, local and religious leaders. NRC will carry out this project regardlessof status and will provide assistance to all categories of vulnerable and at-risk peopleaffected by the crisis
