1 - Needs

NCA’s assessment (2021) showed that lack of access to adequate and safe water supply and appropriate sanitation infrastructure, as well as poor hygiene behaviour, pose a threat to the health and survival of the most vulnerable. Increased demand in rural areas of return on already limited water infrastructure is resulting in lower water supply and greater dependence on unsafe water sources. The proposal target one refugee’s settlement in South Darfur who rely on NCA-operated water supply systems for clean domestic water supply services. According to NCA’s data, average water provision has improved slightly over the past years, but some settlements have yet to meet Sphere standards. Inflation and shortage of fuel to pump water has in 2020 eroded some of the progress previously made. Stress on the existing WASH coverage because of the increased population and its concentration, aging and damaged infrastructure and new found WASH gaps exposed by the COVID-19 pandemic, have also contributed to the rise in the number of people in need of 2021. Unless underlying factors and drivers of these needs are addressed, the number of people in need will continue to grow year on year. The decline in service provision by the public sector is forcing the population to seek health services at the private sector that is dominating the scene with 69.3 per cent of current health expenditure (National health recovery and reform policy and strategic plan NHRRP 2020-2022). According to the 2021 multi-sectorial needs assessment MSNA 72.3 per cent of the population had to pay in full for their health services despite a reported coverage by the national health insurance fund of 67 per cent. During 2021 the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach 43 per cent compared to 57 per cent during 2020. The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels with overall GAM levels of 13.6 percent. The situation is more critical in 64 localities that are classified with very high prevalence of acute malnutrition of 15% and above as per WHO emergency levels classification of which 9 localities fall into catastrophically levels.High malnutrition rates remain, driven by poverty, weak WASH and health systems, and high prices. In 2020, there has been an increase in the cost of basic foods. Recent IPC data show a large decline in the proportion of people who could afford the local food basket. This contributes to an inadequate food intake, which negatively impacts children’s growth from early an early age. Approximately 522,000 children under five are severely malnourished in Sudan. Protection: The recent political changes in Sudan has increased the space for addressing issues related to Gender-based Violence (GBV). In line with NCA’s global strategy, NCA Sudan has launched a program to contribute to ending all forms of GBV in the target populations. The Darfur region has been negatively affected by protracted conflicts with extensive use of violence, sexual violence, trafficking (including the use of child soldiers), and burning and looting of property. A high percentage of the population has been, or is, exposed to trauma due to the long-lasting conflict, and the prevalence of psychological stress in Darfur is high. While the need for GBV interventions increased, Sudan in the past hardly had any space to address GBV and to develop structures, services, and methodology to neither prevent nor provide help for those affected. Interventions are necessary to support communities and individuals to establish solid systems to deal with protection issues in their own communities and reduce threats to vulnerable populations. NCA has continued to support survivors with access to medical services, but this is not enough.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

With the main focus to make the WASH facilities resilient to the impact of the changing environmental (climatic) conditions, ongoing initiatives of planting trees around water sources inside the settlements will continue to be undertaken. In all the camps, indigenous and drought resistant trees will be planted by communities and will be taken care of for proper growth. Apart from improving the outlook of the settlements, the trees will have greater positive impacts in making up the environmental conditions better and will also help reduce soil erosion and enhance groundwater recharge. In 2021, a total of 700 trees were planted in the settlements in both South and Central Darfur States where NCA is having its interventions at. For Health and Nutrition Sector: NCA will strive to maintain global standards for disposal of harmful biomaterials or non-biomaterials within the health facilities through construction/maintenance of safe disposal areas for incineration, construction/maintenance of pits for the placenta, sharps, and bottles disposal at all health facilities supported by NCA while using the existing government systems for disposition of expired drugs.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The NCA DP programme will follow the ACT child protection (CP) and CAFOD Child Protection (2015) policies to ensure children are protected and treated equally irrespective of race, religion, gender, age or abilities. NCA also follows the Code of Conduct for the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and NGOs in Disaster Relief (CoC) for staff and partners with guidance on appropriate and expected standards of behaviour, including with children. NCA & partners will during the first quarter of 2021 complete CP safety audits to assess gaps that might need to be addressed to ensure that children are protected and included in all aspects of decisionmaking affecting their well-being. NCA will strive to ensure safety, inclusiveness, and protection of children in camps and host communities. The DP will create awareness to staff, visitors, and other stakeholders about child protection protocols to make sure that the wellbeing of children is secure and their right to access basic services is understood. The DP additionally strives to create child friendly spaces where children (including any unaccompanied children) can come together, play and talk comfortably. The multi-sectoral programmes are designed to address the immediate and strategic needs of children. NCA has a strong GBV sector with an international GBV advisor leading the overall implementation as well as national officers who are based in both Darfur and Khartoum
