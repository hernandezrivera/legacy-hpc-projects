Project Summary

This project aims to deliver early childhood care and development with basic education till 14 years aged children through diverse interventions for 2023. Total Nos of targeted children are 15630. Among them 13300 children have been targeted from Host community and 2330 children has been targeted from Rohingya community. To reach the targeted learners 11 locations of Teknaf and Ukhiya have been selected from host community. Total learners of 4-5 years are targeted 8300 (Female: 3984 & Male: 4316) and 6-14 years old learners targeted – 5000 (Female: 2500 & Male: 2500). Whereas from Rohingya community total learners targeted 2330 at camp 24, 26 and 27.
As a result, the following actions will be taken:
Access: - To guarantee play-based preparation support for the establishment of a play-based integrated ECCD center for 4-year-old children. Here, there will be integration between the sectors of nutrition, WASH, child protection, and health. 8300 pupils in total will fall into the 4- to 5-year-old age range. There will be 70 ECCD centers built for the benefit of the children.
To give our children, ages 6 to 14, access to a facility for informal education. The B2S (Back to School) program will be introduced to the out-of-school children, and the curriculum and teaching strategy will be developed in accordance with their needs. There will be 5000 students total at Teknaf who are aimed for ages 6 to 14. To rehab the students, 42 centers will be required. For the Rohingya camps at Camp 24, 26 and 27 Total numbers of children targeted are 2330 age level 6-14 years. Therefore, 38 learning centre needs to be established.
Equity and Inclusivity: Teachers, staff capacity building on equity and inclusion, addressing CwD need in the classroom using IEP, gender and inclusion responsive training for teachers and SMCs, inclusive sports and recreation, rehabilitation support through referral to cater to the needs of children, engagement of OPDs/Self-Help Group and accessibility actions at school and community.
Increasing the enrollment of adolescents in schools and providing accessible facilities for adolescent students. Support enrollment drives and need-based interventions to ensure that they continue their education.
Quality Learning: Initiatives with teachers will be taken to build capacity for quality teaching and learning. Where necessary, logistical support is offered. Teachers, staff capacity building on equity and inclusion, addressing CwD needs with IEPs, gender and inclusion responsive training, inclusive sports and recreation, community engagement support. Separation incentives will be supported on a contextual basis for the adolescent girl's continued behavior.
Community Engagement: Participation in the community will compel the kids to stay in school. Parents' conscious effort will be necessary for children under the age of 4 to grow and develop. The key task in this regard will be community engagement activities like parent meetings, SMC meetings, community involvement with varied stakeholders, mother awareness sessions, SRHR awareness sessions, etc.
System Strengthening: Stakeholder involvement will be crucial to preserving the project's overall operations. A feedback mechanism will be developed. Meetings with the SMC and PTA can be strengthened by active attendance. Workshops with a variety of stakeholders, including the Upazila Education Office. Sector cooperation with numerous stakeholders will help this program continue over time. Along with strengthening the educational system at the community level, this project will also establish and implement an inclusive school improvement plan and enhance the school's monitoring and tracking system with an emphasis on gender and disability-specific statistics. Additionally, fostering communication and conversation between the host and the Rohingya through dialogue will promote social cohesiveness for all parties involved and open up prospects for both growth and development.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to the information provided by the education sector, there are clear signs that the children in Ukhiya and Teknaf who are in early childhood, primary, and secondary education are out of school to some extent and there is a gap providing quality education. Only 2555 people aged 4-5 years old were found in the 36017 total population, or 7% of all early aged children. The education sector examines the situation and has set a target of 10,803 for this year (Sector Gap analysis). More assistance for early childhood has to step up with high-quality activities and interventions in order to boost the implementation. Establishing an Integrated ECCD program can therefore be a solution for offering services in the health, hygiene, protection, and nutrition. Similar to this, a sizable number of school-aged adolescents were discovered not attending school in the Teknaf area. There are 1,46,007 populations in total for the age group 6 to 14 years, of which 25,231 represent 17% of the total. Furthermore, there are 821 children aged 6 to 14 in Teknaf, which is an unacceptably small proportion of the city's total population of 80,907 (6 to 14-year-olds). There is a clear support gap that needs to be filled in order to give these children high-quality assistance. With B2S (Back to school curriculum), the informal education system will be the answer for keeping children in school and ensuring their access. Some needs that are still present in Ukhiya and Teknaf are recognized from the J-MSNA (https://reliefweb.int/report/bangladesh/joint-multi-sector-needs-assessment-j-msna-bangladesh-rohingya-refugees-may-2021) that was held most recently. There is a lack of funds to cover tuition and other related costs even after accounting for inflation. Second, there is a COVID-19 infection risk while traveling or at school, and third, the children have fallen behind too much.For children to return to school, to decrease dropout rates, to limit the risk of child labor, and to enroll adolescent children, there is a need for awareness-raising and community involvement. The biggest obstacle to children acquiring an education may be a lack of money or availability to both in-person and remote instruction. Project activities must take physical education and distance learning initiatives into consideration.For children between the ages of 4 and 5, attention must be paid to nutrition education, hygienic upkeep, and neat and tidy activities, as well as the development of the parents' skills and capacity for childcare at home. Children under the age of four are particularly excluded from educational opportunities because enrollment in the host community begins at age level 5. The education of adolescent child, especially adolescent girls, should also be taken into consideration. Contextual incentives must be identified, parents and community leaders must be in constant communication, and support for menstrual health services must be offered. Inclusion is also another crucial component. All children must have access to schools. Children with disabilities (CwD) must find and adapt disability inclusive TLM (Teaching Learning Materials), pilot a tool to assess the learning outcomes of children with disabilities, and create an action plan for all institutions that addresses the intersection of gender and disability. There is no alternative to arranging a series of dialogues and conversations between the host community and Rohingya people to assure secured societal harmony. The educational systems in Rohingya-Host communities require sustained, and a comprehensive approach. In order to enhance service delivery for both the Host and Rohingya communities, there is also a need to improve collaboration between government and non-governmental groups. Under the direction of the education sector, resolving issues such learner duplication, integrated services, and inter-agency or organization Cross learning sharing for quality implementation is equally vital.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project emphasizes rationalization principles when choosing the geographic regions and takes into consideration zero duplication while carrying out the interventions and providing assistance for the children in order to ensure openness and accountability. The adoption of the integrated approach has been prioritized. In order to meet our goal, we have focused on the ECD sector and just the host communities (Ukhiya and Teknaf), where there is a great demand and learning deficit. The target population for this initiative was the 4- to 5-year-old age group in the host community. Additionally, a very specific objective has been established for the children aged 6 to 14 specifically in the Teknaf area, which is 11% (or 5000 pupils) of the overall JRP target. A digital application will be used to store and distribute learner data (Digisys management system). It will guarantee no duplication, accountability, and sustainability over the long term, as well as transparency in project delivery. Only educational activities that don't interfere with other multisectoral activities are the focus of this project. Additionally, there are activities such as connection meetings with all partners to practice learning exchange under the Education Sector guidance. Friendship’s Best practices e.g. Code of Ethics, Clean LC- Clean Home, Wall magazine etc. which are incorporated with the project design. Digital School Information System (DigiSIS) will be used to maintain attendance and particulars of students and teachers as well as to track their performance and lessons uploaded. Lessons will be recorded in a well-equipped studio to be established in Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar by expert hiring from the reputed organization more qualified and have more experience. In classroom, the facilitators will play the recorded lessons through laptop, support and conduct hands on activities to ensure uniformity in the lessons and quality of education.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project is intended to guarantee participation by including CwD inclusion, community involvement to reduce dropout rates, activities to minimize child labor, and ensuring enrollment of teenage children, particularly female children. A child protection risk assessment is also planned to close the gap, and appropriate action will be done based on the situation. This project intends to spread information on child protection as part of an educational program, such as separation and trafficking prevention or mine-risk education. The project will create shared referral systems with child protection professionals. This project will make sure that the learning environment is physically secure, including having first aid supplies on hand and teachers who are trained in first aid, well-known emergency evacuation procedures, equipment and facilities in good condition, and staff restrooms that are separate for boys and girls. Positive discipline and non-violent classroom management techniques

3.2 - Gender

The project also intends to train teachers and SMCs in gender and inclusion-responsive teaching methods, promote inclusive sports and recreation, and provide rehabilitation help through referral to meet the needs of children with significant support requirements. Support the enrollment drive to make learning accessible for adolescent students. To ensure that teaching and learning have an equal impact on girls and boys, women, and men, as well as to eliminate gender stereotypes and advance gender equality, this project implemented gender-sensitive policies, planning, and learning environments. It also mainstreamed gender issues in teacher training and curriculum monitoring processes. Adolescent girls will receive dignity kits. Menstrual healthcare services and Gender segregated toilets with for adolescent girls will receive special attention. Equally significant will be the organization of a community wide SRHR awareness session.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Activities that promote community involvement, such as parent meetings, SMC meetings, mother awareness sessions, SRHR awareness sessions, etc., will be the top priority for ensuring that children stay in school. Parents' conscious effort will be necessary for children under the age of 4 to grow and develop. Additionally, there will be a child protection policy that is available to all staff members and a code of conduct outlining how adults should behave around children. Projects will establish designated child protection focal points to oversee existing arrangements. Any events involving children will undergo risk assessments for child protection, and necessary data and consent will be gathered. Where appropriate, e-safety measures should be implemented. All agreements must have a clause stating that a child protection policy exists or that you will abide by the organization's policy statement. Special measures will be put in place to protect the personal safety of girls and women wit

3.4 - Diversity

To better understand how the initiative may improve the educational system in Ukhiya and Teknaf more inclusive to all pupils, this research will investigate a larger range of diversity dimensions. Students' ethnic backgrounds, native populations, national minorities (if any), gender, sexual orientation, and special education requirements will all be considered throughout the enrollment period. The Project intends to investigate how these aspects interact with one another as well as with more general socioeconomic and geographic issues. In order to promote equity and inclusion in education, the project will identify creative and effective policy initiatives and practices, facilitate the sharing of lessons learned, and disseminate this knowledge among sector and sector partners. It will also identify policy options for policy makers to consider through learning sharing workshops with local government and educational authorities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Stakeholder' engagement including SMC bodies of the school with PTA (parents teachers association), engagement of OPDs/Self-Help group for community sensitization and accessibility actions in decision-making and feedback mechanism to be practiced more which allow them to understand feedback channels, give feedback without facing difficulties. KII, FGD etc. would be held with community to accelerate the process and get their issues resolved. At each school feedback mechanism box will be established. Common feedback platform will be utilized for any inter-sectoral issues come from community feedbacks. For program implementation, intra-sectoral coordination will be followed so that no duplication of service delivery will be confirmed, information sharing will be unique and community engagement through diverse activities can be effective.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Given that the area is vulnerable to disasters, it intends to provide an opportunity for the organization of capacity-building training, staff orientation, and community mobilization in accordance with governmental authorities. In order to mitigate climate change, the project will start planting trees across the neighborhood and closely collaborate with the DRR sector to adopt and sensitize the neighborhood with effective communication trees and functioning DRR plans. With community stakeholders, this initiative seeks to provide DRR training in 11 batches to every union council in Ukhiya and Teknaf. Additionally, teachers will receive DRR training so that pupils can understand it and follow the functioning DRR plan. With the creation of a communication tree and a DRR contingency plan, 150 schools will be covered. Climate and environmental issues will be discussed and spread through a variety of community sessions, such as parents’ meetings, PTA meetings, SMC meetings, etc.
