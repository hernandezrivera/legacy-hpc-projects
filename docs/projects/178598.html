Project Summary

This project aims to support the SNFI response to the immediate needs of the most vulnerable displaced populations in northwest Syria through the (1) distribution of essential non – food items (NFIs) and (2) improve the basic infrastructure and shelter conditions in and out of camps.
The distribution of NFI activities include:
I. Provision of essential NFIs to 60,000 households (300,000 individuals) in northwest Syria through IOM’s rapid response mechanism (RRM) in coordination with the SNFI and CCCM clusters (Sub activity 1.1.1) through both in-kind and cash modalities.
II. Provision of tents to 20,000 households (100,000 individuals) in northwest Syria through IOM’s RRM in coordination with the SNFI and CCCM clusters (sub-activity 1.3.2).
III. Providing procurement, storage and logistics support to humanitarian partners operating inside Syria under the Single Source Procurement (SSP) pipeline, supplying 58,572 NFI kits to reach 292,860 individuals (Sub activity 1.1.1).
IV. Provision of 30,000 winter cash payments for NFIs and winter clothing, reaching 150,000 individuals (Winterization) (Sub activity 1.2.1).
The basic infrastructure and shelter activities include:
I. Rehabilitation of 2,000 unfinished shelter units (Sub Activity 1.4.2)
II. Repair of 2,000 damaged shelter units (Sub Activity 2.1.1)
III. Winterization of 4,000 family tents through internal thermal insulation, external weatherproofing, and raising the ground raising and levelling through gravelling (Sub Activity 1.2.2)
IV. Repair of 1,200 damaged shelter units in collective centres (Sub Activity 1.4.1)
V. Construction of 600 transitional shelter units (Sub Activity 2.1.3)
VI. Camp infrastructure upgrades for 30 informal sites. Infrastructure works include gravelling of roads, construction/rehabilitation of drainage channels and culverts, gravelling of tents off the ground (Sub Activity 1.3.3)
For the shelter activities, the project intends to reach 109,000 individuals in Idleb and Aleppo governorates.
Overall, the project intends to reach 1,044,000 individuals targeting most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls and prioritize family members with high vulnerabilities according to the beneficiary selection criteria (IDP status, no source of income and support, persons with disabilities, elderly, female/single/child-headed households) and identified priority geographic locations).

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, 5.9 million people are in need of Shelter and 4.7 million in need of NFIs across Syria, with the greatest needs concentrated in the northwest where 1.6 million and 2.5 million people are in severe or extreme need of NFIs and shelter respectively (PiN numbers, SNFI cluster). Financial support, NFIs and shelter are the most needed forms of assistance by IDPs, after food, and over 90% of families have reduced access to NFI’s compared to 2020, with unaffordability being the main driver (SNFI Cluster data; HNO, 2021). Additionally, 47% of IDP HHs living in collective centres reported living in damaged centres and 31% of HH residing in damaged buildings reported that they were unable to afford shelter repairs (IDP Report Series 2020, Shelter Conditions). IDPs lack basic household items, and many IDP sites have experienced serious flooding causing widespread damage to household items and tents. The CCCM Cluster reported that more than 152,700 IDPs were affected by flooding between 14 January and 3 March 2021. The S/NFI Cluster reports that over 35% of IDPs in Idleb and Aleppo governorate have been displaced prior to 2018, and more than half of IDPs across Syria have been displaced for more than five years (OCHA Situation Report No.20). An HNAP report noted that HHs who were displaced for less than a year were more likely to be pushed to return (71%), compared to (49%) those displaced for five years or more (Returnee Report Series, February 2021). Thus, it is essential to provide more dignified, transitional shelter to those who have endured displacement for years and are less likely to return. This is particularly pertinent as 28% of IDP HHs in NWS live in tents (IDP Report Series 2020).

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM will work with established IPs and coordinate with the S/NFI Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure needs are addressed and there is no duplication. IPs will select locations and beneficiaries in coordination with clusters on the basis of emerging needs and vulnerability assessments and will be verified by TPMs.For NFI, tent, and cash distributions, IOM will continue to work through its RRM partners to rapidly respond to urgent emerging needs. IOM will procure goods through existing LTAs with trusted suppliers. These will be stored in IOM’s warehouse in Gaziantep or prepositioned inside Syria. IOM will validate requests and release the items for distribution. For Cash for NFIs, IOM will request market assessments prior to approval. IOM will continue to procure and supply NFIs to selected partners within Syria through the Single Source Procurement (SSP) pipeline. Community and technical assessments will be conducted for shelter repair and rehabilitation activities, which will offer one-year rent-free to beneficiaries, while also adapting designs for PwD. HLP due diligence will be conducted for all relevant activities. Camp infrastructure upgrades will be done in flood-affected sites and address the needs. Different tent insulation will be provided (internal, external, ground), coordinating with protection partners to ensure specific needs of PwD are identified. IOM will conduct beneficiary selection based on vulnerability criteria, giving priority to HHs with PwD. IOM and its IP will identify suitable land and develop technical designs in line with SPHERE and the S/NFI’s guidelines for the transitional shelter units, and provide basic infrastructure works.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In NFI/tent/cash distributions, as well as for the construction of the transitional shelter units, tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees is a risk. IOM will mitigate this by involving all stakeholders, communicating the selection criteria, distributing according to verified needs, and establishing a complaint mechanism. Given the unstable security situation in NWS, there is the risk that conflict may impede humanitarian access to certain locations. IOM continuously monitors the security situation and has contingency plans in place, and IOM and IPs maintain communication with local authorities. Additionally, some of the access risks associated with transshipments could be mitigated through the use of cash modalities. For camp infrastructure upgrades and rehabilitation and repair of buildings, there is a risk of eviction of IDPs from sites due to HLP issues. This risk can be mitigated by only starting works once all HLP due diligence has been conducted and written ownership has been received and verified. Additionally, for rehab works, a tenancy agreement clause should be included in any agreements or MoUs signed with the owner of the building. For the repair of collective centres, the physical safety of beneficiaries/communities can be an issue due to the structural condition of the building. Mitigation measures, therefore, include ensuring structural integrity and safety of the buildings targeted.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

For all activities, the risk of SEA by IPs and their sub-contractors exists during the physical implementation phase. For this, key mitigation measures include having all IPs ensure that their staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct, have taken part in PSEA training, have a dedicated complaint mechanism in place, and that TPM teams monitor both the registration of beneficiaries and distributions to ensure activities are carried out in a principled manner.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks include access restrictions due to the shifting of frontlines, aid diversion by armed groups, loss of assets, non-renewal of the UNSC resolution, inflation of market prices (particularly fuel) and COVID-19. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will be in regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents and conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations. IOM also has a dedicated access unit that monitors and facilitates principled access for partners within Syria, and IOM will continue to participate in OCHA’s access working group. On aid diversion and loss of assets, IOM will ensure warehouses are securely guarded and give awareness sessions to staff on how to handle attempted aid diversion, while also ensuring that IPs provide timely incident reports to its access unit and share the information with relevant partners. For a potential closure of Bab Al-Hawa, IOM procures almost all shelter materials from inside Syria, with the exception of insulation rolls and plastic sheets from Turkey. NFIs are thus the main items transhipped, and IOM can preposition these inside Syria before the resolution is up for renewal. To mitigate against the inflation of prices, IOM will conduct strong and constant market monitoring and is exploring non-renewable energy options to reduce reliance on fuel. Additionally, there is the risk that beneficiaries will sell in-kind items or use cash for unintended purposes which IOM will mitigate by tailoring cash to SMEB needs and distributing items to those with verified need of S/NFIs but whose basic needs are already met. The risk of COVID-19 infection to beneficiaries and staff will be mitigated through IPC measures.
