Needs

South Sudan continues to struggle with the impact of its long-standing conflict and the displacement that has resulted and persists until this day. With 1.9 million estimated to remain displaced as of September 2018, and approximately 2.5 million remaining displaced abroad – but potentially considering to return if and when security and the socio-economic situation in the country improves – the young nation faces unparalleled mobility dynamics which warrant close observation. Whilst displacement is becoming increasingly protracted, there is also a visible upwards trend in the number of returns from within the country and abroad. In the context of a fluctuating displacement and return landscape, IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix offers a common service to provide humanitarian partners with timely, regular, and reliable information that supports evidence-based decisions and coordinated life-saving assistance to people in need. The collection, analysis, and dissemination of information on the evolving needs of affected populations in South Sudan remains critical to enable targeted provision of humanitarian response in the locations where it is most needed. It is critical to have a comprehensive overview of the comparative needs across the country. Continued primary data collection on multi-sectoral needs, displacement and returns, intentions and perceptions, is thus indispensable. Humanitarian response for IDPs remains strongly focused on the Protection of Civilian (PoC) sites, even though it is only 10% of IDPs who are hosted there. Increasingly, the call to expand services outside the PoC sites is heeded, but responders lack information on the numbers, locations and multi-sectoral needs of displaced populations in host community settings. Through mobility tracking, DTM aims to fill this information gap, seeking country-wide coverage with baseline assessments as well as village, neighbourhood and site level assessments, to inform a comparative analysis of needs and enable holistic response to IDPs and returnees alike. To complement the mobility dynamics and sectoral needs information captured through mobility tracking, it is necessary to collect information at key transit hubs and border points to gain a better understanding of the day-to-day movement of mobile populations and the decision-making factors which underpin their journeys. Flow monitoring allows a better understanding of points of origin and destination, fluctuations in the scale of movement over time, feeding into country-level as well as regional analysis on migration trends. Flow monitoring at PoC sites is critical to identify new dynamics such as beneficiaries seeking livelihood activities outside the displacement sites, undertaking visits to check on their property, and moving seasonally to plant or harvest. Understanding such dynamics is key for strategic planning on durable solutions and in order to identify possibilities to support safe returns. Intention and perception surveys are another tool which needs to be rolled out more regularly to understand how the changing political and socio-economic landscape may affect people’s decision-making process with regards to returns. In a context of limited resources and widespread needs, it is also critical to have accurate planning figures and mechanisms that ensure accountability to affected populations. Through biometric data management, DTM supports assistance providers in conducting targeted distributions. New biometric registrations, as well as regular verification and authentication of already registered beneficiaries, are necessary to ensure that assistance can continue to be provided in a targeted and accountable manner.

Activities

DTM will expand mobility tracking activities to reach country-wide coverage, and publish bi-monthly reports detailing numbers, locations and priority needs of IDPs and returnees, as well as providing comparative analysis of mobility dynamics over time. DTM will continue to conduct flow monitoring at transit hubs, border points and displacement sites across the country, in order to provide timely and comparative analysis on migration routes, return trends, circular migration, and the factors driving movement. In consultation with partners, DTM will identify priority areas for ad-hoc data collection exercises, such as village assessment surveys (VAS) to provide an in-depth understanding of infrastructural gaps in areas or high return; intention and perception surveys to understand pre-conditions for return and help predict future return movements; and in-depth needs assessments to facilitate coordinated multi-sector responses. DTM will continue to biometrically register new beneficiaries based on demands of partners and in close coordination with WFP, whilst conducting verification and authentication exercises to ensure beneficiary records remain up to date and relevant for the provision of targeted assistance. Expansion of biometric registration in hard-to-reach areas will help to avoid duplications in distribution and enable food assistance to reach more beneficiaries in the areas where it is most needed. To enhance joint analysis and create a solid evidence base for operational decision-making, DTM will work closely with other members of the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG), thus supporting the ICWG’s decision-making both for immediate, life-saving interventions and for longer-term strategic planning to pre-empt and mitigate risks and to reach those most in need. In order to enable the above activities, DTM will maintain its Juba-based team of data, analysis, research and reporting experts, as well as operations staff in Juba and sub-offices to implement data collection activities in the field. DTM will continue to expand its network of enumerators, locally recruited to ensure enhanced reach for data collection even in hard to access areas. DTM will regularly produce reports and disseminate findings to the humanitarian community through its website, mailing list, and presentation of findings in meetings.

Consultations with affected people

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

DTM regularly consults with humanitarian partners and with other IOM units to identify how the unit can best support information needs of partners. It further consults with its locally recruited staff to identify paths for expanding data collection in difficult to reach areas. Through its data collection, DTM is in touch with affected people and captures their views on which gaps in services need to be filled most urgently. DTM thus seeks to enhance accountability to affected people by providing other actors with the needed information to trigger an adequate response to identified needs.

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Direct beneficiaries are humanitarian partners benefitting from the information disseminated, which has an indirect effect of affected populations who benefit from subsequent targeting and response. Humanitarian partners are familiar with the data collection activities of DTM, and will be consulted throughout the project to further enhance impact of the proposed activities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the implementation of activities, the specific needs and potential access barriers of different population groups are taken into account, for example by catering with special mechanisms for the most vulnerable during biometric registration and data management activities. DTM strives to hire increasingly gender-balanced teams so as to ensure that female beneficiaries can feel comfortable to reveal information on their specific needs.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address inequalities, such as those in the uneven provision of services to IDPs displaced outside the well-known PoC sites and collective centres, whose sometimes comparable or bigger needs remain under-recognized and under-served (by filling the information gap and providing information both about their location and their multi-sectoral needs).
