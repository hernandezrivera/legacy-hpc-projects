Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (AAH) and the Rural Center for Sustainable Development (RCSD)’s proposed project in 2022 will target the most vulnerable families in Area C (including Bethlehem, Hebron, Jericho, Jerusalem, Ramallah, and Tubas governorates) and in Hebron’s H2 area. These groups suffer from no, or limited, access to water and sanitation services due to Israeli restrictions on construction and development in the area, as well as the massive demolition and confiscation of WASH structures that took place during 2021 (51 structures according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 2022). This makes them particularly vulnerable to the threat of forced displacement. Further, the COVID-19 outbreak and its associated impacts, have added additional challenges to these communities to meet hygiene standards.
The 53 target communities, the majority of which are farming and herding families dependent on livestock and agricultural activities to maintain their livelihoods, heavily rely on water availability, quality, and affordability.
The lack of water has forced families to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as reducing their daily water consumption as stated in the WASH Vulnerability Map 2021, depending on unsafe water resources, or reducing their expenditure on basic needs such as health and education to afford the high cost of water.
In line with Strategic Objectives 1, 2 and 3, the project aims to:
1. Decrease target communities’ vulnerability and dependence on trucked water or unsafe sources of water by constructing and rehabilitating water networks, and household cisterns.
2. Improve communities’ access to sanitation facilities through the construction and provision of household WASH facilities such as latrines. Families often disregard these facilities and prioritize other basic needs such as food and water. This negative coping mechanism has left many households in poor health, hygiene, privacy, and dignity conditions, which are contributing to household members’ vulnerability to face hygiene related diseases.
3. Mitigate the risk of flooding during winter through the supply and installation of storm water systems for areas in serious needs. This will prevent target communities to experience further assets losses, displacement, and disrupted access to livelihoods services.
4. Increase hygiene awareness through trainings and workshops on subjects ranging from hygiene promotion to water saving techniques and cistern management, at both household and community levels.
All these activities will mitigate the effects of the Israeli occupation on water access and thus improve the target communities’ livelihood prospects. The project will reduce the exposure to forced displacement of an estimated 158,308 people (39,047men, 37,585 women, 41,657 boys, 40,019 girls, and 2,253 PWD) and provide them with the means to show resilience in the face of the aforementioned challenges.
The proposed project is guided by AAH Gender and Protection policy and AAH staff endeavor to consider safety and dignity in the design and implementation of all humanitarian interventions. Accountability and participation will be ensured through a detailed Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework specifically developed to ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner and through applying AAH’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism to guarantee the inclusion of communities’ suggestions into the project. AAH seeks to achieve equal and impartial access to vulnerable populations, aiming for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible including through community consultations and focus groups to further achieve beneficiaries’ empowerment and sustainability beyond the project’s lifetime.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, nearly 620,000 people across the West Bank reside in areas that are either not connected to piped water services or are very poorly supplied. Area C communities, particularly Southern Hebron, East Jerusalem periphery and the Jordan Valley, are particularly affected by this issue. Approximately, more than 300,000 Palestinians in Area C are directly affected by the Israeli restrictions and control over WASH-related infrastructure and water sources. As per the 2022 HNO, water scarcity in Area C is one of the main factors leading vulnerable families to poverty and food insecurity, as well as further deteriorating living conditions and hygiene practices. Also, due to the sequences and impact of COVID-19, both communities and service providers have been put in a difficult position considering their limited means to obtain necessary water and hygiene material to practice and enforce preventive measures. Additionally, most Bedouin and herding communities in Jericho, Ramallah and the East Jerusalem periphery receive water through old water pipes laid over-ground. Irregular water supply, the small size of old water pipes, high water losses and the destruction of water pipes by settlers and the ICA (e.g., in the R317 communities of South Hebron) restrict access to safe, sufficient, and affordable water. Data from 2022 HNO, 2021 WASH vulnerability Map, and other assessments made by AAH and other partners, validated by the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), highlighted that most of the targeted communities consume less than 50 l/c/d and use rain harvesting cisterns and trucked water as coping mechanisms for the water shortage. Thus, adequate water storage capacity is vital for vulnerable families to harvest rainwater in winter and store trucked water during dry months. The Rainwater Harvesting Master Plan previously finalized by AAH has identified locations suitable for rainwater harvesting at community level in the South of the West Bank. AAH, through the West Bank Protection Consortium, uses a community-based approach to assess on a continuous basis than 70 communities in the West Bank. It established that many families in Area C lack latrines and do not have sufficient water storage capacity (< 5m3). Most of them must buy water using small tanks (3.7m3 tractors) instead of large tanks (10m3) with prices up to 30 NIS/m3 as the trucked water cost is affected by road conditions, tank capacity, distance and fill up time. High water prices and limited income force families to reduce their water consumption to as little as 30-50 l/c/d and to less than 30l/c/d in some cases. Women, many of whom are widowed, disabled or divorced, must borrow water from relatives and neighbours, which is then stored in small containers or kitchen pots. According to AAH’s sex and age disaggregated data and gender analysis, men and women have distinct tasks: men provide payments for water truck services, while women are responsible for the storage and use of water. AAH and educational directorates conduct regular assessments and coordination meetings on WASH facilities in schools in Hebron, Bethlehem, East Jerusalem, Tubas, etc. including as part of WASH in school Task Force lead by the MoE and AAH. These meetings, field visits and interviews with women, men, students, and teachers indicated that many schools are not connected to a regular water supply and/or have poor WASH facilities. Substandard sanitation facilities in schools increase children’s exposure to health risks such and reduces the institutions capacity in preventing or overcoming the spread and impact COVID-19, unhealthy social environment, and the school dropout rate among girls, specifically because of privacy issues.

2 - Activities

The project will achieve its objectives through: (1) The rehabilitation and construction of water transmission pipelines, water networks, and water storage capacity for vulnerable communities including Bedouins and herders in Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, the Jordan Valley, Tubas and East Jerusalem periphery. AAH will rehabilitate and/or install 33 water networks and rehabilitate 9 wheeled water tanks. (2) The construction and rehabilitation of household cisterns in Area C and seam zones. According to the Area C Water Master Plan endorsed by PWA, 12 household cisterns will be constructed in South Hebron Area for communities that cannot be connected to a piped water service at this stage. Based on a socio-economic baseline survey, female headed households and families with members with disabilities will be prioritized. The activity is cash based and will be implemented directly by the households when feasible to contribute to dignified assistance, reduce the risks of material getting controlled by the Israeli and increase time and cost efficiency. (3) The construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in institutions (handwashing, latrines, sewer systems):a) For 6 schools in Area C. This will improve student’s access to adequate, safe and healthy WASH facilities, crucial in times of COVID-19 recent waves, and will also improve their general educational environment, thus upholding the children’s right to education. b) For 4 HCF in Hebron. This shall support these health care facilities in improving the standards of the WASH facilities, which will have a direct impact on the quality of services provided to vulnerable people. (4) The provision of access to sanitation and hygiene for communities in Area C: This activity will be composed of the following components: a) Rehabilitation of 122 vulnerable household sanitation facilities in 4 governorates (Hebron, Jericho, Tubas, and Ramallah) covering 8 communities in Area C. This activity is a cash-based one and shall be implemented by the beneficiaries under the direct supervision of AAH & RCSD field staff. b) The provision of 10 mobile latrines for one community in South Hebron.c) The provision of 132 hygiene kits while conducting hygiene promotion awareness campaigns. AAH shall provide these kits to all HCFs targeted in this project, as well as all the communities targeted by activity (a) above. (5) The construction/ rehabilitation of storm water network/ rainwater drainage systems: This activity shall include the supply and install of culvert to drain water floods for two communities in two governorates (Jericho and Tubas) (6) The development of WASH services operational and management plans and analysis: Via the implementation of a hydraulic map and conducting technical capacity building training at Bani Na’em & Yatta in Hebron governorate. This plan will help in designing WASH interventions in the area, contributing to provide equal and impartial access to all. AAH will coordinate with the PWA, Ministry of Education (MoE), Ministry of Health (MoH), UNICEF, and other organizations to avoid gaps and overlap. Stakeholders and partners in the targeted locations (municipalities, village councils and RCSD), who have been involved in the design of the project, will support the collection of baseline and end line data to ensure gender needs and priorities are considered. Together with AAH, and based on their extensive experience in implementing proposed activities, RCSD will be responsible for monitoringproject’s implementation, for the collection of feedback from project beneficiaries and handover. RCSD staff will continue to be trained on designing and monitoring WASH activities in sensitive areas, respecting the principle of Do No Harm, following AAH policies and including PSEA considerations.

3 - Indicators

By improving access to basic WASH services to sustain livelihoods and avoid/respond to displacement, support the WASH advocacy strategy and improve coordination platforms, the proposed interventions will contribute to improve the safety and dignity of women, men, girls and boys, and the overall protection of the targeted communities. Expected results include: 1. Improved water quality for vulnerable families: construction and rehabilitation of water networks will decrease the dependency on trucked water and will deter families from sourcing unsafe water. Catchment area improvement, chlorination of cisterns, hygiene promotion campaigns on hygiene practices and cistern management will encourage behavior changes and contribute to a reduction in waterborne diseases. 2. Increased availability of water supply. Men and women will participate in the planning, design and implementation of the activities. Families will reduce their expenditure on trucked water, consequently they will have additional disposable income for other basic needs. The family member responsible for fetching water will have more time to fulfil other household activities. 3. Improved awareness, knowledge and hygiene practices: Hygiene promotion activities and awareness raising will be provided at household level, with a special focus on women for topics regarding domestic consumption management, as per the previously conducted gender analysis of household roles and responsibilities. The core hygiene promotion messages will be hand washing, efficient use of water,domestic hygiene, cleaning and maintenance of WASH facilities and water storage tanks, especially relevant in times of COVID-19.4. Strengthened capacity and resilience of vulnerable communities to respond to adverse environmental conditions resulting fromnatural disasters, as well as respond to the pandemic. The distribution of water, rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, and non-food items (plastic tanks) will improve and strengthen the capacity of vulnerable communities to withstand adverse environmental conditions. 5. Improved information management, coordination and dissemination of information among the cluster members and other stakeholders: the use of GIS and statistical software in data collection will facilitate data comparison and analysis; ad hoc databases will be set up to collect and compare information collected during the project implementation; complemented by AAH’s data management system. Elaborated data will be shared with relevant stakeholders and made available through regular reporting. KAP surveys and general project monitoring and evaluation will be carried out throughout the entire project in coordination with other agencies operating in the area; AAH will contributeto exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community and to be used for advocacy purposes. Expected indicators and targets for the project are: • Water expenditure decreased by 30%• Water consumption increased by 30%• Interruptions of water supply in the network decreased by 30% • Water loss in the network decreased by 30%• Number of small expensive water deliveries (with tractors) decreased by 60%• Water storage capacity for 12 families will be improved through the construction/rehabilitation of water cisterns • WASH facilities in 6 schools and 4 health care facilities improved through construction/rehabilitation of handwashing, latrines, sewer system• Living conditions for 235 women, 233 men, 213 girls and 222 boys are improved through the rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities • 33 communities improved their access to water through the construction/ rehabilitation of water pipelines, as well as the rehabilitation of water wheeled tanks • 2 communities improved mitigation and prevention of floods and storm water drainage • 132 hygiene kits provided to the vulnerable communities benefiting from “rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities” activity, as well as specialized kits to the targeted HCFs.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike will be able to reach out to the project with complaints, suggestions, and any other feedback through the mechanism managed by AAH MEAL Unit. AAH actively promote the existence of its feedback mechanisms through information sessions and information material (e.g., flyer) handed over during projects’ activities (e.g. assessments, distribution, training session etc.). Our programs maintain fluid communication with all rights holders regularly and extensively throughout the project management cycle. Our team consults and meets with rights holders when carrying out needs assessments in relevant beneficiary areas and employs a series of methods to ensure that their perspectives and priorities are addressed in the project design. These methods used to receive feedback include interviews with key informants, home visits, group discussions (separating men and women to ensure a safe space when discussing sensitive subjects), questionnaires and regular monitoring sessions to ensure that the project is appropriate and relevant to their needs. The information collected through complaints and feedback mechanism or directly in the field will be regularly fed into our programming to ensure no harm is done and to guarantee continuous improvements of assistance delivered respecting internal humanitarian standards, including informing the budget. Beneficiary feedback will be analysed and presented in a systematic way, including to project partners that we work with. Learnings following this feedback can often be used across projects and we make sure this is shared among relevant stakeholders so that complaints are taken seriously and can be anonymous where necessary, to make beneficiaries feel safe in expressing their opinions. The AAH MEAL unit will be in continuous contact with the Program and Coordination teams to analyze the data, address concerns, respond to complaints and work with the project team to adjust the project.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming and Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. AAH has mechanisms in place to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct and our held accountable if they do not respect it. AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Tip Sheets, along with learnings from various interventions and external evaluations. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. Risks assessments and analysis of the protection context are let for all the projects, and even more so when implemented with at risk groups (e.g. minors) or in areas where risk of doing harm is exacerbated (e.g. Area C in the West Bank or inside health facilities in COVID-19 times). In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH also continuously works on understanding gender roles and how its interventions might impact them without aggravating existing power relations between genders. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, which focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. COVID-19 protective measures and equipment will be applied throughout all interventions to further prevent harm being done to beneficiaries, communities and staff members.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, and Independence. During the course of the project, AAH will ensure all projects are easily accessible to those most at need, avoiding risks to the beneficiaries. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. At a global level, AAH relies on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistleblowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE ́ towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented to report these behaviours to headquarters. Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated by providing compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and in 2019, AAH conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
