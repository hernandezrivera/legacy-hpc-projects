Project Summary

Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a significant challenge in Cameroon, particularly within the Northwest region, where socio-political instability has exacerbated the vulnerability of women and marginalized groups. Many survivors face challenges such as economic instability, limited access to healthcare, and social stigma. Snail farming offers an opportunity for financial empowerment and resilience, providing a sustainable means of income generation while promoting self-reliance among these survivors. providing livelihood for 100 survivors of gender-based violence in Mezam Division through sustainable snail farming, enabling them to achieve economic independence, improve their livelihoods, and reintegrate into their communities with dignity. The “Provision of Livelihood for 100 Survivors of GBV through Sustainable Snail Farming” project aligns with UNIMAC’s mission to uplift marginalized communities and address the impacts of GBV. By focusing on vocational skills, market linkages, and community support, this project promises to create a lasting impact in the lives of survivors, providing them with the tools to rebuild their futures and contribute positively to their communities

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The project seeks to implement community-level prevention interventions aimed at changing attitudes, behaviours, and social norms around gender inequality and GBV in communities across target communities within Mezam Division. Based on the existing databank of vulnerable maidens who have suffered some kind of gender-based violence, we shall support livelihood activities for victims and empower them to be community advocates. It shall lead to the emergence of a community of lived experience. A major livelihood support activity envisaged for the project is Snail farming (heliciculture), this is a sustainable agricultural practice that requires minimal resources and has a high market demand. The current market for edible snails is growing in Cameroon, presenting an opportunity for survivors to gain financial independence. The project is an innovative and sustainable solution to empower GBV survivors in the Northwest Region of Cameroon. By empowering them through snail farming, we can address both their immediate economic needs and contribute to their long-term resilience and recovery. The project shall equally reduce the vulnerability of survivors through civil documentation.
