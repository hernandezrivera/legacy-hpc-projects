Project Summary

The current context in the oPt is marked by imposed closures, high political tension linked to Annexation Plan, and an on-going global pandemic. In the Gaza Strip, blockades and political division continue to marginalize the population and the lock-down imposed deteriorated the already fragile economic situation reaching a poverty rate of 54% of the population. In the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the Education Cluster
identified children with disabilities as one the affected by the protracted and acute crisis: besides their lack of basic assistive devices and technologies that would facilitate their access to education and required psychosocial support (PSS) and remedial education. COVID-19 situation and the closure of schools pushed boys and girls with disabilities in special and mainstream schools to be neglected by the distance learning modalities due to the lack of its inclusivity enhancing the high risk of drop-out. Through its running education projects, HI piloted a distance learning modality (presented as good practice at the international IASC conference) that includes the use of online distance learning and offline education support for those families with no access to internet. The action will replicate and upscale the current experience to ensure children access to accessible and inclusive online and offline distance learning modalities.
Building on the previous experience the project’s objective is to increase the access to quality inclusive education for children with disabilities in improved education facilities. The first component focuses on community actors to identify and provide tailored services for out-of-school and those at risk of drop-out children with disabilities and their families to support children’s enrolment and continued access to education. The second component will aim at building the capacities of schools, teachers and parents to respond to the needs of affected children. The project falls under Education Cluster’s objectives 1) increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and 2) – Children and school staff in vulnerable schools are supported in preparing and responding to emergencies. Through preventive measures, remedial programs and school-based, remote and outreach psychosocial services. HI will financially and technically support Teacher Creativity Center (TCC) as local implementing partner, that is a main actor in supporting the development of Inclusive Education in UNRWA and MoE schools, besides their networks with teachers and communities. The project will respond to the first and second Education Cluster objectives through the identification of out-of-school children, the provision of personal support for children with disabilities: enhance their mobility, provide visual and hearing assistive devices, transportation, school kits, education materials and stationaries, school-based, remote and outreach structured psychosocial services. HI will also support the provision of holistic services through referrals to other stakeholders. Furthermore, HI will aim to develop an enabling educational environment through the provision of technical support for mainstream and special education service providers. These will be supported to respond to the needs of affected children through the development of adapted processes and tools benefiting both children with and without disabilities. HI will further provide educational materials, tools, and resources and create a welcoming and accessible educational environment for children with and without disabilities.

1 - Needs

By end of August 2020, Gaza Strip went through total closure leaving 560,000 students at home without a formal and systemized education support and increasing level of stress and anxiety among children, parents and teachers. Children from low income households and children with disabilities (CWD) had been identified as more affected by the ongoing situation.The 2017 census of the Palestinian Census Bureau of Statistics indicated that 35,675 CWDs live in the oPt. HNO 2021 states that 9,780 of children living with disabilities, face challenges in accessing quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment. Between 6-17 years old, 46% of CWDs never enrolled in education, 43% of them live CWDs in Gaza and those enrolled remain at high risk of drop out. The Education Cluster identified a lack of education assistive devices to facilitate their learning, adequate transportation means, adapted school facilities and staff capacity. HI conducted a rapid assessment in September 2020 in 3 special schools, through focus group discussions involving students with disabilities, parents and teachers, aiming at identify the main gaps and needs to design a responsive action. Parents are concern on the possibilities of their children with disabilities to be able to continue their education in mainstream schools due to lack of informational and physical accessibility measures, and limited capacities of teachers to respond to the specific needs of children with disabilities. The lack of capacities of schools’ staff had been confirm by the MoE special education directorate in the concept paper submitted to the education cluster (September,2020). In line with new request of distance learning modality, teachers from special schools declare the lack of opportunities for capacity building and technical support. The distance learning platforms of MoE and UNRWA are not accessible and inclusive for children with disabilities. Limited skills to support their children and request availability of educational material and stationaries had been stated by the caregivers.Lack of accessibility to internet connection or smart device as lack of accessibility to curriculum materials, since the material are not adopted to different disability types, had been identify in EducAid Covid-19 rapid assessment. For who have access can still be in need of support or face additional difficulties mainly noise, lack of privacy, electricity cut, not adopted desk and furniture and unsuitable home atmosphere. Another concern shared that distance learning may increase the chances of children with disabilities to drop-out from education as distance learning is not inclusive. During September 2020, 83 boys and 49 girls with disabilities have been identified as out-of-school, 64% of them (39% girls and 61% boys) were never enrolled in education. The remaining were registered in special schools (51%) and mainstream schools (49%), and 1 every 2 children dropped-out from education at the pre-school and basic education levels. Due to their need for physical support in transfer from and to wheelchair, parents indicated that girls with disabilities are more likely to face sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse while growing-up. Parents, therefore, prefer to keep them at home and restrict their movements. In the actual lockdown situation, an increase of domestic violence from parents towards children with disability had been recorded. In conclusion the pandemic is not impacting only on access to education, causing loss of learning, especially due to the lack of a full functional inclusive distance learning programs accessible to all the children respecting the principle of leaving no one behind, but also the school closure bring to lack of school-based psychosocial support and opportunity to participate to extracurricular activities impacting on the mental health of the children and the ability of support of the teachers and schools staff. In line with the HNO 2021, an increase need of MHPSS had been identified in Gaza Strip where, since the start of the pandemic, there have been 44 attempted suicides involving children, compared to eight in 2019. The Protection Cluster estimates that some 198,000 children in Gaza are in need of structured protection and psychosocial support due to risk of severe and moderate mental health conditions, while nearly 36,000 children require individual case management. Additionally, over 274,000 adults, including caregivers, are also in need of various MHPSS services.

2 - Activities

Built on a two-fold strategy, the project’s objective is to increase the access to quality inclusive education for children with disabilities in improved education facilities. The project will target the five governorates of Gaza Strip and targeted schools will be selected in coordination with the MoE and the Education Cluster. The first component focuses on community actors to identify and provide tailored services for out-of-school children with disabilities in the basic education school age (between 6 and 14 years old) and their families to support children’s enrolment. This will be achieved through a sequence of activities:1.1 Identify school-aged boys and girls with disabilities who are out of schools with the support of CBR programs and disability service providers through awareness raising and initiatives (Environmental building); 1.2 Refer identified school-aged boys and girls with disabilities to specialized rehabilitation services, child protection services, special education services and mainstreaming schools in collaboration with the existing referral networks of HI partners and referral pathways, that will improve their inclusion in the regular schools according to their social support plan (Remedial); 1.3 Provide mobility, hearing, visual and educational assistive devices and technologies for school-aged boys and girls with disabilities in need to overcome environmental barriers preventing equitable access to education (Remedial for the education cluster); 1.4 Provide transportation for vulnerable school-aged boys and girls with disabilities to enable their access toschools and reduce their risk to dropout from schools (Remedial); 1.5 Provide educational materials and stationery to school-aged girls and boys of poor and vulnerable households to maintain their access to regular education and distance learning (Responsive); 1.6 Provision of disinfection and hygiene materials for families of children with disabilities at the school age and targeted primary schools.1.7 Support home reasonable accommodation for school-aged boys and girls with disabilities to facilitate their access inside their homes (Remedial); 1.8 Provide school-based psycho-social and outreach psychoeducation for school-aged boys and girls with disabilities, their parents and teachers (Responsive) using HI and international guidelines on PSS methodology (i.e. PM+) and quality assessment (I.e. SDQ and DASS). The second component will aim to build the capacities of schools and teachers and parents to respond to the needs of affected children (pre-school and primary school students, between 4 and 14 years old) through the following activities:2.1 Support targeted school to develop inclusion action plans through the use of School Inclusiveness Assessment Tool (SIAT) (Remedial); 2.2 Support the adaptation and accessibility to active offline and online education resource platform and distribution of 100 device to guarantee the accessibility to remote education, even with no internet availability, to the most vulnerable school-aged girls and boys during emergency and non-emergency situations (Responsive);2.3 Support technically and financially mainstream and special schools to develop an accessible child-friendly environment for school-aged boys and girls with and without disabilities through school adaptation and accessibility action, targeting both physical and intellectual disabilities, tailored based on the structures' needs assessments (Remedial); 2.4 Provide technical support to male and female teachers of pre-schools and schools to respond to the personalized needs of boys and girls with disabilities (Remedial); 2.5 Provide educational materials and stationery for female and male teachers in pre-schools, mainstream and special schools in order to provide inclusive education through regular education and distance learning (Responsive); 2.6 Provide educational counselling support for school-aged boys and girls with disabilities, their parents and teachers to support them in the implementation of distance learning and provide the required support for their children (Responsive). Targeted beneficiaries will be active participants in the implementation of the project activities. As an example, parents of children with disabilities will be supported to conduct awareness raising sessions to identify children with disabilities at the community level, support the facilitation of peer to peer support with other parents and will be involved in the project steering committee. Teachers will be involved in the implementation of exchange meetings with other teachers to share their experiences and good practices.

3 - Indicators

HI will ensure that the project is monitored and evaluated based on HI’s Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation (PME) policy. In line with the policy, the following system will be established: - Planning: set up of project steering committees, project steering tools, and quarterly risk assessments. - Monitoring: project quarterly reviews, monthly data collection, beneficiary database, administrative and financial tools, and regular self-assessments.- Evaluation: satisfaction monitoring visits, and final participatory evaluations. Project managers and technical teams will be responsible for the implementation and monitoringof the project. They will provide direct technical support to the partners and teachers. HI’s regional technical unit and inclusive education technical advisors will monitor and guide for the project to be in line with the national, international and HI standards through site field visits and technical support missions, in addition to remote support. In terms of indicators, the following will measure the progress and impact of the project: -Outcome1:60% of 500 (225 girls and 275 boys) with disabilities in Gaza Strip respectively enroll in pre and primary schools; -Output 1: 1.1 500 (225 girls and 275 boys) in Gaza out-of-school children with disabilities at the school age (6-14 years old) are identified; 1.2 70% of identified out-of-school children with disabilities at the school age receive special and mainstream services based on their specific needs through referrals; 1.3 100 (45 girls and 55boys) school-aged children with disabilities in Gaza benefit from assistivedevices; 1.4 25 (12 girls and 13boys) school-aged children with disabilities in Gaza benefit from home adaptation; 1.5 500 (275 boys and 225 girls) school-aged children benefit from the provision of educational materials and stationaries.1.6 500 (275 boys and 225 girls) school-aged children benefit from the provision of disinfection and hygiene materials.1.7 20 special and mainstream schools benefit from the provision of educational materials and stationaries.1.8 20 special and mainstream schools benefit from the provision of disinfection and hygiene materials.1.9 500 (275 boys and 225 girls) students between 6 and 14 years old and 500 (100 males and 400 females) parents receive school-based and outreach psychoeducation and psychosocial support services; -Output 2: 2.1 10 pre- and primary schools are more accessible and equipped with educational equipment/materials;2.2 1 accessible digital educational resource platform to be used during emergency and non-emergency situations has been developed to guarantee right to education even in the limitation of access to education buildings due to Covid or conflict escalation; 2.3 100 (45 females and 15 males) preschool and primary school staff improve their capacities to respond to the personal psychosocial and educational needs of girls and boys with disabilities between 4 and 14 years old.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In line with its strategy of intervention, HI will implement the activities of this proposed project in partnership with local organizations representing the local communities, both civil society organizations and mainstream and special schools. In a partnership relation defined into a signed agreement, HI will be responsible for the implementation of specific activities and for the support to partners, while local partners (CSOs and mainstream and special schools) will implement other activities such as services provision to girls and boys with disabilities and their parents. The beneficiaries will be active participants and contributors to the implementation and monitoring of project activities. At the level of monitoring and evaluation of the project, each project in HI has a steering committee, which includes parents’ representatives, partner’s representatives and project teams.In 2018 HI has launched the implementation of safeguarding mechanism based on 3 policies: Policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Protection policy (CP) and Anti-fraud, bribery and corruption policy. The launch of the mechanism monitored by the head office and the regional program direction has included training to HI staff and selection of safeguarding focal points. HI safeguarding mechanisms provide – among others elements – minimum standards for prevention and response to Code of Conduct breaches by HI, partners, or contractors who represents HI. As well as, awareness to beneficiaries on their rights to raise their voices against such breaches.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Accessibility is at the core of the project and will be ensured to all beneficiaries considering theirs needs and gender, through physical access to services and through consideration of their specificity by professionals –especially interacting with children with disabilities. Additionally, all project and technical tools/materials will be tailored to suit the specificity of the Gaza Strip context and needs of children with focus on children with disabilities.
