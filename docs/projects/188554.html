4 - Needs

Children comprise almost half of the affected population. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. In 2020, the UN verified 2,388 of such violations against children in 12 out of 14 governorates, with significant variations by region, illustrating persistent trends of violence though not the full range of protection risks affecting children.258 Deepening poverty continues to fuel harmful coping mechanisms and strain the capacities of families and communities to protect children, particularly adolescent girls and boys: child labor, including its worst forms, is reported in all governorates and in 22 percent of assessed communities as frequently occurring.259. Access to basic rights is further jeopardized for children without official birth certificates and children with disabilities. Many of them also suffer marginalization, stigma, and discrimination, whilst facing heightened child protection risks. Separation from caregivers is a persistent issue across Syria. Death of a caregiver (15 percent for boys and girls), economic need (25 percent for boys, 13 percent for girls), child marriage (11 percent for boys, 44 percent for girls), and child recruitment (10 percent for boys) are among the top-cited reasons for family separations. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The risk is that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles. Mitigation measures: - Improved integrated programming approaches & roll-out of cross-cutting issues, including contextualization of participatory approaches. - Systemic engagement of affected individuals throughout the entire project cycle. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Mitigation measures: - All staff & representatives are oriented on Child Safeguarding policy, Code of Conduct & Face to face induction. - Local reporting procedures are implemented. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained. Mitigation measures: - Strengthened capacity building of CP team. Likelihood: High Impact: High Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys, and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately. Mitigation measures: - Safe programming & confidentiality awareness-raising sessions are delivered to staff. - Information related to CP cases is shared only on the need to know basis.- Emergency case closure SOPs in place; contingency plans. Likelihood: High Impact: High


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

TOTOL is invested in the Centrality of Protection and is developing an action plan that focuses on capacity building of staff, the embedding of conflict sensitivity and protection analysis, strengthening safe programming and child safeguarding approaches, improving risk management, and integrating protection into advocacy work. In addition, TOTOL integrates protection mainstreaming across its programs, and these principles guide the implementation team to ensure safe and quality programming. Building on the foundation of all staff members being trained on Child Safeguarding, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and Safe Programming, TOTOL is also expanding capacity building of staff in conflict sensitivity, protection analysis, and participatory approaches. A child safeguarding risk assessment, gender, and GBV audit, and conflict sensitivity analysis are conducted for each program. These identify gaps, inform the project design, and mitigate protection risks for children and their families while accessing TOTOL facilities and using TOTOL services. In addition, TOTOL has prioritized more sustained capacity-building for staff related to disability and disability inclusion, as well as on gender mainstreaming and GBV.
