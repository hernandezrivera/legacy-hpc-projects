Project Summary

The proposed intervention sets to alleviate the vulnerable status of Lebanese and migrants in Lebanon, due to the ongoing socio-economic crisis which has highly affected and impeded the health sector. Through this project, Caritas is set to provide contributions to patients share of hospitalizations fees for life-saving and non-COVID-19 related hospitalization for Lebanese and Migrants. The intervention will focus on persons with low and middle incomes (due to the high loss of value, the minimum wage is less than 1.5 USD/person/day). Older people, people with disabilities, and those with serious medical issues living in Akkar, North Lebanon, Mount Lebanon, and Bekaa and regarded to be below the poverty line.
CL has been implementing health-related interventions, providing primary and secondary healthcare services to vulnerable communities across the country. In 2020, CL delivered more than 500,000 services to 38,500 beneficiaries. Over 170 physicians from different specializations are operating through CL’s 10 Primary Health Care (PHC), dispensaries and 9 Medical Mobile Units (MMU) on a daily basis. Likewise, CL has existing contracts in place with over 30 hospitals in Lebanon detailing the means of engagement; and a majority of these hospitals are contracted by UNHCR. As such, through this intervention, Caritas will aim to tackle objective 1 and 4 of the ERP health chapter.
This program will be a direct continuation of an already existing program set to finish end of October and that has reached the maximum targets, and still have a great number of patients in need. As part of a visibility plan, Caritas had set out a social media campaign to advertise the service being provided and had coordinated with municipalities and other partners for referrals. A high number of cases were assessed but many were rejected due to the low unit cost set. As such, this project aims to increase the cost to cover Lebanese beneficiaries after NSSF coverage or after MoPH coverage and migrants.
After the case is assessed and is found eligible and authorized, the support will be provided either through reimbursement to the patient or direct payment to the hospital. The average value for Lebanese is 1,000 USD per case, with a ceiling of USD 1,500 for extraordinary instances, based on prices specified by the Ministry of Public Health and CL's previous experience. (Sayrafa rate of 12000 LBP). For migrants, the unit cost will be at 500 USD as some cases only require 50-60 USD while others require up to 500 USD and sometimes more in exceptional cases. Moreover, during an emergency, the MoU will act as a safeguard, ensuring that the patient receives lifesaving assistance after confirmation from the CL focal point, even before any funds are transferred.
As a second activity, and in order to mitigate further disease for an already vulnerable community, CL will provide hygiene/dignity kits to 500 Lebanese and migrant women(70% migrants, 30% lebanese), to ensure they are maintaining good hygiene practices and enhance their protection environment during the pandemic. The kit is in line with the WASH sector standards as well as UNFPA guidelines.
As a result, through this intervention, CL will target 430 vulnerable patients (350 Lebanese, 80 Migrants; 60% female and 40% male) and provide them inpatient hospital coverage and 500 Lebanese and migrant women will receive hygiene kits. Beneficiaries will be referred from the social department, outreached with the help of 3 social workers, directly referred by the hospital and/or other organizations (mostly MSF or hospitals for Migrants). A special focus will be given on outreaching for beneficiaries from Akkar. CL is now strongly present in the area through its social, youth and health departments, mainly Qobayat, and has been cooperating with the area hospital after the fire outbreak through the provision of supplies to the hospital and provision of medical services through the MMU.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Objective 1 - Act. 1.1 – Advertise, Target and Outreach: Beneficiaries are referred to CL through different channels (social centers, CL database, hotline for SHC, other NGOs, hospitals etc.). Likewise, outreach is conducted by 2 CL Social workers through social centers and during MMU area visit (ex. in Akkar). Social campaigns will be translated to different languages to ensure better access to migrants.Act 1.2 – Initial screening and social assessment: Following the social and medical assessment by the social worker and physician, the patient answering the criteria can then benefit from the assistance. Act 1.3 – Approve or rejection of case for payment: the patient will be told of the cause of rejection to ensure transparency. Act 1.4 – Payment of PCR for hospital administration. Act 1.5 – Payment or reimbursement of SHC. Act 1.6 – PDM: The patient share of 200 beneficiaries will be covered under this project. A sample size of 55 beneficiaries will be interviewed based on confidentiality level of 95% and 5% of confidence interval. Under Objective 4 – Act 2.1 – Outreach and select beneficiaries that will receive hygiene kits. Act 2.2 – Procurement and distribution of hygiene kits through CL social center and migrants center. Door to door delivery will be considered for special cases.Act 2.3 – PDM: A sample size of 100 beneficiaries will be interview to ensure satisfaction of services.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

CL will adopt the Inter-Agency Network's recommendations to ensure gender sensitive approaches to better understand the increased risks as well as PSEA prevention and response and ensure women’s protection through the provision of a holistic service (complementary services available such as case management, ECA, safe shelter referral). CL uses a variety of feedback methods to identify and minimize negative consequences of projects as fast as feasible. Negative implications will be addressed by staff as soon as they surface, thanks to ongoing monitoring and evaluation of the project. CL guarantees that program is designed to address the target population's health issues while ensuring that they are actively involved in establishing their own safe environment. Social cohesiveness is achieved by avoiding the dismissing of marginalized communities and ensuring that all beneficiaries are supported, either directly through this intervention or through referral to another similar service.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Caritas Lebanon is a strong advocate of Accountability to affected beneficiaries and other stakeholders. Caritas has a number of well-practiced quality assurance mechanisms and accountability strategies in place. Constant communication between the project coordination team and the medical staff and follow up with the hospitals and beneficiaries ensures good interaction between all stakeholders involved in the project. This allows feedback for rapid responses to changing dynamics. Additionally, this program has a hotline for SHC since last year and a separate hotline for feedback and complaints. The Do No Harm principles are embedded in the project design, and social stability factors are taken into account. The project builds on existing deep-rooted relationships developed with Lebanese and migrant communities. Services have been updated by engaging with different communities and lessons learned from last year’s intervention.
