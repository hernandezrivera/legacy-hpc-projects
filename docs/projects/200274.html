Project Summary

The multiple crises impacting Lebanon and its institutions have left thousands of children without the basic right to education and are faced with major protection risks threatening their immediate/long-term wellbeing and opportunities. As an emergency response, the proposed project will promote equitable access to inclusive educational opportunities for socio-economically vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinian and Migrant children impacted by the multiple crisis in Beirut, Mount Lebanon and Baalbeck/Hermel Governorates. In the light of the current situation, where public schools are being closed for a prolonged period of time (Jan ’23), retention support and catch up programmes become even more relevant and needed.
In Lebanon, children are witnessing one of the major violation to the basic right to education, also considering that children attending public school are usually the most vulnerable of the Lebanese society. This will have a major impact in the long term period with a generation that has lost several years of education, pushing children out of school and feeding the inevitable spiral of poverty
Moreover, recognizing the link between child protection and educational outcomes, the project will fully integrate CP in Education to support children and families severely affected by the crisis cope with the stressors of the situation through protective, safe, and inclusive environments. Building upon TDH IT’s long-lasting experience in preventing and responding to protection threats faced in various communities, this education project will make use of the technical expertise, guidelines and tools developed by TDH IT in collaboration with UNICEF and the CP WG. CP and Inclusion specialists will be part of the implementation of this project, providing training, guidance and tools to be used by Education staff to ensure that children with disabilities are not excluded from education/CP services provided, and are provided equal opportunities to benefit from the project. This approach focuses on enhancing the skills, knowledge and tools available for staff to better assist each child and family, while enhancing families and community awareness of children’s rights and how the crisis is affecting them.
In response to the limited data available on the educational status and barriers faced by Lebanese children as a result of the crisis, needs assessment will be conducted considering both in-school and out-of school Lebanese children in specific locations where TDH IT has years of experience implementing Formal/NFE/CP programs, as well as strong connections with the communities.
Community-based retention activities will be implemented, complemented by PSS support, awareness raising sessions and in-kind support (food and educational materials needed for learning). TDH IT’s CP-Education integrated approach will allow the identification of households’ educational gaps and the child protection risks families are exposed to. Engagement of key stakeholders and duty-bearers at community and governance levels will take place to increase their accountability in upholding children’s rights and foster inclusion during this emergency response.
The project will be implemented in line with Sphere Protection Standards and CHS, and National Sectors technical guidelines and SOPs.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Retention activities will be provided, focusing on an individualized family engagement and support approach to address the unmet educational needs of most vulnerable children identified and their caregivers. Caregivers will be provided with tools to develop their resilience through socio-emotionallearning approaches and practical material assistance such as food and items needed fordistance learning. Accordingly, caregivers will be enabled to play an active role in theirchild’s learning path, while local stakeholders will be engaged to ensure access toessential services for families and to promote social stability. Children with disabilities willbe provided with inclusive educational opportunities, assistive devices and access tospecialized therapies. Whenever possible, families will be supported through multipleactivities and will be followed-up on individually by TDH IT M&E and program teams toensure their needs are met and their engagement in educational activities is maintained. Cash for education will be provided to support most vulnerable children to remain in school. Teachers of the children will be supported through Capacity Building and trainings to enrich their curriculum and to be supported in managing challenging classroom environments.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The intervention will respond to protection risks faced by vulnerable Lebanese and Palestinian and Migrant, incl. children at risk of exclusion from education, CwDs, undocumented individuals. All child protection cases identified during outreaching and education activities will be safely identified and referred. For high risk activities (e.g. awareness raising on SGBV or cash assistance), strict guidelines will be followed in line with CP Sector and SGBV Task Team guidelines. Awareness will be raised on CP and SGBV through customized activities on the risks and consequences of SGBV using age-appropriate language and material to reach children and caregivers. All staff, partners and service providers involved will receive trainings on TDH IT Code of Conduct, PSEAH policy, Child Safeguarding policy and Gender. TDH IT will ensure collection and analysis of gender/age/disability/nationality disaggregated data.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

TDH IT utilises several accountability approaches and Community Feedback Response Mechanisms as part of its implementation to ensure AAP. TDH IT has a hotline for Case Management reporting which gives individuals the opportunity to feedback in a confidential manner, as well as ‘Feedback and Complaints’ boxes in its centres for collecting beneficiary feedback and complaints. Over the course of Covid-19, during blended or distance learning, TDH IT utilised WhatsApp to capture feedback from beneficiaries on how sessions were delivered and their content, with changes and adaptations made throughout the pandemic. TDH IT clearly communicates with communities and households on the selection criteria for participation in individual activities, for example why specific households have been selected for cash assistance programs. Households’ visits are also conducted to follow-up with each individual family. Feedback is also recorded during distributions, either at distribution points or at house
