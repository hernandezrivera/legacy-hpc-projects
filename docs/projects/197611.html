Project Summary

arche noVa has been active in Northeast Syria since 2019, implementing with its own structure and field team projects in the sectors of WASH and informal education. After more than 10 years of Syrian crisis, a major part of infrastructure, water networks and public schools remain not functional. Low purchasing power making most households very vulnerable to food security and depending on humanitarian aid.
Since September 2021, arche noVa implements a multisector food security project in rural Ar-Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor governorate. Low rainfall and drought like events during the last years are major reasons for reduced availability of water at households as well as in agriculture sector. Lack of clean drinking water represents the greatest and most comprehensive threat to the population. Restoring the function of water networks and pumping stations is essential for building structural resilience. Agriculture and thus the resilience of the majority of the inhabitants in NES are also significantly dependent on the water and irrigation infrastructure. The restoration of the irrigation facilities is therefore planned for the beginning of the project. The availability of arable irrigated land is a precondition to improve food security in the frame of the project activities. The project will facilitate landless IDP and vulnerable host communities to access land, seeds, livestock and knowledge. Higher crop yields also mean better availability of food in the market and higher incomes and purchasing power. Without increased purchasing power, the economy cannot develop either and young people remain unemployed. Many of the young adults have had little opportunity to attend school in recent years. The establishment of vocational training centres in rural areas will provide opportunity to create a solid basis for self-employment in relevant sectors. Ideally, this will also have a direct impact on the agricultural sector,
The project will benefit from synergies of overlapping location and beneficiaries as well as complementary activities of education and the multisector humanitarian aid project under the HDP NEXUS approach.
Overall objective is it to strengthening the resilience of vulnerable IDP households and host communities through increased food security, income generation activities in relevant sectors, and improved and safe access to safe drinking water and sanitation.
(1) Food security in 8 communities in the project region has increased due to improved access to land, agricultural inputs, crop diversification and improved practices.
(2) The incomes of vulnerable households in the project region have increased as a result of vocational training and self-employment.
(3) Inhabitants of the communities in the project region have improved access to clean water and sanitation and the number of waterborne diseases is reduced.

3 - Needs

Even with the level of current assistance, most inhabitants of Ar-Raqqa depend on external support and the needs are worsening as the PIN increase to 15.3 million in 2023, the PIN in Ar-Raqqa increasing by 6% to 0.7 million people, showing an severe inter-sectional severity of needs. Still, almost 70% of Syrians are in need of assistance, mostly affecting female headed households and persons with disabilities on a most severe level. The project area of Ar-Raqqa 86% of the households have been unable to meet their basic needs. The top two unmet needs in the governorate are FSN and LLH opportunities. The ongoing drought is affecting both harvest and water resources in general. Precipitation has be below normal in 2022 with higher than average temperature. As a result, the total harvested are has halved in Syria. Exemplary, barley production reaches only 10% of the level of the 2020harvest, affecting the fodder value chain and small livestock keepers to provide fodder for their herds. Most input prices and food communities are increasing and process are volatile, affecting planning ability of farmers. The water quality of surface water sources is decreasing due to lower volumes and pollution, likely resulting in public health risks, seen during the Cholera crisis in the last quarter of 2022. This is linked to decreasing condition of sewer networks, water stations and distribution networks and an increasing use of unpiped, untreated water sources from 47 to 52 percent.

4 - Implementation of activities

With it's own staff arche noVa is implementing the activities directly. It involves following modalities: Carrying out a technical assessments and evaluation of ongoing activities: regular site and obervation visits, PDMs, pre- and post tests and community consultations in form of FGDs.General registration of beneficiaries based on community sessions, vulnerability and sustainability scoring.Activities include following modalities• Cleaning of irrigation channels with cash-for-work modality• Identifying and renting suitable available land for cultivation / livestock for households without access to land based on soil assessments of available land• Procurement of seeds, livestock, small equipment and inputs through direct distribution• Setting up vocational training centers and develop training modules on relevant vocations such as sewing, beautification, solar panel and electric installation and cooling equipment by hiring local trainers• Providing start-up funds and technical support to establish self-employment • Contracting service providers to rehabilitate water pumps and water supply networks, with direct oversight by site engineers• Provide hygiene promotion through training and hiring of daily workers to communities directly, • Provision of water quality test kits and training for technical personal of pumping stations

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

This project emphasizes on assisting vulnerable groups such as women, unemployed youth, persons with disabilities, elderly to have access to the proposed activities. Therefore, AN mainstreams all vulnerability criteria in their beneficiary selection criteria, and aims at understanding the possible frictions inside the targeted communities (e.g. risk for women in their families for being chosen over men, risks for widows or women headed households in communities). A close coordination mechanism with (protection) and mine action partners in the field will ensure that protection risks are identified and updated by their expertise. This will contribute to our capacity strengthening and ensure continuity for future projects. All staff will understand and sign the respective organisational Code of Conduct and Child Protection Policy. The policies will be made available in a form that is widely understood by the community. All provisions and distributions will mainstream the consideration of gender and protection specific measures such as place and time of distribution, availability of same gendered staff and accessible modality of payments. This is especially relevant for cash for work, distributions and hygiene promotion.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The proposed project considers PSEAH by prioritizing the safety and dignity for the beneficiaries in general. AN adheres to the Do-no-Harm principle at all stages of the project cycle firmly in line with IASC‘s AAP Guidelines. AN will train all new staff and sharing of best practices amongst the field offices to agree on common approaches with regards to People with Special Needs (PSN) and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and Child Protection. All staff has signed, is trained and will follow AN Policy on Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) it at all stages of organizational level. AN requires all contractors to submit their own or sign and adopt AN PSEA policy and Code of Conduct. Throughout the project, special focus will be given to prospects of at least maintaining gender relations and gender empowerment. Several barriers for women participation especially in Cash for Work measures were already identified in prior needs assessments due to the cultural norms. Those aspects are addressed from a gender sensitive perspective during the planning of the proposed intervention. During community sessions, affected populations will be consulted in order to identify their own protection risks, including related to cash, benefits and self-protection mechanisms.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- IDPs and returnees remain in their current locations- administrations/governments do not prevent AN's operation in the country and in the targeted areas - Physical access to the implementation area and targeted population is not severely restricted and/or prevented by military actions- IED's are previously cleared by mine action actors prior to the intervention - No restrictions are placed on the procurement of goods and services by government actors- Government actors agree to be included in monitoring and handover of activities- foreign currency is accepted and inflation remains manageable in local and national markets- No market limitations for program suppliesRisks:- potential access restrictions due to administrative barriers set up by the local authorities- access and health risks for staff and communities- IDP population might fluctuate and relocate on short notice- Conflicts arise out of selected beneficiaries in the community- extreme weather is affecting activities, services as well as procurement in generalMitigation:- communication to donor for changes in implementation modality- transparency and regular communication with stakeholders at all stages of project implementation- inclusion of beneficiaries in communication/ application of vulnerability assessments- adaptation of measures in case of a change in IDP/returnee living situations- remote management structures/protocols are prepared for phases with access restrictions
