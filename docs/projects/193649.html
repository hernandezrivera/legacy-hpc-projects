Project Summary

In facing a state of chronic humanitarian crisis characterized by protracted psychological distress and deteriorated living conditions as a result of the longstanding Israeli blockade, internal Palestinian political divides, and recurrent escalations of violence, this project aims at contributing to mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence through delivering quality specialized mental health and MHPSS services to the vulnerable people as per the following interventions:
1. Provision of specialized mental health services for 1000 children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of psychosocial distress, including medications as needed. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multidisciplinary teams at GCMHP's three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah, and Khan Younis.
2. Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) for 1200 adults/parents and children affected by conflict-related violence and crises in 5 mobile clinics. 5 Psychologists will provide psychotherapy and counseling sessions to affected persons at 5 mobile clinics that will be hosted at CBOs distributed among the 5 governorates to facilitate reaching the cases in their communities, with less stigma, and free of charge. In order to enhance availability of MH services and make them accessible to population. The GCMHP’s full-time psychologists will provide their therapeutic services at 5 mobile clinics hosted by CBOs, and refer cases in need to medications to GCMHP community centers. These mobile clinics will be located in all Gaza governorates (North, Gaza, MZ, Khan Younis, and Rafah). GCMHP is used to select CBOs based on specific criteria including eligibility, target groups, locality, field of work, privacy, policies, safety measures, etc. Case mangers working in the different organizations’ members in the HC and PC and relevant working groups (GBV, MHPSS, CP, etc.) will refer cases with severe symptoms to GCMHP mobile clinics and community cetners in order to receive MH services.
3. Provision of free telephone counseling service for 1200 children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centers due to economic, social or emergency settings barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psychoeducation, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centers to receive specialized mental health services.
4. Building the capacities of 50 workers from protection (GBV,CP, MHPSS) staff (nonprofessional) through training on identification and referral via GCMHP trainers.
5. Building the capacities of 50 non-child protection professionals through training and supporting them in identification and referral.
6. Building the capacities of 50 healthcare providers and community workers through training them on MHPSS, including mhGAP.
7. Conducting 24 mental health awareness/de-stigmatization (face-to-face) public meetings and 5 online interactive discussions with local community members via GCMHP professionals. 600 members are expected to participate in the face-to-face meetings, and 500 to participate effectively in the online interaction discussions where GCMHP professionals will answer the questions and interact with their concerns.
8. Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns, findings and observations among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.
9. In addition, GCMHP will strengthen the referral pathways between GCMHP and other protection and health service providers and will share the project updates on OCHA's online systems under the protection and health clusters including area, gender and age disaggregated information.

1 - Needs

The MSNA, 2022 has revealed that an estimated 63% of Gaza residents were assessed to be in need of humanitarian assistance, and 17.8% of households reported experiencing difficulties in accessing services due to mental or physical difficulty. Furthermore, 40% reported at least one member of their household experiencing signs of psychosocial distress of trauma. GCMHP, as the only NGO that provides specialized mental health services to the population of Gaza is confirming the high needs for mental health services. GCMHP cooperates with the most of local service providers in the Gaza Strip in many aspects regarding joint needs assessment, referral, training, supervision, awareness, advocacy, therapy, and rehabilitation. The protection analysis update issued in August 2022 showed that the priority protection risks in Gaza involve torture, ill-treatment, psychosocial distress, and deterioration in mental health including psychological or emotional abuse. GCMHP, in the delivery of its services, targets the community, direct/indirect survivors of Human Rights violations, traumatic events, and GBV and VAW and tortured cases. In 2021, 330 cases were subjected to torture and human rights violations and received required services.For the period from 1/1/2022 to 30/9/2022, GCMHP noticed a significant increase in the community needs for community MH services which included psychoeducation, consultations, counseling, psychotherapy, rehabilitation, psychological first aid (PFA), capacity building, supportive supervision, stress management training and caring for caregivers. This increase came in light of the current complicated situation resulting from the occupation, blockade, intra-division, COVID-19 pandemic, and all offensives in the Gaza Strip. These compounded crises have increased the complexity and harm of the humanitarian situation among the population. In such conditions, GCMHP in cooperation with other service providers provided its community mental health services to the affected population. For the period from 1/1/2022 to 31/8/2022, GCMHP coordinated with MoSD, MoH, organizations members in Health and Protection clusters and with partners in local CBOs to obtain lists of victims and affected people by ongoing crises. GCMHP teams provided PFA services to 6,775 affected people, including (1,808 women, 1,387 men, 1,699 girls and 1,794 boys) and 87 Pwd (43 women, 26 men, 10 girls, 8 boys) as direct beneficiaries in addition to referring 289 cases (74 women, 45 men, 73 girls, 97 boys) to specialized mental health services at the GCMHP community centers. Moreover, GCMHP received 542 cases (57 men, 107 women, 198 boys, 180 girls), 15 among them were PwDs at its five mobile clinics distributed on the five Gaza strip governorates.Besides, and within the same period, different service providers including INGOs, NGOs, CBOs, and ministries referred 717 cases (67 men, 140 women, 312 boys, 198 girls) to the GCMHP three community centers. The year 2022 witnessed also a noticeable increase in the number of callers who were seeking psychological counseling via hotlines. The free telephone counseling service benefited 1,971 cases (815 women, 585 men, 234 girls, and 337 boys) including 81 Pwds. The telephone counselors referred also 324 cases to the GCMHP community centers for advanced interventions, in addition to, 323 cases (116 women, 112 men, 46 girls, and 49 boys) to other external service providers. GCMHP witnessed a signiﬁcant increase in the number of cases who received its therapeutic services, where, 3,938 cases received psychotherapy at GCMHP community centers in 2021, compared with 3,177 cases in 2020. Furthermore, within only three-quarters of 2022, the clinical services reached 3,672 cases (1325 women, 1356 men, 409 girls, and 582 boys) including 84 Pwds. The age segregation for them was as follows: (1,326 Men (466 from the 18-30 years, 752 from 31 to 59 years, 108 from 60 years and above), 912 Women (444 from the 18-30 years, 768 from 31 to 59 years, 144 from 60 years and above), 582 Boys (286 from 0-10 years, 296 from 11 to 17), 408 Girls (185 from 0-10 years, 223 from 11 to 17)). This reflects the noticeable demand for GCMHP services. This demand comes either from the community, with increased rates of self and family referrals, or from other MHPSS service providersThe last clinical assessment and analysis conducted by GCMHP on 30/6/2022 showed that 26.3% of the new cases were suffering from depressive disorders, 15.4% suffer from anxiety disorders, and 14.6% are from Trauma and stress-related disorders. These percentages are regardless age or gender, but when considering children separately, 24.2% were suffering from Trauma and stress-related disorders, while 13.3% were suffering from anxiety disorders, and 11.3% were from elimination disorders. For adults, 38.4% were suffering from depressive disorders, 16.8% from anxiety disorders, and 10.9% from obsessive-compulsive disorders.Unprecedented daily requests from many service providers, especially front-line workers are still submitted to GCMHP to receive training, supervision, and care. In the first half of the year 2022, the GCMHP’s training courses trained 260 professionals providing services to vulnerable groups. This includes providing supervision and stress management sessions to 47 professionals of UNRWA administrative staff. In addition, GCMHP in cooperation with Physicians for Human Rights implemented a joint training that targeted mental health professionals working in the community mental health centers managed by GCMHP and MoH. The professionals were 45 therapists (17 men, 28 women) from GCMHP and 54 therapists (33 men, 21 women) from MoH. The reporting period witnessed also the provision of specialized training on techniques of child psychodynamic therapy. Moreover, GCMHP trained 260 professionals on mental health issues, stress management, supervision on supervision, violence prevention interventions, MH and HR issues.

2 - Activities

To achieve the project objectives, GCMHP will implement the following activities: 1) Provision of specialized mental health services for 1000 people including 400 children (164 girls and 236 boys) and 600 adults/parents (270 women and 330 men), 5% PwDs who have displayed symptoms of psychosocial distress. This includes survivors of human rights violations, GBV cases and tortured cases. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multidisciplinary teams at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khan Younis. GCMHP used clinical procedures manual as the main guideline for providing specialized mental health services. It is worth mentioning that GCMHP community centers and facilities are equipped and suitable for the inclusion of all age and gender categories and also people with disabilities and facilitate the accessibility to its services through different tools such as Free hotlines, platforms, transportation and home visits. The therapists will provide an orientation to the patients about PSEA and relevant confidential complaints mechanisms. 2) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) for 1200 persons including 720 adults/parents (324 women and 396 men) and 480 children (196 girls and 284 boys), 5% PwDs affected by conflict-related violence. 5 Psychologists will provide psychotherapy and counseling sessions to affected persons at 5 mobile clinics that will be hosted at community-based organizations CBOs covering all Gaza Strip governorates to facilitate reaching the cases in their communities, with less stigma, and free of charge. The psychologists at mobile clinics will provide psychotherapy and counseling sessions to cases with mild and moderate symptoms who do not need medications. Severe cases and other cases in need for medications will be referred to GCMHP community centers. The psychologists will provide an orientation to the patients about PSEA and relevant confidential complaints mechanism.3) Provision of free telephone counseling service for 1200 persons including 303 children (123 girls and 180 boys) and 897 adults/parents (422 women and 475 men) who couldn’t reach mental health centers due to economic, social or emergency settings' barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psychoeducation, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centers. This service provides another differentiated aspect, where all GBV and protection cases will be detected and referred to women's protection organizations and other service providers in Gaza Strip. The trained counselors will provide an orientation to the patients about PSEA and relevant complaints mechanism. Children are cases addressed by mothers or fathers seeking help. Parents are provided with psychoeducation and counseling about how to deal with their children's problems (emotional, behavioural) especially during and after crises. Boys and girls (85% of them 16 -18 years) call hot line to receive individual counseling. The annual snapshots of this service 2021, showed that the parents discussed problems regarding 397 girls (47% from age 0 to 9 years, 33% from 10 to 15 years, and 20% from 16 to 18 years), and 645 boys (48% from age 0 to9 years, 38% from 10 to 15 years, and 14% from 16 to 18 years). 4) Building the capacities of 50 protection (GBV, CP, MHPSS) staff (25 men and 25 women) through training and supporting them in self-care and stress management. GCMHP use international manuals for self-care. This will be provided through two days training course, for each of the three groups. 5) Building the capacities of 50 non-child protection professionals (25 men and 25 women) through training and supporting them in identification and referral, via GCMHP trainers. GCMHP has a long experience in providing this training to many service providers. Two days training course, for each of the three groups will be conducted. GCMHP received referrals from all organizations and members of local clusters, networks, and relevant working groups (such as: PC, HC, MHPSS-WGs, CP-WG, GBV-Ws, Disability-WG, PNGO, etc).In cooperation with these clusters and working groups, GCMHP provide SMH services to those referrals. In addition, GCMHP uses service directory platform to report referrals.6) Building the capacities of 50 healthcare providers and community workers (25 men and 25 women) through providing them with three training days on MHPSS, including mhGAP, that would contribute to integrating mental health into health facilities. Each training for the three constructing groups will be five hours per day. GCMHP is using the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings as a training guideline, in addition to using the MH-GAP Guide.7) Conducting 24 mental health awareness/de-stigmatization public awareness meetings and 5 online interactive discussions with local community members via GCMHP professionals. 600 members are expected to participate in the face-to-face meetings, and 500 to participate effectively in the online interaction discussions where GCMHP professionals will answer the questions and concerns. 8) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns, findings and observations among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.GCMHP Users: the users of GCMHP community centers are adults/parents and children who are direct or indirect victims of human rights violations. Individuals have direct/indirect personal experience in the context of violence. The access of beneficiaries to the GCMHP is ensured by addressing economic, physical or social limitations (providing transportation for cases, conducting home visits, and free telephone counseling).

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

GCMHP is committed in its charter to professional ethics and respect for the privacy of the target groups under the care of GCMHP. Case files are kept strictly confidential and not disclosed without the consent of the client (patient) and/or family. Being mindful that actions and services respond to assessed needs, and placing physical, social, emotional, and mental wellbeing at the centre of the care provided. In addition, GCMHP is a learning organization that regularly applies analysis, studies and assessments to identify the needs and mitigate risks. Also, please consider the following:First: GCMHP will implement the project activities at its community centers with all arrangements and safety measures for the clients and staff guaranteed, with full adherence to precautionary safety measures. In addition, in crises, the GCMHP Therapeutic services provided to clients at the community centers were continued with taking mitigation measures which include reducing the exposure through increasing the duration between the follow-up sessions for clients (from 3-5 weeks instead of 1-2 weeks), in addition, to conduct home visits and deliver medications needed to clients. Second: GCMHP made adaptations such as setting ramps and railings to its facilities so that all individuals and groups can access and use the facilities safely and with dignity. Third: all GCMHP facilities, policies, clinical procedures, systems, forms, templates, and instructions ensure the confidentiality and privacy of patients and respected all forms of consultation, counseling, and personal information sharing. On the other hand, the therapy rooms are well separated from public spaces or the waiting area. GCMHP facilities and services respect the gender needs and concerns especially the women and girls’ needs, (GCMHP services can be accessed by women and girls, as more than 50% of GCMHP beneficiaries are women and girls). GCMHP was and still is requesting consent to collect and use data and makes sure the beneficiary is clearly informed of all services and has the capacity to give informed consent. GCMHP has also a well-designed and protected MIS system for patients that secure data storage. Fifth: GCMHP has many policies such as a gender mainstreaming policy, child safety policy and security policy that make sure that there is a variety of staff (male, female) who are well trained on Gender policy. Sixth: GCMHP was and still distributing PFA toolkits/gifts to the children and medicine in the times of lockdown at home away from the public eye. Seventh: The GCMHP services including telephone counseling ensure that services are provided at an adequate time when individuals can realistically access them. Eighth: GCMHP has MEAL system that considers data disaggregated by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g. persons with disabilities). Ninth: to ensure and strengthen the PSEA policy, GCMHP updated the recruitment forms and contract templates and included standard clauses on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance. These templates will be used also in all projects implemented by GCMHP.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

GCMHP is always working to strengthen its accessibility to vulnerable groups considering the crises and emergency context. GCMHP adapts and equipped its facilities whether the community centres or the mobile clinics with hygiene and safety measures and considered the barriers that may prevent affected communities, including the crises-affected groups, from accessing its services, so for example, GCMHP tried to overcome the movement restrictions via reaching the communities via online facilities such Zoom and Skype, WhatsApp, Social media outlets and SMSs. In addition, GCMHP established mobile clinics in the most marginalized communities and also mobilize PFA teams to conduct home visits. Also, GCMHP is coordinating with MoH and MOSD to deliver mental health services to all affected beneficiaries who can benefit from the services either remotely or via home visits. Also, GCMHP disseminated its psychiatrists and psychologists work specific phone numbers to reach the service anytime, anywhere. Moreover, the GCMHP staff had trained on delivering the needed services remotely in addition to delivering that training to many service providers and staff to be able to deliver quality remote services. Different awareness materials through different outlets and encouraging referrals for those in need of mental health support had always been considered. A special emphasis is placed on reaching out to the most vulnerable including persons living with a disability or suffering from high levels of trauma. As the only NGO in the Gaza strip providing specialized mental health services to cases referred by all organizations working at all levels of MHPSS for all 4 risk levels of the risk pyramid (for example child protection organizations, medical care centers, women's care centres, etc). Also, following the defined referral pathways GCMHP refer cases to those organizations and also using to provide the needed capacity-building programs. A flexible response approach ensures marginalized groups, areas impacted by new eruptions of violence, or emerging and urgent needs can be responded to. The GCMHP strategy has also a value regarding independence and non-political affiliation, as GCMHP is a non-political, independent organization. It is not influenced by political parties or ideologies. Mental health care is provided solely on the basis of need, without discrimination or preference. GCMHP is a key member in many clusters such as PC, HP, MHPSS and CP WGs, and PNGO’s rehabilitation cluster. GCMHP is always consults with the Protection Cluster including the GBV and Child Protection sub-clusters, about power dynamics in the area of intervention and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access or discrimination against particular groups. GCMHP coordinate also with specialized agencies to identify individuals and include them in the program interventions. On the other hands, GCMHP provides its unique service of specialized mental health services. GCMHP receive cases from protection and other clusters’ case managers and also refer cases to other clusters according to the respective referral pathways. GCMHP has an existing referral system to receive referrals from other organizations. This system is generalized among all PC members and well operated by GCMHP and partners. GCMHP was and still is sharing factsheets and information with the Protection cluster, including the Child Protection sub-clusters by using many forms like 4 Ws and 5Ws matrix and other templates. GCMHP do not assume an “open door” policy is enough. It makes sure that there are other possibilities for submitting complaints that do not require the beneficiaries to expose themselves to project staff. It opened all social media platforms and websites to the public to provide any feedback or complaints.
