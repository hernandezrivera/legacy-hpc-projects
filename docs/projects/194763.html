Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements gender-sensitive life-saving WASH interventions and provide communities with access to safe water and sanitation as sustainable mitigation for hunger causes. After more than eleven years of conflict, the recent economic downturn and water/climate crisis, all compounded by disease outbreak, basic services and infrastructure in the country are facing unprecedented stress and deterioration, as confirmed by AAH needs assessments. Women, children, IDPs, elderly and disabled people are often those most vulnerable and in need. AAH’s mandate consists of supporting functional authorities in responding to crises and rebuilding resilience and preparedness capacities. Within this framework, AAH prioritizes sustainable WASH projects, while continuing to provide life-saving WASH services when lack of access to basic WASH needs becomes severe or critical. AAH operates through three offices in Damascus, Aleppo and Hassakeh, and provides a wide range of WASH services in Aleppo, Al-Hassakeh, Hama, and Idleb. AAH has MoUs with the Ministries of Water Resources, Local Administration and Environment, Education and Health. AAH actively engages with the WASH, Health, Nutrition, Food Security and Agriculture Sectors and other sub-sectors and technical working groups. AAH keeps in close coordination with SARC, as well as with Governorates and local administrations. In NES, AAH coordinates with UNICEF and NES WWG for its interventions in Hassakeh. AAH interventions focus on locations with WASH severity 3 and higher as per the HNO, and the identification of potential projects is done through integrated (WASH, Health, Nutrition, FSA, mainstreaming Gender, Protection and Environmental Sustainability) and WASH-specific technical needs to support AAH’s multi-sectoral programming approach. Gender roles and the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys, age-groups, PwD and IDPs are analysed and addressed. The identification of WASH interventions in public health facilities will be based on the severity scores of the Health Sector. All interventions will aim at meeting SPHERE standards, and address community identified priorities. Emergency WASH services include life-saving water trucking, water tank distribution and water quality tests. The rehabilitation of water systems carried out by AAH includes both the rehabilitation of networks and water stations. Environmentally sustainable solutions for power supply such as solar panels installations are prioritized, along with connections to public electrical grids, while generators are used as a last resort. AAH also rehabilitates sewage networks, including the drainage systems and the installation of septic tanks, with a significant positive environmental and public health impact, avoiding the pollution of lands and eventually of aquifers. The solid waste management system is supported through improving waste collection at different levels. All water and sanitation system rehabilitation is followed up with support to ongoing operation and maintenance capacity, to ensure a sustained service provision after handing over to authorities. Public health risk communication and community engagement activities tailored according to age and gender target the most at-risk groups, such as children and PLW. Protection and Gender will be mainstreamed in all activities. AAH’s Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism will receive community feedback and inform monitoring and learning processes. This project will reach 312,043 people (157,802 female, 154,241 male, 63,740 CU5, 109,221 children 5-17, 119,387 17-59, and 19,695 over 59 years) . All activities will be monitored to assess the impact and take corrective actions if needed. Feedback mechanisms are also in place to ensure accountability to affected populations. Approximately 70% of the funding required is already secured.

3 - Needs

AAH conducts integrated (WASH, FSA, H&N) and WASH-technical needs assessments to holistically investigate the inter-linkages. Gender roles and the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys are analysed. AAH targets locations with a needs severity score of 3 and above with multiple vulnerabilities. Assessments in Aleppo (Maskana, Membij, Jabel Saman, Hritan), Daraa (Izra), Hama (Madiq Castle, Muradeh, Suran), Idleb (Abu Thour, Khan Shaykun), Rural Damascus (Qatana, Jirud, Sarghaya) identified that community members have less than 30 l/p/d of water and rely on costly private water trucking from unsafe water sources. Water tanks are needed (Maskana, Hama, Hassakeh) to ensure enough and safe storage of water. Sewage systems in many locations are damaged leading to unsafe disposal practices. The solid waste management systems also present several gaps with garbage often piling in the streets. Hygiene materials are unaffordable and women and girls cannot access appropriate items for menstrual hygiene management. Hassakeh city relies on humanitarian agencies for water trucking, boreholes in Tal Hmis, Yarobiah and Qamishli need rehabilitation, and the water pipelines in Hassakeh city need repair. The above issues represent serious public health risks compounded by the current cholera outbreak, the power crisis and the economic downturn which further hinder both availability and access to WASH services as per SPHERE standards.

4 - Implementation of activities

Emergency WASH: AAH provides life-saving water trucking, water quality tests, distribution of tanks and light maintenance of boreholes. Water systems: AAH rehabilitates damaged and malfunctioning water systems, including the repair of boreholes, solar upgrades, connections of water stations to the electric grid, repair of elevated tower tanks and the water networks at community level; repair and equipment of water testing facilities. Provision of water through public networks enables greater control on both quality and quantity ensuring an affordable service. Sanitation systems: AAH repairs sanitation systems at different levels: pipelines, manholes, and septic tanks. In 2023 AAH will rehabilitate the system in Areesheh town where assessments have been made and approvals granted. Support to solid waste management includes provision of garbage bins for safe disposal in coordination with municipalities. Operation and maintenance support includes technical capacity building for technicians, PPE, tools and spare parts provision for sustained service provision after handing over to the local authorities. Repair of WASH facilities in public institutions as the water and sanitation systems in public health centers are often damaged and therefore unsafe and inaccessible for all community members, impacting the quality of services provided in the facility. Public health RCCE activities tailored according to age and gender target the most at risk groups, specifically children and PLWs

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

AAH staff is trained on Gender and Protection mainstreaming. Limited or unsafe access to toilets for PWD, elderly, women and girls: all Protection standards are followed including lights (prioritizing solar energy), locks on the inside part of the toilets and the respect to minimum privacy. The locations of the toilets will be agreed with women, men, girls and boys, elderly and disabled and discussed with the authorities to further ensure the users’ protection. Inadequate quality of water: When AAH rehabilitates water systems, the quality of water is closely monitored so that it complies with the WHO quality standards. When distributing water through emergency water trucking, AAH regularly monitors the quality of water in different points of the distribution routes, not only at the consumption level. Regular cleaning of water tanks and trucks is also done. Child Labour: AAH ensures the proper registration system for staff, consultants and contractors, and includes child protection charters within contracts and HR procedures. There is a zero tolerance policy on child labour, SEA or any other kind of abuse.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to SEA. AAH prohibits SEA by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of PSEA policies is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in dismissal. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect, support awareness raising of the policy and report any instances. Beneficiary safety and dignity is ensured through mitigation measures such as separate lines at distributions, gender segregated latrines, male and female staff in the field, obliging contractors to follow AAH's Code of Conduct, conducting trainings for field monitors on interacting with beneficiaries, and setting up systems to encourage local communities to be involved in AAH activities and share their feedback. All employees and representatives of AAH are trained on Safeguarding, Protection and PSEA and are signatories to AAH’s ethical policies and Code of Conduct, and are therefore obligated not to engage in sexually exploitative behaviour linking sexual favors with the receipt of aid, engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age or be involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH staff and communities AAH works with have access to safe and effective reporting systems. AAH will investigate allegations of SEA in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.

Mitigation: situation closely monitored with timely communication to donors. Fraud risk. Likelihood: medium Impact: high.
