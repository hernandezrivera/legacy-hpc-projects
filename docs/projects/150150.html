Project Summary

The specific objectives of this project are as follows:
• Improve food security and nutrition of vulnerable households
• To provide poor men and women with space and support to realise Income Generating Activities (IGAs) for self reliance
The project aims to contribute to HRP’s Country Strategic Objective 3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The project aims to reduce the immediate food gap of 600 vulnerable households (HHs) (3360 individuals) by providing fresh food to those HH’s with no/or very limited access and raise awareness on nutrition and health in order to improve eating habits and consumption, particularly for children and women. The project also aims at enhancing the personal and household economic capacity of selected (100 HHs) vulnerable breadwinners, particularly women, who demonstrate entrepreneurial behaviour by establishing at least 40 income generating activities to sustain households’ food security and improve self reliance. Finally, the project will address key gender injustices by reducing specific food insecurity, poor nutrition and health practices and discrimination against women and children.
The project is aligned with the Food Security Sector (FSS) specific objectives 2 and 3 by providing direct food assistance and addressing the inaccessibility of income opportunities by enhancing skills and supporting IGAs.

Needs

Food insecurity currently stands at 40% in Gaza, thus contributing to further socio economic deterioration. According to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) the level of poverty in Gaza is four times higher than the poverty percentage in the West Bank and female headed HH’s in Gaza experience a poverty rate 1.3 times higher than those of male headed HH’s according to a gender analysis carried out by Oxfam in March 2018. In 2017, the average household monthly expenditure in Gaza declined from USD 1.063 in 2016 to USD 782. With families losing their purchasing power, and the economy becoming ever more strangled, vulnerable families are not able to cope with the increase in vulnerability levels. Adding to the already deteriorating situation, the cuts in funding imposed on the PA and the UNRWA by the US Government will affect the delivery of emergency assistance to vulnerable families in the Gaza strip. Since the beginning of 2017, the failure to achieve reconciliation between the PA and Hamas has led the PA to continue its imposition of measures on Gaza by reducing salaries of more than 50,000 PA employees by 30-50%. This is exacerbated by the delays in transferring cash assistance for over 74,000 vulnerable HH’s. Whilst these measures have a direct impact on the affected households, it also affects the wider economic situation in the Strip due to reduced cash injection into the economy. Moreover, weakened PA support to major public sectors, including the health sector, has rendered these sectors inadequate in meeting increasing demand. In September 2018, Oxfam carried out a Vulnerability and Risk Assessment (VRA) in Beit Lahia and Beit Hanoun areas. In addition to the overarching root problem facing the population, namely the Israeli blockade, one of the issues that was prioritized by the community group representing the two areas including Um El Nasser was PA Salary cuts and its implications on the general population, particularly vulnerable groups. The other five problems that were identified were debt, lack of compensation to affected farmers, the bulldozing of agricultural land, urbanisation and increased population density, water aquifer and seawater pollution and the lack of accountability of local actors. The group, which consisted of representatives from municipalities, civil society organisations, NGOs, farmers, fishermen, youth and women activists have proposed cash generating activities (micro financing and micro grants) for youth, women and men to protect their HHs. There are around 7000 HH’s which are eligible for the MoSD social safety net allowances according to assessments carried out in 2016, 2017 and 2018 by the MoSD, but remain on a waiting list. The PA measures being imposed against Hamas and strong gender norms add an additional layer to the suffering of people in Gaza that have a negative and devastating impact on vulnerable groups, especially women and girls. There are numerous factors that contribute to high gender discrimination, such as limited access to productive resources (land, labour, fertilizer, credit, technology, extension and markets), which negatively affects food availability for female headed HH’s, and gender inequality in allocation of HH resources, including those related to nutrition. For example, it was reported that women in the HH tend to eat less in order to prioritize children and elderly. There is also extremely high dependency on women within the HH due to lack of support systems. Vulnerabilities, and food insecurity and poverty in particular are therefore deeply gendered in Gaza, generating a devastating impact for women and girls.

Activities

Activities are tailored to respond to the specific needs of different age groups and sexes by targeting HH’s who have more children (under the age of 5), women and elderly to address their s specific food and nutrition needs. A training will be conducted that will target a pool of vulnerable beneficiaries who have the potential to alleviate themselves from poverty, before providing capacity building on technical skills and guidance based on gender and age perspectives, marketability, and sustainability. Additionally, awareness sessions will be held on women empowerment, female participation and decision making to address the vulnerabilities of women. Activity 1: Provide Fresh Food Voucher (FFV) to 600 vulnerable food insecure HHs, and conduct complementary nutrition and gender awareness raising. 1.1: Weekly FFV’s to the value of 320 NIS per month for a duration of 6 months to be provided for 600 vulnerable HHs (3360 individuals) 1.2: Hold 30 nutrition and health awareness sessions for at least 500 women and men to increase their awareness on good feeding practices for children, pregnant and lactating women and nutritional information on specific diseases impacted by nutrition. The activity has been coordinated with Health & Nutrition Cluster. 1.3 Hold 5 public awareness sessions on promoting gender justice and female empowerment. 1.4 Conduct a weekly market survey to update ceiling prices of commodities in shops. Activity 2: Support the creation of small IGA’s 2.1: Improve technical and personal skills (includes visioning and developing a business idea etc). of 100 HH breadwinners 2.2 Advance technical capacities of 40 breadwinners this includes training on managing a small business, book keeping, marketing and sustainability). The agriculture related trainings will be coordinated with MoA. 2.3: Provide grants to 40 IGA’s BNFs of Activity 1 will be selected from previous Oxfam waiting lists of those who previously applied for the programas well as from the waiting list of the MoSD provided by Oxfam’s partner MA’AN, the initial list will be consulted with MoSD to check the initial eligibility. Selection criteria will be based on lack of access to food and fresh food items, socio economic conditions, the negative coping strategies, specific nutrition related problems, and protection related issues. The full selection process will be done in consultation with the FSS and MoSD, the final list will depend on their vulnerability scoring as well as MoSD and FSS cross checking. Female headed HH’s will be prioritised as they have less opportunities to secure income. Women of all target groups will be provided with nutrition and health sessions as they have a bigger role in taking care of the nutrition of the family and the handling of food & health issues at the HH level. BNFs of Activity 2 will be selected based on Oxfam’s previous waiting list of BNFs who have proven to possess basic capacities. MA’AN will conduct comprehensive needs assessment, pre selection of BNFs, and distribution of voucher booklets to BNFs, as well as a weekly market survey, monitor the redemption process at the shop, and organize nutrition awareness sessions, IGA trainings and orientation sessions. Gender awareness activities will be carried out through Women Affairs Centre. BNFs list of Activity 1 will be provided by the MoSD and the final eligible list will be cross checked with FSS. Activity 2 BNFs’ list will be crosschecked with micro finance agencies others conducting IGA at the time of the project. Multi year planning for activity 1 will add value to further testing of the system to allow scale up of the voucher system. This year, the project aims at supporting 40 of 100 breadwinners. Next year, progress will be tracked and further support will be provided.

Indicators

Oxfam is committed to genders-sensitive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) across all its programs and projects which ensure equal access to Oxfam project services for males and females from all age groups. Oxfam also developed a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability which forms the basis of M&E system for all Oxfam projects. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies will be applied. The CAMSA approach comprises how gender-relevant needs and issues are monitored during the implementation and how project reviews and evaluations take gender sensitive issues into serious consideration. All tools, data gathering, data analysis, reporting, recommendations and action points are gender and age disaggregated where required. Throughout the project, MEAL will be undertaken in accordance with a comprehensive M&E plan which will track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe, and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. Data collected will be gender sensitive and disaggregated by age and using different tools such as questionnaires, Key Informative Interviews (KII), stories of change, and focus group discussions (FGDs). In terms of accountability, Oxfam launched its Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) in September 2018 which provides wider access for diverse and vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts and concerns, provide feedback and submit complaints in a safe and private manner. This system uses ICT for receiving feedback either by an online link, verbal feedback or recording complaints received by other channels such as the hotline and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers the received feedback to the relevant program staff and will be monitored by Oxfam MEAL Officer.
