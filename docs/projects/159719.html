Project Summary

The proposed project intends to improve emergency and sustainable adequate safe water, sanitation facilities in the IDPs and host communities living in four regions (lower Shabelle, lower Juba, Banadir and Bay). Specifically, Mercy corps will rehabilitate six boreholes four in lower juba and two in Bay, Mercy corps would further make sure enough public water storage facilities in 10 boreholes within the target four regions and install 10 water tanks (each 25-barrel capacity tank), including maintenance and water treatment, with the full and equal participation of women and men in the household, community or institution to support construction and rehabilitation of appropriate WASH facilities.
Mercy corps through it is local implementing partner (JF) will rehabilitate 40 shallow wells in lower Shabelle, on the other hand Mercy corps will rehabilitate 20 shallow wells in lower juba and while addressing sustainable water issues. Mercy corps will also provide life-saving water trucking to 200HH for a period of three months. On sanitation mercy corps plans to construct 200 gender sensitive flood proof latrines with hand washing facilities across the four target regions, in order to address shortage of household water containers and general safety of water at household level through the use of water treatment tablets and the use of different containers for storage and collection.
To rollout the cost recovery- based water supply system, at the outset of the project, Mercy corps will establish community based WASH committees (in total 120 committees). These WASH committees will be the community representives entities and formed with a gender balance and proportionate inclusion of vulnerable groups and ethnic minorities to ensure that the decisions of these committees are accepted and honoured across the community. In some locations where men often dominate WASH committees, auxiliary women- only WASH committees are formed to managed water points and acts as a collective voice for women regarding gender-related issues. The projects aim to reach 52632 households comprising 23, 684 males. 28,948 females, 10,632 boys and 1200 girls. In addition to that mercy corps is currently working in IDPs in Baidoa, Kismayo, Banadir and Marka implementing Somalia Emergency and Early Market Recovery (SEAM) Programme funded by USAID/OFDA aimed at life-saving interventions through water provision, sanitation and hygiene services. The proposed project will complement Mercy corps response efforts and that will enable service delivery to the most deserved in terms of WASH services, Atotal of 1500 persons will reached through hygiene promotion training and hygiene promotion forum campaign as part of Mercy corps contribution towards enhancing and eradicating the affected population towards AWD outbreak.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigating interference in beneficiaries’ selection: The project will ensure accountability to affected population by:  Being transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them.  Setting up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in hygiene kit pack, feedback box at distribution site, phone lines  Setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff.  Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place.  Organizing awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works.  WASH staff and water committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should receive protection mainstreaming training.  Identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalised groups is represented.  Ensure all WASH staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. Ensure all staff, implementing partners and volunteers in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).

The project will address the humanitarian needs of the displaced people and vulnerablehosts’ community in a manner that drives beyond service delivery, to one that buildsresilience and the ability to withstand future shocks; preserves safety and dignity in orderto improve the lives and self-reliance of displaced populations while simultaneouslyaddressing the impact on host communities. Mercy Corps will work with all respectivestakeholders starting with the participation of affected persons, the WASH, Protection andHealth Cluster Coordination team at local and national level and the relevant GovernmentMinistries. The participation of all stakeholders will build a strong foundation for disasterrisk reduction through empowerment, synergies of approaches and complimentaryprograming across sectors. Hygiene kits beneficiaries and beneficiaries of sanitation andwater assets will be identified jointly with IDP Leaders and local authorities; this willensure effectiveness in addressing real needs which is necessary for sustainability. In linewith Mercy corps principles, the implementation will be anchored on beneficiary buy-inand ownership mechanisms accountable to the beneficiary, the donor, other fellowimplementing partners and the local administration. This will help identify and assignroles and responsibilities at local community level, activation of local structures isnecessary for sustainability. Also MC has in place accountability mechanisms to ensurethe beneficiary and stakeholders get detailed and accurate information relevant to theproject and they can also provide feedback. Mercy corps has a team of technical andadministrative managers and officers at various levels who can ensure the quality of theintended outputs guided by a set of standards including the SPHERE. Training of CHWs,teachers and local authorities in various subjects including DRR will improve capacity tosustain the interventions and reduce their risks to future shocks.
