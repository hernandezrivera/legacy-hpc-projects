Project Summary

In this project, CEFOCSS is proposing to distribute combined vegetable, crop and fishing kits to 450 HHs (8119 people) in Kolapach&Aatr3 of Canal/PigiCounty, 615 HHs (9350 people) in (Toch &Nyadin) of Fangak County, 550HHs (1092 People) in (Chuil& Pading ) of Nyirol County, HHs 330 (6400people)in (Yidit ) of Akobo West 560 HHs (9730 people) in ( Motot)Uror Counties and 3400HH( 6400) in Ulang( Nyagore) County , as per the HRP 2021FSL targets. This will be followed by provision of training on improved agronomic practices as well as fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics to 5,000 farmers/fishermen (1500 Chuil of Nyirol, 1500 Toch & Nyadin of Fangak& 1000 to Kolapach&Atar3 in Canal/Pigi. And 1000 to Ulang (Nyagore)
CEFOCSS is also propose to provided livestock’s services, such as vaccines and drugs to 20,000,herd of cattle’s and 15000 shoats in Fangak South and Kolapach of Pigi Couny
The trained farmers/fishermen will be expected to pass on their knowledge to the remaining farmers/fishermen. The knowledge gained from these trainings will be of importance in building the community resilience to future shocks eg floods and famine . These activities are aimed at improving production of vegetables, crop and fish hence contributing towards reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetable, crop and fish products will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and better dietary diversity score
The project will reach the most vulnerable 41,091 people (20,645women, and 20,446men) which correspond to 450HHs in Kolapach and (Atar3 )of Canal/Pigi, 615HHs,of Fangak( in Toch)&Nyadin, 550HHs in (Chuil) of Nyirol, 330HHs, for (Yidit) of Akobo West and 560HHs in (Motot) of Uror Counties of Jonglei Sate. And 3400HH in Nyagore of Ulang County of Upper Nile State

1 - Needs

The fighting that started in 2013 has become precarious taking on various forms and manifested as sub-national violence exacerbated by cattle raiding and revenge killings among the YouthThe HNO for 2021 estimates that 8.3 Million people will need humanitarian assistance. Food, WASH, health, Nutrition and protection are ranked top among the humanitarian needs expected in 2021. This situation is as a result of multiplicity of factors all exerting pressure on the humanitarian needs in the country. . In both Counties of Jonglei state. violence has persisted through cattle raiding and revenge killings resulting in population displacement and a feeling of insecurity which prevents populations from accessing their farms to cultivate. The IPC report of December 2020 indicates that 78.3% of the population in Jonglei will be in IPC 3,4&5. This proportion is expected to rise to 84.7% by the pick of the lean season. In Fangak county as a whole, about 153,425 people will be in need of humanitarian assistance while in Nyirol ,Akobo West Ulang and Uror, 650% of the population, translated to 67,380 persons will require humanitarian assistance(IPC, Dec 2020). Environmental factors have also contributed greatly to poor livelihood outcomes being experienced in the country. South Sudan has continued to experience conditions of prolonged drought, erratic rainfall and flooding which in 2020 reached levels that have never been experienced in the country for a long time. This affected the physical infrastructure and worsened the already poor state of these physical infrastructure. A recent multi-agency assessment reports shows that flooding affected crop in farms before maturity and has greatly compromised the food security situation for 2021(IRNA Nyirol and Akobo West, August 2020). Due to the flooding, Malaria and other water borne diseases have been on the rise and are expected to persist in 2021(IRNA Fangak, October 2020). It is reported that over one million people in 43 counties along the Nile River have been affected and this situation is expected to persist in the first quarter of 2021 (HNO, 2021). The COVID-19 Pandemic first case was reported in South Sudan in April 2020 and has continued to threaten to the stability of the economy. COVID-19 had a negative impact on the economy. South Sudan is a net importer of food and other commodities. COVID-19 led to closure of trade routes with neighboring countries which resulted in scarcity of these commodities, increase in food prices and increase in inflation. The country has also continued to witness decrease in oil revenues as a result of decrease in international oil prices and persistent insecurity in the country. In a country where oil contributes 90% of the countries revenues, such a decrease has a had a net negative impact on the economy. Basic commodities are scarce to find in the market and when available prices are high, including price for food which is imported from neighboring countries (WFP, March 2020). The joint market monitoring initiative report of December 2020 not only reports increase in cost of food in Fangak Market but also lack of some of the basic food supplies in Pig ,Nyirol ,Akobo West and Uror due of insecurity and floods Market (JMMI December October 2020 . Owing to the COVID-19, it was difficult to conduct any surveys in the country. However, previous SMART survey conducted in Fangak and Nyirol Counties revealed a GAM rate of 16.2% and SAM prevalence of 3.3% among children under five (ACF, Sept 2019) and in Ulang a Mass MUAC Screening showed a proxy GAM of 11.8% and SAM prevalence of1.9% this are considered significant particularly because of the season in which the data was collected, but the findings also identified several pockets of malnutrition among the population in the expansive country ( ACF )Mass MUAC Screening ,in four counties that is Fangak,Nyirol, Akobo West and Uror Nov 2020

2 - Project activities

CEFOCSS proposes to provide crop, vegetable and fisheries kits to beneficiaries to help them produce their own food to ensure self-reliance. The project will also train beneficiaries and impart them with skills and knowledge to enhance productivity. Activities will be categorized into rain season and dry season with the aim of ensuring a year round production cycle. This is to ensure increased availability of food even during the dry season where access to vegetables becomes a challenge and therefore complicates the food insecurity and malnutrition situation. Dry season training will focus on water efficient farming methods to produce sufficient and nutritious vegetables for consumption and possibly sell for some cash. Main season activities will concentrate on crop production specifically maize, sorghum and legumes. Fisheries activities will cut across the main season and dry season as there are sufficient fishing grounds for both seasons. The initial phase will start with community mobilization and sensitization which will discuss project content with beneficiaries and stakeholders. This will ensure accountability and transparency but also encourage buy-in and up-take of project activities. The project will also establish community structures that will be involved right from inception to completion of the project. These community structures will take the lead on beneficiary selection, providing feedback on project progress and general management of project activities. There will be a strong coordination at county level with other partners to reduce duplication and ensure efficiency and wider coverage. The project will emphasis integration with other sectors to achieve greater impact in meeting household needs. For instance, the project will support establishment of kitchen garden within Nutrition centres and target mothers and caregivers of children enrolled in nutrition program with training and input support. This is to ensure that those children discharged from nutrition program do not relapse but have sufficient and nutritious food at home. The project will also incorporate WASH messaging at home, especially with reference to preparation and utilization of food, human waste disposal and provision of WASH items including water containers and purification tablets, cooking utensils and food preservation containers from other partners. The project will also incorporate Food for Asset (FFA) activities. Intended activities will include construction of dykes around farms to prevent perennial flooding, excavation of drainage channels, construction/rehabilitation of access roads, de-silting water pans to collect water during the rainy season, excavation of shallow wells. FFA will help communities establish communal assets, mitigate effects of flooding on food production and enhance preparedness and response to flooding which has become a yearly occurrence in greater Upper Nile.Specific activities are: • Establish and train beneficiary’s selection committees (BSC), Seeds distribution • Restoring crop production: -Provision of drought and floods tolerant seeds for cereals and legumes, fertilizers, training of extension farmers and workers on climate smart agriculture -Crop Production training for food and cash crops -Post-harvest handling storage and processing• Enhancing access to water for productive purposes : -Rehabilitation and polarization of water points -Provision of Garden Kits • committees (CDC) and project steering committees (PSC) • Create Synergies with CEFOCSS protection department and other stakeholders to enhance safe access to the distribution sites and other project sites for the beneficiaries and staff• Provide and conduct fair distribution of quality production inputs to 34,691which corresponds with 5,781 HH (60% Female headed HH and 40% Male headed HH) vulnerable IDPS 40% and Host Community 60% by May 2021 for crops kits and January to December 2021 for fishing &vegetable kits in Akobo West 7500

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

We have an established safeguarding task force in CEFOCSS to ensure all the issue of sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse (SHEA) are prevented .We carry out safe guarding monthly sessions to ensure all our staff are on board and are observing the safeguarding policy that includes also the code of conduct which is annexed to staff contracts and emphasizes on zero tolerance on any sexual misconduct. We also ensure we advocate for gender balance as early as at recruiting level of staff and of during the formation of project steering committees aiming at 50% female where possible, this is aimed at creating safer space for our women beneficiaries to raise any safe guarding issue that may arise. We will also work closely with our gender department to broadcast safe guarding localized messages at distribution sites as well use the existing referral pathways in case any cases of sexual abuses are reported at the community level.The protection officer will be provided time to explain to beneficiaries concerning PSEA as well as the role of community in ensuring PSEA. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will involved in identification and selection of distribution and training sites. Distribution and training venues will be located in central places for ease of accessibility to all persons, but also in safe locations. This is to ensure that women and girls are not exposed to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse as a result of their involvement in any project activity, including training or inputs distribution. Distributions will also have to be conducted and completed on time to allow women and girls time to reach home before dark. CEFOCSS will also provide a feedback mechanism. All beneficiaries will be sensitized on the availability of this feedback mechanism for their use. Confidentiality on any information provided through the feedback mechanism will be ensured to build the confidence of the community to use this feedback mechanism. complains registered will include Sexual Exploitation and Abuse or any attempt of such. These complains will be handled with uttermost respect and confidentiality and will be handled firmly. Inputs will be provided in small quantities. This is to ensure that beneficiaries will not have another financial burden to transport the inputs. Such financial burdens can expose these women and girls to sexual Exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEFOCSS has a feedback and complaint mechanism system which consists of suggestion box, stakeholder’s project review meetings, complaint desk at the field level and a dedicated staff focal point based at the field and Juba level. We also have 2 trained people (male and female) tasked at the project steering committees to handle any complaint that may come up at the community level and escalate to CEFOCSS focal point at the field level any issue that they are unable to handle. Also during distribution we always put up a complaint desk to handle any issue that may arise during the distribution.Feedback provided from these meetings will be used to adjust subsequent plans to ensure project is aligned to the context and needs of the beneficiaries. Secondly, there will be a project Implementation committee (PIC) at every Payam. This PIC will be involved in beneficiary identification, selection and registration. The PIC will include representatives from the vulnerable groups including women, disabled, youth and elderly. The PIC will also be involved in project monitoring and evaluation. Feedback provided by PIC at every stage of project implementation will be used to improve on project delivery. At project level, the project directly engages Lead Farmers on voluntary basis. Lead Farmers are representatives of the community. They will be the contact between the project and beneficiaries. Communication on project objectives and expectations will be done primarily through the lead farmers. Lead farmers will also provide feedback on project implementation. The Monitoring and Evaluation team working with the protection team will institute a complaint registration and resolution mechanism. Complaints will be registered at every stage of project implementation and solutions sought for immediately where possible or referred to other levels for resolution. During beneficiary registration and inputs distribution, beneficiaries who feel unfairly left out will be free to register their complain and have their grievance addressed either by the project team or referred to higher management for resolution. Same mechanism will be instituted during training and any other project implementation stage. There will also be structured means to collect data from beneficiaries. There will be a post-distribution monitoring (PDM) conducted after every distribution. The feedback provided from these PDM will be adopted to improve on service delivery. The project will also conduct post-planting and post-harvest surveys. Similarly, feedback provided from these surveys will be used to improve programming including future projects.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

We will conduct inception workshop to inform the target community about the project and their entitlement as well as the role of each stake holder, in the same meeting we will constitute project steering committees (PSCs), Seeds distribution committees (CDCs) and beneficiary selection committees (BSCs) that will work closely with CEFOCSS program team at the mobilization stage and through the implementation.Feedback provided from these sensitization meetings will be used to tailor project interventions to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will also be very instrumental in providing feedback on the project. Selection criteria and targeting of beneficiaries will be discussed with the PIC. Feedback from PIC will be used to adjust the selection criteria and targeting. PIC will also be consulted to ensure training and distribution locations are easily accessible and are safe for all beneficiaries. The PIC will also be involved in monitoring project progress. During these monitoring sessions, views from the PIC as well as views of the direct beneficiaries will be used to adjust subsequent schedule and type of activities so as to address the needs expressed by the beneficiaries. For instance, if certain trainings were not well understood, they could be offered again. Feedback on timing, quantity and type of inputs will be received through PDM. This feedback will be used to adjust subsequent distributions. Based on the feedback, the project will adjust on duration of time spend in training or distribution centers. Frequency of training will also be adjusted based on feedback received from beneficiaries.
