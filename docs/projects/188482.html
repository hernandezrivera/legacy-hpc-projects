Project Summary

The Darna IV project aims to support health and protection services in the urban area of Raqqa, where more than 300,000 civilians have returned since the cessation of hostilities. The project includes the full support of the maternal health detachment of the Raqqa National Hospital Al-Hilal, with activation of a pediatric department with capacity of 35 pediatric beds. The goal is to insure lasting and quality access to safe and free maternal and child health services. Furthermore, UPP, will conduct information, awareness and prevention activities on themes of protection, as the violence against women and minors and the psychosocial well-being, to increase the capabilities of self-protection of the most vulnerable people.

4 - Needs

In Raqqa governorate there are over 230,000 “people in need” with level 4 (extreme) needs and access to main services is considered by them as the main priority (35%). Health services present have been reactivated, but most of the basic clinics have a very limited capacity from a qualitative point of view: the services are reduced to essentials, specialized personnel are absent, operating only a few hours a day, often there is no electricity, with obvious consequences for the storage of medicines, the facilities are not inclusive and easily accessible by patients with disabilities.Despite women's health as key health need in NES, most services where available are far from minimum standards. Failure to cover needs could lead to an increase in the use of traditional midwives outside appropriate structures, with implications for quality and potential adverse effects on the health of women and newborns.The needs are even more evident related to pediatric health. At the moment, in fact, in the whole area of Raqqa there are only 25 beds dedicated to pediatric patients. As evidence of the almost total absence of this service for the population, from the 2 health centers currently supported by UPP / IP in Raqqa, an average of 200 children are reported monthly to private structures with an important economic impact on families, in a context in which the population struggles to find economic opportunities to get out of the crisis.

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention includes daily carrying out of healthcare services relating to maternal and reproductive health with 24/7 coverage for obstetric emergency management. This includes basic maternal health services such as consultations prenatal, postnatal and package CEmONC in line with the WHO protocols applied by IP health staff and monitored by staff UPP expatriate doctor specialized. Besides, it includes daily carrying out of neonatal health services with 24/7 coverage for emergency management / neonatal resuscitation. Furthermore, thanks to the work of community health workers, home visits will be carried out following the birth, to monitor the health of the newborn in the first 72 hours following the birth. The activation of a pediatric department in the same hospital district, shall ensure the daily and free provision of pediatric health services and cover an important gap identified in the urban and sub-urban area of Raqqa. Services will include a 24/7 pediatric ward with 32-bed coverage, 2 nurses and an ever-present pediatrician, in addition to pediatric consultations with 8-hour coverage 5 days a week and an emergency room service. with 24/7 coverage for pediatric emergencies. Moreover, periodic training and updating activities for hospital health staff to improve infection prevention and control mechanisms in the hospital will be provided, with particular attention to covid-19 prevention practices.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The intervention will ensure a comprehensive implementation of the measures mitigation of security risks identified, including gender-based violence, SEA (sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers), discrimination towards most vulnerable groups is unfair access to health services, with particular reference to individuals with disability. Specifically, UPP and IP have developed and apply a code of conduct in line with the protection of minors, against gender violence, PSEA (protection against sexual exploitation and abuse) and other basic provisions to ensure intervention dignified humanitarian and in line with the principle of "do no harm".UPP is also co-chair of the Whole of Syria initiative for an inter-agency network for the prevention of the SEA and the activation of a common complaint system - community based complaint mechanism. The initiative aims to strengthen the protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) as well as promoting the communication and feedback system on the services provided by humanitarian actors by the beneficiary communities. In the field, UPP coordinates for service mapping, feasibility studies, and regional strategy with the Health and Protection Working Groups (HWG, PWG) as well as other stakeholders, for the alignment of health intervention with the priorities identified by WHO in the NES and with the Global Protection Standards, including human rights and the prevention of risks related to mines and explosive devices.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UPP and local IP have developed and apply a code of conduct in line with the protection of minors, against gender violence, PSEA (protection against sexual exploitation and abuse) and other basic provisions to ensure intervention dignified humanitarian and in line with the principle of "do no harm". UPP is also co-chair of the Whole of Syria initiative for an inter-agency network for the prevention of the SEA and the activation of a common complaint system - community based complaint mechanism. The initiative aims to strengthen the protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) as well as promoting the communication and feedback system on the services provided by humanitarian actors by the beneficiary communities. PPOs will carry out awareness-raising activities through the dissemination of information on human rights and protection issues among the beneficiaries of the Raqqa hospital. In addition, the PPOs will conduct prevention activities through individual sessions on rights and services for potential vulnerable cases, through home visits and inside the Raqqa hospital. Finally, the PPOs will inform the population accessing the services of the Raqqa hospital about the available feedback system (Feedback, Complaint and Response Mechanism) and will sensitize the beneficiaries on the protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A key element for the implementation of the project is a context of stable security that allows the supported structure, the Raqqah hospital, to remain operational 24/7 to allow the population access to life-saving services and basic health services for primary and secondary health. It is essential that local UPP staff and IP maintain access to the city of Raqqah to ensure constant presence and monitoring of the activities carried out in the structure. Furthermore, it is of primary importance that coordination mechanisms remain operational at local and regional level, to ensure a referral system, both for health and protection, effective and efficient to and from the facility. In the current scenario, where a global pandemic intersects with a fragile geopolitical context, the main risks linked to the action are the following:- Contraction of covid-19 by IP health staff operating in the hospital and suspension of activities for quarantine- Reactivation of military operations between SDF, Turkish Government (GoT) and affiliated militias with extension of the front line following the operation "Peace spring”, Especially in the Ain Issa area. This would involve further waves of displaced persons and possible evacuation of expat staff with probable impact on activities. These main risks do not necessarily imply a suspension of activities, if the operational and access conditions for local staff and IP remain intact.
