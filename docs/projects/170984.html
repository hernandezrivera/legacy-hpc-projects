Project Summary

Somalia has been affected by a combination of climatic and non-climatic shocks: conflicts, droughts, floods, protracted displacement, inequality, marginalization and poverty. For instance, heavy and erratic Gu’ rainfall that started in April 2020, caused flush floods which contributed to displacement of an estimated 893,000 people across Somalia while in Kisimayo District alone had 9,560 families affected by flash floods of which about 370 families were displaced, leaving their original settlement area for higher grounds. Some 2.6 million internally displaced persons (IDP) living in 2,000 sites in Somalia face serious risks of evictions, marginalization and exclusion; and lack access to essential services such as shelter, water and sanitation (OCHA Somalia, 2020). IDPs comprise 30% of Somalis in IPC 3 and 4 requiring lifesaving assistance in 2021 (FSC HNO page). Kismayo District hosts over 7,000 IDPs spread over 139 IDP camps. It is the third largest city in Somalia and the capital of both the Lower Juba region and the Jubaland State. Many IDPs and returnees have ended up settling in Kismayo where they can get assistance from international organizations as well as get open spaces where they can set up temporary shelters – albeit as squatters with no defined tenure. The dire humanitarian situation in Somalia has been exacerbated by the Covid-19 global pandemic which has caused suffering and death in virtually all corners of the globe. Somalia has not been spared and has so far suffered 3,847 confirmed infections and 99 Covid19-related deaths as at beginning of October, 2020. The pandemic has also had a devastating effect on food security and livelihood for the millions of Somalis who are not able to go about their life normally. Prior to the floods in the early part of the year, the population in Kismayo District were affected by impact of the COVID-19 directives that led to increase of prices of food and Non-Food Items (NFIs). According to market assessment done by CARE International in April, prices of food items increased by an average 17 percent while those of Non-Food Items (NFIs) increased by 12 percent following COVID-19 related restrictions.
SIF has planned an integrated response to the on-going humanitarian crises in Kismayo, Lower Juba through contributing to improvement of livelihood and food security among the IDPs, poor host communities and returnees in the area. The project will focus on addressing their weakened capacity to recover from effects of conflict and cyclical climatic shocks by implementing activities that will improve immediate access to food and non-food items and to promote activities that ensure that the vulnerable households being supported are able to have sustainable livelihoods beyond the emergency support. Currently, the basic foodstuffs are available from the local markets which are well supplied by both local producers as well as from outside Kismayo area.
The overall objective of the project is to contribute to the improvement of food security and livelihood of the most vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), poor host communities (HC) and returnees in Kismayo. The project has two specific objectives: (i) Improve access to food through provision of UCT to 900 vulnerable IPD and poor host community households (7,200 individuals); and, (ii) improve livelihood opportunities and resilience for 600 vulnerable IDP and host community households (4,800 individuals).
Beneficiaries will be assessed using the following criteria: They must (i)be from the geographically targeted areas; (ii)be considered poor or very poor; (iii) come from households with large family size (7 persons or more); (iv)hail from a vulnerable HH headed by woman, elderly, PWDs, child, or chronically ill persons; (vi) newly arrived/displaced IDPs; and/or (vi) households that are living in temporary shelters due to lack of means.

Major activities of the project 1. Preparatory activities (i) Dialogue Introduction meetings with the local authorities and community representatives.(ii) Staff Recruitment Any additional staff required will be competitively recruited from the local labour market after staff rationalization to ensure efficiency. (iii) Baseline Survey; The partners will carry out a baseline survey using Open Data Kits (ODK) to document the situation, especially among target beneficiaries to set baselines for the project and incorporate the views of the community. (iv) Targeting and registration of beneficiaries The partners provide clear criteria based on vulnerability levels for targeting and selection of beneficiaries. Outcome 1: Improved access to food through provision of UCT to 900 vulnerable IDP, Returnees and HC households. Output 1.1: Immediate access to unconditional monthly cash transfers of $85 for 900 HHs (7,200 individuals/vulnerable persons) over a period of 9 months; ActivitiesActivity 1.1.1: Unconditional cash transfers and post distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys. The transfer value per household per month is based on the MEB in the geographic location; also aligning with the harmonized ECHO DFID guidance on super region values, in line with recommendations from findings of the consortium partners’ baseline survey. Outcome 2 : Enhanced livelihoods for 600 HHs through production support for poultry farmers, fishermen and cash for work. Output 2.1: A total of 200 HHs benefit from distribution of poultry and an additional 100 HHs from veterinary services for their animals. ActivitiesActivity 2.1.1: Provision of Poultry to 200 vulnerable households in Kismayo; Vulnerable families, mostly agro-pastoralists who engage in small scale farming along the riverine areas of River Juba will benefit from poultry (Each HH will get six chickens - one cock and 5 hens) to boost their household income and nutrition. Activity 2.1.2: Construction of chicken shelters. Shelters for the chickens will be constructed through cash for work using local labour. Activity 2.1.3: Provision of veterinary services for the 200 HHs receiving chicken and 100 HHs already with livestock from the target communities. A qualified veterinary officer of the partner seconded by the Ministry of Agriculture will carry out vetting of the poultry, vaccination, farmer training, and maintenance of the their health. The veterinary doctor who will be seconded by the Ministry of Livestock will be on a stipend to ensure the service is offered on an ongoing basis. SIF already has a running MoU with the Ministry on provision of veterinary services to SIF's beneficiaries. Output 2.2: A total of 150 Fishermen receive support through training and fishing equipment to improve productivity. Activity 2.2.1: Formation of fishing groups.The partners will identify 10 existing fishing groups each with average of 15 members (3 exclusively by women). The groups will be able to plan and work together for mutual benefit. Activity 2.2.2: Training of 150 fishermen on environmentally friendly, ecologically prudent fishing practices and basic business skills. The training will focus on, inter alia: basic financial literacy; basic business modeling, marketing and fish value chain; understanding the fish and its ecology; basic health, safety and hygiene; and, waste management. Activity 2.2.3: Distribution of fishing equipment among 10 fishermen groups (150 fishermen) to enable them increase productivity and consequently improve their HH incomes. The additional equipment will include boats, engines, fishing gear, as well as boat rehabilitation. Output 2.3 250 HH benefit from cash for work activitiesActivity 2.3.1: Cash for Work Activities 250 vulnerable HHs will be engaged through cash for work (dislodging of river banks, gabions and reinforcement of river banks with sand bags) to enable vulnerable HHs earn income. The intervention will target youth and women.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

SIF plans to use the UCT intervention to provide emergency assistance in a flexible manner which will enable the vulnerable persons to meet their variety of needs, including access to food, water, healthcare, thus allowing them to build and support their livelihoods. The modality is most preferred because, other than the flexibility it offers, it is safe to implement given the current Covid-19 restrictions and the imperative to protect the staff and the beneficiaries from unnecessary interaction and possible cross-infection. In a PDM done in July 2020 by SIF after UCT on Covid-19 affected populations, 83% of respondents interviewed preferred cash as opposed to vouchers or in-kind support in delivering food assistance. The rationale therefore is based on: (i) the beneficiaries will have complete freedom and flexibility to spend the aid money on what is best for them and on things that are not normally found in standard kits, rather than having the partners dictate what they should spend the money on, (ii) the model will give dignity to the beneficiaries who have been affected by disasters, and provide a sense of ownership and control in a time of crisis, (iii) the money will be channeled into the local market to contribute in a small way to enhance livelihoods of the small traders in the area, iv) this mode of intervention will ensure that there is limited contact of persons hence ensuring the spread of the disease is kept at a minimal.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Most of the basic household items and food are available in the market. However as a result of COVID-19 effects, prices have increased. Prices for food commodities such as rice, wheat flour and sugar have increased though marginally. White sugar and fish were the commodities reported to be unavailable in the local market. The fishing activities have been affected by rising sea water levels causing a temporary shortage. The quality of the available items was ranked as medium and perfectly fit for meeting current needs of households. It was also reported that more than 95% of vendors within Kismayo District accept mobile money transfers/withdrawals. Studies done by various agencies through the CWG show that the market is stable and release of these levels of cash grants into the market will not adversely affect the market stability. The common consumer commodities are readily available and the supply side of the market is able to comfortably absorb additional cash injections without causing undue pressure on the prices which affect the consumers leading to artificial price hikes and inflation. In terms of commerce, there is sufficient infrastructure to support mobile payments in the local market and therefore aiding in movement of money through the local electronic/mobile platforms. Mobile telephony penetration in Jubaland is very high with most IDPs preferring to receive cash grants through mobile money (SIF Assessment, September, 2020).

Please provide further explanation

Support to food security and livelihood initiatives for the community while assisting in strengthening of local structures brings with it added benefits of peace building and cohesion. Reduced poverty also creates the necessary (though not sufficient) environment for the communities to strengthen socialstructures for societal sustainability. In this project local structures like the IDP camp representatives, village heads from the host communities, Jubaland Refugee and IDP Affairs Commission, Ministry of Religion and Interior will all be involved at some stage and this from past experience in similar interventions creates a platform for positive engagement and for other underlying likely causes of conflict to be address before they reach critical levels. A good number of conflicts in the region have been resource-based and once people are assisted to address their basic needs including food, water, shelter and basic health, then the risk of conflict is reduced considerably.
