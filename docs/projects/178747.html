Project Summary

The project aims to improve the quality of life of persons with disabilities (PWDs) and their family members through rehabilitations and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). Assistance will be provided to PWDs who lost access to medical services due to COVID-19 outbreak via mobile teams and house-to-house visits. The project target is 630 PWDs living in overcrowded camps and sites, and overpopulated areas in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates in northwestern Syria.

4 - Needs

According to 2021 Mine Action Needs, for each known explosive accident, an average of 1.5 people are killed and 2 people are physically injured. Among direct victims of explosive ordnance of have lost a body part or function, almost 50% suffered a limb amputation, and 20% lost a sensorial function (eyesight, hearing). 2 of 3 survivors will sustain lifelong impairment. In northwestern Syria, there are a lot of PWDs including victims of war. 40% of IDPs older than 12 years have some disabilities. More than 76% of households in IDP camps have at least one PWD in their family members. Majority of them are facing serious difficulties as mentioned above. On the other hand, humanitarian activities for PWDs are halted or postponed in reality. Among them, loss of medical access would be a critical factor to worsen PWDs quality of life. In target areas, public transportation was stopped after COVID-19 outbreak, which is the main cause of preventing PWDs from medical access. Additionally, 74% of Syrian population over 54 years old is PWDs. A high ratio of elderly people in the areas made medical access difficult. According to the report from our partner (HIH) conducted in the part of Dana Sub-district and Afrin Sub-district, more than 1,500 people lost medical access and required assistance. Since this survey is conducted in limited areas, it is assumed that more PWDs in the target areas required assistance. To respond the situation, through the project, AAR Japan aims to improve the PWDs’ and their family members’ quality of life through physical rehabilitations and MHPSS.

5 - Implementation of activities

1st. Selection of Target Areas: AAR Japan shall select potential target areas in Idleb, Dana, Ma'arat Tamsrin sub-districts in Idleb Governorate and Afrin sub-district in Aleppo Governorate considering difficulties of medical access. HIH field team shall visit then the areas checking actual situations of selected areas such as public services and the number of PWDs. Accordingly, AAR Japan shall determine the project areas. 2nd. Verification of Beneficiaries: AAR Japan shall first collect data of PWDs from medical institutions, local councils, and other actors, then select vulnerable PWDs, with reference to AAR Japan’s standard. 3rd. Assessment of Individual Needs: Based on the collected data, HIH field team shall carry out house-to-house visit. Using the needs assessment form prepared by AAR Japan, HIH will assess needs based on the condition and degree of disability. 4th: Development of Individual Assistance Plan: AAR Japan shall organize a committee to select 630 households to provide support. HIH shall draft assistance plan for each household, and AAR Japan shall then confirm and authorize the plan. 5th. Delivery of Individual Assistance: HIH field team shall visit each household to provide assistive devices and specialized services. when delivering the assistive devices, Physiotherapists will be there to instruct beneficiaries and their families on proper use of devices. 6th. Monitoring: AAR Japan M&E Assistant shall join HIH field team to confirm activities are implemented properly. AAR Japan’s physiotherapist in Turkey shall review the monitoring reports, and provide feedback when necessary. Satisfaction survey and PDM will be conducted after all the planned services are delivered.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks related to provision of services: local population may become reliant on assistance and medical capacity provided by humanitarian organizations. In addition, External support to local communities undermines long-term capacity sustainability. The mitigation measures for these risks: coordinating with development organizations to ensure establishment of local medical capacities; and "developing projects that can be sustained on a long-term basis without NGO support". AAR Japan and its partner uphold the 9 commitments of the CHS throughout the project, putting the Code of Conduct in practice. To avoid conflict with communities or camps due to misinformation of the services, pre-coordination with local councils /camp managers shall take place to explain services to beneficiaries, and to ensure beneficiaries understand their rights and entitlements. Paying particular attention to women, children, PWDs, and other vulnerable members, measures such as allocating a female staff in the field team will be taken to facilitate communication with communities and people affected, and to ensure that their feedbacks are properly reflected. All complaints will be addressed in a confidential and appropriate manner. In case complaints and tip-offs regarding sexual abuse or exploitations are received, the PSEA Officer shall intervene; an investigation team, under direct chain of command of Chief Executive Officer in HIH Turkey Office, shall report to PSEA committee – the committee is held with the participation of other organizations – and verify facts on the case. HIH MEAL team shall report misconducts in the field in accordance with policies on preventing such acts.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As a precondition for the project to be implemented, HIH staff members must be able to safely operate in the selected target areas; however, these risks are assumed: deterioration in security situation, intervention by armed groups, closure of border between Turkey and Syria, restriction of humanitarian activities within Syria by Turkish or Syrian governments, and the outbreak of COVID-19. Depending on the situation in the areas of operations, the project will be suspended until safe operations for HIH staff members can be ensured. In case no signs of recovery in security situation or improvement through coordination with relevant actors can be seen, AAR Japan shall consider changing access routes and target areas, or ceasing the project. Security assessment will take place before any field activity to check the security situation in the planned area of operation. To mitigate the risk of the spread of covid-19 in northwestern Syria (21.000 As of 21 January 2021), both AAR Japan and HIH have adopted their own policies to prevent Covid-19 infections among the staff members and beneficiaries. All staff members are required to abide by polices and restrictions imposed by the organizations and the local authorities, wear masks and gloves, wash hands on regular basis, measure body temperature, and take other precautious measures as necessary. In addition, HIH staff members have received a training recommended by WHO on Covid-19 prevention and control; field activities shall also incorporate safety guidance developed by the relevant clusters. Placing the safety of the staff members and beneficiaries at the utmost priority, field activities will be postponed and rescheduled in case any security concerns are confirmed.
