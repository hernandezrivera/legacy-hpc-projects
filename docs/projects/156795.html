Needs

Complex and interlinked protection needs continue throughout Syria, driven by a range of situations including active hostilities, new and protracted displacement and dynamics linked to return increasing the stress on already overburdened communities. According to needs assessment initiatives, the occurrence of multiple protection issues in the same community is not uncommon . This environment continues to require a sustained presence of protection actors, capable of interacting with communities, to intervene and provide specialized services to respond to the most urgent needs, to mitigate risks and to contribute and build a favorable environment for the entire population, with particular attention to groups with specific needs and/or who are at risk. In several areas, the protracted effects of the crisis have disrupted social services and weakened institutions; have put strain on family coping mechanisms and community safety nets; and have left a legacy of psychological distress. The socio-economic impact of the crisis remains a main cause for families to continue to be highly dependent on humanitarian assistance and to resort to harmful coping strategies, which affects women, adolescent girls and children in particular. Such conditions affect multiple groups within communities, including the displaced, returnees, hosting communities and Palestine refugees. In addition, the strain and poverty generated by the protracted crisis may also cause marginalization and the neglect of groups with specific needs such as the elderly and persons with disabilities. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a major protection risk, particularly in forms such as domestic and family violence, child marriage and the fear of sexual violence and harassment. The lack of understanding for the need for GBV services in some communities remains high, indicating a need to strengthen GBV awareness, referral pathways, as well as outreach and prevention activities. Disruption of traditional safety nets and the reduction of livelihood opportunities, in addition to sudden displacement, put many women and girls at risk of various types of GBV that affect their lives in different ways, aggravating protection concerns such as psychosocial stress. (Protection HRP strategy 2019 draft) According to Al Tamayoz presence in East Ghota from June 2018 till January 2019 targeting men, women, boys and girls through distributing food and In-kind assistance in addition to health services, and according to survey that has been conducted by Al Tamayoz many needs have been observed regarding protection issues: 90% of women are exposed to protection risks, the ratio of female hose hold families is about 73%, Early marriage is observed in 70% of the families. Most women prefer not to talk about their risks and there is a huge need for raising awareness about GBV issues. Elderly percentage is high in East Ghota and old people are at higher risk of being deprived from protection services.

Implementation of activities

Activities will be in line with sector objectives No. 3 and will be implemented in a new WSS center in Hamoria- Rural Damascus in addition to 2 mobile teams that can reach Jesreen and Beit Sawa , the staff will be deployed from Danascus that will include PSS providers, case managers, administrative, financial staff, and volunteers. The staff will receive adequate training and capacity building regarding GBV issues.Planned activities are: 1-Provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including case management, psychosocial support, individual and group counselling, and reinforce referral pathways 2-Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a particular focus on most at risks groups, this will also include awareness raising sessions and dignity kits distribution in addition to life skills training and vocational training 3-Tranin GBV actors

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: difficulties in movement due to security issuesLikelihood: low. impact: mediumMitigating measure: avoid conducting the activities afternoon or at late time, avoid conducting activities in orchards Risk: staff turn overLikelihood: high. impact: highMitigating measure: capacity building for more than the required project staff to maintain a continuity of the core activities, Use technical resources to continuously train staff.Risk: lack of electricity and water in the area that might affect the implementation efficiency Likelihood: high. impact: mediumMitigating measure: support the center with generator or led lamb, support the center with tank for water Risk of staff refusal to work in far areas from their homes Likelihood: high. impact: highMitigating measure: support transportation for staff (rent a bus to take the staff from Damascus and return them back according to agreed-upon working hours) Risk: lack of well qualified staff in the area of implementation due to the lack of education opportunities during the past years Likelihood: high. impact: lowMitigating measure: capacity building for some people from the implementation area. Risk: Displacement of women, girls and staff during project implementation, this will affect the mobility of beneficiaries/staff and results in low impact of the project. Mitigating measures: train staff on mobile approach and follow up with displaced women and girls
