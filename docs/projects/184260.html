Project Summary

FAO Somalia will support the WASH Cluster’s humanitarian efforts in Somalia to address the impact of drought and water insecurity on vulnerable communities. This project will contribute to the WASH Cluster’s strategic objectives 1 and 2 by supporting and informing humanitarian safe water interventions.
After two consecutive seasons of poor and erratic rainfall distribution in 2021 and weather projections indicating a third below-average season from October-December 2021 (Deyr), Somalia is currently experiencing severe drought conditions in many parts of the country . It is expected that the forecasted rains will not be as effective as what is needed to reduce the drought conditions, and a return to normal rainfall patterns is required. This will have an effect on livelihoods, income sources, food security, and water security. Drought impacts are more severe in central regions (Jubbaland, Southwest State, and Galmadug, and parts of Puntland) where the consecutive seasons of poor rainfall have led to deteriorated water resources and pasture conditions. This has triggered livestock migration and increased competition for water resources, potentially increasing opportunity for conflict; a Conflict Analysis conducted in 2019 indicated conflicts between farmers and pastoralists are exacerbated during dry conditions when unclear boundaries lead pastoralists to bring livestock into agricultural lands to reach pasture and water.
The proposed project aims to provide up-to-date information on the strategic water sources that communities depend on, especially during periods of drought conditions. The livelihoods of Somali communities heavily depend on water resources, and information on this scarce resource is crucial for communities’ long-term planning of economic activities and interventions. For the pastoralists, provision of water for livestock is as important as domestic water supply. For such communities lack of sufficient quality and quantity of water could trigger water borne diseases, as people share with their animals the small amounts available.
FAO, through the Somalia Water and Land Management Unit (SWALIM), has maintained a database of water sources across the country, which was last updated in 2019 through the support of UNICEF. This database has information on the operational status, quality, and quantity of water points, and can be used to make informed decisions on water supply for domestic and other purposes, in line with communities' demands. Through this project, a one-time national survey will be carried out to update the national database on the current status of existing water sources, and at the same time map newly established sources. Using the updated database, key monitoring sites will be identified in consultation with the Ministry of Water from the Federal and State Governments. Weekly monitoring will be done at the selected sites, and in 15 locations telemetric groundwater monitoring station installed. Government staff will also be trained on the job for all these activities, as a way of capacity building.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

FAO has a toll-free call center that is open for beneficiaries to anonymously report sexual harassment and any other exploitation cases they may encounter during project implementation. All reported cases are thoroughly investigated, and action taken accordingly. The hotline number is disseminated through posters, radio messages and bulk SMS for all beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. Field monitors are also sent out regularly to the target communities to consult with beneficiaries, track progress and evaluate the impact of interventions. FAO also uses the call center to verify that the target beneficiaries identified in the selection process meet the selection criteria. All partners and FAO staff are trained on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and adhere to a strict code of conduct on sexual harassment in all its forms.
