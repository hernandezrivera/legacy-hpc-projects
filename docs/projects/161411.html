Project Summary

Help Restore Youth (HeRY) aims to support complementary livelihoods activities to 9,672 vulnerable households through provisions of fishing and vegetable kits, and related building trainings. This project targets vulnerable communities in the two payams of Mayom Wel and Wunlang in Aweil East County. These locations whose access to agricultural inputs: seeds, farming and fishing tools are limited. This is to mitigate the risk of the vulnerable 9672 households from sliding into famine (IPC Report June, 2019). The project aims at increasing the availability of alternative sustainable livelihood coping strategies, supplement dietary intakes and diversified livelihoods for the vulnerable households. HeRY, on behalf of the Food Security Cluster, will be responsible of the overall implementation which includes community engagement, beneficiary preparations, distribution of inputs as well as providing related training skills. The project will be implemented in close collaboration with relevant stakeholders, who among others include, NBeG State FSLC, States Ministry of Agriculture, RRC, CADs and the communities. HeRY will also be responsible for carrying out monitoring and reporting project activities. The project is expected to lasts for 6 months.

1 - Needs

In the FEWS NET South Sudan Food Security Outlook of September 2019, it is projected that through January 2020 Increased food access is expected to reduce the magnitude of household food consumption gaps in most areas, though households who lost crops to flooding or did not harvest and households who face more limited access to markets or humanitarian food assistance are still expected to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan of 2019 in 2020, in Aweil East alone, at least 335,201 persons will need food assistance with 35% (117,000) in crisis while 25% (84,000) in emergency classifications. Notably these two categories combined account for at least 60% of the total population in Aweil East. Also the climatic conditions in Aweil East this year have drastically changed for the worse over the past half of the year; from a delayed rainfall in May to floods by August, 2019.The drivers of the disaster in Aweil east include climate change characterized by delayed rains/prolonged droughts followed by flooding that engulfed almost the entire county. Also the present macroeconomic crisis has abated low purchasing power and limited available livelihood opportunities including access to markets.Resultantly, households have been displaced from their homesteads; there has been increase in incidence of livestock and human diseases, loss of planted crops to floods and a net reduction or depletion of in food stocks at house hold level. This situation in combination and interaction with other factors have resulted in the need for replacement of food reserves at house hold level; need for reconstruction or re-establishment of shelter which adds to the burden to the already stretched Households; need for complimentary livelihood activities and human skills to provide much needed income and tap into the opportunities such as fishing and vegetable gardening supported by the availability of water sources.
