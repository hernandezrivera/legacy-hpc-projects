Project Summary

**Mercy-USA for Aid and Development in this project will support the most vulnerable people in NW Syria in many components:
1-Provision of better dignified shelter solution by piloting innovative shelter approach which comprises steel metal frame and sandwich panels (total shelter unit size25.5 sq. meter) in camps in Maaret Tamsrin sub-district to accommodate 446 HH, 2802individuals (Men 545- Women 626- Boys 706- Girls 925) with ramp in each shelter unit ensure accessibility for people with disabilities in all shelter units and facilities, privacy, and safety for all vulnerable groups.
Lighting pools will be provided in the targeted camps for the main roads to ensure GBV mitigating and protection risks might arise
Furthermore, Mercy-USA will install special latrines from the Turkish type with handrails for persons with disability to access the facilities.
Shelter units will be purchased by open tender and will be delivered in packs to Syria. The shelter units will be installed by contractor. On the other hand, infrastructure preparations including gravelling, ramps, PWD latrines and handrails will be done through contractor
2-Cash for winter (150 USD) for each HH one-off during November and December 2023 in Idleb, Maaret Tamsrin, Darkosh and Jandairis sub-districts
Number of beneficiaries for cash for winter who will be targeted will be 11,000 HH - 59,031individuals (Men 12,208- Women 14,075- Boys 17,082- Girls 15,666)
3-Provision of in-kind eco-friendly biofuel, and stove kits (in line with SNFI activity 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance) in Maarrat Tmasrin, Idleb, Dana, Jandairis sub-districts from January 2023 until February 2023
Number of beneficiaries for in-kind fuel: 10,000 HH - 50,000 individuals (Men 6000- Women 9000- Boys 17000- Girls 18000)
Total number of beneficiaries who will be targeted in 2023 is 21,000HH - 109,031 individuals (Men 18,208- Women 23,075- Boys 34,082- Girls 33,666) from them 10,000 HH who received one round of in-kind fuel in January and February 2023

3 - Needs

Over 2 million IDPs live in last resort sites, the vast majority in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates, in particularly inadequate, temporary shelters such as tents and makeshift shelters which provide limited protection against the elements and require frequent replacement and/or repair (Syria HNO 2022 draft) According to Mobility and Needs Monitoring report which was launched by HNAP in October 2022:**Total population in NW Syria 4.59 million people from them 2.8 million IDPs**The priority is NFI of IDPs at sub-districts level: for 60% in Idleb , 63% in Maaret Tamsrin, 63% in Darkosh and 41% in Jandairis According to the recent gap analysis by Shelter NFI cluster : huge winter assistance gap was identified in the targeted locations Based on IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM): As of October 2022, the CCCM database includes 1,430 IDP sites that host 1,859,651 IDPs or 354,655 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 74,983 IDPs are reported to be persons with disabilities

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA Risks:•Overcrowded during the Fuel distributions/ redemption which may cause SEA•Mixed distribution lines for males and females•All distribution staff are males•The distribution is not accessible to all gender groups•No shared clear information about timing and content of distribution•No clear procedure regarding loss of card/voucher•Taxation on the assistance•No complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) in place Risk mitigation:•Ensure extend distribution days to avoid overcrowding•Separate lines for males and females in all distribution/redemption process •Ensure gender balance in the hiring process, ideally to be 50%of the team are female•The distribution sites will be chosen to be accessible to all gender groups especially female HH and disables•Clear information about timing, contents, and procedures about loss of cards/vouchers of the distribution will be shared with all beneficiaries beforehand and will be existed in the banners in the distribution sites•Sensitization about the scope of the project to all communities with highlighting “This assistance is Free” and this statement will be printed on the beneficiary cards.•Different CFM channels will be in place such as WhatsApp, hotline, email, phone as well as physically by MEAL team.•PSEA awareness materials (such as posters and pamphlets) and reporting hotline will be visible to beneficiaries at the distribution site and/or are included in distribution kits.•Shelters with doorways ramps for PWD
