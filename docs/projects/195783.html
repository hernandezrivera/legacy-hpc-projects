Project Summary

The project is designed by responding to the most crises- affected people in NW Syria with life-saving interventions Food baskets, RTEs, hot meals, and free and subsidized bread distribution the project will be conducted in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate in these districts (Maaret Tamsrin, Ariha, Dana, Salqin, Harim, A’zaz, Afrin, Ma'btali , Albab and Jarablus districts) to contribute and enhance stabilization of people.
The project will be targeting both IDPs and host communities to empower both to face the negative coping strategy and mitigate the tension that could come out between the communities.
The project will contribute to increase access to monthly emergency food provisions for recently displaced households and the most vulnerable host community and contributing to improving the food security status of food insecure people in Idleb Governorate over 2023, with secured fund about 40% from QC fund. The project concept is in line with Sector objective 1: “Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance”
QC will be Provide the same interventions:
1.1: Distribution food basket (FB): (10.000) for 1250 for displaced households and vulnerable host community members, individual 6250 over 8 months, Number of food basket, 10,000*$75=$750,000
1.2: Distribution Ready-To Eat Rations (RTERs): 5000 RTERs for 5000 HHs of newly displaced households, Number of RTERs, 5,000*$30=$150,000
13: Distribution Cooked meals for 1,000 HHs, individual 5000, # of cooked meals, 30,000*$2.5=$75,000
1.4: Distribution free and subsidized bread for more than 21,500 HHs of IDPs and HC, individual 162,000, Distribution of bread will be over 10 months, Number of MT provided flour 5,000* $450=$2,250,000.
QC will contract with the bakeries for free bread distribution and will coordinate with the LCs who response on the bakeries and subsidize bread.
QC will be responsible for flour purchasing and distributed it to the bakeries in addition to the yeast.
Modality of distribution will be in distribution points and door to door for the most vulnerable people (women and PWD) as the following criteria:
- the households from the IDPs.
- living in the camps.
- have a more of five members.
- in the family one member or more from the PWD, or has chronic disease.
Through these interventions QC will target 143750 BNFs during 2023.
The total of goods' cost $3,225,000, and 10% of it is secured.

3 - Needs

Based on the HNO report 2023, The food security crisis in Syria is indeed ”a protracted crisis”, and remains characterized by very complex drivers and contributing factors, from economic worsening and weakened local currency, to drought-like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity and limited energy supply and population movements. as of September 2022, the national average price of a WFP standard reference food basket reached SYP 329,526 a new record, up 40 percent since the onset of the crisis in Ukraine and nearly increased four-fold in two years. The food security situation of Syrian families continues deteriorating which will lead to a possible increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023. According to WFP RBC’s remote food security monitoring, in 2022, (especially female-headed) reported facing food shortages and engaging in some sort of food coping mechanism to maximize the use of little available resources. for instance, households adopting IPC crisis or beyond food coping alike mechanisms The continues deterioration in food and nutrition situation and the adoption of negative coping strategies has impacted the resilience of households. As many as 77 per cent of households reported buying less expensive and less preferred foods resulting in poorer diets (63 per cent with low and medium diets). In addition, more than 48 per cent of households reported never consuming animal proteins.

QC remains committed to Do No Harm principle. By taking into consideration equal access, safety and dignity and ensuring accountability to affected people. The targeted beneficiaries will be selected based on defined vulnerability criteria to ensure that needs of most vulnerable had been covered.The followings are some expected protection alongside with its mitigation measures. To protect beneficiaries and Women particularly from any exploitation, discrimination and PSEA, posters will be published showing that all services are free of charge and QC adhere to Zero-Tolerance principle.Protection and GBV risks:1- Difficulties for PWDs to access for proposed support. Mitigating measures:• adopt door to door modality to reach food items and bread for PWD - training for field staff to deal with vulnerable .2- Crowding during distribution of food basket and bread that may cause infection with COVID19Mitigating measures:• Appling all protective measures (WHO recommendations)• Managing separate waiting and entry lines at distribution points, hiring females staff to deal with women.3- Violate PSEA risk: Signing PSEA and child safeguarding by all staff. Assigning complaint mechanism for SEA issues. policies of Code of conduct, PSEA, child safeguarding is mandatory for all staff and vendors working with QC, in addition to PSEA banner and posters which include PSEA network phone number and email will be hung in all sites.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

QC has a clear strategy to mitigate the risks of PSEA, the strategy includes direct and indirect beneficiaries of our programming, adults in wider communities in which we work in. It also includes QC’s staff, partners, suppliers sub-contractors and any other individual engaged by QC's programs. Particular attention is needed for vulnerable groups, including women and children, and those most at risk of discrimination based on disability, gender identity and sexual orientation, age, and religion. Our approach to PSEA is through the following:1- Ensure that all staff, representatives and third parties are aware of the high standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect individuals of SEA. 2- Having an organizational culture based on accountability where there is zero tolerance for PSEA. 3- Have an endorsed reporting mechanism for staff, beneficiaries and partners to feel safe to report violations, and then 4- ensure that reports are handled in a manner that respects due process, and swift and credible investigation for any violations. And actively promote fair investigation and sanctions.5- mitigating risks in programming by identifying the risks and designing the programaccordingly. Starting from needs assessments through project design (shelter layouts, kits components) to the implementation to closing phases. 6- Comprehensive operational, HR and mgmt. measures are also required to PSEA. By conducting relative vetting and checks of staff, suppliers.

Do-no harm approach is not consideredMitigation: Good community engagement to explain the project scope and adopted criteria.3- Financial Risk: Fluctuation in prices and USD exchange rates Mitigation: Conduct an in-depth market assessment for project deliverables during designing phase, and pre-procurement process. 4- Health risks A-Low quality of flour and food items (The validity period has expired) Mitigation: Quality Assurance will be essential criteria for procurement and contracting for flour.
