Project Summary

Somalia is currently experiencing a drought of emergency proportions. In Gedo and Nugaal regions, the severe drought has affected the area for the failure of the four consecutive seasons causing failed harvests leading to livestock deaths, migration of communities in search of water, and increasing malnutrition rates among children. According to technical release report by FSNAU and FEWSNET on September, 2021, regions under Jubaland state of Somalia had a total of 1,065,470 people who are projected to be acutely Food Insecure from October to December 2021. These people are in different category as follows; IPC2 (stressed)- 594,930, IPC3 (crisis)- 404,280 and IPC4 (emergency)- 66,260. Over 25% of the people expected to be in emergency are in Gedo region. The population groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods. The following are considered areas of concern and need urgent nutrition and health support interventions. The impacts of drought continue to aggravate food assistance needs in Somalia. The Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEW NET) reports are indicating that Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and the outcomes are expected to remain widespread through January 2022. Nearly 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of livelihood assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of the year, Moreover, approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including nearly 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. It is likely that sustained, large-scale humanitarian food assistance and government support since January 2021 have mitigated the magnitude and severity of food insecurity. Despite minimal damage in early to mid-2021, Desert Locust will continue to pose a serious risk to both pasture availability and crop production across Somalia. From October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season.
In Nugal region of Puntland state, HADMA with the support of its partners conducted monthly monitoring of humanitarian situation of Puntland and based on these assessments and the post Gu’ assessment on the outcome of the last assessments that was conducted by teams consisting of HADMA, line ministries, FSNAU, FEWSNET, and other UN Agencies and INGOs from July 2021, concerning the Gu’ rainfall status, pasture, livestock body conditions, markets, commodity and livestock prices, terms of trade etc. Average Gu’ 2021 rainfall was below normal or failure and the distribution over space and frequency was almost poor in the all livelihoods in Puntland regions. The Actual Dayr rainfall is missing in many parts of Puntland livelihoods zones. The coping options accessible to the poor households are very limited. The Destitute/IDPs households have additional distress of coping strategies such as reducing meals per day or skipping meals which can lead to nutritional deterioration and Water availability is limited as most of Barkeds are empty due to the previous Gu’ below average rainfalls and the actual Dayr rainfall failures. As a results 188,888 HHs of Puntland Population consisting of Rural, Urban poor, Nomadic and IDPs are estimated to be actually in crisis or emergency based on the assessments conducted HADMA, FSNAU, FEWSNET, and line Ministries.

1. - Projects activities

To achieve the proposed objectives NCA will undertake the following activities which are in line with Food security cluster objective. The specific activities to be undertaken will be largely be cash based interventions to improve households purchasing power and food access for households in emergency situations, this will be mainly live saving emergency responses that will exclusively target communities faced with acute food insecurity and classified to be in IPC 3.The target groups include the displaced households both the old and newly displaced, returnees and host communities severely devastated by the droughts who have completely lost their income and food sources opportunities. The broad-based focus is to save the lives of the target households by extending to them opportunities to immediately access food either through improving their immediate income sources and purchasing power to access food from the local markets and or direct in-kind food transfer.A total of 1900 Households (11,400 persons) will be reached through the following activities. 1. Unconditional cash transfer targeting 1500 households during the peak period of emergency 2. Conditional Cash transfer through Cash for Work that targets 200 Households (1200 individuals), the main activities under the conditional cash transfers will be rehabilitation of communal assets (Water catchments) de-silting of dams, 1 in Garowe and 1 in Eyl. 3. Agricultural support to farmers through provision of assorted agricultural inputs to the Agro-pastoral farmers from Nugal region Garowe and Eyl district, the target number is 200HHs ( (1200 individuals)

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The key justification for using the cash is give option to beneficiaries how to spend and decide on their households needs and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities, cash are considered by many as more dignified than receiving in kind. other justification for using cash is stimulating local markets, cash-based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

In August 2020, NCA participated in the Joint market monitoring initiative that has largely informed this response in terms of what activities can be linked to CTP. Internal rapid market surveys have been undertaken to determine commodity prices but this is subject to change with the changing market trends resulting from disruption of normalcy pegged on climatic conditions and conflict displacements limiting access to markets in some area as a result of floods/conflict etc. NCA is also a part of the Cluster led forum on MBP with the aim to share experiences, learning and improve generally cash programming across cluster partners.
