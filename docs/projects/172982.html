Project Summary

The project interventions will focus on supporting18,000 vulnerable households affected by conflict and disasters, people with special needs (PSN) will also be targeted and have access to ESNFI assistance and aim to reduce mortality and morbidity, mitigate health threats and improve living conditions. The project will contribute to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Strategic Objective 1, 2. and 3.
The project interventions will focus on supporting 18,000 households in west, Central Darfur, Khartoum and South Kordofan people who are affected by the unpredictable conflict and disaster in targeted locations through provision of ESNFIs intervention. The project will support 10,000 households of newly displaced, communities living in camps as protracted IDPs but in desperate need of ESNFIs and disaster affected households with NFI items from the common pipeline and further 5,500 HHs will receive shelter kits and 2500HHs will participate in several trainings. The project plans to reach a number of approximately 18,000 House Holds in the project period, of which, an estimated 50% will be women, girls and 50% men and boys. The project will ensure that the target communities will have adequate ESNFIs items to ensure their most basic human needs as it relates to the maintenance of health, privacy and dignity and its easily within their reach. SORR will undertake periodic joint inter-agency assessments with special consideration to female headed households to inform the project on the new and emerging situations in the area. Post-distribution surveys will be an integral part of the project undertaken with ESNFI project or other partners of the sector in the locations to evaluate the effectiveness of the distributions. SORR is a partner in the ESNFI sector and have been working in the distributing NFIs and constructing ES and transitional shelters since 2014. SORR will conduct needs assessments and verification of beneficiaries of ES/NFI to ensure priority assistance is given to people with specific needs (PSNs), EVI’S and the most vulnerable in line with agreed sector guidelines. Assessments and distributions will be done in collaboration with stakeholders, community, women’s associations, on the ground. Covi-19, Hazard mitigation, Transition to recovery will be undertaken to ensure capacity is built with special consideration to role of gender, skills built and environment is protected. SORR will coordinate with stake holders like UNHCR, UNOCHA and partners on the ground during implementation. Additionally, SORR will procure and distribute shelter kits to vulnerable disabled older persons, chronically ill, PSNs based on needs on the ground.

1 - Needs

While new displacement has reduced in recent years, people have continued to be displaced due to localized armed clashes and intercommunal violence, as well as hazards such as floods and disease outbreaks in both conflict-affected and non-conflict-affected states. Overall, an estimated 1.2 million people are in need of life-saving emergency shelter and household items (ES/NFIs) across Sudan. This includes those affected by conflict, floods or other hazards; those using negative coping mechanisms; and people in need of shelter and NFIs to ensure their minimum living standards, dignity, and to contribute to their physical and mental wellbeing. IDPs and returnees are highly vulnerable without shelter to protect them from the elements and from further exposure to health and protection risks. According to the HNO 2021and assessments conducted in these targeted states indicates needs for NFI and shelter items for returnees, newly displaced and disaster affected people.The methodology of the assessment included key informant interviews, focus group discussions, observation and review of secondary sources of information. The HNO continued to reveal that ES/NFI was one of top needs raised by beneficiaries in the targeted locations and this was done through focus group discussions with the community leaders, Shieks and Omdas at the field level to identify the neediest affected beneficiaries, SORR has been operating in Darfur and Khartoum since 2010.SORR further expanded into south Kordafan this January 2020. SORR implements multi-sectoral projects including the implementation of shelters and distribution of NFIs. SORR has strong financial and management systems in addition to a presence in the targeted location, SORR is ES/NFIs partner in distributions of NFI's and emergency shelter responses like flood, fire incidents and manmade crises in Khartoum, South Kordofan, Central and West Darfur in coordination with both OCHA, partners on the ground and UNHCR. Priority for ESNFI assistance will be given to the newly displaced persons, returnees, protracted IDP’s, disaster affected and Persons with Special needs (PSNs).This information was collected through the assessment tools and HNO 2021which are key informant interview and focus group discussion which are very effective to meet with community members and community leaders with consideration to gender which were conducted separately by the mission team and focus group discussion for them to hear their voices and ideas on how we can address their needs. The additional tools used for the assessment are observation tool which always is reflected in the field visit report of SORR field staffs during the implementation of NFIs distribution for targeted communities. Targeted project location have been prioritized by sector HNO 2021 due to needs in the locations, newly displaced IDP caseloads and limited sector partners on the ground.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection MainstreamingThe project directly addresses Do no Harm principles by ensuring extensive communication with the community (including leaders, women, youth, and children) to determine the needs that can best be supported by SORR in the implementation period. Additionally, issues such as the hiring of staff, the procurement of materials, and the distribution of NFIs will be looked at in a manner that directly address the context and maintains the Do no Harm principles by not discriminating against any specific populations and by being aware of the dynamics present. SORR has introduced one of the most unique targets since the departure of HelpAge international in 2016, by proposing protection services to the older persons and their immediate family members in most volatile contexts. As a standard practice, SORR follows protection standards, principles and guidelines in its programme designs, assessments, implementation, monitoring and evaluations for all humanitarian programmes, projects and activities. This is embedded in our programme frameworks which are operationalized through accountability and quality framework which specify clearly modalities to engage beneficiaries, accountabilities, transparency, quality standards and human resource practices required to deliver our programmes.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In line with SORRs accountability framework, mechanisms will be set up to ensure beneficiary accountability at all levels including channels for beneficiary to file complaints and report issues associated with fraud practices, a vice where SORR has zero tolerance. The complaints mechanism will be set up at the onset of the project in consultation with beneficiaries so that they can identify and agree with SORR the best channels for reporting them. SORR staffs are expected to act in line with the SORR Bribes and Protection policy. SORR staffs will also receive training and sign to these policies to ensure awareness of the procedure that are in place for detecting and reporting misconduct or misappropriation of funds if put in place at organizational level. SORR will conduct Focus Group Discussion (FGD) for different gender and age groups during needs assessment and verification, so that it can appropriately capture gender/age specific needs and try its best to accommodate this in its response planning. SORR will also try to ensure its accountability, by conducting Post Distribution Monitoring so that it continues improving its response. Also, in line with Cluster Response Plan, SORR try to incorporate local youth and women as much as possible as distribution/ offloading staffs in the response, while implement quick survey on distribution day to hear feedback of beneficiaries, by interviewing number of beneficiaries at the distribution site (at least 20% from less represented gender) to hear his/her evaluation on appropriateness, effectiveness and coverage of the distribution. PDM monitoring will be conducted by SORR and Sector Field M & E officer. Regarding exit strategy, beneficiaries that will be trained on IGA activities will have access to improved household income thus reducing dependence on NFI items and skills build on flood and fire during awareness sessions will enable beneficiaries respond to such hazards and sustain items distributed.
