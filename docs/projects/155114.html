Project Summary

The overall goal of the WHO emergency response strategy is to reduce morbidity and mortality among the Rohingya population and host community in Cox’s Bazaar. The emergency response builds on existing health infrastructure, partner network, community resources and structures. The response integrates resilience building components to foster sustainable solutions to the Rohingya crisis and for the affected host community. In support of this aim, WHO supports the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Bangladesh, the Civil Surgeon’s Office, Cox’s Bazar, Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner’s Office (RRRC), and works in conjunction with sector partners, in particular those in the health, WASH, protection and nutrition clusters.
The objective of the contingency plan is to ensure the prevention and timely response to outbreaks of diseases with epidemic potential and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone.
This project targets the 1,242,441 population in need from both the refugee and host communities.
Cox's Bazaar is prone to natural disasters such as floods and cyclones with the monsoon and cyclone seasons last 9 months. The influx of almost one million refugees from Myanmar to Cox's Bazaar has overwhelmed the government systems in a district that is already vulnerable. The makeshift shelters are at high risk of being destroyed from flooding, landslides and cyclones. Health facilities catering to the population are also at risk of damage or destruction. Heavy monsoon and cyclones increase the chances of flooding; latrines overflow and contaminate water points that increase risks of disease outbreaks. The risk of vector borne diseases such as dengue, malaria and chikungunya remain high. In addition, cyclones can destroy the available infrastructure, cause mass casualties, trauma and injuries. The damage to health facilities, limitations of road and communication access will further hinder and delay access to emergency services for the affected population.
The Government of Bangladesh with WHO has been leading the coordination of the health sector since 2017. In 2019, WHO will work with the Government of Banagladesh to ensure effective coordination of health sector emergency preparedness and response, deploy rapid response and field investigation teams in response to public health events and pre-position medical supplies in strategic locations to ensure quick access for healthcare service provision during acute emergencies.

Needs

Cox's Bazaar is prone to natural disasters such as floods and cyclones with the monsoon and cyclone seasons lasting 9 months. The influx of almost one million refugees from Myanmar to Cox's Bazaar has overwhelmed the government systems in a district that is already vulnerable. The makeshift shelters are at high risk of being destroyed from flooding, landslides and cyclones. Health facilities catering to the population are also at risk of damage or destruction. During the monsoon season of 2018, experts noted critical concerns for at least 102,000 people living with a risk of flooding or landslides. 32% percent of these households include were most vulnerable with female-headed households, elderly or disabled. Heavy monsoon and cyclones increase the chances of flooding, latrines overflow and contaminate water points that increase risks of disease outbreaks such as cholera or hepatitis A or E. Although the oral cholera vaccine campaign conducted in 2018 lowers the immediate likelihood of a big cholera outbreak in the refugee population, the risk of vector borne diseases such as dengue, malaria and chikungunya remains high. In addition, cyclones can destroy the available infrastructure, cause mass casualties, trauma and injury. Furthermore, damage to health facilities, limitations of road and communication access will delay access to emergency services for the affected population.The Government of Bangladesh with WHO has been leading the coordination of the health sector since 2017. The contingency plans for monsoon and cyclones for health sector were developed and implemented in 2018. However, the contingency plans will need to be updated taking into account the lessons learnt in the past year, the recommendations of the after action review of the past year response, and the new challenges and events as they arise. Gender and protection need be taken into account while designing and conducting interventions, monitoring and evaluation, needs assessment and data analysis. More than half of the refugee population are children and adolescents (less than 18 years old); 52% of the population in both refugee and host community are females. A large number of refugees are still recovering from the emotional and physical trauma faced before and while fleeing from their homes including violence, rape and torture. Cultural and social barriers exist for girls and females to utilize available services on sexual and reproductive health, mental health and psycho-social support. Moreover, incidents of gender based violence continue to be reported in the camps. During times of natural disasters or communicable disease outbreak, access to healthcare becomes even more difficult for young children, adolescents, women and the elderly. Therefore, health interventions during emergencies must ensure that the particular needs of the most vulnerable sub-groups are met.

Activities or outputs

The health sector, co-led by WHO, is working in a coordinated approach within the scope of the Inter-Sectoral Coordination Group (ISCG). WHO’s response strategy is aligned with the overall Health Sector Strategy as articulated in the Joint Response Plan 2019. WHO will conduct activities will achieve the following major outputs:Output 1: Health Sector Emergency Planning and Response is effectively coordinated. • Hold Regular task-force meetings for preparedness and response planning for monsoon and cyclones• Update Health sector contingency plan with inputs from the Government, task-force ; ISCG; military and recommendations of the after action review of 2018 contingency response • Conduct a simulation exercise for the contingency plan• Support the Government of Bangladesh to strengthen institutional capacity for disaster risk reduction• Support the Civil Surgeon's Office to activate the Health Emergency Operations Center and maintain the functionality as needed • Coordinate the deployment of surge medical teams Output 2: Rapid response/case investigation teams timely deployedMaintain the early warning alert and response system for epidemic prone disease among affected populations during emergencies• Conduct training on field investigation and response to public health events during emergencies• Deploy field investigation teams in response to disease alerts• Conduct public health risk assessment during acute events Output 3: Operational support and health logistics provided• Use the propositioned stocks in strategic locations to support health facilities with life-saving and essential medicines, supplies and necessary health logistics and undertake emergency procurement• Provide support to mobile medical teams The data on patient consultations will be segregated by age and sex in order to tailor response to specific groups promptly. Based on the analyzed data, specific actions will be targeted at population in the appropriate age or/and sex groups."
