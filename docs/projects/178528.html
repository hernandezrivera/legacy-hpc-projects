Project Summary

The purpose of the proposed interventions is aimed to enhance the national capacity to respond to the continuing health needs for the affected displaced people, returnees, and host communities in north east Syria, challenged with protection concerns.
VIYAN will provide an integrated package of services spanning public primary health sector including strengthening mental health and psychosocial support component.
The supporting modality for the public PHCs will cover infrastructural rehabilitation, facilities operationalization, comprehensive and standardized PHC services provision both diagnostic and treatment procedures, strengthening the inter-sectorial and external referral system as part of strengthen the national capacity by incentiving drivers, maintenance for the vehicles as well as covering the operational cost for 24/7 work to the designated hospitals (HSK national hospital, Derek national hospital, Sur Hospital and Abu-Alhamam Hospital) with case-by-case follow up by VIYAN medical staff, promoting the knowledge of the targeted populations by conducting continuous awareness activities in areas of need, carrying out a capacity building component throughout the project lifetime covering topics such as (PM+, PFA, EWARS, first aid, IPC practices, SRH and medical case management) for the national healthcare workers.
Services provided under the proposed interventions will address speciﬁc needs of women, men, girls, and boys, including older persons and persons with disabilities. All phases of the intervention will rely on close coordination and consultation with affected populations, as well as key stakeholders, including national and sub-national clusters, Directorate of Health, and relevant line ministries.
The target beneﬁciaries will mainly be IDPs out-of-camps, returnees, and host populations in areas of high severity of needs that continue to experience inadequate public basic services, overwhelming existing resources and are yet to experience relatively stable security as well as areas out-of-coverage and hard-to-reach areas.

4 - Needs

Against the background of the continuing humanitarian crisis since 2011, an estimated of 12.4 million people need health assistance and over 47,000 confirmed COVID-19 cases across Syria as of March 2021 (2021 Needs and Response Summary) with only 58 per cent of hospitals and 53 per cent of primary health care centers (PHCs) in Syria are fully functional (HERAMS, Q3 2020).Prone to political instability, violence, corruption, armed conflict, and natural disasters and disease outbreaks, North East Syria deemed to be at “very high risk” of a humanitarian crisis requiring international assistance.Health Actors will therefore focus on delivering sustained and qualitative essential health services, strengthen medical supply chains across all modalities, ensuring availability of trained, qualified health care workers, enhancing early warning surveillance systems and ensure health system and laboratory capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver timely response to disease outbreaks, Strengthening COVID-19 preparedness and response pillars (2021 HRP).Health Infrastructure in NES is under considerable strain, which according to (2020 HeRAMS annual report) in DEZ: out of 107 public PHCs (33% are partially functioning and 66% are non-functioning while 9% fully damaged and 21% partially damaged), in Al-Hasakah: out of out of 100 public PHCs (54% are partially functioning and 46% are non-functioning while 7% fully damaged and 23% partially damaged). The overall population in both DEZ and HSK governorates live below the minimum standards for healthcare workers (8.5 health staff per 10,000 individuals) (NES Severity scale is severe / extreme).

5 - Implementation of activities

A1: Provision of quality primary healthcare including NCD care, MHPSS, ANC, vaginal deliveries, trauma care and referrals in DEZ and HSK, through supporting six public PHCCs (Tal Kocher & Sofya in HSK – Aleizba, Hreiji, Abu Alkhatir & Bahra in DEZ). Assistance will be through the supply of essential medicines, equipment, medical supplies, incentives to PHC staff, PPEs, minor rehabilitation works, purchasing of essential furniture and ensuring a stable power and water supply as well as supporting the referral system. VIYAN will ensure that PHCCs are staffed (30 Doctors, 20 M-Nurses, 11 F-Nurses, 2 Midwives, 13 APHs, 6 PHs, 12 lab technicians, 10 Psychologists, 24 CHWs, 6 Data Entries, 13 Service Staff, 12 Guards, 12 Ambulance drivers) A2: Contribute to the improvement of health services through capacity building and supportive supervision of health personnel and CHWs, in which they will be trained on mhGAP, PM+, PFA, COVID19 case management, IPC, SRH and hygiene promotion. The proposed activities will be delivered through well-established coordination and reporting mechanisms including regular attendance of NES HWG meetings, MHPSS Sub-working group & health authorities’ meetings, reporting on 4Ws, Activity Info and EWARN. The project will be monitored and evaluated by MEAL Manager Erbil and MEAL Oﬃcer NES who will ensure data collection forms are designed and completed through internal reporting system consisted of monthly log-frame reports and periodic feedback reports using quantitative & qualitative tools. A3: Rehabilitation of the targeted PHCCs with the aim to restore the national minimum building requirements (repairs to the masonry system, replacement of damaged doors and windows as well as the sanitary facilities)
