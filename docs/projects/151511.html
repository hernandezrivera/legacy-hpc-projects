Project Summary

The project aims at identifying and support vulnerable population in emergency areas in Puntland exposed to Gender Based Violence (GBV) including children survivors of violence.
The project tackles both prevention and response realms through a combination of activities which rely on the consolidated experience in the sector together with long-term presence of GRT in the area of intervention.

Needs

Humanitarian needs remain high in Somalia due to unprecedented weather phenomenal. According to UNHCR, twenty years of conflict and waves of drought have uprooted a quarter of the country’s 7.5 million people. The floods destroyed basic infrastructure such as GBV one stop centers, hospitals and schools further weakening the fragile humanitarian context. The conflict was also a driver of displacement during the reporting period. UNHCR-led Protection and Return Monitoring Network Somalia recorded 407,366 displacements during the reporting period. This displacement situation increases the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV.The number of people in IPC 4 (Emergency), according to the results of the post-Gu food security and nutrition assessment by FSNAU and FEWSNET, have confirmed that the overall food security situation in Somalia has improved. While the number of people in need has declined from 5.4 million in February to 4.6 million, it is still higher than it was prior to the drought crisis two years ago. Gender-Based Violence remains a serious concern, particularly for women and girls in Somalia. The protective environment remains weak for the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and civilians affected by the clan conflicts, regions where the military offensives by the AMISOM and Somali National Army against the Al Shabaab took place, regions affected by the floods, forced evictions and where life-saving services are either limited or facing closure due to funding constraints. GBVIMS continue to show high prevalence of sexual violence, physical assault, and other forms of GBV among women and girls, particularly those living in the IDP settlements (GBV Sub cluster bulletin).The development of psychosocial stress up to severe mental disorders is one of the direct consequences of such situation, which make specialized mental health interventions an unavoidable necessity (Somali Government & GBV Sub cluster, 2017).

Activities

1. Safe and confidential identification of GBV survivors and children survivors of violence, referral and follow-up.2. Direct emotional support , orientation and confidential documentation.3. Development of short and long term Psychosocial rehabilitation plans for survivor (counseling and family/community mediation). For those cases that need a prompt intervention, the Psychological First Aid will be provided by the GBV counsellors. Referral pathways will be established to the local services, according to the long term experience of GRT in mental health in Puntland.4. Medical support for survivors with existing health providers.5. distribution of dignity kits to GBV survivors6. Training in PSS (related to GBV) and Clinical Management of Rape(CMR) for (health) service providers.7. Large scale awareness campaigns on GBV and protection issues. Marking of GBV related calender events (Zero tolerance to FGM, 16 days of Activism)8. Neighbourhood forum discussions at camp level on GBV9. Consultant training on income generating activities to GBV survivors10. Community workshops involving key influential community leaders (sheiks, traditional leaders/elders) on GBV Preventionlivelihood support to GBV survivors11. Trainings to MOWDAFA staff + GRT field Staff on MHPSS and alternative care12. A mental health need assessment will be conducted in order to assess the prevalence of mental health disorders among the target population in the IDP camps, related protection issues and access to services.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The targeted population, the local community, the local and national government leaders will be involved at all levels of implementation and monitoring to ensure inclusion of all people.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

All genders- women and men, children- boys and girls will be involved at all stages of project implementation.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Having the beneficiary population in relatively secure locations away from main conflict areas, offering psychosocial support and protection through government security agents.
