Supporting extremely vulnerable and food insecure crisis affected children in Sudan to access education services through school feeding activities

Basic Info

Project Name

Supporting extremely vulnerable and food insecure crisis affected children in Sudan to access education services through school feeding activities

Start Date

01/01/2020

End Date

31/12/2020

Project Summary

The school feeding program in Sudan seeks to enhance enrollment and retention, regularize attendance and address short term hunger through the provision of a daily meals and fortified snacks in schools and through take home rations leading to overall better learning outcomes for extremely vulnerable and food insecure children, while working closely with other development partners.

1 - Needs

Out of 9.6 million out of school pre-school and basic school age children (5-13 years), 3.6 million are out of school of which 1,816,282 girls (49.9%) and 1,820,503 boys (51.1%). While the proportion of girls among out of school children reduced from 52% in 2014 to 49.9% in 2017 the proportion of boys increased from 48% to 51.1% during same period. Majority of out-of-school-children, nearly 2.2million, are of age 6-11 years (Dimension 2 in the 5DE framework); followed by 704,315 children at pre-primary school age (D1) and 687,083 at lower secondary age12 -13 years(D3). The out of school children in primary education school age children 6 – 13 percentage is 17% for each gender and 35% for both. Of the 3 million children, out of school in the basic education category (ages five to thirteen), the number of out-of-school-children of primary school going age is 1.96 million. In Blue Nile, Kassala and Red Sea States 46.8 percent, 45.1 percent and 29.4 percent, respectively, of the children of primary school going age are out of school. Gender disparity is highly varied across states. In Red Sea State, almost 25 percent of primary school going age girls are out of school whereas in West Darfur it is 53 percent. Over the past decade, the rate of enrolment has increased from 62 percent to 68.6 percent only highlighting the critical need for education sector partners to work collaboratively and holistically in efforts to achieve SDG4. Low education is identified as a factor in the high prevalence of child marriage in Sudan for girls. Moreover, families cover 63 percent of the cost of education whereas the government contributes 37 percent. Sudan has been affected by economic instability as the monetization of fiscal deficits, loose monetary policy, devaluation of the Sudanese pound, the reduction of fuel subsidies and the elimination of wheat subsidies have contributed to rapid inflation. In addition, the capacity of government institutions to tackle the emerging challenges of economic instability and climate variability is limited. The displaced children are adding pressure to the already poor existing education facilities and because of the limited capacity. Moreover, the vast depreciation of the currency and low purchasing power of households will have immediate impact on education and more children won’t be able to afford schooling costs. By being out school, the risk of children being abused, exposed to violence, explosive and unsafe labour is becoming high. Priority was given to localities with severity ranking 4 and 5, only few localities with severity 3 were considered by type of vulnerability, age and gender from IDPs (new and protracted), returnees and vulnerable residents/host community. According to the Government of Sudan, about 36 percent of the population lives below the global poverty line, with 25 percent being classified as living in extreme poverty. Based on the latest education statistics book for 2016-2017 the level of completion for boys is 40.2%, for girls is 44.6% and for both is 42,3% reflecting a critical problem which school feeding, among other education’s partners interventions, would try to address its rout causes, of which short term hunger and drop out are two of them.The targeting is based on Food Security indicators and the unit of measurement is the locality ie if the locality id said to be vulnerable, all students without exclusion would be included in the school feeding programme. The affected people influence the programme starting with the organization of communities into Parents and Teachers Associations and feeding committees which consist of these in addition to cooks and students forming a management for the food assistance provided.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1- Continue to undertake needs assessments to identify priority areas of high food insecurity, low education indicators in cooperation with partners. 2- Undertake daily provision of hot meals to extremely vulnerable and food insecure children of school going age through partners. 3- Plan and implement the distribution of fortified nutritious snacks to extremely vulnerable and food insecure children of school going age through partners. 4- Capacity development and enhancement support for improved implementation by partners. 5- Enhance coordination and participation in sector inter agency as well as bilateral groups.
