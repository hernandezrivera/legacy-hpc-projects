Project Summary

In Syria, access to WASH services has been severely affected by factors such as conflict, economic decline, power shortages, and climate change. This has resulted in limited access to clean water and sanitation facilities for the population. Approximately 13.56 million people, including women, girls, children, and individuals with disabilities, require WASH assistance. In Dar'a, 61% of the population faces insufficient water access, leading to the use of unsafe alternatives.
To address these challenges, the WASH project focuses on improving WASH conditions in schools and communities. It includes rehabilitating latrine facilities in schools, distributing hygiene kits to promote good hygiene practices, and installing solar energy systems for boreholes to ensure a reliable source of clean water. The project prioritizes areas with the greatest needs based on severity scores.
Implementation of the project follows established SOPs, including planning, reporting, procurement, and quality assurance. Continuous monitoring and evaluation are conducted to assess the effectiveness of interventions and gather feedback from stakeholders. The EPDC has extensive experience in similar projects and has strategically located offices to effectively reach communities in need.
The project aims to create a healthier learning environment, improve hygiene practices, and provide sustainable access to clean water. Stakeholder engagement is crucial, ensuring interventions meet the specific needs of the target communities. EPDC commits to providing monthly reports, and adjustments are made based on monitoring and evaluation findings.
Overall, the WASH project addresses the urgent need for improved access to water, sanitation, and hygiene in Syria, considering the specific challenges faced by different communities and prioritizing the most affected areas.
WASH project prioritizes the rights and safety of vulnerable individuals, including women, girls, young boys, and the elderly. Gender equity is promoted, and measures are in place to protect against PSEA. A code of conduct and Complaint Response Mechanism are implemented to address these issues. Data collection tools consider gender and age, and community participation efforts incorporate gender considerations. EPDC aims to hire a gender-balanced team and collects protection-related data disaggregated by sex and age. Child protection and safeguarding standards are upheld, and a safe environment for children is ensured. A comprehensive risk assessment is conducted, and mitigation measures are implemented for different age groups. Monitoring, evaluation, and beneficiary feedback are utilized to assess services. The project follows the principle of 'Do no harm' and promotes inclusive participation. Measures are implemented during distributions to prevent PSEA, including gender-specific queues and priority lines.
external risks include conflict and insecurity, international sanctions and continued instability, further depreciation of the Syrian currency, and intervening events as COVID-19 resurgence and EQ. The measures for mitigating these risks involve conducting security assessments, maintaining communication with local authorities, identifying alternative routes, advocating for streamlined fund transfers, and monitoring prices and markets. risks related to interventions and the organization itself, include increased violence against women and girls, gender-based discrimination and exclusion, limited access to information and services for women, barriers to accessing resources, extortion and embezzlement, weak accountability and complaints mechanisms, and exclusion of vulnerable groups. The suggested risk-reducing measures involve inclusive response design, participatory activities, cultural sensitivity, gender analysis, staff training, internal controls, robust complaints mechanisms, transparent selection criteria, coordination with relevant stakeholders, and implementation of accountability mechanisms.
