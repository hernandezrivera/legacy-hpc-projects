Project Summary

WPE programs focus on women and adolescent girls being supported emotionally, psychologically, and physically while providing further knowledge on GBV, GBV forms, risks, and prevention. IRC (WPE) acknowledges the significant protection concerns women and adolescent girls in Syria face, WPE rounds its functionality in order to, directly and indirectly, adhere to the prevention and intervention measures required to ensure the increased safety measures are deliberated for women and adolescent girls. The IRC works closely with the GBV sub-cluster in Turkey as one of the major protection actors in the humanitarian field. As a member of the GBV sub-cluster, IRC programs supported sub-cluster-raised protection capacity building, protection knowledge, and protection management training material. IRC is an active member of the GBV sub-cluster and accordingly, All activities will be implemented with close coordination with the GBV Sub-Cluster and will follow the GBV Sub-Cluster guidelines, SOPs, and tools, with a focus on the GBV case management guidelines and GBV M&E tool kit, GBV awareness tool kits, cash for protection, Individual Protection Assistant (IPA) and case fund GBV criteria.
IRC implements GBV prevention and response activities including case management, awareness raising, and structured and non-structured PSS, including PM+, Girls Shine, and Young Mothers Group, establishing women committees and training GBV and non-GBV actors.
The accountability and Learning (MEAL) team, supported by the WPE team will conduct GBV needs assessment to assess the needs in the community and design the activities to be in line with the assessment result. In addition, the MEAL team will conduct focus group discussions (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) to understand the community’s perceptions of IRC’s services. Moreover, A data quality audit will be conducted on a quarterly basis to ensure that data is complete, correct, and consistent. The audit will also verify source data against what was reported. the MEAL team will also conduct a satisfaction survey with the beneficiaries and the MEAL team will use GBV M&E Toolkit as a guideline in designing MEAL activities and tools.
IRC will provide a variety of technical support to its partners, including:
- Conducting multiple assessments with the partner to help know their technical capacity to develop the capacity building plan accordingly
- Conducting various training related to the partner activities according to their need’s properties
- Provide supervision sessions to the partner filed teams and any needed support upon request
- Provide training on IRC's new curriculums and activities to help develop a comprehensive response
- review and develop the partners' technical guidelines and SOPs
Finally, - During the upcoming year, IRC will work on providing training to the health actors on GBV basics, Safe referrals, and making the health facilities more accessible for women and girls, along with working regularly on updating the health services mapping to ensure safe referrals. Also, IRC through the planned activities will be working on sharing awareness messages on reproductive health through new mother support club sessions, while also working with health committees under the AMAL initiative on engaging healthcare providers to help highlight the unique needs of women and adolescent girls and the specific barriers they face in seeking services at health facilities.

3 - Needs

As the Syrian conflict persists into its tenth year, an estimated 14.6M people remain in need of humanitarian assistance (21% increase compared to 2020 in northwest Syria (NWS) there are 2.7M. According to the 2021 Syrian Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the physical destruction and protracted cycles of displacement contributed to a steady erosion of individual and community protection mechanisms, generated high levels of psychological distress, disrupted the availability of services and strained existing community support structures.( Syria 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview). COVID-19 has further exacerbated these challenges, especially for vulnerable groups and individuals, including women and girls. Those effects include an increase in GBV, strained mental health, and inadequate sexual and reproductive healthcare as well as the heightened reinforcement of underlying unjust gender divides(Friedrich Ebert Stiftung, Covid-19, and Women in Syria, URL.). According to COVID-19 AND WOMEN IN SYRIA DEEPENING INEQUALITIES Women and girls face daily protection risks. The rates of GBV have risen inside Syria in general, including child marriage, intimate partner violence (IPV), sexual violence (including rape as a weapon of war, sexual harassment, sexual exploitation, and sexual assault) polygamy, and forced marriage. According to Voices 2022, Women and girls argue that GBV has not decreased; instead, they perceive persistent and, in many cases, rising levels of violence.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection of our beneficiaries is of paramount importance to the IRC particularly as it may be perpetrated by humanitarian workers, including IRC employees, volunteers, interns, Board Members, incentive workers, partners, and all those that work on behalf of our mission.IRC is guided in its preventive efforts by the UN Secretary-General’s Bulletin on “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse, ”published in 2003, and incorporates the Bulletin’s six core principles. IRC’s Beneficiary Protection Policy further expands on these six core principles to include a prohibition on all types of exploitation and abuse of our beneficiaries. IRC understands the unequal power dynamics between employees and beneficiaries and therefore has created a code of conduct, PSEA policies, and feedback and complaint mechanisms.Moreover, the IRC considers that: •Exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries, whether of a sexual nature or not, by IRC Workers constitutes an act of gross misconduct and are, therefore, individually or together, grounds for immediate termination of employment. •IRC workers are obliged to create and maintain an environment that is free of sexual exploitation and abuse. When an IRC worker develops concerns or suspicions regarding abuse or exploitation by a fellow worker, whether employed by IRC or another agency, he or she must report such concerns utilizing the IRC’s Global Reporting Guidelines.
