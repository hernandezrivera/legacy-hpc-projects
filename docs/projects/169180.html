Project Summary

This project aims to support the physical rehabilitation of the severely damaged hospitals, to provide them with HR support, and to procure needed equipment for hospitals in order to enable them to restart service provision. The project also entails the establishment of proper medical waste management at affected health facilities. Support to the reconstruction of the central drug warehouse will be included in the project in order to ensure the warehouse’s urgent functionality.
The Beirut explosion caused further depletion medical supplies and PPEs as facilities were overwhelmed with casualties of the blast and utilized supplies while caring for injured individuals. Through this project, WHO will procure trauma kits and surgical kits to replace the depleted stocks in the hospitals that received trauma cases, and to ensure their preparedness to receive casualties resulting from the clashes happening in riots and protests. In addition, the country is currently is in the community transmission phased of the COVID outbreak and the number of cases in increasing; a cumulative number of 10 952 positive cases was reported on 20 August. Thus, it is of utmost urgency to replenish the stocks of PPEs for 20 hospitals receiving COVID-19 cases in and outside Beirut.
WHO will also contribute through this project to the strengthening of primary health care services with a focus on ensuring establishment of community mental health centers to respond to the emerging and rising needs in mental health. Furthermore, WHO will support the integration of mental health into the primary health care package. This is in line with the National MHPSS action plan and will be implemented in close coordination with the National Mental Health Program at the MOPH. After the blast, the need for essential medications (acute, chronic, and mental health medications) increased by at least 25% based on initial rapid assessment. Therefore, WHO will procure 3 months’ worth of stock of essential medications for the PHCs in close coordination with the PHC Department at the MOPH. Moreover, WHO will be supporting the environmental assessment of the impact of the explosion and will develop guidance based on the results of the assessment.

1 - Needs

Based on assessments conducted by WHO and other organizations, the below needs were reported:-Need for physical rehabilitation for the MOPH warehouse, the 9 damaged hospitals and the 15 affected PHCs in Beirut-Depletion of PPEs and trauma and surgical supplies in hospitals that received the high number of casualties resulting from the blast-Need for essential medications increased by at least 25% for the affected communities. Around 300,000 people have lost their homes and will need many acute medications. This is coupled with the fact that injured persons will also need additional medications especially pain relief and atopic products. -Reported increased need for primary health care services, including mental health services, for affected communities.- Given that the country is in community transmission phase and given the ongoing manifestations that are expected to cause an increase in the COVID-19 cases, it is of utmost urgency to replenish PPE stocks for hospitals both in and outside Beirut.-The explosion had negative implications on the environment; there is a need for a robust environmental health assessment and for guidance based on the results of the assessment.
