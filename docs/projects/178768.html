Project Summary

In 2021 Shafak organization will respond to the increased GBV concerns of the crises affected women and girls through centre-based activities in five Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS), Shafak RH facilities and community outreach teams (12 teams) deployed in Harim, Idleb, Ariha districts in Idleb and Azaz, and Jarablus districts in Aleppo. GBV prevention and response activities will be delivered to 51540 individuals from IDPs and host communities with focusing on most vulnerable people including adolescent girls, elderly women and PWDs.
The proposed project will offer gender and age appropriate prevention and response GBV services including:
Provision of one-off unconditional Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) in the form of direct cash or via a voucher modality, empowerment activities through provision of small grants for women to be able to start new small projects/businesses with the goal of achieving economic in dependence, GBV case management, Young Mothers Club program, life skills, safety audits in 4 locations, recreational and structured PSS activities, and GBV awareness raising sessions and campaign targeting women, men, boys and girls in both IPDs and host community sites, as well as capacity building training for humanitarian actors (workers in other sectors like WASH, Shelter/NFI, Education) on GBV topics will be conducted to reduce risks of GBV and refer to specialized services.
Moreover, Shafak will consult with the community members at the beginning of the project to introduce the project activities and have them involved in the selection of the WGSS locations, as well as Shafak will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on GBV topics such as; GBV core concepts, Safe identification and referral and communication skills to enable them identify and refer people in need to the case management team. Moreover, the community committees will play a significant role in raising GBV issues that are faced by the community and introducing the project activities. Shafak will also coordinate with women-led organizations in the targeted locations who are implementing GBV projects and will respond closely to their needs, specialty technical GBV capacity building training.
Furthermore, as part of response activities, the outreach teams will conduct Psychological First Aid (PFA) within camps, reception centers, and other IDPs collective places. Shafak will engage men and boys in GBV prevention activities through mobile GBV activities, which aimed to mitigate the GBV risks, besides, to make the community more supportive of GBV issues.
Shafak will make sure to use the GBV sub-cluster tools and guidance while implementing the project activities such as the GBV awareness raising tool-kit, Amal initiative and GBV SOPs.
The activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social specificities of targeted populations. All static facilities will be rehabilitated and well equipped to accompany PWDs.
COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken in line with WHO, Global Protection Cluster and the Protection Cluster’s Guidance, including strict enforcement of physical distancing, minimizing of participants per activity to maximum eight people and maintaining the social distancing between BNFs and staff, the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) by staff/beneficiaries. In case of new and uncontrolled breakout of COVID 19, Shafak will shift most the project activities to online modality to avoid transmission of COVID 19 between the project BNFs and staff.
Overall, the project will target 51540 individuals (23920 W, 2370 M, 18950 G, 6300 B)

4 - Needs

The persistence of armed conflict, economic deterioration, displacement and the COVID-19 crisis all have a gendered and disproportionate impact on girls and women in Syria. This impact is manifested primarily through the gender based violence (GBV) that girls and women are subjected to. Women and girls living in Syria continued to face high levels of GBV, especially child/forced marriage, intimate partner and family violence, sexual harassment and sexual violence, denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse, in almost all walks of life, resulting in very few spaces where they feel safe.In the households, women and girls have taken on additional roles traditionally associated with men, including as heads of households and principal breadwinners and although they have more responsibilities, this does not automatically translate into decision making power. This contributes to increased pressure and stress for women to provide for their families, often in absence of any other support from family members. Consequently, they are exposed to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at their workplace (Draft HNO 21)The consequences of GBV on women and girls are manifold and are often interlinked. A woman who has experienced sexual violence will most likely have experienced physical, verbal, and emotional violence to subdue her and make her less resistant. Thus, physical violence and sexual violence can cause injury, disability, and in some cases, death. Harmful traditional practices can also have consequences for girls. In most other cases, early forced marriage results in a loss of childhood, i.e. the right to play and learn (Voices from Syria 2020).

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be delivered through five WGSS, Shafak RH facilities as an Integration with health/SRH, in addition to 12 mobile outreach teams. The project will include prevention, mitigation, and response GBV activities for 51540 individuals as the following:1-Safety audits in four IDPs camps 2-Non structures PSS /recreational and PFA for 15000:5000W,7000G,3000B3-Structured and non-structured awareness sessions for 19000: 8000W,5500G,2200M,3300B. GBV and COVID 19 topics will be covered in addition to GBV tool kit.4-Structured PSS activities for 7000: 4000W, 3000G.5-Empowerment activities and vocational training for 6500: 3500W, 3000G.6-Young mothers club for 350 adolescent girls.7-GBV cases management for 1400: 900W, 500G8-IPA and cash and voucher assistance for 2000W.Besides, Shafak will conduct GBV mainstreaming training for 250 humanitarian works and community committee’s members, in addition to building the capacity of 40 of the newly recruited staff on GBV topics. Collaboration between the WGSSs and Shafak health facility will be maintained using the referral pathway to refer GBV cases between the WGSSs and RH services. Moreover, the project activities will ensure the inclusion of PWDs in the project activities through: rehabilitating the centres to be friendly for PWDs, building the project staff’ capacity to deal with them, and providing transportation to enable them reach to our centers and participate in the activities.Finally, MEAL activities will include feedback and pre/post-tests for PSS activities, in addition to applying the complaints mechanism. Also GBV supervision activities which will be in place to monitor the quality of GBV case management using the GBV M&E tool kit.

Risks: 1. Sexual exploitation and abuse by the project personnel. Risk2. Tension between IDPs themes selves, and with the host community as well. Risk 3: Transmission of COVID 19 to project staff and BNFs due to project activities:Mitigating Measure: 1- All staff, volunteers and associated personnel should sign the policy on PSEA and child safeguarding, receive induction on PSEA and safeguarding children and are aware of their obligation to report harm to children/SEA/misconduct. All staff and volunteers receive regular training/refreshers on PSEA and child safeguarding Policies. Shafak has developed a policy on PSEA and child safeguarding and communicated it to all staff in local languages. Make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to a code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA. Set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children 2- clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the activity. - harmonize the assistance delivered as per Cluster instructions and guidelines. - coordinate with the local council in the targeted area as well as being active in field coordination 3--Project team will follow all COVID 19 prevention measures to avoid the risk of transmitting the disease between the staff and beneficiaries.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security related SOPs in place.1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to pre-empt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly.Risk 2: Loss and/or damages to program equipment and supplies and/or staff injured or killed due to airstrikes or other security-related incidents; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:2.1 Ensure adequate security/surveillance at Shafak warehouses, facilities, and offices.2.2 Security related SOPs in place.2.3 Minimizing value/quantity of items at any given site while ensuring balance with programmatic needs and contingency.Risk 3: Road cutting due to internal clashes and IED detonations; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:3.1 providing clearance for the field team before conducting any missions, identifying safe roads from/ to the both centers and informing staff to use it.3.2 Avoiding areas with a history of explosion/bombardment incidents.Risk 4: Aid diversion, corruption, fraud; Likelihood: low; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:4.1 Appropriate mitigation measures within the procurement process (including mandatory reviews from Compliance and other relevant departments).4.2 Anti-fraud, anti-corruption, code of conduct, humanitarian principles and other relevant trainings provided to all staff.
