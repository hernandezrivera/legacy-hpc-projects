Project Summary

Horan Foundation (HF) within its strategic response plan for 2023, will target two areas in Northwestern Syria, Aleppo where the total PiN (severity 3-5) is 1,469,924 and the number of children with disability is 102,895. Idleb where the PiN is 1,168,878 and the number of children with disability is 1,821, as highlighted in the HNO 2023 for the Education sector. where in 2020 an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase. however, the education actors couldn't fully respond to the needs of the 2022 estimate of out-of-school children.
HF works to strengthen the capacity of the education facilities in Northern Syrian and enhance the possibility of delivering better spaces for learning in schools to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children aged 5-17 years This designed project is
to enhance the overall status of schools through repairing to return to their schools or enhancing the current condition of schools.
HF will rehabilitate 40 damaged schools in Afrin and Dana.
400 classrooms and 20000 beneficiaries will benefit from this.
The rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged classrooms and WASH facilities; sanitation systems, bathrooms, and adequate water for personal hygiene and cleaning toilets relying on INEE standards. After the rehabilitation of the schools, the project will provide furniture; such as desks, chairs, and blackboards.
HF has extensive experience in rehab works for damaged buildings and schools, more than 6000 Houses were rehabilitated through teams of HF funded by many donors, and SCHF is one of them. in addition, HF rehabilitated about 9 schools in the Syrian crisis that were damaged in Southern Syria.
HF will select 40 damaged school facilities in coordination with the education authorities in both Idlib and Aleppo (where most Students are present in those areas according to the HNO 2023), the Education cluster,
and other NGOs to take over the rehabilitated schools for providing other education services after the completion of the project.
where the aim of implementing the rehabilitation work for those 40 schools in Afrin and Idleb is in order to provide a better place for the students to have proper, safer, and more adequate education. Those schools are meant to be run by Syrian NGOs that are providing education services while they can not provide rehab work. In this context, HF will provide needed services such as rehab work starting from construction works and WASH facilities in the context of the Cholera outbreak, not to forget providing proper furniture for those schools.
special attention will be given to children with disabilities through providing needed facilities for children with disabilities regarding ramps that facilitate their entrance to the schools and classes or using bathrooms or any other services in schools.
This project is linked to HRP Strategic Objective 3 to Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities.
Specific Objective 3.1 Improved access to basic services, electricity, water, education, and health.
While till the moment no funds have been secured for this project

3 - Needs

According to the HNO report 2023, There has been a 4% increase in the sector PIN, with 6.9M children and education personnel needing emergency education services, Attendance rates are similar for boys and girls up to 11 years, after which there is gender disparity with more boys not attending. Only 11% of four-year-olds and 36% of five-year-olds attended any type of learning, limiting school readiness. Rates of non-attendance were highest in Aleppo (16%) and Idleb (15%). additional data sources estimate that 2.45 million of whom are out of school, in addition to another 1.6 million children who are at risk of dropping out of school, due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19 & Cholera outbreak, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out. Schools have suffered immensely as a result of both targeted attacks and collateral damage. It is estimated that in Aleppo Governorate alone there are 42 - 55 million tons of rubble and as per OCHA reports. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. The destruction and damage of schools have led to an acute need for additional learning spaces for both host community children and internally displaced children residing in these areas.

4 - Implementation of activities

HF will rehabilitate 40 damaged schools. we will target the schools in (Aleppo/Afrin district- Idlib Dana) where its severity scale is 5 (based on HNO2023). Schools will be repaired to targeted areas that promote equity and the physical safety of learners, teachers, and other education personnel. Schools will be designed to give careful thought to who uses the learning space. Spaces will be appropriate to the sex, age, physical ability, and cultural considerations of all users. Entrances and exits will be repaired to allow students, teachers, and other education personnel to exit safely in an emergency.Project activities will include Coordination with Local authorities including local councils and Education directorates, Education clusters, and Local Education actors for the preparation of the phase-out of this project, rehabilitation activities will be repairing damaged classrooms, roofs, doors, and WASH facilities; such as sanitation systems, bathrooms (Girls, boys, and CWD), solid waste disposal facilities, drainage facilities, and providing adequate water for personal hygiene and cleaning toilets that rely on INEE standards. Sanitation facilities will be accessible for PWD and will maintain privacy, dignity, and safety. Toilet doors will lock from the inside. To prevent sexual harassment and abuse, separate toilets for boys/men and girls/women will be located in safe, convenient, and easily accessible places.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Violence against education facilities and education providers. (likelihood is medium & impact is high). Mitigating Measure: the risk will be monitored by HF field staff in addition to the report received from the Safety & Security team. all works will be stalled in the low-security locations and be prepared to apply either the contingency or exit plan.Risk: facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including Covid-19, cholera, and other communicable diseases. (likelihood is medium & impact is high)Mitigating Measure: improvement to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected. This may include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electricity, and WASH facilities, where HF will provide the school facilities with Covid-19 safety protocols (prevention measures, awareness raising, referral).Risk: GBV, sexual abuse, or facilities are not disability-sensitive in the rehabilitated schools’ facilities. (likelihood is low & impact is high). Mitigating Measure: This risk will be monitored basically by HF's M&E team. Referral path system and Feedback and complaint mechanism are adopted to contribute to solving the issues.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection risk/threats: 1. SEA by humanitarian workers male-dominated, gatekeepers, and potential other beneficiaries. 2. Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurs (crowd control) Likelihood: High. Impact: High in terms of reputation, and trust. mitigation measures: 1. Establish a feedback mechanism 2. Recruit female staff and train them. 3. Train staff on code of conduct 4. All staff contracts to include PSEA 5. Community committee with equitable representation of women 6. Train staff on Inter-Agency Coordination in Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms 7. Ensure distribution is well-organized (e.g. identification of sites/environment first; set up several distribution spots on sites; do joint distribution with another partner). Protection risk/threats. 1. Interference with beneficiary targeting/ 2. The beneficiaries list is not regularly updated by the Local councils. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Train staff on targeting/vulnerability criteria as well as community mobilization 2. Staff work/support the local councils in preparing the beneficiaries list 3. Meetings with community leaders/gatekeepers on targeting criteria etc. 4. Set community feedback mechanism Use well trained and gender-balanced outreach team that could check if some locations are not included in the beneficiaries list The target group for this project was both hosts and IDPs who are vulnerable and crisis-affected.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. The security situation does not severely degrade 2. Strong cooperation with local council, Representatives of targeted communities. 3. Stability of food items prices 4. High control of COVID-19 and Cholera during distribution, and commitments for all stakeholders involved in the project for the IPC measures for COVID-19 and Cholera. Risks and mitigation: 1. The bad security situation in the target location Likelihood: Low - Impact: High Mitigation: in case of occurrence: Suspension of project activities, reporting to the donor, continuous monitoring for all potential security risks, and sharing plan B with Stakeholders. 2. Local authorities and representatives of local council or other forces interfered in project activities and selection of BNFs Likelihood: Low - Impact: High Mitigation: Signing MoUs with local councils and representatives before implementation. 3. Fluctuation in prices and availability of proposed rehabilitation items (Blocks, iron, plastic sheets..etc) Likelihood: High – Impact: Medium Mitigation: Updating the market assessment for the items, after advance coordination with the Education cluster. 4. Community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community is not served/cannot access or if the information offered is not consistent between communities. Mitigating Measures: regular consultations with communities, focus group discussion FGDs, feedback boxes.
