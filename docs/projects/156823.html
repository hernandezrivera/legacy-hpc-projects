Project Summary

This project aims to run and support a specialized hospital that provides comprehensive child and maternal health services, including women's surgery and hospitalization, as well as ambulances and a specialized department for infants and neonatal care.
Where the hospital consists of outpatient clinics that have a clinic for women and children functioning on a daily basis to provide diagnosis and treatment services for women and children and referral cases that need admission in the hospital.
Emergency: manages the emerging pediatric and gynecological cases and provide appropriate treatment and referral of cases that require admission to other departments.
Gynecological department: It consists of 15 beds for women, where women who are being prepared for surgery, in addition to the gynecological conditions that require admission to the hospital.
The Department of Childhood Illness: It consists of 10 beds allocated for children referred from the emergency dept and other facilities in the locality.
Pediatric and phototherapy: 6 incubators and 3 devices for 24-hour phototherapy. Cases are received from the emergency and outpatient departments in the same facility, in addition to referred cases from primary health care centers and other maternity and child health units. In addition to the neonates born in the hospital who need admission and neonatal care.
Labour and delivery: Receives cases of planned delivery from the outpatient department as well as emergency cases.
The Department of Women's Surgery: consists of two operating rooms that provide cesarean delivery services and women's emergency and elective surgery.
The previous sections are supported by the 24-hour detective department as well as the pharmacy.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, Provision of health services continues to be disrupted by ongoing hostilities. Attacks on health have rendered 46 per cent of hospitals and primary health facilities in Syria as either partially functional or not functional129, while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained andpredictable access to populations in need. Sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need across the country. 37 per cent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services; an estimated 41 per cent of Syria’s adult population requires treatment for one or more non-communicable diseases (NCDs); and at least 15 per cent of the population requires mental health and psychosocial services (MHPSS). However, the infrastructure and referral system that remains functional after close to eight years of crisis is inadequate to cope with existing needs.In 2014, Al Bab city which is located in eastern rural Aleppo was taken over by the Islamic State, which rendered 3 years of absence of free-of-charge health facilities and reported displacement of medical human resources, in addition to the death of more than 70 health staff in eastern rural Aleppo due to recurrent targeting. These attacks and lack of human and financial resources led to a huge gap in provision of health services, which has been reflected to different pillars including primary and secondary health care.In 2017, the situation in eastern rural Aleppo became considerably stable, whereas the hostilities in north east Syria against IS in ArRaqqa and Deir Al-Zour caused mass population movement into eastern and northern rural Aleppo. And later in 2018 the evacuation from besieged areas in northern rural Homs and Rural Damascus governorates. All these movements and changes in control led to a sudden increase in the population of more than 285,000 in Al Bab subdistrict and the majority of the population are IDPs.According to HeRAMs and 4Ws reports by Health Cluster/Turkey hub, the health situation in Al Bab area is collapsed due to the low availability of the services provision which is supposed to meet the sudden increase in the population who settled in the area recently after military operations in north east Syria, not mentioning the inadequate number of human resources in pediatrics and gynecology support of health facilities delivered from The bombing.The population in Al Bab subdistrict counts to 285000 people, including 36 thousand children younger than five years and 21000 PLWs. There are approximately 10000 IDPs in the proximal camps, according to recent reports from OCHA 2018.

(about 20 beds/ 1st phase)• Screen and refer children under the aged 6 59 months in the catchment areas of the MCH for acute & moderate malnutrition.• Provide targeted supplementary feeding and referral for children, pregnant and lactating mothers with moderate or severe acute malnutrition and complicated obstetrics cases.• Conduct mobilization activities focusing on reproductive and child health for pregnant women by CHWs. • Provide essential mental health and psycho social support services through the targeted MCH.Activities:• Establish a well equipped and staffed Mother and Child Health (MCH) center in Al Bab, including family planning, maternal, infant, young, child and adolescence nutrition counseling corner in the MCH.• Train MCH health staff on the use of clinical protocols and guidelines, including B&CEmONC, acute malnutrition, and patient data collection.• Train technical staff on triage and BLS (Basic Life Support) and APLS (advance Pediatrics Life Support) for emergency staff (Pediatrician, doctors, nurse, midwives and Obs/Gyc specialist).• Support and supervise MCH management and administration through performance standards monitoring and coaching.• Train field M&E officers on MEAL activities and protocols.• Train Community Health Workers (CHWs) for health outreach, and referral.• Support the provision of essential medicines, medical supplies and equipment to direct beneficiaries; and the provision of reproductive health and safe delivery kits and medical equipment to MCH, including basic and comprehensive maternity and children and neonate units needed, in accordance with WHO EPS guidelines.• Collect and share aggregated and monthly health information data on a DhiS2 system via the targeted health facility. Protection Risk Analysis

In maternal and child activities the proposed maternal and child health and reproductive health training, as well as the related PHC strengthening activities, prioritize health issues affecting women and children. With most of the deliveries in Syria requiring medical intervention, the proposed training on emergency obstetric care (EMoNC) for midwives will work to improve health services needed exclusively by women. Furthermore, our organization emphasis on training midwives will facilitate meeting the needs of the most vulnerable groups who have limited access to health facilities for multiple reasons such as disability, distance from health facilities and displacement. our organization adopted mitigation measures including, training materials and operational protocols emphasize the need to observe, record, and address differences in health status affecting women, men, boys, and girls. To the extent possible, our organization requires and/or advocates for the use of sex and age disaggregated health data to monitor trends in health access and status that disproportionately affect one or more gender or age groups. Our organization proposed training of district level health on Health Information Systems will further contribute to this effort. Messages promoted through health staff, community outreach workers, and awareness raising campaigns also address issues related to women and men’s access to services, different dietary requirements for pregnant versus non pregnant women, and age specific nutrition feeding practices for infants and young children.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Access to targeted locations and beneficiaries.- Continued funds based on approved plan.- Security situation does not deteriorate.- Acceptance and cooperation by stakeholders.1. Attacks (several types) against Health Facilities and Staff:During the period of January - September 2018 documented 134 attacks across Syria, exceeding the incidents reported throughout the year 2017 (122 incidents) as reported through SSA surveillance system.266 casualties (97 fatalities and 169 injuries) from medical staff and patient/beneficiaries were resulted from these violence incidences.Mitigating measures:- Increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL).- Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare in order to enable the role of higher levels of liaison and steering groups in advocacy and taking the appropriate measures addressing this issue.- Assessment of the safest and least vulnerable areas. Training all workers on first aid and evacuation of wounded. Ensure that the hospital are equipped with full safety supplies such as fire extinguishers, first aid kit etc. 2-Limited availability of qualified personnel that limit the ability of the hospital to provide services. Severity: High. Mitigating measures taken: To provide the program with a comprehensive assessment of the current availability of staff, doctors and staff in and around the hospital to ensure a realistic, feasible and appropriate management and financial proposal. 3. Gender Based Violence:Health services are the first to seek help in for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries. It is critical that health providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services.Mitigating measures:- Prevention and management of relevant cases to be a life-saving intervention and ensure access to affected women and girls through presence of female health staff.- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.- Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) should be ensured.4. Equitable access for vulnerable groupsMitigation:- Vulnerable groups are included in the planning and design and construction- The project will be Regularly revised and updated of regular, contingency, and emergency plans with emphasis on Do No Harm principles, specifically in scenarios involving sudden mass movements of IDPs and significant returns activities.
