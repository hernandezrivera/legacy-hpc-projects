Project Summary

Maram proposes to support the CCCM cluster to respond to the immediate needs of the new arriving IDPs in NW Syria by utilizing the great experience it has in setting up and managing camps to establish and manage 2 new planned camps in Idleb.
The established new camps aim to provide the population with an adequate shelter as well as comprehensive multisectoral humanitarian services in an integrated manner.
The selected lands for establishing the camps are public lands and located in Idleb/Harim, their overall area is about 550,000 sqm, and can fit to accommodate 18,333 IDPs; 3,667 Households.
Maram proposed intervention is planned over 12 months, and will be implemented into 2 stages, the preparation stage and then the operation stage.
(1) The operation stage for 2 months includes site preparation activities (are submitted on HPC under shelter cluster)
(2) The operation stage will be for the remaining 10 months; and will include the following activities:
• IDPs relocation and registration
• Coordination mechanism to provide multisectoral services
• Promote Community Participation.
• Establish and maintain Accountability mechanism.
• Maintenance of camp different facilities.
• Contingency Planning: activities to mitigate any potential risk (that may include security deterioration, flood, raining, etc.). This would include set and train staff and IDPs on concrete evacuation plan, provide firefighting equipment, tents replacement and NFI kits distribution.
• Sustainable service provision activities (WASH services).
Maram will oversee and coordinate multi-sectoral lifesaving services within the camp, including WASH services, psychosocial support, education, health, food and non-food distributions. It views camps not as a final solution, but as interim situations in which it is still paramount to uphold each individual’s right to life, safety, and dignity during displacement.
Hence, Maram seeks to provide basic emergency services to the IDPs upon their arrival to the camps (6 emergency months) while working to provide sustainable services during the second phase (6 months post the emergency), as follows:
Phase (1):
- Site Preparation technical/infrastructure activities/shelter activities
- Basic WASH facilities establishment/WASH activities
- Water trucking/ WASH activities
- Garbage collection/ WASH activities
- Solar energy system and lighting poles/ CCCM activities
- Fire Safety equipment/shelter activities
- Tents and rub-halls ground insulation and installment/shelter activities
- Coordination of multi-sectorial services provision (FSL, NFI, Health, Protection, Education)/ CCCM activities
- Allocate a place for medical point in the camp for regular health services/ CCCM activities
- Allocate a place for a school in the camp (utilize potential IDPs expertise if possible)/ CCCM activities
- IDPs relocation and registration/ CCCM activities
- Regular maintenance of camp facilities/ CCCM activities
Phase (2):
- Construction of water network and high water tanks for sustainable water provision/WASH activities
- Construction of football playground for children/shelter activities.
- Construction of garden and female outdoor spaces/shelter activities.
- Construction of multiple basic market stalls to allow IDPs engage in livelihood activities and enhance their resilience/shelter activities.
- Capacity building trainings for camp committee members/CCCM activities
- Vocational trainings for IDPs (with focus on women participation), some trainings will aim at learning the IDPs how to take care of the camp facilities as part of exit strategy interventions/CCCM activities
- Reliable Information Management System, AAP and community engagement mechanisms will be integral parts of implementation from the start of the project/ CCCM activities.

4 - Needs

The recent hostilities in NW Syria have prompted new large numbers of Syrians to forcibly flee their areas and seek temporary shelters that could provide them with assistance and protection.From the beginning of December 2019 till end of January 2020; the CCCM Cluster tracked around 1,021,275 displacements; and while about 20% of those IDPs sought IDP sites, 80% preferred to stay outside IDPs sites, this percentage reflects the reluctance of the IDPs to seek the IDPs sites as shelters because a large number of these sites have a spontaneous nature, have no legal claim to the land on which they were established, are not managed by accountable humanitarian actors and therefore; they lack the provision of dignified protection and comprehensive multisectoral humanitarian assistance that meet IDPs needs.According to HNO of 2019; It is estimated that 34% of the sites’ population cannot receive minimum food assistance on a monthly basis, and approximately 25%-26% of the population in sites, do not receive adequate shelter and NFI assistance respectively, while 15% of the population faces challenges in receiving proper sanitation services. Furthermore, IDPs suffer from overcrowding, lack of privacy in bathing areas, poor shelter conditions; in addition to sites safety and security concerns.However, with the continued influx of IDPs as a result of the continued hostilities, IDPs continue to take refuge in IDP sites, and it was reported that there is a 16% increase in the number of IDPs seeking IDP sites this year comparing to last year, and that against the backdrop of overcrowding; there is limited absorption capacity in IDP sites that some sites are running more than 400% above their actual capacity (HNO 2019).During the frequent CCCM Cluster meetings, there was an emphasis on the need to establish standardized planned camps that are well structured and could provide protection and integrated multisectoral services for IDPs, and with increased number of IDPs; this need

5 - Implementation of activities

Proposed activities:1) Preparation and mobilization: Hire and train the field team. Conclude lands agreements. 2) Coordination of services: Work with the donor and relevant clusters to identify NGO service providers to provide different services (FSL, NFI, health, education, protection). Coordinate and monitor the provision of the multisectoral assistance. 3) Maintenance: Provide routine maintenance to the camp facilities.4) IDPs relocation: IDPs relocation to the camp: this includes community sensitization, vulnerability assessment, arrangement of IDPs transportation (when needed), IDPs registration, tents allotting, induction briefing about the camp.5) Accountability: Establish gender-balanced camp governance mechanism to enhance community participation in decision-making through holding fair elections for leadership so IDPs can select their representatives. 6) Community Committees: To enhance the community engagement; community committees will be formed from the camp representatives who were selected from the IDPs. These committees will be in different specializations and will ensure the representation of all groups of the IDPs, including the most vulnerable groups.7) Trainings: Provide CCCM capacity building trainings for the staff and the committees’ members. 8) Accountability: Set up the system for complaints and feedback. 7) M&E: Verification of needs by FGDs. Following up the daily activities and submit weekly reports. PDM exercise after each distribution activity. Maintain the AAP mechanism of IDPs feedback
