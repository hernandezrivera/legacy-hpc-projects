Project Summary

Islamic Relief Worldwide is a humanitarian organization has been working in South Sudan since 2004 and providing humanitarian support for the flood, drought and conflict affected people in need including provision of WASH services at Warrap state (Tonj north county) Central equatoria (Kajokeji and Yei counties) Eastern equatoria (Kapoeta East and Kapoeta north counties) Islamic Relief South Sudan (IRSS) has developed “Enhancing resilience for equitable WASH services for IDPs, returnees and host
communities project to respond and increase resilience capacity of the food, drought and conflict affected communities to achieve the following objectives:
i. Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition
ii. Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5
iii. WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation
iv. Community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures.
The proposed action intended to provide lifesaving inclusive WASH services to 44;556 (Men 11,139, women 12,476, Boys 10,248, Girls 10,693 & PWD 6,683). people in need in Warrap state (Tonj north counties) Central Equatoria (Kajokeji and Yei counties) Eastern equatoria (Kapoeta East and Kapoeta north counties). This project is anticipated to positively impact on the lives of people through increasing access to improved water supplies systems/facilities, restoring dignity in women and girls through provision of gender sensitive sanitation infrastructure and hygiene services/products for women and girls, boys and men, elderly people, people with disability including people suffering chronic illnesses.
To achieve the project objectives, Islamic Relief South Sudan will be focusing on the Right Based Approach linked with the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) approach to address the pressing WASH needs and gender/protection related concerns in order to minimize or mitigate the common causes of vulnerabilities.
This project strives to provide timely and equitable access to clean and safe water supply, gender and vulnerability sensitive water supply and sanitation infrastructure and hygiene services/products through construction of new water facilities/sources (Hand pump boreholes, solarized water yards. Conduct rehabilitation activities for the nonfunctional/defunct water sources/schemes, and sanitation facilities such as institutional latrines, incinerators, hand washing facilities. The project shall as well integrate the aspect community capacity building strategy through awareness campaigns, formation and training of Community based WASH structures such as; Hygiene promoters, leaders, Water Management Committees (WMC) and Hand Pump Mechanics to improve on operation and maintenance for sustainability purposes.

1. Needs

Humanitarian situation in South Sudan continuous to deteriorate due to multiple factors like; endemic violence and conflicts which has constantly led to access constraints and operational interferences to project locations, equally public health challenges and climate change resulted in to the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and predominant drought in most parts of the country particularly in eastern equatoria state (Kapoeta East and North counties) and Warrap (Tonj North County) which have created severe impact on people’s livelihoods, impedes access to nutritional needs, limits access to education for school children, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) and health services in the proposed project intervention areas. Peoples access to safe and clean water and improved sanitation remains very low due to the highlighted challenges. According to Inter-sector findings including refuges 2024, People in Need are 8.5 million, out of the 8.5M PIN, over 5.5 million continues to lack access to basic WASH services. This clearly depicts that majority of the population still battles with waterborne diseases, malnutrition and very low Household production capacities thus, low income levels and recurrent sufferings amongst Households within the country. The impact of floods, linked to the effect of climate change, and high level of food insecurity including the Sudan crisis has increased water and sanitation needs in Islamic Reliefs programme intervention areas such as; Tonj North, Yei, Kajokeji, Kapoeta East and North counties respectively. The cumulative impact of flooding since 2020 up to 2022/2023 contributed to the destruction and damaging of water facilities across the country. For instance, Tonj North remains most affected with over 224 Hand pumps being submerged in water whereby directly exposing those water facilities to high risk of pathogenic contaminations which has far reaching negative health impact on the vulnerable population. Furthermore, 381 are non-functional with multiple defect conditions, according to September 2022 IRNA’s report-WASH Cluster. This has created more vulnerability among the affected population and intensified existing vulnerabilities which include but not limited to high poverty rates, high waterborne morbidity rate and occurrences of SGBV due to limited access to basic WASH services. The WASH Severity Classification of 2024 categorized, Kajokeji in priority 5, Kapoeta East in priority 2 &North priority 5 and Tonj East county in priority 3. Population in these locations continuous to lack access to essential WASH services such as clean water supply and valuable WASH NFIs for SAM children/caretakers, IDPs returnees and host communities, dignity/MHM kits for woman and girls, and Household latrine construction materials. They require critical WASH intervention to improve the living conditions and dignities of communities in those locations. According to IRSS multi-sector needs assessment, 30% of HHs must walk more than 30 minutes to fetch water in Yei; 50.6% of HHs must walk more than 30 minutes to fetch water in Tonj North, 5.5% of HHs use surface water (ponds), river/stream and unprotected hand dug well as source of drinking water in Yei; 56.7% of HHs use surface water (ponds), river/stream and unprotected hand dug well as source of drinking water in Tonj North, 84.8% of HHs do not have enough containers for water storage in Yei; 68% of HHs do not have enough containers for water storage in Tonj North, 70.3% of women and girls do not have access to their basic hygiene products (kits) in Yei; 66.5% of women and girls do not have access to their basic hygiene products (kits) in Tonj North, 1.3% of HHs use the bush to defecate (practice open defecation) in Yei; 99.5% of HHs use the bush to defecate (practice open defecation) in Tonj North, 80% of HHs have not received WASH assistance in the past 3 months in Yei; 67.5% of HHs have not received WASH assistance in the past 3 months in Tonj North.

2. Project activities

This project intends to provide timely and equitable access to safe and clean water supply, safe sanitation and improved hygiene services to 44,556 (Men 11,139, women 12,476 Boys 10,248, Girls 10,693 & PWD 6,683). vulnerable people affected by the conflicts, flooding and drought in the targeted project intervention locations which includes IDPs, returnees and host communities. Based on the needs highlighted, IRSS will undertake the following activities: - Drilling of 30 new boreholes installed with Indian MKII/III hand pumps (6Tonj North, 6 Kapoeta North, 6 Kapoeta East, 6 Kajokeji and 6 Yei). –Construction/rehabilitation/upgrading of 6 solarized water yards (02 Kapoeta Eat, 02 Tonj North, 06 Kajokeji). - Rehabilitation of 40 nonfunctional boreholes (8 Tonj North, 8 Kapoeta North, 8 Kapoeta East,8 Kajokeji and 8 Yei). - Construction of 22 blocks of institutional latrines at Nutrition and health facilities (12 in Tonj, 12 blocks Kapoeta east). Training of 88 water managementcommittee (WMCs) with 60% women representation for proper operation and maintenance of the various water sources - Training of 80 community based hygiene promoters 20 Tonj North, 20 Kapoeta North, 20 Kapoeta East, 10 Kajokeji and 10 Yei) - Training of 55 community hand pump mechanics 15 Tonj North, 10 Kapoeta North, 10 Kapoeta East, 10 Kajokeji and 10 Yei) - Provision of 5,000 WASH NFIs kits to IDPs, returnees and host communities. Distribute WASH standard kits to support 500 SAM children/caretakers. Conduct 25 CLTS triggering sessions across the project intervention areas. Support 600 Households to construct their own latrine with distribution of 225 sets of latrine digging materials (45 Tonj North, 45 Kapoeta North, 45 Kapoeta East, 45 Kajokeji and 45 Yei) - 15 sessions on integrated WASH & GBV mitigation and prevention will be conducted across all targeted areas. - 30 sessions of hygiene promotion, awareness and campaigns will be conducted in Communities, public places, health facilities, nutrition centers and schools. - Conduct one (01) KAP survey across the project intervention areas.
