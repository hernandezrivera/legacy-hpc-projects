Project Summary

The proposed intervention is a response to increase food insecurity in Gaza. According FSS-HNO 2022, two million people (36.7% of the population) are estimated to be moderately or severely food insecure, making almost two out of five Palestinian suffering from food insecurity. In 2021, more than 1.5 million are in need of assistance (seventy-three per cent located in the Gaza Strip and 27 percent in the West Bank). The intervention will be in line with FSS- Objective 2”Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”. The project will increase food security for vulnerable HHs Rafah and Khan Younis through providing direct access to food and providing support through unrestricted cash modalities.
The intervention will be integrated with SCI-nutrition and child protection current interventions and will provide a database for the potential beneficiaries. It will also contribute to addressing malnutrition, particularly with children and will increase the impact of our health/nutrition interventions. Applying a cash-based voucher modality will empower food insecure households to prioritize and meet their own needs while enhancing their access to sufficient food intake. Through the intervention,-400 vulnerable HHs (approx. 2,200people) will be supported by e-vouchers for 4 months and another 300 HHs (approx. 1,650 people) from families that have especial vulnerability (children with disability, children with chronic disease, children experiencing to protection risk) with cash assistance for 3 months. SCI will provide unconditional cash support for vulnerable HHs. (MPCA will use CWG recommended Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). The monthly amount to support families to secure their basic needs is 1,046 ILS/HH).
SCI will target exceptionally vulnerable HHs such as HHs with children under 5, pregnant or lactating women, infants, child/female-headed HHs, and HHs of extended size only. The food E-voucher value is expected to cover 100% of HHs` food needs factoring in coping strategy practices that they may use to complete their needs such relying on less preferred and less expensive foods, borrowings and assistance from relatives SC will implement the project in partnership with Save Youth Future (SYF). SYF will conduct the socio-economic assessment to select the beneficiaries, sanitization sessions with beneficiaries and monitoring the selling points.

1 - Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade, the deteriorating economic situation and the recurrent conflict events and public employee crisis have resulted in high rates of food insecurity in Gaza. The unemployment rate for the second quarter of 2021 was 44.7 percent in the Gaza Strip (FSS-HNO). MSNA findings in July 2021 indicate that 53 per cent of surveyed households in Gaza reported having received the assistance of some type since the beginning of the escalation, while ninety-four per cent reported wanting to receive assistance in the future. Cash (FSS-HNO). Increase vulnerability and financial crisis undermining the PA ability to cover to support the poor families. The PA's ability to maintain the National Social Safety Net (SSN) is at risk in terms of the amount and frequency of support. As of September 2021, the 116,000 most vulnerable families registered under the SSN, received only one round of assistance out of three. According to the MSNA (July 2021), 36 per cent of households in the oPt reported members losing their job permanently or temporarily because of the COVID-19 outbreak (HNO 2022). Increased poverty has affected great volume of households further weakening household income stability and purchasing power which in turn has affected the local market, farmers and small businesses hence more laborers will lose their jobs. In the eastern parts of Rafah and Khan Younis areas where HHs depend on agriculture to secure their income, the farmers face many securities, financial and environmental challenges that led to income reduction and greater food insecurity. In last escalation, the income of many farmers deteriorated due to destruction of farming assets and losing crops. SCI with partners conducted a socio-economic assessment in rural three localities in three localities south Gaza (Khoza, Al Nasr, and Al Shoka) to identify the root causes of malnutrition. The result of assessment revealed that the average monthly income per capita is 43.8 ILS for the 450 surveyed families).Severe pressures experienced by civilians in the OPT resulting from the occupation have been linked to gender-based violence (GBV), school dropouts, as well as child marriage, forced marriage, and early high-risk pregnancies while service providers struggle to meet the population’s needs due to limited resources (“Gender and Wars in Gaza Untangled: What Past Wars Have Taught Us?”, June 2021).The May 2021 escalation of hostilities and the COVID-19 pandemic have reinforced pressures facing women, girls, boys and men in the OPT. The pandemic has also exacerbated inequalities, inequities and discrimination based on location, age, and geography. Women and girls are disproportionately impacted by gender norms and pre-existing inequalities. Globally, COVID-19 has affected women and girls in multi-dimensional ways, particularly those facing multiple, intersecting forms of discrimination (GENDER ALERT: NEEDS OF WOMEN, GIRLS, BOYS AND MEN IN HUMANITARIAN ACTION IN PALESTINE –Sep 2021)The assessment shows that poverty increases the challenges health risks for children who often get sick, and mothers would have to take care of them adding to their burden

2 - Activities

The project is in line with FSS STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. SC will select the beneficiaries based on socio- economic criteria in coordination MoSD based on PMTF formula to befit from food –voucher. The selection of beneficiaries for MPCA will be based on the previous method. The essential criteria will focus on the HH income, family size, level of poverty, access to food, HH food consumption and food insecure families with multi financial vulnerabilities.SCI will use the results from current nutrition and protection interventions to identify the target communities ‘ and HHs vulnerability related to low income such as malnutrition and children suffering from multiple child protection concerns. SCI will coordinate with the existing Community Protection Committees (CPCs) and CBOs to ensure community active participation and acceptance of the intervention modality. The intervention will target 2 vulnerable HHs (approx. 2,200 people) in Rafah and Khan Younis with food vouchers and 300 HHs (approx. 1,650people) with cash assistance. This methodology has been designed to ensure HHs` food security needs are met to the extent that the risks of malnutrition and insufficient level of food intake are as much mitigated as possible. The food voucher e-voucher amount will be based on the MEB developed by the Cash Working Group (CWG). The transfer value for food voucher is set at US$438 ILS/HHs per month, which covers the food requirements of an average family of 6, calculated based on the FS sector standard of 1,800 kcal/person/day for 30 days. Food items allowed list will be based on the list of food items in the FSS basket with the addition of fresh fruits and vegetables, fresh meat (chicken and beef) and eggs. This assistance will be topped-up for the duration of the project, 4 top ups per each HHs. The E-voucher value is expected to cover 100% of HHs` food needs factoring in coping strategy practices that they may use to complete their needs such relying on less preferred and less expensive foods, borrowings and assistance from relatives or host families and use of available savings. SCI will provide unconditional cash support for 300 vulnerable HHs for three months , 3 top ups ( MPCA will use CWG recommended MEB) 1,046 ILS in monthly installments in line with the Cash Working Group (CWG) endorsed MEB 2.0 Transfer Value). SC will target the beneficiaries for cash assistance based on above Socioeconomic selection criteria with prioritizing the families facing additional vulnerability such as families with children with disability, children with chronic disease, in addition to HHs who's children are exposed to child protection risk associated to poverty. Through integration with SCI ongoing child protection project, the children who needs deeper protection intervention will be referred to child protection case manager to offer appropriate assistance. SCI will provide MPCA assistance as an immediate and core intervention for the 3 months of the project. This intervention is in line with the methodology utilized by Food Security and Cash Working Group. MoSD will provide the list of beneficiaries that will be crosschecked with the Food Security Sector to avoid duplication of beneficiaries. Under this output, SC in partnership with SYF will implement the distribution of the MPCA to the most vulnerable HHs (approx. 1925 individuals). The MPCA is managed using ATM cards issued by the Bank of Palestine “Sanabel”. The amount of each top up is based on the CWG recommended Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) set at Israeli Shekels (ILS) 1,046 ILS per month .Field monitors will follow with partner the distribution of the MPCA, and sensitization sessions for beneficiaries will be conducted including learning sessions for using ATM Cards. Specific project activities will include: I – Provision of CVA support 1. SCI and partner will conduct rapid assessment of needs, gaps, and market functionality as part of the feasibility risk.2. SCI will kick off and preparation of partners.3. Communication with stakeholders, FSS and CWG.4. SCI will coordinate closely with MoSD to obtain the preliminary list nominated beneficiaries, selection, registration, and baseline.5. SCI will work on the distribution preparation (contracting with merchants and Palpay financial service provider).6. Project partner will conduct orientation sessions to beneficiaries on the use of e-vouchers and sensitization session. SCI with implementing partner will conduct 35 sensitization sessions for target HHs and key community members to explain the planned intervention and discuss the pre-selected food and non-food items.7. SCI and the SYF will work on distribution of E-vouchers and redemption and price monitoring in point of sales.8. SC will lead monitoring e-voucher use (Post-Distribution Monitoring). The partner will support SC in facilitate access to beneficiaries.9. SC will conduct End-line to measure outcomes and the impact of the project. SC will conduct Mid- and post-distribution assessments will monitor the effectiveness of the cash transfers, and impact on alleviating access limitations to basic services and provisions. Beneficiaries, both children and parents, will be asked about the process. Post-distribution monitoring will be doneTo ensure protection and safety of target population, SC will ensure that all staff involved in the implementation of project activities, specifically those in direct interaction with beneficiaries are trained on PSEA and safeguarding policies. Also, all contracts with partners, field workers, suppliers will include a special clause on PSEA and CSG policies. Moreover, orientation sessions for target beneficiaries will be delivered to explain their rights, what can be reported.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The MEAL system of SCI considers monitoring function as a live function for systematically tracking the implementation of actions that form part of project/program resulting in effective, performance-based project/program management. It provides basic tools to collect information to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of the project. SCI developed Quality Benchmarks (QBMs) lists to ensure high quality standards are maintained across the stages of Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) interventions. QBMs monitoring visits will be conducted to project activities on a monthly basis and reported to program management for their considerations and actions. In addition, to ensure evidence-based learning and implementation, the project will conduct different assessments and studies to inform the continuous improvements and learning. At the early stage of the project, the team will be working on having the Baseline study assessment that will give the base-values of the different goals, outcomes, and output indicators. This includes Food consumption score, coping strategies index, and household diet diversity index. Accountability mechanism and feedback channels will be established and disseminated at the start of the project period, those include the toll free hotline, compliant boxes and community sessions. For the CVA component, SC pays special attention to ensure that beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any concerns and make use of their preferred communication channel. Therefore, the sensitizations sessions will be utilized to raise the awareness about the rights and responsibilities of the beneficiaries and vendors. Moreover, field monitors will be conducting spot checks at vendors’ places to ensure their compliance to agreed standards. To ensure access of vulnerable groups/individuals, CPCs and CPFPs are also involved in raising awareness of the communities on the different available accountability mechanisms provided by SCI

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The Project is designed through a child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse from staff/representatives (including volunteers). We ensure that activities engaging with children and their careers have informed consent, are non-exploitative and do not increase risk of harm or vulnerability. From the child-safeguarding angle, we ensure that all concerns, allegations, and incidents are reported, recorded and investigated according to our local country procedures and hierarchy of reporting within 24 hours. Moreover, SCI embed with CSG the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), our complaints handling mechanisms are robust enough to elicit and deal with PSEA related reports and we do regular awareness training with our staff, partners and community on abuse and the duty to report. SCI will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions amongst community members. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits and cluster consultation. SCI will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed. The project will address adaption guidelines for implementation, to reprogram/adjust programming at country level due to COVID-19 to minimize the harm. Among the main risks SC is considering is are 1: Repeated Child Safeguarding incidents of abuse and exploitation, sexual harassment (PSEA) for our beneficiaries, for that oPt staff and partners are aware of key safeguarding messages and practices and how to report. SCI will train staff and partners on new DATIX cloud. Awareness raising amongst partner’s staff and beneficiaries including banners, brochures and posters in the distribution location and service providers. Free hotline is in place with clear complaints mechanism to report and respond to any abuses. SC have engagement with local protection partners, relevant institutions and NGO network to deal with protection incidents. Also, COVID-19 outbreak is another risk, for that internally we have formed a Response Team (COVERT) which acts as the primary coordination and facilitation body for the country office preparedness.Established regular communication and coordination with the PA ministries, cluster, UN and the global SCI to assess needs and potential interventions.Use of telecommunications for follow-up and counselling if feasible. Staff trained on COVID-19 prevention. Include COVID 19 testing/examination in our private health insurance policy for all local staff.Share our advocacy messaging on COVID-19 with the partners for their information and in case they want to adopt these messages in their own advocacy efforts. Impact addressed by adaption guidelines for implementation, donors mostly showing flexibility to reprogram/adjust programming and providing additional funding at country level due to COVID-19. Distribution of PPE, HHs hygiene kits as Medical Masks, gloves, disinfectants, focusing on the community based health workers and medical at AEI clinics.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to both women and men according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create women only groups to facilitate their participation. The selection criteria will focus on female headed HHS. SCI, together with project partners, will identify the problems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse. SCI Gender Advisor, supported by the SCI Health Advisor, will ensure gender mainstreaming in the assessments and interventions. SCI will ensure that the selection criteria prioritizes the needs of pregnant/lactating women and children with malnutrition and/or CWDs from larger and women headed HHs. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI’s policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities, Chronic, diseases or who exposed to protection risk ito assist in their access to food security and basic needs
