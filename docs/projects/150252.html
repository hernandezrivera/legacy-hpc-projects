Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect the endangered livelihoods of farming households in the ARA in the Gaza Strip to help them maintain ability to sustain an independent, dignified and productive living. This project will target the most vulnerable farmers whose productive assets are in need of protection/restoration in order to facilitate economic recovery. Agriculture based livelihoods will be supported and promoted by increasing agricultural production and productivity among low resilience female and male led households in the ARA through the rehabilitation and reconstruction of key agricultural infrastructure and assets. In doing so, this project will contribute to socio economic stabilization and will increase the resilience of farming households and communities through increased income and subsequent reduction in vulnerability and aid dependency. This project contributes to Strategic Objective 3 of the HRP 2018 2020 and is planned as a multi year project with the same activities recurring over two years in different selected locations each year, as there is a pressing need for such an emergency intervention. This multi year intervention will contribute to socio-economic stabilization and will increase the resilience of farming households and communities through increased income and subsequent reduction in vulnerability and aid dependency.

Needs

Based on a consensus reached by the ARA core group of the Protection Cluster, the ARA is composed of a ‘no go zone’ (0 – 300 metres from the fence) and a “High Risk Area” (HRA) which ranges from 300 m. to 1,000/1,500 m. from the fence depending on location along the Strip. A 2014 FAO assessment based on a land cover map provided by the European Union’s Joint Research Centre estimated the total arable land in the ARA prior to the 2014 conflict to have been 76,273 dunums with an estimated 11,465 dunums of cropped land and 26,621 dunums of non cropped land affected either wholly or partially by the conflict. Approximately 10 800 households (61 560 individuals) reside within the HRA and are comprised mainly of farmers, Bedouin and refugees. Almost half (49 per cent) of HRA households rely on agricultural, 14 per cent on government employment and six per cent on private sector employment, with the remaining 31 per cent relying on income from casual labour. Low resilience farmers in the ARA typically lack the financial resources required to rehabilitate agricultural land in order to resume normal levels of cultivation. An additional restriction faced by farmers in the ARA is that placed on the height of crops – only crops up to 80 centimeters in height are permitted within the ARA by the Israeli authorities. As a result, farmers are prevented from replanting fruit trees removed during land leveling incursions by Israeli forces and must depend solely upon vegetables and other crops, which yield a lower return in markets, for their income. When compared with perennial crops (trees), annual crops (e.g. vegetables, cereals and legumes) require significantly more work and services on a daily basis, a role that is performed mainly by women and children. Women are also typically responsible for selling the crops at market. Taking this into consideration, improving the production and productivity in the ARA will result in a lesser work burden on women coupled with higher income for them and their families. Low resilience farmers in the ARA are characterized by limited access to resources and public services, low daily income (average NIS 9/USD 2.5 per adult per day) and a high percentage – often over 50 per cent – of income spent on household food consumption. Moreover, according to the findings of a field assessment conducted by FAO in 2018 in response to Gaza’s deepening electricity crisis, farming communities in the ARA in the Gaza Strip reported that the cost for planting one dunum had increased significantly as a result of the increasing electricity deficit. Overall, low resilience farming households are more exposed to protection risks, have limited capacity to absorb and respond to shocks, and are more likely to employ negative coping strategies than their more resilient counterparts. Women farmers are most impacted by these as they bear ever increasing levels of responsibility both inside and outside the home. According to the Gender Alert Report (2015) by UN Women and OCHA, women’s limited share of assets makes them increasingly vulnerable during difficult economic times. Overall, the work of rural women in the agricultural sector is under recognized and women farmers are considered to be “helpers” regardless of the level of responsibility they have for the household’s agricultural work. Data from PCBS indicate that 18.2% of economically active women work in agriculture and constitute 30% of agricultural employees in Gaza, thereby masking biases that exist with regard to recognizing the contribution of rural women in agricultural labour including as unpaid family workers in this sector.

Activities

As FAO’s primary government counterpart, the MoA will be consulted and closely coordinated with before and throughout project implementation and, as with all FAO projects, the appointment of a Focal Point by the MoA will be requested to lead the engagement of the MoA in all phases of implementation. Through this project, FAO will provide time critical, in kind and technical support to targeted farmers through the rehabilitation and valorisation of land and greenhouses, rehabilitation/reconstruction of critical water facilities and provision of fruit fly traps. FAO will rehabilitate 1000 dunums of arable land for the purpose of cultivating vegetables and other field crops, each targeted dunum will be provided with essential nutrient elements in the form of organic fertilizers and basic chemical fertilizers. A further 200 dunums of greenhouses will be targeted for rehabilitation with in kind inputs (plastic sheeting, insect proof nets and wood panelling for fixing sheeting and nets). According to the MoA – GDPP, support to maintaining greenhouse productivity is a critical activity, since the 10,000 dunums of greenhouses in the Gaza Strip play a significant role in agricultural production, particularly during winter. Each dunum of greenhouse produces 3-4 times more than one dunum of open field and requires agricultural labour for around 300 days per year. However, windstorms and high temperatures experienced in 2018 have drastically reduced the shelf life of these materials, with farmers unable to afford to replace them due to falling market prices and reduced revenues. As a result, the agricultural productivity of greenhouses is at risk if support is not provided. All project activities have been designed in consultation with the communities to be targeted by this project in order to respond to their needs which were subsequently verified by FAO field staff through continuous consultations, field visits and daily interactions. Prior to project implementation, in each of the targeted communities Local Committees, engaging beneficiary representatives, local authorities and the MoA, will be formed to participate in the beneficiary selection process, to provide support during distribution of inputs, and to assist the FAO project team with follow up activities. Outcome: Minimum 31830 low resilience farming households (HHs) in the ARA have their productive capacities, income sources and food security restored/protected. Output 1: 1000 dunums of land and 200 dunums of greenhouses rehabilitated for agricultural production. Activity 1.1 – 500 farming HHs supported with organic and basic chemical fertilizers for 2 dunums each in: East Khan Yunis (200); East Deir al Balah (200); and East Beit Hannoun (100). Activity 1.2 – 200 dunums of greenhouses rehabilitated with plastic sheeting, insect proof agricultural nets and required wood panelling for 1 dunum per HH in: Shoka (50); Al Fukkhari (10); East Khan Yunis (40); East Deir al Balah (50); and North Gaza (50). Output 2: Essential water facilities rehabilitated/reconstructed for sufficient irrigation of crops. Activity 2.1 – 5 x irrigation wells rehabilitated in North Gaza (3); East Deir al Balah (1); East Khan Yunis (1). Activity 2.2 – 50 x plastic water harvesting ponds rehabilitated with plastic sheeting, shadow net, safety fence and water pump (with fittings) in: Shoka (20); Al Fukkhari (10); East Khan Yunis (10); and East Deir al Balah (10). Activity 2.3 – 20 x concrete water harvesting ponds supported with layer of concrete, shadow net, safety fence and water pump (with fittings) in: Shoka (5); Al Fukkhari (5); East Khan Yunis (5); and East Deir al Balah (5). Output 3: 3000 dunums of fruit orchards supported with pest control traps and pheromones. Activity 3.1 – 1 000 HHs supported with fruity and red palm weevil traps for 3 dunums each (1 trap per dunum) in: North Gaza (300); East Gaza (300); Deir Al Balah (200); Khan Yunis (100); and Rafah (100).

Post distribution monitoring will be conducted by interviewing a sample of female and male beneficiaries in order to monitor use of the inputs distributed, prospects for sustainability of the intervention as well as overall satisfaction of beneficiaries as part of FAO’s accountability to beneficiaries. Project impact will be assessed by comparing the results of a baseline and end line survey. A Grievance Redressal Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until final decision and the subsequent follow up. The procedure includes several well-defined steps tied to specific time frames including: preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post distribution mounting as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant.. All project activities and results will be coordinated with the Food Security Sector to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned shall be shared with all Food Security Sector stakeholders. In addition, FAO will maintain coordination with the protection cluster and the Humanitarian Country Team in order to ensure alignment with and support of advocacy initiatives.
