Project Summary

The project main goal is contribute to support in improving food security and livelihood status of conflict and disaster affected, vulnerable and small scale-farming households among IDPs, Returnees, and host community in Talodi and Leri localities in South Kordofan and Sharg Jabel Marra (E JM) in Central Darfur and Katayla and Nyala South Darfur State to achieve outcome 2. “Improve availability and accessibility to sufficient, quality and nutritious food to individuals impacted by protracted crisis who suffer from food insecurity levels for enhanced resilience during the period of 12 months.
The project is expected to target 26,000 beneficiaries, 1400 HHs (M/F) 7000 persons IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community will provided with climate smart crop and vegetable inputs and agricultural tools and vaccination and treatment of 55500 heads of livestock (goat, sheep, cattle) for 750 HHs (3750 persons), 250 HHs (1250 persons) will be provided with cash/E-voucher to purchase animal feed/folder, saltlick and water fees and awareness rising in groups of 20 participants for 1400 small farmers and relevant stakeholders about the impact of post-harvest losses on food insecurity.
100 CAHWs will be trained on pest and animal diseases control, animal vaccination and treatment, 200 VAC 30% female on climate-smart crop & vegetable seeds management and 100 agriculture extension workers.
1000 persons (M/F) will be provided with vocational skills training to IGAs and supported business kits, Training of 1000 in life skills for income generating activities/small businesses grants and establishment of 100 village saving and loans association (VSLA) and 100 women and youth will have ToT training as field agent on VSLA establishment and management and formation of 100 VSLA
250 HHs (1250 person) will be provided with goats, training of 500 women on FES to strengthen natural resource management and training of 500 women on food processing and marketing and supported with Food processing materials
Cash voucher is design to be used as substitute for purchasing items (e.g. seeds, tools, livestock, animal feed and veterinary supplies and services). Instead of providing vegetable seeds, small ruminants and poultry to women to address their livelihoods needs according to beneficiaries’ preferences within the total amount value that is printed on the voucher. ASSIST included the cash voucher system because it promote a diverse range of seed varieties based on farmers’ preference and it increase agricultural production and productivity and enable returnees, Vulnerable host communities involved in small holder farmers to ensure seed security, livestock production and animal health as farmers and livestock producers have access to quality input, goods and services.
Gender mainstreaming, ASSIST will set a benchmark to collect, analyze and use sex-disaggregated data and a target of 53% of staff will be women, 60% of staff completed basic gender training and learning sessions, and 100% of the project reports have specific gender results and lessons learned. Secondly, Resource allocation to project activities will ensure that 100% of the project activities achieve IASC Gender with Age marker code 4(M) by the end of 2020. Finally, ASSIST will have financial tracking mechanism to quantify funds disbursement for promoting gender and women empowerment.
Accountability to affected populations are part and partial strategy adapted for this project to ensure women are involved, participate, have equitable access to resources and decision making and access to employment, IGA, gain skills, and cash for building agriculture and livestock assets while consulting them for feedback, complains grievance handling to determine beneficiary preference at design, implementation and evaluation phase which are an integral part of this system with checks and balance through spot check and key informant interviews.

1 - Needs

Sudan comprehensive food security assessment conducted from November 2018-Februry 2019 indicates that, 55.4% of HHs in South Kordofan and central Darfur states spending 75% of their income on food, and unable to create/invest in livelihood assets and over 70% of HHs in Talodi and Leri localities in South Kordofan and Sharg Jabel Marra (E JM) in Central Darfur and Katayla and Nyala South Darfur States cannot afford 1 local food basket due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high food prices.it indicates that 54% of the HHs resort to livelihood based coping strategies, 41% of the targeted HHs headed by women are food insecure compare to 29% HHs headed by men although more than 94% of the HHs had women involved in preparation, digging, planting, weeding and harvesting, less than 54% of the HHs involved women in marketing of the harvest.As per FEWSNET Food Security report February-September 2019, the production is estimated to be 33% and 23% lower than last and 5-year average in South Kordofan, Central Darfur and South Darfur states, as result of drop in the harvested area which is due to shortage of fuel, cash, high cost agriculture input and production. The situation expected to be worse for IDPs in SPLA-N controlled area who have been inaccessible for both assessment and assistance deliveries are expected to be in IPC phase 4 in the peak of the lean season. IPC June-August 2019, issued August 2019 indicates that, 5 localities in South Kordofan and 9 localities in central Darfur state and 21 localities in South Darfur are estimated to be in IPC phase 3 and above with more that 20% of the HHs already suffering from poor consumption with 51%, 19% in Kadugli and Talodi and 15% for Golo, 24 for Nyala are acute food insecurity in phase 3 and with GAM 17-30% and SAM rate of 0.5-4.9 with the situation expected to be worse in SPLM-N-controlled areas.A rapid interagency assessment led by OCHA took place on 3 September 2018 in Golo – central Jebel Marra indicates that, 442 displaced households in Golo and its surroundings, the majority of the displaced are women and children. All the internally displaced persons (IDP) are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. In addition to the IDPs in local communities (145,841), recent returnees (47,943) are also highly vulnerable given the protracted conflict, chronic poverty and recurring natural disasters and epidemics. There is need for humanitarian and recovery support in Golo across all populations. A joint inter-agency needs assessment conducted by OCHA from 28 January – 24 February 2019 in Nyala indicates that, the majority IDPs are women and children and left behind their belongings, assets and livestock and lost their livelihoods and income generating activitiesInter agency assessment report conducted in El leri from 12-26 October 2018 indicates that, IDPs livelihoods depended on agriculture, pastoralism and other crafts. But, a majority of the IDPs are unable to re-establish their livelihoods and have lack of adequate food at household level. Although, food is available in the markets, but it expensive. IDPs are unable diversified diet. They earn a limited income through daily wages, offering themselves for seasonal agricultural labor. Women work as domestic workers, cutting wood for sale, charcoal making, hay collection and minding livestock. Although IDPs in El Leri have good access to land, but this access to agricultural land could not become self-sustaining as they had challenges accessing agricultural inputs due to high prices. Livestock diseases were reported and vaccination coverage was inadequate.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Deforestation and desertification, degradation in the environment and the loss of forest due negative livelihood practices in South Kordofan, Central Darfur and South Darfur States such as tree cutting for firewood resulted into deforestation at alarming rate which influencing rainfall amount and contribute into increased soil erosion & desertification and reduce soil fertility resulting low production and long dry spells. Also humanitarian-aid related activities, especially construction, can lead to soil deterioration, desertification, loss of livelihoods and conflict as a result of competition over land and resources. Extensive livestock practices such as irregular movement and overconcentration poses significant threaten to already fragile ecosystem, which further undermine the agricultural productive and pastoral capacity. The excavation of soil for brick making has a significant environmental impact and is a potential health risk (flooded pits; vector breeding ground). In some places soil excavation around tree roots has destroyed established mango orchards. In addition the accumulation of solid and medical waste has significant health risk for human and livestock, they pose both a direct health risk as well as risk of contamination of soil and water sources such as livestock that might consume plastic bags. The project design, activities and budget will contributes to increasing the resilience of selected vulnerable households (IDPs, Returnees and host community) in South Kordofan, and Central Darfur through provision of climate smart crop and vegetable seeds, provision of post harvest techniques for storages and processing, Village agriculture committee (VAC), training Community animal health workers, provision of vaccination and treatment inputs for livestock considering digging dumping sites for proper disposal of vaccine waste, demarcation of livestock routes, water catchments, and support agro-business in farm and off farming production to improve food security through climate change adaptation measures sustain agricultural and livestock production. Training women on FES to reduce use of wood for cooking as well as alternative energy sources. Extension services support for farmers to improve their knowledge land management practices in agriculture and livestock production. Establishment of VSLA will ensure the sustainability of self-managed micro-finance services at the community level in South Kordofan and Central Darfur. The project will strengthen structures and capacities within the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry, Fisheries and Animal Resources in strengthening agriculture and livestock extension service in Talodi and Leri localities in South Kordofan and Sharg Jabel Marra (E JM) in Central Darfur and Katayla and Nyala South Darfur State. This facilitates the implementation of measures focusing on sustainable natural resource management at selected locations of the project with the support of the extension agents/workers.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design and implementation we will ensure consideration for protective spaces for women and children to provide food security and livelihoods services are in safe environment and accessible, a confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system will bein place and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations, or exploitation. The beneficiaries and surrounding community will be aware of the dates and location of project implementation, The location of, to and from where the projects is implemented is safe and accessible to everybody and provision of assistance must not put the beneficiaries at any kind of risk.All FSL Staff will have to sign a code of conduct agreement or zero tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse, confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system is in place and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations. Feedback on the complaints should be regularly be provided to the beneficiaries, beneficiaries have been clearly informed of the cost, if any, of the services or that the services are free Gender-sensitization at community level will continue to be built into FSL training programmes.
