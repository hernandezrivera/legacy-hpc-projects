Project Summary

Since 2017, northern Mozambique has been marked by a non-international armed conflict, with severe human rights and protection impact on the civilian population. The conflict has led to forced displacement of over a million people, most of whom are women and children. Conflict-related sexual violence (CSRV) is a major concern in Cabo Delgado. As in most contexts, there is a likelihood of underreporting of sexual violence, including cases that would amount to sexual torture, due to insecurity, fear of retaliation, stigma, and discrimination.
Many cases reported seem to occur in the context of conflict-related displacement and involve a range of alleged perpetrators. Groups most vulnerable to sexual violence might include single, pregnant, and elderly women, female heads of households, widows, and young men and persons with disabilities. Additionally, there are reports of sexual violence involving persons who were abducted and held captive, sometimes for several years, and subjected to sexual violence including sexual slavery by NSAG. Those who escaped or were released from NSAG might then be detained for alleged association with NSAG. When returning to their communities, many people face stigma and discrimination as well as challenges, including those related to physical and mental health. Contextual, legal, and socio-economic barriers prevent survivors/ victims of sexual violence from accessing justice and effective remedies.
The project aims to enhance monitoring, analysis and reporting on conflict-related human rights violations in Cabo Delgado province, Mozambique, with a particular focus on conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) and deprivation of liberty for alleged association with NSAG. This will contribute to more targeted humanitarian responses as well strengthen preventive and corrective actions taken by State institutions, civil society and UN humanitarian actors. The objectives of the project are to: (i) document cases of conflict-related human rights violations, particularly CRSV and violations in the context of detention, and refer victims to humanitarian and other relevant actors for medical, psychosocial, legal and other assistance; (ii) support capacity-building of justice actors, the National Human Rights Commission and security and defense forces on CRSV; (iii) enhance advocacy capacities with State authorities to address specific cases and situations and seek accountability and remedies for victims.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Protection Monitoring: OHCHR will document and follow-up cases with victims and local actors in the targeted districts in order to identify patterns of human rights violations, which will strengthen UN regular monitoring, analysis and reporting, including inputs for the Secretary General Annual report on CRSV pursuant to Security Council resolution 2467 (2019). this will also contribute to enhance early warning analysis and early action to prevent and mitigate risks of conflict-related human rights violations, including sexual violence and deprivation of liberty. Case Management and Referral to Protection services: OHCHR will interview victims and follow up on their cases in order to verify human rights violations committed against them, referred them to legal and psychosocial services, and present their case to competent state authorities and advocate for corrective action on those individual cases and situations of concern. This will also support UN coordination at provincial and national level to address CRSV cases and other conflict-related human rights violations, including through establishing a referral system. Capacity building and technical assistance: Support awareness raising and improved responses on prevention and accountability for human rights violations in the context of armed conflict, with a gender perspective, including detention, through joint capacity building activities to administration of justice and security/defense actors in Cabo Delgado. Priority areas in Cabo Delgado: Pemba Cidade, Metuge, Chiure, Muidumbe, Ibo, Quissanga, Nangade, Mocimboa Da Praia, Mueda, Palma

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Through monitoring, analysis and reporting, the project will contribute to identify potential risks faced by the affected group (victims of conflict-related human rights violations), address specific cases and situations with competent authorities, UN actors and civil society partners, and support the development of mitigation measures aimed at reducing such risks in the long term. A range of risks affecting victims in situations of displacement or return can be observed and registered through monitoring work to ensure no further harm is caused, such as risks related to: women and girls living in close proximity of military bases and personnel (risk of sexual violence); people living in areas where access to basic services, mostly health and education, is impacted by the conflict or military presence; people with enhanced vulnerabilities imposed by the displacement, loss of livelihoods and lack of access to humanitarian assistance; revictimization of persons who escaped or were released by NSAG, and who were detained and prosecuted for alleged terrorist related activities. Human rights monitoring and documentation of individual cases is guided by the principles of do no harm, confidentiality and informed consent, and is informed by OHCHR methodology. Human rights information can be inherently confidential and sensitive and, therefore, there are specific risks related to protection of victims and the handling and sharing of data that will need to be accounted in the project implementation phase.Given the on-going armed conflict in Cabo Delgado, security and access constraints may impact project implementation in some targeted areas or districts, rendering it impossible to carry out activities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The promotion and protection of the rights of persons with disabilities is a priority area of work of OHCHR Mozambique.
