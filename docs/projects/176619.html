Project Summary

Reconstruindo Vidas aims to serve 4,000 IDP households (20,000 individuals) and 1,000 host family households (5,000 individuals) by providing emergency WASH NFIs, emergency latrines and bathing areas, and dissemination and knowledge sharing of relevant hygiene messaging include COVID-19 and waterborne illnesses.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Goal: Conflict-affected families in Cabo Delgado and Sofala province, Mozambique live with dignity in safe communities meeting minimum living standards. CRS/Caritas Pemba will assist 5,000 IDP households with increased access to safe and dignified WASH infrastructure, through market-based interventions where feasible and appropriate. CRS/Caritas will assist the IDPs relocated to specific government resettlement sites which include access to land for agricultural use and/or persons affected by natural disasters and/or highly vulnerable host community population. Rebuilding Lives and Communities will provide technical assistance, construction materials, and specific WASH NFIs through a market-based response where feasible. CRS/Caritas aims to support the most vulnerable families and will conduct a verification exercise to determine eligibility based on lists provided by the government and verified by the clusters. CRS will hold discussions with the larger community to review the shelter options and the support needed for construction. CRS will also hold discussions with most vulnerable groups including those with disabilities and the elderly to understand their needs, and to plan together how they can be supported in equal access to emergency WASH infrastructure and NFIs. CRS/Caritas will explore opportunities to engage community members and potential for income generating activities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CRS is committed to mainstreaming protection and gender in all emergency response efforts to promote safety, dignity, and meaningful access, especially those who may be more vulnerable and marginalized including those with disabilities and older people. CRS applies a framework to support the mainstreaming of protection principles into humanitarian activities. The framework mirrors the Global Protection Cluster’s approach, based on the principles of ensuring access to impartial assistance and services, especially for the most vulnerable and at-risk groups; accountability to beneficiaries; support for the development of self-protection capacities; and ensuring ‘do no harm’ by adapting activities in ways that mitigate identified protection risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

WASH interventions will consider protection issues in implementation and use, and safe spaces for women and children will be created in newly settled sites to improve project implementation and provide spaces to discuss protection issues. This project will highlight referral pathways, anonymous feedback, whistleblower protections, basic education on common protection concerns in an unstable environment.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Consultations will be done with individuals living with disabilities and the elderly to identify specific adaptation needs for infrastructure. Infrastructure will be adapted for these needs. During registrations, CRS will use the Washington Group Short Set of Questions to identify people with disabilities. Project monitoring will include quarterly and sex disaggregated focus group discussions (FGDs) which will serve as a check on the inclusion of vulnerable individuals (where feasible), and all output and quantitative data will be sex and age disaggregated. Where FGDs are not feasible due to COVID-19 risk mitigation strategies, data will be collected remotely through the KoBo platform. Secure messaging services and surveys will be done by project staff keeping two meters away from project participants, in small groups adhering to government regulations regarding the number of people in one gathering.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Materials, including timber and bamboo, will be procured directly by families at local vendors or input fairs using a voucher system. Before signing contracts, CRS will engage in an identification process with local vendors. These vendors will be assessed based on a diverse set of criteria, among which will be sustainability, harvest practices, and supply chain structure. CRS will give preference to working with certified vendors but will also advocate and encourage any vendor not employing these practices to adopt the proper bamboo harvest practices. Bamboo is not considered to be of limited supply.
