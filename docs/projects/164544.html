Project Summary

From the beginning of the Syrian crisis, Rural Damascus, Quneitra, Hama and Aleppo governorates witnessed important air strikes in addition to heavy artillery shelling targeting residential areas and vital facilities such as hospitals or schools, resulting in mass displacements and destructions. In this context, children have been exposed to important protection risks, ranging from lack of access to documentation, psychosocial distress, early marriage and gender-based violence (GBV) to physical disability, injury or death.
Through the proposed intervention, SIF will contribute to achieving objectives 1, 3, 4 and 5 of the 2020 Protection Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria through providing psychosocial support (PSS) to children, including the identification of cases facing protection risks, their access to specialized services and case-management. The project will also include awareness raising on a high range of protection concerns such as GBV, child protection (CP) and risk education (RE) toward explosive hazards. The proposed intervention will be part of an integrated program which will consist in providing both CP and education assistance in centers and public schools given that both sectors’ needs are huge and intertwined in the targeted localities.
SIF is already implementing CP programs in centers located in Rural Homs and Rural Hama and aims at scaling up its intervention through providing assistance to a total of 3,000 conflict-affected children in 4 centers in As-Saan and Karnaz (Rural Hama), and Eastern Kwaires and Jebel Saman (Rural Aleppo). The intervention will also provide 2,000 children with CP in public schools in Quneitra and Khan Arnaba (Quneitra Governorate), and Kafr Batna and Duma (Rural Damascus). These districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and SIF field assessments. It is important to note that all locations are rated 4 and over out of 6 as per Protection Sector’s severity scale, except for Eastern Kwaires which is only rated 2 whereas grave protection needs related to child early marriage, child labor and exposure to ERWs were reported during SIF field assessments (as detailed in Needs section).
The proposed intervention will consist in:
- Establishing 4 integrated-services centers combining Education and CP activities in each targeted area of Hama and Aleppo governorates;
- Providing psychosocial support (PSS) and recreational activities to children, including parents and caregivers through parenting programs;
- Conducting community GBV prevention and empowerment activities;
- Conducting community-based RE regarding explosive hazards;
- Providing specialized services through case management and referrals;
- Building the capacities of facilitators, counselors and case managers to prevent and respond to specific CP issues;
- Building the capacities of outreach volunteers (ORVs) to conduct data collection, awareness sessions and case identification and management, under the supervision of SIF case managers, in the surrounding communities of As-Saan, Khan Arnaba, Kafr Batna and Duma;
- Establishing CP committees involving community members and SIF case managers with the objective of clarifying basic protection, increasing awareness and knowledge about child protection risks and needs in the communities to prevent a decline in the sustainability of the outcome once the project ends.
SIF will consider and ensure gender-and-age aspects across all sub-sectors of focus – CP, GBV, and general protection. Cultural preferences and sensitivities will be considered through consultations with the staff – the majority of whom will be from local community – and the affected populations. Specific needs of children, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups have been considered in the design of the intervention, and will be continuously evaluated throughout the implementation.

4 - Needs

SIF’s response will build on extensive protection risks identified by 2020 HNO and SIF field assessments conducted in December 2019. GBV: Boys and girls are subject to violence including intimate partner violence, sexual violence, early marriage, forced marriage, deprivation of freedom of movement and forced pregnancy. SIF team reported severe GBV issues, even though cases remain under-reported and difficult to identify due to the absence of case management services and societal constraints preventing GBV survivors from seeking help. Child labor: High prevalence of child labor is preventing school attendance and exposing children to a high range of risks, including injuries, work exploitation and harassment. Lack of civil documentation: In the targeted areas which used to be outside Government control, families reported facing challenges in obtaining civil documentation because of their “concerns regarding approaching authority” (HNO 2019), the unavailability of institutions delivering official documentation as well as the unaffordability of such procedures. Psychosocial distress: Children’s experiences of violence in their daily lives, community, schools and homes, along with repeated displacements, loss of or separation from family members and friends, dramatic deterioration in living conditions, divisions in their community, and lack of basic social services is profoundly affecting children’s well-being and development, and putting them in a state of psychological distress. ERWs: SIF team reported that around 75% of the children assessed are still exposed to mine risks as a consequence of the hostilities. In addition of causing serious injuries, explosive hazards also prevent access to properties, basic services and livelihood, thus limiting the scope for recovery and reconstruction efforts.

5 - Implementation of activities

Child protection assistance will be directly implemented by SIF in all the targeted areas of intervention and will be complementary to SIF education programs. Assistance will be provided through three different intervention modalities, depending on the context and the needs identified. - Public schools: in Quneitra and Rural Damascus, child protection activities will be implemented during the week-ends and holidays in public schools, in coordination with SARC, DoE and MoE. - Integrated service centers: in Hama and Aleppo governorates, child protection interventions will be implemented in centers established by SIF 5 days per week in the absence of sufficient and suitable public facilities in these targeted areas. Centers will be rented and furnished and facilitators, counsellors and case managers directly hired by SIF will have been provided with compulsory training on CP. In these centers, children will be provided with both non-formal education and community-based child protection services. - ORVs: ORVs will be deployed in order to reach the population of surrounding villages of Karnaz, As-Saan, Eastern Kweires and Jebel Saman. In each targeted area, child protection interventions will be conducted by skilled facilitators, counselors and case managers (50% men, 50% women) further trained by SIF. SIF will ensure daily monitoring on the project progress to ensure that standards of project implementation and fund resources are being effectively implemented. Participatory approach will be undertaken through a complaints and feedback mechanism. Discussions will be gender-disaggregated and conducted by women facilitators when necessary. Pre and post-tests will be conducted to evaluate the impact of the awareness and training sessions, while the project implementation will be monitored according to the work plan and program indicators.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Children and protection personnel at risk of armed attack, unexploded ordnance or injury death on their way to/from SIF activities. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: training on RE; collaboration with relevant authorities to identify safe locations and ensure their support in the safety of the project implementation. 2. Children victim of abuse or violence from SIF staff or from other children. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: application of Child Safeguarding measures: recruitment of staff according to the safe recruitment process; training of all staff and associates who engage with children on child protection, identification of children victims of violence, first line support and safe referral pathways, safeguarding policies and procedures; signature of code of conduct also posted on walls; establishment of effective child-friendly complaint mechanism; awareness sessions on children's rights and GBV; PSS sessions on child protection risks and protection measures, similarities and differences/accepting others, communication and collaboration; sufficient number of adult women working in the learning environment/CFS to protect and reassure girls attending activities. 3. Community reluctance towards protection services, stigmatisation and violence against victims of GBV and violence against staff providing protection services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: gender-balanced staffing; men and boys participating in awareness sessions on GBV; case management in coordination with other services; data protection policy ensuring confidentiality; child-friendly room ensuring confidentiality and privacy during case management and counselling; careful assessment of risk before taking any decision always respecting the principles of do no harm and the best interests of the child; identify solutions that are seen as culturally acceptable to the family or community and that uphold the rights of children.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. The security situation puts SIF staff and beneficiaries at risk. Mitigation: SIF will proceed to a daily follow-up of the security situation in the targeted areas and put the activities on hold until the security situation is stabilized. 2. SIF is not granted the necessary approvals to implement activities in public schools. Mitigation: SIF has a good relationship with MoE but, in case SLPs in public schools would not be approved, SIF will engage in negotiation with MoE to obtain these approvals. 3. Perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner leading to tensions. Mitigation: selection of localities based on the Protection sector severity scale analysis; community consultations; coordination with other service providers; prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable; monitoring and feedback during and after service provision; develop schedules, in collaboration with children and caregivers, and with consideration for religious and other activities. 4. Inflation rate increases. Mitigation: SIF has already included the inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly in coordination with the donor. 5. Parents/caregivers discourage their children from attending SIF activities. Mitigation: in case the parents would refuse that their children attend awareness sessions, door-to-door campaigns will be organized. A dedicated SIF team will visit the families and provide them with the necessary information about the importance for their children to attend the sessions. 6. Unavailability of qualified manpower resources in the targeted locations. Mitigation: SIF will introduce campaigns and new methods of recruitment to reach quality staff from the same project locations.
