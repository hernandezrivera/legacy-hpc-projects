Project Summary

ACTED will aim to improve access to HLP services for IDPs through 2020. This will support the Protection Cluster Objective 3: Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population. Activities will be coordinated by ACTED in its role as camp manager of the Juba PoCs, and will be focused in urban Juba including the peri-POC site area and Mangateen.
To fulfill the above objective, ACTED will focus on the following:
1. Awareness raising and information dissemination activities in the PoC regarding HLP rights and services.
2. Coordination of protection, in particular HLP, partners in the PoC through a designated focal point.
3. Logistical support to HDC (primary HLP partner) to enable them to upscale and expand services, as well as referrals to HLP partners.
ACTED’s protection intervention will be closely integrated with its multi-sectoral response, particularly Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) and ongoing plans to adapt approaches to essential service provision (health and education). As camp manager, ACTED is convening a consortium to operate a project in the peri-PoC area and Mangateen to improve services and therefore equalise the service provision disparity with populations outside of the PoC. This will be linked with wider efforts on durable solutions, supporting PoC populations who wish to return to urban Juba – and potentially to other parts of South Sudan - to do so. Support to HLP services will be integral to ensuring that this project is able to holistically support IDPs who voluntarily seek durable solutions.

1 - Needs

Years of conflict have led to significant levels of displacement across South Sudan. Populations of internally displaced people have sheltered inside Protection of Civilian camps, which were created in 2013 to meet a short-term emergency need. Given that many POC site residents come from locations lacking even basic services, the comprehensive provision of humanitarian services inside POC sites has created a dependency and many residents cite reluctance to be without these services as a reason to stay. This is problematic and perpetuates displacement so long as the state cannot provide services at this level outside of the POC sites. Furthermore, it is hoped that in light of R-ARCSS implementation as part of a revitalized peace process, an improved security situation will lead to a conducive situation for returns to permanent places of residence for increasing numbers of South Sudanese IDPs. The September UN Secretary General report on planning for POC sites called for more critical thinking as to the longer-term structure of such sites to respect the principle of do no harm. The underlying agreement is that approaches to POC sites need to reflect new realities and make attempts at changing the status quo for better outcomes for residents in and around the sites themselves, as well as in Mangateen where an ex-POC site and host community populations face similar challenges. That all POC site residents are currently eligible for humanitarian assistance based on their status alone has driven the development of a comprehensive range of humanitarian services inside the Juba POC sites including schools, hospitals and expansive communal facilities. This differs from assistance provided to those outside of the POC sites, in urban Juba, in the peri-POC site area and most other areas of South Sudan, where it is targeted and needs-based – including for the population displaced to Mangateen. Over time, the level of assistance inside the POC sites has become disproportionate to the assistance provided outside. The outcome is a population inside POC sites with an unsustainable level of expectation with regards service provision, surrounded by local communities in the urban and peri-POC site area who often do not benefit from such a range of services. Broadly, to support durable solutions for populations currently residing in the Juba PoCs, the following pre-conditions are necessary:1. Equalising service provision by offering increased education and health services which are also available to populations in the urban and peri-PoC site area.2. Ensuring continuation of humanitarian service provision (FSL, WASH etc) in areas such as Mangateen where ex-PoC residents are currently residing following protection risks inside the PoC.3. Supporting access to and understanding of Housing, Land and Property services for affected IDP populations.4. Coordination of centralised protection services for PoC residents.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Coordination of protection servicesACTED as camp manager in the PoC will coordinate protection partners, including HLP partners, to ensure access to comprehensive protection services for camp residents. These services will be coordinated alongside other service provision.Output 2: Support high-priority HLP cases through partners.ACTED will provide logistical support to enable partners (particularly HDC) to support high-priority HLP cases on a case-by-case basis, in collaboration with existing initiatives, such as the work carried out in the POC sites by HDC and UNHCR. It is anticipated that support will focus on households who have registered an intention to leave the POC sites but have an unresolved HLP issue running for more than six months. ACTED will also support by providing referrals.Output 3: Information dissemination and awareness raising on HLP rights and servicesAs part of its CCCM activities, ACTED establishes and strengthens community governance and leadership mechanisms. Leadership committees (camp management committees, women committees, youth committees etc) will be trained specifically on HLP issues and services to ensure information dissemination. Further awareness raising will be conducted through communication with communities activities in collaboration with HLP partners. Through our coordination role, ACTED will work with other HLP partners to ensure that duplication is avoided.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

ACTED will work with HDC’s team of HLP lawyers, UNHCR and other operational expert partners to seek solutions to high-priority cases in collaboration with existing initiatives. This will be supported by broader local authority engagement initiatives.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project is targeted at IDPs currently residing in the PoC. Working with existing community networks within and outside of the POC sites and in Mangateen, ACTED and partners will hold discussion fora and trainings to support individuals to identify, unwrap and take steps to resolve safety issues and risks faced by those in the communities, focusing specifically on children and women. Key target groups will be teachers, religious leaders, influential community figures, youth and new parents. Activities will involve risk mapping and risk mitigation planning and this represents a “soft” component of understanding better specific issues faced and specific solutions identified at a local level.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

As discussed above, the project will work closely with local stakeholders including community leadership, state leadership and the RRC.The project is in line with the Secretary General report on the PoCs and aligns with the longer-term planning on PoCs.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with the goals to ensure durable solutions for returning IDPs, as well as addressing historic service imbalances with populations outside of PoCs through increasing service provision.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Implementation and success of the R-ARCSS remains a key assumption as it links directly with the perception of safety and security of POC site residents as well as those residing in urban Juba. This move to a transitional government symbolizes cooperation and political will from higher levels of government to strive for the peace and development of South Sudan. Building the trust of communities within and outside of the POC sites is a fundamental component of the success of this project and will run throughout its entire course. This element of the project will use the experience and expertise of UNMISS CAD given its lengthy engagements in the peri-POC site and Mangateen areas, and rich understanding of the local and broader political dynamics. During the initial phases of the project, ACTED and partners will hold “sensing” meetings with various levels of leadership, including women and youth, inside and outside the POC sites as well as in and around Mangateen. Humanitarian partners will also be encouraged to conduct an updated protection risk assessment in the peri-POC site and Mangateen areas. Engagement with Jubek state leadership and RRC is a critical component of this activity.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project integrates service provision elements intended to ensure investment in longer-term, sustainable service provision outside of PoCs by integrating them with national systems (e.g. integration of new health facilities with the Health Pooled Fund).

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ACTED conducts protection analysis at onset of interventions to identify potential risks to beneficiaries and underlying factors. ACTED utilizes a do no harm matrix during assessment which has a focus on HLP analysis. ACTED is aware that prevailing gender norms present challenges. A study by David Deng for the South Sudan Law Society in March 2019 about HLP issues in South Sudan found that land ownership customs were not favourable to women, who were consequently less likely to seek a solution. Therefore, women will be prioritized in awareness raising activities, and will be consulted throughout the project through womens groups.Within the PoCs and in Mangateen, ACTED establishes and supports inclusive community governance mechanisms. These groups will be consulted throughout with a focus on ensuring all minority groups are included in consultations and project design.The project is targeted towards addressing the risks related to dispute on land ownership and intercommunity conflict over access to resources. Providing HLP services should support reduction of risks related to the former; the other part of the project related to service provision should support reduction of the latter by addressing historic imbalances. However, these changes will be very closely coordinated with UNMISS and dependent on the prevailing security situation, to ensure that they remain minimal and the project will do no harm.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba that will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in WBeG, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report (verbally) complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. Feedback sessions will be arranged in coordination with leadership groups including women and youth groups to ensure broad participation. ACTED will work in collaboration with other protection partners to ensure feedback mechanisms reach the most vulnerable.
