Project Summary

Overall context of crises in Ukraine
The human toll of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine is critical, with 4.4 million people affected by the crisis, of whom 3.4 million require humanitarian assistance and protection. The shelling of urban areas and civilian infrastructure means that 60 per cent of 200,000 people living along the 457-kilometre ‘contact line’ in the Government-controlled area (GCA) are affected by shelling regularly, and almost 40 per cent are affected every day. There is just under a million individual crossings of the ‘contact line’ each month, which is rapidly becoming one of the most mine contaminated stretches of land in the world. Food insecurity has doubled since 2016, with 1.2 million people food insecure, and there are escalating cases of multi-drug resistant TB, HIV and even polio. HIV prevalence among pregnant women in conflict-affected oblasts is significantly higher than the national average. These impacts are aggravated by Ukraine’s extremely harsh winter, severe restrictions on humanitarian access and limited livelihood opportunities for those affected by the crisis. As hopes of a political solution have waned, so have people’s savings and their ability to cope. Four years into the crisis, millions of people, including 1.6 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), half of whom are the elderly, are being forced to make impossible choices between food, medicine, shelter, heating or their children’s education. The unique demographics of the crisis in Ukraine is that the elderly make up almost 30 per cent of people in need. Those who are most vulnerable are increasingly resorting to risky means to cope, including survival sex, trafficking and alcoholism. Today, the dire humanitarian crisis in eastern Ukraine is protracted and complex, whilst the response is severely underfunded and largely forgotten by the international community.
(HNO2019)
Logic of project intervention is based on Theory of Change and aimed to contribute to resolve main problem of affected population by elimination of core causes according to following program concept:
Main problem
Basic needs of affected population in health are not secured
Resolved if:
basic needs of affected people in mental and physical health are secured
Core cause 1
Lack of access of beneficiaries to needed goods and services
Eliminated if:
Access to necessary goods and services or to appropriate product/service providers is ensured. Beneficiaries have appropriate conditions to keep goods.
Core cause 2
Lack of purchasing ability of beneficiaries to cover basic medical needs
Eliminated if:
Beneficiaries have financial resources to procure necessary medical goods and services
The intervention is to be in GCA with more focus on 20 km contact line.
According to HNO2019, the overall problematic in the area of intervention described as following:
*Around 1.3 million people within 20 km of the contact line in need of life-saving and essential health services (HNO 2019).
*Physiological signs of distress, such as difficulty sleeping, and emotional difficulties, including grief, fear and crying for no reason (2018 IMC MHPSS Assessment)
Core causes related to access:
*According to the Health Cluster, two-thirds of households do not know where to access psycho-social services, and 17 per cent of rural households report that there is no place to access services in their settlements (2018 REACH Winter Assessment)
* Lack of access to medications and treatments remains life-threatening for many patients, who have diabetes, cancer, and cardio-vascular conditions, including hypertension, which affects over 50 per cent of people of over 50 years old in the conflict-affected area. (HNO 2019)
*The conflict has isolated rural GCA health facilities from major health centers located in urban areas in NGCA due to increased distances, travel times to essential health services and impeded access to referral hospitals and pharmacies (HNO 2019)
Core cause related to purchasing ability:
*Conflict affected older people are particularly at risk. Most rely solely on their pension to cover basic cost of living, and this is often insufficient to cover food, medicines and utilities. (2018 Baseline report, HelpAge International)
Framework of project intervention for 2019-2020 to eliminate core causes and contribute to resolve main problems is following:
Overall goal: Basic needs of 12080 IDPs and residents of buffer zone are secured
Physical Health
Outcome: Beneficiary has improved or stabilized physical health condition.
Output: Beneficiary obtained conditional and restricted cash/voucher for life-saving medicine and services amounting 1000 UAH per months 4 times or 4000 UAH as one payment.
Objective: Conflict affected population access quality life-saving and essential health care services
Activity: Distribution of 31680 monthly medical cash grants/vouchers to 7920 people by mobile medical workers
Geographical scale: Donetska and Luhanska oblasts GCA, 80% in 0-20 km contact line, 20% in area excluding 20 km along the contact line including remote rural areas of Kharkivska, Dnipropetovska and Zaporizska oblasts.
Mental Health
Outcome: Beneficiary has improved psycho-emotional condition
Output: Beneficiary obtained up to 6 psychological individual consultations, participation in group work sessions, informational event
Objective: Conflict affected population access psycho-social and mental health support (4160 beneficiaries)
Activities: 1 Provision of individual consultations to 2080 beneficiaries by personal meeting or by phone. 2. Provision of group 1008 sessions to 2080 beneficiaries in the settlements by mobile psychologists. 3. Provision of 192 information events in the settlements.
Geographical scale: GCA, 0-20 km contact line
Project uses such feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries as on-line form, boxes for complaints and recommendations, hotlines in each centers. In addition, post distribution monitoring allows to improve the quality of activities
Gander age equity of beneficiaries and protection issues for access to the aid is taken into account in design of distribution process as well as accountability mechanism and participation of local communities.
To provide access to the most vulnerable people in need project uses several approaches that are efficiently worked out in 2018, namely:
- high-level access is provided by mobility of teams who assist people in remote areas, especially rural ones with bad infrastructure
- targeting of people who are exactly in high-scale humanitarian crises is provided by selection of the most vulnerable affected people with highest needs paying strong attention to the income level and coordination with other local aid providers
- protection is provided by home visits to households to evaluate critical needs and gender roles
- mitigation of tensions between those who obtained assistance and those who did not is provided by participation of local activists in settlements of distribution in process of registration, needs evaluation and distribution approach.
- sustainability of beneficiaries is provided by using possibilities to redirect beneficiaries to Caritas transitional and social projects as well as services provided by other NGOs and governmental agencies.
Caritas Ukraine as implementing partner of interventions of Caritas Germany funded by GFFO has experience of efficient implementation of humanitarian projects since 2015. Interventions in the first half of 2019 will be covered by the approved and currently running project (15.12.17-31.07.19). For the following 1,5 years a follow-up project is planned. The activities are implemented through 6 local centers located in Mariupol, Kramatorsk, Severodonetsk, Zaporizhya, Kharkiv and Dnipro. Capacities include highly trained field staff and management, safety& security system and MEAL system that are integrated at the field level.

Ukraine Humanitarian Response Plan 2019

Plan fields

Needs

Physical health (HNO 2019)Residents in both GCA and NGCA experience difficulties in accessing appropriate health care services due to insecurity and disrupted transportation. People with disabilities, families with young children and the elderly, are among the most vulnerable. Many cannot easily move independently, while in many areas there are no available health care facilities within an accessible distance for timely emergency care.The irregular supply of medicines, medical supplies and diagnostic equipment deprive those highly dependent upon the availability of life-saving care, such as insulin-dependent diabetics; those in need of hemodialysis and blood bank services; and mothers and infants in need of appropriate pre- and post-natal care. Obstetric and prenatal care, along with childbirth services are lacking and are difficult to access.While most health care services are considered to be free of charge, real out-of-pocket costs borne by the affected population have significantly increased (e.g., transport, diagnostics, and medications) deterring many and further diminishing their health and resilience.Mental health and protection (HNO 2019)Lack of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services is a key gap in protection and health sectors. Psycho-social support (PSS) remains one of the most requested forms of assistance from conflict-affected people. Two-thirds of households do not know where to access psychosocial services, and 17% of rural households report that there is no place to access services in their settlements.MHPSS assessment indicated that 61% of those surveyed stated that alcohol and drug use was the main problem in their community, followed by caring for people who are on their own (52%) and mental illness (41%). While PSS issues continue to be reported amongst all age groups, there remains a gap in MHPSS services for working-age people.Women and girls face higher vulnerability due to crisis-induced risks such as increased exposure to violence and exploitation. This increase in gender-based violence further exacerbate overall needs. Gender- and sexual-based violence (including conflict-related) remain much underreported and are not adequately addressed through available outreach and health services.
