Project Summary

The proposed project will address urgent lifesaving Gender Based violence in the selected counties of Panyijiar,Leer, Mayendit, Mayom, Paraing and Abiemnhom. The project will build on the strong foundation Coalition for Humanity has in the southern Unity in the implementation of key protection interventions including GBV, general Protection with support from South Sudan humanitarian fund (SSHF), Behavior change communication (BCC) currently implemented in greater Leer with support from MEDAIR, GBV targeting distribution points in Leer and Mayendit with support and partnership with Care/WFP and the Conflict Transformation with support from GIZ .CH already has a good footprint when it comes to GBV and based on the needs and gaps, will expand service to rest of the counties identified in this project. In these counties, GBV issues have been leading concerns with rampant reports of rape, sexual violence against women and girls as well as biased traditional practices such as early and forced marriages.
Women and girls have been subjected to inhumane treatments especially during the height of the conflict where they were captured and forced to marry commanders of the armed militias, forced labour, rape, sexual violence and lack of psychosocial and counseling for survivors. The project will address this service gaps for survivors by putting in place mechanisms for reporting and providing urgent PSS, Case management and counseling within the established women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS) and new ones.
The project targets an overall total of 12,000 beneficiaries broken down as below (disaggregated by sex)
Women= 4,800
Men= 1,200
Girls= 4,200
Boys= 1,800
Total: 12,000
The proportion of beneficiaries in each county shall be determined by needs and severity at the time of intervention. However, focus will be placed on already ongoing work such as in Panyijiar, Leer and Mayendit to ensure there are no gaps in service delivery to vulnerable survivors of GBV and other protection concerns.
This GBV project is designed in line with GBV sub cluster objectives and the strategic objectives of the HRP2020. CH will align the project activities to the needs on the ground including strengthening collaboration we had with non-protection partners such as Concern Worldwide in other programming such as shelter and NFIs, WASH, Health and Food security and livelihoods. We will continue to collaborate with Medair in our Behaviour Change communication (BCC) project in Leer as well as the Care collaboration on GBV project linked to the food distribution sites with WFP in both Leer and Mayendit County.
The strategic expansion in the new locations in this project are areas where Concern worldwide, our partner continue to operate with funding from SSHF and other external funding. This has yield results and provided complementary in term of better use of limited resources against huge needs. The current GBV project will be implemented, integrated and mainstreamed across other projects such as WASH, Health, FSL in all locations we have present in. The project will be in response to dire humanitarian situation evidenced by many reports in public domain including CH internal assessments reports in areas targeted for this support. The IPC report released in September 2019 indicates that 6.2 million (about 54%) of the population is in IPC 3. 4. 5. Four million (about 39%) in IPC 4. And rest in Phase 5. This information is corroborated by IOM Displacement and Tracking Matrix (DTM), which places over 1 million as internally displaced across the country. The REACH reports confirm that more than 50% of people interviewed in various counties in the country indicated GBV and other protection concerns are most issues affecting or worrying them. In order to ensure sustainability and change of attitude against vulnerable women and girls and to achieve our object, CH will strengthen its collaboration with partners such as GBV sub cluster, PC, UNMISS and other actors.

1 - Needs

The crisis in South Sudan is continuously having negative impacts on the entire populations with particular adverse effects experienced by women, girls and children. While the peace agreement seems to hold until now, there has been localized conflicts in many part of the country characterized by inter-communal violence, revenge killings, violence against women and girls. The number of IDPs and returnee continue to increase with IOM displacement and Tracking matrix (DTM) report placing IDPs figures at 1, 420,189 (March 2019). The destruction that was brought about by the conflict with negative effects on girls overstretched the service of humanitarian partners in the country. The GBV situation remains dire with high level of rape cases, sexual assaults and domestic cultural practices biased against women and girls. Women and girls continue to fall victims at the hands of the allied militias, gangsters and youth groups. The protection environment remains one of the worse when it comes to humanitarian outlook. Most of the population in the Panyijiar,Leer, Mayendit, MayomPariang and Abiemnhom counties are either in Protection of civilian sites (POCS) or internally displaced camps in hard to reach areas for safety or outside in the neigbouring countries. Therefore humanitarian assistance shall remain the only option in 2020. According to the recent IPC reports, more than 55.4 % of the 6.21 million are in needs and fall in phase 4 & 5, which is a worrying, situation. This projection is higher than for 2019. OCHA snapshot reports point to the same direction. The information has been corroborated by our internal CH assessment reports and partners in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Guit, Pariang, Abiemhnom. Furthermore, the REACH reports describe the situation in South Sudan generally and Unity State in particular as a “complex humanitarian crisis, restricting humanitarian access and hindering the flow of information required by aid partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to populations in need”. More than 50% of the population interviewed reported protection concerns such as insecurity, lack of access to various services, Gender basis violence and insecurity for girls and women as major concerns. All these reports confirm gaps and needs pointing to urgent support needed to help the most affected population whose livelihoods depend on humanitarian assistance. From the protection monitoring and assessments reports by CH both in Panyijiar and Leer Counties, which are entry points (Tayar and Adok ports) for returnees, it was reported that over 1,000HHs Returnees returned. Most of these are vulnerable women and girls who need urgent support. Partners report high protection risks among IDPs, Returnees and Host communities. Food aid is constrained, shelters and NFIs, WASH and health especially the reproductive and maternal health becoming life threatening. Most health facilities and services were rendered non-functional due to conflict across most the counties being targeted in this project. A recent Youth Engagement assessment conducted by CH in southern Unity Counties of Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar confirm fights and disagreements over girls as a leading cause for killing among boys/youth. The armed youth also engaged in revenge killings, communal violence and serious Gender biased violence. (CH assessment report 30th Aug-12th Sept 2019). Besides, the implementations of peace deal where deployment of troops in cantonment sites or barracks near community settlement is heightening tension especially in locations targeted in this project in former Unity state. Examples of these locations included but not limited to Mirmir in Mayendit, Dingding in Leer. In this case, CH will work closely with partners like Protection cluster, GBV sub cluster, UNMISS and other protection partners and non-protection partners to ensure potential protection risks mitigation, prioritizing the protection of the civilians and the rights of women and girls restored.

2 - Activities

Activities and Outcomes.Activity 1.1.0: Five (5) women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS)constructed with local materials and equipped with basic amenities within the 5 counties of Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar,Pariang and Abiemnhom.Activity 1.1.1: 600 women and girls provided with income generation activities (IGA) and life skills training within the established WGFS. The training will include bead making, sewing, bed sheets decoration and basket making with local materials. Another 500 women and girls will be train on psychological first aid that will include crisis intervention, peer support and individual and group counselling and this will make a totaling of 850 women and girls assessing WGFS. Activity 1.1.2: 750men, boys, women and girls will be trained as a GBV champions on addressing GBV cases and how to prevent it. Activity 1.1.3: 150 Survivors (Anticipated) supported and taken through the comprehensive case management. Activity 1.1.4: 10, 000 community members including local leaders are provided with information through massive awareness and advocacy sessions to highlight harmful cultural practices against women and girls.Activity 1.1.5:Strengthen internal institutional policies,standards and practices within CH to drive action and accountability on GBV and Gender equality in humanitarian service delivery as linked to commitment to Call to Action on Protection from GBVie.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Coalition for Humanity as a humanitarian organization is cognizant of the rights of our beneficiaries and the environment we operate. We recognize and advocate for the human rights in general and especially for vulnerable women, girls and children. We are guided by DO NOT HARM principle across the board and ensure that our staff, consultants and volunteers are guided by our strict code of conduct which do not condone any abuse of authority and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse.As part of these efforts we ensure that our beneficiaries are brought on board in all intervention that we do by consulting them and taking their views into consideration. By this they all feel valued, right recognized and become part and parcel of everything that will happen within the communities. We advocate on behalf of our beneficiaries with different actors both government and other oppositions to help reach to the vulnerable households for assistance that they greatly need.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project targets internally displaced persons (IDPs),returnees and host communities without access to basic service. Priority benefits are channelled to the most affected communities with limited access to other support.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project is linked to the community system with loose connection to government system, as it is emergency humanitarian response only. We however, cooperate with the local authorities, different categories of beneficiaries such as women, girls, boys and people wit special needs to incorporate their views from time to time and part and parcel of the entire response cycle. The project primarily meets the immediate life-saving need of women and girls but tries to provide training and advocacy and awareness to change the bad mindset especially against women and girls.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with Humanitarian response plan which is national in nature. However, since this is an emergency response, government participation except in provision of security,coordination and facilitation is quiet limited. The project primary beneficiaries includes IDPs,returnees and host communities with limited attention to refugees. The project is aligned with GBV Sub cluster and General protection objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

CH takes all necessary precautions to ensure that our activities are conducted in manner that does not bring more harm to the affected population. Therefore to do that, we under thorough risk analysis before any intervention to ensure the environment in which we provide humanitarian service is safe for our beneficiaries. While the priority of this project is immediate and emergency, we strive to ensure that it has lasting effects on the community to change their lives in the long term. We work with established project management committees (PMC) and local leaders and beneficiaries to ensure that they views are incorporated and inform the way the project is implemented. This result in maximum benefits to them

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project designed include the participation and the views of the community. Depending on the project duration, the community are able to secure the sustainability of the ongoing project and take it up for further support to the vulnerable members. The project will build on our emergency work in most of the targeted locations where we have in place trained GBV groups, Extension workers and other local leaders who are GBV champions.
