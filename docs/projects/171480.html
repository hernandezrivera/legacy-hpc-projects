Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect the livelihoods, improve the resilience, productive capacity and enhance the food security of vulnerable farmers and herders who are living in Area C in north of West Bank and in Jordan valley.
The objective is drawn from the Country Strategic Objective: SO1:
“Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced
This is also in line with FSS guidance for Immediate assistance and livelihood support for farmers in Area C and Jordan valley of the West Bank. This is also in line with the FSS main two results 1- Agricultural assets and productive capacities of farms and damaged productive assets (wells, irrigation systems, cisterns, greenhouses) restorated.
2- Food supply chain continued and on track. Market access for Palestinian agri-food products through a multi-faceted approach improved and developed.
Additionally, the project is in line of the clusters plans of the ministry of ministry of agriculture in Area C in North of West Bank and in Jordan valley.
The main activities will focus on rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for farmers and herders including promotion of technical capacities and small scale production units, and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience in most vulnerable 37 communities in Nablus, Tubas, Jenin and Jericho governorates.
The proposed activities will focus to improve the access to the essential resources (water, lands, etc) and provide inputs to increase the production capacities of the targeted farmers and herders in the selected areas. As part of CARE strategy, and given the nature of the protracted crisis, CARE work closely with and through AIDA toward coordinated advocacy, for accountability for IHL violations in Area C.
The project targeted communities are located in most vulnerable areas in Area C in West Bank and in Jordan valley who most of them were under threat of annexation or soldiers or /and settler’s violence

1 - Needs

Palestine’s context became more complicated as result of the deterioration in the political situations accompanied by dramatic deterioration in the health situation as a result of the COVID 19 pandemic. The challenges originating from the protracted confict, economic stagnation, high unemployment, gender inequalities and high rates of poverty and food insecurity continued to deteriorate. In the WB, the prolonged period of slow economic growth, restrictions on movement, trade, investment and access to land and water resources, together with higher food prices, continue to erode the fragile livelihoods of poor Palestinians. According to the HNO 2021, Approximately 28 per cent of the food insecure, or some 600,000 people, are in the West Bank, where the COVID-related deterioration added to the hardship generated by the coercive environment linked to the Israeli occupation. Despite the only marginal increase in unemployment between the first and second quarter of the year (both below 15 per cent), the impact of the pandemic is expected to be fully reflected in the employment statistics covering July-December. The livelihoods of households and entire communities located in, or relying on access to, Area C and EJ continue to be undermined by the Israeli authorities’ destruction/confiscation of structures lacking building permits, restrictions on access to farming and grazing land, and the lack of adequate law enforcement on violent settlers. Palestinian Bedouin and herding communities are disproportionately affected due to their location in sensitive areas and their reliance of agriculture-based livelihoods, as their sole or main source of income. The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of target communities, based on a number of key sources, including a rapid assessment conducted CARE in September-October, 2020 and visited different communities in Area C/ North and middle Jordan valley in Nablus and Tubas districts, revising assessments for similar ongoing or recently ended projects implemented by CARE targeting several communities (Safe vegetables and sheep and goat dairy products). Field visits were conducted to the proposed communities and meetings with the local councils and local CBOs, and beneficiaries were conducted to come up with the most urgent needs in these communities that guided the design of this action; with taking into consideration the gender needs and age groups. COVID 19 response plan, HNO (2021), Joint Rapid Gender analysis between CARE and OCHA also informed the design of the intervention All of the activities proposed reflect the needs realized at the led level, and which also are in line with what has been identified from HNO/MoA. All farmers reported a dramatic drop in their income as a result of COVID19 as the lock down, movement restrictions, lack of access to markets and inputs resulted in cash shortages and inability of many of the farmers to cope with the crisis. The findings show the high constraints in rehabilitating their Green Houses, accessing to water and land, high needs to support sheep and goat breeders through provision of inputs like dairy processing tools, seeds and forages. Additionally, agriculture roads rehabilitation came as highest priorities to improve the access to land, and water. According to a RAPID GENDER ANALYSIS TO INFORM THE 2021 HRP in oPt conducted by CARE/OCHA food and nutrition security is also connected to challenges associated with the Israeli occupation. Women & children in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers in this sector, reflected in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility, and limited technical preparation. As a result, food insecurity has also disproportionately impacted women/children and marginalized groups. ALL proposed activities were developed in close coordination with MOA in each district

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Accountability to the affected people: CARE is committed to assuring accountability to the communities we serve and stakeholders in all aspects of the project, the design of the project took into consideration the different needs of the different groups in the communities (sex and age), where the communities informed the design of the project activities, the composition of the teams, selection criteria, he formation of the local committees that would include representatives from the different groups and actors in each community including women and youth, frequency of the services, etc. Monitoring will have carried out through on sight monitoring of distributions and activities; monitoring system will be designed to include segregation of data by sex and age, Partners will follow the same monitoring guidelines and through reporting provide information. CARE has integrated a ‘Do No Harm’ approach by integrating ‘social and culture related checks’ into project design. CARE’s extensive field experience highlights the understanding of gendered power dynamics in the targeted areas, and identifies the potential impact of project activities on the social fabric of the community it works with, as key aspects to minimizing any unintended negative social impact. The project intervention has considered all factors (social, economic, and political) to design the intervention without added burden of responsibility for women and risk of backlash from men, which can result for instance in increased incidences of GBV. CARE has Zero tolerance toward sexual exploitation and abuse and child abuse (PSEAH) and has in place strict mechanism for reporting, investigating and follow up. A yearly refresh courses on PSEAH are provided to CARE and Partners teams. At the same time Availability of various options for reporting on sexual harassment and other wrongful conduct, including anonymously to a third party via the internet or phone call is ensured. CARE’s Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; anti Fraud and Corruption; Anti-Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH) should be signed by all project’s staff/teamA monthly meeting will be conducted at community level to update beneficiaries and communities leaders about project development and challenges, which will enable beneficiaries to provide recommendations to maximize the benefits and highlight lessons learned. As most project activities will be carried out in sensitive restricted Area C, a comprehensive risk analysis and security incidents will be discussed and updated in a participatory approach with the target population and by engaging men and boys. Consequently, mitigation measures will be carried out to reduce potential risks adhering to the principle of “Do No Harm” CARE is currently Co-chairing the PSEAH working group in Palestine with UNFPA and will leverage its experience and capacity to support the implementation of the project.
