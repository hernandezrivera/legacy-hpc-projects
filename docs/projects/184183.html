Project Summary

Primary health care visits and vaccination coverage have sharply declined in Somalia amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, raising concerns that vaccine preventable diseases such as respiratory infections and AWD as well as malnutrition could be going untreated, all while floods have forced hundreds of thousands of people from their homes. While the healthcare system’s resources are already limited and overstretched, this development could easily degenerate into a public health crisis without sustained outreach to those at risk.
As is the case with disasters, flooding and droughts heightens the risk of family separation, and causes severe distress for children due to the shock and loss of homes and disruption in daily life.
According to the Somalia health sector Strategy (2018-2021), the health and wellbeing of the Somali people remains in a critical situation with some of the worst health indicators in the world and the country is ‘off-track’ to reaching health related SDGs. Under-5 mortality is very high (137 per 1,000 live births), Coverage of routine immunization is low in all the districts of Lower Shabelle. In six targeted districts of the project coverage of Penta-III is 54% and measles vaccination coverage is 69%.
Referencing AYUUB Multi-Sectoral Assessment report on jan 2021, There is high risk also of increased waterborne diseases including AWD Cholera and malnutrition rate due to open defecation since no service is available in the newly located camps in the disricts of Lower and MiddleShabelle region mainly Merka, Afgoye, Wenlaweyn and Jowhar districts
These four districts were among the most areas hosted highest IPDs movement, mainly escaped from relapsing flood waves since April 2020,The report highlights that the new floods, COVID19 affected people are still suffering from lack of health Service that accelerated concerns on earlier health problems. there for AYUUB is seeking and proposing with the interventions to Reduce of excess morbidity and mortality due disease outbreak driven by drought and Floods and climatic shocks and conflict IDPs and non-displaced and to Contribute the improvement in physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement and nondisplaced; including people with disabilities by targeting 35,400 (8500 Women, 8400 Men 9100 Boys &9400 Girls) in Merka, Qoryoley,
Kurtunwarey , Barawe, Wenlaweyn and Jowhar in Lower and Middle Shabelle regions in The targeted beneficiaries are IDP's &Vulnerable hosted communities especially families with malnourished children .Through this project health activities will be carried out in the sense of sectoral response of the bellow activities in the program Implementation
- Activity 1.1. Support to early detection, investigation and response to disease (e.g. outbreak, COVID 19) through public awareness, HCW training, monitoring (EWAR/COVID19) and supplies Activity.
2.1 Health awareness, social mobilization, education and advocacy activities in disease control, general wellbeing, health and nutrition, MHPSS, disabilities, AYUUB will be conducted 4 mobilization sessions each months totaling 48 sessions for the Project Implementation
Activity 3.1. Clinical health care services, provision of life-saving emergency and essential health care, through fixed and mobile outreach services (e.g. essential Primary Health Care, child health, trauma care, communicable disease treatment, NCD detection and treatment, STD treatment, TB screening and treatment), AYUUB will Support 12 health facilities functional with all the necessary requirements by Targetted 35,400 (8500 Women, 8400 Men 9100 Boys &9400 Girls)
