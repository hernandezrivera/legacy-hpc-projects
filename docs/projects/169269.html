Project Summary

Following the Beirut Port Explosion, which claimed 180 lives, injured over 6000 and caused widespread material damages, primary and secondary data collection identified widespread multisector humanitarian needs, notably in terms of shelter (in the form of support to rehabilitation), food and livelihoods. Regarding shelter, complete destruction of buildings and infrastructure was reported within a 2 km range from the port, and buildings up to 8 km from the explosion site reported extensive structural damage.
As specified in the Lebanon Flash Appeal 2020, it is estimated that the Port explosions directly impacted 291,180 individuals living in 72,265 apartments located across 9,700 buildings within three km of the epicenter. While it led to some displacement to non-affected areas, a majority of affected households continue to reside within their damaged buildings. The Shelter Sector has classified the building damages into three levels: Level 1 (light), Level 2 (moderate) and Level 3 (severe).
Humanity and Inclusion (HI) can support the Shelter response through two different modalities:
- Cash for shelter (rental support) targeting families who have or will be forced to relocate due to structural damages or destruction of their apartment/house;
- Minor repair targeting families living in apartments/houses lightly damaged (level 1) and Rehabilitation of apartments/houses moderately damaged (level 2) through cash for shelter (for purchasing of materials and hiring of labour).
In addition, HI’s approach is to mainstream disability (physical, sensory and cognitive) and address the potential barriers faced by persons with disabilities, older people and other groups in post-disaster shelter programs. Therefore, HI’s shelter response will be to build back better; ensuring that shelters repaired or rehabilitated are disability-inclusive when needed (for persons with preexisting disabilities or new disabilities caused by the Beirut port explosions).
Vulnerable areas are prioritized for HI’s Shelter response based on a combination of factors including level of damage sustained from the explosion, pre-existing socio-economic vulnerabilities, and areas under-served by other humanitarian actors. The specific area(s) allocated to HI for the implementation of its Shelter response within Beirut and Mount Lebanon are to be allocated by the Shelter sector (Upon geographical split between shelter actors, external coordination stakes, number of HH targeted, etc. internal referrals by rehabilitation and MHPSS/Protection components are complemented by other modalities such as hotline, external referral, community focal points, etc).
In total, the project targets 612 individuals (180 Households (HHs), by applying an average of 3.4 members per households [The Labour Force and Households’ Living Conditions Survey, Central Adminis]). Disaggregating the total, 30 households will benefit from rental support, 75 households will repair their houses (level 1), and additional 75 households will rehabilitate their houses (level 2). Out of the 150 houses repaired/rehabilitated, 30 (20%) will be made accessible for persons with disabilities.

Plan fields

1 - Needs

REHABILITATION OF DAMAGED SHELTERS (LEVELS 1 & 2)People surveyed in early assessments reported the urgent need to repair broken windows and doors, as well as the need for cement, electricity cables and balcony railings. However, Lebanon’s economic crisis, which wiped out 70% of savings for many Lebanese, will make it very difficult for people to fund repairs to their homes. Prices of construction and repair materials such as wood, aluminum and steel for windows and doors, as well as glass are likely to increase due to higher demand and their potential scarcity on the market will make it very difficult for people to fund repairs to their homes. The most vulnerable households might face prolonged displacement or live in hazardous conditions without basic services such as water and electricity, or privacy. Even those that find equipment and can afford to cover the expenses face strict cash withdrawal limits imposed by Lebanese banks. As a result, they might also have to wait before their houses are habitable again [ACAPS, 12 August 2020].NEED OF ALTERNATIVE SHELTER SOLUTIONS While some households who have had their shelters destroyed are able to stay with family and friends, move to a different location, or rent another place, some do not have the means to re-shelter themselves (financial or a support system to rely on). Some of them are now homeless or living in insecure and uninhabitable conditions (level 3 damage). This is particularly true for migrant workers, domestic workers and refugees, isolated elderly persons and LGBTIQ+ persons. Assessments have found that displaced or homeless persons are reluctant to go to Lebanese Red Cross shelters outside of Beirut and have expressed the will to remain in their neighbourhoods or close by. Overall, the Shelter sector has highlighted the critical need for cash for rent at this stage of the response.SHELTER NEEDS OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES According to WHO, 15% of the global population has some form of disability. Persons with disabilities are one of the most vulnerable and socially excluded groups in any crisis-affected community. They may be hidden in homes, overlooked during needs assessments, and not consulted in the design of programs. The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities highlights the importance of the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all aspects of life including in situations of risks and emergencies. It is therefore timely to ensure that the post-disaster shelter assistance adequately reflects the specific needs of people with disabilities and older people. The shelter needs of persons with disabilities (PWD) in Lebanon are rarely taken into account. Building standards pay little attention to the accessibility of private homes and public infrastructure and subsequently so too does the construction industry. HI’s Shelter response aims to build back better by rehabilitating damaged shelters and making them accessible for this vulnerable group, may it be for persons who have been injured and are disabled as a result of the explosion (physical disability, vision loss, etc.), or for members of a vulnerable household with pre-existing disability. The Shelter teams will provide the technical guidance and bills of quantities for households to make specific adjustments to their shelters to facilitate mobility and independence for beneficiaries with physical disabilities, such as constructing access ramps to enable wheelchairs to move in and out of the house, or adjusting bathrooms for such purposes. The needs of persons with sensory and/or cognitive disabilities will also be addressed in shelter repairs and rehabilitations. The participation and consultation of disabled beneficiaries in the process will be crucial to understand and best meet their specific needs. In order to do so, an accessibility envelope will be allocated to the project. It is estimated an extra 10% of the budget is necessary to render a shelter accessible.

2 - Activities

HI’s activities will focus on provision of cash for rent for vulnerable households forced to relocate, living in unsafe and uninhabitable conditions or homeless due to structural damages or destruction as a result of the Beirut Port Explosion, as well as essential repairs of housing to ensure safety and protection to vulnerable households affected by the explosions. The project entails three main activities:- Activity 1: Cash for Rent, targeting 30 households -among the most vulnerable- for an estimated period of 3 months. The activity aligns with the Sector SO 1 / Response priority 1 (Cash for shelter targeting families who have or will be forced to relocate due to structural damages or destruction of their apartment/house);Activity 2 – Cash for shelter/level 1, targeting 75 houses. The modality is conditional cash for purchase of material and hiring of labour, with provision of technical guidance and follow-up throughout the reparations; payment in three tranches. The activity aligns with the Sector SO 1 / Response priority 2 (Minor repair targeting families living in apartments/houses lightly damaged (level 1);Activity 3 – Cash for shelter/level 2, targeting 75 houses. The modality is conditional cash for purchase of material and hiring of labour, with provision of technical guidance and follow-up throughout the rehabilitation, payment in three tranches). The activity aligns with the Sector SO 1 / Response Priority 1 (Rehabilitation of apartments/houses moderately damaged (level 2)).Out of the 150 houses targeted for repair and rehabilitation (namely activity 2 and 3), estimated 30 houses (20%) will be made inclusive for PWD (accessibility envelope).
