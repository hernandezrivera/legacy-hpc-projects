Project Summary

The project aims to address the complex challenges faced by Syrians due to prolonged crises and especially persons with disabilities (PWDs) and their families as they are among those most affected. It focuses on the depletion of financial capital, loss of human capital, erosion of social capital, and systemic inequalities. It provides concentrated resources and expertise that offer an integrated, multifaceted modular approach to revitalizing individuals' and communities. It is structured into three interlinked areas of work: Social Protection and Inclusive Socioeconomic Empowerment, Social Inclusion, and Social Cohesion.
The severe stress Syria has been under since 2011 due to several crises, including armed conflict, economic collapse, the COVID-19 pandemic, and an earthquake in 2023, have led to vast infrastructure damages, increased poverty, social tension, brain-drain due to youth migration, and an erosion of social capital. Data from 2022 showed an alarming 90 per cent rate of people expected to be living below the poverty line and 85% of households being unable to meet basic needs (HNO 2023). It is estimated that 16% of female-headed households are completely unable to meet basic needs , and more than 60% of persons with disabilities are out-of-work (Human Development Report, 2021).
To address these complex challenges, our approach integrates long-term developmental and peace-building approaches with efforts to fill protection gaps. Implementation includes cash assistance conditional to inducing positive health and education outcomes. Additionally, we aim to implement hybrid intervention consisting of cash for work and skill development schemes to yield positive impact. In parallel, the labour market component will consist of a skill development program. Activities also focus on gender and disability inclusion as blueprints of social inclusion and create learning experience to advocate for additional intersectionality and social structures, with possible expansion to include youth and elderly. The project also proposes the delivery of in-kind assistance with focus on the health and wellbeing aspects of human capital. This includes the provision of assistive products for persons with disabilities to alleviate financial pressure and to contribute to the health and autonomy of beneficiaries. In addition to the inclusion of mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS), to improve cognitive resilience which is essential for social cohesion. As for the PSEA Strategy, UNDP will prepare training and awareness gender materials according to UNDP guideline, then conduct training/orientation/ awareness sessions on promoting gender sensitivity behaviors with targeted communities and institutions of civil society.
The projects faces the following Risks, as follows are the Assumptions and mitigating measures:
1- Capacity of Local Partners: sensitization, awareness-raising and capacity development of our partners will be prioritized.
2- The comparative advantage of vulnerable groups in leading peacebuilding efforts: We will provide tailored training and resources to these groups and the creation of opportunities for representation.
3- Access to electricity and internet: After conducting a needs assessment, If access is found to be limited, we will explore partnerships to facilitate improved access, or adapt alternative solutions.
4- Contact approach efficiency: Ensuring that all facilitated interactions between groups
are under conditions that promote safety, equality and inclusion, common goals, intergroup cooperation, and support by social and institutional authorities.
5- Additional crisis and political and economic instability: The project design should be adaptive and flexible to adjust to changes in the country's situation.
6- Funding Inconsistencies: Enhancing trusted partnerships and keep donors and stakeholders regularly informed about the progress of the project and challenges faced.
