Project Summary

In alignment with 2020 WASH Sector strategy, International Medical Corps (IMC) proposes to build upon its emergency hygiene promotion programming and provide emergency WASH assistance and capacity-building support to conflict-affected populations in North East Syria and in Hama. The proposed program will target 205,027 beneficiaries (53,307 males, 79,961 females, 28,704 boys, 43,055 girls). Sixty percent of them are IDPs, 20% returnees and 20% are hosts, whereby IMC will continue its close work and coordination with Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) branches in Deir-Ez-Zor, Al Hasakeh and Hama to respond to the emerging needs of the newly displaced populations, displaced population living collective shelters and camps, and other vulnerable population. IMC and SARC teams identified the following locations and areas for 2020 intervention: In Al Hasakeh, the proposed program will target 88,727 individuals (23,069 males, 34,603 females, 12,423 boys, 18,632 girls) residing in 62 IDP shelters, Al Hole and Areeshe camp. In Deir-Ez-Zor, the teams will target 55,100 individuals (14,326 males, 21,489 females, 7,714 boys, 11,571 girls) residing in Kharita, Zghir Shamiyeh, Ashara Gharibeh and Mahkan host communities, while in Hama, teams will target 61,200 individuals (15,912 males, 23,868 females, 8,568 boys, 12,852 girls) residing in Taqsis, Maar Dis, Tiba Elemam, Tiba Elesm, Zwr Al Massaleq and Karnaz. These areas have been selected based on the priorities areas identified within the 2020 HRP by the WASH Sector as well as the findings of the rapid needs assessments conducted by IMC, in areas where IMC has secured access through SARC.
In order to help communities in maintaining a healthy and safe hygiene environment, the intervention will adopt a resilience-oriented approach and through training and capacity building activities, will directly engage target populations in efforts to promote best hygiene knowledge, attitudes and practices.
Recognizing the specific health and protection concerns of conflict-affected women, men and adolescents (boys and girls) the proposed program will be open to all population segments, ensuring their meaningful participation in the design, implementation and monitoring of the intervention. Through education and training of community volunteers (especially females) the program will contribute in empowering them to become not only beneficiaries but also service providers within their own conflict-affected communities.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2020 preliminary findings for the WASH sector, access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations and IDPs living in informal and formal settlements, who therefore continue to be highly dependent on humanitarian assistance. The comprehensive WASH assessments conducted between June and August 2019, indicate that in addition to water and sanitation issues, 23% of the garbage is not disposed appropriately and 15% of the population is served with a low frequency garbage collection, contributing to the increase in leishmaniosis cases in some areas of northern Syria where the disease is endemic. In terms of affordability and accessibility of WASH NFIs, the assessment indicates that although hygiene supplies are available in most of the local markets, they are too expensive for the majority of the population and even less accessible for vulnerable categories of displaced, returnees, female-headed households. Shampoo, washing powder and cleaning liquid were reported as being unaffordable by 20-25% of households. In October 2019, International Medical Corps had conducted rapid needs assessment in IDP collective shelters in Hasakeh city, where the findings revealed the needs to provide essential hygiene NFIs and address several WASH issues such as raising hygiene promotion awareness among specifically mothers and children towards maintaining better hygienic behaviors.. Moreover, International Medical Corps WASH team, who has access to areas of Deir-Ez-Zor and Hama governorates, have observed the needs and therefore propose to extend its WASH activities to these areas.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities will focus on improving the personal and environmental hygiene and reduce morbidity and mortality risks related to water borne diseases, and they are linked with 2020 HRP document and 2020 WASH Sector strategy. IMC will directly implement the activities, in collaboration with SARC branches, targeting priority geographic areas of affected communities, including those hosting newly displaced population. Activities will include delivering hygiene awareness sessions for adults and children (through specifically designed activities based on age and gender), distribution of hygiene NFIs and capacity building of SARC and community volunteers on hygiene promotion related topics. The activities will be closely coordinated with other actors operating in the same areas, whereby IMC will coordinate with WASH sub-sector and concerned sector partners (UN agencies, SARC, INGOs and NNGOs), to ensure complementary and coordinated interventions and avoid overlaps and duplication. IMC continuously monitors and analyzes the changing conflict and security dynamics in its areas of operation and implements monitoring and measuring specific components, applying the Do No Harm approach and take into consideration special needs of people with disabilities. . IMC recognizes that women, men, boys and girls are affected differently by the crisis. As such, a gender-age sensitive approach will be adopted during assessments, ensuring that women, men, boys and girls are given a voice in the decision-making process. Target communities will be actively engaged in the design, planning, implementation and evaluation of activities. IMC will put in place a beneficiary feedback mechanism, whereby beneficiaries can communicate and express their feedback, suggestions or complaints about IMC services, including about the possible negative consequences of its programming. IMC Syria MEAL department will collect, record, analyze and respond when relevant, to all beneficiary feedback.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

IMC is committed to mainstream protection considerations at all stages of the project cycle. At the assessment stage, men and women will be given a chance to express their different concerns, experiences, needs and capabilities through gender and age disaggregated FGDs including persons with disabilities. This will identify gender and age specific needs related to unequal access to WASH services and potential protection risks. IMC will select community volunteers from both sexes. All IMC staff are trained on protection mainstreaming principles, according to the specific positions and tasks. Likelihood: low, Impact: high •Mitigation: regular monitoring of supplies’ expiry date; and carry out timely disposal of damaged/expired items. • Residual risk level: low.Risk 3: SEA and safeguarding risks. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation: 1) IMC policies and procedures in place, including effective, safe and confidential reporting mechanisms. If any report/complaint are received at field level, they will be immediately shared with Ethics and Compliance Department at HQ, which will review the information received and conduct an appropriate investigation as needed.2) All staff provided with training and refresher training on PSEA and safeguarding issues; 3) Community feedback and response mechanism in place, to identify potential issues or higher risks. Residual risk: Medium

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1- Transportation of goods from Damascus to project sites is delayed and/or suspended • Likelihood: medium Impact: high • Mitigation: Constant and regular follow-up with concerned authorities and stakeholders to ensure timely dispatch of supplies based on distribution plans. When feasible, procure goods from markets in targeted locations. • Residual risk level: medium. Risk 2: Low/inadequate quality of distributed items: • Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. IMC has put in place comprehensive quality checks procedures at each stage of NFIs procurement and delivery. Risk 3: The awareness messaging delivered through community volunteers is of low quality and/or incorrect, which has a negative impact on the hygiene practices in the target community. • Likelihood: medium. Impact: high • Mitigation: The selection of community volunteers for the campaigns takes into consideration their capacity to understand and effectively present hygiene messages as well as their motivation to learn and to deliver these messages equally to all beneficiaries. The work of the volunteers is closely monitored throughout the campaign by IMC and SARC and several feedback mechanism are in place for target beneficiaries to rate/evaluate the volunteers’ work. • Residual risk level: low.
