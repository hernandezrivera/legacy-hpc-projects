Project Summary

It has been more than 15 months since the conflict broke in Tigray. The war has affected every form of life in Tigray. Detoreriating food and nutrition security situation; lack of access to basic services and materails including medicine, fuel, banking, communication, transportation; poor access to water, hygiene and sanitation services; protection concerns including protection and support for SGBV survivors and IDP children; high case of internal displacement are among the main areas that need humanitarain response.
The water sector is among the highly impacted sectors by the armed conflict over the past 15 months. The rapid assessment carried out the regional WASH Cluster (forum of actor responding to the WASH emergency situation including the Bureau of Water Resources, International and Local NGOs and relevant UN agencies) indicated that of the total 18,757 rural water supply schemes about 60% (11,254 water supply schemes) are currently non-functional. The main factors contributing to non-functionality of water supply schemes are:
• Lack of spare-parts in local market to undertake maintenance of water supply schemes: due to the conflict markets have become non-functional and the supply chain have been broken thus about 55% of non-functional rural water supply schemes currently are not providing the required service due to lack of spare-parts to maintain the schemes;
• Non-functionality of government support system to communities to ensure functionality of water points: with the government line office responsible for providing technical support to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Committees (WASHCOs) such as Tabia Water Technicians and Woreda Water Office functions are not providing the necessary technical support due to the conflict, insecurity and as public servants in rural areas are yet to receive their monthly salary since the conflict broke (for than three months);
• Rural water supply schemes damaged by the war (conflict): considerable number of rural water supply schemes (more than 5,000 water supply schemes) are reported to be damaged and became non-functional due to the conflict; and
• Lack of access to necessary chemicals to periodically treat the water points and maintain water quality: water supply schemes need to be treated periodically with chemical to maintain required water quality in rural areas. This treatment has been conducted by the health and water supply functions of the government at Woreda level. However, such treatments currently are not in place thus there is no mechanism to periodically treat the water in rural areas of Tigray due to breakage of supply chain of the required chemicals, insecurity and inaccessibility of considerable parts of Tigray region and lack of service provision by the assigned public sector functions (health and water related functions at Woreda level)
The proposed project does focus on restoring access to water for war affected communities in 24 rural woredas. The objective of this project is To restore access to potable water supply for 39,600 people (of which 51% are female) in 25 rural Woredas of war affected rural communities of Tigray.
The above stated objective is planned to be achieved by providing permant solution through rehabiltation and/or maintenance of water supply schemes, mainly:
• Complete Rehabilitation of 35 Hand dug wells
• Major Maintenance of 170 Hand dug wells
• Complete Rehabilitation of 15 Spring developments
• Re-organize and/or strengthen 220 WASHCOs to support better management and operation of maintained/repaired water supply schemes

1. - Projects activities

Planned activities: 4.1. Complete Rehabilitation of Hand Dug Wells: 35 HDWs in 17 WoredasHDWs are simple and relatively most inexpensive water scheme to build in areas where the ground water is shallow. It involved manually digging on average depth of 17 meters (including soft and hard digging). The well is circular shape with a diameter of 1.8 meters and lined with either stone masonry or precast concrete rings. Rehabilitation of HDW is the reconstruction of existing but non-functional hand dug wells because of long services (serving more than 10 years), as a result poor functioning of cylinder pedal, stand breakage stand bolt, knot breakage and Rode corrosion. Therefore, for these reasons the apron will be replaced with complete Afrideve-full set, which is reasonable lifespan for water points. Poor functioning of cylinder, pedal stand breakage, stand bolt and knot breakage, and Rode corrosion and low yield so that to increase their depth. Approximately 6 meters of aquifer penetration is mandatory in order to have a continuous water supply during droughts. Since rocks with varying degrees of hardness cover the majority of Tigray, explosives are used virtually in construction of wells to blast the rock and hence have better penetration of the aquifer. To implement this technology one technician with the set of materials/equipment (including Diesel generator, Dewatering pump, Bosh Hammer drill, Tripod, Ladder, 1.5m Pulley, Rope (16mm), Exploder and field vehicles support will complete FOUR HDWs Rehabilitation per annum. 4.2. Major Maintenance of Hand Dug Wells: 170 HDWs in 25 WoredasRehabilitation of HDW is the maintenance of existing but non-functional HDWs because as a result of poor functioning of cylinder pedal, stand breakage stand bolt, and knot breakage and Rode corrosion. Therefore, for these reasons the apron will be maintenance or partially replaced with Afrideve. Poor functioning of cylinder, pedal stand breakage, stand bolt and knot breakage, and Rode corrosion. To implement this technology one technician with a set of materials (including Diesel generator, Dewatering pump, Bosh Hammer drill, Tripod, Ladder, 1.5m Pulley, Rope (16mm), Exploder and field vehicles support will complete FOUR hand dug well scheme per annum. 4.3. Complete Rehabilitation of Spring Development (CRSPD): 15 SPDs in 13 WoredasSurface SPDs occur where groundwater emerges at the surface because an impervious ground layer prevents further seepage downwards. SPD water is usually fed from a sand or gravel water-bearing ground formation (aquifer) or a water flow through fractured rock formation or at the contact of two geological formation. The main parts of the SPD capping are a drain under the lowest natural water level, protective structure providing stability and a seal to protect surface water from leaking in. The structure is made of masonry. Overflow pipe guarantees that the water can flow freely out of the SPD at all time. The SPD development is possible only in the areas where there are perennial SPDs and the topography allows for SPD protection and gravity conveyance. Rehabilitation of SPD is the redevelopment of existing but non-functional SPDs because of long services (serving more than 10 years), as a result poor functioning of cylinder pedal, stand breakage stand bolt, and knot breakage and Rode corrosion.
