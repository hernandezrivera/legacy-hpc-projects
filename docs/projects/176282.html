Project Summary

In South Sudan 5.2 Million; women, men and children are in need of sufficient healthcare services, (SS HNO 2021). Service coverage are still low in some areas and women disproportionately bear the burden of morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition, communicable diseases/ including diseases of potential out breaks, sexual and reproductive health.
South Sudan has poor health indicators for instance maternal mortality ratio is 789 deaths per 100,000 live births as young girl in South Sudan is three times more likely to die in childbirth than to finish primary school. <5 Mortality is high at 75/1000 live births (HNO). Generally, most of these indicators are poor in states that have considerable hard to reach populations. The Complex emergency that combines both man-made and natural disaster in 2019- 2020 led to large scale humanitarian crisis. Severe flooding, inter communal violence and effect of COVID-19 Pandemic impacted the pre-existing vulnerability in Jonglei state, Measles outbreaks and poor coverage in health services after the departure of MSF is worsening humanitarian situation in Pibor. The nutrition situation, has deteriorated in the counties and the Famine Review Committees confirms the extremely critical levels of acute malnutrition from the exhaustive mass screening conducted; estimating Pibor and Verteth payams likely to pass the IPC Famine thresholds of 30% for GAM based on WHZ for children aged 6-59 months.
Due to high vulnerability of the population within Pibor County; JAM intends to contribute to the humanitarian Strategic Objective, “reduce morbidity and mortality, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, of the most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5. Anchored to the Health Cluster Objectives 1,2, and 3. In health Sector Objective 1: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities. JAM intend to support the Supplementary Immunization (SIA) excises, that includes Measles and other VPD (Vaccine Preventable Disease) campaigns to children between age 0-15 years (Polio) and 6 Months to 15 years with Measles. Vaccination of women of reproductive age (15-49 years) against tetanus; Training of recruited both qualified staff and vaccinators will be supported; movement of the staff, vaccines and other supplies in the identified site; during the exercise.
In Second Health Sector Objective2: To Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through Basic package of health and nutrition in a PHCU and essential emergency clinical package per the cluster. JAM targets to establish one (1) PHCU of the target 11 by the cluster and support 2 mobile services in Pibor County. Through engagement of qualified staff, basic packages of essential health services will be provided and this includes clinical Consultation services to 10,000 beneficiaries of the cluster target 73,046 in a total population of 222,297 Pibor County; It is also expected 21% (3892) of children ≤ 5 years (18553 – Male 1007; Female 8546) will access curative services of common/ epidemic diseases like Acute Respiratory Infection (ARI), Malaria and Acute Watery diarrhea. Provision of minimum reproductive health package to women that includes Pregnant and Lactating women (10%) of the target 15,000. JAM will recruit trained midwife who will provide both Antenatal, Postnatal care, and support delivery; the midwives will provide continuous training to maternal community work and access to RH supplies through UNFPA. Children will be supported to access routine vaccine from the project site this includes BCG, DPT1/Penta and Measles among other vaccine schedule by the 1 year of life. In Mental Health and Psychosocial support and SGBV, staff will be trained on case management to ensure eligible cases are referred accordingly.

1 - Needs

The prolonged conflict and limited Government investment have had a devastating impact on the country’s infrastructure and basic service delivery. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. Out of approximately 2,300 health facilities, more than 1,300 are non-functional. Of the functioning health facilities, 57 per cent are supported by humanitarian and development partners and many remain inareas that are not easily accessible by the communities, as they are further than 5 km from a functional health facility. (HNO 2021).The January Pibor County Service Deliveries Assessment and supporting preparedness/response plan to Implementing partners in Response to December 2020 IPC results provides a further insight on service gaps within Pibor County.Pibor County experienced multiple emergency due to floods and inter-communal violence for the last two years (2019/2020) with significant impact on healthcare, existing health infrastructures were damaged by floods and some were vandalized during inter-communal violence. Community situation analysis indicates that WASH and livelihood has deteriorated, the hygiene practice is poor and water in fracture were contaminated worsening the already crippled healthcare in Pibor. Main health partner in Pibor was MSF operating at Pibor PHCC, Gumuruk PHCU and Lekuangole PHCU. MSF was providing inpatient and outpatient treatment, antenatal care and immunization services at both Gumuruk and Lekuangole PHCUs and secondary healthcare at Pibor PHCC. However, MSF began to scale down activities in Pibor in 2019, pulled out of Gumuruk and Lekuangole in December 2019 and left Pibor PHCC in December 2020 with a big gap. Currently LiveWell is operating at Pibor PHCC and Verteth PHCU, UNH operates mobile clinics at Gumuruk and Lekuangole while Medair took over nutrition stabilization centre and operates emergency healthcare at very low scale.In Pibor, there is no upgraded PHCC, and referrals are a challenge due to lack of eligible ambulance services. Most of the cold chain fridges were destroyed during the time of conflict or there not existing. Poor Geographical topology and poor road network contributes to breakage of weekly vaccine supply, mainly when it is raining and the road is inaccessible; while vaccine dropout rate between DPT/Penta1 and DPT/Penta3 coverage is ≥ 18% (Routine Facility data). Apart from RDT stripes techniques, health facilities do not have a well-established laboratory services. Diseases like TB can only be diagnosed clinically to be transferred to Juba town. Referrals of such diseases and other complicated illness including HIV has been a big challenge since the current services lack of continuum of careMoreover, most of the deep lying villages that cannot access health service within a day and go back to their villages due to poor road network and lack of transport.Disease outbreaks increasing their vulnerability to malnutrition, while malaria cases are high. In the Year 2020, number of AWD Cases were 4975, Pibor registered 23 cases of Measles, 12641 cases on ARI and 28192 cases of malaria. Although the need assessment did not Pick the CFR (Outbreak Case fatality rate) and CMR (Crude Mortality Rate) related to measles in Pibor; the high number of unreported community death to the facility was noted.With the above mentioned challenges; there is need to re-establish proper health system including upgrading primary health care center with proper Obstetric and emergency services with clear referral system; laboratory investigation; mental health and psycho social support, HIV and TB services within the locality. Their need to provide essential clinical package through mobile in order to reach out the underserved communities. There is need to recruit qualified staff, renovate facilities and train the staff to offer comprehensive services.The lack of Medical commodities and supplies in Pibor is huge and therefor their need to have advance preposition of supplies.

2 - Project activities

JAM has Vast experience in Pibor County due to it’s nutrition and food security interventions that give a better understanding of the context. The current Health gap in Pibor County is huge in a midst of COVID 19 Pandemic. In this project JAM implementation will be guided by the 6 W.H.O pillars of health system and the country policy and guidelines. The proposed response will work in coordination with MOH, W.H.O, UNICEF, UNFPA and CHD for technical guidance, access to medical supplies and commodities including (IEHK), Surveillance reporting and coordination through the cluster. The plan is to contribute to real time disease surveillance through EWARS/IDSR and Maintenance of Health information systems and timely submission of validated data including the 5W. In line with Sector objective 1, 2 and 3 JAM will undertake the following: - In Screening/detection – The project intends to provide basic screening of common ailment including Screening of under 5 and PLW for malnutrition with MUAC; screening for malaria and other common diseases. In Essential Health care Package and Management of common illnesses/Treatment – The project will be integrated to the nutrition intervention to ensure management of acute malnutrition; JAM will provide curative services to 10,000 individuals with major on treatment of communicable diseases and non- communicable diseases: capacity building of the local available staff on (IMNCI); under 5 consultations through Integrated Management of Neonatal and Child illness (IMNCI); above 5 Consultation in the establish 1 static and 2 mobile sites. Provide - MCH (ante natal care, uncomplicated/normal delivery) to the target 5% of the target population to pregnant women; JAM will ensure staff are trained on basic EmONC, provision of focused Ante- Natal Care and emergency obstetric care to pregnant women, using skilled personnel and provision of postnatal care; provision of LLINs and Clean Delivery Kit (CDK) will be considered. This s aimed at improving maternal health and reducing <5 morbidity. There will be provision of routine EPI and Mass vaccination and ensure weekly transportation of vaccines with other supplies. Treat, minor wounds and establish the ORS corner. The project will ensure TB and HIV cases access treatment through referral of cases to the PHCC and support in routine follow-up. Access and utilization of IEC Materials, Clinical guidelines and treatment protocols from the core-pipeline or IEHK modules. The organization will access facility Monitoring and reporting tools, registers e.g (Outpatient, ANC, Vaccination registers) through the SMOH.Health Workforce - Recruit key technical qualified staff (Clinical Officer, Nursing Officer, Mid-wives, Laboratory assistance, dispenser) and many other staff and this will be guided by Essential Package for Health. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

JAM has strong safeguarding policies and procedures in place to maintain the safety of the staff as well as the beneficiaries. The sector leads will be superintending the protection of the target groups and ensure do no harm is correctly integrated. JAM has a gender policy that includes GBV andsafeguarding that is adopted within the country. Project staff and each sector lead will also be trained on protection, gender equality (mainstreaming), with a focus on the PSEA, child protection, and SGBV. The project applies a strong community based approach from the formation of the project to the monitoring and evaluation stage. The integration of protection and gender staff in theproject team will help the implementation of key activities to apply proper safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. In the health and nutrition component, community consultations with women, men, girls, and boys of all ages and abilities will take place to determine the service safety. Health services will include confidential entry points and the facility staff will be trained to fasterdetect indicators of violence and to be able to manage SGBV cases. All the involved staff like Nurses, community workers will be trained to provide beneficiaries information; health services, will help to manage SGBV cases and provide support to the survivor through utilization of Pibor CountyReferral Pathway. The participation (consultation) of community members especially women and girls, will ensure a gender-sensitive and culturally appropriate project approach. Sex, age, and disability disaggregated (SADDD) data will be collected by accessing facility registers such as morbidity registers, disability tracking sheet, program admission registers and referral slips. The raw data is then shared and uploaded on the health management information system—standard systemswithin the respective clusters. These standard tools support the collection of disaggregated data which allows the team to monitor gender equality. While in the PSEA the staff will share the information using the organization Policy to the field focal person who will use the stipulated referral pathway and referral cards/ slips and other modalities to provide information in a confidential way; itis expected that the shared information through the referral pathway and communication mechanism will trigger support to the survivor and handling the perpetrator through appropriate manner and ensuring of, do no harm.
