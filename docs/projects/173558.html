Project Summary

The overall goal of WHO’s activities in the area of Health Management Information Systems (HMIS) remains to support the government in data gathering and utilization for evidence-informed policy making across all pillars of the health system, especially in the area of health financing. In 2021, the WHO will organize workshops with SMoH representatives to identify information priority areas and conduct baseline studies to analyze the maturity level of the HMIS to measure them. The studies’ results will inform joint planning to strengthen state capacities.
WHO will advance the SMoH capacity for information gathering, reporting, and management through training for state Disease Surveillance and Notification Officers (DSNOs/ADSNOs) on DHIS 2 usage, data collection, reporting, and processing at all levels of the state health systems. To advance electronic data utilization, WHO will support the states with the necessary infrastructure. At the SMoH level, WHO will facilitate workshops on evidence-informed policy making, planning, and budgeting and leverage these workshops to support the development of the states’ AOPs for 2022.
In addition, WHO will support the monitoring of service platform functionality by conducting HeRAMS assessments across the BAY states. In Adamawa and Yobe, the HeRAMS assessments will be complemented by a health service quality assessment, adapted from the Service availability and readiness assessment (SARA) methodology. To support the Borno state government in the relocation process of IDPs to safe areas with adequate health service coverage, WHO will conduct a rapid assessment in the area of Human Resources for Health. In addition, WHO aims to finalize a health workforce registry and link it to HeRAMS. Furthermore, WHO will improve the suboptimal data collection on mortality and Adverse Event Following Immunization (AEFI) at all levels of the state health systems.
Risk communication
Risk communication and community engagement
Effective risk communication is the key to prevent risky behavior among populations. It is especially important to prevent outbreaks of infectious diseases from becoming epidemics, e.g. by promoting physical distancing or personal hygiene during the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2021, WHO will continue to strengthen the local capacities to conduct effective risk communication by training journalists across BAY states on health reporting during outbreaks, but also by engaging social media influencers as well as mobile and stationary service providers. In addition, WHO will provide proactive risk communication material tailored to specific beneficiaries like IDPs and other vulnerable groups through different platforms, including house-to-house campaigns, social media, and radio.
Outbreak Preparedness and Response
The BAY states continue to experience outbreaks of epidemic-prone diseases, including measles, meningitis, viral hemorrhagic fevers, and cholera. WHO will continue to support the BAY states in the prevention, detection, and response to these hazards, including through response vaccination campaigns for Polio and other infectious hazards. To prepare for outbreaks, WHO will support the SMoHs in developing response plans for epidemic-prone diseases and preposition medical supplies and commodities for case management and case-based surveillance
In 2021, the WHO will engage in strengthening laboratory capacities across all three BAY states to improve early detection and response capacities. To this end, WHO will provide training to public health laboratory staff in the diagnosis of epidemic-prone diseases like Cholera and Meningitis, but also on quality management as well as biosafety and security. Besides, laboratories will be provided with equipment and reagents for safe and sound sample storage and testing. Finally, training will be provided at LGA levels to ensure safe sample collection, packaging, and transportation.

1 - Needs

Health Resources and Service Availability Monitoring assessment report presents baseline data from 809 assessed health facilities across 27 LGAs in Borno State. Only 41% of the health facilities in those LGAs are fully functional, while up to 48% of the facilities are not functioning (including fully damaged ones) and 11% partially functioning. Additionally, 64% of the assessed facilities were either fully (31%) or partially (33%) damaged with only 34% of the facilities not damaged. Non-functionality of the health facilities resulted from damage to infrastructures (60%) and lack of access due to security constraints (32%). From the fully and partially functioning facilities, just over 25% had operational in-patient services, 20% had trauma services, and 25% had the necessary stocks of essential medicines and supplies. The increase in insecurity and hostilities has had serious implications for health service delivery as the conflict continuously hampers the movement of health workers and patients, the supply of drugs and other commodities. This leads to a shortage of skilled healthcare workers, particularly doctors, nurses and midwives to provide services. Increased new displacements has contributed to shrinking of humanitarian access in BAY States with resultant shifts in access and potential increases in humanitarian needs. Cost: Access to health services is also hampered by high medical costs as reported by 75% of IDPs, 61% of returnees and 80% of non-displaced households Gender based violence remains a key challenge in the BAY states, especially with female IDPs who are frequently exposed to sexual violence, forced prostitution and childhood marriages. Mental health remains a health challenge in BAY States, statistics indicate that around 20% of affected people in humanitarian emergencies develop mental health conditions. Critical gaps also remain in functional primary and secondary health services. Other common challenges in accessing health services are related to high direct and indirect costs associated with healthcare, including the price of quality medicines.In order to address the increased risk of outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases within the communities affected by low/no access to health services, WHO will support the state governments to enhance epidemic preparedness and capacity building to effectively respond to emergencies in the targeted areas through.The deployment of mobile technologies at wards and LGAs level to improve disease surveillance and reporting by the deployment of mobile phones. Based on the lessons learned in previous initiatives and leveraging the deployment of health service delivery platforms in the affected communities, WHO will increase capacity of the MHT and CORPs to quickly notify potential outbreaks and alerts through the procurement and distribution of mobile phone equipped with specific alert notification application. The clinician in the MHT who is usually a nurse or midwife will be responsible for reporting and carrying out surveillance diseases. While for the CORPs, which is one-man team, each member will be assigned with responsibility of reporting diseases among children under 5 years of age in their community. The identified staff will be trained on case definition, detection and timely reporting of diseases of priority through the use of mobile technology disease surveillance. This will involve the procurement of Android mobile phones with the capacity to run the EWARS and the electronic platform Argus to implement eIDSR as agreed with the Borno State Ministry of Health for the phased replacement of EWARS. eIDSR is an electronic replica of the already existing paper-based IDSR where health facility focal points collect morbidity and mortality data from their sites and send aggregated data weekly and monthly to the LGA level. The implementation of this electronic reporting is done on the basis of strict data protection protocols.

2 - Activities

Training of surveillance focal persons and health Facilities clinicians on surveillance reporting through the EWARS/ eIDSR platform. Conduct Sensitization of health care workers on identification and reporting of measles, cholera, meningitis, viral hemorrhagic fever and other priority diseases including COVID 19. Support cross-border surveillance activities among populations at inter-country borders through community sensitization and setting up ad-hoc survaillance system that will respond to international public health events. Conduct Health Resources Availability and Monitoring System (HeRAMS) survey and workshops in BAY states, which is an approach for monitoring health facilities, services and resources availability in that provides essential information to support decision making and coordination of health sector actors in emergencies. This will provide timely identification of needs and gaps in health systems through evidence-based decision to support advocacy and resource mobilization. Mapping of hotspots areas at high risk of transmission for cholera, measles, cerebro-spinal meningitis and viral haemorrhagic fever to the conduct of risk assessments targeting preventive interventions across the BAY states. Conduct trainings and run simulation exercises for government health care workers, health sector partners and LGA Disease Surveillance and Notification officers on timely verification of all outbreak alert and rumors. Engagement and training of active case search teams during outbreak response. Printing and dissemination of IDSR data tools and Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials (community and standard case definitions, FAQs, posters) on identification and reporting of measles, cholera, meningitis and viral haemorrhagic fever to enhance immediate disease reporting. Strengthening the capacities of the State and LGA Rapid Response Teams (RRT) through trainings to conduct rapid detection and respond to public health emergencies. Support the development of multi-hazard and contingency plans in BAY states. Procure rapid diagnostic kits (RDT) for epidemic prone diseases and preposition them in the peripheral laboratories at LGA levels and in tertiary and secondary health facilities to provide screening tests for diagnosis of cholera, meningitis and other selected priority diseases to support detection and control of disease outbreaks. Training of RRT and Risk Assessment for suspected outbreaks; Training of M&E officers on DHIS2/HMIS Reporting, Conduct Vaccine coverage surveys and quality of service assessment;
