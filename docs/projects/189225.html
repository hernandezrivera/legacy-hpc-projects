Project Summary

Panyijiar and Rubkona Counties are two of the Nine Counties in Unity State currently grouped in WASH Severity Classification 4. The two counties have been beset by conflict and instability since the 2013 civil strife in South Sudan; characterized by localized violence, inadequate infrastructure development, and the situation even compounded by incessant floods for the last three years (HNO 2022). This has aggravated the WASH needs in the aforementioned Counties thus recording poor WASH indices; low access to clean water per person per day, the average distance to the nearest clean water source exceeding the standard 500m, and access to safe sanitation facilities are inadequate. In Rubkona and panyijiar Counties like the rest of South Sudan, 18 percent of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water, of those, 12 percent of women and girls reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns (FSNMS, 2021). Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO), therefore, proposes to support most vulnerable communities in Rubkona and Panyijiar Counties; returnees, IDPs, and host with emergency WASH services; Distribution of WASH kits to unit “caretaker/SAM child”, WASH in nutrition facilities; with focus on water supply and hygiene) in Communities with high GAM and WASH vulnerability, Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies, conduct WASH specific activities for mitigating WASH GBV-During Waterpoint rehabilitation, Construction or rehabilitation of Sanitation facilities and distribution of WASH NFIs, Construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure, Build/rehabilitate water points with flood-resilient designs, Build/rehabilitate toilets with flood-resilient designs and carrying out safety audits before conducting WASH activities. HACO intends to reach 24,000 Individuals in Panyijiar (12,000) and Rubkona (12,000). The response targets 51% IDPs, 35% Vulnerable host,s and 13% Returnees in Rubkona County.

1 - Needs

There is still limited access to basic WASH services in South Sudan owing to a chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, conflicts, and repeated flooding with massive destruction of facilities (HNO 2022). In effect WASH Needs are widespread over the country and most severe in the greater Upper Nile with Panyijiar and Rubkona Counties recording low WASH indices and classified in WASH Severity Classification 4. The aforementioned counties particularly have unfavorable WASH, Health, Nutrition, and GBV indicators; in WASH, indicators such as persons having access to 3 liters of safe drinking water per day, households having access to a safe water source in a distance of 500m or less, Water points being safe from fecal contamination are not men, women, and girls do not feel safe at water points; 18 percent of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water, of those, 12 percent of women and girls reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns (FSNMS, 2021). 5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The purpose of the CVA is to empower WASH user committees to be able to sustainably use the WASH resources in their communities. Additionally Provision of cash based assistance is a form of market support in functioning markets. This allows for WASH supplies/NFIs that are available in the markets to be procured by beneficiaries from the local markets.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project beneficiaries in areas with functioning markets; can allow community members engage in petty business which can empower them to be able to contribute towards rehabilitation and or maintenance of WASH services in their own communities. This also allows for market support necessary to allow markets to function normally. The targeted beneficiaries will be those that are difficult to reach but have markets where WASH supllies/NFIs can be accessed at fair prices in the local markets.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All groups, including women and children, especially girls, will be consulted on the location of WASH infrastructure (latrines and water points). Safety and accessibility audits will be conducted for WASH activities particularly access to WASH infrastructure and provision of WASH NFIs, in collaboration with protection partners and communities. Responses from the AAP desks will be used to inform project implementation modalities. It is likely some complaints will be on the project implementation process, therefore project processes or implementation modalities will be modified in response to complaints.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HACO project team will ensure that Women, girls, the elderly, and people with injury/disabilities are directly given messages or information (through their representative groups, designated leader) related to the WASH activities conducted. All the affected groups in the Community will constitute the AAP committees and the AAP committees will comprise Women, Persons with disabilities, youth, and elderly persons, in this way individuals who are old, illiterate, women, and girls will feel comfortable complaining when they feel aggrieved.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Information about the HACO Code of conduct and PSEA policy will be shared (written and verbal), with households and community groups and contacts for whom to report to if they witness/are subjected to misconduct. HACO project implementation team will sign the PSEA policy and Code of Conduct as an appendage to their contracts. they will also be trained on the Organization PSEA policy. Communities will be explained in outreaches the zero tolerance for SEA in the Humanitarian community and be told that Humanitarian assistance is free and they will be given referral pathways and contacts should there be cases of SEA during project implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Humane Aid for Community Organization will Provide information to all groups about the WASH project, through multiple information dissemination strategies-verbal and written, the duration, scope, and limits of the project for purposes of expectation management.The proposed project will have Accountability to the affected populations' committees, which will man community complaints and feedback desks. project beneficiaries will record their complaints to the AAP committee members and they will get feedback to their complaints from the feedback desk.
