Project Summary

This project intend to provide Child protection appropriate sex and age support to more vulnerable boys and girls from displaced families and from host families as well in Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon. This will be done through creation of Child friendly spaces for recreation activities and provision of psychosocial support to affected children; cases management, provision of SGBV support to victims and sensitization for prevention of any cases of child and SGBV abuse. the project will give a special attention also to strengthening and setting up child protection base community committee for monitoring, sensitization for prevention and management of any kind of Child protection abuse a community level.

Needs

According to the MIRA conducted in June 2018 in the North-west region, in the affected areas, both displaced and host populations face numerous protection problems ranging from the lack of identity and marital status documents to the restriction of travel. In addition to this, there has been a rise in violence against women, of whom 18% have reported being abused in several forms (forced labor, physical abuse and torture). These practices are aimed more at displaced people. However, 16.2% of the female population in the host community report witnessing cases of killings of civilians by armed groups, arrests or detentions. The districts of Balikumba, Batibo and Babessi are the ones that record the greatest cases of violence and killings, especially among the displaced population, where there are more than 460 missing, more than 200 cases of violence, 849 wounded. Affected populations: They consist of more than 5,000 internally displaced persons and 102,783 people from the host community in the 13 localities. Humanitarian needs: Access to identity and civil status documents for refugees, displaced persons and host populations is also a priority for many populations. 25.3% of those surveyed expressed the need for protection against attacks on civilians and 30.6% said they needed psychological care for trauma. Although none of the households surveyed said a member of the armed groups had victimized them, they said they feared the surge of violence among the young population and their exposure to the blackmail of armed groups. Base on those finding identified the main needs to be address are the following: in the short term, it is necessary to: (i) sensitize the police and the populations on the rights, the respect and the human dignity in order to relax as much as possible, the modalities of displacements populations; (ii) train people on community mechanisms for the prevention of violence and the protection of children. In the medium term: a survey should be conducted for the identification of separated children, unaccompanied children and children with special needs; set up child-friendly spaces in communities; provide support to households by developing IGAs with the most vulnerable families.
