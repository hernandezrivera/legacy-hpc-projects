Project Summary

In Ethiopia increased food insecurity across the country along with below average crop production, and food price increases caused Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) admissions to stagnate at very high levels since February 2021. Between January to April, country-wide SAM admissions were 25 per cent higher compared to the same period last year.
As per the nutrition cluster update report, Majority acute malnutrition cases are concentrated in the displaced population both in camps as well as those in the host communities in the conflict affected areas of Amhara Afar and Tigray these regions account to more than 25% of the total burden. Nutrition screening assessments conducted in Tigray, Afar and Amhara showed that proxy-wasting prevalence consistently was above 20 percent for children 6 to 59 months, and over 40 percent for PLW. Acute malnutrition among women is of significant concern in Northern Ethiopia, cases of adolescent pregnancies are rampart this is further complicated by the breakdown of the health system. The disabled, elderly, orphans and child headed household have also been identified to be vulnerable to malnutrition, they are more likely to have a physical disability, face barriers to accessing assistance and services, IDPs face an increased vulnerability to increased risk of disease outbreaks, epidemic due to poor access to prevention and treatment of common morbidities and childhood illnesses, and elevated malnutrition rates where children PLWs and elderly are particularly at risk.
On-going armed conflict, intercommoned violence, climatic shocks, and disease outbreaks including COVID-19 continued to be the key drivers of humanitarian needs in Ethiopia during the first quarter of 2022. Conflicts have erupted, deteriorated, or escalated in various parts of the country, with severe impacts on civilians and particularly on vulnerable and marginalized groups. Fighting has taken place in various areas, including Metekel of Benishangul Gumuz Region, In addition, the security situation in bordering Amhara and Afar region of Tigray has deteriorated due to the spill over of the Tigray conflict. During the first quarter of the year in 2022, a total of more than 60,000 IDP has been displaced due to the drought happen in Tigray to Amhara region via Raya Kobo woreda. In Benishangul Gumuz, lack of access entailed a substandard response to the needs of affected communities. The pop'n continues to bear the brunt of violence in some parts of Ethiopia, with restricted access to essential services highly affecting their livelihoods. Although at different scale, access restrictions have impacted local communities in Amhara and Benishangul Gumuz and bordering the Benshangul Gumz woredas of Oromia regions, where hundreds of thousands of people have been displaced since 2018-2022.
The project will cover all kebeles of the targeted woredas of South West Ethiopia, Bneshangul Gumez and Amahra regional state with enhancing the local health system including OTP and SC sites to deliver quality CMAM and IYCF service provision to conflict-affected communities. This project primarily focuses on providing life-saving Acute Malnutrition treatment, care and support to affected and displaced under-five age girls and boys, and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) who are suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). The project will also strengthen further the local health system and build the capacity of health workers (HWs) and health extension workers (HEWs) to respond to already doubled primary health care and nutrition services in all targeted intervention woredas. Besides, the project strongly works with district health offices in maximizing Family MUAC, best practices of IYCF throughout the implementation period of this project. MCMDO will deploy Nutrition technical project staff in all of the targeted woredas to serve the need community

1. - Projects activities

The major activities of the project are; Restore and expand capacity for life-saving nutrition interventions through health facilities and outreach activities for children under five suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) either through the MHNT in communities with high influx of IDPs (including host communities) and accommodation centres.  Treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) of children under five, both from IDPs and host communities living in the affected districts;  Logistic support and transportation for nutrition supplies for therapeutic feeding including, RUTF and therapeutic milks (F75 and F100) and other supplements and nutrition equipment from Regional/ Zonal to the target district.  Support the supply chain logistics for nutrition;  Improve the quality of the24 hours inpatient treatment and care in stabilization centre through on the job training and mentoring with MCMDO technical staff Strengthen the HEW using the MCMDO technical staff on the management of acute malnutrition at community level Provide basic OTP and IYCF-E training for health extension worker in the targeted woredas Provide basic 7 days long IMAM training for district health professionals  Implement Active case finding through MUAC screening for detection, referral, and follow-up, and also monitoring of the nutrition situation;  Undertake nutrition assessments/ surveys for assessing the nutritional status of the populations at risk, and for Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) acute food insecurity and nutrition analysis; Promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices, and overall Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) best practices messages for caregivers of children under two either though the awareness sessions and cooking demonstration in the communities with high influx of IDPs (including host families) or through IYCF Corners that will be associated with the health fix posts at the accommodation centres. Support any campaign with the right procedure thorough Technical and logistic manner.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Response are designed considering the real needs at the community level. MCMDO conducts quick assessments to identify the scope and types of humanitarian responses needs. The assessment is a joint assessment which involves the local government, sector offices, the community representatives, affected communities and other key stakeholders. MCMDO assessment teams use standardized templates to capture the existing response needs. Key informant interviews, Focus Group discussions, document review and site visits are some of the basic tools used to capture the key information to design the response. MCMDO also conducts a discussion before the design of the response with the sector offices to discuss and agree on the strategies, activities and targeting. Using these inputs, MCMDO compiles the information and produces assessment reports which are used as a sole information to design the responses for resource mobilization. As resources are committed by donors, MCMDO submit the response to the regional or zonal stakeholders for appraisal. The stakeholders review and sign the project agreement as the project is designed jointly and reflects the primary needs of the affected communities. The response is aligned and harmonized with the existing government and other actors plans to avoid duplication of efforts and realize complementarity to meet the wider need of the communities. MCMDO advocates the stakeholders and the community to contribute resources including resources in cash, in kind, technical support or other forms of contributions. This helps the response to be sustainable and the beneficiaries and the stakeholders to ensure the ownership of the response outcomes. During the implementation of the projects, the stakeholders, community representatives and other actors engaged during the design stage will be part and parcel of the implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of the response. MCMDO invites the government counter parts to periodically review the performance and achievements of the response, provide feedbacks and capitalize the best practices. The beneficiaries and community representatives lead the implementation of the activities at community level. They are engaged in Beneficiaries selection & verification, delivery of response/support, modalities, efficiency, effectiveness and relevance of the response. Moreover, MCMDO will design and implement compliant and feedback mechanisms at grassroots levels which helps to ensure the accountability to the affected Populations (AAP). Such systems will be reviewed periodically and assess their effectiveness as well as relevance to the targeted communities. Thus, the targeted beneficiaries and the local stakeholders will make meaningful participations and lead the response to contribute for the realization of the planned objectives and achieve wider impacts on the lives of the beneficiaries.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Beneficiary accountability and feedback mechanisms is one of MCMDO quality standards reflected as a cross-cutting issue throughout its programmes. MCMDO has engaged the targeted beneficiaries and local actors in all stages of the project. In this response, there is a witnessed challenge to conduct an assessment and engage the affected communities due to the humanitarian challenges. The project will establish robust feedback mechanisms to get feedback, complaints and other information from the community. This will be confidential and appropriate channel. Since the communities served by the project have different levels of understanding and awareness, multiple feedback mechanisms will be established. Suggestion boxes, color codes, free hotline and email accounts will be established and beneficiaries will be informed about theses mechanisms to deliver their grievances/compliant, comments/feedbacks. Each challenge will have its own focal person or committee to review the feedbacks and provide appropriate response which includes correction measures, provision of additional information or continuous discussions as needed. Continuous discussions will be held to address the issues raised from the community in a way that addresses their concerns. On the other hand, local governments and community representatives will be part of the committees regarding accountability and compliant mechanisms. This helps to improve the quality of the project implementation.
