Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to the reduction of excess morbidity and mortality resulting from common
infectious diseases, vector-borne diseases, and epidemic-prone diseases because of multiple shocks (effects of Covid-19 restrictions, economic crisis and high food
prices, impact of flooding on livelihood, and sub-national violence) among the vulnerable populations in South Sudan. This aim will be obtained through the provision of time-critical, life-saving frontline quality health services, capacity building of health care workers, and strengthening of disease surveillance systems in targeted locations. The WHO emergency Mobile Medical Team (eMMT) provides integrated health services in deep field locations through mobile medical outreach modality. The eMMT comprise of experts from different fields of medicine often deployed strategically to provide gap-filling services in areas with serious unmet health needs. Such areas may include locations without static health facilities or those with no active health partners, locations with access constraints and those receiving high numbers of returnees, IDPs or refugees. The mobile clinics will offer outpatient consultations for common conditions, minor procedures, screening and treatment for malnutrition, immunization services, and referral services. Besides, WHO will train community health care workers to ensure continuity of health service provision in targeted locations. Rapid assessments will be conducted to identify the unmet health needs in project locations. meanwhile, the Rapid Response Teams (RRT) will be deployed alongside the eMMTs to conduct case verification and investigation of suspected disease outbreaks. In addition,
WHO will enhance capacity for disease surveillance and reporting to enhance detection of epidemic-prone diseases and continue to provide timely technical support for appropriate response by the Ministry of Health and other health partners.
The routine surveillance system will be enhanced through the deployment of mobile phone-based EWARS reporting by the static and mobile clinics attending to the needs of the community. This will enhance weekly reporting by facilities to ensure early detection of and response to disease outbreaks.
WHO will also conduct a risk assessment for common diseases such as measles and cholera to ascertain the need for preventive vaccination campaigns and guide the planning process for preventive vaccination campaigns as well as secure adequate vaccines as guided by the risk assessment findings.
Through its network of experts, both within and outside the country WHO will conduct targeted and focused capacity building of health workers working in the health facilities. The proposed capacity building will cover how to run mobile outreach programs, disease surveillance and outbreak response, case management, and infection prevention and control (IPC) within health facilities, and Clinical
Management of Rape. This project will also aim to procure and distribute emergency health kits to support health partners responding to emergency health needs across the country.

1 - Needs

Recurrent conflicts in South Sudan and lack of durable peace since December 2013 has led to social-economic marginalization, deprivation and lack of basic services. In 2020, the country relapsed into the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence in selected hotspots, a second consecutive year of major flooding and the impacts of COVID-19 pandemic leaving an estimated 8.5 million people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance (HNO 2021). An estimated 1.6 million people are still internally displaced and another 2.2 million are refugees and asylum seekers in the region. The country also hosts an estimated 302,137 refugees from neighbouring countries.The country is currently experiencing one of its worst Food insecurity situation. An estimated 5.8 million people would be in emergency Phase 4 and 5 Food security situation through December 2020 to March 2021, this number will be approximately 7.2 million between April to July 2021 (IPC 2021 report). Consequently, an estimated 1.4 million children aged between 6 months and 5 years are currently facing malnutrition, 313,319 of them are severely malnourished. An estimated 483,383 pregnant and lactating women are also acutely malnourished. As a result, Global Acute Malnutrition in South Sudan remains persistently high, the FSNMS 2019 report recorded GAM rates among children under-five at 16.2 percent above the minimum threshold of 15%. South Sudan health systems has been significantly affected by these years of recurrent shocks experienced by the country rendering it incapable of responding adequately to emerging and re-emerging public health threats including delivery of required services to its population. The country also remains among the worst globally on key health performance indicators. Generally, there is a weak health system, inadequate health and Nutrition service coverage and higher health vulnerabilities among population resulting in high morbidity and mortality. Currently <2 % of the country’s national budget goes to the health sector limiting service availability to its population and Low financial risk protection. There is low health worker density reported at 3.5 (per 10,000 population) as most Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed, 4/5 health facilities are managed by NGOs and only 44% of population lives within a 5 km radius of a health facility (HSSRP 2020/22).In 2018, the World Health Organization conducted Service Readiness Availability Survey, the report indicated that only 1% of health facilities in the country had the minimum required infrastructure, meanwhile 8% had all essential equipment needed to perform Outpatient Department consultations (OPD). It further reported that 68% of health facilities in the country were functional. As a result, coverage of essential services is low for instance, vaccination coverage is only 43 per cent for essential vaccines meant for children under 1 year, PENTA 3 (diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B and haemophilus influenza). The country has suffered from multiple public health threats: Ebola virus disease and responded to COVID -19 pandemic in 2020, Rift Valley Fever, Rubella, Malaria, Measles, yellow fever and Hepatitis E. Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity (64%) and mortality (45%) of OPD cases. The national HIV coverage is low at 22%, the national TB treatment coverage stands at 65% (16,323/25,000) however coverage; whereas Hepatitis and STI programs are almost non-existent. Consequently, there is high burden (36%) resulting into death mostly from communicable and non-communicable diseases including Neglected Tropical Diseases and vaccine preventable diseases. Maternal Mortality Ratio stands at 1150 per 100,000 live births and Infant and Under-5 Mortality Rates at 62 and 96 per 1000 live births respectively.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WHO has a zero tolerance policy regarding SEA. SEA allegations will be reported through established community-based complaint mechanisms that have been established in high-risk locations in the country and through hotline number numbers availed on PSEA posters. Any cases reported will be investigated following WHO internal procedures on addressing SEA, which ensures a victim-centered approach, and confidentiality is maintained. Disciplinary action will be taken against staff who are found to have engaged in SEA. All WHO staff are well trained on WHO SEA policies, reporting mechanisms and whistle blowing mechanism to ensure protection of staff and communities we serve.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from the most vulnerable members of the community will be used to support mainstreaming of vulnerable and at risk groups needs into regular programming and advocate for inclusive programming that takes care of the needs. It will also be used to increase representation and participation of at risk and vulnerable groups through community meetings, direct participation of at risk groups in the day today implementation and monitoring of project activities Where protection risk has been identified in the feedback from affected people, WHO will use the information to engage the protection cluster to increase protection assistance and safeguards for beneficiaries of health services. This can be in terms of walking distance to the health centre, or queuing time at the facility during service delivery to affected people. Finally, feedback from vulnerable members of the communities will be used to strengthen and make operational the service delivery referral mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

WHO has a strong framework for humanitarian accountability given its Cluster leadership on Emergency Health during crisis and its role as the Health sector lead in development setting. As such the project will adopt context specific approaches to implement accountability to affected people, and provide beneficiaries information about the project while ensuring the functionality of complains and feedback mechanism with the view of making sure affected people at all levels are consulted, informed, are participating and given timely feedback. Gender and age structured Focused Group Discussions (FGD) will be used to ensure that children, adults as well as male and female gender are able to inform the choices the project will take Project inception meetings will be used to provide information to health stakeholders, community leaders and selected beneficiaries. Important information such as the project purpose, target group and criteria, project benefits, donors and how people will participate will be provided during the inception meetings. Meanwhile community meetings will also be used to disseminate project information as well as receive and respond to community feedback/complains.Feedbacks and complaints will be reported through established community-based complaint mechanisms that have been established in high-risk locations in the country and through hotline number numbers availed. Community members will be involved in the implementation of complaints mechanism. Community leaders and health facility management committees will be engaged during monitoring and supervision visits to provide feedback on the provision of healthcare at health facilities in the targeted locations. WHO stakeholder feedback tool will be filled and stamped by a representative of the beneficiaries or community leaders where WHO mobile medical teams are deployed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

WHO will analyze beneficiary feedback with the view of understanding best practices and things that are not working. Consequently, WHO will reintegrate recommended best practices to increase support for affected people. Where feedback exposes gaps in health programing, WHO will design and implement remedial actions in line with needs expressed by the beneficiaries including provision of clarifications in the case of limited information. Sample remedial actions could be increased participation of affected people, increased engagement of community leaders to create a two-way communication with local partners, community and opinion leaders and beneficiaries. In some cases, beneficiaries’ feedback will be used to strengthen referral mechanisms to ensure affected people access services that are not provided through the project. For instance, networking with the protection cluster on issues related to PSEA.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The inter-agency health kits that are being procured will include post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) modules. PEP is one of the primary component of clinical management of rape cases. WHO mobile medical team will train health care workers in Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). WHO will work with the GBV Sub-Cluster in strengthening the referral of GBV to safely access treatment in supported health facilities.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5
12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This project will ensure health services are taken closer to the affected communities through mobile clinics. Locations of deployment will be selected with gender and protection consideration ensure ease of access by key risk population particularly women and children, elderly, and people with disability, and at-risk groups. For instance, distance and average walking time will be considered in the selection of locations. As part of mobile medical outreaches, the project will aim to provide nutritional screening of children under 5 years old with the view f identifying malnourished children as early as possible. The identified malnourished children will be linked with the existing nutrition intervention in targeted areas. Those with medical complications will be referred to stabilization centers for further management .
