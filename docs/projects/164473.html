Project Summary

A socio-economic development project that adopts the principle of "microfinance" established by the Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation, provides lending services for men and women on favorable terms, establishes a work culture and the principle of self-reliance and provides self-employment opportunities for Syrians.
The program aims to provide an opportunity for those who have the skill, ability, and desire to work freely to obtain the necessary money to advance their own activities and develop these activities, which helps to create new job opportunities and increase the income of beneficiaries.
The project will provide financial inclusion and lending services to 475 new clients. The loan amount reaches $ 600, which is a continuation of the Kareem Microfinance fund Project, which has so far supported nearly 150 loans.
* The project targets the poorest men and women, who do not have any financial assets that allow them to obtain bank loans due to the lack of financial security (collateral) such as land and the transfer of salary or a guarantor.
*The project will provide financial inclusion and lending services to a number of customers in the first phase. On monthly repayments, loans will be extended to other customers. It is expected that the increase in the number of customers a year after the first loan is disbursed, will reach 30% new customers which starts a new loan cycle, applying the same system of interest-free, productive loan, and recovered loans.
*Conditions of Solidarity Lending Group:
-Composed of 3 to 5 borrowers who chose to work together and bear full responsibility of that choice
-Separate project for every member of the group
-One loan for every project and one loan for every family
-Commitment to the number of relatives as per relative policy
-Group members must live in the same district (10 minutes away)
*In each lending group, a member of the group is elected to be responsible for collecting the monthly payments and delivering them to the foundation, and these amounts are then recycled into new loans for new beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

As HNO 9/12/2019 v6, the crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrian people, thereby affecting their ability to sustain adequate standards of living, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access essential infrastructure and services. These factors have deepened poverty and weakened communities’ resilience to cope with internal and external shocks. The World Bank reclassified Syria as a Low-Income Country (LIC) in 2018 implying that the Gross National Income per capita has fallen below US$ 996. Against this backdrop, there are 9.9 million people inside Syria requiring Early Recovery and Livelihoods support given the cumulative impact of the crisis on the population and broader macro-economic developments. Approximately 60 percent of males and females who are part of the workforce are without sustained access to employment due to lack of livelihood and income-earning opportunities as well as vocational skills. Such high rates of lack of sustained access to employment is a result of years of crisis with women, female-headed households, people with disability and elderly being particularly vulnerable. According to the needs assessment issued by Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation on 25/11/2019: 79.2% of the young are responsible for supporting their families, while 20.8% are not. 57% of assessed communities, the proximate percentage of joblessness is between 25_50% (some) among the young, more than 50% in 29% of assessed communities and less than 25% in 14% of assessed communities.• 85% of KIIs said that due to the lack of sufficient capital.• 10% of KIIs said that due to the lack of experience and skills.• 5% of KIIs said that due to the insecurity.
