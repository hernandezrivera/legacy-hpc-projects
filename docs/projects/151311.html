Project Summary

Hiraan region is located along the Shabelle River due to this; the region is susceptible to recurrent floods, but it also suffers from drought and clan conflicts. Although regional and district administrative structures have been established, they are relatively ineffective primarily due to lack of resources and constant internal strife. This further aggravate their health & nutrition and hygiene situation. With this intervention Cesvi aims to address these outcomes with a multi-sectoral approach.
Firstly in regards to the health sector, the project will provide outreach activities (mobile clinics and voucher referral system) to reach rural and displaced population and conduct nutrition education and hygiene and health promotion. Cesvi will also provide ANC and PNC to PLW, conduct routine immunization and provide capacity building of front-line health.
In regards to nutrition, Cesvi plans to admit, screen, and treat children U5 with moderate acute malnutrition and severe acute malnutrition. Pregnant and lactating women will also be supported by providing them with vitamin A, deworming, multiple micronutrients and IYCF counselling.
Finally the project intends to respond to the WASH concern through training of community WASH committees; construction of gender sensitive latrines and/or latrine desludging; water trucking via voucher/cash grant; HKs distribution (inclusive of emergency HH water treatments) for flash flooding. The project will also rehabilitate strategic water points as well as provide fuel and spare parts for shallow wells/boreholes.
Project location is Beledweyne and the activities target riverine rural village, thus agropastural beneficiaries. Project target are both IDPs and Host Community with a focus on female and children in particular in the nutrition sector.
Total beneficiaries of the action will be 22,860 (Female 11,269; male:11,591; children U5 4,572{boys 2,240, girls 2,332}.

Needs

Despite changes in the early part of 2018 following GU rains, there is still a huge humanitarian need in Somalia among IDP population. This is due to the impact of dry season, displacement, conflict and marginalization; moreover the expected above average rainfall will rains in Ethiopian highlands contributing to the increase in riverbank in Somalia thus increasing the risk of flooding along the rivers causing displacement, outbreak of diseases and destruction of properties. 5.4 million Individuals still need humanitarian assistance, including 2.8 million children. Malnutrition rate in Somalia is the worst in the world with GAM rate of as high as 21.9% which critical in the IDP camps and 13.2% in the riverine areas. Around 1.2 million children under-5 years old are anticipated to be acutely malnourished in 2018 and 232,000 anticipated to developed severe acute malnutrition. The widespread inadequate or absence of access to health care services, safe water, and good sanitation facilities increases the danger of infection. Additionally, more than 5.7 million individuals require essential health care services, including maternal and child health. One in eight children die before the age of five due to disease outbreak such as AWD/cholera and measles which continue posing a threat to children with 6,979 suspected measles cases (72% under-5) and 5,704 cases of AWD/cholera with 39 deaths reported since start of 2018.More than 4.4 million individuals require WASH intervention, with 3.5 out of 5 individuals without adequate safe water to address their basic need (drinking, washing, and cooking).Moreover, exclusion and discrimination of minorities and other marginalized groups continue requiring humanitarian support with more than 76% of GBV survivors from the IDP camps in the country.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Recognizing that without access to basic services and livelihood opportunities, durable solutions cannot be achieved, as well as that displacement is not simply a humanitarian issue but a development and state-building challenge, Cesvi will implement a strategy that involve all key stakeholders in a multi/integrated programme. The action will address not only the urgent life-savings needs of the affected communities but, at the same time, in the long-term vision, it will address both the needs and vulnerabilities of protracted and drought related IDPs and host communities through a building resilience approach. This will be possible thanks to the Cesvi longstanding experience in building resilience in the region with BriCs Consortium with whom Cesvi is implementing a long-term multi-year resilience programmes in Mudug Region providing support and expertise to the community in term of early warning system, training of CDMCs and local authorities in DRR, climate change adaptation and resource management, strengthened community-based drought mitigation and preparedness measures, training on Natural Resource Management (NRM) and watershed/pastureland management and business skills training. The proposed project is linked with the government and community system and it will enable beneficiaries to be legally entitled to access to the projects’ services. Thanks to this integrated strategy and the sufficiently stable environment, displaced people and affected communities will have the opportunity to opt for the local integration solution in the areas where they took refuge while increasing their coping capacities to face recurrent droughts and crisis.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

CESVI has transparent and accountable beneficiaries selection criteria jointly validated by the different stakeholders including beneficiaries themselves. Selection of target locations are done in consultations with the stakeholdersand principles of equality is strictly observed in all interventions.As a first step, the action will update the existing map of risks/exclusion in order to identifying appropriate target and mitigation steps and acontinuous monitoring in the area will be necessary to adapt activities to new risks/exclusion Cesvi has consolidated acceptance in the area of intervention gained through years of support to the communities implementing inclusion of vulnerable groups in all sectors, as a cross-cutting issue. H&N: teams of CHWs and CNWs will be active to reach rural and displaced groups which are the most likely to lack access to quality H&Nservices. WASH: hygiene promoters will be active to engage the community’s members in all wash related activities ensuring inclusion of the most vulnerable groups.FSL: in consultation with community’s leaders and local authorities’, the beneficiary’s selection criteria will be outlined in order to ensure inclusion of most vulnerable categories (especially women). The agency strengthen the protection of disabled persons making infrastructure adaptation in the facilities and providing training on disabilities to the staff. The beneficiaries privacy will ensure the respect of cultural and religious practice, as well as the participation. Teams of community’s mobilizers will actively engage the community’s members ensuring involvement of the most vulnerable groups. They will build direct contacts with each marginalized group ensuring effectively representation and avoiding discrimination.Information related to the services provided will be shared through various means of communication to ensure the community is reached by health community workers consultation, door-to-door, poster and radio will be used.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

A gender sensitive, participatory approach will be adopted at all stages of the project to ensure that adequate and efficient services are provided by equally involving women, girls, boys and men of all ages in the project design. Adequate access to appropriate wash and health facilities is fundamental in the protection of people, particularly for female. The staff will work in coordination with protection actors putting in place measures to prevent and respond to physical and sexual violence arising from use of or lack of wash and health facilities (gender sensitive latrines). During the implementation of the activities all categories and layers of the population will be involved through the Consultative Community Forum. Consultation with women and also marginalized groups will be organized to collect information about specific, Women participation to the Water Management Committees will be promoted to guarantee decision-making and responsibility for water are equally shared by women and men. Moreover, H&N activities will be implemented through a protection sensitive approach that will foster the dignity of the affected population. This will include a proportionate number of female health staff, experienced staff working with children and training on human rights abuse, GBV response and referral path. Also, consultations and documentation will be kept confidential and private: separate examination rooms will be provided and it will be established an information sharing protocol for abuse survivors avoiding to expose the victim to further trauma. Confidentiality will be a priority and lists of beneficiaries and personal data will be kept safe trough effective data protection measures implementation. Moreover, heath facilities will be provided with PEP kits for GBV survivors and CMR clinical management will be implemented. In order to ensure the accountability towards the affected population, mechanisms for anonymous feedback submission will be put in place.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

One of the major risk of the Somali environment with a potential high impact is the worsening of the security situation due to clan conflicts, terrorism and political controversies that can limit the movements, resulting in a restriction of access to facilities, increased demand for health care services, population displacement, increased morbidity and mortality with a possibility of halting the intervention. As a mitigation measures Cesvi has operational safety procedures that include clear risk assessment procedures, evacuation plans and suspension for the staff and it collaborates with local authorities and with community to guarantee acceptance, access and security of properties and staff. Regular contacts with the authorities at the regional and national levels will be maintain to ensure that valuable supporters and local authorities remains engaged and supportive of the programme. Restriction access to the facilities and the increase in the health care demand will be mitigate thanks to the mobile clinic and in coordination with others humanitarian actors to guarantee adequate response in case of conflicts.One of the consequences that can arise from armed conflict and violence explosion is the lack of support from communities, national and local authorities, traditional leaders with significant jealousy particularly between host community and IDPs. As a mitigation measures Cesvi can counts on strong existing relationships as it has a consolidated presence in the areas characterized by the spirit of cooperation and trust between staff and local authorities and the community. This will contribute to ensuring a smooth development of the project promoting Cesvi's perception as a capable and reliable agency. CESVI will also strengthen community committees and rehabilitate community infrastructures to mitigate such conflicts. Regular meetings with the communities will be held to explain the program and to share the criteria used for the selection of beneficiaries.
