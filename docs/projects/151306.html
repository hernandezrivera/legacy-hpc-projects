Project Summary

The project intends to provide access to lifesaving and sustaining food security interventions to 24,000 crisis affected population in Banadir, Middle and Lower Shabelle Regions. The project will provide Cash Based interventions, rehabilitation of farming infrastructure through canal rehabilitation, provision farm equipment and tools, supply of certified seeds, fuel subsidies, training of farmers on contemporary farming techniques, provision of fertilizers and provision of agricultural extension services. It is envisioned that the interventions will go along way in improving food security in the target regions and enhance community resilience to shocks in future.

Needs

According the recent Food Security analysis by FSNAU in September 2018 an improvement was recorded with regard to Karan rains. However, acute food insecurity, malnutrition and livelihood needs remain with an estimated 4.6 million people in urgent humanitarian need (1.5 million crisis and emergency, IPC Phase 3&4 and 3.5 million in stress, IPC Phase 2), including an estimated 2.5 million children. In central Regions, pasture and water remain scarce this is coupled with increased demand for pasture and water that cannot match the marginal improvement in rainfall and subsequent productivity. Two in five Somalis remain acutely food insecure, more than 2.6 million people are internally displaced and the seasonal assessment results indicates severe levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition among IDPs. The residual impact from the 2016/17 drought, combined with the recent floods, cyclone, displacement, evictions etc., continue to undermine opportunities for food security improvements especially for the most vulnerable Somalis. The forecasted of El Nino phenomena and associated abnormal rainfall in last quarter of 2018 is expected contribute to an increase in production while at the same time risks of shocks, including loss of production to river flooding and flashfloods are real. Indeed, the expected above normal rains may cause the deterioration of the situation of IDPs who are already in a precarious situation. Emergency and lifesaving interventions proposed by AV targets populations in emergency and crisis situations inBanadir, Middle and Lower Shabelle Regions which will be supported with farm tools distributions, fertilizers, certified seeds, Agricultural extension services, canal rehabilitation/construction, ploughing of farms, fuel subsidies to put more land under irrigation, training of farmers on contemporary farming methods and provision of unconditional cash Transfers to food insecure IDPs households who will account for 55% of the targeted.

Activities

Output 1: Immediate Improved access to food through predictable cash transfers for 2000HHin Bal’ad, Afgoye and Mogadishu • Activity 1.1: Provide Unconditional cash through Food vouchers valued at 70 USD to 2000 HH for 3 months per selected households of IDPs and host community, with consideration to women headed households, HHs with malnourished children (boys and girls) and elderly persons with no care. Output 2: Improved food production and community resilience to shocks through enhanced farmer’s capacity• Activity 2.1: Training of 200 farmers on contemporary farming methods • Activity 2.2: Distribution of emergency agricultural inputs packages to 200 farmers. The package will be comprised of: provision of tractor hours, and assorted certified seeds to 200 (40% women) in Balcad, Afgooye and Jowhar districts of Lower and Middle Shabelle Regions.• Activity 2.3: Distribution of assorted fertilizers to 200 emergency affected farmers • Activity 2.4: Provision of agricultural extension services to farmer to give them guidance on best farming practices and enhance quality of their produce• Activity: 2.5: Rehabilitation/excavation of farm canals

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Project will operate in an areas that are under the control and govevance of the federal government of Somalia and HirShabele State Administration. The project will also liaise with communities and local authorities and will foster acceptance and realization of long-lasting solutions to the target groups food security problems that include capacity enhancement of farmers on contemporary farming methods and providing them with best practices and quality extension services and cash provisions to IDPs. This will prevent them from falling back to further vulnerabilities. The project envisages skills transfer to farmers through provision of training on farming techniques and extensions services and this would prevent farmers from losing their harvests and enhance their yields. This will gradually prepare for smooth exit although Aid Vision will provide technical backstopping to enhance sustainability.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The major risk associated with the project is exclusion of women headed households, marginalized and minority groups during targeting. To mitigate this, the project will give special attention women headed households, marginalized and minority groups, people with disabilities, elderly and households with large family size. The project will prioritize needs and needs only and adopt community-based targeting methodology.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will target both genders for training , community management structures and also final beneficiaries. Project will provide timelines for training and community engagements that are conducive for both gender to benefit.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project will provide alternative livelihoods for youth who would have joined retrogressive groups like Al-shabab. The project will rehabilitate agricultural infrastructures thus contribute to reduction in resource based disputes among communities. The project will cushion target beneficiaries communities from exposure to gender-based violence given reduction of their vulnerability.
