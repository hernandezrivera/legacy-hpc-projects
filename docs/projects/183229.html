Project Summary

Since the start of demonstrations in October 2019, the Lebanese Pound has lost more than 90% its value, leading to a year-on-year inflation of 120% between May 2020 and May 2021 (Lebanon Emergency Response Plan 2021 – 2022, August 2021). Food prices increased at a staggering rate of 400% between January and December 2020 (WFP, 2021). While past crises were primary affecting refugees, asylum seekers and migrants, the weakening of the already fragile Lebanese infrastructure has drastically impacted Lebanese as well, resulting in more than 2 million people living below the poverty line and in need of assistance (Lebanon Emergency Response Plan 2021 – 2022, August 2021). This context together with the lack of reliable electricity, medical supplies, and medication and shortage in specialized medical personnel has consistently increased the vulnerability of the local population and hugely increased the burden on an already over-stretched public services system.
HI worked for a long time using a multidisciplinary approach and on the combination/ connection between MH and rehabilitation support. Throughout this project, HI will promote the integrated approach (physical rehabilitation and mental health) at PHCs level and will be transferring it through regular monitoring visits, technical follow-up meetings… considering that this approach has been proven essential in the recovery of users in different contexts, mainly in emergencies.
In this regard, 11 PHCs have been selected for support. They will be distributed as follows: 4 in Beirut (PHC Center at Karantina Governmental University Hospital, Al Zarif Medical Center for PHC, Nweiry PHCC and Al Msaytbeh Center for PHC), 4 in Tripoli (Al Iman Medical Center for PH, Al Zahraa Medical Center for PHC, Ibn Sina Medical and Social Center for PCH, and Al Ikhlas Medical Center for PHC), and 3 in Baalbeck (Al Chahed Faraj Balouk Center for PHC and Amel Medical Center for PCH, third To Be Identified with the DoPHC). The project will support in extending the working hours of the rehabilitation and mental health departments at the PHCs level while also covering support costs to maintain the centers operational.
Vulnerable areas are prioritized based on a combination of factors including pre-existing PHCs providing mental health and rehabilitation services as part of, or parallel to, their primary health care services, pre-existing socio-economic vulnerabilities, distribution of migrants across selected areas – in Lebanon 60% of migrants are located in Beirut, while 40% are distributed in different areas in Lebanon, where 3,719 migrants are located in Baalbak – Hermel and 3,300 in Tripoli area (Migrant Presence Monitoring (MPM) Baseline Assessment – Round 1 August 2021 Lebanon) – and areas under-served by other humanitarian actors.
In close collaboration with the Ministry of Public Health (MoPH), the project activities will be implemented. The project will also ensure cooperation with other active health service providers in the targeted areas. The intervention targets individuals living in highly vulnerable conditions with limited access to health services (including rehabilitation and mental health).
The project will tackle the main constrains to access health services, mental health and rehabilitation, which mainly are lack of people’s knowledge of health services and limited availability of free of charge services due to caseloads or due to limited running costs in public health services.. Accordingly, supporting the most vulnerable to access health services is urgently needed and paramount to save lives.
In addition, outreach in the same locations with be guaranteed, in order to support the population with essential integrated and inclusive health messages. The outreach team will as well distribute dignity kits in collaboration with UNFPA, with whom a channel of communication is been recently establish to avoid duplication.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The project, in line with Objectives 2 & 4, aims to ensure that the most vulnerable people affected by the economic crises have access to inclusive, safe and quality health services to meet their critical needs. It envisions 3 expected results: 1) PHCs are supported to handle increased caseloads for Rehab and MH. Activities: a) covering the running costs and replacement of nonfunctioning equipment and ADs to 9 PHCs; b) incentivizing 18 health personnel to increase working hours; c) provision of PPE to the MHPSS and rehabilitation departments at the selected PHCs. HI will benefit from experiences in other countries and will start supporting one PHC in each area for 3 months and then scale up the number of PHCs to 9, benefiting from lessons learned collected from the first 3 months of implementation. HI will ensure the existence of dedicated budgeted technical team which will conduct regular monitoring visits to PHCs to ensure the adherence to international standards and frameworks of rehabilitation, in addition to technical meetings to discuss the cases. HI team will ensure that monitoring tools are existing and used by PHCs team. 2) Vulnerable populations have access to dignity kits, and inclusive, safe and quality health services through multi-disciplinary outreach. Activities: a) Deployment of multidisciplinary outreach teams; b) community-based accessible awareness sessions (topics: C-19, routine immunization, etc.); c) provision of dignity kits for women and girls.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Given the imminent protection risks, links will be made with HI’s protection intervention to ensure individuals’ needs are addressed through a strong cross-sectorial referral mechanism; this ensures populations most at-risk of being excluded are identified and included in the response. Concerning GBV, the outreach team will be supported in understanding the concept, effectively refer when needed to specialized services. Institutional policies are in place to ensure PSEA and protection mainstreaming. Concrete actions are distribution of leaflets on HI’s complaint mechanism; dissemination of specific key messages on PSEA. As for conflict sensitivity, the outreach team will be accompanied to understand the concept and apply integrate ways of working to support social stability in their work. C-19 SOPs are in place for field staff and HI’ premises; staff care is within the framework of HI contracts. Teams have access to free support to ensure their well-being and address concerns.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

HI project ensures beneficiaries and stakeholders will have the right to access information about the project activities via different channels such as posters, leaflets and during outreach home visits. During outreach, the vulnerable individuals participate in a needs assessment to express their personal needs thus allowing HI teams to personalize the response accordingly. Stakeholders can raise their voices and concerns through Feedback and Complaint Mechanism reception modes (complaint phoneline, email, boxes, HI staff etc.). HI will ensure that staff and professionals are trained on this mechanism and beneficiaries are briefed. In the project inception phase, a detailed MEAL plan will be defined which includes indicator matrix, calculation methods, progress tracking against targets and MEAL activities calendar. Systematic monitoring will occur as well to oversee activities implementation. MEAL tools will be designed by MEAL unit and project teams, with consultation of beneficieries
