Project Summary

This project in partnership with CARE aims to address the emergency and urgent needs of vulnerable populations in the northwest of Syria, through coordinated, multi-sector rapid response and support in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. Through the hospital and the primary healthcare centre in Azaz city in Aleppo governorate; Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and protection/GBV services are to be provided to community members in selected locations, as well as referral services through the ambulance system that is functioning in selected locations in Aleppo and Idleb governorate. Also, this project aims at disseminating Covid-19 awareness messages through trained Community Outreach Workers’ (COWs) teams.
To strengthen its response to the complex and multisectoral needs of survivors, Violet will integrate GBV response into the health facilities under this project. The GBV intervention in this project aims to "Survivors of GBV and individuals at risk of GBV have increased access to safe and confidential GBV services through AMAL Hospital and PHC" in Azaz city.
The GBV staff in AMAL Hospital and PHC in Azaz will target women, girls in particular, including women and girls who live with disabilities, and also the elderly women.
Violet will ensure to conduct a risk assessment and analysis to understand the safety and accessibility of the services and develop clear and targeted recommendations and action points while establishing GBV services. Also Violet will strengthen privacy and confidentiality for all women who come for health services through infrastructure improvements, and look at the representation of females in clinical and administrator positions in the health facilities and recruit dedicated GBV staff to be located in the health facilities and PHC. Violet will develop GBV Case Management Standard Operating Procedures including GBV CM tools, Data Protection Protocols, and update the GBV service map that is available by the GBV Sub-Cluster in NWS and update the relevant referral pathways for multi-sectoral support in the operating area. Violet will do refresher training on the already existing protocol on CMR and ensure compliance with it. Also, ensure/improve educational materials available to clients on topics related to GBV in the health facilities. Vıolet will collaborate with the GBV SC to participate in the case management coordination efforts to benefit from inter-agency capacity development opportunities and already developed tools related to case management and data management.
1,250 women, girls, and men will be targeted by Violet GBV intervention within Amal Hospital and PHC, starting from train health facility medical and non-medical staff on GBV core concepts and safety referral. Whereas the GBV staff will be trained on Community-based Protection, PSEA & CSG, and PFA, and in-depth cases management to GBV cases workers and PSS counsellors, targeting a total of 120 staff members.
GBV Case Workers will provide specialized GBV services to survivors and the at-risk cases based on survivor-centred GBV Case Management to a total of 100 women and girls cases.
Violet PSS counsellors will provide cultural and age-specific individual and group psychosocial support sessions targeting GBV survivors and at-risk cases in a total of 850 women and girls.
Violet Young Mother Club facilitators will provide YMC cycles and sessions for women and girls to address the holistic needs of pregnant adolescents and first-time mothers, targeting 180 women and girls.
Violet will use the GBV SC M&E Toolkit to 2nd edition 2021 to monitor and evaluate the implements and reports in line with the inter-agency guidance.

5 - Implementation of activities

3.1: Train health facility medical and non-medical staff on GBV core concepts, and providing safe referrals within the health facility, the resources wıll be GBV SC package and CARE curriculum for Health staff on GBV. The GBV staff will be trained to improve caseworkers capacity on case management using GBV SC SOPs and guidelines and CARE CM package.3.2: Provide GBV CM for GBV survivors and the at risk cases: Delivering CM services after establishing the Violet CM SOP in support from CARE and in-link with GBV sub-cluster SOPs and IASC GBV Guideline. Referral pathways will establish by mapping the available services and updated on monthly basis. All cases will be reported in CM tracker, and receive full CM steps, registration, evaluation, safety plan, case plan, implement the plan, follow-up and monitoring, and case closure, moreover, any internal or external referrals with follow-up.3.3: Provide psychosocial support services: PSS counselors will provide cultural and age-specific individual and group PSS sessions targeting of GBV survivors and at risk cases. The cases will have a personal opportunity to receive support and experience growth during challenging times in life. Moreover, they will give the cases the opportunity to feel heard and meet other individuals who have gone through similar events and have felt many of the same things.3.4: Provide Young Mother Club sessions for girls and women: Violet will establishing young mother clubs (YMC) (max 10 beneficiaries per group). The YMC curriculum developed by CARE will target pregnant teenagers and women between the second and sixth months of pregnancy. Sessions will be co-facilitated by a health worker (such as a midwife or nurse) and a psychosocial worker.

1- Violence against Health Facilities and GBV workers: Likelihood Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts. Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare. Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas.2-Women and girls are not able to access to GBV service: Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Monitor the risks on the access to health facilities, and provide any kind of support like transportation. Moreover, encourage the community key members to facilitate access.3-People with disabilities are not be able to access the school Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Monitor the risks on the access to health facilities, and provide any kind of support like transportation. Arrange all rehabilitation needs for the health facilities building. Design the activities appropriate to PwDs.4- The community acceptance for the GBV intervention is low Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Conduct awareness-raising sessions with the community leaders and community-based organizations about GBV. Modified the GBV key messages and activities to fit the social and cultural context.5- GBV Interventions negatively affected the community and doing harm Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: Permanent monitoring of intervention process with the application of risk analysis and safety plans for all cases and services. Using a survivor-centred approach, ensuring that full informed consent is obtained and that the intervention is committed to the humanitarian and protection principles. Focusing on evaluating the impact of the intervention on an ongoing basis.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Border crossing between Turkey and Syria remains open for humanitarian assistanceRisks: Indiscriminate attacks on civilians and health infrastructure (IED, VBIED). Intensification of the conflict resulting in shifting frontlines, mass population displacements or drastic changes of the context. Exodus of professional staff to address the risk of instability, contingency plans are made and include predominantly the emergency evacuation of staff and equipment and shifting the services to where the community moves. Security team carries out regular security assessments of facilities and closely monitors developing security situations to determine if facilities remain open and continue to provide full range of services or limited emergency services during periods of increased hostilities.Assumption: The COVID-19 situation is still increasing Risk: Access loss to GBV servicesMitigation: Violet is addressing by improving the hygiene and wash protocols and materials in the facilities. Decrease the number of beneficiaries in the group sessions, and keep the social distance in both group and individual sessions. Resume the activities through an online approach in-link with all data protection protocol and respect confidentiality, with providing internet cards and use additional resources if needed by other projects, sectors, and donors. Keeping the donor updated with any additional fund required.
