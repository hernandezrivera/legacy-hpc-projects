Project Summary

The overall purpose of this project is to strengthen protection of the right to health for all Palestinians in the occupied Palestinian territory. The project comprises four objectives:
1. Enhance monitoring of attacks on health care and strengthen the protection of healthcare;
2. Enhance monitoring of barriers to health access;
3. Enhance advocacy for the right to health, including the prevention of health attacks and improved health access;
4. Enhance the integration of a human rights-based approach to health and empowerment for patients and health workers.
Expected beneficiaries include 60,000 patients (18,000 in Gaza and 42,000 in the West Bank) requiring secondary care outside Ministry of Health facilities and 3,770 frontline health workers (770 in Gaza and 3,000 in the West Bank) susceptible to attacks on health care in the occupied Palestinian territory.

Implementing Partners

World Health Organization will continue to work closely with partners in the Ministry of Health and Palestinian Coordination and Liaison Administration for the purposes of:
- Strengthening data collection on barriers to health access and attacks on health care (the latter within the global Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare or SSA)
- Capacity building for the right to health, including monitoring and reporting obligations for human rights treaties and conventions relevant to the right to health and collaborative work to define an indicator set to strengthen right to health monitoring.
Other key implementing partners for work on monitoring of barriers to health access and attacks on health care include:
- Palestinian Red Crescent Society (PRCS)
- Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS)
- UNRWA
- Union of Health Workers Committees (UHWC)
- East Jerusalem Hospital Network
- Terminal authorities for exit monitoring from Gaza
WHO intends to build the capacity of local partners for advocacy on the right to health through work within the Health Cluster to implement the oPt Advocacy Strategy, adapting messaging and ways forward in line with health needs and priorities.
WHO will also continue to work closely with UN and human rights organizations to facilitate availability of quality and timely data to support advocacy, legal processes and accountability mechanisms.

1 - Needs

The Health and Nutrition cluster partners estimated that 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian health interventions across the oPt, of whom two out of every three are in Gaza. Among them, the partners identified 104,764 people across the West Bank and Gaza to be in need of advocacy measures specifically to improve their access to health care. Additionally, more than 5,000 frontline health workers are in need of strengthened protection, including monitoring of attacks on health and other protection interventions. 1. Barriers to health access and attacks on healthcare continue in the occupied Palestinian territory, with obstacles higher in the Gaza Strip compared to the West Bank in recent years. In August 2019, 33% of patient applications to access healthcare out of Gaza were unsuccessful, while 16% of West Bank patient applications were unsuccessful. Approval rates differ according to age and sex – for example the approval rate for men aged 18 to 40 (35%) was less than half the approval rate for children aged less than three (82% F, 81% M), children aged 4 to 17 (73% F, 73% M) and those over 60 (81% F, 75% M). Children continue to travel for healthcare without accompaniment of parents – in 2018, more than 1,800 children were granted permits for more than 5,000 appointments without the accompaniment of parents. Meanwhile, barriers for health staff, ambulances and medical supplies continue. In the Gaza Strip, in the context of the Great March of Return from 30 March 2018 to 30 September 2019, three health workers have been killed and 832 injured in 550 recorded incidents against health staff and facilities in the Gaza Strip. 115 ambulances have been damaged, as well as 10 other forms of health transport, 7 health facilities (including 3 medical points). In the West Bank in 2019, 37 health attacks had been recorded as of the end of September, including the killing of a health worker and a number of incidents where health staff were prevented from accessing Palestinians who had been fatally wounded. 2. Collective efforts are essential to effectively advocate for improving health access and strengthening the protection of healthcare in the occupied Palestinian territory. WHO forms a cornerstone of these efforts through the activities outlined, including provision of reliable and impartial data on health access and attacks on healthcare, as well as through numerous advocacy products through different channels. WHO as the specialist UN agency for health is the reference point for health information for various UN reporting, as well as for political-level diplomatic briefings – and the data collected through advocacy monitoring continues to be used in these different fora. Similarly, WHO works closely with health and human rights partners to promote a human rights-based approach to health. 3. Full needs assessment for health access barriers can be found publicly in WHO monthly reporting, WHO annual reports and the WHO/Health Cluster monthly situation reporting (focused on barriers for those injured in demonstrations). WHO complements regular monthly quantitative needs assessment for health access with collection of qualitative information, including case studies. In addition to continuing quantitative data collection on attacks on healthcare, WHO carried out full review of implementation of the Surveillance System for Attacks on Healthcare and protection needs assessment in late 2018/early 2019.

2 - Activities

1. Enhance monitoring of attacks on health care and strengthen the protection of healthcare. In 2018, WHO implemented its Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA). The SSA is an online global system for strengthening data collection on health attacks, aiming to improve reliable and impartial data; advocacy to prevent attacks and protect health workers; and build best practices. WHO oPt conducted workshops in 2018 to expand the number of SSA partners registered to input, incorporating those who are most susceptible to attacks - including the Ministry of Health, PRCS, PMRS, UNRWA and UHWC. WHO was able to build on its existing system for data collection with key partners, including the Ministry of Health, to implement this system. Of the five countries in the initial implementation phase, the oPt recorded the highest number of incidents and injuries to health workers, in the context of Gaza’s Great March of Return. 2020 planned activities: - Provide training and equipment for key partners to improve timely and quality data collection for attacks on healthcare; - Provide disaggregated data on health attacks to health, protection and human rights partners for effective advocacy and reporting, and strengthened accountability; - Provide personal protective equipment to partners to strengthen protection against attacks on healthcare in the Gaza Strip. 2. Enhance monitoring of barriers to health access. Since 2008, WHO has worked with multiple partners, including MoH, the Coordination and Liaison Administration, terminal authorities and local authorities, to improve the collection and disaggregation of data on health access, establishing a system for regular monitoring and reporting. Analysis is carried out monthly, with production of WHO monthly and annual reporting on barriers to health access. Gaza patient access data is of high quality, but requires improvement in the West Bank. Broader barriers to health access, and implications for the health system, require strengthening. 2020 planned activities: - Provide training and equipment for key partners to enhance timely and quality data collection for barriers to health access - Provide disaggregated data on health access for health, protection and human rights partners for effective advocacy and reporting, and strengthened accountability. 3. Enhance advocacy for the right to health, including the prevention of health attacks and improved health access. 2020 planned activities: - In consultation with the recent review to strengthen advocacy effectiveness, develop a health sector-specific advocacy strategy with health partners, on the basis of the advocacy strategy of the Advocacy Working Group - Conduct capacity building activities through workshops with Health Cluster partners, including local NGOs and the Ministry of Health, to strengthen health advocacy activities and response - Produce advocacy materials on the right to health, incorporating messages on health attacks and barriers to health access in Gaza - Represent health partners in the Advocacy Working Group and provide liaison through Health Cluster coordination fora. 4. Enhance the integration of a human rights-based approach to health and empowerment for patients and health workers: - Develop an indicator set for monitoring the right to health with the Ministry of Health and key health and human rights partners - Enhance referral pathways for the major patient groups requiring referral out of Gaza through in-depth analysis of structural gaps, processes and patient outcomes to inform service strengthening and advocacy messaging - Carry out workshops and focus groups with patients and health workers to enhance the capacities of patient groups and community advocates to improve participatory models of care reduce barriers to health access.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

1. Protection of confidentiality of all patients and health professionals on whom data is collected. Where disaggregation includes very low numbers, the project will seek to protect confidentiality by not publishing such numbers without prior consent. 2. Full consent sought for collection and use, including storage and publication, of any personal information, including narratives and photographs. 3. WHO sets high standards regarding the code of conduct of all employees and contractors. There are internal processes to raise concerns, including through the mechanisms of an ombudsman and ethics office. There exist whistleblowing mechanisms to report confirmed or suspected harmful acts, abuse of positions of authority and conflicts of interest by employees or associated contractors.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

1. WHO monitors beneficiaries, disaggregating by age and sex to address potential age and gender barriers to access. Similarly, WHO advocacy programme is working to strengthen disaggregation of data by geography and for key demographic categories to improve information on health inequities, specifically through implementation of work to outline right to health indicators, in partnership with the Office for the High Commissioner of Human Rights in Palestine. 2. WHO advocacy team serve as disability focal points, and collaborate in UN-wide efforts to strengthen information and equal access for persons with disability in the occupied Palestinian territory. WHO will seek specifically to improve disaggregation of data by disability and improved understanding of disability needs in oPt. Currently, approximately 5% of beneficiaries for this project live with disability. 3. WHO specifically addresses other vulnerable groups in analyzing barriers to the right to health, including communities identified as vulnerable in UN vulnerability profiles. These include specific communities in Area C, the Seam Zone, H2 of Hebron, East Jerusalem and Gaza. Demographic vulnerability can compound geographic vulnerability, and WHO has sought specifically to highlight this through case studies (for example, in its Palestinian Voices publication, the case of a person with disability living in Area C).
