Project Summary

This integrated child protection and GBV response project targets three woredas in Afar region (Afambo, Asayita and Dubti woredas) two woredas in North Wello (Habru and Gubalafto woredas) in Amhara region, responding to the child protection and gender-based violence concerns exacerbated due to displacement attached with the conflict and systemic traditional practices. The project will be implemented in partnership with Mother and Children Multisectoral Organization (MCMDO). GOAL will be responsible for the three woredas in Afar region (Afambo, Assyita and Dubti woredas) and MCMDO will intervene in two woreda in Amhara Region (Habru and Gubalafto woredas)
The project aims to make a significant contribution to the mitigation of and response to gender-based violence and child protection problems in the targeted woredas by assisting survivors to become more confident and comfortable in accessing support and strengthening the existing referral pathways.
Both five woredas are affected by the war and received displaced persons from other conflict affected woredas. In both proposed interventions GOAL has adapted its approach and established a system to mitigate any GBV and Child protection problems and respond any possible to GBV incidences. GOAL has been implemented integrated community-based child protection and GBV response project by BHA fund we received through IRC in Asayita, Afambo, and Dubti woredas from 1 April 2021 –31 March 2022. For the last one-year GOAL Ethiopia (GE) and MCMDO have been supported GBV survivors, orphans, and vulnerable children, established Safe Spaces, and provided psychosocial support and awareness for the affected community in the three woredas in the region. GOAL was conducted baseline and endline survey to understand the protection needs and gaps in those woredas and identified that denial of resources, FGM, sexual, physical, and emotional abuses were the commonly recognized GBV type. Child marriage is also a commonly known child abuse in the area and were affecting more girls than boys. GOAL and MCMDO proposed a joint child protection and GBV intervention based on the experience we had in the field.
The integration will be reflected in awareness-raising events planning, shared prevention messages preparations, and strategizing response activities. Besides, we will explore existing referral pathways and strengthened relationships with local service providers to improve coordination between local protection actors.
Through the provision of case management services, psychosocial supports, capacity building activities, referrals, community awareness sessions, dignity kits provision and strengthening the coordination and local capacity of the community, GOAL and MCMDO will address GBV and child protection problems in all intervention woredas and contribute to the achievement of cluster objectives. The project will reach 93,049, beneficiaries(Dubti 20471, Assayita 20471, Afambo 16749, Gubalafto 20471, Habru 14888) through this integrated child protection and GBV response project all intervention woredas.

1. - Projects activities

GOAL and MCMDO designed the activities in line with the Country 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan and encompass activities prioritized under thematic area of GBV and Child protection. GOAL Ethiopia has been implementing an integrated child protection and GBV response projects funded by BHA and UNOCHA in Afar region and MCMDO in North Wello zone - Amhara region. This project intends to address the two critical gaps identified in the assessment through implementing community-based integrated child protection and gender-based violence programming. By establishing referral pathways, strengthen the coordination and provision of GBV and CP response, capacity building trainings for protection and non-protection actors; the affected community will be assisted to access to life-saving assistance and develop positive coping mechanisms. The following detailed project activities are designed to address the protection problems in those target woredas. Gender-Based Violence activities: - Provide GBV case management to 100 vulnerable women & girls and GBV survivors - Provide PSS services to 10000 women and adolescent girls including through women & girls friendly spaces and community-based structures- Procurement & distribution of Dignity Kits for 2000 women and adolescent girls.- Conduct trainings on Case management and psychosocial support for 75 health workers, social workers, and case workers. - Conduct public community awareness raising campaigns on GBV & PSEA for 90,549 community members. - Conduct trainings on GBV for 120 Non-GBV actors (GBV mainstreaming, pocket guide etc.) - Provide Legal assistance to 100 vulnerable women & girls and GBV survivors- Provide training for 100 women & girls on vocational skills and support them with startup capital for IGAs.- Conduct trainings for 150 community-based staff members, paralegals Child protection Activities: - Awareness Raising and community engagement on child protection issues for 9326 community members.- Capacity Building, system strengthening and Child protection mainstreaming and integration within other sectors for 60 non-CP actors.- Mental Health and Psychosocial Support for 5000 children- Case Management (Excluding FTR) for 250 Orphan and vulnerable children- Case Management (FTR and Alternative Care} for 100 UASC.- Reintegration and other community-based risk mitigation and empowerment services to for 25 girls and boys with specific needs- Strengthening Family and Caregiving Environment for 1200 parents and caregivers

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Effective coordination and collaboration with key partners and community groups will ensure the achievement of project objectives. GOAL and MCMDO will attend regular coordination meetings at Woreda, Regional and Federal levels. Both GOAL and MCMDO participates in woreda technical working group meetings, and regional cluster meetings while available. GOAL share 5W regularly with the protection cluster and attend meetings. Both GOAL and MCMDO have participated in IDP Coordination Groups and will ensure to coordinate with relevant actors to foster complementarity among responses - including with UN agencies, and all relevant government sector offices, clusters, and other protection stakeholders as required (sharing reports, documentation, etc.) at all levels – Federal, Regional, Zonal, and Woreda. GOAL’s and MCMDO’s principles around coordination and collaboration with different sectors ensure favorable conditions to implement all project plans effectively and efficiently.GOAL and MCMDO will keep regular communication with the partners and coordinate and monitor the intervention to make sure same standards and best practices are followed by the partners and the achievements are in line with the expected results. Any issue in terms of implementation delays, security or any other risks will be timely discussed, and a joint solution and decision will be made.Interventions will be complemented with other sectoral activities in targeted woredas wherever possible. The GE and MCMDO presence and the complementarity of the interventions will have vital importance for protection interventions as it helps in strengthening internal referral networks for addressing the dire needs of the most vulnerable people of the community.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Ensuring accountability towards our project participants and partners through participation, feedback, transparency, information sharing, appropriate activities that respond to the needs, and appropriate use of resources. Engagement with local communities is crucial. GOAL and MCMDO ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle and that consultation is ongoing. GOAL and MCMDO considers it of paramount importance that beneficiaries have access to information about their entitlements, have access to the Code of Conduct, and are able to provide feedback. During any community activities, discussions are held in advance with the affected community, project participants, community leaders, and authorities. Information is provided about the project, the donor, targeting criteria and entitlements, our Code of Conduct, and ways to contact the organization.GOAL is a member of the Core Humanitarian Standards Alliance. A Community Response Mechanism (Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism) is established for all locations including Afar for stakeholders, including beneficiaries, to be able to provide complaints or feedback. Beneficiaries will be informed of this mechanism during activities, entitlement posters will be printed and displayed at relevant sites. The CRM is designed for direct and indirect project participants, community members, local and national government, partners, and GOAL and MCMDO staff. The core principles of GOAL’s and MCMDO’s CRM are confidentiality, accessibility, safety, and transparency. GOAL has dedicated third party (independent from operations) in-country resources in place to manage the CRM. All staff is trained on accountability measures and CRM, and comprehensive guidelines and SOPs are in place. Community members and project participants are provided with clear information about how to contact us to provide feedback, request information, make complaints, or express grievances. Contact details are provided for field staff, the Regional Manager, and the Country Director. In addition, details of a dedicated “Call me Back” phone number and e-mail address in GOAL’s and MCMDO Head Office in Addis are provided. During distributions, representatives (male and female, and literate if possible) are selected by the community as ‘Community Feedback Members’. The community can also make contact directly. All grievances are dealt with in a serious and confidential manner and shared with GOAL and MCMDO’s Ethiopia’s Head Office in Addis. The most serious complaints are dealt with directly by the in-country management team.Entitlement posters will be printed and used in the women's and children's safe spaces in pictorial form and any written items will be in the local language. Representatives from the local population will be engaged as part of the CRM to provide feedback or request clarification on the project from both GOAL and MCMDO staff and will be fully engaged throughout the project activities.Monitoring visits will also address accountability questions to establish the level of knowledge of the beneficiaries of the selection criteria, their entitlements, GOAL’s and MCMDO’s Code of Conduct. Both GOAL and MCMDO are committed to a having a culture of evidence-based learning and accountability.
