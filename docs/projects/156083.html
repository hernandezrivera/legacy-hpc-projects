Project Summary

The project will continue MTI's regular distribution of medical equipment and supplies to 20 health facilities in Northern western Syria.
As of January 2019, and in partnership with local NGOs, MTI transferred 11 shipments of medical equipment and supplies from the USA to Aleppo and Idleb governorates in NW Syria. MTI has been able to transfer one container on average every 45-60 days to NW Syria. Each container has an approximate value of $700,000. In 2017, MTI delivered a total of 4,044 pieces of medical equipment and supplies for a total value of $ 3,004,859. On average, one container is distributed to 20 primary and secondary health care facilities.

Needs

MTI in coordination with local partners continues delivering shipments of Medical and Health Product (MHP) from USA to Syria, via Turkey. Bombings and other attacks in northern Syria have led to deaths, injuries, destruction of property and severe shortages of health services. Shortages of medical and pharmaceutical products have been amongst the biggest concerns of the population and the health providers. Medical consumables distributed by MTI in northern Syria are paramount to improving health indicators. This equipment and supplies will contribute to providing vulnerable populations (including pregnant women, female-headed households, children under ﬁve, elderly and people living with disabilities) with an improved access to healthcare. Additional shipments of targeted MHP will be sent every 45-60 days, with the content adaptable, depending on needs in Syria. MTI maintains a close contact with local authorities in the areas of distribution. Hospitals, PHCs and other facilities determined for distribution activities were selected considering a number of criteria, namely urgency of need, security, geographical importance and population. This information was obtained through regular contact/meetings with Health Directorate, who had access to veriﬁable and thorough ground data. List of medicines and equipment are deﬁned in consultations with the Turkish Red Crescent and the local Syrian Health Directorates. This project served an estimated total of 44,000 female and 55,000 male in 22 different medical facilities in Aleppo and 22 facilities in Idleb.

Implementation of activities

MIT receives, organizes and packs donations of medical equipment and supplies then MTI’s team of staff and volunteers compose and load the containers at Seattle Port in the USA. MTI makes a donation to its partners based in Turkey. Partners receive the container at Mersin port and work directly with the (Turkish Red Crescent) and AFAD (Disaster and Emergency Management Presidency) to obtain the approvals for containers, pass customs, organize the transportation from Mersin to Bab Al Salamah and Bab Alhawa border crossing points and delivers the goods to the Aleppo and Idleb Health Directorate warehouses. MTI and its partners, TRC and the Heath Directorates altogether decide the distribution plan. The Health Directorates under the monitoring of MTI and partners distribute the contents of the containers to the health facilities with well documentation (GRN, Photos and success story).

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Staff is targetted by criminal or armed activities aiming to steal the products. Mitigation: Conduct security assessment before distribution, collaborate with local actors to ensure support to distibution activities and show its impact, avoid transferring large shipments in insecure locations, avoid shipping during night hours, maintain regular communication with field staff during distribution, maintain low visibility during shipping and distribution.Probability: Possible the severity: is high 2- Staff is harmed due to military and conflict-related activities during shipping and distribution. Mitigation: Conduct security assessment before distribution, reschedule shipments to relatively safe periods, maintain regular communication with field staff during distribution.Probability: Possible the severity: is high

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Bad weather damages the products during transportation. Mitigation is to avoid shipping products during bad weather forecast, ensure that warehouses and trucks are protected against bad weather. Likelihood: Possible. Impact: High 2- Products are stolen during transportation or storage. Mitigation: Collaborate with local health directorates and local authorities to ensure safe transportation and storage, hire guards if necessary and choose storage and transportation roads known to be safe. Likelihood: Possible Impact: High 3- Long customs process in Mersin result in additional clearance fees. Mitigation: Ensure that full documents are received prior to shipment arrival. Ensure proper communication with shipping agent, TRC and AFAD to avoid delays. Likelihood: Possible. Impact: High 4- Inappropriate handling of products at the end health facility. Mitigation: Provide training for facility staff if necessary, check warehouses prior to shipping, conduct regular visits to targetted facilities to verify the practices of medical commodities handling. Likelihood: Possible. Impact: High
