Project Summary

In order to build capacity, increase ownership and clarify responsibility for the management and maintenance of WASH infrastructure, GOAL is in the process of implementing the UNICEF WASH FIT framework for adoption across the health and nutrition facilities. WASH FIT is a: “practical guide that provides a description of a risk-based, continuous improvement framework and associated tools for undertaking water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) improvements as part of wider quality improvements in health care facilities. WASH FIT extends beyond water quality to address sanitation, hygiene, health care waste and other aspects of environmental health and health care facility management and staff empowerment. The guide contains ready to use tools to help implement WASH FIT and step-by-step instructions for each stage. GOAL Sudan will develop plans together with the SMoH to introduce the WASH FIT approach gradually or pilot it, in our supported health and nutrition facilities.
GOAL Sudan take a holistic approach to WASH in our areas of operation. Quality water supply, hygiene promotion, sanitation facilities, training and management, and environmental waste are all elements of our WASH program, which is implemented as part of a multi-sectoral response. Nutrition, WASH, and Health activities are all linked.
In GOAL’s nutrition and health facilities, where existing water facilities are not adequate or available, GOAL will rehabilitate existing water points with hand pumps which are located near the GOAL supported facilities, to ensure they have access to safe water. In addition, targeted water points contribute to improving access to sustainable and safe water in selected villages where existing waterpoints are non-functional. Water committees responsible for the appropriate management of the water supply (and collection of water user fees for operations and maintenance) are trained on management of water systems, awareness on over-pumping and the importance of ground water recharge. In GOAL-supported health (including nutrition) facilities, clinic staff are responsible to for the management of waste including biological waste. GOAL will construct incinerators in all GOAL supported health facilities in order to ensure proper disposal of medical waste; these will be operated and managed by the clinic staff. All the clinic staff will be trained on the use and maintenance of incinerators. However, the training will particularly target the facility cleaning staff, guards, and in some clinics a selected staff member to ensure that they handle and put the waste into the incinerators without endangering their own health by making sure that they handle waste for disposal using the Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and that sharp boxes are not emptied into the incinerator but rather they are disposed into the incinerator together with their contents (sharps) to further reduce the risk of needle pricks. GOAL currently covers the cost of fuel for running incinerators for supported facilities as part of running costs but will work out a way to sustainably cover this. In addition, during the project period GOAL will conduct universal precautions training for MAs, Pharmacists, MWs and vaccinators. In addition, our Nutrition Impact & Positive Practices (NIPP), School Health Club, Care Group, and other community education activities, all include hygiene promotion as a key topic to improve and address hygiene-related community health concerns.

1 - Needs

GOAL considers WASH needs on their own and as interlinked with health, nutrition, and FSL needs. GOAL and inter-agency needs assessments from Q4 of 2018, as well as GOAL’s baseline data collection from Q3 2019, found critical WASH needs in Talodi, Habila and Rashad localities, with both the general population as well as health facilities in particular unable to access clean water, without adequate sanitation infrastructure and services, and using poor hygiene practices that can contribute to disease. In Talodi, the Water Supply Unit receives no funds to ensure proper operations and maintenance of the water systems. According to a GOAL assessment conducted in December 2019, 50% of the handpumps are non-functional forcing health facilities to fetch and purchase water from long distances, exponentially increasing the risk of contamination during transit. An inter-agency assessment found out that only 45% of Talodi’s population of over 42,000 (plus 30,000 gold miners working nearby) was covered by functional water sources. Much of the population still uses seasonal ponds dug to store water from the rainy season (hafirs) shared by human and livestock. In Habila, a similar situation remains and is compounded by the poor groundwater situation, where most boreholes can only yield adequate flow only for a small hand pump, yet only 58% of the pumps in the locality are functional. Otherwise the population and service providers rely on hafirs in Habila as well, where water requires filtering before it is safe to use. In Rashad, GOAL assessments found that 25 hand pumps and 10 water yards non-operational, which GOAL is beginning to address with existing funding, but the groundwater situation in Rashad also limits the yield of even the most precisely drilled boreholes. Hygiene and environmental health are critical components of integrated programs as well, to prevent spread of diseases, reduce vectors, promote good nutrition, and allow for proper IPC in health facilities. According to Kutum’s July 2019 MICS report only 23.7% of people surveyed in the targeted areas knew even three of the five critical times to wash hands, 34.9% of people reported washing their hands at least three critical times with no significant change from 2016. In all localities in South Kordofan open defecation is common due to low latrine coverage 60% for Kutum, 69% for Habila, 68% for Rashad, 70% for Abukarshola and 70% for Talodi in general (but only 5% for IDPs in Talodi). GOAL has integrated with community health, nutrition, and Behaviour Change Communication (BCC) community awareness raising activities through Care Groups, School Health Clubs, and NIPP Circles, together with hygiene campaigns and sanitation events.
