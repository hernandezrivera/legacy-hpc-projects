Project Summary

Africa has the highest rate of education exclusion with Over one fifth of children between the ages of 6 and 11 out of school, with one third of youths between the ages of 12 and 14. According to the UIS data, almost 60% of youths between the ages of 15 and 17 are not in school. Barriers to education are particularly peculiar for girls, children from minority ethnic groups, children with disabilities and children living in conflict areas. Amongst the many barriers to education in Africa, the most common are direct cost and traditional practices, crisis, poor legal enforcement of education policies. In Cameroon, poor access to education and degrading quality of education is common place in the North West and South West Regions which are in the midst of a four-year long socio-political crises. One of the direct consequences of the socio-political crises is mass internal displacement, which has resulted in the saturation of neighbouring regions, especially the West and Littoral regions. In the West region, this mass immigration has resulted in insufficiency (and inefficiency due to increased teacher to student and student to classroom ratios) of educational institutions, and increased vulnerability for children whose parents can’t afford fees, forcing them to carry hawk or do some kind of work to raise funds to support the family.
As a fundamental human right, every child deserves to receive quality education; this does not only guarantee the future of the child and his/her nation, but the entire world at large. However, in Cameroon, the incessantly growing number of IDP children and out-of-school children presents as a deprivation of this human right. Young girls who should be in school and advancing their educational career are presently being prepared for marriage to men who are more than double their age because their parents are no longer able to support the cost of raising them and catering for their needs. This marriage doesn’t just steal childhood from these young girls but equally reduces the probability that these girls will ever attain quality education, as marriage within the context they find themselves is directly related to child birth and motherhood.
Children living with disabilities too face increase vulnerability and educational neglect within the West region in these times as their specialized needs are placed as secondary to the provision of meals; especially as most of them cannot contribute to family income as their counterparts who are without disabilities can. Disability friendly environments and institutions that support their holistic education have also grown inadequate to tend to the needs of their ever-growing population within the West region.
Against this backdrop of current happenings in the West Region of Cameroon, Our project “Providing assistance and improving access to education for vulnerable children in the West Region of Cameroon” seeks to provide the much needed assistance to educational institutions within the west regions, in view of rehabilitation and expansion of already existing institutions, as well as provision of essential equipment (especially disability friendly equipment) to said educational institutions. Also, with primary on young girls, this project seeks to provide support to parents, guardians and other care givers who are IDPs within the West region in view of procurement of educational necessities for their children; necessities like uniforms, fees, books and shoes.
Equally, the project will provide economic support to the most vulnerable IDP households

Plan fields

1 - Besoins / Needs

Out of School Children: The concentration of IDPs within the Littoral and West regions of Cameroon has greatly increased the pressure on available resources, especially educational resources. A multitude of children are out of schools and on the streets hawking in a bid to support their families at the expense of a dignifying future for themselves. This is supported by UNICEF statistics which hold that 600,000 people are out of school in the rural and semi-urban areas thanks to the North West South West crisis. The result of children and youths who are out of school is an increase in crime wave and an introduction to a life of crime for vulnerable children in a bit to meet ends meet. The inability for IDP children to attend schools and for host communities’ schools to provide quality education to the greatly increased number of children in need of education is primarily hinged to financial adequacy on both parties. In that regard, this project comes in to meet this need and enable schools to serve the children who are in serious need of education if they are to carve out a life of dignity for themselves in the years ahead.
