Project Summary

This project seeks to improve the health status and nutritional well being of 168,056 refugees and 53,178 host nationals in Maban County through the delivery of quality and comprehensive secondary health care services at Maban County (Bunj) Hospital.

1 - Needs

Both the refugee and host populations in Maban County have experienced prolonged periods of displacement and upheaval that have interfered with their access to basic healthcare and education. Many have been exposed to conflict, food insecurity, gender-based violence, and poor sanitation; all of which have contributed to a high incidence of complex health conditions that cannot be treated at the primary healthcare level. Malakal Teaching Hospital was the next nearest hospital in Upper Nile State, but it shut down following the intense conflict. The next feasible referral option for refugees needing secondary care and SC services, if there is no hospital in Maban, is the Juba Teaching Hospital. The Juba Teaching Hospital is inaccessible by road from Maban and the surrounding areas, with limited air transport at a prohibitive cost for the general public.Samaritan’s Purse will improve the health and nutrition status of the population by ensuring continuous availability of secondary health services and 24/7 emergency care in Maban. While some patients are self-referred, most patients accessing care at Bunj Hospital come through strong referral networks with primary healthcare providers and nutrition centers located throughout Doro, Batil, Gendrassa, and Kaya Refugee Camps, and the host community. Samaritan’s Purse, in partnership with UNHCR, will continue strengthening these networks through ongoing collaboration with the Ministry of Health (MOH) and NGOs implementing health projects in the surrounding area. This will be done through the monthly referral meetings that bring together all partners to discuss referrals and come up with ways of strengthening the linkages. In 2019, SP will work towards strengthening feedback mechanisms between the different partners through the partner referral focal persons. Many gaps in health services persist in Maban, despite existing interventions. Among these, the most prominent are the limited treatment options for chronic illnesses and lack of surgical specialization; a negative trend was also observed in maternal health in 2018. For instance, maternal health records as of October 2018 showed 10 mortalities, which was a concerning increase from only 3 recorded in all of 2017. Most of the maternal deaths observed in 2018 were related to delayed/late referrals who arrived at Bunj Hospital in critical condition. In November 2018, SP held a meeting with all partners to discuss the concerning maternal mortality increase; all agreed to refer high risk mothers to Bunj Hospital as soon as their cases were detected at the primary health centers. The discussions also concluded that each maternal health partner would have a focal person responsible for referrals at the primary health centers. While the hospital alone cannot address all chronic illnesses and surgical needs for the community served, SP will continue advocating for increased engagement from the MOH and County Health Department (CHD) to include Maban in national programming for chronic illnesses. SP will also engage with specialized surgical teams, and seek available space in the hospital to host clinics for conditions such as cataracts, fistulas, and orthopedic care. One of the primary objectives in 2019 will be to build the capacity of national staff nurses through continued medical education, with an emphasis on hospital bedside training and management of specific medical cases. In previous years, SP focused largely on building the capacity of new nursing assistants through both classroom and on-the-job training. In 2019, SP will continue to strengthen current efforts and focus on building the capacity of staff through clinical instruction, bedside training, and continuing medical education. At the end of the year, the project will have achieved the following objectives:Objective 1: Health status and nutritional wellbeing of the population improved.Objective 2: Population has optimal access to reproductive health and HIV services.

2 - Activities

The project will offer medical services from the static location at Bunj Hospital. The hospital is already established and linked for referral with primary healthcare centres (PHCCs) and primary health care units (PHCUs) run by cooperating partners in the camps and the government/County Health Department (CHD) within host communities. In recognition of spiritual needs among the patients, SP’s chaplaincy will provide prayer, counseling and biblical encouragement for patients and staff, while providing compassionate care and services to all without regard to gender, nationality, religion or ethnic groupings. By ensuring timely and upgraded training of staff, SP will continue providing the best care possible to patients through clinical practice and service to all. In 2019, SP intends to sponsor scholarships for clinical care training for the locally sourced staff, who will help in enhancing the quality of service delivery, while preparing a future handover of the facility to the community/government. Samaritan´s Purse has engaged universities to serve as a clinical attachment for internships. This will also serve as a recruitment mechanism long term. In line with SP´s mission, SP will continue supporting the vulnerable populations in Maban in times of calm or crisis. Activities include surgical and inpatient services, maternal and newborn health services, HIV/AIDS prevention, care and treatment, laboratory, diagnostic, and imaging services, communicable disease management and tuberculosis treatment,chronic and non-communicable disease management, a stabilization center, pharmaceutical management, sanitation and hygiene messaging, and continuous capacity building.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. Maban County Hospital’s population of concern is refugees from BNS, Sudan, who have settled in Maban County, and members of the Maban host community. The hospital aims to serve over 221,234 vulnerable beneficiaries (168,056 refugees and 53,178 host nationals). Approximately 51% of the refugee population in Maban County is female, with approximately 42% of the refugee population ranging between the age of 5 and 17 years. In the host community to be served by the project, 50.9% are females, and 39.3% are between the ages of 5 and 17 years.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Samaritan’s Purse’s work in the health sector in Maban County is recognized by UNHCR, the county government, UN agencies, and international non-governmental organizations (INGOs) working in the area. In 2019, SP will continue participating in the monthly inter-agency coordination meetings convened by UNHCR in Maban to discuss emerging refugee issues and develop plans to address them. Further, SP engages with and involves the local refugee structures and leadership, such as sheikhs, Omdas and religious leaders within the refugee community in project planning and implementation, as necessary. Additionally, SP will continue participating in all ad hoc meetings called by the Commission of Refugee Affairs (CRA) and the monthly inter-agency meetings called by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in 2019. Samaritan’s Purse will participate and actively contribute to cluster meetings organized in Juba and the health and nutrition coordination meetings organized by UNHCR at the Maban level. Samaritan’s Purse will continue cooperating with UNHCR in efforts to provide quality secondary health services and ensure proper and timely implementation and monitoring of programs. Samaritan’s Purse regularly attends the emergency preparedness and response meetings in Maban and Juba. In 2018, SP and WFP had a field-level agreement (FLA) through which SP provided and continues to provide food commodities for refugees in the camps of Doro and Batil; an application has been submitted to WFP to continue with these services in 2019. Samaritan’s Purse also has a good working relationship with UNICEF, which is a partner in this proposal for the provision of therapeutic foods and medical supplies as GIK in the nutrition program. Continued discussions with all actors involved, especially the camp-managing organization (the Danish Refugee Council), is necessary to identify the service gaps related to this initiative

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?
6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

At the country level, security information is constantly being monitored in close liaison with the United Nations Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS), for any threats to the operation. Locally in Maban, SP attends the weekly security meetings and liaises with UNHCR as the security focal point. Samaritan’s Purse will implement project activities in conjunction with all local and international stakeholders to avoid duplication of efforts or the creation of a parallel delivery system.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

In addition to providing secondary health care, SP aims to support the MOH in building its capacity to serve the long-term health needs in Maban County through preparing local staff to fill key roles at the hospital and advocating for increased support of health programs. These programs would include initiation of improved treatment availability for chronic conditions and non-communicable diseases (NCD) such as diabetes, epilepsy, cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and chronic respiratory diseases. Further, SP plans to continue building the capacity of local staff to work in the hospital, through quality trainings and provision of relevant materials. As SP envisions a hospital that is completely independent, a gradual process began in 2018 and will continue into 2020; the process involves separating SP field office leadership and administration from that of the hospital. As a deliberate sustainability strategy, SP anticipates that this process will continue enhancing excellent and quality service delivery.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.2: Services are for all members of the community. While there have not been formal Focus group discussions (FGD), the services that are available particularly for women and girls, such as the antenatal clinic, Family Planning, pediatric and maternal and child (MCH) & reproductive health services are available at times which are dictated by the community. The staff understand that women often need to travel long distances for services and there are professional staff on duty at all times to meet their needs. Family members are welcomed to stay with their sick relatives. The hospital wards are open plan, maximizing visibility of all patients by medical staff but also of be each other. This may reduce privacy, but it provides protection for all as patients develop a comradery and tend to advocate for each other’s needs. Patient care areas are well lit throughout. 8.9: SP understands that some of their hospital patients or beneficiaries travel very far to access their health services increasing their vulnerability. The hospital is open 24 hrs, 7 days a week. Travelers will not be left outside until morning if they are arrive at night. Families often stay in the hospital grounds while their family member is being cared for. Some food is provided for caretakers and patients. Patient care areas and verandas are kept lit throughout the night. 8.13: MCH respects patient confidentiality and privacy. Patient information is protected in particular, sensitive testing and documentation, for example with regards to PMTCT. The consultation room is separate with a closing door. Other patients waiting do not know when a patient inside is being offered counselling and testing for HIV and ledgers are locked away each day. Ledgers are not left open in patent care areas. Staff are trained and receive refresher training on respecting patient confidentiality. SP takes the safety and privacy of their beneficiaries seriously and disciplinary measure would be taken in an event.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The review of service provision is supported by beneficiary feedback, received and incorporated on a regular basis. Currently, refugee camp coordination meetings occur on a monthly basis; in these forums, refugee community members provide feedback on camp management and the services offered. Although Bunj is not the primary health facility catering to their needs, feedback of their experience at Bunj is shared if they are referred from their respective PHCCs/PHCUs. This feedback is shared with SP at monthly health and nutrition coordination meetings. In 2019, feedback at the hospital will be formalized with the installment of a secure suggestion box (the box ought to have been constructed in 2018, but will be completed in 2019) that is easily accessible for all those receiving care at the hospital. In addition, the crisis counseling team will administer a questionnaire when meeting with patients during their weekly care sessions. Patients will be assured that completion of the questionnaire is confidential. Feedback from the suggestion box and questionnaires will be compiled by the Hospital Administrator and prioritized for action, accordingly.
