Project Summary

The project aims to enhance resilience, livelihood & improve food security of 1881 farmer's HHs with capita of 9217 (2544 men, 2525 women, 3871 children and 277 elderly) serving 7280 dunums. In response to the HRP 2021 3rd strategic objective & in line with 1st and 2nd FSS HRP 2022 objectives. Through land rehabilitation of 570 dunums to enhance productivity & livelihoods including machinery work, stony retaining walls&
fencing. Rehabilitate 470 dunums of land with fencing to protect from wild animals. Short term employment for 183 skilled and unskilled workers to rehabilitate 560 dunums with 3360 working days in highly sensitive areas in area C where no heavy machinery is allowed to access. Rehabilitate 23km of agricultural roads,16 cisterns, installing 5km of water carries, 35 livestock barns/animal shelter to enhance access to land protect livestock& ensure productivity. Planting 500 dunums of fodder seeds that will serve farmers with livestock fodder and protect land from settler’s attacks and confiscation. Distributing agricultural inputs and PPEs for 210 farmers (olive harvest tools, grass cutters, water tanks), these inputs will facilitate the work of women men farmers and minimize the confrontation with Israeli's settlers&PPEs.Rehabilitate 135 home gardens directed to women farmers to enhance self-sufficiency while ensuring the presence of a cistern and target 105 women food processors to rehabilitate the space of processing or provide production inputs. The project prioritized 29 communities shared by FSS 2022 response matrix; in Salfit, Qalqelia, Nablus, and Ramallah all in area C. This project is based on a rapid need assessment (RNA) conducted by PARC in Sep. 2021. Furthermore, the project adopted lessons learnt from other similar projects in the targeted areas.The targeted locations like Salfit suffers from the severe attacks from Israeli settlers, daily confiscation from Israelicivil administration, limited access to water and struggle to access agri- lands. In Qalqelia the targeted locations are in close contact to settlements and segregation wall, Israeli occupation forces prevents farmers from accessing their lands and the eastern side of Qalqelia suffers the limited access to water. Nablus encountered many episodes of
confiscation of Agri-lands and heavy expansion of settlements which led to violent escalations harming local communities. Additionally, this project targeted the highly sensitive locations in area C where farmers stated a real threat to confiscation and theft by settlers in Ramallah. PARC partner ACPP will play a role in documenting the human rights violation and conducts the fast dissemination to international community due to their history and experience in food security sector, working with local communities on DRR plans and risk mitigation in addition to protection mechanisms. The proposed project will adopt human rights-based, gender mainstreaming and do no harm approaches with respect to core humanitarian standards. To ensure accountability and community engagement project team will announce and
formulate Local Committees (LC). which will ensure beneficiaries access to information hence provided aid. The LC will ensure neutrality in accessing the provided humanitarian aid, giving high priority to women, children, elderly and people with disability. The LC will be constituted of both men,women and Pwds. Furthermore, clear selection criteria will be made with 10 extra marks to Women headed households, people with disabilities, elderlies with diseases. To ensure protection mainstreaming and PSEA PARC has a safeguarding policy with clear standards and zero tolerance approach towards
any form of exploitation. All staff members participate in trainings on PSEA. All activities implemented will ensure to abide by protection of COVID-19 SOP.

1 - Needs

In preparation for this project proposal PARC conducted a rapid need assessment (RNA) in September 2021 to verify on the previously selected locations targeted for HRP 2020 proposal. 7 internal meetings were conducted to prioritize the most vulnerable targeted locations based on PARCs accumulative experience, lessons learned from OCHA-HF funded projects, FSS HPC need analysis 2022, FSS humanitarian needs 2022, OHCA statistics and data. The need assessment ensured participatory and human rights-based approach in its methodology which was carried out via desk review, phone interviews, meetings with relevant stakeholders, women clubs, and focus groups discussions with beneficiaries. The focus group engaged 96 beneficiaries (75 men, 21 women) including elderly and people with disabilities from the different targeted areas. This was carried after communication to coordinate with key actors in the area and relevantstakeholders (Governorate bodies, MoA, OXFAM, CARE, ACH, Save the Children, AAA, ASDC, Village councils’ members and mukhtars). In addition to coordination with MOA and FSS based on the suggested mechanism . The need assessment aimed to determine the needs of all vulnerable groups with special attention to women, children, elderly and people with Disability. Through designing activities that improves accessibility to agricultural lands, livelihood and productivity which will contribute to enhancing the food security of HH benefiting women, children and people with disabilities. Part of the desk review of the RNA included OCHA reports that highlights the vulnerability of targeted communities in Nablus, Qalqiliya, Salft and Ramallah. Stating that in 2021 in area C 555 structured were demolished out of which 388 demolitions are Agricultural facilities. Also, according to FSS HNO 2021, communities in Area C are faced with the demolition and confiscation of agriculture structures, and restriction on rehabilitation of productive assets, movement and settlement expansion, all contribute to livelihood instability and deterioration of the socioeconomic environment. Moreover, a This requests key actors to quickly support farmers to be able to use Build Back Better approach. At another level the Jerusalem Aid &Human Rights Centre report stated that 18 communities in Salft are suffering from the enormous expansion of settlements.18 communities in Salft are surrounded with 22 settlements with a total of 43,478 dunums under Israeli jurisdiction. This threatens the humanitarian living conditions and rights of Palestinians living in Salft. The RNA and the meetings with MoA prioritized the most vulnerable locations as follows:Salft (Sarta, Deir Istia, Kufr Hares, Marda, Salfit gov.)Qalqiliya (Al Mudawwar, Hajja , Immatin, Izbat alAshqar, Izbat Jalou’d, Izbat Salman, Jayyus, Jit & Azzun Atmeh) Ramallah (Deir Bzee’, Deir Jreer, Om Saffa, Na’lin, Al-Janieh, Burqa, Ras Karkar and Al-mughayyer) and Nablus (Qusra, Doma, Aqraba,Beit Furek, Beit Dajan, Salem & Der El hatab). The proposed project aims to addresthe needs of the targeted location to ensure that their food security, livelihood and assets is protected and enhanced through: land, road, cisterns, water carriers, home gardens and animal barns rehabilitation while ensuring the presence of cisterns or using rainfed crops, seeds distribution, provision of agriculture inputs, rehabilitate production areas for women food processors or provide them with production inputs. In addition to PPEs to protect from COVID-19. Consequently, the proposed intervention will respond directly to the Vulnerability measures identified by OCHA which categorize the Qalqiliya targeted communities (Jayyus, Hajja,Izbat Salman, Izbat Al Ashqar, Al Medawar and Izbat Jalou’d) to be high/ Medium vulnerability in regards to access to land, and protection. Noting that a high percentage of the farmers are females and parents, hence project interventions will enhance access to land which will ensure HH protection and productivity. During the FGDwith women farmers, the majority confirmed the need to focus on road, animal barn, home gardens rehabilitations and provision of production inputs. As road rehabilitation will ensure their accessibility to agricultural land since women participate with men farmers in collecting crops especially during olive harvest. Increase protection since women will not be forced to walk long distances to reach their lands but can use personal cars. At a community level, road rehabilitation enhance access to ambulances and firefighter trucks. In regards to barns, women confirmed that rehabilitation will facilitate their working conditions in the animal barns and minimize efforts. Moreover, interviews with women club representatives confirmed that families living under acute poverty will have a chance to utilize the home garden for self-sufficiency and sell the excess to make extra income. Hence enhancing their livelihood and economic conditions. Women club representatives also stated the need to provide support to women small scale produces who need to enhance the quality of production by either providing equipment’s to increase efficiency or rehabilitate the working space. PARC staff will participate on PARC safeguarding training including PSEA and communication methods clarified to ensure that local community have a clear understanding on the PSEA available channels.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a well-articulated feedback and response mechanism (FRM) that is put in action from the design phase until closing. FRM at PARC is ensured with respect to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). The CHS adopted by PARC will ensure to provide a safe/dignified space for targeted population to express their feedback and complaints. In June 2021, PARC updated its accountability policies (information sharing, complaints and feedback and participation) and the Safeguarding policy hand on hand with Accountability specialist from Christian Aid. Accordingly, PARC consulted with local communities to identify the most appropriate channels suitable for them. Hence, a dedicated email (Complaints@pal-arc.org), complaint boxes distributed across the governorates, in addition to the MEAL Officer phone number (Whatts App). These three channels will be utilized to receive feedback and complaints. Then, based on PARC accountability policies the complaint shall be registered into a well-designed complaint form that will be tracked and documented through the MEAL documentation system. Additionally, all project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer within the project MEAL framework. With reference to CHS, complaint mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience will be encouraged to claim their rights. Field staff will announce the availability of the three feedback mechanisms for the local communities and ensure that any complaint or feedback shall be addressed will be taken seriously including any sensitive complaints. Any complaints received shall be protected in accordance to data protection protocols adopted by PARC. Feedback mechanisms will also be promoted and implemented in coordination with local committees (LCs).In regards to safeguarding complaints (SEA), PARC has a well-articulated policy with clear standard data protection procedures and will take any sensitive complaint seriously when proven correct no tolerance policy shall be applied.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No Harm" is seen as a way of ensuring accountability towards the local communities. This approach utilized community participation, information sharing, feedback and response mechanism with respect to Core Humanitarian standards. Which is also in line to environment protection and gender mainstreaming. The designed project adheres to social, political, economic and health context of targeted populations and is based on comprehensive understanding verified through the participatory needs assessment and updated risk analysis. In regards to social context, PARC understands the conservative local Palestinian context and act on that in all project phases taking into consideration the different gender needs such as women’s, PwD, and children have safe access to services. Political context, all proposed interventions will take into consideration the sensitivity of its location in which a low-profile working approach will take place. This will happen by supporting the local community to document the Israeli violations. Additionally, PARC team will report any violation and incidents to official bodies like OCHA. Finally, at economic level the project interventions aid to support local population to empower their production abilities and access to their assets. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analyse potential risks that will be taken into consideration during implementation. PARC team adheres to the developed Standard of Operation SoPs for the implementation of the different activities, field visits, M&E, complaint and feedback mechanism to ensure all safety and security measures are taken against COVID-19. PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholders in the need assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, and community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ensure optimal community participation, outreach, and transparency in service provision. PARC ensures a transparent selection process which avoids social conflict during the implementation. The project is designed based on Human-Rights Based Approaches (HRBA) that includes women, youth and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). HRBA; focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, none discrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human rights instruments are an integral part of PARC work.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at communities. Gender mainstreaming and protection approaches will be integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ensures to engage local community members, VCs and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive understanding of the targeted community’s needs including the participation of women and women led cooperatives and clubs. The findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a Project Local Committee that represents the targeted population and include key actors from the area and representatives of Community Based Organizations which ensures optimal outreach, transparency and ownership to vulnerable community groups. The project Local Committee and PARC team plays a role in ensuring the access to information to all community members through a clear gender sensitive project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and PARC’s social media platforms. PARC team develops a well-defined selection criterion that responds to the needs of the targeted population and ensures accessing the most vulnerable( Children, PwDs, elderly, women headed HH… etc). This will be hand on hand with the project local committee. An open call of applications will also ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ownership, transparency, good governance, gender balance at beneficiary and community level. Weekly meetings will be held between the municipality, project local committee, community members and PARC field officer for each community. PARC accountability system follows a clear mechanism of complaint registration, reporting and solving, is accessible by the municipality and local community members. Regular follow-up of complaints received from communities, VCs, and Municipality are performed with PARC’s M&E unit, including when necessary, addressing OCHA for larger external complaints concerning security and overall occupation related to protection risks. Meaningful access, participation, and empowerment: through the established LCs which ensure optimal outreach to affected and vulnerable target groups. Additionally, PARC will share any human rights-based violations to relevant clusters. Special attention will also be given to people’s access to services under restrictions due to Covid19 outbreak, and online consultations and virtual meetings will be available to targeted beneficiaries to communicate their challenges and voice these needs/rights, which PARC will ensure sharing with relevant stakeholders.
