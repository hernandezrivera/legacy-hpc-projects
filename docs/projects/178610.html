Project Summary

The project is divided into five main components:
first. Support six OTPs/TSFPs:
This is to ensure continuity and increased coverage of services for treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) among children aged 6-59 months; and to maintain continuity and increased coverage of services for management of acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women (PLW).
second. Support fifteen RRTs:
to maintain increasing delivery of life-saving nutrition services in high needs and remote locations through including screening and referral, distribution micro-nutrients supplements, and providing Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E) consultations.
For SAM cases without complications, the RRTs will provide screening, treatment and follow up, and referring the complicated cases to SCs to ensure the complete of CMAM program cycle.
RRTs and OTPs Approach:
- CMAM & IYCF programs :
1.Screening, treatment, referral and follow up children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished
2.Provide emergency IYCF counselling for mothers with moderately malnourished children
3.Distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women
4.Disseminate messages on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices,
5.Monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes
6.Support health/nutrition education and promotion through IEC materials in line with cluster recommendations during COVID 19 pandemic,
7.Nutrition Supplements distribution
8.Crafting innovative approaches to nutritional counselling, including supporting mothers in breastfeeding infants, through telehealth and mobile phone services;
9.Integrating nutrition supplementation with other routine child and antenatal services.
third. Capacity building
the employees who received the necessary training will be more able to perform in their jobs.
Most of the health and nutrition workers had some weaknesses in their technical skills.
Those training programs will be a good opportunity for them to strengthen those skills and refresh their information the thing will reflect on the quality of the services delivered .
in the field of IYCF & CMAM
fourth. Barrier analysis and surveys
A Barrier Analysis (BA) will used after identifying behaviours reported from affected people on health and nutrition practices that are preventing a target group from adopting a preferred behaviour.
The approach involves a cross-sectional survey, carried out among a sample of 45 “Doers” (those who practice the behaviour) and 45 “Non-Doers” (those who do not), for a total of 90 participants for each behaviour.
fifth. MIYCN through mother and child friendly spaces
The model fits within an Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF) intervention and not only caters for mothers and children under 24 months, but for women of reproductive age and children up to 59 months (if there is not Children in Emergencies (CiE) program to meet their needs) and their families.
• Providing adequate nutrition services (OTP/TSFP/IYCF-E/BSFP) for women, children and adolescents girls
• Support for pregnant women (ANC-PNC-Family planning)
• Promotion of good health and hygiene
• Support for young mothers and families
• Family planning, emergency contraception and support to survivors of gender-based violence (GBV)
• Identify and refer cases of health danger signs (C-IMCI/ICCM).
• Provision of a safe recreational space that benefits women and infants
• Encouraging men to support women’s and infant’s health and nutrition
• Psychosocial Support to families
• Information and support around protection issues
• Support to adolescents (girls and boys with priority to girls)

4 - Needs

As the Syria crisis enters its tenth year, the scale, severity, and complexity of humanitarian needs remain extensive. This is the result of continued hostilities in localized areas, new and protracted displacement, and sustained erosion of communities’ resilience during more than nine years of crisis. Syria is still one of the biggest and most complex crises globally. The Syrian crisis created more than six million displaced Syrians inside their own country.COVID-19 has aggravated the situation further and according to the (HNAP-COVID-19 Vulnerability Map), out of the total population of 4,337,914 in northwest Syria there are 2,920,717 individuals at high COVID -19 risk, and most of the sub-districts in NWS have an Insufficiency of the COVID-19 health services.According to population figures August 2020 the project catchment population is 187500 and percentage of IDPs is 57% and those locations are high priority according to SCHF 2021 First Standard Allocation Paper.During IHR/AFAQ geo-prioritization exercise, based on a recent nutrition surveillance report in March 2021 and after coordination with Nutrition cluster we found: the average of GAM rate is 5.4% with some pockets reach to to 4.7-7.6% in Maaret Tamsrin, Qourqeena, Darkosh, Janudiyeh, Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Kafr Takharim, Atareb, Daret Azza maternal malnutrition average is 21.2 % in the same pockets (18.1%-30.6%), (MAD) for Children U2 average is 23.4%, as well as the prevalence more than 30%.Malnourished children have weakened immune systems and may face a greater risk of dying from COVID-19. Coverage of other essential nutrition services for children and women may also be affected as a result of COVID-19 transmission mitigation efforts.
