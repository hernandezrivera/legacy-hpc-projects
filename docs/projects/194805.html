Project Summary

The outbreak of heavy fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) on Saturday April 15 in Khartoum and in other locations throughout the country, in particular in North and South Darfur comes during a proposed transition to civilian rule. As of today 27 May 2023, over 822 people have been killed, and more than 6,800 injured, according to the Sudan medical association. The ongoing fighting is exacerbating an already difficult humanitarian situation as well the already existing discrimination and violence against women and girls. Different kinds and types of incidences such as sexual and gender-based violence, missing and commercial sex by forces are already being reported in Saraf Umra, El Fasher and Kabkabiya. The humanitarian activities have been interrupted in many states due to widespread insecurity and looting. However, women, youth and NGOs have immediately organized themselves and are delivering humanitarian assistance. Humanitarian spaces and government premises, hospitals, universities, and private businesses have been subject to looting, severely impacting the delivery of life-saving humanitarian aid to vulnerable people.
Kabkabiya and Saraf Umra are under rule of RSF and they are forcing people to pay money or millet for them for their security services otherwise you will be arrested or killed. The communications between native administrative leaders already started and the issue has been discussed and agreed to decrease these kinds of activities. All types of violence occur against people in El Fasher, Kabkabiya and Saraf Umra. Large number of missing people, large number of people affected physically and mentally special women and children, people with special needs not able to move from place to other place to rescue him/herself and girls used for commercial sex.
The intervention will rescue missing people (people separated from their families due to the war including people with special needs, children, girls and women) and recover physically and mentally affected people and return them to their families, then provide some cloths and food items to them and provide counseling till they recover well, delivery of 65 group base psychosocial/emotional support sessions to 1,950 affected people in El Fasher, Kabkabiya and Saraf Umra to recover them from trauma including the rescued people, individual sessions will be conducted according the cases. Dissemination of UN regulation 1325 which prevents Gender Based Violence in emergency situation, to raise the awareness of community specially the leaders of SAF and RSF in Kabkabiya and Saraf Umra through their native administrative leaders who are working as ceasefire committee in North Darfur currently, radio programs and drama will be used for awareness raising. Provision of a platform for networking amongst organizations and services to provide linkages to support systems which facilitate the rehabilitation of people in need of care and protection to enable them gain confidence, and self-esteem. This platform will be the DAID 24 hours telephone outreach emergency system and What sap group, This telephone outreach system receive all kinds of calls and messages from affected people. These calls can be broadly classified into Intervention /Assistance Calls, Medical Assistance calls, Abuse/Rescue and missing calls, Legal Assistance calls, Psychosocial/Emotional Support and Guidance calls, Referral to other Services calls or remnants of war calls. DAID will direct the caller to the right existing services base on his/her need. Capacitation of existing community networks to support DAID and protection cluster in sharing information and supporting DAID in organizing all protection activities in project locations. Awareness through press and electronic media. Women to women, teacher to community, community to community, child to community approach will be followed in order to spread the awareness in public. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The key protection risks related to the project activities will be assessed and relevant protection mainstreaming measures will be implemented. The fulfillment of this indicator means that a project competently assessed the key protection risks connected to the project activities and then designed and effectively implemented relevant protection mainstreaming measures that minimize the risks. Determine the degree to which the indicator is fulfilled by using the following methodology:1) Use review of documents and interviews with key informants (e.g. the project staff) to assess whether protection risks related to the project activities (e.g. in-kind distributions, trainings) were identified. If they were identified, assess how they were identified and whether the assessment considered all four areas of protection mainstreaming: - prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm - ensuring meaningful access - ensuring accountability - ensuring participation and empowerment Consider also assessing whether the overview of key risks was updated in the course of the project, as it is possible that new risks emerged and/or some became more significant. A similar update should then be done for designing and implementing relevant measures. 2) If the risks related to project activities were identified in a comprehensive manner, use a review of documents and key informant interviews (e.g. with the project staff) to assess whether relevant measures were designed to mitigate the risks. Consider whether the measures were designed for the vast majority or for all the identified risks.3) If relevant protection mainstreaming measures were designed, use a review of documents and key informant interviews to assess the extent to which they were implemented. The indicator is met when the assessment concludes that the key risks were comprehensively assessed, and that relevant measures were designed and adequately implemented. As there are no defined standards of what comprehensive / relevant / adequate means, the assessment needs to be done by an independent person experienced in protection mainstreaming. The person needs to justify any conclusions s/he makes.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

DAID has made Accountability to Affected People (AAP) a priority in its Strategic Plan 2021 – 2024. In June 2020, DAID adopted an institutional strategy and action plan for scaling up AAP. Its scope covers the whole organization in its all locations. It is based on two fundamental premises: first, progress will be achieved only through a systematic and coherent organization-wide approach that is embedded in existing organizational processes and systems; second, AAP must not be a stand-alone concept but rather be an integral part of good quality programming in development and humanitarian situations. Accountability to those we serves has always been a part of our work in championing the rights of vulnerable people as expressed in many policy commitments. DAID’s approach to accountability builds on the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) definition of AAP as: “An active commitment to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the people humanitarian organizations seek to assist”. DAID is also guided by the IASC’s five Commitments on Accountability to Affected People/ Populations (CAAPs) and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) which sets out nine accountability commitments which places: “Communities and people affected by crisis at the centre of humanitarian action and promotes respect for their fundamental human rights. It is underpinned by the right to life with dignity, and the right to protection and security as set forth in international law.
