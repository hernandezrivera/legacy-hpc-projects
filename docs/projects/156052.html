Project Summary

The collapse of the health system in northern Syria (Aleppo and Idlib governorates) has pervaded every component of the healthcare continuum and has resulted in the breakdown
of the availability of human resources, pharmaceuticals, vaccinations, and medical supplies and equipment. Availability of health services in northern Syria is ensured
mostly by NGOs. Despite this, health continues to have acute problems including lack of health systems structures, poor articulation of emergency and referral systems
and poor articulation of efforts among primary and secondary levels.
The situation is even more constrained due to the limited number of humanitarian organizations that can operate in the area. the severe gaps in healthcare provision and
the limited response of agencies operating in the Northern Syria (the Euphrates shield, Olive branch and rural Edlib) areas calls for an improved response based on PHC strengthening as well as
the secondary health care services.
The Proposed project aims to respond to immediate emergency requirements for most vulnerable communities as well as cover the gaps in the health system to avoid any
future collapsing in the system.
The proposed project will cover the salaries and running costs of the following facilities in the following target locations in order to improve access to health care services.
Primary health care based on the updated Essential Health Service Package agreed in the health cluster for northern Syria
IDA 12 PHCs Level 2 in each of Aleppo and Idlib governorates
The proposed project will cover the health needs of both male and female, with a special focus on children, women and elderly.

Needs

Across Syria health-related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to healthcare due to a combination of damage and destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources, and imposed access constraints. These latter constraints constitute a particular barrier to healthcare in hard-to-reach areas and those with high severity scores, including areas that recently witnessed changes of control. According to the WHO Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA), 137 targeted attacks on health care took place in Syria during the first 10 months of the year, resulting in a reported 97 deaths and 165 injuries , compared to 122 verified attacks in 2017, reportedly resulting in 73 deaths and 149 injuries . Both these attacks and resource constraints impact the functionality of health facilities. The June 2018 Health Resource Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) data indicated that across Syria (48) per cent of health facilities are either non-functional or partially functional, with (173) facilities completely destroyed, and a further (402) facilities partially destroyed and in need of rehabilitation. The national ratio of fully functional Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCs) of 2.25 per 50,000 population is above Sphere Standards, but the distribution is highly unequal, with 107 of 271 surveyed sub-districts reporting having no fully functional PHC, and an additional 9 sub-districts not meeting minimum standards. Nationally, one functional hospital bed is available per 1,000 population, which is equivalent to Sphere standards but low when measured against a global average of 3 per 1,000 population. As for PHCs, availability of and access to hospital services is not distributed evenly across the country. Attacks on health as well as population movements have contributed to the depletion of human capital engaged in healthcare provision, especially with regards specialized services. Across the country, the availability of doctors, nurses and midwives in the public sector is below internationally recommended standards, with only 2.45 health staff per 1,000 population available, against the Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) minimum standard of 4.45 per 1,000 population . A recent assessment showed that in 127 out of the 267 sub-districts surveyed, female respondents mentioned the unavailability of female midwives and/or gynecologists in health facilities as a serious problem faced by women in their community . In the whole of northern Syria, approximately 50 gynecologists serve a population of about 4 million people. Active hostilities also restrict access by creating fear, as evidenced by responses to a household survey in one of the governorates. When asked about unmet health needs, almost half of the respondents reported fear of moving outside of their homes as a main barrier to accessing health services .The combination of rapid onset displacement, protracted displacement, as well as rapid shifts in areas of control has put existing health service provision under significant strain. This has been exacerbated by restrictions on access as well as unreliable availability of financial, material and human resources, which have prevented health partners from rapidly scaling up the response.

Protection Risk Analysis

This project proposal has been designed on the basis of needs assessments consisting of interviews with key informants within the target population.The project staff will be hired whenever possible from the target area itself, ensuring intimate knowledge of the context and good acceptability of the staff by the community.IDA has an incorporated beneficiaries feedback into their existing feedback system, through suggestion boxes at each PHC, which are collected and reviewed by the M&E field officer on monthly basis. The M&E Officer will obtain direct patient feedback during the monitoring process. All patients feedback will be registered in complaints tracking sheet, the tracking sheet will easily show the statues for each complaint (such as category, time cycle, decision, extra), the beneficiary will get the result of his/her complaint if possible( since the anonymous complaints are acceptable). IDA team are trained on service provision according to humanitarian principles, as well as, IDA will implement meetings with camps management, Local Council and community leaders as well as with Health Directorate to know the affected population suggestions and needs to improve the services provided. Prohibition of Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse (PSEA)IDA continues to stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct. Therefore, IDA and the contracted sub-partners are obliged to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Managers at all levels have a particular responsibility to support and develop systems that maintain this environment. The partner should ensure that systems are in place to collect and effectively manage feedback from communities
