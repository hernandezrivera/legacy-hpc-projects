Project Summary

The socio-political crisis in the NW and SW regions of Cameroon, now in its sixth year, characterized by continued fighting between State security forces (SSF) and non-State armed groups (NSAG, targeted attacks, destruction of property, arbitrary arrest, kidnappings for ransom, extortion of money and continued use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs) (OCHA, 2022), has left more than 1.08 million people from NW, SW, LIT and West region in need of humanitarian assistance (HNO, 2022). More especially, the insufficient investment of Cameroon Government in basic social services, poverty and limited response provided by Humanitarian actor in the Northwest and South west regions continue to exacerbate the water and sanitation need of affected population including neighboring regions (Littoral and West regions). The crisis has had serious negative effects on water, hygiene and sanitation with mostly children, women and girls highly affected, as they are the ones who often access WASH facilities. The situation further compounded with the public health crisis of COVID 19, cholera outbreak and monkey pox which have hit the already crisis affected communities. In September 2022 SitRep, Out of 12 517 cases and 254 deaths reported at national level, the South-West region represent 48% of cases (6017) and 36% (93) of deaths, marking 2022 as the second most affected year by cholera for the past 25 years with more than 13,000 cases and 284 deaths of cholera reported (HNO, 2023). The risk factors are still much evident in these affected regions such as lack safe of access to safe water, sanitation facilities (latrines) and poor hygiene practices that poses a thread to the lives of these vulnerable children and displaced families. It is therefore the responsibility of humanitarian actors and service providers to scale up humanitarian response and delivery of life saving humanitarian needs and services to the affected persons in most insecure and inaccessible locations. It is within this framework that this project in its response will prioritize the safety and dignity of the targeted children, women and girls, as well as affected host population and will reduce the risk and exposure to gender-based violence and other violence related to accessing WASH facilities. Our project will provide WASH humanitarian assistance through all modalities with specificity to 6500 individuals directly utilizing improve water supply service with systematic control of water quality at household level, 1750 households will directly utilize improve sanitation facilities through the support and construction of Eco-friendly boifil latrines, and the provision of latrine kits to displaced families and vulnerable host communities. 15,625 Individuals reached with community sensitization messaging on critical handwashing and good hygiene practices through information and communication materials, door-to-door and cholera tools to efficiently address cholera risk factors and other possible outbreaks associated to poor hygiene practices. 18750 individuals will benefits complete WASH kits through all modalities, 1875 vulnerable adolescent girls and women within reproductive age will be reached with menstrual hygiene kits. 600 beneficiaries will be trained on how to access humanitarian assistance as well as capacitated on how to provide feedback and complains to humanitarian actors. The adoption of community based approached will be the central response strategy through consultation and engagements of all relevant stakeholders and community groups for an impactful, dignify and sustainable WASH service delivery to the vulnerable affected population. For a more responsible intervention that respect the dignity of beneficiaries, the project will mainstream accountability to affected population (AAP) within the response from project inception to the exit strategy and ensure humanitarian principle are respected at all stage of the intervention.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The insecurity in the NWSW has trigger multiple displacement and most displaced persons in the West and Littoral regions lack meaningful and inclusive access to WASH facilities and services, because lots of humanitarian support target mostly in the North West and South West, while a greater population of the displaced persons in the Littoral and West are not reached with WASH support in humanitarian WASH response services. Moreover, as of 2022, out of all humanitarian aids provided to the affected population, only 3.31 per cent of beneficiaries were provided safe drinking water and basic sanitation, as a result of multiple challenges at the funding and implementation levels. Most people reached are from the host population (62 per cent), IDP (25 per cent) and returnees (13 per cent) (OCHA 2022). This report also indicated that multiple displacements across the NWSW caused mainly by violence with over 7,212 persons were reportedly displaced to nearby bushes and villages, with Manyu (SW) and Bui (NW) divisions reported as the most affected. Most of the displacements are temporary, as displaced persons return to their homes once the security situation allows although return to discover all their properties burned or seized by armed men. This demonstrating the need for more urgent, adequate and inclusive humanitarian assistance to the affected population. Crucial needs in terms of handwashing facilities are reported in Manyu division, but little or no significant activities are implemented in these areas because of administrative constraints, insecurity, and insufficient funds as well as public health challenges. Addressing the root causes of cholera outbreak, monkeypox and other WASH related diseases coupled with suffering due to the crisis will need sufficient access to safe drinking water and sanitation services. awareness raising as an easy, effective, and affordable way to prevent disease and save lives. Our project will provide humanitarian assistance through innovative approach in tackling the root cause of some public crisis due poor access to safe water and sanitation service. The project will support 9852 vulnerable individuals with sustainable access to improve and safe drinking water with systematic control of water quality at household level through the construction/rehabilitation of borehole with solar powered system or electric pump system, water supply system powered by gravity depending on the specificity of assessed communities and the rehabilitation/construction of stand taps. To address the issue difficult access to safe and improved sanitation services, 2400 men, women, boys and girls will benefit and directly utilize improved sanitation facilities through the support of vulnerable families with the construction of Eco-friendly biofil latrines which is less costly, requires limited space, environmentally friendly and make use of local materials. 20500 Individuals will be reached through community awareness raising campaign by community health workers to sensitized the local population on critical handwashing and good hygiene practices through the distribution of information and communication materials, door-door sensitization, community adapted tools to address cholera risk factors and possible outbreaks related to poor hygiene and sanitation practices . 13375 men, women, boys and girls will benefit from complete WASH kits through all modalities, 1875 vulnerable women and adolescent girls will be reach with menstrual hygiene kits,80 water user committee will be trained while 40 water user committees will be established and redynamised to effectively managed water infrastructure . The project will be implemented in the South West ( Fako (Muyuka, Limbe I, Limbe II, West Cost, and Buea), Manyu (Manfe, Akwaya, and Eyoumojock), Meme (Kumba 1, Kumba II, and Konye), Ndian (Bamusso, and Idabato) Kupemanueguba ( Tombel)) and Littoral (Douala 4, Mbanga, Loum subdivisions).
