Project Summary

Through this project, DRC aims to work alongside local authorities and affected communities to address protection and meet basic needs in food security and livelihood, shelter and non-food items and HMA. DRC will be targeting Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) host communities and returnees in Central Darfur, Khartoum, and South Kordofan. In addition to direct and immediate humanitarian assistance, DRC remains committed to supporting communities in achieving durable solutions following years of conflict and displacement. Recognizing that durable solutions are not something that can be achieved in isolation, DRC’s strategy in Sudan is to build partnerships around durable solutions projects, working closely to offer holistic opportunities on durable returns, by assisting displaced and returnee communities.
Throughout its interventions, DRC works closely with national humanitarian actors and relevant Government of Sudan ministries to ensure that the activities planned are in line with government broader standards and plans as well as community needs, and that local duty bearers have the capacity to build upon the integrated program that DRC will implement and sustain its positive impacts.

1 - Needs

The contextual analysis underpinning the proposed DRC (Danish Refugee Council) 2022 Integrated Response (multi-sector) program for Sudan draws primarily on external sources such as: the Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 and Humanitarian Needs Overview, sector/cluster reports, which DRC cross-references with its internal sources including: DRC Sudan Strategic Program Document for 2021-2022, regular situation reports, rapid assessments, etc. Sudan is classified among the least developed countries. In 2021, an estimated 13.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Humanitarian needs in Sudan are not limited to conflict-affected areas. Sudan faces competing humanitarian crises and formidable development challenges, leaving millions highly vulnerable and, at a broader level, threatening the country’s stability. IOM DTM data from August 2021 has concluded that there is a cumulative presence of “3,036,593 IDPs, 969,397 permanent returnees from internal displacement, 141,495 seasonal returnees, 92,696 returnees from abroad, and 399,946 foreign nationals currently residing in Sudan.” In Central Darfur, South Kordofan especially but also in Khartoum, a strong protection need is observed. In Central Darfur and South Kordofan, the scarcity of protection actors and functional national services providers is critical. In Khartoum, a host state for large numbers of refugees and migrants, the resources available to address their complex needs fall far short of what is needed to mitigate the protection risks and vulnerabilities that result from a chronic lack of access to services and difficulties in receiving adequate assistance and integration. The Sudanese population’s ability to cope with shock is severely undermined by the protracted economic crisis, which has caused hyperinflation and reduced purchasing power nationwide. Protests are occurring across the country as people are pushed into further financial distress. Extensive known and suspected minefields and unexploded ordnance restricts access for commu-nities in the affected states to land and basic services including health care, education, safe water sources and livelihoods, and has been a driver of forced displacement and intra- and inter-communal tensions for decades. Over 2,100 casualties from mines and ERW have been recorded, including 623 people killed and 1,548 injured, between 2002 and 2019 , although the true extent of accidents caused by mines and explosive ordnance is expected to be much higher due to un-der-reporting, particularly in remote and non-government-controlled areas. Sudan’s mine action program was nationalized in 2013, however, the political transition that followed the 2019 revolu-tion, and the national priorities to establish peace and enable economic recovery signal the time for enhanced and coordinated mine action efforts. Equipping NGOs with response capacity in order to respond high FSL, S/NFI, HMA and protection needs remains critical in 2022.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In 2022, DRC Sudan will focus on four sectors (based on OCHA’s coordination clusters in Sudan) these are: Protection, Food Security & Livelihoods (FSL), Shelter & non-food items (SNFI), Humanitarian Mine Action (HMA). With regards to the protection program, DRC focusses on 3 main program pillars: community-based protection/PSS, case management, and protection monitoring, and includes activities which identify, mitigate and respond to protection needs and incidents. The GBV response will focus on establishing and/or rehabilitate women centers to complement wider protection programming with community outreach for awareness and sensitization and to provide individual case management services. DRC will select the locations of the space based on need and priorities of communities. In addition, DRC DRCs structures CBPN at the local community level, such as village or neighborhood, as opposed to at a larger locality level, thus increasing the buy-in and participation of local communities and taking into account local dynamics in a conflict sensitive manner. Dignity kit distribution is not limited to distribution but is coupled with sharing information on GBV, available GBV services, GBV helplines numbers, and messages on PSEA. Dignity kits distribution is entry point to raising awareness and reaching women and girls. In order to respond to food security and livelihood needs, food and in kind distribution will be provided as well as agricultural support depending on the needs of each area. In Central Darfur, S/NFI kits will be distributed to respond to recent population displacement. Finally, an HMA action if foreseen in South Kordofan, state with highest landmine contamination as well as in Central Darfur where old and new battlefields are contaminated by UXOs and landmines. HMA activities are anticipated to utilize the capacities of non-technical survey and multi-task teams to implement activities such as manual mine clearance, battle area clearance, and explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) to supported thousands of individuals in contaminated areas. Collectively the integrated intervention proposed will make a significant contribution to reducing humanitarian needs in line with HRP objectives.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Shelter and NFI distribution can have an environmental impact as many materials contain single-use plastics. DRC will continue to look into sourcing more local and environmentally friendly NFI and shelter materials whenever possible. In addition, DRC will seek to source locally available food assistance to avoid the environmental impact of importation and transportation over long distances.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing projects, DRC develops activities and selection criteria based on a context specific risk analysis and continued engagement with local communities. All activities aredesigned in consideration of the risks and vulnerabilities of local communities and targetbeneficiaries, and include components aimed at mitigating risk factors and strengtheningprotective factors in the community. Throughout implementation, DRC will conductongoing protection monitoring in order to identify new risks and vulnerabilities and assessthe impact of our programming on the protection environment. While DRC has thesestructures in place, it will spend the beginning of 2022 providing training andcapacity building opportunities for all staff around protection mainstreaming to ensurethat there is not only a culture of protection mainstreaming but also the capacity tomainstream protection effectively.
