Project Summary

The project’s overall objective is to enhance responses towards the education of children with and without disabilities in emergencies.
This project falls under Education Objective 1 - Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children. Education Cluster activities: Provide essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that school environments are safe for students. And Supporting conflict affected school children with disabilities with assistive devices, appropriate transportation, specialized learning materials, or adaptations to school infrastructure. The project aims at responding to the urgent need of children with disability (CWDs) in terms of their access to schools. The project will provide assistive devices to CWDs who face difficulties in accessing to school and whose educational achievement is negatively impacted by the unavailability of assistive devices. The assistive devices will include, hearing aids, glasses, etc.
A total of 678 hearing aids will be provided to 339 children with hearing loss enrolled in public and special education schools.
Education Objective 2 - Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school-based psychosocial services. Education Cluster activities: Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by the education-related violations. And Provision of remedial education to vulnerable school aged children (for example, students suffering from home arrest, injury or ex-detainees, Children with Disability, IDPs children, children at risk of dropout)

Needs

According to the Deaf or Hard of Hearing (D/HH) Survey by (ASDC, 2016), in the Gaza Strip there are about 3200 school age children who are DHH receiving education services through different approaches. 35% of them are having hearing loss that is more than severe which is make it difficult to be included in regular classrooms and the rest of them are having hearing losses that are less severe and with hearing aids perform better in mainstreaming classes. Children who are profoundly deaf are receiving educational services at special schools for the deaf where they mainly relay on signed language and/or total communication. Due to the late detection of hearing loss and the difficult economic situation, families can’t afford fitting their children with the need assistive devices that could help them improve their language. Therefore, it become so difficult to include them in mainstreaming classes due to their poor language and social communication skills. The latest research (ASDC, 2018) of the number of children who have mild to severe hearing loss and are included in regular schools showed that 1200 children with hearing loss are enrolled in the schools (estimated). Children who are already at schools and are in need for hearing aids (127 Female, 169 Male) with age (6-18), the children who are at 5 deaf schools and ready to be included in regular schools (19 Female, 21 Male) with age (7-14), and the children who are in kindergartens and special schools (25 Female, 18 Male) with age (5-7). 62% of children are not having suitable amplification systems. This lack of the suitable amplification system, the classroom environments, the lack of awareness and the acceptance of the DHH children has dramatical negative impact on their performance in the school setting. It is obvious that the key to the success of the DHH children in the regular school setting is the use of suitable amplification systems which helps improving their social interaction and academic performance.Education Cluster identified 303,607 children (149,878 girls and 153,729 boys) with 12,619 education staff adults are in need of school-based psychosocial support, 467,394 children (241,554 girls and 238,459 boys) in need of emergency education supplies and 24,389 children (12,121 girls and 12,268 boys) need remedial education to prevent dropout and ensure access to educationThe already dire living conditions for children across the Gaza Strip further deteriorated since 30th of March 2018. Children participating in these protests are particularly exposed to violence: according to the Ministry of Health, 2,947 children (the majority are boys) were injured, comprising 19 per cent of total injured as of 30 June, 2018 . Many children are deeply traumatized from injuries and distressed at having witnessed rest-hand egregious injuries on family and community members. Mental Health and psychosocial (PSS) issues resulting from this exposure to violence, contribute to children experiencing learning difficulties and falling behind in school. Children with Disabilities (CWDs), including children injured during the current March of Return events, are the most affected by the deteriorating situation, as many of them lack basic assistive devices that would facilitate their learning in the classroom. Others have suffered injuries that may keep them hospitalized or with a permanent disability where they will require remedial education support. According to the 2018 MoEHE statistic (Number of CwDs within MoEHE schools) , there are 3062 children with disabilities (1481 F, 1581 M) with age (6-17).

Activities

Physical rehabilitations will be designed for each school based upon current facility set up. Assistive devices will be selected and distributed to meet individual children’s needs, based on age, gender and disability. - Conduct physical rehabilitation and adaptations for 15 MoEHE schools in the most vulnerable communities across the Gaza Strip to be more friendly, inclusive and protective for children mainly CwDs. -Provide 678 hearing aids will be provided to 339 children with hearing loss enrolled in public and special education schools. (127 Female, 169 Male) with age (6-18), and (25 Female, 18 Male) with age (5-7). -Provision of assistive devices for children with visual disabilities in need in 15 MoEHE schools. - Conduct of catch up/remedial classes in Arabic and Math to school children (including injured children from March of Return demonstrations which have movement restrictions, distress symptoms or learning difficulties) for the duration of two school semesters in 15 MoEHE schools, and home visits for the injured children from March of Return demonstrations which have movement restrictions.- Provide structured PSS activities including singing, drama, dancing, painting and story-telling) to boys, girls including injured children from March of Return demonstrations and their caregivers in 15 MoEHE schools and 5 special schools.- Conduct teachers stress management workshops for male and female teachers in 20 schools, which are facing high pressure environments working with large class sizes, stress management trainings will support teachers to deal with their own stress, anxieties and pressures, as well as to best support children.-Conduct training on INEE Minimum Standards for Education: Preparedness, and Response, for male and female teachers in 20 schools.
