Project Summary

The protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria entered into its thirteenth year in 2023. The situation in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States continues to deteriorate as the effects of shrinking humanitarian space and increase in needs because of other natural disasters including flooding, further displacements and disease outbreaks . There are significant and severe humanitarian needs across Nigeria, caused by poverty – further deepened by high rates of inflation – lack of access to basic services, weak rule of law, widespread insecurity and criminality, and the impact of climate change. Four population groups are identified as in need of humanitarian and protection assistance across the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states: (i) internally displaced persons (IDPs) living in camps or camp-like-settings; (ii) out-of-camp IDPs living in host communities; (iii) returnees; and (iv) host communities. The latter also comprises communities affected by conflict that may not necessarily host IDPs or returnees.
More than 239,000 people were affected following severe flash flooding in the MMC and Jere LGAs in Maiduguri after the Alau Dam in neighbouring Konduga LGA breached its banks on the 9th of September 2024, following structural damage and continued heavy rains in Bama, Damboa and Gwoza LGAs. In Maiduguri, the most affected areas include Gwange, Bama Road, Maiduguri Zoo, Post office, State Secretariat, Lagos Street, Shehu’s palace, the Maiduguri Main Market, Customs / Gamboru areas, Budum, Specialist Hospital and Post Office general area. Government authorities evacuated residents in high-risk riverine areas to several locations into 6 camps with most of the IDP households being settled in Ali Sheriff, Bakasi Camp, Yerwa GGSS, Dalori, Teachers Village Flood IDP Camp and Asheik Jarma Primary School. The Rapid Response Mechanism, following coordination with OCHA and the AAWG conducted rapid needs assessment to understand the immediate needs of the affected households settled in the camps. The RNA was conducted for between the 11th and 12th of September 2024 using a household questionnaire and WASH checklist in the 4 camps of Ali Sheriff, Bakasi Camp, Yerwa GGSS and Asheik Jarma Primary School.
A nationwide cholera outbreak remains a major public health concern across Nigeria. As of mid-October 2024, the country had recorded over 14,000 suspected cholera cases, resulting in a death toll of 378, with an average case fatality rate of 2.7 per cent. This is according to the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC). The northern states of Borno, Adamawa, Jigawa, Yobe, and Kano are the epicentres of the outbreak. The incidence of cholera has been exacerbated by flooding and inadequate water and sanitation infrastructure.
The shrinking humanitarian space according to European Commission is largely due to deteriorating security and restrictions imposed by the government and mistrust towards humanitarian actors. The Covid-19 pandemic has made WASH needs and response more complex because of existing vulnerabilities in BAY States. Self-organized and government-led movement of people, (new displacements and returns) have constrained development of durable WASH solutions as the outlook constantly changes. On the other hand, protracted displacement is straining host communities because of the high pressure on the existing, already-overburdened water points and sanitation facilities.
According to the Nigeria Situation Report dated 28 Oct 2024, over 300 people were reported as dead with another 1.2 million affected by floods in 33 states in Nigeria. In Borno State, humanitarian partners have reported that recent flash floods in Dikwa LGA have severely disrupted humanitarian operations in Dikwa, Ngala and Rann areas. Floodwater reportedly cut off the primary access route between Ajiri and Dikwa, in the Bakassi area, stranding humanitarian convoys and essential supplies in Ajiri town.

1 - Needs

Year 2023 had five themes endorsed as the key drivers of the humanitarian crisis: conflict and displacement, food insecurity and malnutrition, disease outbreaks (including cholera), floods, and camp closures and involuntary relocations and resettlement . As part of the 2023 Humanitarian response plan (HRP) for Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states, the funding appeal by United Nations and partners for 2023 is US$ 1.31 billion to respond to the critical needs of 8.3 million people needing life-saving humanitarian assistance . Access to sanitation is fragile from wear and tear of emergency-type construction, and seasonal flooding. WASH needs in the Adamawa and Borno states entail water supply in form of deep boreholes, sanitation and hygiene sensitization. WASH NFIs are also part of the humanitarian needs especially for the population affected and displaced by floods in Yobe states. Low sanitation access raises rates of open defecation and reduced handwashing elevates the risk of a major outbreak within the susceptible community. A critical gap for the sector is sanitation for persons with special needs, women and children to ensure safety and equitable access; solving these issues of proximity and design of the facilities is likely to require additional resources OCHA, 2021. Effects of extreme weather conditions have worsened the situation. Local markets are also facing acute shortages of essential commodities as commercial vendors are unable to restock their shops due to the impassable roads.According to the UN-OCHA NIGERIA Situation Report updated Nigeria last updated on 14 Mar 2024 the humanitarian community has identified approximately 2,500,000 are in need of WASH assistance with a projected $87.2M funding gap required.

2 - Activities

To contribute to the overall WASH objective and ensure populations affected by displacement have reduced risk of exposure to WASH related diseases, NRC in collaboration with local actors will provide the full WASH package for both hardware and software approaches required for access to safe water, sanitation, hygiene promotion whilst contributing to behaviour change to over 120,000 beneficiaries. Primarily target areas are Borno and Adamawa States through standalone WASH interventions alongside complementary WASH interventions. The WASH response will be based on identified needs and vulnerability criterion. In cases where scaling up WASH intervention is extremely challenging due to conflict, congestion, space limitation, physical access and terrain, priorities for the WASH will be to reach those disproportionately affected, reduce vulnerability, build resilience by including preparedness and contingency planning for WASH disease outbreaks, flood and other identified risks. NRC will implement according to Sphere standards, sector guidelines and applicable Nigerian Standards such as, the 2007 Nigerian Standard for Drinking Water Quality. Interventions will include both programmatic and advocacy efforts to address the needs, potential access constraints and protection. NRC WASH during this period will use this opportunity to enhance its focus on durable solutions by strengthening integrated programming, ensuring that the responses clearly establish viable links between relief, recovery and development. There will be focus on developing durable WASH programming in areas that are secure and conditions are conducive. NRC WASH will also focus on opportunities for innovative WASH and ensure scale-up and replication in other locations where possible. This will be linked to research and impact monitoring. Implementation will be done in line with NRC Environmental Minimum Standards to ensure at a minimum there is no harm to the environment and the occupants as these forms the basis for sustainability and resilience. A key component of the structure and approach of implementation is safe inclusive programming (SIP). Enhancing knowledge of the community inter alia their different challenges, needs and preferences will form the basis for implementation. Markets Based Approach will be a priority in collaboration with the Livelihood and Food Security (LFS) Core Competency NRC WASH will continue to invest in strengthening knowledge of WASH markets in NE Nigeria year 2025. Market assessments will link to multisector assessments and WASH assessments, and other assessments used in situational analysis. WASH CC will prioritise on possible options including the Water Supply market system, looking at supporting market actors to increase access to clean water for vulnerable, displacement-affected communities. WASH team proposes on assessing the contractors they currently use for the construction of water points and water sources. This assessment will use a market based approach to identify the potential for NRC to offer structured technical guidance to increase quality, efficiency, and management of this traditional water infrastructure. In addition, WASH intervntions will identify positive and sustainable environmental friendly energy sources and water structure models (e.g., elevated water tanking). NRC with the different stakeholders will explore on how to expand these environmental friendly technology to hard-to-reach locations and link this with markets to ensure availability of materials for repairs and maintenance. Moreover, NRC WASH staff will explore how to facilitate linkages to water catchment (e.g. rainwater harvesting) systems to ensure sustainable water supply for agricultural purposes. NRC will use evidence gathered will be used to determine the best modality to reach and provide sufficient, appropriate assistance to beneficiaries

7.1.d - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

In the case of infrastructure inputs including both software and hardware WASH, community members and relevant government officials will be reinforced in their capacities, in order to maintain and repair structures after the project lifespan, and to mitigate the impacts of environmental risks, such as flooding

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Adequate emphasis will be ensured in all WASH programming to ensure privacy, dignity and to consider protection issues for women, men, girls, boys and persons with disabilities in the targeted communities. Provision of WASH facilities in schools for children both for girls and boys will reduce protection risks such as not going out for defecation in open areas wherein they may face abuse, threats, or violence. All the WASH design should be developed in collaboration with community to promote user-centred designs. In the proposed intervention, NRC WASH response will ensure: 1. There is gender equity in recruitment of outreach workers notably WASH committees, Community based workers, hygiene promoters. 2. The four principles of protection mainstreaming are fully integrated into implementation across all sectors (meaningful access to assistance, prioritizing safety, dignity and doing no harm, accountability and participation, empowerment of beneficiaries). 3. WASH response will ensure continuous engagement with members from both host communities and IDPs, adhering to the age, gender and diversity mainstreaming principles. NRC will implement according to Sphere standards, sector guidelines and applicable Nigerian Standards such as, the 2007 Nigerian Standard for Drinking Water Quality. Interventions will include both programmatic and advocacy efforts to address the needs, potential access constraints and protection. NRC WASH during this period will use this opportunity to enhance its focus on durable solutions by strengthening integrated programming, ensuring that the responses clearly establish viable links between relief, recovery and development. There will be focus on developing durable WASH programming in areas that are secure and conditions are conducive. NRC WASH will also focus on opportunities for innovative WASH and ensure scale-up and replication in other locations where possible.NRC shall continue to liaise and closely work together with community and religious leaders to coordinate community engagement, as well as protection analysis and beneficiary selection to ensure that the action benefits the people in need. NRC will ensure adequate community engagement throughout the programme to strengthen an integrated, coordinated, gender-responsive water, hygiene and sanitation programme. Engaging with the users, residents, displaced people motivate them to be active participants in the response rather than passive beneficiaries. It makes interventions more effective, efficient, and sustainable. The WASH intervention’s community engagement will cover four areas: i. Community participation, ii. Community mobilisation including behaviour change interventions,iii. Community management andiv. Accountability.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

WASH team will ensure beneficiaries are informed that services are provided free of charge so that there is no expectation that beneficiaries must pay for services, reducing the risk of transactional sex for services. The feedback complain mechanism will also be strengthened in all field locations with active numbers visibly posted at all service points to report any abuses and misconduct. The MEAL team will conduct random checks in communities to actively inquire about rumors or reports of abuse. WASH intervention will incorporate measures to promote the safety and well-being of project participants considering the needs and protection of women and adolescent girls specifically especially during the distribution of MHM kits. NRC will promote women’s participation in decision-making processes, ensuring seeking their opinion with regards to siting of water and sanitation facilities, including the provision of gender-segregation visibility for shared and communal WASH facilities, integration of risk mitigation messages during hygiene awareness raising, and implementing community-based awareness campaigns on GBV prevention. The NRC has a reporting mechanism that uses dedicated sessions including KI and FGD to sort out the most appropriate ones in each location of operations. The Compliance Team will continue conducting several COC trainings (this includes PSEA, and the Code of Conduct Reporting Mechanism) to staff and casual workers in collaboration with the HR team to ensure suspensions and other investigations can be carried out fast and smoothly through the channels of complaints for further compliance.
