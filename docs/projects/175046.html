Project Summary

The 2017-18 Pakistan Demographic and Health Survey highlights 28% of women experience physical or sexual violence, 34% of ever-married women experience spousal violence, whether physical, sexual, or emotional. For provinces struggling with human insecurity such as Baluchistan, Newly Merged Districts and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, the data are more alarming; respectively 49 %, 66% and 53 % suffer directly from partner violence of all kinds. Although demographic data are missing, programmatic work indicates that the situation is not better for the 1.4 million registered Afghan refugees holding a Government of Pakistan issued Proof of Registration card; of these a third living in 54 refugee villages (32%) and two-thirds (68%) of the registered Afghan refugees and asylum seekers that live in urban or peri-urban areas alongside their Pakistani host communities. An increase of gender-based violence (GBV) victims by 20% due to COVID-19 pandemic has been registered globally. A country already plagued by some of the highest rates of domestic violence and GBV in the world, Pakistan is struggling to respond to increased cases of such violence in the context of managing the COVID-19 outbreak for its 200 million citizens and about 1.4 million refugees mostly found in Baluchistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Approximately 8 million women face some form of GBV in one year in Pakistan. Pakistan is prone to natural disaster and rapid needs assessment conducted for Floods in Sindh province highlighted significant protection sector needs with more women and girls at risk compared to men (including S/GBV) with 56.9 percent in rural and 50 percent in urban areas. In order to respond to this situation, the following activities are proposed;
1. Facilities in targeted districts will be selected through proper assessment to be upgraded and strengthened to provide integrated GBV and SRH services to women and adolescent girls including CMR through trained staff.
2. The selected health facilities will provide access to women and adolescent girls to integrated Mental Health and Psychosocial Support services/ Psychological first aid in health facilities such as RHCs/BHUs including Women (Health) Friendly Spaces. The selected facilities and trained staff will provide case management through multi sectoral referral mechanisms to be established at the district level in coordination with relevant stakeholders.
3. Awareness raising and sensitization sessions in the catchment population will be conducted with women, girls, men and boys in catchment population on different forms of GBV (SRH) and available prevention and response related services. The outreach community sessions will include mobile services including community based psycho social support sessions, referral services to GBV and SRH services, access to people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups such as transgender, women and elderly headed households at the door step. These services will be aimed at development community based protection mechanisms.
4. Public media campaigns on GBV awareness and available services including electronic, social media and IEC material will be carried out to reach out to a large segment of population. Information on GBV will be further linked with COVID-19 and other humanitarian issues.
5. Coordination mechanisms and structures will be established at national, provincial and districts level to establish and strengthen multi sectoral GBV coordination mechanism in close coordination and collaboration with relevant government departments.
6. GBV focused helplines will be established or strengthened with services including remote psycho social support services, referral and remote case management services.
7. The frontline GBV workers and auxiliary staff will be protected against COVID-19 through trainings on IPC and provision of PPEs.
