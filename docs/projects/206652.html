Project Summary

Taking into consideration existing need of IDPs and host communities population affected by conflicts, the project aims at benefitting displaced and host community girls and women living in the displacement districts of Pemba, Ibo, Quissanga, Metuge, Montepuez Balama and Namuno and returns districts of Mocimboa da Praia e Palma thought provision of quality life-saving, prevention actions and protection mainstreaming, actions in General Protection, Child Protection, HLP and GBV.
In line with AVSI vision and mission, the approach will have the people at its centre, where the person is helped to be aware of his or her value and dignity, is a protagonist in his or her own and his or her community's integral development, even in contexts of crisis and emergency. Each project is therefore conceived, in a multisectorial and integrated way, as a tool to promote this awareness in all those involved, has in itself a need for communication and sharing, and exerts an impact capable of generating positive change.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project is fully aligned with the Protection cluster and aim to contribute to reach the following objectives: - Provide quality life-saving services to address inclusive protection immediate risks and needs (including MHPSS, CP, GBV and HLP) of most vulnerable populations in priority areas. - Mitigate protection risks (including MHPSS, CP, GBV and HLP) of most affected and vulnerable populations in priority areas through enhanced preparedness and resilience capacity - Strengthen the protection environment and enhance the protection of affected populations, including related to MHPSS, GBV, child protection and HLP, and through collective and intersectoral action. This project is aimed to implement and improve prevention, case management and referral system to identify and manage cases of vulnerability among children, women and people with special needs in Pemba, Ibo, Quissanga, Metuge, Montepuez Balama and Namuno districts as well as the return areas of Mocimboa da Praia and Palma. The main objective is to strengthen the case identification system, community participation and capacity to respond, as well as coordination between the response services, involving line local authorities and creating a close link with the case workers. They will be also able to provide protection and psycho-social support services for children, adolescent, women, caregivers and communities as well as to other vulnerable individuals as elderly people and people with specific needs. AVSI is already working closely with DPGCAS and SDMAS, using the same identification and reference forms, involving staff in training and holding monthly meetings to coordinate the referral system. This approach is aimed at sharing the work with the local authorities, in order to support and allow them to carry on after the end of the project. At the end of the project the trained activists shall continue supporting their communities and might be supported by the district departments of Acçao Social and Education. While for GP and CP the project will work stongly on case management and first response/service provision, on GBV special focus will be given to vulnerable women and girls, with particular effort in working on root causes of GBV, AVSI will rely on its long experience on urban planning and on providing assistance to resident communities in acquiring the DUAT, to support the displaced population (being them IDPs or returnees) on the HLP assistance based on indication of the local authorities. Will also create capacity on HLP rights to both local leadership and affected communities, primarily in areas of intervention where implements urban or shelter programs, to reinforce an integrated approach. The Action we look to target 63.323 beneficiaries: 20.000 of GP, 18.000 of CP,17.298 of GBV, 7025 HLP.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

• Setting up Animators Groups • Community Awareness and caregiver’s engagement: Parents and community will be made aware about the importance of their commitment and engagement in their children’s wellbeing and how they can contribute, facilitate and improve it • Identification and training of a network of community based volunteers (CBV). In order to encourage the sustainability of the initiative and to foster the appropriation of the essential mechanisms of protection by the target communities, people for each neighbourhood will be selected during the awareness-raising sessions held in the communities by AVSI staff in order to become community based volunteers, playing a role of “antenna” in the community. • PSS training for communities volunteers, Animators and Case Workers: In order to encourage the sustainability of the initiative and to foster the appropriation of the essential mechanisms of protection by the target communities, communities volunteers and caregivers will participate in a specific training aimed at developing their capacity of identification of children and community members in need of psychosocial support and to give them facilitation skills to handle awareness sessions on stress management, positive parenting, positive discipline, child rights and protection and social inclusion in their communities • Recruitment of case workers and training of case workers: Case workers will follow up on cases referred by the community based volunteers and the animators and will work hand in hand with them to increase theircapacity of identification of CP cases, while supervising the activities in the multipurpose spaces • Provide Technical and Strategic Support to CP Committees and Government Structures to Develop Child Protection Policies, or Conduct Assessment, or Build internal Capacity to Implement Community Based Programs • Women Friendly Spaces: AVSI will set up WFS/CFS/MPCs in order to create recreational and PSS mechanism among the population who suffered by displacement • Complaints mechanism and GBV report will be set up to avoid and refer any possible abuse • HLP mechanism will be create to support the IDPs on its new settlement in order to the law and providing capacity building and awareness in areas of intervention • Legal assistance on certification will be deliver for those households that lost their legal documentation

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

AVSI will be engaged in creating a protected and safe environment, in particular in an emergency context, focusing on the improvement of most vulnerable categories well-being and family support through case management and provide access to basic services. Animators/facilitators groups are aimed at creating a protected family environment: providing psychosocial support in emergency and case management are two key actions to be planned in all the interventions. AVSI will conduct regular surveys and safety audits to monitor, identify and mitigate potential risks, and will regularly coordinate with partners through participation to Cluster meetings and district coordination system. A big effort will be put to mainstream protection across each project component and to have regular multisectorial assessment performed in the implementation areas, to ensure that possible route causes for additional protection risks are assessed. Embedded AAP and CFM system ensures to limit risks for beneficiaries. Case Management activities allow the identification and management of the most vulnerable cases. Through a mapping of the districts, AVSI is able to have a clear picture of households and their residences.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

AVSI will provide information to women, girls, boys and men in affected communities about the organisation, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver so that communities and people affected by crisis are fully aware of the expected behaviour of humanitarian staff, including organisational commitments made on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. (CHS 4.1 and 5.6). AVSI’s staff will collect feedback in appropriate ways (e.g. groups are segregated by sex and age, confidential where necessary) and make sure the analysis of the feedback is disaggregated by at least age and sex at minimum so that there can be comparisons of the issues according to roles and norms. Complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritises the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages and refer complaints that do not fall within the scope of the organisation to a relevant party in a manner consistent with good practice (CHS 5.7). AVSI will, at all time, assess the implementation of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and other collective standards including the IASC AAP commitments, Gender Equality Programming, Minimum Operating Standards on PSEA; and the Best Practice Guide to establish Inter- Agency Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms (CBCM) and its accompanyingStandard Operating Procedures through self-assessments, peer review, third-party verification or certification

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Inclusive activities are mainstreamed in all AVSI projects. Animators and Case workers are trained on safe identification and referral and according to the needs, specific topics are tackled concerning physical or mental disability. In some cases, special educators are hired to provide continuous support and mentoring to teachers on the inclusion of children, from needs assessment to progress monitoring, as well ascoordinate the work of specialized service providers. Activities can be implemented in different context (non-formal, formal, or informal). In different projects we engaged also specialized services – speech therapy, psychology, psychomotor therapy, mobility assistant for the visually impaired children, etc both for the assessment of child’s needs and for the provision of support services. Referral pathways are always mapped and shared. The engagement of parents for these activities, in the parental meetings to discuss the child’s progress and problems is fundamental.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed activities will adopt an environment-friendly approach by trying to increase awareness in the affected communities. Particular attention will be given to community sensitization involvement on environment and waste management, as well as possible measures of climatic DRR, particularly in urban settings. Main vector to promote environmental good practices will be children and youths, through peer clubs and mainstreaming environmental concepts in PSS and recreational activities. With a strong community engagement and participation; on rehabilitation activities, specific attention regarding the site selection and sourcing of materials, favoring the use of local resources, like the locally sourced building materials, sun-cured soil bricks produced in affected communities and the limiting of the use of timber wherever feasible, for all rehabilitation and construction. Finally, health and hygiene education will have a component of key messages regarding the environment.
