Project Summary

The proposed project provides life-saving and life-sustaining core and seasonal assistance in addition to rehabilitation of damage and unfinished houses. The project supports war affected households (both host communities and IDPs) by rehabilitating partially damaged houses and distributing NFI kits to displaced people in Dana subdistrict in Idleb and in Afrin in Aleppo. In total, 3,500 HHs will benefit from the activities of this project (4,308 boys under 18 , 4,280 girls under 18, 4200 men, 4688 women). The rehabilitation will target 300 houses that are partially damaged houses where people are living in or have returned to. NFI kits will be distributed to 1000 HHs that have been displaced from different areas into Idleb and Aleppo (300 kits to the same beneficiaries of the rehabilitation). In winter, the project targets 2500 HHs with winter assistance (restricted vouchers) to protect people from harsh weather conditions.

4 - Needs

As the war enters its ninth year in Syria, the humanitarian situation is becoming worse.2019 witnessed several forced displacements from north Hama and south of Idleb into Idleb and Aleppo. December 2019 witnessed increased attacks and hostilities on the south of Idleb which resulted in the displacement of thousands of people to Idleb and Northwards. These attacks resulted in new wave of displacements towards the north of Idleb and Aleppo. According to CCCM report on 29th of December (HNAP), 283,898 people (56.037 men, 61,156 women, 80, 120 boys, 86,585 girls) were displaced between the period of 1st and 29th of December 2019; 56, 000 were displaced into camps and 13,000 arrived to collective centers. 205,366 were displaced into Idleb governorate. These people have needs of life saving services including FSL, NFI and WASH as they had to flee their homes with only some back bags that include some clothes and essential items. To make the situation worse, the displacement was compounded by winter temperatures, heavy rains and economic instability. According to the Humanitarian update issued by OCHA on 23rd of December, the latest escalation in violence "exacerbates an already dire humanitarian situation for people, particularly in Idleb Governorate, where in recent weeks, tens of thousands of people have been newly displaced in the hostilities. These newly displaced people add to the over 400,000 that were displaced as a result of hostilities since the end of April, many of them multiple times". According to HNO 2020, Up to one million IDPs are residing in last-resort sites in the north-west and north-east, a significant proportion of whom are in improvised shelters which will need replacement/ repair in the next year. The MSNA data also shows that female-headed households, youth, elderly and people with functional difficulties have reduced access to basic services including shelter, as well as reduced livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to NFI

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will compromise Rehabilitation of 300 partially damaged houses (Activity 2.1.1), distributing 1000 core NFI kits ( Activity 1.1.1) in addition to distributing 2500 vouches for winterization (1.2.1). The activities of the project will benefit 17,500 beneficiaries (4,308 boys under 18 , 4,280 girls under 18, 4200 men, 4688 women) Rehabilitation of 300 partially damaged houses: 300 HHS will benefit from this activity (1500 beneficiaries) HIHFAD will receive applications from beneficiaries who are in need of house rehabilitation, the application will include the ID of the applicants in addition to property ownership documents that will be verified by the community. In case of rent, there must be a contract for at least six months from the completion of the repair. Two members of the staff (one male and one female) who are trained on HLP will be responsible for any HLP issues confronted during implementation of the project. A technical needs assessment of houses will be conducted before starting the implementation of the rehabilitation works. The house should be partially damaged, and the cost of all rehabilitation works will not exceed $800 for each house. The selection of beneficiaries for the Shelter rehabilitation activities will be based on the following criteria: Families who have returned to their damaged houses. Female headed households (both from host communities and IDPs); Families with chronically ill members or with disabilities (both from host communities and IDPs) - People from host community who still live in their damaged houses; Affected people will be consulted about what they need to be rehabilitated as a priority. The criteria and final locations will be shared with the Shelter&NFI cluster to avoid any duplication. While winterisation vouchers will be distributed to people in the camps after coordination with NFI cluster. People will select the heating items they want with cost of $120 per HH.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment of womenand girls) Mitigation: 1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 - Train staff oncode of conduct – 3 - Include code of conduct in all staff contracts that address any SEA complaints. Risk: Some beneficiaries (most vulnerable) might be at risks such as harassment during the distribution of NFI kits if the distribution points are not organized:Mitigation: 1 - allocate one day for distributing to female headed households whenpossible. 2 - Separate between men and women during the distribution 3 - Give priority tovulnerable people (elderly and pregnant women) in addition to door to door distribution to people with disabilities. Risk: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from propertyowner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated. Mitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP DD. 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signedRisk: Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees.mitigation: 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Access restriction due to local fighting between different arm source groups in the targeted location which may restrict the access. The local fighting might delay the implementation of the activities. Mitigation: HIHFAD Security team will monitor the situation on the ground and send reports when necessary. The works will be suspended during the fighting and resumed after assessment of the situation. Assumption: the security situation remains relatively stable which allows people to continue living in the area and the activities of the project to take place. Risk: Closure of the border for commercial shipments or changing some regulation related to allowed items to be exported to Syria may have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities. Mitigation: 1. Explore alternative supply lines through local procurement 2. Communicate regularly with logisticcluster to have valid information about new or changing regulations. Assumption: The crossing borders are open for humanitarian and commercial shipments.Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment of womenand girls) Mitigation: 1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 - Train staff oncode of conduct – 3 - Include code of conduct in all staff contracts that address any SEA complaints.
