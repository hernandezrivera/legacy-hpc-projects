Project Summary

TAS has designed this proposal to provide Shelter and NFI assistance for the most affected people in NWS to improve their living conditions through:
- Provision of Cash for Winter assistance (150 USD per HHs) for seasonal and supplementary winter core NFI items such as of heating fuel, stoves, winter clothing kits and winter NFI kits. This activity will be through voucher modality (target: 7,500 individuals /1,500 HHs - $150 USD per HHs).
- Installation and construction of 250 dignified Shelters/temporary transitional shelters (target: 1,250 individuals/ 250 HHs - 250 Shelter units)
- Improving the Site Infrastructure/ HH level improvements implemented (target: 1,250 individuals /250 HHs) through land preparation, drainage system, water & Sanitation networks, and lighting poles in addition to all relevant infrastructure needed
- Rehabilitation of the damaged and affected houses -150 houses (target: 750 individuals/ 150 HHs - 150 Shelters).
The total estimated beneficiaries of the proposed project are as follows:
- Shelter Component: 2,000 individuals / 400 HHs
- NFI Component: 7,500 individuals / 1,500 HHS.
Following the priorities identified in the Humanitarian Response Plan 2022, TAS has designed this project to enhance dignified living and address acute needs for 1,900 HHs/ 9,500 individuals living in Idleb with different ages and vulnerable cases (23 % men, 25 % women, 26 % Boys, and 27% girls ) taking into consideration Gender and age diversity through integrated Shelter and NFI interventions.
All activities within the proposed project will be according to the Shelter cluster objectives and will be designed according to the SPHERE standards.
In addition, the planned intervention will mainstream protection principles especially “Do no harm” throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls. During this project, TAS will consult with the community and individual IDPs taking into account the specific need of each group, TAS also will involve the women and girls through Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) during the community outreach plan.
The main objective of the proposed project is to reach 9,500 individuals targeting most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls and prioritize family members with high vulnerabilities according to the beneficiaries selection criteria (female/single/child-headed households, IDP status, no source of income and support, people with disabilities, elderly,...etc.).
TAS will ensure high-level coordination with all parties involved in the project (SNFI cluster, OCHA Clusters, Donors, local authorities, other NGOs, and other related parties) to implement the project in good quality and avoid any duplication.

3 - Needs

Nearly 12 years of war and economic collapse have left over 15 million people in Syria in need of humanitarian aid. Some of the most critical needs are in the north‐west of the country, where 4.5 million people, mostly women and children, are trapped in a war zone along the border with Türkiye. The substantial increase of people in need of humanitarian assistance as a result of continued conflict, and deteriorating living conditions in northwest Syria remains a key consideration for mobilization of funds and provision of life‐saving assistance [HNO, draft 2023].During 2021 and 2022, TAS has implemented several Shelter projects by using sustainable solutions (dignified shelter and damaged houses rehabilitation).On Nov 2022, TAS conducted RNA to identify the Shelter and NFI needs in NWs, it was confirmed the poorly available services of shelter as well as the huge gaps in the infrastructure in the IDP sites, particularly in Idleb area. Therefore, TAS designed this project to reach 9,500 individuals, improve their living conditions and provide a dignified life.

4 - Implementation of activities

The Activities will be implemented as follow:1- As for the in-camp interventions, an assessment will be conducted to the targeted camps in order to select the flood-prone, highest population density and the worst infrastructure status in order to be targeted with the proposed transitional shelter solution for 250 HHs. In addition to the shelter solution itself, the infrastructure will be improved as well including graveling works, drainage systems, and any other relevant needs in order to apply a settlement-based approach and promote the living conditions in the selected site.As well as the HLP verification including verification of land and housing ownership, engaging local land and housing actors for community ownership2- As for the shelter repair activity, 150 shelters will be repaired. Those shelters will be basically damaged or affected houses. The BNFs will be the most vulnerable IDPs living in flood-prone areas and characterize basic living conditions during winter. HHs consisting of the elderly and PwDs will be prioritized. During the project phases, TAS will coordinate with SNFI Cluster to avoid overlaps with other agencies, in addition to coordination with local authorities and relevant parties.In addition, TAS will monitor the project operating directly and provide reports for the donors and 4Ws as well as TAS will evaluate the project after completing the planned activities.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: The vulnerability of children and women in conflict circumstances highly increases and places them in higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations.Mitigation strategy: 1- Staff personnel will sign code of conduct and child protection policy.2- Most of the management and field staff had attended workshops to increase the awareness of PSEA cases.3- the compliance mechanism will be defined and shared with beneficiaries.Risk 2: There are not enough humanitarian staff (both male and female) at the distribution site monitoring the distribution process Mitigation strategy: 1- Ensure oversight of distributions by aid workers who are trained on PSEA, Codes of Conduct and how/where to report misconduct.2- Ensure field level PSEA Focal Points in distribution teams and/or that staff involved in distributions know how to report or refer any PSEA allegations received.3- Deploy gender balanced teams to support projects for disaster-affected communities.4- Consider wearing clearly marked organization logos on shirts/jackets/caps for ease of identity by beneficiaries.Risk 3 : when challenges and issues arose during distribution, they are not handled well, and there is no female employee if the beneficiary was a female1. Train field workers on PSEA2. Ensure female aid workers are present and involved in the resolution of the challenges3. Sensitize affected communities on PSEA and who/where to report
