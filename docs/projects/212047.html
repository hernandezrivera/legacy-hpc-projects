Project Summary

In Hassakeh city and its western rural countryside, one million people have faced reduced access to water for nearly two years, since September 2019, due to repetitive and sustained disruptions of water provision from the Allouk water station at different intervals with the harshest and longest interval in summers, where temperatures in summer in Hassakeh governorate can reach close to 50 degrees Celsius. Allouk station is the main source of water, located near Ras Al Ain town. Over the past few months, 70 percent of the Hassakeh population including IDPs, because of water cuts, is suffering dire consequences where people are facing a desperate situation which has reached breaking point., and leads people to resort to unsafe coping mechanisms.
Humanitarian organizations in Hassakeh are trying to fill the gaps and respond to the many needs through running water trucking activities, but the overall accessibility to water and sanitation facilities remains an issue. In addition, Humanitarian organizations, installed a number of water tanks in main streets in Hassakeh city and Tal Tamer town and filled with water and it is sometimes unsafe and contaminated, to help respond to some of the water needs where the needs has been so much higher. As well, Hassakeh population has resorted to digging surface wells its depth ranging from 10-70 meters despite the water is contaminated Some are using this water for drinking without treatment which exposed them especially children to a variety of harmful pollutants and pathogens and the possible health effects those potential contaminants can have. Moreover, while Hassakeh governorate is currently witnessing an alarming increase in COVID-19 cases, the limited access to water also seriously hampers the hygiene measures essential for the prevention and treatment of the virus, as drinking water is crucial for the survival just as hygiene is a priority amidst a pandemic that requires an abundance of water for prevention.
This project proposal aims to respond to the core needs of the vulnerable displaced families to meet the immediate survival needs of WASH materials of the most extremely vulnerable people including women and children especially children less five years old who are the most susceptible to risk of disease transmission, as well, the host communities through provision of water storage tanks where even when water is available through water trucking activities in which is accessible to some people while most of the displaced families at their displacement areas have no enough water storage containers to get their needs of water. Provision water tanks to these families will help them to have access to safe water and raise the people hygienic conditions, and will provide long-term sustainable resilience to those most in need for water storage tanks.
The targeted beneficiaries are those who have been forced from their homes in Ras Al Ain and its countryside, where around 19,000 families comprise around 84,500 people have fled the insecure situation to southern safe areas mainly to Tal Tamer town and its countryside as well to Hassakeh city and its countryside, and many people families have been displaced multiple times who exposed to grave protection threats, before resorting to schools and other collective shelters, makeshift camps, as well a huge numbers of displaced families have been accommodated by host communities who have been suffering poor conditions of livelihoods, in which they become under further pressure, and exhausted due the Syrian crisis which enters its tenth year, where the displaced families and the host communities continue to bear the burden of ongoing conflict which marked by unparalleled suffering due to the shortage of humanitarian support.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

It is truly concerning to hear about the dire situation in Hassakeh city and its surrounding areas with the severe water shortage affecting the lives of so many people, especially vulnerable populations like women and children. The lack of access to clean and safe water not only poses immediate health risks but also exacerbates the challenges faced during the ongoing pandemic.The project proposal to provide water storage tanks to displaced families is a crucial step towards ensuring access to safe water and improving hygiene conditions. By targeting the most vulnerable populations, especially young children who are at higher risk of disease transmission, this initiative can have a significant impact on the well-being of the community.
