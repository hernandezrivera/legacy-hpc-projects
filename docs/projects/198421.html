Project Summary

The ETS will provide humanitarians across Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states with reliable communications services to enable an eﬃcient and safer response, ultimately saving more lives. ETS services allow humanitarians to implement eﬃciently their activities and carry out their jobs safely in areas where services from local services providers are unstable or non-operational. In addition, the ETS will continue to build the capacity of local partners, will map the communication needs of the affected population in North-East Nigeria and evaluate the possibility of implementing a pilot project to address identified needs for communities.

1 - Needs

More than a decade into the crisis, communications infrastructure remains severely affected, characterized by a lack of – or non-existent – reliable and safe telecommunications, mobile networks and Internet services, especially outside of urban areas. This gap was identified as of one the main impediments to enable humanitarian programming and ensure the safety of humanitarian actors when operating in a complex operational environment. To enable a coordinated humanitarian response and the safety and security of humanitarian personnel, the ETS was activated in November 2016 in Nigeria to provide vital common communications services - Internet and security communications - to the humanitarian community responding in the most-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. Shared security telecommunications services are provided in the capital states in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states, and Internet and security telecommunications in the eight humanitarian hubs gradually deployed across Borno State. Given the criticality of the ETS communications services, especially in deep-ﬁeld locations, a continuous power solution is required to ensure provision of services 24/7. In August 2020, the ETS received the required hybrid power solution equipment for its infrastructure, the hybrid solar power solution but the installation has been delayed due to the COVID-19 travel restrictions. In 2021, the hybrid solar power solution was installed in all ETS location to ensure ETS services are available 24/7. ETS services currently being provided will be maintained, upgraded and expanded in line with the evolving needs on the ground. The ETS continuously assessed the communications needs across North-East during frequent ﬁeld missions and consultations with partners through dedicated local ETS Working Group meetings and coordination in Maiduguri.

2 - Activities

The Emergency Telecommunications Sector (ETS) will provide reliable communication services to humanitarians in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states, allowing a more effective and safe response and ultimately, saving more lives. ETS services enable humanitarians to carry out their activities more efficiently and safely in locations where local service providers' services are unstable or non-operational. In 2022, the ETS collaborated closely with its partners, notably UN agencies and international non-governmental organizations (NGOs), to ensure a coordinated response and assistance to the whole humanitarian community. Throughout 2023, the ETS will adhere to the following principles:1. Continue to provide coordination and information management services, to guarantee a consistent response and reduce duplication of effort. The ETS will attend key sector meetings and will host ETS coordination meetings in Maiduguri. Update ETS information products, such as dashboards, situation reports, infographics, operational documentation, and meeting minutes, will be created and distributed to global and local partners on time. These resources will also be provided on the ETCluster.org platform's Nigeria page.2. Maintain internet and security telecommunications services in the ten Humanitarian hubs across Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa states that are currently covered by ETS. In 2023, the sector will continue to provide security telecommunication services to the metropolitan regions of Maiduguri, Damaturu, and Yola. The sector's internet connectivity and security telecommunication services would be maintained at humanitarian centers in Bama, Banki, Damasak, Dikwa, Gwoza, Maiduguri, Monguno, and Ngala.3. The ETS intends to adopt a hybrid power solution to ensure ETS services are available 24 hours a day, seven days a week and a plan to upgrade the current hybrid power-supply solution to ensure ongoing ETS service provision at humanitarian hubs. To supplement the existing solar backup system for ETS VHF radio infrastructure and to overcome fuel shortages affecting generator power supply at the hubs in addition to secure an environment friendly power sourcing.4. Maintain capacity-building programs for humanitarian actors and their government colleagues. On a demand basis, the ETS will give training on security telecommunication processes to the entire humanitarian community for them to make effective use of the ETS security telecommunication services. Technology-based training and capacity building activities will also be provided in partnership with the sector standby counterparts and humanitarian partners.5. Assess the affected population's communication needs to support them to make informed decisions, under a new initiative to improve information dissemination systems in the IDP camps in North-East Nigeria enabling technology for communities.6. ETS will be developing an on-demand mobile data connectivity solution for use in remote regions with no hub or internet access. This could be utilized for ad-hoc activities conducted by the UN and NGOs in these locations, as well as assessments of security or program needs.
