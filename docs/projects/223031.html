Project Summary

Boys and girls continue to face various risks such as violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and are in acute need of child protection, though sustainable community based protection networks and accountable justice systems. Protracted conflict compounded by current flooding extremes which has affected at least 1.4 million people across 43 counties and influx of people displaced by Sudan crisis; coupled with prevalent cyclical waves of inter/ intra communal violence, revenge killings, children and women abduction including forceful recruitments and cattle related fighting continue to impact vulnerable populations, particularly among girls and boys who have witnessed various atrocities and at risk of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and anxiety disorders. This has been coupled with loss of friends and family members further affecting mental health and emotional development of children.
Recent IPC November 2024 project at least 7.69 million people including 2.1 million children will suffer from acute malnutrition through June 2025.This coupled with deteriorating macro-economic conditions and high cost of food and other essential commodities has further contributed to negative coping strategies including child labor and early and forced marriages (UNICEF,2024; IPC November 2024) in addition to increasing cases of both forceful and voluntary recruitment of children into armed groups (UNMISS, 2024). Most vulnerable and displaced populations’ needs are further worsened by protection concerns including GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse, predominantly among girls and women with an uptick incases of GBV cases; which are often under-reported due to stigma and lack of legal assistance.
Women and girls continue to face increased risks of harassment, assault and sexual violence, early marriages with estimated that 52 percent of girls are married between the age of 15 and 18 (Child Rights Coalition, 2016), poor rule of law which continues to prevent perpetrators from being held accountable for violations. Proposed project which will target 8,100 beneficiaries comprised of 450 men, 650 women, 3,500 boys and 3,500 girls including people with special needs/ disabilities. Proposed initiatives, which in line key Child Protection Area of Responsibility objectives that will ensure vulnerable and at risk girls, boys, men and women including children with disabilities have improved and sustainable access to quality lifesaving child protection; with reduced suffering of boys , girls ,women and men including children with disabilities who are at risk or experience violence ,abuse, exploitation and neglect including GBV through provision services.
Proposed action will respond to the child protection needs via child rights and child protection awareness, including strengthened psychosocial support, case management and strengthening community based child protection networks including families to prevent and respond to violence against children and other protection concerns including recruitment and use of children in armed forces and groups, family tracing and re-unification, child protection response monitoring, support and referrals among others.. The project will ensure quality programming, inter-cluster synergies, Accountability to Affected Populations, regular context analysis and advocacy on environmental conservation, ensuring conflict sensitive programming by upholding Do No Harm ethos.

1. Needs

According to the protection cluster and AoRs South Sudan 2025 Protection Needs and Response Plan, South Sudan is expected to experience a severe child protection crisis in conflict-affected areas in 2025. Significant issues include family separation, recruitment into armed groups, and violence, particularly affecting adolescent girls and boys. Vulnerable urban children may resort to gangs, while children with disabilities face heightened risks and limited access to services. We shall address these above-mentioned needs by raising community awareness of child protection. Awareness plays a crucial role in child protection by empowering children and families to recognize and prevent child abuse and neglect, such as cases of early marriage and recruitment by armed forces. After the awareness, families and children will be better prepared to identify and report these issues. We shall also address the above needs by mapping available child protection services to update the referral pathways in Aweil East County. Updating and documenting the multisectoral referral pathways and training service providers on how to safely refer children with physical or mental health or other protection needs will increase access to services among children from the host, IDP, and returnee communities who are in need. The multisectoral services will include schools that will provide safe spaces for the children, spaces for healing, and integration. Conducting training for men and women on CPiE to build their capacity and those who are part of community-based child protection networks on CPiE will empower them to identify and refer children at risk to support services. Additionally, it will promote a safe, dignified, and protective environment and improve the impact of all humanitarian actors by applying the do no harm principle and proactively reducing risks and harm. Similarly, the protection cluster South Sudan protection analysis update published in June 2024 explained that refugees and returnees from Sudan also face significant family separation during their journey, including vulnerable groups like women, older people, and people with disabilities finding themselves without family or community support in transit settlements. Protection monitoring data from April and May 2024 indicate that 50% of key informants reported instances of family separation. This project will provide community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers of children with disabilities who have been separated to promote their recovery. The PSS activities will speed up the recovery process and prevent problems that children may encounter in the future to increase their resilience and coping skills. The efforts of this project to provide comprehensive PSS services will be crucial.

2. Project activities

We shall achieve the 2025 HRP protection cluster objective to achieve the goal of ensuring the static provision of critical protection assistance and services (lifesaving), including specialized response services, while prioritizing a collaborative, child-focused approach, with the engagement of local communities, government, and partners to enhance effectiveness to deliver tailored responses to address the specific needs of the individuals, considering age, gender, and disability by carrying out the following activities: 1) Conduct Child Protection Service mapping and update referral pathway in Aweil East County and share the outcome among all stakeholders operating in the project area (# of Child Protection Service mapping and referral pathways updated at County level); 2) Conducting training for men and women on CPiE to build the capacity of men and women as well as those who are part of community-based child protection networks on CPiE, reaching 100 (50 males, 50 female) participants (# of women and men (community leaders or humanitarian, CP partners and government social welfare staff) trained on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE); 3) Provision of comprehensive case management services and referrals to children (including children with disabilities) to 7,000 (3,500 boys and 3,500 girls) and 1,100 adults (450 men and 650 women). The case management process will start with the identification of children with disabilities, assessment of their needs, development of a care plan, and referrals for other health needs/inter-agency referrals for other unmet needs (# of boys and girls who have received comprehensive case management services including material support); 4) Provision of community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers of children with disabilities (including children with disabilities) to reach 7,000 children (3,500 boys, 3,500 girls), and 1,100 adults (650 Women and 450 men). Trained caseworkers will conduct the PSS and will include recreational sports, as well as training on artwork, beadwork, embroidery, and other skills (# boys, girls, men, and women accessing specialized, structured, and non-structured, community-based mental health and psychosocial support); 5) Conducting 48 community awareness-raising sessions on child protection. We shall conduct awareness and prevention messaging through our caseworkers and existing community-based protection networks targeting men, women, boys, and girls with messages on the rights of children and the inclusion of children with disabilities (# of awareness-raising sessions conducted with children and caregivers on CP issues, protection risk, counseling, and positive parenting).
