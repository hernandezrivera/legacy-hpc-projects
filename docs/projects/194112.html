Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in increasing safe access to CP services for 521,756 children and adolescents in CD, SD, ED, WK and Khartoum States, which will contribute to the HRP CP strategic objectives (for 2023). This project is covered 4 sub-sectors of Protection includes GP, CP, GBV and Mine Actions.
The intervention is mainly designed to meet immediate lifesaving and emergency needs of the vulnerable people mainly women, girls, children, including disable children, ethnic minorities and others with special needs.
This project will carry out extensive consultations with local community and line ministries as an entry point for smooth project implementation. HOPE embraces community based protection approach by building capacity of 30 community based protection networks of 450 members, 30 community volunteers, 36 animators, 16 social workers and 30 service providers and government officials in localities of Bendasi, Zalingie, & Wadi Salih in CD, Ad Du'ayn, & Assalaya in ED, Jabel Awlia, & Khartoum in KH, Beliel, Kas, Nyala Janoub, & Nyala Shimal in SD, Ag Geneina, & Kereneik in WD, and Al Idia, & AnNuhud in WK on Protection to take major roles in project activities. The participants will be equipped with necessary skills and knowledge to implement community driven solutions to their local communities. 3,000 vulnerable separated boys and girls are identified using outreach methodologies, such as mobile activities, awareness raising about services. 960 Girls and boys are provided with specialized child protection response services, including case management for child survivors of sexual violence, child labor, child recruitment, UASCs and children in need for alternative care. 15,000 of targeted families with risk factors reached through family awareness raising on child protection, home visits, and parent/caregiver support groups. 1,050 of girls/boys as vulnerable and at-risk children are provided with gender & age appropriate (structured) psychosocial support and life skills including literacy and numeracy. 30 Communities are mobilized to support children through activation of child protection networks and community volunteers. Human resource capacity to respond to child protection concerns among child protection actors and other duty bearers is strengthened, targeted 300 of men and women trained on child protection, run and maintain 10 CFSs, procure and distribute recreation PSS and dignity kits, Conduct child protection assessments, and 100 of girls and boys with Disability received PSS.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ CBPN as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

Clashes between SAF and RSF have been ongoing since 15 April, particularly in and around residential areas in Khartoum, Darfur stats except East Darfur as well as a north part of West Kordofan state and capital city of NK. As of 13 May, the Federal Ministry of Health reports that at least 700 people have been killed and over 5,200 injured since the onset of the fighting. The number of casualties is likely to be higher, as many individuals were reportedly unable to reach hospitals, services facilities and markets due to movement constraints. Several cities have faced heavy bombardments and clashes, and associated unexploded ordnance, causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical water, health care and power infrastructure. Looting and destruction of health facilities, markets and government institutions was the main feature in the Khartoum and Darfur states. Ongoing violence has caused extensive damage to infrastructure, leading to water shortages, blackouts, communication disruptions and incidents of looting. Large sections of the capital, including factories, banks, shops and markets, have been looted, damaged, or targeted by rocket attacks. In Khartoum and Darfur, the inter-communal, inter-tribal conflicts, and violence that started in 15 April which are affected the safety and security of people exacerbating the risk of sexual violence especially when displaced or during looting the capital cities. Women and girls continue to face the risk of GBV including sexual violence when they leave the villages or during attack by looters. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. After the longtime of conflict and prolonged humanitarian crisis, the emergency situation in Khartoum and Darfur raised intensively by clashes erupted between SAF and RSF, and then between tribes in WD as well as negatively affected children especially in the targeted localities. The CP severity ranking in the targeted localities mostly is between 4-5 and the People In Need (PIN) PiN for CP are 20,655 in Bendasi, 46,350 in Wadi Salih, 101,298 in Zalingie, 67,637 in Ad Du'ayn, 14,829 in Assalaya, 89,352 in Jebel Awlia, 22,582 in Khartoum, 180,587 in Beliel, 64,812 in Kass, 14,877 in Nyala Janoub, 156,709 in Nyala Shimal, 170,178 in Ag Geneina, 106,633 in Kereneik, 18,111 in Al Idia, and 18,035 in An Nuhud.In the targets’ localities there are a huge gap in child protection services specially in some Darfur areas and Khartoum state for the below reasons: • Ongoing conflicts and newly displaced has resulted in severe negative impacts on people in Khartoum and Darfur; casualties, trauma among children, females, and other groups. • Children on the move to escape the violence and a number of children are unaccompanied and separated.• Reports of grave child rights violations including Killing and Maiming, and street children are more at the risk of recruitment, and SGBV are reported. • Increase in the cases of children getting recruited by armed groups reported, particularly in Khartoum and the Darfur regions. • Children are distressed because of witnessing violence, loss of family members, displacement and insecurity and are in urgent need of psycho-social support and normality.• Child, women, and young females have been at risk and live within a very destructive environment; do not enjoy their rights, possible incidents of GBV, lack of health services, and many other issues affecting their lives. • Lack of protection services especially services to address Sexual Violence and Family tracing & reunification, and referral system. • Lack of awareness of parents of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC). • Poor capacities local mechanisms of Community-based Child Protection Networks for effective protection issues and concerns resolution and absence of follow up for action taken. Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to Protection services

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure protection mainstreaming, HOPE will use the below actions: • Conduct regular consultations with particularly high-risk groups of the affected population such as women, girls, and children with disabilities. • Support community engagement and interaction. • Establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms. • Foster community-based Disaster Risk Reduction plans/strategies; to support activities that contribute to child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention including psychosocial support, and referral pathways; • Teachers, learners, and parents, will be oriented on topics such as protection, human rights, sexual and gender-based violence, and non-discrimination approaches to access humanitarian aid. • Ensure that all multi-purpose center construction is in line with accessibility standards for persons with a disability, cash-based assistance will be provided to children with disabilities to access tools and materials to enhance their learning. • Pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner. • Conducting community safety audits and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. • Maintain transparent, inclusive, and adequate communication with communities on available services; and by consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selection of services, feedback after service provision. • Guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability adolescent boys and girls, children without adult care, the elderly, and children and adults with specific needs ensuring that programming is tailored to respond to the needs of adolescent boys and girls. • Involve community actors (especially CBPN, and parent) in the project planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation to promote participation and empowerment. • Involving and consulting CBPN in planning and conducting a monthly meeting at the community level to avoid causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will consider AAP through the community participation in designing, developing and implementing of the project activities. This was partially done through consultation meetings and focus group discussion at earlier stage of the project design. The focus group discussions have also included women and girls to capture their distinct needs, particularly for dignity kits. The project applies participatory approach by involving beneficiaries in the development of the implementation plan. Prior to project start, HOPE will conduct kick off workshop for project partners including local authority and local community to share information on project services, make commitment statements by partners and agree on their roles & responsibilities. CBPN members and community volunteers will receive a full briefing of project commitments, targets and accountability. Monthly meetings and information sharing activities will be delivered to the community to inform about project services. The project will establish a community based feedback and complaint mechanism, comprising of a team of 5 trained community volunteers in each location. CVs will be accessible to the community to receive their feedback and complaints and meet bi-weekly to review and respond under support and coordination of Child protection Officer. Community will be made aware of this mechanism. A specific tool (Beneficiary Satisfaction Form) has been designed for this purpose. TOR and operating procedure for these mechanisms/ volunteers will be clearly developed and shared. This mechanism will be complemented coupled with a complaint box to enable submission of private, confidential or sensitive complaints. Process and time for response will clearly communicated to beneficiaries.
