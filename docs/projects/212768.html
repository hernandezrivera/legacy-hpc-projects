Project Summary

1- Needs: NES is renowned for its agriculture, particularly wheat, barley, corn, and watermelon, vital for trade and export. However, challenges such as dwindling cotton production due to water scarcity, vast deserted lands from EO, and safety concerns persist. A December 2023 iMMAP report highlighted 25 new blocked agricultural lands, 19 infrastructures, 15 non-agricultural lands, and 14 roads due to EO, underscoring the urgent need for safe access restoration. The extensive explosive contamination in NES, including landmines and Cluster Munitions, significantly impacts the humanitarian situation, impeding recovery and development. According to the HNO 2023, approximately one-third of Syria's populated communities are contaminated, with 218 EO-related injuries or fatalities reported.
2- Intervention: The intervention targets improving safe land and infrastructure access in NES, aiming to reduce EO threats and support economic and social recovery. It focuses on engaging 77,966 individuals, including men, women, girls, and boys, IDPs, returnees, and residents, through Community Liaison activities. Awareness campaigns will be conducted to release 5,068,143 square meters of land, with 680 community focal points, 110 teachers, and humanitarian workers trained in EO Risk Education (EORE). Specialized training courses will be provided, and EO items will be safely disposed of. Gender and diversity inclusion are key priorities. National NGOs will be capacitated through training courses, enabling sessions in challenging-to-reach areas. The intervention will primarily focus on Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh Governorates, with potential expansion to Deir Ez-Zor contingent on funding availability.
3- Protection Risks Analysis: Risk1: Unequal provision of operations to diverse populations with varying needs and locations. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Apply a conflict-sensitive approach, coordinating with community leaders for inclusive activities
Risk2: Security instability endangering the team during clearance operations. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Strictly follow safety SOPs and continually track team members.
Risk3: Landowners resisting clearance due to perceived knowledge of risks. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: Essential coordination with authorities, community leaders, and members.
Risk4: Exclusion of women and girls from consultation and information gathering in community surveys and non-technical activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure the organization's team is well-trained on inclusion, leaving no person behind during activity implementation.
4- PSEAH Strategy: To prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, the project will implement five standards: ensuring safe recruitment, conducting risk assessments, ensuring appropriate communication, responding seriously to allegations, and establishing a feedback mechanism.
5- Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures: Assumptions include semi-stable security allowing operations, timely approvals from local authorities (NESMAC), satisfaction from community leaders and members, equal service provision, and participants' comfort with tests. Mitigation strategies include following safety SOPs, maintaining regular communication with authorities, consulting the community, adopting conflict-sensitive approaches, and explaining the purpose of tests to participants.
