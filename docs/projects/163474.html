Project Summary

This project includes 2 main components:
Component 1 - The Programme-based approach (PBA) funding in its 3rd year of implementation will pursue the scale up of the IRC multi-sectoral programming, building on successful implementation of emergency basic needs, WASH, and protection programming. The IRC will prioritize its response to the divisions of Ndian, Memeand Fako. Depending on confirmation of complementary funding for the southwest, the flexibility of the PBA funding would be leveraged to begin programming in Lebialem division, a hard-to-reach area, enhancing scale, reach and capacity for responsive and adaptive programming in the volatile context of the SW. In the NW, the IRC will prioritize its interventions in Mezam and/or Momo divisions in complementarity with other funding sources to provision of basic needs and services to targeted populations, where the PBA funding will be used to build on its small-scale programming to ensure stronger foundation and programming sustainability in Mezam and/or Momo division. The IRC will remain flexible to shift geographic focus within these all those divisions, based on confirmation of most recent needs and gaps analysis, clients’ feedback and IRC’s operational capacity – including the confirmation of complementary funding which would allow for scale up of response. In addition, a contingency line will be kept for SW/NW to allow for rapid response to emerging needs in a flexible manner.
Component 2- The humanitarian crisis in the Southwest and Northwest region of Cameroon has exposed the local population to large scale protection risks and human rights violations, and impacted vulnerable households’ capacity to meet their emergency food needs. Through this Action, the IRC seeks to improve the protection and food security status of vulnerable people affected by the crisis in Southwest Cameroon, assisting the most vulnerable people to stay safe, access assistance, recover from violence and claim their rights. The IRC will do this through an integrated program of Protection prevention and response activities including food voucher programming to mitigate further protection risks resulting from the use of negative coping mechanisms. Prevention activities will include information provision, awareness-raising, and sensitization activities on protection risks and available safe protection, legal and administrative services, while strengthening the capacity of community-based organizations and justice actors. Response activities include provision of Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) for emergency needs, emergency psychosocial support (PSS) services to GBV survivors within safe spaces in health facilities, and support for health service fees and transport costs for identified emergency protection cases. Furthermore, the IRC will provide emergency food assistance to the most vulnerable and at risk of additional exposure to potentially life-threatening protection risks due to reliance on negative coping mechanisms to meet emergency food needs.
In addition, as co-lead of the Protection Cluster in the South West, the IRC will work closely with protection actors to ensure harmonized information sharing, capacity building, and effective operational and programmatic coordination to ensure synergies are systematically explored and enabled.

Component 1 :The IRC Strategic Action Plan has prioritized two signature outcomes and twelve linked core outcomes. The two signature outcomes and 9 core outcomes were selected for the PBA scope of application.In order to achieve the 1st signature outcome “People meet basic needs and avoid negative coping strategies”, , and linked outcomes below, the IRC will provide vouchers for food and non food items to support households to meet their immediate basic needs and avoid the use of negative coping strategies. To achieve the 2nd signature outcome “People are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm” and related outcomes, activities will focus on provision of critical information, reduction of protection risks through community-based protection activities, supporting access to basic services for persons with specific needs, including provision of GBV prevention and response services.
