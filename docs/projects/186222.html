Project Summary

Reach Out proposes a Food Security Intervention in Lebialem Division, the most remote and most affected location of the Anglophone crisis in Cameroon. 827 households that are extremely food insecure have already been identified and the community has granted access to the area.
This project proposes 3 months of emergency assistance to the communities of Takwai, Atongeh, Lebong, Nchembin, Bechati, Folepi, and Bellah. The intervention will be complemented with baseline and endline food security assessments following cluster guidelines.
This project will help rectify a mistake of the humanitarian community operating in the NW/SW: that the most affected, vulnerable location of the North-West and South-West, has been neglected for 4 years, and that those most likely to die of hunger are left unserved and will continue to be unserved throughout 2022.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In late 2017, the political crisis in Anglophone Cameroon devolved into a civil war. One of the most violently affected locations was Lebialem, the most remote division of the South-West Region, which terrain is favorable to guerrilla warfare. UN Resolution 2417 condemns the starving of civilians as a method of war. Despite this, in 2018, the Cameroonian military began a blockade of food items moving between Djchang (the nearest safe location, a central market) and Fontem (in Lebialem) virtually stopping all access of food from outside the division. This caused some stir in the local news and among humanitarian actors, however, Lebialem is also the smallest Region, the most remote, and humanitarians don’t usually have the funding or the access to operate in the area. The problem has been quietly forgotten. Today, the blockade still remains in place, and after 3 years its effects are getting more extreme.In mid-2021, Reach Out conducted a food security assessment in the area with funding from WFP. Reach Out has been working on food security since the beginning of the crisis in most of the affected areas, but our staff had never seen such dire situations. The dwindling population feeds on what they can. Most eat only once a day and every day of the week most households eat the same: leafy vegetables with plantain and red oil. Sometimes, somebody fishes in the river or kills a rat mole, or catches two or three snails. Some eat just boiled plantains and red oil. Lebialem is the only Division of the North-West and South-West with no reported beneficiary and a 100% gap reported by the Food Security Cluster this 2021. The Cadré Harmonisé in October 2021 identified Lebialem at crisis level. Lebialem is also being deserted. According to Reach Out's assessment in June 2021, those who remain are the most vulnerable: older persons, persons with disabilities, the extreme poor, those who don’t have the strength or the resources to displace to safe areas and have no choice but to stay. It is in these communities that Reach Out identified 827 households with extreme food insecurity in June 2021. In September 2021, the village council of Bellah (one of the communities targeted) wrote a letter addressed to Reach Out. They complained about the absence of an intervention despite the overwhelming needs. They confirmed in writing the existence of the blockade “we can only bring food sometimes from Djchang, but most of the time it gets intercepted by the military, with the notion that we are taking it to feed the “amba boys” (NSAG) Gender analysis: The target communities display male-dominated cultures. About 40% of the female adult population is illiterate, as a result of the prioritization of male education in the past. Women marry very early and have an average of 5 children. They have the right to farm and access land, but they do not usually have legal ownership of the land, limiting also their access to credit. Women rarely farm the more lucrative cash crops but rather focus on subsistence agriculture and kitchen gardening, and often use unprofessional methods with insufficient inputs. If they grow and sell commercial crops, these are the least profitable. There are women support groups and cultural associations in all villages. Boys and men are target of violent attacks and live in constant fear, they are also affected by the frustration of their new economic situation.People with non-binary gender identities are stigmatized, abused, and usually hide their preference.
