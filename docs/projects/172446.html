Project Summary

Since the Government of National Accord (GNA) regained control of all of Tripoli on 04 June 2020, civilians have been quick to return to their homes despite warnings of explosive leftovers in the liberated areas. This resulted in an alarming spike in explosive ordnance (EO) accidents, including by anti-personnel mines. HALO has identified thirteen new types of EO including tripwire threats, anti-personnel and anti-tank mines that have not, or rarely, been seen in Libya before. Over 200,000 civilians have been displaced as a result of the Tripoli crisis since April 2019. As the majority of IDPs have stayed close to their home municipalities, returnee movements between municipalities in southern Tripoli continue to increase rapidly. HALO has been among the first responders in Tripoli and worked jointly with the LibMAC to develop an emergency response plan. HALO now provides critical mine action capacity consisting of multiple non-technical survey, multi-task and mechanical clearance teams.
In Sirte, approximately 37 per cent of the city still requires mechanical clearance. As the former stronghold of Daesh in North Africa, two bouts of large-scale fighting since 2011 have left much of the city in ruins. Following the withdrawal of forces under the Libyan National Army (LNA) from Tripoli, Sirte has once again become the dividing line in a stalemate conflict between east- and west-aligned forces with considerable military build-up on both sides. The presence of Wagner Group mercenaries and recently reported mine accidents in and around the city indicate that mine laying in Sirte follows a similar pattern as observed in Tripoli. In Sirte, HALO maintains two mechanical clearance teams and has trained up staff to safely identify and work around the newly identified EO threats, following the eventual withdrawal of foreign mercenaries.
HALO’s experience to date demonstrates that the greatest added value that we bring to the humanitarian mine action sector in Libya is the introduction of new clearance methods on the one hand (i.e. mechanical clearance in Sirte and Tripoli) and capacity-building of the national mine action authorities on the other hand (i.e. clearance, survey, data management capacity). In Tripoli, HALO is introducing manual clearance methods needed to safely remove new types of EO including tripwire threats and is committed to continuing related capacity-building with the LibMAC, particularly related to quality assurance procedures. Activities in this proposal foresee a continuation of HALO’s clearance capacity in Tripoli and Sirte with the possibility to expand to other locations in eastern Libya, e.g. Benghazi or Derna.

1 - Needs

Tripoli:Following the withdrawal of forces under the Libyan National Army from Tripoli, an alarmingly high number of EO and mine accidents among civilians was registered in its southern municipalities between late May and August 2020. As risk education campaigns were launched, the number of newly reported accidents has since decreased. However, the humanitarian situation in areas along the former frontlines remains dire, characterised by a complete lack of service provision by humanitarian and development actors. Humanitarian mine action remains urgently needed, as people are barred from returning to their homes until areas have been surveyed and hazardous items safely marked and removed. In addition, mine action is the first step to enable other protection sector organisations to provide much needed services. Preliminary data from HALO’s ongoing socio-economic baseline assessment of southern Tripoli (based on 385 surveys) demonstrates awareness for explosive threats in respondents’ surroundings and a clear need for humanitarian mine action. - 51.7% of respondents feel unsafe in living, walking and traveling around their area due to the presence of ERW/mines.- 37.4% of respondents have (either themselves or a household member) come into contact with an explosive item.- 17 respondents had a household member injured as a result of a UXO accident during or post an explosion.- 83.9% of respondents have experienced at least some disruptions of the electricity supply caused by damaged/broken infrastructure.- 53.3% of respondents’ households have been economically impacted by the presence of mines/ERW. Sirte & Eastern Libya:According to HALO’s socio-economic baseline data gathered prior to the start of clearance in Sirte, 51 per cent of respondents feel unsafe living, walking and travelling around Sirte due to the presence of ERW. Moreover, 19 per cent of surveyed households have experienced an ERW accident, half of them resulting in the death of a family member. Humanitarian need is high in the eastern region of Cyrenaica as well. Large areas of Benghazi remain uninhabitable and the level of ERW contamination is estimated to be ten times higher than in Sirte. The eastern town of Derna was under siege for two years and is strewn with unexploded ordnance. The large scale and systematic clearance of potentially contaminated rubble and damaged buildings allows affected people to return home and rebuild their lives.

2 - Activities

HALO’s activities in Libya focus on mechanical clearance and low-risk rubble removal, manual clearance techniques including tripwire clearance, non-technical survey, and capacity-building with the LibMAC. Current operations focus on southern Tripoli and Sirte and can be expanded to other locations, e.g. Benghazi or Derna. HALO deployed the first mechanical clearance teams to ever operate in Libya. Mechanical assets are procured and armoured in-country and deployed to clear infrastructure, including through rubble removal, sub-surface mine clearance, and mechanical clearance of partially collapsed buildings. Mechanical mine clearance teams consist of both mechanical operators, who are trained in safe and efficient handling of heavy plant, as well as manual clearance teams, who are trained in inspecting potentially contaminated spoil using specialised equipment including metal detectors and protective gear. In Tripoli, HALO deploys multi-task teams trained in manual clearance techniques to safely remove newly identified EO threats including through tripwire and battle area clearance. Drawing on HALO’s global experience in using satellite imagery and GIS and mapping software for accurate planning of clearance work, HALO is conducting comprehensive baseline survey of southern Tripoli, based on results of the Tripoli Crisis ERW Contamination Mapping and Response Planning project, an open-source media monitoring project. HALO’s field assessment of Sirte determined that 37 per cent of the city are in need of mechanical clearance. In Misrata, HALO completed a technical assessment at Misrata Ammunition Storage Area following explosion on 06 May 2020. Lastly, HALO was invited to Libya by the LibMAC and has fostered capacity building of the national mine action authorities ever since, including by introducing quality control procedures for mechanical clearance and survey and formalising the land release process for mechanically cleared land.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.2 All activities are implemented based on our conflict sensitivity strategy, which accounts for the risk of unintended favouritism. Recruitment is conducted in a transparent manner, supported by relevant government institutions and the LibMAC; all staff are recruited directly from mine-impacted communities, ensuring direct return of investment to affected communities. Areas of operation are selected based on humanitarian need and suspected levels of ERW contamination, as tasked by the LibMAC. 4.20 The main protection risk lies in local residents trespassing on the designated task sites for mechanical clearance/ERW removal and thus increasing the risk of ERW accidents. HALO is implementing an elaborate community liaison strategy that includes ad-hoc risk education sessions by community liaison officers in safe distance from the task site. The emphasis of these RE sessions is to explain the purpose of HALO's work and make sure beneficiaries are aware of safety distances requirements.
