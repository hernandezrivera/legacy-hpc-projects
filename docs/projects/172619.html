Project Summary

This project aims to assist in addressing existing and expected critical humanitarian WASH concerns in Zimbabwe resulting from the socio-economic crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic and resulting loss of income as well as the impact of climate change. The project seeks to rehabilitate non-functional institutional WASH infrastructure particularly that of the overburdened and under-equipped healthcare and school systems. It will also address emergency community WASH needs and enable an enhanced government response by fortifying existing system-based support in close coordination with stakeholders.

1 - Activities

Key activities to be implemented consist of: 1) improving access to water through rehabilitation or construction of new water points, water storage tanks, and associated infrastructure within affected communities; 2) improving access to hygiene through the placement of community handwashing stations at strategic locations; 3) improving access to water, sanitation, and healthcare through rehabilitation of key IPC and WASH infrastructure at HCFs; 4) improving WASH in schools through messaging and water, hygiene, and sanitation infrastructure rehabilitation; 5) strengthening of existing WASH and health systems through training and capacity building of District Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), Community Health Clubs (CHCs), Environmental Health Technicians (EHTs), Community Health Workers (CHWs), and local leaders on topics such as IPC, COVID-19 community planning, water quality management (WQM), drinking water safety and security planning (DWSSP), waste management, and DRM; 6) promotion of key messages on health and hygiene in communities using methods aimed at positive, sustainable behavior change; 7) pre-positioning of critical WASH NFI kits for those affected by potential flooding and outbreaks of water-borne illnesses.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Community participation is crucial in the proposed project, as well as district and provincial level government stakeholders to best understand the needs as they arise for potential flooding, the level of needed rehabilitation in critical healthcare facilities and the prioritisation of these, and the need for trainings at community and district level to support effective humanitarian assistance. Feedback mechanisms will range from hotlines, help desks, in-person discussions, and suggestion boxes that correspond with the OCHA recommendations, in order to provide all participants and beneficiaries a safe and trusted option for providing feedback, comments, questions, or complaints.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All water point rehabilitations will take into consideration the location of the water point, the criticality of its location, its history, and its potential negative impact on the surrounding environment including the groundwater levels. NFI kits will ensure no materials will be included that may be harmful to the environment. As part of the capacity-building and training to strengthen local systems, DRM will be a part of the curriculum to make communities and local leadership more aware of the environment around them, how to be responsive and adaptable to climate change, and how to conduct community response planning.
