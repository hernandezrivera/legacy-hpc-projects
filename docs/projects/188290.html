Project Summary

The project aims at providing integrated services to enhance resilience and protection from fire risks, and protection risks and to enhance the self-resilience, empowerment of IDPs for better governance/participatory management of camps and IDP settlements, especially for women, adolescent girls, the elderly and people with disabilities in NW Syria, sub-districts of Dana, Maaret tamsrin in Idleb and subdistrict of Azaz in Aleppo.
The project will target 45 IDP sites (which will be also targeted by other shelter and protection SDI projects to ensure complementarity) with 10000 HHs, 56,000 individuals, mostly women and children roughly disaggregated as 12320 men, 13440 women, 15120 boys and 15120 girls with about 5600 PWDs, 14000 children under 5 and 5040 elderly people above 60 years old for the year 2022 and a similar number for 2023.
Selection of sites will be done according to the needs and availability of HLP rights in coordination with SNFI and CCCM clusters, local authorities and camp management based on needs assessment. HLP due diligence will be conducted according to relevant clusters guidelines.
SDI will be keen to adhere to IASC standards and international principles in relation to GBV, PSEA, and inclusion and accessibility of people with disabilities. The project will ensure the formation of IDP committees in these sites including the participation of women and PWDs. Coordination with all the abovementioned stakeholders and committees will be maintained throughout the project cycles to ensure active participation and accountability. Camp management and different committees in the camps with receive awareness-raising on safety & security, Covid19, complaint mechanism, GBV, PSEA in addition to camp management and cross-cutting issues through sessions, training and banners, which be available to all camp residents. Multi-sectoral gap analysis will be conducted and submitted regularly to CCCM cluster.
To ensure the safety of IDPs and enhance protection against fire incidents that might happen on large or small scale. The intervention will focus on two aspects:
1. Increase the awareness of IDPs residing in those camps on safe practices against fire incidents from small to large scale, causes and harmful effects; the targeted age/sex groups will include children and women.
2. Set up fire units within targeted camps and equip them with necessary tools (fire kits); in addition to selecting one out of every 75 IDPs, (total 746 IDPs) voluntarily to be trained on firefighting of fire ranged from low to medium scale by a technical team from SCD (Syrian Civil Defense).
The project will also include the care and maintenance in camps such as clearing drainage channels, replacing/refiling fire extinguishers and light repair to latrines, lighting, water tanks, fence...etc. It will form a committee responsible for these activities follow during and after the project. cash for work CFW modality will be preferred for conducting care and maintenance activities. Cash for work workers will be selected from the IDPs living in the camps who are willing to participate in care and maintenance works and have the needed skill or ability, and they will receive training and simple tools. This will enhance the ownership of the project and self-monitoring among IDPs.
The project will be handed over to existing camp management/committees upon project closure after ensuring that the site management is acquainted with accountability and humanitarian principles, site management, care and maintenance in IDP sites, safety & security and existing CCCM Syria cross-border cluster tools and guidelines and provide open channels with CCCM to keep regular reporting of needs, ISIMM, displacement figures and other key information requested by CCCM through the training provided by SDI. In 2023, SDI will aim to complete the handover and follow-up of the sites and target other camps in need of assistance with similar activities and a similar number of IDPs.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, in NWS, 1.7 million people live in 1,389 IDP sites with inadequate access to shelter, water, food, and health support. 45 percent of IDPs in NWS are living in IDP sites that need camp management, 83 percent of IDPs are living in sites with a critical level of population density. IDP sites are particularly vulnerable to incidents, like floods, fires and high-speed winds. From January to October 2021, CCCM in northwest Syria reported 864 incidents that led to 57 injuries,15 deaths, and the damage/destruction of 32,010 tents.88% of IDP sites lack camp management, proper site planning, infrastructure and services and over 900,000 people are living in tents, makeshift or substandard shelters. MSNA 2021 data indicates that 96 percent of HHs in IDP site settings have shelter adequacy issues. According to the CCCM cluster, Afrin and Dana are severe while Maaret Tamisrin is catastrophic on the severity scale. The camps in these three subdistricts host 1,050,000 IDPs (more than half the IDP population in NWS) with 50% IDPs in sites lacking management systems and proper site planning (higher fire risk) and 65% lacking access to basic services. Overcrowded sites increase COVID-19 and GBV risks. HLP is a real challenge, with large populations residing on land without a valid HLP status. Most affected population groups are women, children and PWDs who face GBV and protection risks due to lack of services and lighting while 66% of HHs have at least one PWD.

5 - Implementation of activities

SDI will keep coordination with stakeholders in Syria, partner organizations and clusters throughout the project to ensure participation, complementarity and secure multi-sectoral assistance. HLP due diligence will be conducted to ensure owners' and IDPs' rights. SDI will create IDP representative committees, and sub-committees and provide them with awareness-raising on camp management, humanitarian principles, protection, security, and topics related to their roles in the camps while ensuring having members of different gender and age groups and PWD to enhance protection and participation. Fire systems will be created including selecting and training fire wardens, fire points and distribution of extinguishers in a way that ensures the participation of people from different gender and age groups and the best positioning of fire points and the fastest action in case of fire. Care and maintenance activities will ensure keeping the existing infrastructure running and functional through the creation of a committee and selecting IDPs from the camp as cash for work workers and provide them with the necessary training/briefing. Field spot-checks, active and accessible feedback mechanism, FGDs, questionnaires &periodic PDM, challenges & lessons learned log will be in place to ensure accountability and beneficiary satisfaction. The project will be handed over to in-camp committees upon project closure in coordination with CCCM cluster will ensure follow-up and enhance sustainability.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: violation of property rights, hindrance to activities or beneficiary eviction. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high; Mitigation: Conduct HLP due diligence according to clusters guidelines. Risk: Child labor, risks on workers' safety and health. Likelihood: high; Impact: high. Mitigation: zero tolerance to child labor and requirements to provide safety equipment to laborers will be included in the code of conduct and contracts signed by contractors and followed up. Risk: Explosive remnants of war and mines. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high; Mitigation: select clear sites and conduct awareness. Risk: The spread of Covid19 across NW Syria. Likelihood: high, Effect: high. mitigation: Adherence to prevention measures according to Cluster and WHO such as social distancing, using PPE and proper hygiene, and awareness-raising. Risk: Lack of participation and accountability to beneficiaries; Likelihood: moderate, Effect: high. Forming and involving representative committees including women and PWDs in all stages of the projects, including needs assessment, designing the project activities and HLP verification in addition to active information sharing through meetings and focus group discussions. An effective and well-advertised complaint mechanism is in place. GBV risks; likelihood: moderate; impact: high; mitigation: Beneficiaries will receive awareness about GBV, and complaint mechanism. Staff members will be aware of GBV referral pathway. PSEA risk discussed in section below


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA risks; likelihood: moderate; impact: high; mitigation: SDI staff, contractors’ staff, camp management, and all committees will receive awareness about GBV, PSEA, and complaint mechanism. The code of conduct, read and signed by all SDI and contractor staff, includes GBV, PSEA with zero-tolerance policy in place. ​All staff members will be well informed and committed to PSEA principles. Large PSEA banners will be visible to all beneficiaries in camps with complaint number of both SDI and UN. Also PSEA zero-tolerance banners/posters will be posted in visible places to all staff. Feedback mechanisms will include hotline, email and fixed complaint boxes in work locations besides info on social media. Mechanisms will be well-announced on banners/posters in all targeted locations and during training. Complaints will be received by the accountability officer in Turkey. A separate hotline&email (05523997418, WhatsApp&Telegram, psea@sdi.ngo) will be advertised for sensitive complaints, like, PSEA, and will be received by or referred to the protection coordinator/PSEA FP (female) in Istanbul office. All complaints will be dealt with anonymously and confidentially. All staff members will be well informed and committed to PSEA principles and aware of GBV referral pathway. Female staff will be present within the team in all positions in the field including, implementation team, MEAL team, social workers and within IDP representative committees.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: Access to all project locations is secured and safe; Risk1: Access restrictions or danger on staff and beneficiaries due to security or HLP issues. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high. Mitigation: Security assessment, HLP due diligence, coordination with local authorities, staff training, safety equipment, evacuation plan, relocation (plan B). Risk2: Closure of border crossing might affect bringing multicluster assistance to beneficiaries, especially tents and NFIs. Likelihood: High; Impact: Low. Mitigation: using local materials and advocacy. Assumption3: Needed material, workshops, and skilled and non-skilled workers are available. Risk3: Unavailability of material or workers might hinder the implementation of activities. Likelihood: very low; Impact: high. Mitigation: RNA and market assessment, procure items from a wider geographic range. Risk4: Children or residents might fall in pits or get injured during construction (of fire points) or care and maintenance works; Mitigation: Awareness raising, clear warning signs, safety tapes..etc to keep civilians out of danger zones. Risk5: Fire leading to the destruction of tents and endanger the lives of residents; Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high. Mitigation: provision of awareness, training and equipment is one of the main components of this project. Risk6: low-quality works. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: moderate. Mitigation: close follow-up, spot checks and PDM. For protection risks, please check relevant section
