Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to provide Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to the populations affected by the devastating flooding in Borno state on September 9, 2024. This initiative is designed to support vulnerable households during the critical lean season, which exacerbates food insecurity and economic instability. By offering financial assistance over a three-month period, the project seeks to stimulate recovery efforts, enhance resilience, and improve the overall well-being of the affected communities. The primary beneficiaries of this intervention will include:
- Vulnerable Households: Families in the host communities of Maiduguri, particularly those with limited access to resources and livelihoods.
-Women and Children: Special attention will be given to women-headed households and children, who are often the most affected during crises.
Key Objectives
1. Immediate Financial Relief: Provide cash assistance to help families meet their basic needs, including food, shelter, and healthcare, during the lean season. 2.Economic Recovery: Enable households to invest in income-generating activities and essential services, fostering economic recovery post-flood. 3. Community Resilience: Strengthen the capacity of affected populations to cope with future shocks through targeted support and empowerment initiatives.
Implementation Strategy
- Assessment and Targeting: Conduct a rapid needs assessment to identify the most vulnerable households and ensure equitable distribution of cash assistance.
- Cash Distribution Mechanism: Utilize a secure and efficient cash transfer system, ensuring that beneficiaries receive their funds promptly and safely.
- Monitoring and Evaluation:** Establish a robust monitoring framework to track the impact of the cash assistance on household recovery and resilience, adjusting the program as necessary based on feedback and outcomes.
This project is crucial in addressing the immediate needs of the flood-affected population in Maiduguri, providing them with the necessary support to recover and rebuild their lives during a challenging period.

1 - Needs

Between September to October 2024, some LGAs of Borno state including Maiduguri, Jere, Dikwa, Mafa, and Ngala Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Borno State, Nigeria, experienced severe flooding due to the overflow of the Alau Dam and other rivers within the Labdo river from Cameroon. This disaster followed a warning from the Nigeria Meteorological Agency (NiMet) about potential severe floods across 148 LGAs in 31 states, including Borno. The flooding has displaced over 400,000 individuals with many forced to seek refuge in internally displaced persons (IDP) camps, such as Bakassi, Dalori Camp, and Asheik Jarma School among others. The floods have submerged more than 2,850 homes, aggravating an already critical humanitarian crisis in a region previously affected by conflict, insurgency, and climate disasters in a period of soaring economic hardship. The floods have impacted approximately 239,000 individuals, with displaced populations primarily made up of vulnerable groups, including women and children (82%). These groups face heightened risks of starvation, poverty, gender-based violence (GBV), limited access to essential hygiene products, and health complications due to inadequate shelter and poor Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. These initial estimates include affected people in some of the internally displaced persons (IDP) camps in Borno State. Numbers are expected to change as more information becomes available. The floods have forced some of the affected people to move spontaneously to the Muna IDP camp, which was already hosting over 50,000 IDPs. Government authorities have evacuated residents in high-risk riverine areas to several locations. Approximately 50,000 people have been evacuated to the former Bakasi IDP camp, which has been temporarily reopened and is already at full capacity. Other temporary shelters include the former Dalori IDP camp and the Asheik Jarma school in MMC. The El Miskin camp, housing approximately 7,000 IDPs, more than half of them children, has also reportedly flooded. Access is severely restricted in many areas, affecting critical supply chains, and disrupting access to schools, healthcare facilities, and markets. Schools in Maiduguri have been temporarily closed for two weeks due to the flooding.The immediate needs of the affected population are multifaceted and urgent. Food, water, sanitation, and shelter are the most pressing concerns, with 24% of the displaced population identifying them as priorities. Contaminated or inaccessible water sources have heightened the risk of waterborne diseases, while 42% of respondents reported feeling unsafe using camp latrines due to poor conditions and fear of structural collapse. Economic vulnerability is widespread, as many households have lost their primary sources of income, and 10% of the population prioritized cash assistance to meet their basic needs. To meet the immediate needs of the displaced population. CATAI is proposing to intervene with multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) and water, sanitation, and hygiene. The MPCA component will allow the displaced population to meet their multifaceted and urgent needs in dignity and in a way that best fits their needs. The WASH component of the intervention will provide life-saving hygiene support and rehabilitation of WASH facilities to curb the risks of open defecation and the spread of infectious diseases.

2 - Activities

CATAI will conduct introductory meetings to communicate the selection criteria for beneficiaries of the Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) program. These meetings will establish a Community Project Implementation Committee (CPIC) comprising key stakeholders, including district representatives, IDP camp leaders, and members from the Borno State Emergency Management Agency. The primary focus will be on identifying households affected by the recent floods, with particular attention to those facing severe vulnerabilities, such as unemployment, lack of financial support, and loss of income sources. Key points to be addressed in the meetings include: Purpose of Assistance: Clearly define the target group and the rationale for the assistance. Target Locations:** Identify and agree on specific areas for beneficiary registration. Committee Formation:** Select committee members responsible for the registration process and develop a registration schedule. CATAI will perform a market assessment in relevant centers to evaluate market functionality and accessibility. This assessment will include mapping financial service providers to facilitate the smooth transfer of cash assistance to flood-affected households. Monthly Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives (JMMI) will be conducted to assess market conditions, focusing on key commodities necessary for the displaced population. The findings will inform the Cash Working Group (CWG) regarding the MPCA transfer value and help mitigate potential negative impacts on the market, such as inflation or commodity scarcity.To ensure protection throughout the program, CATAI will implement protection mainstreaming principles tailored to the local context. This includes establishing separate collection points for men and women to enhance safety and dignity. CATAI will also raise awareness about available hotlines for complaints and feedback, ensuring accountability and participation from affected individuals. A protection risk analysis will be conducted at the project's outset to inform beneficiary targeting and distribution processes.Once the CPIC is established, CATAI will utilize existing data from site management agencies to identify beneficiaries. This process will involve close collaboration with committee members representing affected populations, ensuring a comprehensive and inclusive approach. Special provisions will be made for individuals with disabilities, including shaded areas at registration sites. Mechanisms for handling complaints and feedback will also be integrated into the registration process.CATAI will conduct an independent verification of selected households, focusing on those meeting specific vulnerability criteria. This verification will utilize a door-to-door methodology to ensure thoroughness and accuracy. Selected households will receive Multi-Purpose Cash assistance, valued at approximately 100,000 NGN, distributed through hard tokens redeemable at approved financial service providers. Clear communication regarding the distribution process will be provided to beneficiaries. Ten days after each cash distribution, CATAI will conduct PDM to gather feedback on the effectiveness and impact of the assistance. A sample of households will be surveyed using tools developed by the CWG, with trained enumerators collecting data to inform future programming. These activities are designed to ensure effective communication, proper beneficiary selection, and the smooth implementation of cash assistance to support flood-affected households in Maiduguri.
