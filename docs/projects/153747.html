Project Summary

The conflicts which have been experienced by the people of South Sudan have made civilians loose life, fight one another, and commit many atrocities. The decades of conflict have seen the land of South Sudan with explosive hazards and many square meters of land have been thought of being contaminated. The exact extent of contaminated area is unknown with large areas in need of survey and clearance even though as UNMAS tries as much as possible to hand safe land back to the community. At the start of 2018, almost six million people in South Sudan were living with the threat of ERW, including more than 1.8 million South Sudanese internally displaced since the outbreak of fighting in 2013. 163,000 of these people are IDPs and host community members from Twic East and Duk (Jonglei) and Terekeka (Central Equatoria). According to UNMAS, surveys of internally displaced persons identified a fear of ERW as among the most significant reasons for their inability to return home. UNMAS has claimed that the socio-economic cost of mines and ERW in South Sudan in terms of interrupted agricultural production, food insecurity, halted commerce, and the lack of freedom of movement is “incalculable.” In 2017, agricultural production in South Sudan dropped compared with the previous year, attributed in large part to the mass migration of populations and inability to access safe land to cultivate crops (Min Action Monitor South Sudan 24 July 2018).
The purpose of this project is to help the individuals and communities living in Twic East, Duk and Terekeka to be safe and live a safe life without threats associated with mine/ERWs. The project will benefit 25,000 people (6,000 women, 4,000 men, 8,000 girls, and 7,000 boys) who include IDPs and returnees, and host community) who have a lot of potential in socio-economic activities which can be of benefit to the nation economic building.
In addition to the threats posed to the safety of the community members, explosive hazards prevent the safe movement of the populations including those who must flee active conflict. During such transitions, the most vulnerable groups such as women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities sometimes lose their lives on the way if the journey takes too long before they finally find a place to settle. Explosive hazards preclude the effective delivery of humanitarian aid and inhibit socioeconomic development activities. Terekeka, Twic East and Duk have critical mine actions needs and mine risk education (MRE) activities will help provide both direct and indirect protection to the community members. MRE activities are key to ensure that people’s behaviour towards unexploded ordinances and ERW within their surrounding change for better. Funds towards mine action projects by other humanitarian actors will also enable individuals return and settle in their previous homes free of any harm and danger.

Needs

Estimating the true extent of landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) contamination within South Sudan boundaries is a real challenge. In Twic East, 2 HAs have been confirmed in March 2018 killing 2 children and injured 9, while 3 HAs have been confirmed in Duk at the same period. Contamination has been recorded in Terekeka where 47 HAs covering area of 1,420,101 m2 have been reported.This is significant especially in the areas of Jonglei where CINA has been implementing mine risk education (MRE) activities for more than 5 years now. Despite several years of interventions by different mine action actors in regards to MRE awareness activities in Jonglei with support from UNICEF and UNMAS, many community members are still engaged in practicing risky behaviors that cause injuries and death. This is evident also by the fact that although a significant number of mine accidents that are reported through mine action non-governmental organizations and UN, the exact number of mine casualties in the Country is still not known and is considered to be more higher than the number of the cases reported (IMSMA, 2003-2017). The community members need to receive more MRE to be in the know of the dangers encompassing their lives when they approach unknown substances that can be harmful to their lives or to those who live among them in the same locality. Such unknown objects may include anti-personnel mines, anti-vehicle mines, abandoned explosive ordinances, unexploded ordinances among others. It is therefore imperative that MRE activities should be integrated in all humanitarian and development plans and strategies by different mine action actors. While the current revitalized peace agreement is being celebrated across the Country an influx of returnees are expected to be crossing borders back to their origin homes within South Sudan. These type of population stand at a more higher risk of injuries and death from mines and ERW due to lack of knowledge of local threats compared to those who had continued to live in the area during and after war. The returnees, refugees and IDPs are also less likely to have received MRE activities within the areas where they would finally settle which will pose their lives at danger as opposed to the host community. There would be need to conduct MRE activities targeting children especially those without appropriate care. Children who are living without family structures or with no care from foster families are often subject to dangers associated to mine risk because most of the time they are on the move in search of food, shelter or otherwise. Men and boys who are tasked with grazing, hunting or any outside activity together with women and girls who move from place to place gathering firewood are all at high-risk of coming in contact with mine and ERWs.

Activities

The community-led MRE activities will involve a number of activities for the success of the project. These will include the below: 1. Print Information, education and communication (IEC) materials such as t-shirts, posters with key mine risk education key messages.The information will help communicate to the affected population on the dangers of the mine objects and ERWs and to help them understand what they need to do when they notice any foreign/unusual objects. 2. Conduct mine risk education awareness raising campaigns. Children are said to have never constantly obeyed their parents but have never failed to imitate them. With children playing and walking across infected places in their communities, they will be vigilant to do and act as their parents/caregivers/elderly people do. The children will also act as agents of safety when they relay the information that is given to them. 3. Conduct sessions with child protection, health and FSL staff to improve coordination in service delivery of MRE activities.Having an integrated approach across all sectors will help Mine action actors receive information related to mine/ERWs fast and help in effective decision making. Equipping every sector with key MRE messages will help the humanitarian actors take care of their own safety as they go around responding in their project locations. 4. Support extremely vulnerable mine/ERW survivors with case management referral servicesMine/ERWs has changed the lives of many especially those with acute actions such as death, loss of limbs, among others. These acts sometimes leave the dependants who are mostly children as their parents normally go out in search of food, firewood, grazing and they meet their fate while on the search. Supporting the mine/ERW and PWDs survivors gives them an opportunity to start their life again and build it up without being a ‘burden’ to others as maybe perceived. 5. Conduct awareness raising sessions on key MRE messages in schools.During war, buildings such as schools were used as hide outs and a place to kill in mass. Most abandoned school building during the war which could have been renovated maybe harbouring some of the hazardous objects. Educating the school children to play on only safe areas keep them out of danger. The children consequently share the information with their family members when they return at home thereby reaching a bigger target group as compared to educating people from house to house. 6. Conduct one-day training 100 teachers on MRE messages to impart to the children at school.Teachers who are trained on MRE messages directly share the information with the children at school during break times or as the school schedule may allow. Other than waiting for appointments to have all the children sit together, teachers can be trained and impart the same knowledge. 7. Carry out awareness raising session to children in the community.Not every child in South Sudan is lucky to get access to education. There are children on the move (street children), children without appropriate care and UASC who may not be in any of the classrooms but can be met within the community such as child friendly spaces (CFS). Having all categories of children educated on MRE messages will be key to the safety of their environment. CINA will deliver risk education to conflict-affected populations, particularly targeting the vulnerable groups such as the returnees, IDPs and children. 8. Establish and support 3 MRE teams. One team in Duk, one team in Twic East and one in Terekeka. Each RE team will be composed of four staff each, including 1 MRE Officer/TL, 2 MRE Assistants and 1 driver. The teams will be trained and accredited and provided with Task Dosiers to conduct MRE activities in the target locations9. Identify and report any suspected hazard areas, mine incidents and accidents as timely as possible for quick marking and clearance by Mine Clearance agencies.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Intercluster working group meetings will be an entry point for CINA to respond to issues that could be emerging from communication with communities working group and CINA will strive to provide the needed technical support including actors mapping, harmonization between organizational approaches and charting the way forward. Information dissemination to beneficiaries is via MRE materials (leaflets, banners, posters, T shirts), radio messages, community sessions and training.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, CINA will be using the already standardized MRE messages which can be obtained from UNICEF. These are critical messages that warns, educates and advises the community on the dangers of mines and ERWs, safe areas to be used, what kind of objects should be avoided, areas which could be infected by mines or ERWs amongst other messages. They are specifically designed to communicate to the community people and those who use particular areas even though they could be just passers-by. The messages are designed to speak to all age-groups with different colours conveying a message such as red means danger and white means the area is safe to use.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible, CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve this.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

There has been engagement with community members (men, women, boys, girls, and children) because effective communication strategy is two way and helps CINA in ensuring that there is accountability toward the affected population and such partnership builds on strengthening the service delivery and stronger decision making by the community on matters which affect them directly. Those consulted include youth groups, women groups, PWDs, Key informants, fellow mine action actors, community & church leaders among others. However, there will be revision of the feedback mechanisms to ensure that concerns of the affected population are addressed and the community understands how CINA and other mine action actors operate in the project areas. Through the DPOs, the mine survivors and PWDs will be able to channel their communications to the organization. CINA will also work with the existing structures including the government administrative units at the payam levels.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The community-based feedback mechanisms that are already established by CINA in the child protection sector will continue to be entry points for receiving and addressing issues related to mine/ERWs survivors. Beneficiary Complaint mechanisms will be established at the community level and feedback will be collected using a form, complaint box or hotline number. Banners on SEA awareness and reporting will also be displayed at the CFS sites and in the schools. In particular, CP desks in the schools will be used to receive feedback from community members.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CINA in its monitoring and evaluation activities engages all the project stakeholders at all levels. The engagement at the community level is even more critical because that is where the beneficiaries are located and they can tell whether the project has an impact or not. Engaging the community does not just start and end with the local authorities on the ground, however, CINA will work with different categories of people including the vulnerable groups such as DPOs, mine/ERWs survivors, parents of children with disabilities among others.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Conducting mine risk education activities is very critical in the area because being in contact with UXOs, ERWs and other mine objects are a matter of life or death. Therefore CINA is compelled to save lives by providing the protection risk analysis to all the host community, returnees and IDPs in Jonglei. MRE staff will inform other mine action actors such as UNMAS, UNMISS and the State and non-State parties of any incidents or casualties so that decision making and response are aligned to saving more lives. Through collective protection risk analysis, CINA will ensure that the principle of Do No Harm is observed and that humanitarian responses do not violate international humanitarian laws. MRE activities will be integrated within the current CINA programmes of child protection, health and FSL. Best practices show that protection mainstreaming offers more quality response since many needs are met across gender and age-groups.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

There are various barriers to access for the mine/ERW survivors which include but not limited to negative attitudes towards PWDs, long distances to schools, poverty, insecurity in some areas, negative cultural perceptions, lack of assertive devices, lack of road pathways for PWDs, bad roads, lack of special needs schools and teachers, inaccessible school facilities and infrastructures such as ramps, toilets for PWDs. CINA will work closely with other mine action actors to try the wide gap that has been caused by the above challenges. Advice will be provided where services can be offered to those who are challenged by access

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will consider monthly quality assurance monitoring of MRE activities to check whether all children in schools, community have been accessing MRE activities in line with SOPs and , FGDs with caregivers and community based child protection committees will be conducted to establish this. Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

CINA will educate the community members on how to mark the locations where the hazardous objects are located with the famous marks used by UNMAS. All the beneficiaries will be urged to share the information with their neighbours, friends, passersby to take note of the marked signs and keep off. All age groups will be involved in information dissemination related to the mine objects, UXOs and ERWs. A further action to notify UNMAS and follow ups monitoring until the ordinances are cleared will be taken including notifying the local authorities who will also inform the community leaders to be vigilant of the infected areas.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

CINA treats all vulnerable groups of people as human beings without impartiality. The PWDs, mine/ERW survivors will not be considered like objects of war but people who are in need. CINA M&E Manager will access all information related to the beneficiaries which will inform for decision making. There are mixed MRE Teams which comprise both men and women so that it would be necessary for the beneficiaries to give information to whichever gender they feel more comfortable with. This is because some cultural norms may hinder information flow and some may feel inferior to give valid/sensitive information.
