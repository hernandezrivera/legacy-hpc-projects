Project Summary

During 2023 Violet aims to implement projects to improve the livelihoods of the most vulnerable in NWS and infrastructure rehabilitation through the implementation of the following activities:
1. Creating temporary employment opportunities through the implementation of Cash for work related to ERL sector to rehabilitate and restore the community infrastructure. Targeting 1650 people
2. Youth capacity building through the provision of Vocational training to enable them to start their own businesses targeting 200 peoples
3. Provision of Medium cash grants to allow the vocational trainees to start their own businesses targeting 200 people
4. Establishment of VSLAs groups within targeted communities to provide increased access to business, emergency, or social funds for its members in distress. Targeting 25 groups of 500 people
5. Improve the access of the farmers to farmlands through road rehabilitation in order to support the farmers in marketing crops, transportation, and marketing their products as well as improving their access to the farmlands; the proposed target for this activity is to rehabilitate 10 KM of roads serving 6000 people within targeted communities.
6. rehabilitation of 10 basic productive infrastructures (Markets/warehouses) within targeted communities serving 3100 people within targeted communities.

During the project implementation, Violet will prioritize women-headed households, PWD, youth, and other vulnerable groups to participate in the program. Moreover, the project will be designed and implemented in a way to ensure the inclusion of PWD in the program implementation, mainly the vocational training and Business grants. To this end, Violet will ensure the training halls are PWD-friendly spaces and easy to access.
VO will ensure CVA coordination and harmonization at the area level with other aid providers through ERL cluster and filed level coordination meetings and FGDs prior to each activity to avoid any possible overlapping with other aid providers and also reaching the most vulnerable households with support.
Furthermore, VO, through this project, aims to foster social cohesion within targeted communities and building a sense of ownership by local communities through the inclusion of different community groups, including the most vulnerable in the project activities design and implementation.
Regarding CFW activity: referring to the Cash for Work Minimum Recommended Actions Guidance for NWS developed by CWG and ERL partners: https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/stima/document/nws-cash-work-recommended-minimum-actions-mar-2021Violet will ensure Do No Harm approach/principles in this specific activity through consult community/local stakeholders at the area of implementation on project design, especially on the daily wage rate, number of work hours per day, and project duration to ensure CFW daily wage rates do not distort local labor market and draw workers away from local employment opportunities, therefore harming local producers and arbitrarily introducing non-sustainable practices.
All proposed activities will be delivered in adherence to the humanitarian and do-no-harm principles and ensure equal access all beneficiaries in need without discrimination while inclusively engaging them in decision making throughout the whole project phases. Violet will work to ensure gender and protection are mainstreamed into all activities by prioritizing the specific and often sensitive issues related to gender, disability, age, and origin among the communities. The data has been analyzed with age and sex and disability perspectives.
Collecting and monitoring data disaggregated by sex, age, and disability condition. Monitoring and overseeing barriers to access to services. Violet has an active feedback and response mechanism There are many channels to receive and deal with complaints.
Out of the project amount, 65,000 USD is already secured.

3 - Needs

According to the 2022 Syria HNO, Early Recovery and Livelihoods needs continue to rise. Expectations of a further increase in the cost of living, including reduction of subsidies and general price increases, as well as volatility in the exchange rate, among others, are projecting onto an additional deterioration in terms of real income and purchasing power across all population groups. Employment creation is expected to be insufficient to significantly change the negative income/expenditure gap in the short term. A period of further economic contraction is expected, with reduced production, increased rates and severity of poverty, and further socio-economic deterioration affecting both IDPs, residents, and returnees. Furthermore, displacements from areas still facing hostilities to relatively stable areas are likely to continue increasing demand for essential services, employment opportunities, and the risk of intra and inter-community tensions involving residents and displaced. Durable Solutions are critically needed to enable returns, reintegration, or resettlement for displaced persons¬ across Syria. Needs associated with Durable Solutions are multi-sectoral, requiring, among other responses, rehabilitation of Health, WASH, Agriculture, and Education services and infrastructure, resumption of economic activity; protections and support for HLP rights, in parallel initiatives to foster social cohesion among the returnees and the host communities.

4 - Implementation of activities

VO aims to respond to emergency needs by the implementation of different activities with an appropriate modality based on the needs, and BNFs preference availability, however; this project includes CVA, so prior to each activity, VO will assess market systems functionality/capacity to absorb increased demand for goods and services and potential impact on markets and non-cash recipients accessing the same markets.Moreover, will engage project stakeholders in the design and implementation to ensure the local ownership of the project. Thus, VO will sign MOUs with the LCs demonstrating their roles and responsibilities. Moreover, VO will ensure close coordination with the ERL cluster and humanitarian actors in the field to avoid potential overlapping.During the implementation, VO will ensure the BNFs protection and do No Harm principle by consulting the BNFs on the distribution location, Timing and arranging the priority ques for elderlies, women, and PWD, but also through training the staff on the Organization code of conduct and PSEA policy. Additionally.To avoid the tension that may occur between the communities and different groups, VO will include a % of the most vulnerable HC members in the project but also will announce the project criteria and make sure it's clear to the BNFs and activate the CRM mechanism.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of market volatility:Likelihood: medium; Impact: HighViolet will keep monitoring the markets and prices in the markets and will adapt to the changes in close coordination with the cluster and CWG guidance2) At-risk groups do not receive needed servicesLikelihood: Medium; Impact: MediumCoordination with relevant sectors, collaboration with local protection team to ensure equitable access to services, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, systematic use of Washington Group Questions as a tool to include disability in the selection, considering intersectionality in targeting3) HLP risk:likelihood: low, impact: highMitigationAbide by the HLP guidance document, Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed MOU, and Have an HLP section in M&E assessment form The assistance is tailored to each group by considering their specific needs - e.g. PWDs with limited mobility and women with higher protection risks. When conducting activities, protection and genderconsideration will be mainstreamed. For instance, during the activities, the ability of men/women/PWDs will be considered for the work load and distribution of assets. as well as their access to information to participate in the provided activities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

VO targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements.Potential risks:1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the servicesMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasize on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision.2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staffMitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded distribution sites and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation or delivery, participation of protection team in the distribution if neededViolet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure the participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in thedesign of the activities and monitoring


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: activities implementation is stopped by violence or interference by the opposition groupsMitigation 1: monitoring the security situation and reporting criminal violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation.Risks 2: fees might be collected by the LCs or camp management in return for assistance leading to Aid diversionMitigation 2: Ensure the humanitarian nature of the project by communicating the red flags with the BNFs and community leaders. In case of aid diversion, the activity will be suspended, and the incident will be reported to OCHA. stakeholders will be involved to facilitate the activities and get their supportRisk 3: markets are not functioning properlyMitigation 3: VO confirmed the markets are functioning, and the security situation allows the implementation. In case the situation changes, VO would consider relocating the areas of intervention where the required conditions are met.Risk 4: Diversion of cash when using (MTA)Mitigation 4: VO disposes of 3 FWA with MTAs for all CBR activities, and its currently in the process of contracting with new MTAs. the MTAs will be vetted by an ATC check (in both UK and USA ATC platforms). Risk 5: Tension might occur between the HC and IDPs if targeted BNFs of cash assistance (in USD) are only IDPs, Particularly with SYP devaluation and the Covid-19 outbreak, when HC HHs/non-BNFs are economically vulnerable, and they lose their purchasing power.
