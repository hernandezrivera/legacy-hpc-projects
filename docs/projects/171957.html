Project Summary

The protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine is in its seventh year, and multiple attempts at ceasefires have yielded little respite from ongoing violence. Conflict has compounded pre-existing economic and social vulnerabilities which challenge the ability of the population – particularly the most vulnerable groups, including women and the elderly – to achieve economic security and are exacerbated by the effects of COVID-19.
Food security remains of concern within 20km of the LoC, especially for women and the elderly, who make up most of the population in the area (59% and 34%, respectively). According to the ACCESS consortium (2020), 96% of the elderly, or those 60 and older, (75% of which are women) stated they need food assistance and 68% indicated food as the 1st priority for them. Only 53% of older men and women indicated they had food stock for a period of 2-7 days. 43% of the elderly (86% women) live alone - when coupled with the fact that 81% of older people have limited mobility and may need the help of others, accessing food/markets becomes extremely difficult. Additionally, the onset of COVID-19 has added an extra level of precariousness to the situation. An ACTED-led rapid market analysis (April 2020) showed significant changes in accessing markets and goods since the deterioration of the epidemiological situation: 86% of residents within 20km of GCA reported goods increased in price since February 2020, 38% reported shop and market closure in the area, and 15% reported essential goods were not available. Average price of food in April had increased 11.6% compared to February. This is especially difficult on older men and women as 99% rely on a pension as their main source of income (ACCESS, 2020).
Food insecurities created by COVID-19 coupled with those predating the pandemic have made the need for humanitarian assistance that increases purchasing power and access to food even more urgent.
ACTED therefore proposes an intervention to increase the coping capacity of some of the most vulnerable conflict-affected populations within 20km of the LoC in Eastern Ukraine.
First, ACTED will address levels of food insecurity among conflict-affected populations just following winter, when household resources are depleted and with the expectation that the rapidly increasing COVID-19 cases will only compound this situation, with a likely early exhaustion of HH resources as restriction measures mean a loss of livelihoods; and during the next winter, the season when food insecurity levels are observed to be highest. The project thus aims to reduce food insecurity of vulnerable individuals, especially women and the elderly, within 20km of the LoC. Targeting settlements with access to functional markets, ACTED will provide unconditional cash transfers to 4,400 individuals at the value of 825 UAH/person/month in 2 distributions: 3 months of assistance in spring 2021 to 1,400 individuals (in 1 tranche), and 5 months of assistance in winter 2021 to 3,000 individuals (in 2 tranches).
Second, ACTED will distribute grants to the most vulnerable (women, heads of households who are unemployed but ineligible for pensions, single mothers, people who lost their income source as a result of COVID-19) to support their ability to produce food and/or generate additional revenues. 525 individuals (250 HH) will be supported with grants valued at 1,200 USD to increase overall consumption patterns and cover basic food needs. Grants will also cover expenses such as the purchase of consumables, value investments (greenhouses, machines) and training. In addition, beneficiaries will be referred for trainings delivered by partner CSOs or other NGOs/state programs to promote long-term sustainability of livelihoods.
Ultimately, by increasing access to food and restoring livelihoods, ACTED will enhance resilience of vulnerable people in the Eastern Conflict Area.

1 - Needs

As conflict continues, economic recovery lags, and COVID-19 continues to spread, the food security situation remains urgent in Eastern Ukraine. Provision of and access to basic services and markets have been disrupted especially close to the line of contact (LoC), and Ukraine is also one of the most mine-contaminated areas globally (HALO Trust), which hinders agrarian-based farming methods on which communities in Eastern Ukraine may otherwise subsist. Food consumption remains low compared to pre-crisis. Food security needs are highest and most acute closest to the LoC. Within 20km of GCA, 12% of HHs surveyed are moderately to severely food insecure (REACH 2020). There is widespread erosion of coping strategies during/following winter, when food insecurity is highest with 43% of HHs targeted by ACTED during this period demonstrating poor Food Consumption Scores (ACTED 2019).Socio-economic vulnerability remains high in areas closest to LoC (area within 5km) and COVID-19 has compounded the situation. HH who were once less vulnerable, eg. 5-20km from LoC, are now facing severe and extreme need due to increased lay-offs and restricted access to goods and services (ACTED 2020)HHs that are women/single-headed, have a member with a disability, have a member with chronic illness, or are headed by a person above 60 are more likely to experience food insecurity. Single-headed HHs are 40% more likely to be moderately or severely food insecure than the general population, especially those headed by women. Women have fewer economic and livelihood opportunities and are more likely to be dependent on men breadwinners; single women-headed HHs are further strained on food consumption and socio-economic opportunities. Data from REACH 2020 MSNA and ACTED’s 2019 vulnerability assessment show that within the population within 20km of the LoC:- Pensioner and pre-pensioner headed HHs are most likely to be food insecure among age groups (13%, both);- Women-headed HHs are more likely to be food insecure than male-headed HHs (13% vs 9%);- HHs without children and single-member HHs tend to be more food insecure (17% and 21%).- Single-headed HHs are 2.5 times more likely to be food insecure than dual-headed HHs (20%).- Over 20% of HHs with a member with a disability are food insecure.- 38% of HH with IDPs report experiencing problems with accessing food markets (compare to 28% of non-IDP HH)With private investment unlikely, employment and/or entrepreneurship opportunities are limited. Incomes are unlikely to rise, given the economic downturn (closure of certain industries, out-migration of skills workers, etc.). Though unemployment among the working-age population is slightly lower (8.8%) in 2019, COVID-19 will likely increase those numbers in 2021 (FSL Cluster, 2020). As those living within 20km to the LoC is older than average (34% are 60+ yo), largely (59%) female, and often retired or unemployed over the age of 50, they struggle to find livelihoods opportunities. Almost all (99.4%) of older men and women rely on a pension as their main source of income (ACCESS, 2020). Moreover, entrepreneurship grant schemes often impose age limits on beneficiary selection and are unlikely to target areas close to the LoC due to the higher risk of business failure. Most development and recovery programmes do not include older individuals in rural/small-town populations within the 20km zone. However, residents close to the LoC, including the most vulnerable (elderly women living alone, long-term unemployed, single mothers), have shown resilience despite facing an increasing gap between incomes and actual subsistence level. Most HHs, women especially, have engaged in small trade, handicraft, vegetable/fruit gardens or value addition (jams, canning, pickling) even if the conflict has depleted their ability/necessary capital to do so. With support, they could restore their livelihoods, enhance their economic security, and reduce dependency on aid.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcomes • Access to food is increased for the most vulnerable • Livelihoods are restored for most vulnerable Cash-for-food ACTED will distribute unconditional cash transfers (UCTs) to 4,400 indv. living within 20km of the LoC. Beneficiaries will be selected based on ACTED’s methodology which uses HH status to target its HH level food security assessment. Potential beneficiaries are assessed using a CARI based methodology adapted to the Ukrainian context by the FSLC with support from ACTED. Based on REACH and ACTED program data, ACTED will target: • HH of elderly, esp. single isolated elderly; • HH with members with disabilities/chronically ill; • Women headed HH; • HH with unemployed members of pre-pension age (esp. 50-59 yo); • HH with multiple children, esp. headed by single parents; • HH of/with IDPs;• HH which lost their income because of COVID-19.UCTs are an appropriate modality as markets in GCA are functional and communities along the LoC generally have access to food products. An ACTED-led rapid market assessment in April 2020 also showed 91% of people cite cash as the best modality of assistance in the COVID-19 context.In 2019-20, 98% of ACTED’s beneficiaries under its latest food security program spent 77% of cash assistance on food. UCTs are a beneficiary preference: 100% of ACTED’s 2020 beneficiaries were satisfied with a cash modality. Those who do not prefer a cash modality referred to other actors who distribute in-kind.ACTED works through Financial Service Providers (FSPs) covering all locations within 20km, incl. through home delivery. ACTED can thus implement in isolated areas and reach beneficiaries with limited mobility/disability/domestic care burden. ACTED will distribute 825 UAH/person/month for 3 months in early spring (1400 indv.) and 5 months in the beginning of winter (3000 indv.). As per previous years, this methodology implies three months of winter assistance will spill over into 2022 (Jan-March). These three months will not be duplicated in the 2022 HRP.Livelihoods grants In line with nexus/early recovery priorities, the plan for a humanitarian exit strategy, and recommendations of the FSL Cluster for implementation of livelihood activities during COVID-19, ACTED will distribute of 250 livelihood grants to support 250 vulnerable HHs (2.1 people per HH or 525 indv.) who are not eligible for other livelihood programming due to eg. age limits, with livelihoods restoration grants. ACTED will focus its targeting on HHs of unemployed, incl. those who lost their income because of COVID-19, women/single headed HHs with dependents, pre-pensioners, and the elderly. Grants will be provided through cash (through FSPs), vouchers, or in-kind support depending on market feasibility and beneficiary preferences. Grants will cover expenses such as the purchase of consumables, value investments, and training. Beneficiaries will also be referred for training under a complementary GAC-funded program delivered by partner CSOs or other NGOs/state programs.Community input will be taken through community meetings and distribution of materials that describe the program. ACTED will disseminate information on its complaints and response mechanism (CRM), and ask for referrals. Local authority representatives, community leaders and volunteers will be engaged to verify preliminary lists for cash-for-food beneficiaries as per vulnerability profiles targeted under the project to cross-check and notify ACTED if any vulnerable HHs/beneficiaries were not included in the first round. For livelihood grants, ACTED will share information to communities to reach various age and disability groups; ACTED will target and select based on needs rather than status. ACTED will follow-up regularly with beneficiaries to ensure they are able to use the grant efficiently (eg. through referrals to social protection or complementary capacity-building services) and evaluate the effectiveness of the activity post-int
