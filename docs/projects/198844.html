Project Summary

The project will strengthen the protection system, inclusive child protection, MHPSS and general protection through technical capacity building of communities, partners and the government to prevent and respond to violence against girls, boys and their families, with improved access to quality services, coordination, and advocacy on child rights monitoring mechanisms and meaningful children's participation.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The humanitarian situation in Cabo Delgado Province has significantly deteriorated over 2020-2022. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix from June 2022 indicates a total of 869,603 IDPs have been displaced in Cabo Delgado due to insecurity, 55% being children, women represent 24% and men 21%. Increased fear of attacks in Nangade and Quissanga have caused new displacements and migrations into neighboring districts with increased movement towards Tanzania through the border point of Negomano in Mueda district. Save the Children will coordinate with other humanitarian actors and government to provide access to safe, quality, gender responsive, and inclusive child protection services. SC will provide case management and referrals for the most vulnerable girls and boys and caregivers, affected by ongoing violence and displacement in Cabo Delgado and Nampula, including family tracing and reunification (FTR) and protective interim/ foster care arrangements for separated and unaccompanied children. SC will provide structured mental health and psychosocial support services to UASC, their families and communities through signature approaches like HEART, TEAMUP and Child and Youth Resilience Programme in safe child friendly spaces, mobile and home-based psychosocial support. Through Caring for Child Survivors of sexual abuse approach, and aligned to IASC standards for GBV case management, SCI will ensure survivors of sexual gender-based violence (SGBV) access to quality care. SC will strengthen community-based child protection mechanisms in cooperation with local structures and members of communities in collaboration with Department of Social Welfare. The project will enhance advocacy to reduce violence against girls and boys affected by adversity in Cabo Delgado and Nampula, including monitoring and documentation of the protection and human rights violations against children and communities affected by conflict. Target Population.The project aims to reach 75523 people, IDPS and host community (40027 girls, 35496 boys) with integrated protection and survival services for child survivors, focusing on, UASC, CAAFAG and children with disabilities from IDP, asylum seekers and host community in Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Save the Children will implement priority protection activities across districts in the two provinces of operation: Priority activities will include:• Case management. Using the SC Steps to Protect approach, UASC and host children will receive case management and support services including legal, medical, timely and appropriate referrals, MHPSS, including child survivors of sexual abuse. • Family tracing, reunification and alternative care. Identification, registration and access to documentation will be provided for UASC, and CAR. SC will facilitate emergency community-based alternative care including the reunification of separated and unaccompanied children with their families or place in long-term community-based care.• Individual protection assistance through the provision of dignity kits, and n-kind Key targets will include adolescent girls, child mothers, and families hosting IDPs that have experienced multiple displacements. • Mental health and psychosocial support for UASC, families and child survivors of violence. HEART, TEAMUP, and Child and Youth Resilience programme approaches will enable structured focused and non- focused group and individual activities, counselling, and referrals for specialized services in constructed Safe child and youth-friendly spaces. • CAFAAG and the community will be provided with reintegration support to children freed from armed forces, and community-based risk mitigation, preparedness, and resilience programming(Mueda)Community based protection monitoring. Community-based protection volunteers or focal points will be trained and equipped to monitor and report incidences of violence, including grave violations. • Advocacy on the establishment of a monitoring and reporting mechanism in Mozambique. Sc will also provided technical support and capacity building on laws and policies to government and partners on the Kampala Convention, and other international standards relating to the protection of IDPs.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will ensure that the most vulnerable boys and girls, women and men will be identified and reached. Guided by Save the Children Gender Equality policy and leveraging on the global IASC standards for gender equality, the project will conduct gender assessments in the communities using the gender equality marker. Gender and safety audits, will enable identification of deep-rooted gender inequalities including harmful traditional practices to girls boys, women and men, and using participatory community appraisals and risk mapping exercise, SC will empower the community to recognize and do away with harmful traditional practices like child marriage, sex preference among others, that are a barrier to gender equality. The project will work with relevant agencies including the UN, PSEA network and coordination office, and the government to build capacity of girls and women through leadership and equality trainings, mentorship and empower programme, and boys and men champions to create awareness on gender equality and its positive impact to families and the community. The project will develop and utilize standardized protection and PSEA tools, e,g code of conduct, SOPs, checklists, case referral pathways including information education and communication materials (IECs ) that will enable all sectors and programmes to identify and mitigate risks to women and children in the camp settings where we operate. Specifically, it will enable staff, partners and community-based protection structured to safeguard women and children from the risk of Gender-based Violence (GBV), including the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), and other forms of abuse and harm, such as physical abuse, neglect and emotional harm. The protection tools will support safe programming and a safe environment for women and children, including those who have disabilities to speak out.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will ensure access to services is guided and based on a vulnerability assessment that is inclusive, gender-sensitive considering, age and ability. Assessments, registration and inclusion criteria will be guided by the Washington Group Questions ensuring persons with disability are consulted and included in the project. SCI will collaborate with DPOs including FAMOD,AIFO and UN family for strategic planning, advocacy and implementation of activities. SC will work with DPOs to ensure capacity strengthening of partners, communities and government on disability and inclusion standards, tools and approaches and general child protection. A mentorship and coaching approach will be employed for quality assurance, reaching higher numbers of children with disability (at least 5% of total target) improving accessibility and responding to needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Save the Children prioritises the Centrality of Protection and the proposed intervention considers context-specific vulnerabilities, including those based on gender dynamics, and a need for conflict sensitivity approaches throughout the entire project lifecycle. To ensure environmental considerations, SC conducts conflict-sensitive and security assessments, with the aim to understand the context in which we operate, understand the interaction between our interventions and the context, and act upon this understanding to minimize negative impacts and maximise positive impacts of our organisation on conflict factors. SC ensures risk mitigations and management at all levels from senior management to field level, ensuring operations and interventions are safe and adhere to both national and international environmental management standards and practices.
