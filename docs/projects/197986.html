Project Summary

To contribute to the overall WASH objective and ensure populations affected by displacement have reduced risk of exposure to WASH related diseases, Salient will strengthen populations’ capacity to engage in safe practices to promote better hygiene behaviors to limit health risks associated with poor WASH practices. Salient will use a range of technical approaches to provide the hardware required for access to safe water, improved hygiene and safe sanitation.
Target group
Salient H.O will target the most vulnerable individuals and communities affected by crisis including IDPs and vulnerable host communities. Salient plan WASH interventions in the BAY states aims to reach 27,500 persons (15,850 females and 11,650 males). The following beneficiaries will be targeted
1. Internally Displaced Persons: Salient seeks to address the needs of vulnerable persons who have been displaced as a result of the different crises in the BAY States. These include:
a) persons affected by multiple displacements situation with limited access to WASH services; b) protracted displacements, host communities, and IDPs living in collective buildings with limited access to WASH services c) those who are unable to return for security reasons to their area of origin or who have not consented to reintegration in displacement areas for one reason or another.
2. Returnees (includes refugee returnees): These include IDPs returning back to their original locations of displacement.
3. Vulnerable Host Communities: These include vulnerable families living within communities hosting at least 50% IDPs as compared to the host population. Competition over limited resources over time can result to tensions which may cause further harm
4. Persons living with disabilities and those with special needs: These group include the disabled, persons with special needs and the elderly. By bringing water points and sanitation facilities closer to populations, SHO considers that beneficiaries will be less exposed to protection risks and will have more time to engage in income generating activities for women and school attendance for children.

1 - Needs

According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) between 2021 and 2022, the BAY states experienced an increased number from 3M to 5M people in need of WASH humanitarian assistance, across internally displaced persons (+29%), returnees (+ 113%) and hosting communities (+95%).In 2022, access to safe water, dignified sanitation and hygiene awareness was impacted by heavy flooding, camp closures, and the movement of IDPs and returnees. This has led to negative coping mechanisms including drinking water from unsafe sources, increased open defecation and poor personal and household-level hygiene, resulting namely in an unprecedented cholera outbreak. Further displacement due to conflict has led to poor access to safe water, and congestion in camps has resulted in limited access to safe sanitation with partners resorting to manual dislodging facing heavy flooding, moreover with some challenging access to the dumping sites. Hygiene awareness and personal hygiene at the household level have been impacted by the high cost of living which has resulted in a lack of soap at the household level, leaving vulnerable populations, especially the elderly and children under 5 at high risk of diseases.Access to sanitation continues to be diminished due to wear and tear of emergency type construction, continuous displacements and seasonal ooding leading to high open defecation and reduced handwashing practices. Sanitation needs for persons with special needs, women and children to ensure safe and equitable access are a critical gap in the sector, which requires additional resources as other issues of proximity and design of the facilities are considered.

2 - Activities

SANITATION: Improve access to sanitation, by ensuring the availability of gender-segregated sanitation facilities to the affected population, through: • Provision of safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities through desludging and cleaning of latrines as per sector’s standard. • Provision of safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities through construction of new sanitation facilities (latrines and showers); • Rehabilitation and upgrading of existing sanitation facilities; operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities; desludging of latrines; • Provision of hand washing stations inclusive of soap at public sanitation facilities and institutions;• Construction of sanitation facilities in institutions and relevant public places provided with adequate gender segregated sanitation facilities (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) and services as per sector’s standard and in consultation with the community.• Provision of waste bins and waste disposal pits and sites; and transportation of garbage to disposal sites.• Establishment and training of community-based water management committees for boreholes, HYGIENE: SHO will promote in local languages culturally appropriate hygiene messages for social behavioral change through the following activities,• Affected communities benefit from community tailored gender and age-sensitive personal and environmental hygiene messages aiming to promote hygienic behavior and practices through sensitization sessions with community members and stakeholders to minimize the spread of water borne diseases but not limited to cholera and AWD Malaria etc. • Establishment of WASH Committees and provision of WASH facility maintenance tools;• Establishment of School Hygiene Clubs; training of Hygiene Promoters and conducting hygiene promotion activities;• Cholera preparedness and response activities;• Distribution of hygiene, WASH NFI and menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits, production and dissemination of contextualized Information,• Education and agreed Communication (IEC) materials. • Conducting Safety audits on WASH facilities and consultations with key informants.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SHO will ensure Mainstreaming protection through the inclusion of humanitarian protection principles into the crisis response by ensuring that any response is provided in a way that avoids any unintended negative effects (do-no-harm), is delivered according to needs, prioritizes safety and dignity, is grounded on participation and empowerment of local capacities, and ultimately holds humanitarian actor’s accountable vis–à-vis affected individuals and communities. SHO will seek to ensure that affected communities’ needs are addressed and human rights are upheld and their needs fully subscribes to the concept of Protection Mainstreaming and Centrality of Protection. SHO will ensure that it provides humanitarian assistance that is impartial prioritizing all without due regard to legal status since all affected population irrespective of their status are entitled to humanitarian assistance and protection

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SHO will conduct a project inception workshop with all project stakeholders. During this workshop, Salient will detailed project information including budget. Salient will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, SHO will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons with special Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision-making process. SHO will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the key stake holders. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the SHO Protection unit. SHO has already established complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM) in many sites across Borno State which has been functioning well as two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors.SHO has already taken several steps to ensure accountability to affected population: findings from beneficiary surveys, some of which have already been incorporated into the design of this project. SHO will ensure that all interventions are tailored towards monitoring by conducting PDM, Post intervention monitoring and FGCs throughout the project circle.
