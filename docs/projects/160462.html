Project Summary

This project will focus on providing and Increasing equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection, treatment, prevention of acute malnutrition for children U5 years and pregnant and lactating women targeting 2,534 U5 and PLW (2,045 U5 and 489 PLWs) in Canal/Pigi, South Sudan. Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) project will contribute to the achievement of Nutrition Cluster stategic objectives for humanitarian response in 2020:
1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations
2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2020.
3: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations
4: To strengthen nutrition information system for evidence-based nutrition response by the end of 2020. The nutrition project will be integrated into existing projects of health WASH, FSL and Protection that are either supported by HFO or other partners to energize the efforts of humanitarian response in 2020. HFO will mobilize qualified nutrition workers that will run the project right from Juba and field locations of these selected areas by HFO. The recruitment of Nutrition Volunteers will include community members and local authority consultation for their participation in the selection criteria. The project manager will a qualified nutirionist with public health background. The reporting system will be through standardized registers, card and reporting sheets in OTPs and TSFP facilities and that report will be generated to Nutrition Information System that will be shared with Nutrition cluster and WHO. The nutrition response will be designed to ensure that Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) is looked after, at all stages of the response. HFO staff will continue to use the Nutrition Cluster and Global Nutrition Cluster operational framework on AAP that includes minimum six key activities and indicators focused on participation, establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms, transparency and communications, planning and implementation of nutrition responses as well as monitoring. The project response will promote a consultative approach by consulting a wider range of affected communities to ensure communities have clarity and have their inputs on the objectives of the nutrition response, the activities, the location, and their entitlements where the most affect groups are. The nutrition response will consist of a package of treatment and prevention services delivered through the health facility/nutrition site and through community platforms, as per national guidelines. This will be supported through an ongoing capacity strengthening efforts at facility and community levels, including community mobilization and engagement for participation. Furthermore, hard-to-reach areas will be covered through outreach services and Integrated packages of services with Health, Food Security, WASH, Education and Protection partners in areas of HFO operation exists. The nutrition services will be provided mainly through static sites, mostly in secure and accessible areas. Outreach services will be provided in sites with large catchment areas. And HFO will strengthen the management of SAM and MAM in Health facilities. nutrition staff will be trained on non-specialized protection package to identify and refer children with protection concerns for specialized care.

1 - Needs

According to an Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) update September 2019, released jointly by the Government of the Republic of South Sudan, FAO, UNICEF and WFP, indicates 54% of people in South Sudan are still severely food insecure. The report estimates that 10,000 people are currently in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and facing an extreme lack of food, while about 1.7 million are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and another 4.6 million people are experiencing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) levels of acute food insecurity compared to the same period in 2018. While the Greater Upper Nile region continues to be the most food insecure, Canal/Pigi of former Jonglei state will need urgent humanitarian support to save their lives. Of the people estimated to be in Catastrophe in May-July 2019, 10,000 are in Canal/Pigi of former Jonglei State, As of January 2019, Canal/Pigi county has been classified by the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) to be experiencing level 4 acute food insecurity. In total, 92,000 people were estimated to be in need of food assistance with 31,000 people experiencing IPC 3 crisis, 46,000 in IPC 4 emergency. Acute malnutrition has been a protracted problem in South Sudan and one that requires sustained investment. Although the food security situation is severe, the recent improvement can largely be attributed to the Revitalized Peace Agreement (R-ARCSS), signed in September 2018. The decrease in armed conflict has encouraged the voluntary return of farmers, increasing access to livelihoods and improving markets. A more stable political environment has also allowed for improved delivery of humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable populations. However, it is anticipated that within the current shaky R-ARCSS disagreement on implementation modalities will deteriorate again the situation of settlement and already improved security and will cause a return to fighting that will in turn cause severe food insecurity in 2020. Skyrocketing despite continued humanitarian efforts (food insecurity has doubled compared to yearly trends). These target areas are either flooded areas, lack of food productivity and health facility functionality, malaria upsurge and prone to cholera and measles outbreaks. in 2019 Disease outbreak has been a bigger challenge to health actors across the counties affected more than 2018, and it is anticipated that more disease outbreaks are to happen in 2020 due fragile health system structure in South Sudan. The needs are substantial and widespread, and it is apparent that there is an absence of even the most basic services in many parts of the country. The protracted conflict further exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition due to the reduced ability of children under five years of age, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), and chronically ill persons to meet their nutritional needs because of displacement and increased morbidity caused by disease outbreaks, lack of access to clean water, sanitation facilities and basic health services. The Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) response will prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological, physical and physiological needs. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. The response will expand on nutrition specific interventions, as well as reinforce integration of Health FSL, WASH, NFIs, protection and GBV services and linkages with other clusters to address the key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, good child care and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. It will prioritize areas where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and internal displacement coexist. This project will embrace the spirit of integration to synegize the humanitarian efforts.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

In South Sudan, Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) strives to engage beneficiaries in any decision making stage that affects their life and livelihood the AAP is one of our deliverable that all our staff take this seriously to ensure existing community leaders and members participate in implementation process and take ownership of the project rather than watching what HFO is doing afar. During project implementation, a mechanism called participatory review and reflection (PRR) will take place, providing beneficiaries the space to voice their opinions about the project and express their level of satisfaction in the services provided. The PRR will also serve as a mechanism for beneficiaries to participate in joint monitoring activities and express their feeling in owning the project. Beneficiary participation is required to improve the quality of the project implementation, these mechanisms worked very well during first allocation and we will make sure the same way AAP procedures is used at the second allocation of SSHF so that community feels involved in their services. The feedback mechanisms can be sent to most vulnerable through leading groups such as women, youth (girls and boys). Disabilities individuals will be involved in decision making and implementation modalities to get the information (Feedbacks) from project through local language that the community use. Everyone will be informed be the People Living with Disability (PLWDs), girls women and men through local committee that meet regularly with the HFO project staff.
