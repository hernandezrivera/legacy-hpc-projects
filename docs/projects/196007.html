Project Summary

"IOM’s Child Protection project in 2023 aims to continue to prevent and respond to child protection issues and empower vulnerable girls, boys, parents, and caregivers in camps by strengthening the protective environment through multi-sectoral approach and community-based interventions.

IOM programming incorporates age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in line with protective measures and mainstreaming to respond to specific needs, especially to enable girls and boys, children and adolescents, children with disabilities to be instrumental in improving the quality of their life and mitigating risks and dangers in their community and living environment. To achieve these objectives IOM’s prevention and response will focus on enhancing Community-based Child Protection (CBCP) with a participatory approach, allowing to bridge inter-generational gaps to promote social behavioral changes. IOM will continue to strengthen CBCP at the block level in the camp to enhance the capacity of communities to protect children with the participation and inclusion of children and youth. IOM CP will support these groups and clubs (including adolescents, girls and women led groups) by offering a forum to discuss relevant issues, and to enhance their capacity and awareness to empower the wider community to proactively identify and address child protection issues.
IOM will also provide specialized child protection services, such as case management, structured static, and mobile PSS block-level to reach out to boys and girls, including adolescents, with dignified and safe access to services and facilities, with special focus on children with disabilities, children caring for family members and child mothers. IOM PSS support will build on cultural education to nurture Rohingya roots and identity and promoting intergenerational discourse. Aiming to increase engagement with boys, IOM plans to engage more through inter-generational discussions and training on traditional livelihood skills. Parents and caregivers will be provided PSS and positive parenting support, including facilitated group discussions based on the evidence-based curriculum between adults and children / adolescents. IOM will continue to support child survivors of GBV and trafficking in the camps through direct implementation and our IPs on legal interventions.
IOM will closely work with the Government especially the Camp in Charge (CiC) and other relevant government officials in the camp area. IOM will also provide relevant training to law enforcement agencies, like the Armed Police Battalion (APBN) as part of their child protection mainstreaming.
Services will be provided through IOM direct implementation in camp 9 and 20 with a cross-cutting approach IOM closely cooperates with other sectors and IOM Units such as Health/MHPSS, Shelter, WASH, Site Management/ Communication with Communities and Social Cohesion to mainstream child protection, child safeguarding, age, gender and disability throughout programming; to coordinate disaster preparedness and emergency response; to strengthen PSEA awareness and to respond effectively to the Community
Feedback Mechanisms to ensure accountability for the affected population.
IOM is committed to child protection inter-agency efforts and contributes to the CP Sub-Sector, CP Case Management Technical Working Group, MHPSS Working Group and PSEA Network as well as field level coordination."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Entering the sixth year of the Rohingya’s humanitarian situation in Bangladesh, the needs of the refugee population remains dire. Children represent 52 per cent of the refugee population. The environment remains congested coupled with the socio-economic disadvantages, restriction of movement, lack of livelihood and educational opportunities, escalating violence, relocation activities of the government to different camps and Bhashan Char, and environmental hazards, all have contributed to a rapid increase in the vulnerability of children to violence, neglect, exploitation, and abuse. Due to the prolonged periods of uncertainty about the situation of the Rohingya community, the physically and mentally challenging living environment, new protection risks emerged, and further needs have been identified. Children’s wellbeing has been affected by the lack of a daily routine and psychosocial distress, as well as limited access to basic needs and services disrupting children’s development. IOM Protection monitoring indicates children engaging more in child labour, drug dealing, gambling, peer violence, and there is an increasing trend in violence against children, child marriage, child trafficking and GBV incidents. Due to economic struggles, families send their children to work in or outside the camps, where they often are exploited and at risk or being trafficked. The children continue to earn income or stay in the shelter doing household chores, caring for sick and older relatives and getting married earlier. According to the March 2022 Gender Adolescence Global Evidence, around 76% of adolescents have experienced violence at home. The increasing violence against children are perceived by community due to cultural acceptance, parental separation, and parental stress Child marriage is more common in Rohingya camps, and intimate partner violence is widespread. Rohingya girls are twice as likely to have experienced gender-based violence (GBV) (11% of married girls compared to 4.5% of unmarried girls) and 3.7% of married girls report experiencing rape or sexual abuse (compared to 1.3% of unmarried girls). Based on information provided by the Child Protection Sub-Sector, more than half of the Rohingya refugee adolescent boys (55.6%) from the age of 15-17 years were involved in paid work. At similar age group, girls’ involvement in paid-work were 6.7% for Rohingya communities, where more than half of the children aged 15-17 years and nearly one-third children aged 11-14 years were engaged in household work for at least an hour every day. The majority of Rohingya children were occupied in fetching water or gathering firewood as unpaid work. Save the Children’s August 2022 report stated that almost 80% of children felt depressed or stressed sometimes, most, or all the time. Parents and caregivers feel that they are carrying a heavy burden, more than nine out of ten said they feel depressed (92%), anxious (90%) and stressed (96%).For prevention and responding to child protection concerns, community-based child protection needs to be strengthened to promote inclusion, participation, meaningful access to services in a dignified way, especially for children with disabilities and boys, girls and persons with diverse sexual orientations, gender identities and Expressions, and Sex Characteristics’ (SOGIESC) for a safe transition of sustainable community protection mechanism. Empowerment of refugee community, religious leaders, volunteers as well as children/ adolescents through capacity building and awareness to enable them for a greater participation in decision making concerning their life situations; community-led activities and delivering key messages through community members targets a wider audience to reach high-risk adolescents and their families. Individual case management (including legal services), and MHPSS activities need to be increased to address current high volume of protection concerns. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"IOM is going to implement CP activities in camp 9 and camp 20. IOM aims to cover 50% of the CP needs in each camp. The program will focus on case management, community-based child protection, awareness raising, system strengthening and capacity development, and psychosocial support. IOM consistently coordinates and refers to other protection actors if needed. IOM co-chairs the Anti Trafficking Working Group (ATWG) and is an active member of all protection forums. This is to ensure continuity and that all activities are in line with the Protection Sector and do not overlap or become redundant. IOM has GP, GBV, and CT programming in the camps that complement the CP work IOM is doing. IOM can provide a variety of services and offer holistic services to support the children assisted. IOM has also developed good coordination with the CiCs in the camps, that allows the work to be conducted without any issues. "

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Child Protection is mainstreamed in all areas of programming to ensure meaningful access to services being provided children and caregivers. IOM implements a range of multi-sectoral and integrated activities for the refugees in the camps focusing on the needs of most vulnerable children. To mainstream the protection IOM will continue its activities through its specialized sub-units like General Protection, GBV, Counter-Trafficking and research and consultation. The program will closely collaborate and coordinate with Health, WASH, Shelter/NFI, and Site Management. The Program will continue to provide orientation, capacity building and training to different service providers to reduce the risk and avoid causing harm.

3.2 - Gender

IOM mainstreams gender in all areas of programming to ensure meaningful access to services being provided to all refugees. Programming is tailored based on feedback provided by the target population, prioritizing safety, security, dignity, and empowerment. IOM will identify risk factors and ensure the protective environment for individuals impacted by GBV, especially women and girls, individuals with diverse SOGIESC and persons with disability. The organization maintains a do no harm, survivor centered, gender sensitive approach to create a positive environment when identifying and managing a case or raising awareness and working with the community.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

"All areas of IOM programming include age considerations, particularly child safeguarding mechanisms. These mechanisms are embedded at the development, implementation and evaluation phase to continuously assess how we can ensure upholding the best interest of the child at all times. To protect children from any sorts of protection concern the programs will continue to provide training and awareness sessions to staff and volunteers that come in contact with the children. The awareness raising will include topics such as, PSEA, Code of Conduct, and child safeguarding. The program also takes into consideration the different ages groups and tailors activities that engage young children as well as adolescents. The program ensure to include parents and caregivers to ensure the protective environment starts at home and that they are aware of the CP risks and are part of identifying the solutions. "

3.4 - Diversity

IOM adopts diversity considerations in all areas of programming to ensure equal access of services. IOM CP programming is in line with the humanitarian principle of impartiality and provides assistance according to need without discrimination. The program targets the most vulnerable. IOM will continue to be an active participant in the Age and Disability Working Group and ensure Age, Gender, and Diversity mainstreaming.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM continuously monitors protection environment and activities in camps to ensure no harm is being done as a result of activities,& to ensure safe multi-sectoral programming.There are feedback mechanisms such as helplines & a helpdesk available to receive feedback.IOM staff & affiliated workforce are trained on PSEA on how to safely report.Regular induction sessions on PSEA,posters and flyers dissemination. IOM also has a global online complaint platform.IOM has a hotline & regularly disseminates the number,& the community feedback desks available in camp 9 & 20.To ensure AAP, IOM will focus on Information sharing,building local capacity,participation in decision-making process by affected population,inclusion of different community groups,constant communication & coordination among staff,volunteers & targeted population.Aim of the programming is to empower community to respond to the protection risks and decrease dependency on the humanitarian aid.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

As a member of PERU team staff are all made aware about response during times of disaster. The CP awareness raising activities include messaging to the community, particularly with children and caregivers on appropriate response to all relevant natural disasters like – monsoon, floods, landslides, and cyclones, as well as fires. The organization maintains an environmentally friendly approach to providing assistance and tries to reduce paper and plastic waste, as well as limit the usage in the camps. A lot of the CP work is done online, taking into consideration data protection. "
