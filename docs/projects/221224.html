Project Summary

Since 2016, the North West and South West regions of Cameroon have been going through fragile security and continued hostilities, targeted attacks and destruction of property and infrastructure, causing human rights violations and massive population movement. As of June 2023, about 638,421 are estimated to be internally displaced people . While many of the affected people in the regions take refuge within the other subdivisions that are seemingly safe, several of them tend to flee to the neighboring centers around Buea and Bamenda within the NW/SW regions, and others flee to the Littoral and West regions. But with these movements of populations, the services in the various parts of those towns and regions are getting constrained, and therefore there is need for support to strengthen social services in these two regions too. The NW/SW crisis has also impacted other regions. The Littoral and West regions, because of proximity to the South West region, and as a cosmopolitan city, is hosting over 194,065 IDPs in prolonged displacement situations. In the Far North region, the security situation remains critical, with attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) on the increase, 90% of which took place in the Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo sanaga divisions. Clashes between non-state armed groups and state security forces have also increased. Rural areas remain particularly affected as national security entities are concentrated along the main roads and exercise less control over other locations. In addition to the increasingly volatile security situation, the Far North region is subject to the consequences of climate change, leading to droughts and floods and rampant food insecurity. In 2024, heavy rainfall led to a flood crisis affecting 352,000 people in the Far North region. The floods destroyed farming and livestock activities, leaving many families in extremely vulnerable situations. The Far North region is prone to recurrent epidemics such as cholera. These crises continue to force millions of people to move around the Far North region. By October 2024, violence had uprooted a total of 662,000 displaced people, returnees and refugees. The mapping of Safe Spaces carried out by GBVAoR in 2024 in the Far North , shows that there are few of these facilities in areas of need, and that the majority do not meet minimum standards. Gender-Based Violence (GBV) is one the most widespread human rights violations in the world. It affects women and girls disproportionally due to the underlying social injustice and power imbalance leading to gender inequality. Between January and October 2024, the main incidents recorded with GBVIMS shows that IPV represent 80 and sexual violence 13% of cases. It was to note that in North West and South West regions sexual violence represent 31% and 22% involves child abuse. Other GBV incidents reported include denial of resources/opportunities is reported by 36%, physical abuse by 25% and psychological violence by 23%. The main activities will focus on sensitization and awareness raising for communities, emphasizing the participation of men and boys, strengthen referral and legal assistance mechanisms for internally displaced persons and host communities with legal protection needs (detention/documentation, GBV survivors etc.), provide basic life skills support to women and girls, Provide cash transfer assistance for GBV referal and risk mitigation, build the capacity of operational organizations on the holistic response to GBV. UNFPA and partners will continue providing individualized case management and psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and other vulnerable people. Finally, UNFPA, in collaboration with the Ministry of Women Empowerment and the Family (MINPROFF), will also continue to reinforce close collaboration between actors and service providers involved in responding to the needs of women and girls.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Gender-Base Violence (GBV) constitutes a violation of the fundamental human rights and is one of the main symptoms of the unequal power relationship between men and women. GBV seriously affects all aspects of a woman’s life, especially their health, their participation in economic life, and their social integrity. Women survivors of violence are more likely to have physical, mental and reproductive health problems UNFPA’s efforts are aimed to improve the quality of basic services for survivors. Although UNFPA's interventions have had a positive impact in terms of behavior change and the support from community members to survivors and other vulnerable women and girls, there are still gaps to cover. The main activities will focus on sensitization and awareness raising for communities, emphasizing the participation of men and boys, strengthen referral and legal assistance mechanisms for internally displaced persons and host communities with legal protection needs (detention/documentation, GBV survivors etc.), provide basic life skills support to women and girls, Provide cash transfer assistance for GBV response and prevention, build the capacity of operational organizations on the holistic response to GBV. UNFPA and partners will continue providing individualized case management and psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and other vulnerable people. There will include an aspect of mainstreaming GBV in educational settings. UNFPA in collaboration with MINESEC with create gender clubs and introduce comprehensive sexual education. Finally, UNFPA, in collaboration with the Ministry of Women Empowerment and the Family (MINPROFF), will also continue to reinforce close collaboration between actors and service providers involved in responding to the needs of women and girls. Ensure that safe and ethical systems for the transfer of data including confidential personal records of GBV survivors are put in place by organizations and authorities. Considering the increasing needs associated with climate change impact particularly in the Far North , UNFPA will mainstream disaster reduction and anticipatory actions accross different activities.Expected Results: Risk of GBV and SRH is reduced and quality of response improved and sustained.
