Project Summary

Existing WASH infrastructure in Leer, Mayendit and Pariang counties is unable to provide adequate WASH services (water, safe disposal of faeces and hygiene promotion) due to the population increase, risking increased outbreaks of water borne diseases including acute watery diarrhea, cholera, and malnutrition. Inadequate WASH is a major cause of diseases in Leer, Mayendit and Pariang counties and improving sanitation through hygiene promotion is known to have a significant beneficial impact on health in households. In Leer, Mayendit and Pariang counties there are IDPs and host communities without good sanitation because it was not there from the very beginning, or it was destroyed by the many conflicts and flooding. There is need for maintenance of hygienic conditions, through improved availability of latrines, safe water source, waste disposal, bathing shelters, to raise awareness on water and sanitation related health/hygiene issues, especially to raise awareness of water quality in order to successfully distribute Homebase care kits, ( buckets, soap and water purifiers -water flocks sachets/Aquatabs) MHM kits to the community, and to reduce the risk of cholera outbreak, and other sanitation related diseases. The project aims to reparate 80 non-functioning boreholes, Construction of 10 protected shallow wells and reparation of 4 mini-water yard in several Payams of Leer, Mayendit and Pariang Counties so that the IDPs, the returnees and un-displaced population can access water at an average of 750 people (women, men, boys and girls) per borehole which is still below the UNHCR and SPHERE standards quality requirement but is far better than the current average usage of 1,250 per borehole and use of alternate unsafe sources according REACH and February 2022 IRNA Leer & Mayendit assessment report and FSNMS+R27 where only 34% of the community are accessing safe water. Some of the displaced population has been forced to live within hosting population and IDP camps with very limited WASH infrastructure resulting to increased open defecation, overuse of functional boreholes and mini-water yards, institutional and shared family latrines etc. The project envisages to conduct hygiene promotion to influence behavior change from harmful practices that encourage spread of diseases, by involving women, men, boys and girls in the community through community hygiene promotion approaches, water and sanitation management committees, through involving all the stakeholders in the populations that includes people living with disabilities and the elderly. A total of 50,000 people (women, men, boys and girls) will benefit through provision of access to safe adequate water by reparation of boreholes and mini water yards, construction of protected shallow wells and improve sanitation through construction of gender sensitive latrines and hygiene promotion awareness creation. UNIDOR has been able to demonstrate that hygiene and sanitation awareness coupled with the installation of improved water facilities can trigger an improvement in attitudes towards hygiene and sanitation, good health, among the communities in Pariang, Mayendit, Koch, Leer Counties in partnership with SSHF, UNICEF, IOM. UNIDOR programing does conduct GBV safety audit before the construction and rehabilitation of water points, institutional latrines, creating awareness about hygiene and sanitation in advance of the implementation of distribution of WASH NFIs and construction of WASH infrastructure. This approach often guarantees a more enthusiastic participation of women, girls and boys, which leads to a higher level of acceptance and ownership by a larger group of community members and supports greater sustainability of the program by reinforcing the link between water, sanitation and hygiene. This project aims at; to enable the community access safe adequate drinking water, promote construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities while prioritizing the safety of women, girls.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to be affected by conflict and flood disasters in some areas and the threat of conflict in others. In Leer, Mayendit and Pariang counties Unity State, this situation coupled with high levels of poverty, high returns of refugees, destroyed infrastructure, food insecurity and flooding, has caused mass displacement of people and many people (women, men, boys, girls) are residing in IDP camps and within the receiving host communities. These vulnerable IDPs, returnees, host populations continue to suffer more burden due to inadequate and disproportional WASH infrastructure considering the SPHERE, WHO and UNHCR guidelines. This environmental, humanitarian, political crisis has negatively affected the already poor to access basic water, sanitation and hygiene services. According to HNO 2021, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, another consecutive year of major flooding. An estimated 6,159,886 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. According to (FSNMS+R27), despite the fact that major waterways were replenished by the long rainy season, only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34% in rural areas. In certain states, such as Unity, where 33 per cent of households travelling for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities and the elderly. Difficulty in securing enough water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49% of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. Surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods, when latrine pits seep into waterways. Limited access to water is also compounded by 14% households reporting having at least one container to safely store water (FSNMS+R27 and February 2022 IRNA Leer reports). Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points, with 18% of households, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water in the two weeks prior to data collection. Of those, 12% reported of women and girls reportedly avoiding water points due to GBV concerns. Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. Overall, 80% households did not access a latrine. This aggregated figure however hides some extreme geographical variations. In certain states, such as Unity, the percentage of households accessing a latrine is consistently below 10%, with open defecation being the norm (FSNMS+R27 and February 2022 IRNA Leer and Mayendit reports). Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in Leer, Mayendit and Pariang. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition. In Leer the (GAM) prevalence for children 0-59 months was 19.2% while (SAM) was at 6.1% and in Mayendit (GAM) prevalence was 14.6% while (SAM) was at 4.0 (WHO classification’s). The presence of water-borne diseases was also reported, with 16% of households reporting children under 5 had been affected by diarrhea in the two weeks leading up to data collection. Conflict has caused families to retreat to remote areas in Leer County with very limited WASH infrastructure and increased pressure on existing WASH services in host communities and disrupted livelihoods, further perpetuating economic challenges by reducing the income households can allocate on WASH goods and service

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

There are many ways in place for ensuring feedback from the most affected vulnerable community members: WASH management committees (WMCs),Community Health/Hygiene Promoters (CHPs), Handpump Mechanics who are routinely engaged with affected community members and their leaders, will be oriented and given specific context information on how to identify and reach out to the most vulnerable household heads/members, including individuals living with disabilities and the elderly as they will be the direct target of the project. The CHPs, who will carry out routine hygiene promotion awareness campaigns and through households-to-households visits will play greater role in receiving feedback or complaint from the most vulnerable affected beneficiary community members. UNIDOR has designed monitoring and evaluation tools used by M&E and project staff for conducting interviews, and discussions with various affected beneficiaries (men, women, boys, girls, persons living with disabilities and the elderly) who have been directly or indirectly involved in the project. The findings and lessons learned from these interviews, discussions and information from other sources will be documented in form of reports and success stories of interest, to be used by UNIDOR, funding partners and other stakeholders including local authorities and other partners

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNIDOR will conduct interviews, FGDs and separate discussions with gender and age disaggregated groups including random one-on-one interviews with selected beneficiary household head of families and persons living with disabilities and the elderly, women and girls, are centered on four major areas, i.e., relevance and direct benefit, the overall result in the community e.g. is there reduction in sanitation related diseases, is there safe and adequate water, are people practicing hand washing etc., what of the quality of the items given (Home Base Care kits, MHM kits) and sustainability of the project even when UNIDOR exits.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNIDOR staff implementing the project are trained on PSEA and child safeguarding policy. There are appointed focal persons situated in all field project sites to monitor the implementation and compliance of the policies. The focal points are introduced to local authorities and who in turn are made known to the community. All the employees of UNIDOR and any other person conducting any service for UNIDOR are taken through the Code of Conduct and they are to append their signatures in agreement. The inception meetings and Focus group discussions, stakeholders are well informed that the services provided are free and they beneficiaries fully deserve them, and any staff trying to exploit the beneficiaries should be reported to RRC or UNIDOR management. Selection criteria for MHM kits and Home Base Care kits is agreed upon through meetings and FGDs. This ensures that deserving beneficiaries receive the services and items.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This project will directly involve and fully engage the project direct beneficiaries in Leer, Mayendit and Pariang Counties throughout the intervention cycle. Inception meetings and organized Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) will be utilized for effective and efficient social mobilization of girls, boys, men and women including the individuals living with disabilities and the elderly to participate fully in separate consultations so that their views and concerns are reflected and addressed. The consultations will be conducted based on their gender and age groups to allow for maximum participation. A female staff from UNIDOR and or a female staff from GBV partner on the ground will consult women and girls separately by their respective age group for confident building to express their views on the best location for locating of water or gender sensitive sanitation facilities before any construction or reparation takes place to mitigate the protection concerns associated with WASH-GBV activities. Distribution of MHM kits to girls and women will as well be discussed in the same FGDs to ensure that the most vulnerable benefit due to limited resources, time and modalities that are secure and inclusive will be considered through consultations with them together with the female teachers at their schools or community members. The project staff will consult persons living with disabilities and the eledely together and separately during joint supervision/monitoring site visits and FGDs, based on gender dis-aggregated to express their expectations as well as their capacities to ensure their feedback is captured, documented/shared and addressed. Both men and women from beneficiary communities will be trained together in separate trainings as water management committees, hand pump mechanics, community hygiene promoters, and engaged in the various WASH activities like management of water sources, rehabilitation/repair works of communal water points or latrines, hygiene promotion etc. There are many ways in place for ensuring feedback from the most affected vulnerable community members: Water management committees (WMCs), Community Health/Hygiene Promoters (CHPs), Handpump Mechanics who are routinely engaged with affected community members and their leaders, will be oriented and given specific context information on how to identify and reach out to the most vulnerable household heads/members, including persons living with disabilities as they will be the direct target of the project. The CHPs, who will carry out routine hygiene promotion awareness creation through households-to-households visits will play greater role in receiving feedback or complaint from the most vulnerable affected beneficiary community members. UNIDOR has designed monitoring and evaluation tools used by M&E and project staff for conducting interviews, and discussions with various affected beneficiaries who have been directly or indirectly involved in the project. The findings and lessons learned from these interviews, discussions and information from other sources will be documented in form of reports and human impact stories, to be used by UNIDOR, funding partners and other stakeholders including local authorities and other partners. These interviews, FGDs and separate discussions with gender and age disaggregated groups including random one-on-one interviews with selected beneficiary household leaders/members and persons living with disabilities, are centered on four major areas, for example: relevance, impact / outcome, value for money and sustainability aspects of the project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

UNIDOR will develop together with the community members selection criteria for direct beneficiaries for WASH NFIs, this will help prevent any external interference when distributing items since some local leaders have been known to select their relatives as priority beneficiaries. This will be conducted through Focus Group Discussions with different groups in the community – namely Women, adolescent girls, people living with disabilities, elderly, boys -. The quantities will be established and will vary as per the group of persons selected and registered. During distribution of WASH NFIs, the beneficiaries will not be subjected to long distances looking for services, the services will be taken to them even for far and hard to reach Payams. WASH facilities will ensure that siting and construction and rehabilitation follow strict protection measures after consulting the beneficiaries through Focus Group Discussions with the specific groups. Latrines and water points will be made safe through clearing of bushes around them and more importantly ensure reduction of time and distance of women and girls accessing water points. Timing for distribution of WASH NFIs – Home base care kits, MHM kits – will be done during morning hours so us to ensure beneficiaries reach home with their items during the day in a short time. UNIDOR will ensure Confidentiality by appointing focal points to address suspected and any GBV and SEA cases and keeping of confidential information. Regular trainings on PSEA and GBV to all staff will be conducted. Confidential and private meetings and meeting places will be established within the project locations to address GBV and SEA cases.
