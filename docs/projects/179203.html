Project Summary

In this project, NGO X is submitting an integrated approach to Shelter and Winter MPCA. This integrated project will address the multiple vulnerabilities that households and communities are facing after ten years of conflict. At the household level, the program design creates links between its multi-donor funded emergency response program and its food security, winterization and shelter programming, and complements food assistance programming with basic needs assistance via Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), Shelter activities target beneficiaries on this level with repairs and rehabilitation of shelter units and collective shelters to ensure that beneficiaries are living in dignified conditions. On the settlement level, the program will focus on flood prone areas providing preventive measures to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter, in parallel to emergency response road and access maintenance in camps during the rainy season to support access leading to IDP sites in the event of serious degradation on road access caused by flooding.
NGO X will enable highly vulnerable IDP, returnee and host communities to meet their winter needs through a coordinated cash-based response. NGO X will select those areas most vulnerable to extreme winter conditions through analysis of the Humanitarian Needs Overview, Multi-Sector Needs Assessments and in close consultation with the Shelter/NFI Cluster. A fuel market assessment and household interviews will then be conducted in order to ascertain the availability of fuel, market access and functionality.

NGO X will aim to complete winter response by the end of December 2020. Cash will be the default response mechanism as long as minimum preconditions for the use of cash exist. The cash transfer value will be based on the fuel cost which will cover HHs needs for the 40 coldest days of the year with an average of 6 liters of fuel per-HH per-day.

NGO X plans to utilize e-cash to reach a selected number of particularly vulnerable beneficiaries under this activity, priority will be given to child headed households, single/widowed women, the elderly and people with disabilities.
• Winter MPCA of US $ 135 per HH targeting a total of 27,382 households living in particularly cold and mountainous areas.

4 - Needs

An overall 50% of IDPs in NWS are living in substandard shelters which include unfinished buildings, tents and emergency make-shift shelter (HNAP, Oct 2020). 46% of IDP households report they do not have adequate space for their occupants, with multiple households sharing units due to lack of availability of suitable housing (REACH, August 2020).In a quarter of all assessed communities, shelter was indicated as IDPs’ first priority need. 62% report occupying shelters with major damage, with cost being the biggest barrier to repairs. A market assessment carried out by NGO X in September 2020 found that 48% of vendors expect that fuel prices will increase, which will impact on households’ ability to access fuel for heating. An increase in fuel prices exacerbates health and fire risks, particularly with the arrival of winter, as inaccessibility of safe heating sources leads to reliance on negative coping mechanisms such as burning unsafe materials, which in previous years has resulted in outbreaks of fires in IDP sites and other residential areas, as well as triggering the emission of toxic fumes. According to the Maram Foundation (Needs Assessment Report for Severe Floods and Heavy Rain in NWS camps – January 2021) The lack of sufficient storm water drainage infrastructure, and poor shelter conditions in affected locations resulted in varying levels of damage including tearing and flooding or total destruction of 28% of the tents, sewer overflow, formation of stagnant ponds, and damage of earth roads causing muddy ground. This led to the interruption and obstruction of access to and within the camps. Some IDPs were forced to abandon their damaged shelters and belongings to seek refuge in alternative locations.

5 - Implementation of activities

Winter MPCA NGO X will select areas most vulnerable to extreme winter conditions through analysis of the HNO, Multi-Sector Needs Assessments and in consultation with the Shelter/NFI Cluster. A fuel market assessment will then be conducted in order to ascertain the availability of fuel, market access and functionality. Through NGO X’s Vulnerability and Inclusion Criteria, those beneficiaries who are most in need of winterization assistance will be selected to receive assistance. MPCA is the default response modality under this action ($ 135 in average per HH for 27,382 HHs). NGO X’s will carry out both distribution monitoring and post distribution monitoring to assess how beneficiaries spent the cash and whether they had enough cash to cover their fuel and other winter NFI needs. Shelter and Settlements NGO X will target shelters and collective centres classified as moderately damaged (damage level/grade 2) and will rehabilitate 575 shelters and 15 collective centres. NGO X will also assess the household’s or collective centers’ access to water and sanitation and minor repairs. IDP camps that are flood-prone, located on flood streams, or receive heavy rainfall and do not have the requisite infrastructure to cope are targeted with construction and rehabilitation of main roads, secondary roads leading to communal facilities and rehabilitation and construction of rainwater drainage systems. Communal facilities will be provided with exterior solar lights as well.
