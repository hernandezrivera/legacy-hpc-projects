Project Summary

Chronic WASH needs continue to emerge in parts of Upper Nile and Jonglei States, likely exacerbated by disease burden, raging flood waters, food insecurity, and logistical challenges. The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributes to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, and intensifies existing vulnerabilities of affected people, which include high poverty rates, limited access to essential services including health and education, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement (HNO 2023). Escalating inter-communal violence in Jonglei has also increased humanitarian needs and hampered access (UNICEF Situation Report 2023). The influx of returnees and IDPs from Ethiopia into Akobo exasperates the WASH situation since communities are already struggling with meagre resources. Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. WASH vulnerability was also found to be extreme, together with the high prevalence of malnutrition in Maiwut and Manyo counties. Despite the replenishment of major waterways for four consecutive long rainy seasons, 42 per cent of the population do not have access to an improved water source, and 45 per cent need to walk for more than 30 minutes to access its primary water source. This is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities, as well as for women and girls who are increasingly exposed to GBV. The situation is particularly concerning in Manyo where more than 90 per cent of the population reportedly use rivers or puddles as their primary water source (FSNMS Round 28). Untreated surface water and the widespread practice of open defecation create a substantial public health risk, especially during floods when latrine pits seep into the waterways. Limited access to water is also compounded by only 16 per cent of households reporting having at least one jerrycan and/ or bucket to store water safely. Displacement, insecurity and economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting the resources available to maintain wells and boreholes. In the areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water for the people, with the situation worsening when more people are displaced. Insecurity has also affected access to water.
Flooding has hit hard these targeted regions of greater Upper Nile for many years, leaving the community vulnerable to using unsafe water sources, open defecation, exposure to waterborne diseases, and displaced populations. The displaced population in Akobo expressed that there are few available water sources; 95 % of the population survives on surface/seasonal ponds and swamps for example, Wechlul, Kaikuiny and Kuerkew Boma have no boreholes, a Duba pump shared by the whole boma and even hard to pump but most of the boreholes are not functioning (Akobo West Flood Vulnerability IRNA, SCI 2023).
The response aims at reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH-integrated programming with Nutrition and Health, providing access to essential WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5, also providing WASH-specific activities for GBV mitigation and communities exposed to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant infrastructure. 1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan continues to increase despite the signing of the revitalised peace agreement five years ago. In states such as Jonglei and Upper Nile, 33 per cent of households travel for longer than 30 minutes to access any water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities. Difficulty securing enough water is only part of the issue; shallow coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49 per cent of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their primary water source. The situation is particularly concerning in Upper Nile, where as many as 71 per cent of households reportedly use rivers or puddles as their primary water source. In contrast, virtually all households rely on surface water in certain counties, such as Ayod and Manyo. Certain rural areas, such as in Upper Nile State, are particularly at risk as they are unreachable in the wet season due to roads being washed away and existing networks cut off. This will likely force displaced people into urban or already populated areas, causing a knock-on effect on existing WASH infrastructure that is already operating over capacity. (HNO, 2023). Interagency Rapid Flood Assessment conducted the assessment from 28th – 30th Sept 2021 in Akobo West after heavy rainfall floods displaced many people, approximately 9,120 of the population, some households were sleeping inside wet houses (Akobo West Flood Vulnerability Needs Assessment,2021). During the Maiwut assessment, about 17,394 individuals (10,089 F: 7,305M) were reported to be displaced at different Payams of Maiwut. The population contains 11181 Returnees and 6212 IDPs. About 29% of the population are children between 0-18 years, 52% are adults aged 19-59, and 19% are elderly. Amongst the assessed communities, about 2,345 individuals were identified to be vulnerable, meeting at least one of the Nine protection cluster criteria such as 35% are elderly at risk, 5% are separated children, 12% are unaccompanied children, 20% are Women at risk, 5% are people living with Disabilities, 15% are single parents at risk, and 8% are people with critical medical conditions. (IRNA Report, Maiwut Upper Nile, 2021). Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points in Greater Upper Nile, with 18 per cent of households, both rurally and in urban settings, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water in the two weeks before data collection. Of those, 12 per cent said women and girls avoid water points due to GBV concerns. The issue was particularly acute in certain counties, such as Akobo, Mayom and Baliet, where more than 40 per cent of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water. Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. Overall, 80 per cent of households did not access a restroom. Moreover, floods regularly damage existing sanitation infrastructure and given their annual occurrence, communities hesitate to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities (HNO,2023). People prefer to walk long distances in locations like Jonglei and Upper Nile for open defection. Access to safe water and sanitation facilities is a preventive measure for malnutrition by curbing diarrheal diseases. Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence for children 0-59 months was 11.3 per cent, while Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 1.7 per cent. Water-borne diseases in children under 5, such as diarrhoea, indicate poor WASH conditions (SMART SURVEY, SC, 2023). In 2020, 16% of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns. The COVID-19 pandemic also increased GBV incidences due to the measures used to curb the pandemic, with 2 million people estimated to be affected by GBV in 2021. Unfortunately, these regions within the Upper Nile have faced severe flooding for three consecutive years, leaving communities at risk. 2. Project activities

The response will be based on these cluster activities: 1. 18 pump mechanics given refresher training on hand pump maintenance and repair, including the water user Committees. • Drilling of 10 boreholes with resilient materials, Repair and rehabilitate 40 boreholes, each with trained water management committee members-WMCs • Organize and conduct participatory hygiene promotion and conduct training for 40 community Hygiene promoters • Supply WASH Items like (PUR, Aqua tabs, Filter Cloths, Soaps, jerry cans, buckets and buckets with taps. • Chlorinate boreholes in the areas where the displaced have settled. Conduct water quality tests to ensure safety at water points and household levels. Organise refresher training for Pump mechanics and water user Committees. • conduct water quality test before/after repair of broken platforms• Excavation of waste management pits in institutions and IDP settlement sites, provision of pit excavation tools (hoes) for the IDPs. Construction of emergency temporary communal latrines in the displaced sites. Installation of HWF in both institutions and IDP-congested settlement sites. • Community mobilisation and sensitisation on good hygiene practices and health education through a participatory approach undertaken in health facilities, churches and markets on good behaviour targeting 16,500people • Conduct hygiene promotion demonstrating hand washing and mass sanitation campaigns to enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living through secure dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services, • construction of 30 sanitation facilities in communities with consideration for gender-separated latrines and provision of latrine digging tools to the affected community and training of the community on how to construct the HH latrine using available local materials as an exit strategy. • Undertake cross-cutting issues across all activities with a particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, and protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to the affected population and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities. • Distribution of WASH NFIs, capacity building in terms of preparedness through training of pump mechanics and provision of borehole spare parts, training of Water Management Committees for resilience, and water quality testing at water points and community level. Medicair shall apply sustainable solutions, such as installing water sources propelled by solar power, where feasible. Medicair has its drilling equipment and teams with geologists to conduct geophysics surveys before drilling. Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among affected populations to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases through promotion activities, particularly hand washing for displaced and vulnerable communities and institutions. Medicair will mainly focus on health workers, teachers, pupils, and women group leaders while conducting household visits, focus group discussions and training for Community Hygiene Promoters.
