Project Summary

CRS will focus on providing a full package of WASH interventions and will adopt a
participatory approach, mobilizing communities in the design and planning of WASH
activities. CRS programming takes into account the specic needs of each segment of the
community while giving special consideration to the most vulnerable, including children
and people with disabilities. Women will also greatly benet as they will be involved in the
water user committees (WUC) which we intend to have 50% women representation will
have a stronger voice with regards to management of water resources and they shall be
able to use this platform to raise their prole within the community. Improvement of
sanitation facilities at homestead level will benet the whole household and most
critically women and vulnerable persons. At the moment, in most rural areas, women and
vulnerable persons have to go very far from the homestead to defecate. This poses great
challenges to them in terms to their personal security. With improved sanitation, they shall
be able to access latrines even at night within the homestead. Bringing WASH facilities
closer to homesteads ensures that the old and sick are able to access these facilities
without straining themselves by walking far distances.
CRS will ensure adverse environmental impacts are mitigated. Pumping tests shall be
conducted on newly constructed water sources to ensure safe abstraction rates are
determined. Water User Committees shall be trained on water conservation to promote
prudent use of water resources. CRS staff will work closely with the community to ensure
that local materials used in construction are sources sustainable and where possible use
of byproducts of agriculture such as harvested crop stocks and other by products are
reused in construction of latrine superstructures.

1 - Needs

Red Sea State (RSS) falls between latitude 16º and 22º North and longitudes 35º and 37ºEast. It is divided into two distinct physical areas: the coastal strip, the hilly area and theat western plains. Its total area of about 218,887km2 is divided ten localities (mahallias),The topography of the State is diverse, with the desert comprising almost one third of thetotal area. RSS is classified as arid to hyper arid and experiences extremely low, unevenlydistributed and variable rainfall, with 60% of the State receiving rainfall less than 100mmper annum, mostly during the period of November and February. The State is alsocharacterized by extremely high-water deceit on both monthly and annual scales, withrelatively high humidity and temperatures usually in the range of 22-41Cº. Climate changeand related natural disasters (floods, droughts, dry spells) have strong bearing on thesocioeconomic situation of communities and households and can contribute to thenumber of people requiring humanitarian support. Environmental degradation andunsustainable use of natural resources are both contributory factors and consequences ofthe humanitarian situation in Sudan. Moreover, Sudan’s limited water supply from non Nilotic sources also gives rise to humanitarian needs. The presence of Red Sea and Red Sea Hills is responsible for the challenging climate characteristics of the State, which negatively affects water sources. The spread of diseases is high in red sea state. However, access to safe drinking water is limited, with much of the ground water requiring desalination due to the influence of the Red Sea. The RSS is the highest state practicingopen defecation in Sudan. In December 2019 CRS participated in inter agency mission to assess needs of people affected by heavy rains in populations in Tokar. The initial findings in the population of over 40,000 individuals are not accessing to clean water because all the 6 water distribution points of the locality are out of services. as well 10 households latrines are collabsed and the remain are un used because its dangerous and able to collapse. CRS currently Implementing food security and livelihood project andfrom the observation and the led visit report of CRS staff indicated there is huge gap in these localities and these communities have no access to adequate WASH facilities as well have poor hygiene practices. CRS multisectoral (Nutrition, FSL and WASH) project in Haya, Tokar and Dordaib localities ended last September 2019, although the project included some WASH activities but the gap was huge and these communities have very limited access to adequate WASH facilities as well have poor hygiene practices. Althoughin the initial result of S3M conducted in 2018 compared to result of S3M conducted in 2013 doesn't shows any improvement in the status of situation even it went worse is some localities in terms of Sanitation Haya, Halayb and Tokar are become worst than before. in 2020 the flood affected the state negatively and according to inter agency mission report Tokar localities is the worst, all water sources stopped operation for certain period of time as well the toilets are collapsed and the sewerage system of hospital is damaged.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The communities targeted by the Improving and sustaining access to WASH services forVulnerable People in Red Sea State are primarily host communities, with livelihoodactivities mainly based on mixed agro-pastoralism, Drilling new hand pumps, Repairingand rehabilitation of the existing hand pumps, Rehabilitation of one water yards,Rehabilitation and upgrading of existing hand dug wells, Upgrading of the existingpotential hand pump to mini-water yard, Establish and train Water User Committees onwater resource operation, maintenance, management, water quality monitoring andtreatment, Establishment of community based sanitation and hygiene promoterscommittees and training on community led total sanitation (CLTS), Conduct periodiccommunity mobilization and awareness sessions on best hygiene practices, Establishsolid waste management system among the community and support them withnecessary tools.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CRS mainstreams protection into all programs so that activities (i) prioritize safety, dignityand access, in addition to avoiding causing harm, (ii) ensure equality and meaningfulaccess, (iii) include mechanisms for beneciary accountability, and (iv) emphasizeparticipation and empowerment to meet SHF minimum requirements. CRS prioritizes thesafety and dignity of beneciaries and avoids causing harm by preventing andminimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of its interventions thatcan increase vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. The project will ensurethat targeted beneciaries have safe access to assistance and services based on needand without discrimination. Efforts will be made to accommodate people with specicneeds, ensuring access to services. Through the CBCM, target populations will be able tomeasure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints, therebysupporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting people to claimtheir rights, including – not exclusively – the right to shelter, food, water, sanitation, health,and education. The project will ensure that services provided are accessible to people withdisabilities. CRS will adhere to Sphere Principles to implement activities under this projectaccording the following: 1. Prevent: Enhance the safety, dignity and rights of affectedpeople, and avoid exposing people to further harm; 2. Respond: Reduce the impact ofphysical and psychological harm that arises from violence, coercion, deliberatedeprivation and other threats; and 3. Remedy: Assist people to claim their rights andaccess appropriate remedies. These three principles are interdependent and can be carriedout at the same time. The principles promote the right to dignity in three ways: •mainstreaming protection risks, activities and related information across all humanitarianprograms; • integrating specic protection objectives into assistance projects; and •promoting specic and/or specialized protection activities that address specic protectionrisks and violations. In addition, CRS takes care to design program activities that considerndings from a risk analysis. The agency also actively looks for ways to minimize threatsand work with specic at-risk groups, and the organizations they trust. Such approacheshelp CRS understand how best to address needs and any positive or negativeconsequences. In consultation with relevant line Ministries, The Humanitarian AidCommission (HAC) and the communities, CRS will design activities that keep girls, boysand women safe by, for example, protecting against or preventing child recruitment,abduction or separation from family. Special consideration will be provided to the needsof at-risk members of the community, such as separated and unaccompanied children,child-headed households, adolescent girls and boys, children with disabilities andchronicillnesses, and young parents

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will be designed in a manner that humanitarian assistance will be providedin line and in proportion to the needs identied and documented in targeted areas, inadherence with SHF guidelines. CRS will involve the beneciaries and respond to theirfeedback when planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating its program. CRS’accountability includes four pillars i.e. risk management, capacity assessment andmonitoring the performance of implementing partners, monitoring and reporting, andproject and partner auditing. CRS will provide space for communities to shape theirown recovery and for CRS and partners to better deliver on commitments tostakeholders, including the people CRS serves and the resource partners who makeassistance possible. CRS will ensure timely information sharing among stakeholdersregarding targeted communities. CRS will focus on various approaches to collectinformation for community-based indicators. As an example, community score cardsground CRS’ monitoring and evaluation in appropriate and inclusive feedback fromaffected communities and their leadership through each phase of the project cycle.Feedback channels not only respect the right of affected communities to have a say,but often also improve the eciency and effectiveness of aid delivery. CRS willestablish effective feedback mechanisms and a Community-Based ComplaintsMechanism (CBCM) so that CRS and partners can incorporate feedback and addressproblems rapidly, including for sensitive issues like protection against sexualexploitation and abuse (SEA) by humanitarian or development workers. CRS andpartner staff will be trained and oriented on SEA. In line with CRS’ Monitoring,Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policies and procedures, CRS willconsult communities in establishing an appropriate feedback and responsemechanism to give each community the opportunity to identify, report and seekredress for program quality and protection concerns. The system will address bothsensitive and non-sensitive issues while upholding condentially and non-retaliationprinciples. Examples of the format of each mechanism the project is consideringinclude monthly feedback meetings between CRS staff and community members, aphone number where communities can report sensitive issues, and communityleadership committees. Specically, CRS will link project beneciaries with existingfeedback mechanisms in the area for formats like: (1) a complaint desk, wherebeneciaries can lodge a complaint and receive a response in a reasonable timeframe;and (2) Active outreach to project beneciaries by the MEAL Ocer, who will takendings from random beneciary satisfaction visits to regular program managementmeetings, or (3) suggestion boxes to offer condential feedback as appropriate.
