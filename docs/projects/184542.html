Project Summary

According to latest reports and as indicated in the HNO estimated 6.4 million people representing 40 per cent of the Somali population need emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 3.5 million will need safe drinking water, 5.1 million sanitation services, and 6.4 million hygiene supplies. 16 per cent of these are females, 64% children and 4% elderly. Additionally, approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities need WASH assistance through 2022 of which 6,058,603 people are categorized as severe condition. They lack access to safe water and proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to AWD and cholera. Further, the Cluster estimates, 379,757 people are in extreme condition as they access water directly from rivers and surface bonds, practice open defecation and lack access to soap and other essential hygiene materials and are susceptible to communicable diseases
Water is the greatest need in the community and has remained a serious challenge in both Afmadow and Badhadhe district. Water is mainly supplied by the private individuals, who currently lack the capacity to meet the ever-increasing demand due to booming population with the daily influx of people. IDPs are mostly affected by the water problem and the assessed IDPs cannot also afford the water since they don’t have an income to sustain this. The sanitation situation in Afmadow district is 1 latrine per 300 persons. People living with disabilities remain the most affected and are unable to access any water consumption points.
Recent assessment conducted by SFH on the existing water supplies including existing boreholes and shallow wells in Lower juba and particularly Afmadow and Dobley tows shows, 3 boreholes requires urgent and significant rehabilitation due to the overuse and lack of sustainable power supply. In this proposed intervention SFH will select 1boreholes and establish into complete water system. This will entail installation of solar system “solar submersible pump, solar panels and a battery”. Construction of 50M3 elevated concrete water tank, pipeline distribution network and construction of 3 tap stands with 6 facet and benefit 250 persons per tab. Distribution pipelines will be connected to the water tank which will supply water to the tap stands through gravity flow. Each of the pipe line distribution networks will cover approximately 500m length and will benefit approximately 1,500 beneficiaries thus reaching total of 4500 beneficiaries (Boys, girls, men and women-including person living with disability) for 3 pipe line distribution systems. The construction process will include Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) including raised plinths to withstand the structure against flash flood or any other natural disasters. The distance of fetching water will be reduced and water access especially for women will be prioritized through participation on the sites for the pipeline systems, Women will be consulted with in the decision process to identify the locations of the water points to ensure safety and reduce chances of protection risks such as GBV.
Water quality testing will be conducted on the existing shallow wells and the borehole. Automatic chlorine dozing pumps will be provided to disinfect water and decrease the chances of water borne diseases. Water quality tests will also ensure that water is free from fecal coliforms and is fit for human consumption. Community Hygiene Promoters will train the Water management committees on how to maintain the automatic chlorine dozing pumps. This will enrich the community knowledge on water hygiene and will significantly reduce the cases of AWD outbreak in areas of intervention.
SFH will construct 200 Communal Latrines with lockable doors and hand washing stations to eliminate open defecation. Hygiene promotion activities will be conducted through the community hygiene promoters who will be trained and also double to conduct hygiene kits distribution.

1. - Projects activities

The proposed WASH intervention aimed to reach 15,000 beneficiaries (3,120 men, 3,320 women, 3800 Boys, 4,760 girls) to contribute to the reduction of mortality and morbidity precipitated by poor access to WASH services in Badhadhe and Afmadow districts.The proposed intervention target construction of 1 new solar powered/hybrid strategic Shallow wells with 50M3 elevated water tanks in Afmadow districts to reach over 5000 persons (Men, women, boy and girls) including persons living with disability directly with safe and sustainable water. This will include a piping system from the main water sources to the identified sites where the IDP’s and Host families are living. Shared water kiosks will be constructed and will be managed by trained Water committee who will report to the management of the hybrid well. This will enable vulnerable communities to access safe water continuously during and after the emergency period. 2. The response will also 1 strategic boreholes and 15 shallow wells for rehabilitation, protection and chlorination work in Lower Juba. Rehabilitation will focus on: generator repairs, replacement of pumps, pump house repairs, provision of borehole consumables and chlorination for boreholes and for shallow wells, rehabilitation will focus on provision of apron/well head cover, hand pump installations and chlorination. SFH will establish raised platforms and ramps for water points to ensure safe manual lifting to all, especially children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities 3. Design and construct 200 gender sensitive and culturally appropriate emergency VIP latrines with hand washing facilities to eliminate open defection, disposal of child faeces and promote better sanitation practices by the target population. SFH will ensure separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females. The facilities will be clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. The latrines and the bathing facilities will have inside lockers as a protection concern. To ensure access of latrines by persons with disabilities we will install handlebars in the latrines and fit ramps and make the access routes smooths for wheel chairs. 4. Train 30 hygiene promoters (Men and women) on basic hygiene practices, proper handling of domestic water, personal health and environmental care among other. 5. Hygiene kit composed of 2500 bar soap (800g), 2500 Jerry cans of 20L, 2500 stripes of Aqua tabs (10 tablets per stripe), domestic bucket and Face mask will be distributed. To address the gender specific needs-sanitary materials will be part of the hygiene kits as per the WASH cluster standards. 6. Training of 24 Gender dis-aggregated (Men and Women) and disability sensitive water Management Committee members on management of water resources and particularly operation and maintenance. Among the 24, 14 key representatives including PDW will be equipped with tools for effective management. The project staff will have equal gender representation to ensure enough women are available within the project implementation as a contact point for women in the community. In the procurement and distribution of hygiene kits specific hygiene needs of women, girls and older men are taken into consideration. To ensure those with impaired vision and hearing receive WASH messages, both oral and written modes of communication will be used. Child friendly messages will be used to reach both boys and girls with messages. SFH has established AAP committees in most of our operation areas to ensure inclusive, transparent and accountable assistance is delivered. The AAP committees composed of representatives of all community members including Men, women, youth, the elderly, PWD and the local governance. The AAP committees will be involved in the sensitization and mobilization prior to execution of any humanitarian assistance and their participation will ensure inclusive and equitable service delivery.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. Mitigation measures: - a) SFH will consult with different representatives of the community (women, men, boys, girls), persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific Hygiene kits requirements and preferences and prioritize needs based on their needs. b) Provide disability sensitive ramps near WASH facilities to increase access to people affected by disabilities. c) SFH will ensure access paths are smooth and accessible for wheelchair access to allow the disable persons affect hygiene kit distribution point and prevent discrimination.4.2 - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. Mitigation measure:-a) To enhance privacy put locks on the doors to latrines and bathing houses to ensure they are lockable from inside. b) Ensure equal proportion of representation of women and men in water management committees, registration and distribution teams to ensure specific needs of the women are catered. c) SFH staff and contractors are required to sign PSEA policy and Child protection code of conducts.4.4 - Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors. Mitigation measure:-a). SFH will Locate the shallow wells and latrines in visible and central locations that is not far from the community and IDP settlements and by extension liaise with CCCM/Shelter cluster and the particular Camp management agency to consider lighting systems installed along the routes and near the water points. b). SFH will Identify central and accessible locations for distribution of hygiene kits to avoid long travel by the beneficiaries to access the distribution points. c). Hygiene distributions will take place during the day to able beneficiaries’ sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.
