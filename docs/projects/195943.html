Project Summary

This project aims at increasing awareness on explosive ordnance risk education to enhance safety and security to the population at risk (IPC4) in Canal/Pigi county of Jonglei State. CAO for the last five to 8 years has had a strong presence in all these project locations, CAO access pigi /Canal through UNHASS and water transport. Due to high rates of returns, there is a need to provide safe access in areas through conducting risk education on the explosive ordinance and ERW to conflict‐affect populations, mainly the project will target vulnerable groups of IDPs, returnees, and host communities, children, and people living with disability in Canal/Pigi of Jonglei State. The above target counties have been facing explosive hazards that impact negatively on the ability of civilians to safely access essential services which include; education/TLS, CFS, health facilities /centers, and natural resources such as water and farmlands as most of these people are depending on agriculture and small-scale farming which is reducing their coping mechanisms. Contributing to the restoration of a safe and secure environment, the living condition of the rural population especially in the most contaminated areas affect improved outcomes across almost all sectors.
The project will target men, women, boys, and girls affected by conflict. 15,000 (3,500 women, 3,500 men, 4,000 girls and 4,000 boys). The project will be implemented in a period of 12 months and it will cost $300,000. CAO being an accredited partner and having two teams in place, will implement this project directly in the target locations in collaboration with the target population, the local administration, line ministry, and partners on the ground to achieve maximum results. The project activities are designed to increase awareness on explosive ordnance risk education to enhance safety and security to the population at risk (EORE)” It is expected that by the end of the project period, the lives of the vulnerable population at risk of ERW (explosive remnants of war) have increased access to EORE awareness and safe behavior. Women, men, girls, and boys will be better informed on the dangers and safe behaviors regarding ERW in emergencies that will make a meaningful contribution to the population's needs for safe behaviors

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to face threats from unexploded mines and munitions, UNMASS and other Deming organizations have done huge clearance of land in the country that was littered with exploded ordnance during the civil wars, In the recent report by UNMASS, states that 39,920 mines,76.010 cluster munition and 972,354 other items of unexploded ordnance were destroyed and made thousands of archers safe for economic and social activities to take place by the community leaving in South Sudan. The report also mentioned that a total of an estimated contaminated area which is about 18.36km2 approximately 2.572 football pitched with 342 remaining tasks comprised of 183 minefields,130cluster munition strikes, and 29 conformation areas. Contamination of the areas presents Signiant risks to human security and poverty by crippling socio-economic activities such as farming, fishing, trading, and animals’ production. A sizeable tract of land is rendered unproductive which affects the provision of mainstreaming services including health, education, Nutrition, and FSL. The UNMASS and NMAA put efforts to rid of the country out of land mines and UXOs which was heading to success before the 2013 conflict that caused an estimated number of 400,000 people to have been killed, around 1.8 million internally displaced and 2.5 million fled to the neighboring countries, especially Uganda.In addition to the above report, in Feb 2022, IRNA was conducted in Canal/Pigi by DRC ,CAO including other partners on ground, through the focus group discussions with the key informant interview which was carried out, it was reported that remnants of war are being identified as a risk, it was pointed out that Canal/Pigi is a contaminated area following the previous existence of the Arab's barracks within the town, bullets, bombs, mines, and grenade are reportedly present on the ground causing risks to children adults and the animals, CAO With funding from SSHF implemented EORE activities with the from 1ast sept 2021 and end 1st Sept 2022. Through EORE activities which include, awareness-raising, identification, and marking of the findings, CAO reported several hazardous areas to UNMASS, following this, the G4S team been deployed to clear the mines and the UXOs that were identified, marked and reported to the EORE subcluster and UNHMASS for responses

2 - Project activities

Activity. 1. Community Dialogues (Community Meetings). These will be general community meetings where ERW threat, risk-taking behavior, and target groups will be informed, Activity. 2. Community Awareness -raising on EORE, this will be done daily for five days in a week targeting communities in the 2 Counties covered by the project. Activity.3. Drama Sensitization will be based on pre-rehearsed skits with key EORE messages to target populations depending on the kind of risk and behavior being targeted for change. Activity. 4. Public Information Dissemination, will be part of the integrated EORE package to reinforce the strategies mentioned. Activity. 5. Formalizing the set-up of a data collection process and system at the county headquarter level to integrate EORE-related data (i.e. victims and survivors data) into the classic IMSMA forms and share with NMAA and UNMAS database. Activity. 6. Data Collection. CAO will collect Hazardous area and accident information which will be handed to UNMAS, NMAA & UNOPS and used internally for planning and prioritization. Activity. 7. Teachers' and Peer to Peer training on MRE as part of integrating EORE into School Curriculum Activity. 8 Safer Community Plan’ component, which aims to assist in the development of community-based plans and activities in communities in the 2 counties so as to promote a community and people-centered approach to safety. Activity 9, conducting community liaison, Activity 10, identification and marking of the finding, and sharing the reports with UNHMASS, NMAA, and UNOPS.
