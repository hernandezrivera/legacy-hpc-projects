Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address malnutrition and related morbidity and mortality among 200,000 vulnerable people who include 150,000 under five children ( 80,000 girls and 70,000 boys and provide nutrition support for pregnant and lactating women in Burlo Burt, Hiraan region.
The project will ensure that the most vulnerable groups including children, adolescents and communities in the selected localities are able resilient and adopt appropriate practices to reduce their vulnerability.
SYPD will ensure gender and age considerations by targeting the most vulnerable women and children in IDPs settlements and host communities. Each activity will contain a list of age and gender disaggregated data to enable monitoring of progress towards the gender and age equality.
SYPD will strengthen community and beneficiaries involvement and accountability to affected people by forming project committees which will be in charge of receiving any complaints or feedback and dispute resolution. The project committees will be drawn from the project beneficiaries and will be trained on project management and coordination with other stakeholders.
At the inception of the project, SYPD will also hold meetings with the main stakeholders such as community leaders and government officials to create awareness of the project activities, enhance partnerships and strengthen coordination.
SYPD will coordinate with other partners in the same locations the Nutrition cluster to ensure better coordination, complementarity and avoid duplication.
SYPD will establish effective monitoring and evaluation system where every activity will be tracked on a monthly basis and reporting on achievements shared with the Nutrition cluster on time. SYPD will actively participate in the Nutrition cluster coordination meetings and update all activities regularly to the cluster

Needs

Malnutrition remains high in Somalia. Urgent treatment and nutrition support is needed for approximately 294 200 acutely malnourished children, including 55 200 who severely malnourished between now and December 2018. Integrated support interventions should be sustained to maintain recent improvements as well as prevent further deterioration in the nutrition situation. Over 1.5 million people facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support between now and December 2018. Livelihood support is also required for 3.1 million people who are Stressed (IPC Phase 2) - FSNAU September 2018. Hiraan remain one of the hostspots with 114,00 people in Stress phase – IPC2, 42,000 under crisis, 11,000 under emergency phase and 2,000 in catastrophe phase. The worst affected areas include North Gedo Riverine , Beletweyne District (Reverine & Agro-pastoral), South Gedo Agro-pastoral, South Gedo Riverine, Mataban, Jalalaqsi and Bulobute districts of Hiran Region, Juba Riverine, IDP Populations: Bosaaso, Garowe, Galkacyo; Qardho, Mogadishu; Baidoa, Kismayo and Dolow -FSNAU – September 2018.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Building of resilience of food insecure people will be a key component for the project implementation to tackle the key risks and address the underlying causes of food insecurity and vulnerability for IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in terms adequate and sustainable food security and livelihoods. SYPD will work with vulnerable people including forcibly displaced people, both refugees and IDPs and host communities to support resilience building through capacity building in agropastoral practices, income generation and asset creation. Even in the context of protracted displacements, the environment in Hiraan, Lower Shabelle, Bay region, middle Shabelle still presents opportunities for durable solutions and resilience building. SYPD will work with the community leaders, relevant ministry at the local and national level, and local government authorities to find durable solutions including secure land tenure, capacity building and income generation activities, community assets, irrigation systems and hand over mechanisms for community based projects.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries safely access basic services without causing harm by promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the nutrition service provision. In particular, SYPD will promote the participation of beneficiaries by establishing and strengthening project committees and building their capacity through training and resources support. The beneficiary committees will be involved in identification of vulnerable groups and monitoring the project implementation and feedback/complaint mechanisms. SYPD will safeguard against protection risks by ensuring that all services such as nutrition supplies distributions are carried out during the day, in locations close to IDPs residences and clearly guarded with crowd control mechanisms to avoid theft, intimidation and gender-based violence. Care will also be taken to ensure children are not exposed to hard work or exploitation by having to carry heavy items. Each children headed household will be assigned a guardian during the collection of supplies to avoid exclusion and abuse. For example during the provision of nutrition supplies SYPD will adopt approaches that ensure safety and security of women and IDPs and provide dignity for beneficiaries including family unity, respect for cultural norms and the protection for girls and women. Each nutrition centre will be located in accessible locations to avoid exposing beneficiaries ti the risk of violence or theft. Distribution centers will be manned by crowd control personnel to ensure programme and beneficiaries safety.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries safely access basic nutrition services without causing harm by promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of all gender (men, women, boys and girls) in the service provision. In particular, SYPD will promote the participation of beneficiaries by establishing and strengthening project committees and building their capacity through training on gender mainstreaming and resources support. The beneficiary committees will be involved in identification of vulnerable groups and monitoring the project implementation and feedback/complaint mechanisms. SYPD will safeguard against protection risks against women and girls by ensuring that all services such as items distributions are carried out during the day, in nutrition centres locations close to IDPs residences and clearly guarded with crowd control mechanisms to avoid theft, intimidation and gender-based violence. Where men and women are all involved in the collection of items, SYPD will keep separate lines for women and men. Care will also be taken to ensure children are not exposed to hard work or exploitation by having to carry heavy items. Each children headed household will be assigned a guardian during the collection of supplies to avoid exclusion and abuse.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to armed conflicts and violence by nature of the assistance provide by undertaking community consultations during the project mobilization and implementation to understand local dynamics and take due care in project design. In particular, SYPD will ensure the participation of all on affected communities, inclusion of all stakeholders in camp coordination and camp management activities including host communities and IDPs.
