Project Summary

UNHCR aims to support 4000 displaced, host, and vulnerable non-displaced households (24,000 individuals) with Shelter and NFI support in Northwest and Southwest Regions. UNHCR will implement the project through cash transfers at 80% in order to enable affected households to purchase materials that they prefer and to make improvements to their shelters and households. Cash support will aim to support beneficiaries in the purchase of materials and labour to make improvements or repair damaged shelters, while also enabling IDPs to spend it on rental assistance. UNHCR will ensure community-based approach through its partner (s) to reinforce established community representative groups in identifying the needy persons and monitoring the support provided. The affected population will be aware through applicable and preferred complaint and feedback mechanisms that support provided is free of charge and prefer to provide humanitarian support including cash through a female spouse. UNHCR as Shelter cluster lead works with other cluster members and brings together local authorities with UN agencies and NGOs to coordinate, monitor and improve the provision of emergency shelters. The result will be a harmonized effort to support affected people to receive adequate housing and live in a dignified manner.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Destruction of property including the intentional burning of homes has contributed to the shelter needs in addition to the coping mechanism to seek out shelter in safer locations from targeted villages. According to 2024 HNO, the OCHA Multisector Needs Assessment conducted by the International Organization for Migration, a total number of 464,904 of people need of adequate shelter and NFIs, including 207,010 IDPs, 199,659 Returnees and 58,235 Host and Non-Displaced affected by the crisis. The project will target 24,000 individuals (4000 HHs) in Southwest (Muanenguba, Manyu, Fako, Meme, Kupe and Ndian Divisions) and Northwest Mechum, Boyo, Ngoketunjia, Mezam, Donga Matung, Momo and Bui Divisions). For the 2024 HNO, the OCHA Multisector Needs Assessment conducted by the International Organization for Migration switched to a household methodology for the crisis in the Northwest and Southwest which provides reliable results of shelter and NFIs needs in the households. Furthermore, the findings indicated the overall scoring in common for the Northwest and Southwest in type and location of a shelter (official, informal/bush, and collective shelter); Number of M2 per person; Part of the Shelter damaged: roofs, walls, doors, windows; Tenure Type; Where the household prepares food; Source of Lighting; Source of energy for cooking; Whether having NFIs or not. This results in a total (persons in need) PIN for the Northwest and Southwest of 464,146. One of the sub-indicators to measure shelter adequacy was whether the residential unit has been intentionally damaged so this factor, together with the Global Human Settlements built up layer (satellite imagery), highlight the prevalence of the problem and the estimated number of residential buildings that were affected in each subdivision impacted by the crisis. It is estimated that since the beginning of the crisis a total of 9,223 properties were intentionally burnt depriving people of the right to housing. Compared to 2023 there has been a decrease of 35% mainly due to the switch in methodology. The methodology for the MSNA last year was based on key informants while this year on a household assessment which required IOM to visit a certain number of households using a standard household questionnaire. A household questionnaire would be in most cases more statistically accurate than a key informant and makes UNHCR and other partners to reduce numbers of beneficiaries and coordinate well with actors to maximize the use scarce resources available. From last years' experience on CBI, UNHCR will enhance monitoring and evaluation of assistance provided through proper data collection and recording, reinforcing established community groups with fair representation of women and girls, reinforcing preferred means of communicating with the community and reaching out the majority persons in need and providing feedback. UNHCR will implement the project through its partners and ensure localization aspect is applied to be able to reach Divisions and Sub-divisions that are hard to reach due to insecurity, infrastructure, and other reasons. UNHCR as shelter cluster lead supported by the co-lead agency/NGO will ensure that available resources are well coordinated, jointly monitored through joint missions and accountable to the affected population and abide to global shelter standards. As shelter cluster lead will ensure effective coordination among shelter cluster members and collaborate with the local authority, local NGOs and associations, humanitarian and development actors.
