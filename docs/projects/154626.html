Project Summary

The project aims to provide child protection specialized services to 12,000 children (from 4 to 17 years old) exacerbated by prolonged displacement and/or lack of livelihood opportunities living in camps and no camps settings in Erbil, Ninewa and Anbar governorates. This includes also children at risk due to perceived affiliation, possible exploitation and abuse, impossibility to access civil documents and/or basic public services. On this regard a particular attention will be given to youths and adolescents through specific PSS and life skills activities. The child protection services offered mainly consist in case management and structured PSS activities. At the same time through this project TdH Italy wants to build the capacity of local actors (government and local NGOs) as well as strengthen child protection community based mechanisms. Parents, youths and community key leaders will be involved in the process through information dissemination, training, mentoring and community based activities. Finally the proposed activities are strongly linked and integrated with education activities. 6,000 children living in camps and no cams setting in the same areas are supported to access quality education services through non formal education activities (remedial classes, catch up classes and tutoring). Quality and inclusive education will be also reinforced through ad hoc training for formal school teachers on child protection, PSS, inclusive approach and positive discipline.

Needs

Children affected by prolonged displacement and returnees children are risks can be combined for what concern the main child protection concerns and needs. The first among them is the lack of livelihood opportunities with, as direct consequence, and increment of child labour and children out of school. Among all the vulnerable cases followed up through case management activity in 2018 in the target locations, child labour was the main problem identified. The importance of the issue is pointed out also by the Multy Cluster Need Assessment (MCNA) conducted in 2018. Indeed based on the data collected the percentage of HH reporting to have at least one child working in the last 30 days is: 8,5% among IDPs in and out camps and 10% among returnees in Anbar governorate; 12,5% among IDPs in and out of camps in Erbil governorate (Erbil district); 12% among IDPs in and out of camps and 5,5% of returnees in Ninewa governorate (average data between Mosul and Hamdanya districts).The poverty situation of IDPs and returnees children as well as the condition of unsafety and prolonged displacement is affecting also the psychological wellbeing of adults and children. This has consequence also on the parents capacity and family environment in general: GBV, child neglect and domestic violence are indeed growing as registered during community consultations, field assessment and case management activities. Other important protection concerns in the target locations are: (a) lack of basic services (medical care, schools, etc.); (b) lack of proper nutrition and access of NFI (clothes, climatization, etc.); (c) unsafety living conditions because of mines, unexploded hazardous, damaged building, etc.; (d) social tensions and discrimination towards children accused to be previously affiliated to armed groups or to be part of a family with perceived affiliation. All the mentioned factors are common in the proposed area of intervention, with of course some difference among them. In addition to the conflict/displacement related issue, child protection is quite often at risk due to harmful cultural practise (such as girls denied from accessing education and social activities, child marriage, etc.). Always the MCNA assessment funded out that the number of HH reporting at least one child at protection risk is 33% of IDPs in and of camps and 31% of returnees in Anbar governorate; 40% of IDPs in and out of camps and 32% of returnees in Ninewa governorate (considering the average data for Mosul and Hamdanya districts); 30,5% of IDPs in and out of in Erbil governorate (Erbil district). Psychological distress is still assessed as important need both for IDPs and returnees children. From MCNA the percentage of children reported by the HH as affected by signs of psychological stress is: 8% for IDPs children (camps and non camps) and 17% of returnees in Anbar governorate; 11% for IDPs children (camps and non camps) and 9% of returnees in Ninewa governorate (considering the average data for Mosul and Hamdanya districts); 6.5% of IDPs in and out of camp in Erbil governorate (Erbil district). For what concerns the education, formal schools are re-opening quickly but they suffer for the lack of resources and teachers. Children lost two or more years of school and now they are struggling to catch up with the program. This of course increase the possibility of school drop out.Teachers as wells have problems in managing big classrooms with children coming from different experience and with different levels of preparation. In addition to that a significant number of children is still psychological stressed by the conflict, the displacements and by the traumatic experiences passed. This has of course impact on their learning abilities (focusing, concentration, relationship with the class mates, etc.), therefore they are in need of specific support not only limited to education to improve their wellness.Always based on the multi sectorial need assessment, at country level 30% of the interviewed families said they have at least one school age child out of school. 20% of the children don’t have access neither to formal or informal education. 40% of the out school children drop out because of ISIS occupation. Currently the main reasons for not attending the school are: economical (35%); children with disabilities or other severe health problems (15%); cultural reasons (9%). In addition to that other significant data are: lack of certified children (reported by 19% of the household) and lack of formal schools (5% of the families don’t have a functional primary school within 5km and 10% don’t have a secondary school within 5km).

Activities

CHILD PROTECTION COMPONENT:1) case management services (including referral pathways and referrals). CPU composed by social workers and psychologists are deployed in camps and urban areas and they will provide services using a mix of static and mobile approach. The cases are identified through direct need assessment and referral from other actors. Full case management service is provided to vulnerable children from 4 to 17 years old, according with the sub cluster SOPs and using the CPIMS forms;2) structured and unstructured PSS activities: children from 4 to 17 years old in the target locations are involved in unstructured PSS activities address to reinforce resilience, life skills (in particular learning related skills) and positive cope mechanisms. Through the non structured activities children in need of more specific support will be identified and involved in structured PSS activities. These are based on TdH Italy curriculum and include at least 4 sessions for each cycle with 10 beneficiaries for each session. An individual plan is designed for each beneficiary indicating how many PSS sessions and how many one-to-one consultation she/he needs to attend. The activities are conducting by psychologists and PSS workers;3) community based CP activities: these include 3 main actions (a) community mobilization and awareness for all the community member to spread a wider knowledge of basic CP, children care and children rights; (b) parental training addressed in particular (but not limited) to vulnerable children's parents. Training are based on TdH Italy internal curriculum and consist in at least 4 sessions with 25-30 participants each. (c) training, mentoring and support for CP committees and community based mechanisms. Key community members, community leaders and volunteers are involved. The main goal is to build their capacity in case identification, service mapping, referral, CP monitoring awareness and prevention of main abuses.4) capacity building: on one side it's addressed to child protection local actors and governmental actors (MoLSA social workers) to strengthen their capacity in particular on case management. On the other side it's addressed to non protection local actors and governmental actors belonging to different bodies to build a basic knowledge of child protection and children rights;5) preparatory activities for future emergency: among the staff members deployed in the field a group will be selected and prepared to quick react in case of emergency being deployed in different locations based on the need. They will be trained on rapid CP assessment, UASC and PFA. TdH Italy will also give availability of local staff already trained and available to move all around the country to support the CP sub cluster delivering training or leading emergency interagency teams.EDUCATION COMPONENT1) catch up classes for out of school children to support their future enrolment in formal schools;2) remedial classes and tutoring for children at risks of drop out because of lack of preparation, vulnerability, emotional stress etc.;3) alternative learning activities for illiterate boys and girls and for adolescents who might can't be any more enrolled in formal school;4) formal school teachers training on CP, PSS, class management, positive discipline and inclusive education. COMMON/PREPARATORY ACTIVITIES:A) establishment of multipurpose activities centres in retaken urban areas where CP and education services will be offered together with other services provided by different actors which might share the same space;B) field staff training and mentoring on child protection and education related topics;C) field staff induction on code of conduct, child protection policies and PSEA.

Protection Risk Analysis

1) children or families with perceived affiliation are discriminated by project staff and/or other actors;2) children in need of additional specialized services can't be reffered because of lack of actors;3) children or families with perceived affiliation are exposed to risk of unjustified/preventive detention or other protection issues;

Mitigation Measures

1) staff will received specific training and awareness on this specific issues. They will be constantly monitored and mentored. Beneficiaries will have access to complain mechanisms to report any form of discrimination or abuse. About other actors training and awareness will be raised with all the protection and not protection actors including also advocacy at different level in case of misconduct;2) advocacy will be raised at different level highlitighing eventual gaps;3) project staff will be informed about the risks and additional protection risks related to particular cases and they will be informed about how to manage sensitive cases and information.

How is your project accountable to affected population

1) adolescents and adults will be consulted about their needs and expectations on project activities (training topics, awareness topics, learning activities etc.);2) community consultations with the beneficiaries and their parents will be regularly run in each location to collect needs and feedback on the services provided. Based on their feedbacks activities will be designed, revised, re-shaped;3) beneficiaries will be informed directly and through printed banners on the complain collection mechanisms. This includes complain boxes and focal points contacts. Some TdH Italy staff members in each locations will be appointed to collect and go through the complains and to forward them at the appropriate management level depending from the topic and gravity;4) community based activities directly involved the community members and aftern the initial training and induction they will be directly led by the community.
