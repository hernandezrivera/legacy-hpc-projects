Project Summary

ShelterBox and ACTED will enter a partnership to address the escalating vulnerabilities and shelter needs faced by conflict-affected women, girls, boys, and men in Borno State living in protracted displacement who presently lack adequate and dignified shelter solutions. The six-month intervention will endeavour to target 1,548 Internally Displaced Person (IDP) households with critical shelter repair, and fortification during the current rainy season, which typically starts in June and runs until October. The ESK modality will be used to ensure access for vulnerable IDP families to shelters capable of providing safe and dignified living spaces that protect against the harsh elemental conditions.
The Action will supply 1,548 ESKs to conflict affected IDP families currently residing in vulnerable makeshift shelters in Custom House, Jere LGA, as well as those living in prolonged displacement in Mafa GGASS IDP Camp. The breakdown will be as follows: 700 ESK in Jere LGA and 848 ESK in GGASS camp in Mafa. The ESKs will allow for sorely needed shelter reinforcement for increased protection against weather extremities, most notably the current seasonal rains. In doing so, this will ensure shelter viability throughout the upcoming reason season of 2020. Applying a five-member household average (International Organization for Migration (IOM)), it is anticipated that the Action will therefore directly benefit 7,740 individuals.

1 - Needs

Due to the large-scale and often protracted displacement across Borno State, shelter capacities to respond to the enduring needs on the ground have struggled to meet the critical needs faced by IDPs. While the humanitarian system endeavours to provide shelter upon displacement, many IDPs are not always able to access shelter on arrival at camps, resulting in many families proceeding to construct makeshift shelters. This practice can be explicitly observed at Custom House, where many IDP families reside in makeshift shelters. Further, shelters which have been constructed at earlier phases of the emergency intervention have a typical lifespan of six months to one year, yet replacements and repairs are not easily accessed, resulting in large volumes of shelters which are no longer fit for purpose being used by IDPs. This is particularly evident in Mafa GGASS IDP Camp. With the current rainy season, vulnerabilities imposed on IDP families living in inadequate shelter structures becomes critical, as the storms anticipated are highly likely to incur further damage, and even destroy, vulnerable makeshift and damaged shelters. In 2018, storms caused flooding across camps in the North-East, resulting in grave strain on the S/NFI Sector implementing partners, as the need to replace shelters rose dramatically. As reported by IOM, Situation Report August 2018, nine IDP sites in Jere urgently required shelter repair and upgrade assistance due to storm damage. The OCHA Humanitarian Situation Update Report, August 2018, reported damage to over 3,000 emergency shelters due to extreme weather experienced in the rainy season, requiring urgent replacement. Additionally, families that are currently without access to shelter, including those whose homes and property have been destroyed by fire, become suddenly exposed to the elements once more, without adequate access to dignified and safe shelter. These individuals now remain in critical need of shelter assistance as the rainy season approaches, bringing with it risks of exposure to the elements, and significantly increased health and hygiene risks – notably cholera outbreaks - with susceptibility to such risks increasing for those forced to sleep unprotected outside, in dangerous sanitary environs. In 2018, due to the impact of the rainy season, cholera outbreaks were declared across the BAY States, with 3,537 cumulative cases reported in Borno State alone by 31 September, over 1,000 of which were reported in Jere - the prevalence of cholera as a result of the 2018 rainy season signifies the grave need to ensure protection against improper shelter conditions for displaced individuals. On 11 March 2020, IOM reached out following a recent needs assessment they had conducted in Custom House to inquire on ACTED’s capacity to respond to 711 households, comprised of 3,911 individuals, identified in need of ESKs due to damaged makeshift shelters.

2 - Activities

In line with the S/NFI Sector objectives of ensuring access to dignified shelter solutions for displaced individuals, ACTED proposes to provide emergency shelter solutions to 1,548 conflict affected IDP households living in protracted displacement in Borno State. Therefore, the Action will deliver ESKs to 1,548 displaced families currently living in makeshift shelters in Custom House, Jere LGA, and in Mafa GGASS IDP Camp. The ESKs will allow for sorely needed shelter reinforcement for increased protection against weather extremities, most notably the current seasonal rains. The assistance is urgently required to ensure safe and dignified shelter throughout the current rainy season, which typically starts in June and runs until October. The Action therefore aims to significantly decrease critical needs faced by women, girls, boys, and men, reduce protection risks, and significantly increase access to safe and dignified living spaces for target IDP families. The proposed Action seeks to rectify this imbalance in humanitarian assistance, which is disproportionately targeted towards new arrivals, by placing a strong emphasis on the emergency shelter needs of IDP households experiencing protracted displacement. Applying a five-member household average (IOM), it is anticipated that the Action will therefore directly benefit 7,740 individuals. The project also aims to increase protection from diseases as lack of access to adequate shelter increases the health risks IDPs face, with North-East Nigeria prone to outbreaks of cholera, malaria, and Lassa fever. Most notably, in the current climate of a COVID-19 pandemic, IDPs vulnerability to disease is heightened in instances of poor shelter solutions that are typically structurally inadequate for effective mitigation of COVID-19 transmission. For instance, poor shelter conditions can increase respiratory disease, a known aggravator of the impact of the virus. As such, the proposed shelter assistance can play a vital role in mitigating the spread and impact of COVID-19 should an outbreak occur.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

ACTED aims to establish and sustain efforts to mainstream protection in all sectors of intervention, at all levels of the organisation, throughout the entire project cycle. ACTED will work to ensure that safety and dignity are prioritised by placing strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities to take account of affected populations. All activities will be designed with the safety and dignity of beneficiaries in mind. For example, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Moreover, a strong level of support is expected to lead to a degree of self-policing on the part of the community against any incidents of abuse, fraud, theft, or similar protection concerns. To uphold transparency and avoid increasing social tensions in targeted communities, criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be communicated in advance, publicly, and will be liaised with local authorities to minimize security concerns.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED endeavours to utilise participatory methods by giving account to affected populations through the recognition of the dignity, capacity, and abilities of the beneficiary community. As such, the project will use a deliberate programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation, most noticeably using locally recruited staff as enumerators, carpenters, and unskilled labourers. More broadly, the target population will be involved throughout the project cycle, including program design, such as through the needs assessment conducted by ACTED S/NFI Team in March 2020 in which community leaders and members were consulted on their shelter needs to support the development of an evidence-based intervention plan. During the course of the project, notably through the CRM, further community feedback will be sought in relation to the project modalities, quality of service, and impact, allowing to adjust and adapt actions according to the needs, priorities and aspirations of beneficiaries where feasible. Similarly, the PDM exercise will be conducted at household level with a sample of beneficiaries who received assistance, providing the opportunity to contribute to ACTED and ShelterBox’s learning, and how it supports displaced communities together with ACTED in Nigeria.
