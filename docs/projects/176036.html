Project Summary

The promotion of Access to Basic, Quality, Protective and Inclusive Education to 15,000 vulnerable learners, Out of School Children and Youths Aged (3-18) will be focused in Akobo, Nyirol, Uror, and Pibor Counties of Jonglei State; Ulang and Baliet Counties of Upper Nile State in South Sudan; the project will equally support 400 teachers (200M & 200F and 400 PTA (200M & 200F). The programme will seek to improve the access to quality education among the crisis affected children and youths as an effort to promote sustainable basic quality education with incentivized enrollment and attendance to learning, reduce risks of cognitive underdevelopment and malnutrition, and provide access to safe and protective learning environments. The project will targets a total of 15,800 total beneficiaries desegregated into 15,000 children and youths (7,650 B & 7,350 G), 400 teachers (200 M, & 200 W), 400 PTAs/SMCs (200 M, & 200 W); with 15% comprising of persons with disability.
The response aims at enrolling and retaining children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers, establish temporary learning spaces and classrooms, rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces and classrooms, provision of age 3-5 children with ECD kits, establishing gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion, rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion, procuring and supplying Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to students, procuring and supplying sanitary and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to teachers, provision of emergency-affected children with quality education recreational materials, training of teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and Child-centred methodologies to improve teaching -learning process for emergency-affected children, training and updating teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms, provision of life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents, training of teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health and nutrition, training of SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), printing and distribution of appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures, organizing back to learning campaigns while integrating with already existing projects in intra-sectors (especially Health, Nutrition, FSL, WASH, and Protection).
The project equally hopes to enhance psychosocial development of learners besides improving hygiene and sanitation. The project will use its existing M&E system to monitor report, feedback, document, and share evident lessons. The project is also designed to ensure maximum impact is achieved through the proposed interventions besides ensuring accountability to the affected population. The project intends to hold periodic stakeholders/ community representatives’ consultative meetings for enhanced engagements to enhance accountability to the affected population. The project will ensure that the project eliminates the issues of sexual exploitation by enhancing the adoption of code of conduct.
The project will has developed a feedback/ complain mechanism system which will include suggestion boxes, quarterly briefs forums with the stakeholders and reporting desks in specific locations. Risk analysis will be conducted to identify and mitigate potential risks. Mapping of the targets will be conducted to eliminate potential barriers to access. The project will ensure that there is enhanced protection of learners and reduced inequalities while ensuring equity. Gender aspects will be considered in all through the programme by ensuring that the target has a gender segregated approach and gender sensitive.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC classification report, Akobo and Pibor counties in Jonglei State are under IPC 5, while Uror, Nyirol, Duk and Twic East are under IPC 4; the report advocates for immediate and coordinated response to the humanitarian needs of the affected population to areas under IPC 3, 4 & 5. (South Sudan: IPC Results October 2020 - July 2021). In the wake of persistent fragility and economic crisis, an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This is a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. In addition, there are 98,500 school-aged refugee children, of whom 18,000 children are out of school. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures have resulted in an increased number of children in need of education support.(HNO 20201). There are multiple reasons for the worsening education situation, including displacement and wide spread conflict, poverty, damaged and inadequate numbers of classrooms, shortage of teachers (especially female teachers) and relevant learning and teaching resources, cultural norms which deprioritize education for girls and long distances to schools for many children. (OOSC Report, 2018). According to the 2018 education cluster assessment findings, it was evident that nationally, the biggest support required by primary schools is teaching and learning supplies (46.2%), this is followed by school feeding (44.5%), teacher salaries (43.7%), rehabilitation of infrastructure (35.2%) and teacher training (23.2%) (ECA, 2018). Recommendations to break through the supply-side barriers to education therefore include: Implement the food-for-work/school feeding policy, encouraging active PTAs, community constructed classrooms, school feeding, and school garden interventions for primary and secondary schools. Only 6 out of every 10 school-aged child attend school. Less than 45 per cent of those attending are girls. (Schools Attendance Monitoring System database, 2019). Girls are more likely to be excluded from receiving education as parents prefer to send boys to school. This increases the risk of girls ending up in an early marriage. In 2019, about 17 per cent of girls were enrolled in pre-primary, 58 per cent in primary and only 25 per cent of girls received a secondary level education. (FSNMS, 2020). The Impact of Covid 19 on Schools unpublished report indicates that besides the economic barriers such as lose of source of livelihood, leaners enrolments an retention has been affected by the following barriers: lack of materials 22%, lack of books 21%, early pregnancy 27%, early marriage 5%, floods 3%, school fees 16% and Uniform 7%. Additionally the pandemic as affected the academic achievements of leaners as stipulated: learning time loss 86.5%, mental problems 42.2%, protection issues 37.9% and nutrition problems at 23%. Flooding in South Sudan since July 2020 has significantly impacted education infrastructure, particularly in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile, Warrap and Western Equatoria states. Many flood-affected people took shelter in schools. This may affect the first phase reopening of schools as announced by the MoGEI. Since February 2020, a total of 430 schools in 9 states have been damaged/destroyed by flood and violence. These schools were also occupied by people displaced due to floods and violence. (Reports from State Education Sub-Cluster Focal Persons – Schools affected by Flood and Violence, 2020). In light of the above analysis, most children in various learning centers of the project target locations have been majorly affected hence therefore there is dare need to develop an integrated intervention that will ensure that the educations system is restored by increasing access, quality, inclusive and safe learning, while ensuring that they risk of new cholera infections and spread of COVID-19 are significantly reduced.

The project will adopt the following activities for implementation to achieve it's major objective; 1) Support 42 schools with integrated Education in Emergency services.2) Enroll and retain 15,000 (7,650 B & 7,350 G) children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers.3) Establish 25 temporary learning spaces and classrooms. 4) Rehabilitate 15 temporary learning spaces and classrooms.5) Provide 4,000; 2,040 B & 1,960G age 3-5 children with ECD kits.6) Establish 25 gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion.7) Rehabilitate 15 gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion.8) Procure and supply Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to 1,500 (750B & 735G) learners.9) Procure and supply sanitary and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to 400 (200 M & 200 W) teachers.10) Provide 2,000 (1,020 B & 980 G) emergency-affected children with quality education recreational materials.11) Train teachers 400 (200 M & 200 W) in EiE pedagogical skills and Child-centred methodologies to improve teaching -learning process for emergency-affected children.12) Train and update 400 (200 M & 200 W) teachers and 15, 000 (7,650 B & 7,350 G) learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms13) Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to 2000 (1,020B & 980G) youth and adolescents.14) Train 400 (200 M & 200 W) teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health and nutrition.15) Train 400 (200 M & 200 W) SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).16) Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures to 42 schools.17) Organise back to learning campaigns in 6 project locations. 18) Procurement of 1,500 learning desks to the newly constructed TLSs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In order to ensure that the project eliminates the issues of sexual exploitation, the project will be guided by the organization code of conduct that stipulates on the guidelines and measures of how to deter and handle sexual exploitation cases and abuse. The project will also use it's internal mechanism of PSEA reporting to ensure that all the cases are reported and appropriate actions are taken as guided by the policy.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Enable affected populations in all categories including individuals living with disability to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will develop a feedback/ complain mechanism system which will include suggestion boxes, quarterly briefs forums with the stakeholders and reporting desks in specific locations. This system will provide an opportunity for the community, leaders and the beneficiaries to be able to launch any form of complains besides voicing their opinions, perceptions and ideas to the project steering committee. The project will develop a stakeholders forum in which the community and the project steering committee will be meeting to discuss the progress, this forum will be essential in ensuring that community participation and engagement is attained and that there perspectives on the project is considered, this forum will also be used to select a few stakeholders who will occasionally be involved in joint monitoring of the project and report back to the forum.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. For example, a complaints and feedback suggestion boxes will be placed at the school and in strategic public places identified and agreed by the community. Weekly, the project team will open and collect any feedback and complaints deposited in the suggestion boxes. A log of all feedback received will be kept and at the end of every month analysis of the log sheet is done to inform project modifications.
