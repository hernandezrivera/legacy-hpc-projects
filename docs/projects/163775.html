Project Summary

The program aims to improve the living conditions for the most vulnerable communities, including migrants, refugees, and their host communities in Libya in an inclusive and conflict-sensitive manner by enhancing access to basic services in the Health sector.
The specific objectives are:
(1) Basic service facilities upgraded in the municipalities listed through the supply of equipment and rehabilitation/maintenance works.
(2) Relevant service providers’ personnel capacity built in the management, operation and maintenance of the upgraded facilities, including the provided equipment.
(3) Awareness raised among the target population on inclusive access to services.
The program will target the following Municipalities in the East of Libya:
Lot 1:
- Ajdabiya Municipality: Saeed Almagariaf Hospital - Provision of Intensive care units and two ambulances,
- Benghazi Municipality: Children Hospital - Maintenance of outpatient clinic and provision of medical equipment and Sidi Mansor PHCC - Building maintenance and provision of medical equipment
- Kufra Municipality: Kufra Maternity Center - Building maintenance and provision of medical equipment, Libya Polyclinic - Building maintenance and provision of medical equipment, Ribyana Medical Center - Building maintenance and provision of medical equipment.
Lot 2:
- Al Bayda Municipality: Clinic number 3 - Provision of medical equipment, Althawra Teaching Hospital - Provision of medical equipment
- Tobruk Municipality: Al Manara Community PHCC - Provision of a generator.

1 - Needs

The escalation of the conflict in Tripoli since April 2019 and clashes in Murzuq in August 2019 have resulted in increased civilian casualties and further displacement deepening people’s need for protection assistance. Across the country, over 343,180 Libyans remain displaced, including 128,000 people due to the Tripoli conflict while Benghazi remains the principal IPD-producer municipality with 27,080 people, reversing the declining trend in displacement. As displacement has increased, so has the number of Libyans who are returning to their homes, around 447,000 people (with Benghazi being the top 1 of the regions with returnees (189,025)). Despite the crisis, Libya remains an attractive destination for migrant workers due to an economy that relies on foreign labour, higher salaries and historical ties, as well as being a transitory route for people seeking opportunities or asylum in Europe. At least 636,426 migrants are in Libya (IOM, Libya’s migrant report 27 –Aug-Sep 2019) and exposed to protection risks, human rights violations, exploitation and abuse. Migrants were identified in all 100 municipalities, within 567 communities, with 26% of them located in Eastern Libya (IOM, DTM, LBY MVHNA, Report. Dec 2019). The unprotected legal environment for migrants combines with the lack of refugee framework, which complicates the situation of non-Libyans that flee from conflict in their countries of origin and could potentially be considered asylum-seekers and/or refugees. In addition, seven years into the crisis, people across Libya have been increasingly impacted by the deterioration of public-sector services. The dysfunctional public health system (gap in service provision filled by the private health facilities), the under-capacity of the administrative system, the low initiatives or presence of infrastructures to improve the social cohesion of the communities and the non-functional water and sanitation services have resulted in a lack of access to primary and secondary healthcare, a low ability to secure the wellbeing of people and failing sustainable water supplies and waste management.

2 - Activities

In order to improve a better social cohesion and social inclusion among the most vulnerable, PUI will ensure the rehabilitation and the provision of medical equipment of the targeted Health Facilities in the East of Libya. The first phase of the project will be about a comprehensive assessment based on a participative and integrated approach. The project team will undertake a rapid assessment with the main relevant stakeholders to evaluate the priority needs in relation with the Health facilities already identified for the project itself, to ensure safe access to the facility as well as PUI's acceptance. Additionally, it will contribute to the sustainability of the project since they should be included in their local development plans. Then, PUI will conduct an in-depth technical assessment to prepare the Bill of Quantity, the list of furniture and medical equipment to ensure a sustainable reactivation of the services or the upgrade of the facility. The second phase of the project will be about the call of tender and the selection of the suppliers who will be in charge to carry out the rehabilitation of the health facilities and the provision of medical equipment. PUI will lead the procurement process and will put in place internal mechanisms to guarantee a transparent selection of suppliers. Then, PUI program team will monitor and evaluate the quality of the implementation. PUI will also ensure that the relevant authorities will be involved as each phase of the project. The third phase will be about the capacity building of the personnel involved in the management and operation of the facilities through a comprehensive training plan. Finally, PUI program team will define opportunities/modalities for local community awareness campaigns and feedback mechanisms on inclusive access to essential services through various consultations with Municipalities, key stakeholders and community leaders. Then, training, workshops and awareness sessions will be conducted.

4 - Protection risk analysis

-Bureaucratic restrictions on humanitarian supplies: Difficulty in obtaining supplies importation authorisation due to governance crisis, highly corrupted environment and the discretion of the authorities who are frequently changing the procedures and focal points, without any legal basis.- Restriction of movement of personnel and humanitarian supplies and interference in the implementation of activities: Serous restriction of movement of program teams to the intervention areas due to the difference forces, national and local authorities and mushrooming groups in charge of security and access. -New entities (administrative bodies, security forces) are emerging and claiming a role in delivering the necessary authorisations to assess and respond to humanitarian needs.-Major context changes in Tunisia and/or Libya (growing foreign influence, including direct military intervention, the collapse of one of the main party to the conflict, the shifting alliances, the resurgence of extremist groups, including ISIS or major political change, including elections, could potentially put activities on hold if security lessens/if the authorities become more wary of movement restrictions during the project’s implementation time.-Criminal and domestic violence can also increase. Based on the security incidents follow up done by PUI. In the last two months, cases of drug trafficking, GBV (particularly affecting the Sudanese community), kidnappings, and car robbery were recorded by PUI team on the ground.-Authorities restrict access to certain vulnerable beneficiary groups (e.g. Refugees and migrants, IDPs, certain populations in Darnah).-Natural or man-made disasters, especially in conflict, will displace target beneficiaries and disrupt the functioning of the local authorities. -Deterioration in the security situation could lead to insecurity for project staff and beneficiaries, restricted access to inputs and higher prices, limited access to areas of implementation.

5 - Mitigation measures

Mitigation measures linked to community acceptance-Proactive coordination with MoH and local authorities to maintain good relationships and acceptance-Proactive communication with local communities to explain the goal and the targeting processes of Action.- Integration of protection officers in the outreach teams (Under ECHO project) to reinforce the communication and acceptance with the community fabric of the areas of intervention.Mitigation measures linked to access restriction- Active communication and coordination with humanitarian actors in East Libya to develop a common access strategy, including red lines to access regarding the sharing of some documents and the engagement with certain authorities, private companies or individuals claiming to play an (unofficial) role in granting access-Proactive communication with official and traditional leaders, and representative groups claiming their play a role in terms of security and access, will be reinforced and adjusted in order to present PUI mandate and principles.-Security plan of the intervention areas will be updated according to the constant monitoring that PUI is doing, and will continue feeding all along with the project implementation.-A set of SOP documents will be updated and reinforced to reduce risk impacts. If insecurity arises, PUI will adapt its implementation process in order to guarantee equal and safe access to assistance for the beneficiaries.Main mitigation measures related to support and aid aspects-PUI’s team will participate in all the coordination mechanisms both at central and local levels to avoid overlaps of actions and to coordinate response for any unanticipated crisis-Given the absence on the field of the expatriates based in Benghazi office, PUI set up a solid system of remote management.
