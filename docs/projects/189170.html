Project Summary

Grassroots Relief and Development Agency (GREDA) intends to improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 6,693 affected beneficiaries disaggregated by sex and age;

IDPs,( 657 Male 5-17years) 607 Female (3-17years) returnees ( 657 Male 3-17years) 657 Female (3-17 years) spontaneous refugees ( 657 Male 3-17 years) and host community children including children with disabilities ( 567 Male 3-17years) ( 1,597 Female 3-17years) and youth ( 637 girls and 657 boys of (3-17years) in Maridi County, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo County.
The prioritized objective is:-
1: To Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 6,693 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys) in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State.
The main activities to be carried out to realize this objective and achieve the overall goal will include:­
1.1. Conduct sensitization to make raise community awareness of systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.2. Identify and select children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) to access and attend schools in affected areas in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.3. Identify temporary learning spaces established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019 in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.4. Rehabilitate temporary learning spaces or schools in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.5. Organize back to school/learning campaigns in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.6. Train teachers in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties and update them on PSS, CP and referral mechanisms for a better service delivery.
1. 7. Provide children age (5-17) and teachers with teaching and learning materials in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.8. Involve the communities in design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation on the planned or an on-going education project in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties

1 - Needs

Ten years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education.Humanitarian access to essential services as education, remains a challenge in an already complex context.Narrowing it to Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State were the counties badly affected by the multiple conflicts in South Sudan. The multiple conflicts were (1) The conflict between the host Community farmers and cattle herders from the neighboring States (2) The conflict between the Government and Sudan People Liberation Army in-Opposition (SPLA-IO) (3) The conflict between the government and armed groups in Equatoria impacted negatively and forced the majority of the populations not to access basic services due to breakdown of service provision mechanisms. These incursions; within or around aforementioned Counties forced many schools to be nonfunctional hence affecting enrollment and continuity of education for early learners. In a recent assessment by GREDA in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo between October/November 2021 an excess of 10,000 school age children are out of school due to hostilities, poverty and erratic weather patterns have also immensely contributed to drop outs, due to lack of learning structures, and issues surrounding access and safety for adolescent girls. This situation is compounded by the increase in the demand for education by IDPs/returnees children. Though all children who have been displaced are vulnerable, adolescent boys and young men remain particularly vulnerable to participation in dangerous activities and recruitment by armed actors. Adolescent girls are at risk of early and forced marriage, as well as sexual abuse and exploitation due to the low value placed on the girl child. The closure of learning spaces has left separated and unaccompanied minors without opportunities for case management. Education indicators remain amongst the worst in the Country, Far fewer girls make it to the last grade of primary education. For every 100 boys completing primary school, only 63 girls completed the cycle (UIS, 2018). Cultural and social factors play a role in keeping girls from transitioning from primary to secondary education, particularly following the onset of puberty. South Sudan is ranked among the top twenty countries in the world with the highest prevalence rates for child marriage, and, according to the 2010 NBS survey, 45% of women, ages 20-49, were married before they were 18-years-old and 7% of women, ages 15-49, were married before turning 15-years-old. Of women ages 20-24, over half (52%) were married before they were 18-years-old. Of these women, 20% of women with at least a secondary education were married, compared to 59% of those with no education (NBS, 2010). Globally, girls who have completed secondary education are six times less likely to become child brides and have better health and economic outcomes throughout their lives; under-five mortality rates also tend to be higher in child marriages (UNFPA, 2018). The challenge of unqualified teachers within the Counties has continually been a detrimental factor to realizing education goals as many are not conversant with appropriate child-centered pedagogy. This project aims at increasing basic accessible, equitable, protective, quality education in emergency to young children in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback mechanisms will provide GREDA with data and perceptions from primary stakeholders about the quality and effectiveness of their efforts. An ideal feedback process involves the gathering of feedback and the communication of a response, which forms a 'feedback loop.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

GREDA will provide opportunity for participation to the vulnerable members e.g. individuals with disabilities and those not literate to facilitate their feedbacks. GREDA will communicate with understandable language and also use translations as possible to allow beneficiaries understand the project objectives to enable them to provide feedbacks on the project implementation performances.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

GREDA will compose a set of lessons designed to raise awareness about Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, to allow beneficiaries become familiar with a range of measures to combat Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, We will orient the beneficiaries the impact of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse on victims and the consequences for UN Personnel who commit Sexual Exploitation and Abuse through discussion.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

GREDA with numerous tools can be used by beneficiaries to provide feedback, including suggestion boxes, surveys, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), focus group discussions, one-to-one interviews, community meetings, notice boards.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

GREDA will effectively address GBV through a dual approach of changing social norms and behaviours, and building gender-responsive institutions, particularly justice systems, which support survivors and end impunity for perpetrators. If there are conflict and tension within partners GREDA will help in decision-making and assets; attitudes and practices that reinforce female subordination and tolerate male violence (e.g. dowry, bride price, child marriage) GREDA staff will work with relevant internal teams to build complaints mechanisms to ensure that children are able to raise concerns and have a voice, among persons of concern of the need to prevent SGBV and promote gender equality, and about services available to survivors, identify or encourage the formation of community-based networks among persons of concern and assist them in their preventive and information work on SGBV.GREDA Common feedback mechanisms will include suggestion boxes or help desks located at the distribution site that will allows recipients to confidentially report corruption or abuse of power (especially regarding sexual exploitation), and to seek redress.GREDA will respect beneficiaries’ autonomy via provision of adequate consent, ensuring equity, and respecting participants' privacy.Left unaddressed, escalating land disputes that can result in project delays, increased operational, labor and legal costs, and supply chain issues, damage to property, security concerns, and reputational harm will be shared with respective authorities. GREDA will share precautions that can help to prevent the formation of an explosive atmosphere, including by ventilation.
