Project Summary

This project will respond to the need of the most vulnerable displaced 20,000 individuals in Eastern and Western Equatoria State, targeting with static response Mundri East and West counties (WES) and with mobile response Tambura (WES), and Magwi county (EES), over the period of one year. The disaggregated numbers of estimated beneficiaries are: 4,500 men, 6,900 women, 4,100 boys and 4,500 girls. The project targets the most vulnerable IDPs (60%, both newly displaced and protracted IDPs), returnees (30%), and host population (10%), particularly focusing on people with protection concerns and with most acute needs, in order to alleviate their sufferings and foster protection through access to safe water, and improvement of hygiene and sanitation.
The project activities will include direct distribution of WASH kits (each containing 120 aqua tabs/PUR, 3 bars of soap, 1 filter cloth and 2 buckets) to the most vulnerable households, including pregnant and lactating women (PLW), elderly and disabled persons, and children under 5. Also, it includes distribution of MHM kits to women and girls of reproductive age.
The LCED team will identify distribution locations through in-depth assessment, and Relief
Distribution Committees (RDCs) will be established at each distribution site, to facilitate the identification of the most vulnerable households and their needs, through community participation. This activity will also imply training of the beneficiaries on household water treatment, which will be conducted house-to-house by trained community hygiene promoters. The project activities will also include Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with women and girls in order to identify collective water points with secure location and access. At the same time, Water Management Committees (WMCs) will be established and trained in each area, which will be responsible for the maintenance of water points identified.
Another project activity will be the rehabilitation/improvement of existing sanitation facilities, in order to make them secure, protected and gender-appropriate (gender-segregated latrines with provision of locks and doors). This will include other FGDs, 50% with women and 50% with men. These activities will be paired with community sensitization on the use and maintenance of safe water sources and appropriate sanitation facilities, and on the use of correct hygiene and sanitation practices, e.g. the need of washing hands at critical moments, food preservation and proper storage of water safe for drinking, and discourage defecation, especially near open water sources. Community sessions of hygiene promotion will be conducted in common spaces in various locations, accessible by all categories of marginalized persons, by trained hygiene promoters in the course of one year.
Finally, a CBI will be implemented, targeting 10% of the total project beneficiaries. LCED will implement two types of CBI: a cash-for-work program, in which beneficiaries will receive restricted, conditional cash vouchers in exchange for their work in the construction of new temporary latrines (LCED will provide the tools, material and technical supervision) and a CBI targeting vulnerable women, elderly and disabled persons who are not in cannot be targeted in the cash-for-work interventions, with restricted, unconditional cash vouchers for the purchase of essential WASH NFI. This approach will contribute to the resilience and supporting local market economy.

Needs

• Multiple displacement of the population during the course of 2017-18, due to conflict and lack of food, especially in remote areas of Mundri East, Mundri West and Tambura counties (WES), and Magwi county (EES), has negatively impacted the population.• In many counties of WES and EES there is a consistent influx of returnees in some targeted locations of Magwi, Tambura, and Mundri East counties. Since the South Sudan peace process began, returnees from neighbouring counties and countries such as Uganda, DRC and CAR are seen to return to their locations with the increased need for emergency assistance to help such populations to settle.• Areas of passage of cattle keepers such as Greater Mundri are subjected to violence and looting. • The initial rapid assessment conducted by LCED in Western Equatoria revealed that most households lack basic WASH NFIs (e.g. soap, water guards, water storage containers), due to displacement, looting and deterioration of items, lack of access to the markets and little purchasing power. This particularly exposes women to GBV, since men ask for sexual favours in exchange for goods or services. • Remote areas of the targeted counties have not been accessed for most of 2018.• There is lack of basic good hygiene practices among IDPs, returnees and host population, due to lack of WASH items especially water storage containers, but also limited knowledge of such practices and strong cultural beliefs/habits. • There is little access to clean water for majority of IDPs and conflict-affected population in Western Equatoria, which implies difficulty in storing it, and need to sustain hygiene promotion activities for a consistent period of time.• Women, girls, children and elderly people are more vulnerable than other groups when it comes to WASH needs. Pregnant and lactating mothers, children below 5 and elderly persons are more exposed to and affected by environmental hazards and health risks where in lack of essential WASH NFIs and safe hygiene and sanitation practices. For instance, due to lack of water storage containers, people use the same container to cook/eat and bathe.• Open defecation practices are common, due to lack of available latrines and to dangerous habits of the people in need, posing risk of water contamination and water-borne diseases.• Poor household waste disposal, with risk of food and water contamination, and of contracting bacterial infections for children and elderly persons.• Deprivation in times of conflict affects both gender but greatly impact more on women and girls. Assessment findings informed us that there the conflict affected girls and women continue to suffer with lack of sanitary pads especially those of reproductive age. The use of unsafe MHM practices puts them at risk of infections. LCED’s previous projects targeted around 20% of the beneficiaries for distribution of MHM (reusable pads), however, the assessed findings informed us that this number should be increased to at least 50-70% according to the population targeted in a location.• Poor household waste disposal is a big risk to food and water contamination and lead to contracting of bacterial infections for children and elderly persons.• Child-headed households and elderly people are also exposed to other form of violence, and they are not able to live in dignity, because they are not strong enough to cater for their needs (e.g. fetch water). In many remote areas they do not even benefit from humanitarian assistance because the distribution point is far from where they live, and the items never reach them.

Activities

• Conduction of assessment/verifications/community consultations and market assessment, targeting the most vulnerable displaced persons including women, girls, children and elderly people.• Training of LCED staff members on mainstreaming GBV within WASH activities, and particularly on the GBV referral pathway.• Rehabilitation of borehole (hand pump).• Construction of temporary latrines by IDPs/returnees and members of the local community in need, under a cash-for-work program.• Community training on maintenance of water points (hand pump).• Distribution of hygiene and WASH NFIs.• Distribution of MHM kits to women and girls of reproductive age.• Engagement of local suppliers, which will sign a contract with LCED, after defining a ES/NFI voucher package for beneficiaries, and specific fixed prices for each item.• Distribution of restricted, unconditional cash vouchers to female-headed households.• Distribution of restricted, conditional cash vouchers to individuals who participated in the cash-for-work program.• Conduction of community hygiene promotion training.• Conduction of hygiene promotion sessions in the targeted locations.• Provide monthly distribution reports (5Ws) to the WASH cluster for accountability.• Conduction of Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM).

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The LCED field officers will collaborate with the Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs, including representatives of the most marginalized groups), local authorities and community leaders in order to share with the beneficiaries and the rest of the community information on the assistance provided, including:-information on the organization mandate, prioritization criteria and size of the intervention (including resource and access constraints) with the population at the time of the assessment/verification/community consultations, in order to manage their expectations,-information on the use of the WASH NFIs during the distribution, and instructions on how to use the complaint desk/feedback mechanism, and how to report eventual cases of misconduct by the LCED staff or volunteers and GBV to the relevant people/authorities. This will include informing the beneficiaries about their rights and entitlements, from the beginning of the project.-information on the training of community hygiene promoters and criteria for selections during the phase of identification of suitable candidates, as well as stressing the importance of the hygiene promotion sessions (house-to-house or community based).-Information on lessons learnt and key actions to implement changes, conveyed by the RDCs after the PDM

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Different gender and age groups will receive different information according to the assistance they will be provided. For instance, specific messages on Menstrual Health Management (MHM) before and during the distribution of MHM kits. Additionally, specific information regarding the CBI will be conveyed to the project beneficiaries according to their gender group, where women and elderly persons are targeted with restricted, unconditional cash vouchers, while able-bodied men are targeted with the cash-for-work component of this project. Finally, community sensitization on GBV, in relation to the maintenance of safe water points and gender-segregated sanitation facilities, will be done using gender-tailored messages for men/boys and women/girls.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

During the distribution of items, the beneficiaries will use separate queues by gender, in order to avoid harassment towards women, while the LCED volunteers will handpick the most vulnerable people including PLW, elderly persons and disabled persons, and assist them in receiving and transporting their items. Women and girls of reproductive age will be targeted by distribution of MHM kits (from 20% of targeted beneficiaries to 50%). PLW, elderly persons and child-headed households will be assisted not only by distribution of items, but also with transportation of the items to their place of residence. Maintaining safe water points and gender segregated latrines, with doors and locks, will be a priority to reduce the risk of GBV towards women and girls. This will also be paired with community sensitization on GBV and creation of Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs) and Water Management Committees (WMCs) which will be responsible for the maintenance of safe practices. At least 30% of the members of the RDCs and the WMCs must be women, and 30% youth, in order to ensure that needs and challenges of all categories (men, women, boys and girls) are taken into consideration during the implementation of different project activities. Additionally, crowd controllers, enumerators and volunteers that LCED will hire locally should be at least 30% women, in order to ensure an income for both genders. Finally, the beneficiaries receiving cash vouchers will be only women, in order to ensure that the assistance will go to their households and not another family, considering the polygamous nature of many marriages. Also, targeting women with CBI will empower them economically, and as it has been observed in previous project, it will allow them to maintain themselves and their children without having to rely on friends and relatives, hence avoiding exposure to sexual harassment and GBV.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Community consultations do not only include the community leaders, but also Focus Group Discussions with representatives of the vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly and disable persons), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of such categories are addressed. Also, in each distribution location LCED will form a Relief Distribution Committee (RDC), including IDP/returnee/host community leaders, women representatives and youth representatives from the IDPs, and sub-chiefs. The RDCs provide great insight for activity planning and project design, as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

LCED is committed to ensuring accountability to affected communities is prioritised and therefore setting up a community complaint feedback will be one of the major priority areas to address any complaints, provide feedback on any project activities that is vital in improving WASH interventions. The Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs) will work closely with LCED to ensure that IDPs, returnees and host communities are aware of the priorities for the selection of beneficiaries for distribution, and how vulnerable households have been selected and verified. They will also assist the LCED team in the distribution of WASH NFIs, identification of suitable community hygiene promoters and members of the Water Management Committees (WMCs) to be trained, and where mandates will mediate in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries. LCED team will collect all the complaints/feedback and address issues raised to ensure that beneficiaries are satisfied with the approaches and service provided.During the distribution of WASH items, a suggestion box/complaint desk will be set up to address any gaps as reported by the targeted population and this will be managed by the RDCs seconded by LCED and local authorities.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To ensure timely, efficient and effective implementation of project activities, regular monitoring and evaluation visits will be conducted time to time with the active participation of all key stakeholders including the beneficiary communities, local leaders and WASH cluster monitoring team. LCED’s M&E approach will be community centred with a great focus on Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) (inclusive participation) in addressing issues related to access, safety, equity, quality and accountability. This is not only to contribute to accountability but to engage the community for ownership and sustainability.Women, girls and the most marginalised groups will be part of the monitoring and evaluation process. Monitoring mechanisms will include monthly stakeholder meetings including beneficiaries at community level, review of quarterly work plans and achievements with all stakeholders, sharing of progress reports on project activities and household visits by village/community hygiene promoters. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted within 4 weeks after the distribution of WASH items, in order to evaluate the actual benefits derived from the project. The M&E team plans to use the PDM WASH cluster designed tool to track performance as well as draw lessons for learning.At field level, project officers working closely with community leaders and beneficiaries will take lead and report progress on community learning events. Gender and age aggregated data will always be produced to help monitor support to persons with different age and gender. These reports will then be generated by the M&E Officer on a monthly basis and submitted to the WASH Cluster by the LCED Program Manager.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project considers the protection risks for the most vulnerable groups: women and girls, children, elderly persons. Women and girls are greatly exposed to risk of GBV when they practice open defecation, and when they use unsafe water points and unprotected sanitation facilities (without doors and locks). Related to that, the lack of women and girls’ purchasing power often make them victim of GBV, since men ask for sexual favours in exchange for goods or services. Children and elderly people are also exposed to other form of violence, and they are not able to live in dignity, because they are not strong enough to cater for their needs (e.g. fetch water).

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

LCED considered access barriers for different population groups, and will put in place specific measures to overcome them. For instance, in many contexts of South Sudan women and youth are not included in community consultation, or are not able to express themselves in mixed groups (with men or elderly) due to cultural norms. Considering that, during the assessment, verification and PDM, separate Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with representatives of men, women, boys and girls, as well as elderly and disabled people, in order to identify their needs. The LCED will make sure to always have at least one female staff member in the field, in order to conduct FGDs with women.Furthermore, in many remote areas children and elderly people do not benefit from humanitarian assistance because the distribution point is far from where they live, and the items never reach them. Therefore, PLW, elderly persons and child-headed households will be assisted not only by distribution of items, but also with transportation of the items to their place of residence by the LCED volunteers, selected among the local community.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Addressing inequality and protection threats is an essential component of this project. Firstly, women might face protection threats while practicing open defecation or by access water points and unprotected latrines. Considering that, one of the project priorities will be to ensure that all sanitation facilities (existing and newly established) are gender-segregated and provided with doors and locks. Additionally, all WASH activities will be paired with community sensitization on GBV and creation of Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs) and Water Management Committees (WMCs) which will be responsible for the maintenance of safe water points, for instance by defining certain hours in which only women are allow to access them, and by making sure that the water points are clear from bushes and not overcrowded.Secondly, the lack of women and girls’ purchasing power often make them victim of GBV, since men ask for sexual favours in exchange for goods or services. A Therefore, LCED will target women for a tailored CBI, in areas where they have access to the market.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Ensuring access to the marginalised group is LCED priority area. LCED team will conduct an assessment using the WASH Cluster assessment tool to identify the different WASH needs of the targeted population. In collaboration with the local authorities and target beneficiaries, locations agreed upon by the beneficiaries will be identified and rehabilitation of water sources (boreholes) will be facilitated by LCED in addition to rehabilitating existing but functional boreholes. This is intended to provide enough water to the target population while saving time spent in queues or distance travelled to access water.Additionally, this will address the noted challenges faced by women and girls in accessing water including sexual harassment along the way to distant water sources. LCED will also facilitate construction of gender-segregated latrines that will be gender sensitive. Women and girls, elderly and disabled persons will be consulted on the design of a latrine of their choice. Lastly, Women and girls will be consulted on menstrual hygiene kits to help them improve on the hygiene while cash based interventions will also target women to ensure they meet their priority needs not supported during WASH in- kind distributions. women, men, boys and girls will be included in all the trainings of SGBV and HIV/AIDS)M&E Tools; Household survey questionnaires, Focus Groups Discussion guide and observation, activity reports and PDMM&E Indicators:• % of targeted population participating in the design of water sources and latrines• No of old/not functional water sources identified and rehabilitated• % of households accessing improved water source• No. of gender-segregated latrines constructed• % of households with access to sanitation facilities (latrines)• No. of females accessing Menstrual Hygiene Management kits• % of total disaggregated population accessing CBI (men and women)

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The target population will be consulted on the specific locations for the distribution of WASH items to minimise security risks along the way. Additionally, a plan for maintenance of existing water points and sanitation facilities, as well as construction of new sanitation facilities as part of the cash-for-work component of this project. The design of the boreholes and latrines will be influenced by the views of the beneficiaries especially the elderly and women to ensure they are not only safe but accessible. For example latrines will consider safety and protection of young children and the very elderlyM&E Tools; Individual interviews, Key Informant questionnaires, FGDs, Reports, Complaint Feedback/suggestion box and PDMM&E Indicators:• % of targeted population reporting to have experienced any security challenges in accessing WASH assistance• No. of girls/women reporting any sexual harassment• % of target population reporting that the rehabilitated water sources and rehabilitated/constructed latrines were safe and secure

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

LCED in accordance with the AAP guidelines will ensure that project beneficiaries are treated with respect at all times by staff and community leaders as is embedded in all project activities. During the trainings for community hygiene promoters, RDCs and water management committees, this will be part of the sessions to be covered. The community will be allowed to suggest possible ways of helping the marginalised groups’ access services. Clear information about the modalities used in selection of beneficiaries and distribution of WASH items will be shared with the community to ensure satisfaction of beneficiaries. Lastly, the complaint feedback desk will be put in place to address any challenges encountered and also provide feedback.M&E tools; Individual interviews, Key Informant questionnaires, FGDs, Reports, Complaint Feedback/suggestion box, and PDMM&E Indicators:• % of targeted population reporting being excluded from accessing assistance• % of targeted population reporting to have observed special categories given consideration• % of targeted population reporting that the selection criteria was fair• % of individuals reporting that information about assessment/verification and distribution processes was clear for everyone• % of individual reporting that they were respected by staff and other community leaders• % of individuals reporting that their views, complaints and feedback were addressed
