Project Summary

Serious deterioration of food insecurity and the resultant huge food consumption gaps owing to depletion of livelihoods has significantly impacted into the lives of women, men, boys and girls as well as the elderly and those living with disabilities particularly the IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable in Lower Juba Region, Somalia. Their plight is attributed to combined effects of poor and erratic Gu 2021 rainfall worsened by past successive drought seasons and seasons of below average rains and floods that damaged crops and farmland and further displaced local populations in the riverine areas hence below-average Gu crop production whereas they are yet to recover from impacts of past climatic and socio-economic shocks and impacts of desert locust that continue to pose a serious risk to both pasture availability and crop production.
According to (FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Report of September 9, 2021) report, Approximately 3.5 million people in Somalia face acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in late 2021 with far reaching impacts in 2022. Current weather forecasts indicate an increased likelihood of below-average rainfall during the 2021 Deyr (October-December) season across most parts of the country which will adversely affect food security and nutrition outcomes where food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced population. This plight calls for timely emergency life-saving and sustainable Food security intervention to enable the worst crisis affected men, women boys and girls as well as the elderly and those with disabilities in the project targeted meet their immediate and long term food needs through conditional and non-conditional safety nets as well as earn income in form of cash for work to sustain their family food needs in order to mitigate already precarious food insecurity conditions and further restore and protect their livelihoods and strengthen their resilience with suitable food security support programmes to cope with the current and future shocks. The project targets 33000 persons (13728 women, 12672 men, 3300 boys and 3300 girls).

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. The project will ensure inclusion and participation of the most vulnerable groups the IDPs and non-IDPs including minority clans, women, those with disability and the elders in the consultations at assessments to identify needs for the project where their concerns will be taken into consideration for appropriate response by the project. They will in addition be included at all stages of implementation including regular monitoring where their concerns will be part of key decisions in terms of recommendations and suggestion on project implementation thus project covering all their specific needs.2. The project will strengthen protection of women and girls from sexual violence by ensuring project activities are easily accessible by the targeted women and girls hence limiting them from covering long distance and related risk of sexual violence when accessing the services. Moreover, the project activities will be undertaken at early hours of the day to limit women and girls from walking under the cover of darkness back to the homes and settlements to mitigate risk of sexual violence that do occur under the cover of darkness3. The project will be implemented under very strict adherence to PSEA policy where targeted women and girls will be made fully aware of all project activities hence their expectation from the project activities during the project's social mobilization and sensitization process at the project commencement in order to mitigate any form of sexual exploitation and abuse by project staff, local administration staff and the security personnel. In addition, it will ensure that complaints and feedback mechanisms such as suggestion boxes and toll free hotlines. are rightfully in place and easily accessible by both the targeted beneficiaries. Additionally, the project will be implemented under inline with Do no Harm principle where none of the project activities will be a cause of conflict between the community and the targeted beneficiaries .
