Project Summary

CDD-CBM is operating a ‘Child Friendly Space (CFS)’ just beside our ‘One Stop Integrated Health & Rehab Service Centre for all’. The CFS is one of our key programmatic interventions to respond to the educational and recreational need of the Rohingya children. CFS provides safe environment for children in which they can participate in organized activities to play, socialize, learn, and express themselves. The key principles for planning, developing and operating child friendly spaces includes the inclusive and non-discriminatory approach. However, an inclusive and non-discriminatory approach ensures that all children regardless of their class, gender, abilities, language, ethnicity, sexual orientation, religion, nationality have equal access to CFS.

Needs

A rapid assessment was conducted by CDD and ASB on situation of people with disability in the refugee camp. It identified that persons with disabilities are mostly not accessing or not reached by service providers. Persons with disabilities and older persons face discrimination and barriers in the camps, including from service providers. Most older people and people with disabilities were not aware of specialised health services, including opportunities for accessing assistive devices to meet their needs. The persons with disabilities and older people hardly had access to services in Myanmar.CBM and CDD are working together to address along with the implementation of the activities in the camp, namely: ● General limited awareness on the importance of, how to collect disability data and identify persons with disabilities and older persons within the response;● Actors are waiting for more information and technical support on collecting disability data through The Washington Group Short Set of Questions on Disability ;● Few services are accessible to persons with functioning limitation, such as toilets, water sources, access around camps and distance to health and distribution points; this directly impacts person with disabilities and older persons ability to meet their needs;● People with disabilities are not aware of specialized health services, including opportunities for accessing assistive devices to meet their needs;● Persons with disabilities face discrimination and barriers in the camps, including from service providers; The latest data from multi-sectoral needs assessment don in July 2018 by UNHCR with REACH show that 44% of households have at least one person with disability or chronic illness. The data we collected since April 2018 from our services 23% of the beneficiaries are childre below 18 years of old (around 50% boys and 50% girls, among them 11% are children with disabilities - data collected using 6 Wahhington group questions).

Activities or outputs

Children with disabilities have accessed and meaningfully participated to safe inclusive recreation activities and sensitisation to parents will be done on Disability and Inclusion and DRR.
