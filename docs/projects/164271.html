Project Name

Start Date

01/01/2020

End Date

31/12/2020

Project Summary

The project will participate in fulfilling the health needs of both IDP and Host Communities in
Northern Aleppo and Afrin district through continuing current projects and health facilities and provide equipment, medications, supplies and operational costs (salaries, fuel, etc.) for six primary health care facilities, three maternity and child hospitals, three medical mobile clinics, three RH mobile clinics, two MHPSS centers, one MHPSS unit with two MHPSS mobile teams, one TB center, one dialysis center, one rehabilitation center and three immunization centers in addition to implement referral system for emergency and non emergency patients transportation through sixteen ambulances and six non-emergency patients transportation vehicle, as well participate in improving infection control through operating central incinerator for medical wastes for operational health facilitates in northern Aleppo mainly Afrin, Azaz and Albab.

4 - Needs

Since 1 December 2019, nearly 586,000 people have fled from their homes, mainly from southern Idleb and western Aleppo. many IDPs moved towards Afrin, A’zaz and Al-Bab has caused a great burden on medical services provided in these area which was already overwhelmed . Based on a health needs assessments done by SRD. The most prominent health facts and needs identified were:- The most problem facing the respondents are: Shelter with 85% of surveys, 82% Health, 81 %Food.- In 2019, 30% of all faced issues of child health in their family, 13% maternal / newborn diseases, 41% each chronic diseases and Non-communicable diseases, 15% malnutrition, and 9% pregnancy complications. - 76% of IDPs in Afrin district are in need to Medicines, followed by Regular primary healthcare/ General Practitioner with 43%of surveys, 31% Gynecological services, 29% Chronic disease management in addition to PSS / mental healthcare Family planning, specialized treatment (dialysis), Maternal and newborn health, Vaccination, Specialized equipment (wheelchair, prosthetics etc.), General health education and promotion.- Most commonly reported targeted camps is Health facilities are overcrowded with 84% of respondents by HHs surveys, that because of the large number of IDPs arrived to district. Followed by Absence / shortage of health workers with 61%, 54% Lack of medicines at the health facility, 39% Cannot afford to pay for health services, 36% Lack of ambulance services, 35% High cost of transportation to health facilities, 26% No health facilities present or functioning in the community, and 25% Lack of transportation to health facilities.- Securing a services of Complications of NCD cases and provide surgeries services - enhance emergency and non-emergency patient trasporta - need to securing Rape, harassment, family violence services - provide NCD medication, Psychotropic medications, SRH medications and provide Screening and referral to PHCs.

5 - Implementation of activities

SRD activities in 2020 are in line with Health Sector Objectives which include:SRD will support its existing health facilities in northern Aleppo and Afrin including 3 Maternity and Child Hospitals (Aghtrin, Afrin and Jandaris), 6 PHCs (Alsalama, Sejo, Qabasin, Bazagha, Albel, Kafr Safra),3 medical mobile teams (Albel, Shamarikh, and Alharameen camps), 3 RH mobile team in Bazagha, Qabasin and Jandaris, 1 Rehabilitation center in Albab, 1 Dialysis center in Aghtrin and immunization services through 3 mobile teams and 3 ﬁxed center in Bazagha, Aghtrin and Sandi. 2 MHPSS centers in Salameh and Bab Alnour, 1 MHPSS unit in Jandaris with 2 mobile MHPSS teams in Afrin and Jandaris, 1 TB center in Azaz and 1 central incinerator for existing health facilities to improve infection control in Maree.the planned support for all above HFs including salaries, running costs, trainings and other needs for the provision of primary and secondary health care services including minor & major surgeries, deliveries, c-section, ANC, PNC, in patient services as well as rehabilitation, immunization and dialysis services. A set of medical equipment is available at the health facilities.To ensure access and continuity of care SRD will operate 16 Ambulances and 6 non-emergency patient transportation vehicle within referral system along with operating of the central referral desk in AfrinSRD planned activities will meet speciﬁc needs to enable access for people with disabilities to health services in accordance to their speciﬁc situation.SRD will ensure the integration of GBV in Health services as there will be Women Waiting area within SRD HFs, ensure that SRD HFs has available Females staff and training HFs staff on GBV principles. in addition to that, provide CMR services when neededSRD has monitoring plan and tools in place for the project with commitment to insure activities implementation, achieving objective and to submit it with project deliverable through the project’s life cycle

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: The displaced population, most speciﬁcally IDP women and girls might face challenges in accessing SRH/GBV services due to multiple barriers on where/why to access. Mitigation 1: Mobilization activities will take place to share information with the community focusing on IDP women and girls about SRH/GBV services at PHCs, MCs and hospital. Protection Risk 2: Overlap with other SRH/GBV services might occur Mitigation 2: Mobilization activities will take place to share information with the community focusing on IDP women and girls about the SRH/GBV services in the PHCs, MCs and hospital. Protection Risk 3: Referrals to/from other services might be disrupted due to the security situation. Mitigation 3: Regular meetings are conducted with other service providers to facilitate referral pathways and regularly update project staff on up-to-date service providers of surroundings communities. Train PHC and MC staff on the health referral system mechanism. Protection Risk 4: Partners’ staff working in highly insecure environments might not be able to access counseling services. Mitigation 4: Self-care training is available for SRD staff in Gaziantep but currently not inside Syria. SRD organizes social self-care days for their staff. Protection Risk 5: Adolescents withdisabilities might face challenges in accessing the clinics/hospital to attend girls’ club sessions. Mitigation 5: Designated vehicles will be provided to facilitate the access of adolescents with disabilities to the girls’ club at the clinics and hospitals. Duty of Care should be take in consideration to provide for the physical and mental safety, security, health, and well-being of its staff. In turn, staff members have a “Duty of Responsibility” to take care of themselves, to follow organizational policies, and to utilize the services available to support themselves.SRD has solid policies for procurement of goods and materials of this project, which comply with donors regulation.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. IED/VBIED /Mines: staff directly or indirectly impacted by the IED/VBIED attacks.Likelihood: likely. 3/5Impact: Severe.Mitigation: Avoid potential targets. Like military HQs, convoys. CPs. Using routes that used by people all the time. On-going threat monitoring/SRA. Avoid potentially contaminated areas. Hiring locals who are familiars with the situation. Medical Evacuation arrangements. Office locations selected outside of potential threat areas. Very good relationship with local community. Staff first aid training. Compliance with the security manual. Complete the available security training. 2. ARREST/DETENTION/Kidnapping of local staff:Likelihood: moderately likely. 2/5Impact: moderate.Mitigation: Follow code of conduct. Hire local contractors. Build an acceptance with host community. Interventions from CMT/SLT(Field/Higher level). Maintaining transparent and clear communication between staff and community. Staff care initiatives to the detained staff members and their families(during and after release packages). Coordinated efforts for the release of the staff members through other INGOs. Periodic risk assessments/reviews, to proactively understanding the trend. Updated contingency plans to minimize impact due to the detention of key staff.3. CASH ROBBERY: Likelihood: moderately likely. 2/5Impact: moderate.Mitigation: Transfer risk to local companies (HAWALA company). Using banks system to pay for the vendors and partners. Minimize amount of money in the field. Gaining the acceptance of the community.
