Needs

In Somalia, the majority of IDPs are living in a ‘protracted’ displacement situation since more than 20 years and there is no sign that they will return soon to their places of origin. Returns to the area of origin are extremely limited as many displaced families have lost their livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services, which are concentrated in urban areas. Prospects for return are also limited, due to the security situation and access constraints. Most IDPs are settled in informal/ unplanned settlements in deprived conditions and where newly displaced people join those whose displacement has become protracted. The camp-like settings are often a distance away from areas of commerce, health and education, creating a protection risk in accessing such basic services. The distance and lack of road infrastructure leaves the elderly and persons with disabilities particularly disadvantaged. In addition, IDPs often do not own the land that they dwell on or have permanent shelters, leaving them vulnerable to unlawful evictions and abuse of power by landlords. Most IDP sites are governed by ‘gatekeepers’ that often act as informal managers. Trends indicate that forced evictions are relentless; so many are displaced multiple times. The needs of the IDPs are huge and unmet, while new displacements to urban areas as a coping mechanism or in order to access humanitarian assistance remains high.People displaced to informal sites are not having their basic needs met either due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support due to poor targeting of sites. There is need to improve camp coordination and management, and to support/create internal governance structures through community mobilization. IDPs sites require properly trained staff for improving responses and activities in camps, and who can provide information on available durable solution initiatives.

Activities

As the global and country level CCCM Cluster lead agency, UNHCR provides assistance to the existing capacity of the local actors and humanitarian service providers in IDP settlements in case of emergencies or essential needs. UNHCR, through current and active partnerships will enhance the outreaching and protection competences of regional and local government agencies (NCRI, MORDA, JRIA, MoI IAD, MRR&R), and other stakeholders, providing capacity building activities. UNHCR will advocate for public land with government authorities where the returnees and IDPs are willing to locally integrate. UNHCR will advocate for the placement of permanent land/shelters in locations where integration with host communities and access to basic services will be improved. Inclusion of IDPs in the planning process will be crucial for planning peaceful coexistence activities and achieving implementation that enhances integration. The following activities will be implemented throughout Somalia:• CCCM coordination mechanisms are established/reinforced at appropriate levels•Site level information is available and shared with stakeholders •Management of sites and settlements is improved•Improve community participation, living conditions and safe access to services and assistance in selected sites •Involvement and participation of affected community is ensured/ increased •Affected populations living in sites and settlements have information they need to access services •Communication/ information centers (in coordination with local authorities and protection actors)•Sites are safe and hygiene conditions are ensured •Support the protection and restoration of livelihoods to strengthen resilience of communitiesUNHCR will support the Cluster with Detailed site assessments, assessing 3 key areas in the country, tentatively in Banadir, Bari/Bosasso and Sool/Sannag.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The risk of exclusion is significant in IDP settlements. There is a low level of understanding and acceptance of the rights of refugees and IDPs both within the host community and with authorities, as well as prevailing misconceptions of respective roles and responsibilities. Likewise, discrimination against minority clan members is another protection challenge, with IDPs considered to be foreigners and a burden on local resources. There is lack of mutual trust, respect and recognition for the need of social cohesion, inclusion and integration between IDP and host communities. Most IDP settlements are manned by gatekeepers that often act as informal managers. In the absence of the government authorities providing IDPs with assistance and protection, informal managers have often become the primary go to person and provider of support and, to a certain extent, protection. This dependency can lead to exclusion from services that are provided. UNHCR’s CCCM activities focus, among others, on creating coordination structures and mechanisms that help the beneficiaries’ access different services in their settlements. Communication channels will be enhanced to foster information sharing in the camps with other service providers in the targeted areas.UNHCR has a strong protection approach in its CCCM activities, also thanks to the partners in the field that are engaged in CCCM activities. PRMN partners have the added value of a “protection view” that may urge specific responses to stop/mitigate protection risks detected. To ensure that IDPs are aware of their housing, land and property (HLP) rights, UNHCR will undertake awareness raising on relevant HLP laws and advocacy with government for non-discriminatory application of HLP laws in coordination with partners/clusters. Inclusion of IDPs in the planning process will be crucial for planning peaceful coexistence activities and achieving implementation that enhances integration.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Most IDP settlement lack education facilities, secure and hygienic pit latrines, solar lighting and safe access to markets and sources of energy, such as firewood. This leaves many women and girls at risk of SGBV and other abuses and at risk of being excluded from education and services. Children not accessing schools within safe distances lowers their levels of safety and often results in youth engaging in at risk activities. In its CCCM activities UNHCR aims to address these risks by supporting community based protection, effective camp management and assessing the resources of the community and stakeholders to respond to the needs of IDPs.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Displaced women, men, girls and boys in Somalia remain victims of various protection incidents and other human rights violations. Violence is occurring in places of refuge and in areas which are controlled by non-state actors and which are not accessible by humanitarian actors. Continuous displacement and lack of livelihoods has also reduced the capacities of these uprooted communities to access humanitarian and medical aid. UNHCR will continue to monitor closely all protection incidents occurred within the context of forced displacement in Somalia. By advocating and putting in place response systems, every protection incident should get a referral or direct assistance. Additionally, the information gathered through a thorough monitoring system feeds into the wider system to better analyze main protection incidents in the country and better tailor programming to needs. Influenced by the HCT’s Centrality of Protection strategy, UNHCR will pilot monitoring in areas which are occupied by armed non-state actors. Especially in South West State and Jubbaland State, individuals will be identified with strong connections in areas which are currently not included in humanitarian programming and assessments. Expected is that these Independent Monitors will feed protection related information to UNHCR and the wider humanitarian community on specific protection needs among these communities.
