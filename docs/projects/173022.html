Project Summary

This project is to improve food & livelihood assistance to protracted IDPs, returnees & host community in South Kordofan, West and Central Darfur States through provision of agriculture, livestock, livelihood support in localities of Azum, Um Dukhun, Habila, Baida, Abu Jubeiha & El Leri in Central, West and South Kordofan States respectively where the duration of project is one year.
SORR target of 30,000 people among the total 6.2 million people in need as sector target 51% women, 40% children and 15% people with disability in 6 localities mentioned above which will receive agriculture inputs and extension services as well as veterinary services that includes animal health services (including vaccination and treatment) and training of community animal health workers. Support return communities with agro-enterprise training to raise their marketing skills and diversify livelihood opportunities. Vocational training or business skills small targeting youth and women in order to open market opportunity for them and generate new employment opportunities. Natural Resources Management to maintain environment .SORR will coordinate with community leaders, HAC, line Ministries and other stakeholders for project start up. HAC shall support SORR in the project implementation smoothly and successfully as they will facilitate in signing of Technical Agreement and travel notification. Currently there are limited humanitarian interventions in the targeted localities where SORR through the project developed will not cover the whole people who are in need from the sector targeted .SORR has valid registration and presence in West, Central Darfur and South Kordofan States and well acquainted with the communities base on the previous experiences

1 - Needs

HNO 2020 indicated that food insecurity continued to worsen in 2019 due to continued economic decline and natural shock with half a million people were classified as IPC 3 food insecurity or worsen. In 2020, it is estimated that 2.6 million individuals - 14.2 % of the total population will need urgent and timely life-saving food security & livelihood support. Based on several recent food security assessments, the main needs of the most vulnerable communities - including IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities - include physical and financial access to adequate and sufficient nutritious foods through markets or their own food production; and the ability to produce sufficient good-quality agricultural products while ensuring their availability and affordability on the market. Despite a reasonably strong 2018 croppingseason, market availability of staples remained low. Around 77 per cent of household from resident communities, and 90 per cent of IDP and refugee households, still spend more than 65 per cent of theirincome on food. Prices of major food items on the market continue to increase, leading most vulnerable households to resort to crisis coping strategies. The economic crisis, conflict and natural shocks have had a negative impact on the agricultural season and overall food security. Based on the IPC Acute Food Insecurity analysis for June-August 2019, it is estimated that 5.8 million people are experiencing crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and need timely lifesaving food and livelihoods assistance. Out of these, around 1 million people are facing emergency levels of food insecurity (IPCPhase 4), and 4.8 million are in crisis (IPC Phase 3), characterized by highly inadequate levels of food consumption, extreme levels of asset depletion, critical nutritional status, highly inadequate access andavailability of food and adoption of irreversible coping strategies. An additional 11.8 million people are estimated to be in stress phase (IPC Phase 2). In West Darfur State, based on the January-March 2019 IPCprojection, around 1.2 million people are suffering from some levels of food insecurity (77 per cent of the population), of which 334,000 (21 percent) are facing emergency and crisis levels of acute food insecurity(21 per cent). The highest severity of acute food insecurity is recorded in the 56 localities classified as IPC phase 3 and above in the States of Al Gazera, Blue Nile, White Nile, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, Kassala, Gedaref, Red Sea and the five Darfur states. This project will be targeting 30,000 people (6000 HH) in South Kordofan (Abu Jubeiha 1000 HH and El Leri 1000 HH), Central Darfur (Azum 1000 HH and Um Dukhun 1000 HH) and West Darfur (Habila 1000 HH and Baida 1000 HH)This project will have a positive impact that have mitigation measures to reduce negative impacts and improve benefits. This includes 70% of women will be targeted to improve fuel that includes FES, bio fuel and bio gas using agricultural and livestock waste that reduces forest destruction and expose to risk of firewood collection and give chance for forest regeneration reduces cutting of trees for meal preparation and use of solar power for water pump by small scale winter season vegetable production which is environmentally friendly. SORR will involve the community leaders from the start of the project starting from need assessment, design, and implementation and monitoring of the project. SORR together with community leaders shall establish a sustainable channel of communication on monitoring and taking decisions on better practices of the project implementation. Further SORR will create suggestion box or ledger book to file all complaints whether abuse or project is taking different path. SORR field and head office will have hotline cell phone designated for complaints to person who will be focal person.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1.1 Provision of agricultural inputs (seeds, tools and services) to 3000 HH1.2 Provision of livestock inputs (vaccinations, drugs and vet services) to 90000 heads of animals (cattle, sheep & goats) covering 3000 pastoralists households1.3 Restocking of 300 female headed households with 900 goats1.4 Refresher training for 30 community animal health workers (CAHWs) support with kits1.5 Vocational training of 450 people (60% youth and 40% women)1.6 Conduct COVID 19 impact assessments on food security and livelihood targeting 600 people
