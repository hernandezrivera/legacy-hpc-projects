Project Summary

"Building on the existing experience and substantive research already conducted, NONGOR and MOAS are excited about the possibility of strengthening the current manufacturing base and expanding into another village to;
a) Ensure the required production is met to continue to support those requiring masks
b) Provide additional households with much needed income
The current production hub in the coastal village of Shamlapur contains ten sewing machines, a ‘cutting area’ for the preparation of fabric and a packing area where the completed face masks are sterilized with an electric iron. Given the current requirements for ‘social distancing’, there is limited scope to increase capacity in this production facility. However, to supplement the work of this facility a further 30-40 individuals from an ever-growing home-based workforce.
To increase production as required, second manufacturing hub near to the Unchiprang settlement is proposed. This site has the capacity to accommodate additional manufacturing, storage, preparation and packing facilities as well as the prospect of providing much needed income to households in the local community.
Since the opening of the manufacturing hub is in Shamlapur, policies have been developed around cleaning, safe working, quality control and packaging. Given that these systems are already in place, the site and equipment are already available, this additional facility could be operational within a short time from the date of the agreement.
The replication of a proven system is considered preferable to the creation of a new one. In addition to providing the requested increase in capacity the second facility will also allow for income generation and livelihood support in another community.
The completed hygiene items would be handed over to the FSS for distribution in line with identified needs and donor priorities. Individual machinists would be paid a pre-agreed rate per item produced, typically after two weeks of production a skilled machinist can produce 100-150 masks per day with <1% rejection rate based on quality of work.
In addition to providing livelihood activities in support of the current health emergency, the project is also equipping individuals with skills for possible future income generation. The fourteen sewing machines available allow for the training of individuals within the confines of the project in tailoring and sewing. The budget also includes several individuals to cut and prepare the fabric, sterilize and package the completed hygiene items, instruct and up-skill participants and manage the logistics. "

Objectives

Support the District health response in coordination with the Health Sector – provide support to existing and planned isolation, treatment, and quarantine facilities in Cox’s Bazar District and support cross-sectoral home care packages for vulnerable groups (i.e elderly or patient and family of home care patients). Expand programs to compensate for loss of livelihoods of the most vulnerable Bangladeshis in Cox’s Bazar whose livelihoods will be impacted by the pandemic.

Needs

While movement is limited, distributions and some volunteer programmes requiring minimal social interactions are ongoing - highlighting a need for additional disease prevention initiatives. We are prepared to meet needs through self-reliance initiatives in support of the health response (mask making, washable bags) - thus limiting the risk of disease spread while also providing continued access to self-reliance activities when many beneficiaries have experienced a reduction in household earnings.
