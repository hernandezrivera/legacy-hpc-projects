Project Summary

The intervention will respond to the shortage of household water supply in 6 communities in the Gaza strip through: 1) equipping three municipal wells with Photovoltaics (PV) solar energy units to improve pumping water in Al Moghraqa and Wadi Gaza in Gaza and Heker Aljam’ neighborhood in Dier Al Balah, and 2) improve water storage capacity for poor households in Bani sohailah, Al qarara / Khanyounes and Al Bayuk / Rafah.

Needs

The WASH situation in Gaza continues to deteriorate. The severe electricity crisis, the Israeli blockade and the three recent military offensives have put the entire population of Gaza at life-threatening health risks. The rapid assessment conducted by the WASH cluster in August 2017 has already found that the energy crisis has reduced the available water supply from 84 liters per capita per day to only about 54 liters per capita per day. The piped water supply has reduced to only four to six hours per day every three to five days. The poor families in marginalized areas are forced to buy drinking water due to the lack of potable water supply, thus the financial burden on the already poor household budgets is increasing. A socio-economic assessment conducted by SCI in January 2018 in the eastern areas of Khanunis near ARAs has shown that 80% of the households rely on temporary jobs with an average income of only NIS 550 per month. Save the Children (SC) has conducted a focus group discussion in April 2018 with its local partner Bayader in eastern Khanuynis on the impact of the domestic water shortage. The attendants stated that 15% of households rely on vendors to cover the deficit of water pipes. Other households depend on cheaper resources like agricultural wells or reduce their water consumption. Poor families are the most vulnerable and affected by the insufficient household water storage capacity. According to the assessment results, the shortage of municipal water and insufficient water storage capacity, alongside the low level of household income to meet basic hygiene needs, negatively affect households`, in particular poor households` health and hygiene situation and increase their health and hygiene risks. Save the Children has also collected further data in September and October 2018 in the six communities targeted by this project: Dier Al Balah, Al Moghraqa and Joher Aldeek in Gaza governorate; Bani suhailah and Al qararah in Khanyounis governorate and Al Bayuk in Rafah Governorate. Field visits, stakeholder meetings and focus groups were conducted with women and men, complementing Save the Children`s previous operational experience in these localities. The assessment has revealed that the water supply through the water wells and piped network reaches most of the homes for just 4 to 8 hours every second day. The municipalities operate water wells only 3 hours on the power grid and 3 to 4 hours on generators. The current pumping capacity is unsustainable and is likely to decrease as the municipalities rely on external funding to secure the fuel needed to run the generators. The focus group with 17 female participants in Dier Albalah has found that households are only able to fill the roof tanks from the municipal water twice per week (when municipal water and grid electricity are available at the same time). According to the field visits and stakeholder meetings, the families cope with the shortage of domestic water through two mechanisms. On the one hand, they cover the water deficit by purchasing from water vendors, increasing their financial burden. Some secure water from agricultural wells carrying it in containers which tends to be cheaper. Children often help with carrying water covering long distances – when water availability falls on the school hours, this disrupts their attendance of school. On the other hand, they reduce their consumption of water for cleaning and washing, which in turn increases health and personal hygiene risks, particular for women and children.

Activities

The project will directly support the WASH cluster strategic objectives 1 and 2 through a combination of Responsive and Remedial activities. It will target six vulnerable communities in Dier Al Balah, Al Moghraqa and Joher Aldeek in Gaza governorate; Bani suhailah and Al qararah in Khanyounis governorate and Al Bayuk in Rafah Governorate with a total population of over 167,600 people. The interventions will be carried out in partnership with two implementing partners: CMWU will implement the solar panels in the target WASH facilities and Bayader Society will be responsible to supply and install HH water tanks, conduct the hygiene awareness activities, distribute hygiene kits and conduct preliminary and final survey. Responsive activities will include provision of increased access to safe water to targeted children and their families. SC in partnership with CMWU will improve pumping capacity in three municipal water wells in Dier Al Balah (well G32), Al Kawthar (well F264)/Almoghraqa and Al Rayies well and Wadi Gaza desalination plant (No 2) Wadi Gaza to improve water supply for 18,000 individuals. This will include installation of three on-grid solar energy units for the wells with a total capacity of 160 Kwh. A preliminary survey will assess the water supply and hygiene situation in the three areas. Following that, SC and CMWU will develop the technical design for the three solar energy units, select the qualified contractor through a public bid process. SC with CMWU will supervise the implementation. Upon completion, SC will conduct a final survey to measure the intervention`s impact. SC and Bayader will also improve water storage capacity for 1,000 poor households in Al Bayuk, Bani Sohailah and Al Qararah. The households in need will be identified based on a technical and socio-economic assessment. Bayader will select a qualified contractor to supply and install 1,000 polyethylene water tanks with a capacity of 1,500 liters. Bayader will conduct remedial activities to improve hygiene knowledge and practices in the six target localities through delivering health and hygiene messaging to 6,000 people including 3,000 children, and 3000 adults, out of whom 2,000 women and 1,000 men. SCI and Bayader will conduct a pre-intervention survey to identify health and hygiene needs and analyse the intervention plan with community-based organizations and `community active persons`. Twelve focus group discussions will be conducted with women, men and children in an age- and gender-sensitive manner to ensure that health and hygiene awareness messaging is gender-sensitive in its methodology. The methodology will be shared with the cluster. Health and hygiene promotion will be delivered in most vulnerable areas to build community members` knowledge and awareness and improve best practices. Informed by the results of the socio-economic assessment, SC and Bayader will also procure and distribute hygiene kits to 1500 poor households based on poverty assessment results. In integration with other SC programming, the local partner in coordination with SC will establish Community Protection Committees (CPC) composed of 50% female members. The CPCs will facilitate coordination with the community, the development of a DRR plan, female participation, and identification and response to community needs. To ensure full integration with other WASH actors, SCI will closely coordinate with the WASH cluster and area focal points as well as the Solar Energy Coordination Task Force in the Gaza strip.

Monitoring and evaluation will include weekly updates on the number of direct and indirect beneficiaries and achievements, with constant field visits and reports. SC has minimum standards and processes to be adhered to by all team members throughout implementation. Short monitoring checklists with close-ended answers will be used during regular field monitoring visits to periodically monitor the quality of activities and identify and address any gaps or issues. Debrief sessions at the end of the visits with relevant programme staff and an action plan (if required) will help make the visits fully effective. Unannounced spot checks may also be held at selected sites or selected issues as needed. Information obtained from all M&E activities will be used by management for informed decision-making. Data and information are always quality controlled through the SC MEAL team`s periodic checks of accuracy, consistency and completeness in both the source document and the database as well as during site visits. This exercise also applies to quality control of partners` data. Once a year, SC carries out a routine data quality assessment on a sample of sites using a Data Quality Checklist to verify data, find gaps if any and fix errors improving the system and datasets. The SC MEAL team will also support the partners on M&E to further their capacities and sense of ownership. Sensitive data and personal information is handled by both SC and partners in a safe and confidential manner with restricted permission to relevant staff only. SC’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners. Partners will submit weekly progress reports. The Project Officer will conduct field visits and regular meetings with partners and CPCs in order to ensure coordination and quality of work. In addition, SCI will keep close communication with other organizations working in the same area and provide updates on project activities to the WASH cluster. This will promote cooperation, collaboration, and stronger impact in programs to the targeted population. SC has established an accountability mechanism including complaints and suggestion boxes placed at CBOs’ offices as well as a Hotline. SC staff and partners will make sure that targeted communities are informed on the types of feedback mechanisms available through publicizing materials and information sharing sessions. Feedback collection is confidential and findings are recorded and followed-up on systematically by the SC MEAL team. Finally, Save the Children will closely monitor the political and security situation in the targeted project areas, in close coordination with other international organizations present to reduce risks to staff and beneficiaries and mitigate them accordingly.
