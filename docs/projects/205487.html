Project Summary

The outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023 led to an inflow of people fleeing the country. This crisis has affected South Sudan significantly; more than 259,451 individuals have crossed over to South Sudan, including 34,089 children (18,112 girls and 15,977 boys), of those individuals fleeing Sudan, 90% are South Sudanese returnees. Of those fleeing, 49% are women and girls, 46% are below the age of 18 and 4% over the age of 60. Displaced individuals that have crossed the border entry points along the Sudan-South Sudan border have impacted South Sudan's economic stability, particularly in the northern states that heavily rely on imports from Sudan. Particularly Upper Nile and Unity States. The resulting increase in food prices has exacerbated food insecurity and negative coping mechanisms. It is anticipated that over the next six months, 240,000 more returnees will cross from Sudan to South Sudan (UNHCR Aug 2023). The primary motivation of Children and people Fleeing Sudan to South Sudan is to seek protection. As of now, over 34,089 children who have fled have experienced and continue to endure extremely adverse childhood events marked by danger, insecurity, and conflict. These children face an uncertain future regarding their housing, education, safety, and friendships. Many are struggling to cope with events for which they need the support of caring and loving adults to safely absorb. Even children who have fled with their families are living with parents and relatives who have undergone excessive deprivations, threats, and violence. This situation has an immediate and profound impact on the mental health of parents, caregivers and children; rendering them immensely vulnerable to poverty, exploitation, and abuse. The influx from Sudan has further strained host communities that are already grappling with multiple deprivations, putting additional pressure on the existing weak and fragile health system in these bordering states. Currently, 1.4 million children are moderately or severely wasted, and close to a million acutely malnourished, pregnant and lactating women require care and treatment. Micronutrient deficiencies are widespread, with over 40% of pregnant and lactating women suffering from iron deficiency anaemia. The South Sudan Nutrition Cluster estimates that malnutrition in the affected communities is likely to increase by 30% due to inadequate access to quality health and hygiene services and reduced food consumption (both in quantity and quality) owing to increased food prices. Interviews with the affected families and children reveal experiences of insecurity, harassment, and violence during their journey to South Sudan. There are concerns about gender-based violence (GBV), ongoing insecurity, and uncertainty about the future. Currently, there is insufficient support for GBV and child protection in border regions and transit canters to meet the individual needs of highly affected children and the broader needs of affected children within the community. Children and families who have fled violence are accessing humanitarian services, but there is a gap in addressing their growing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs.
Moreover, children at risk of violence, deprivation, and abuse are not being adequately identified for referral services and support by case workers. HACO south Sudan Child Protection Team intends to Respond through Child protection interventions, with a primary focus on addressing GBV, as a high-priority intervention. HACO will also scale up its GBV response, prevention, and risk mitigation programming to address the new and emerging needs arising from the Sudan crisis. Given the current situation in Unity, Jonglei and Upper Nile states. It is essential to provide basic needs, create a protective environment for displaced communities, and support the mental health of displaced populations, particularly those who lack guardians and children with disabilities.

2. Project activities

Returnee children, including adolescents, and their caregivers will be given access to better quality protection services: Unaccompanied and separated girls and boys will be provided with alternative care or reunified, adolescents and their parents and caregivers will be provided with community-based mental health and psychosocial support services, grave child rights violations will be documented and referred for services. Gender Based Violence will be addressed by women, girls and boys being provided with risk mitigation, prevention and response interventions to address gender-based violence through HACO’s programmes. The Displaced and Returnees will be engaged through community platforms in reflective dialogue towards eliminating discriminatory social and gender norms and harmful traditional practices that affect girls and women. Other activities will involve; Case management services – using qualified social work professionals, Integrated services through provision of GBV and CP services at schools, nutrition sites, health sites, food security and livelihood sites. Mobile CP services will be provided to ensure strong outreach and strong referral pathways to reach the most vulnerable. MHPSS programming will be grounded on sports, recreation, peer support, counselling by social work professionals. Youth and adolescent programming activities will be employed to ensure out of school children and street children are supported on case plans to find alternative livelihood opportunities, out of school education services through referral, return to school, peer based MHPSS and recreational services. Sports for development approach will be used to have girls and boys engage in STRUCTURED activities with coaches who are able to work with children to build confidence, improve peer to peer relationships, solve conflict, regulate anger and have fun with children from different ethnic and cultural groups and Women and child friendly spaces (WCFs) will be built and supported to reduce child marriage. The WCFs will have structured activities aimed at protecting girls from early marriage, supporting girls and women with peer support and referral services and supporting women to identify livelihood opportunities. The Empowerment of women and girls through social norms change – awareness raising activities will be adopted to build a protective environment for Women and girls; HACO will through program staff seek to change norms at a community level and confront harmful practices including child marriage, violence against women and children, gun ownership and use.
