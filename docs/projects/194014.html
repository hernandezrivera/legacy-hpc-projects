1 - Needs

This project addresses the issues of protecting children from the dangers of violations in Sudan after conflict between SRF and RSF IN Khartoum , in a residential environment surrounded by displacement camps, host communities and adjacent villages, where illiteracy and poverty prevail, and this is closely related to children facing a set of challenges related to protection from violations, child labor Exploitation, violence. In such an environment, children are deprived of the necessary knowledge and life skills that they desperately need in order to protect and protect themselves from all forms of violations surrounding them. Children also need to build self-capacity, enhance confidence, and protect themselves from violence in order to survive in the future. To respond to the above-mentioned situation, it is necessary to enhance the security and protection of children as a result of their exposure to those risks that affect their health and psychological condition, with an emphasis on not being repeatedly exposed to them. Child protection requires concerted efforts between institutions working in protecting children and working with local communities that need education for a life free of child abuse. The consequences of these violations on the individual, family and society are very serious due to their recurrence and development from time to time, and thus require concerted efforts by all to reduce them. The process of protecting children from violations requires raising children’s awareness, educating the local community, advocating for the authorities responsible for protection, providing psychological and social support, and enhancing participation through empowering and building children’s capacities and providing them with the necessary life skills to maintain security, safety and life. • Recently erupted conflict in Khartoum on 15th April, has largely affected humanitarian activities as large-scale looting, attacks against humanitarian premises, killing and injuring of humanitarian staff were reported. Further the conflict has changed the humanitarian landscape- severity scale of localities were dramatically changed as new hotspots were emerged; thousands of people displaced to the neighboring states; new transit places were established while others camped near the border with the neighboring countries to get asylum.• This is the protection crisis, among the killed and injured, many are children; indiscriminate attacks on civilian populated areas and infrastructure including schools and hospitals; High risk of Children on the street of being associated with organized criminal gangs engaging in looting the market and NGOs premises and also exposed to recruitment and use by parties to the conflict; large scale family separation while on the move with many unaccompanied and separated children were reported, ,children especially adolescent girls are at the risk of sexual and gender based violence among the other protection threats. Unexploded ordinance pose a grave danger to children as many children were reported either killed or maimed due to mines in the on-going conflict • Humanitarian infrastructure (offices, warehouses, vehicles) were either looted or damaged making the capacity to respond to the current crisis make more difficult and expensive. Several local and International NGOs reported that their inability to pay the salary of the local staff due to the collapsing of bank system. Already prevailing economic crisis, scarcity of foods and groceries due to looting of shops and supermarkets, power-cuts and no or limited water supply make the situation worse in the hotspots where active fighting is still going on.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be at the center of the response, and all assistance will be planned and implemented so as to promote the safety, dignity, and rights of affected people. The Child-friendly space and the women-friendly spaces will be guided by the safety audit. In addition, the community will be consulted in the establishment of the center and later provide feedback on the ways the centers are safe for the community. ARCO will consult the Protection Cluster within in the specific area of intervention to have access to the Protection Analysis which identifies threats, vulnerable groups, and existing capacities. In addition, training will be provided to the child protection, women support committees and ARCO protection staff on protection mainstreaming. To create a proper understanding of identified threats, the most vulnerable groups, and the existing capacities of the affected populations as well as services and resources already available. This will help us to identify real and potential risks that may arise in protection programs. Will confirm the threats, vulnerabilities, and negative coping strategies identified in the Protection Analysis Report by consulting with the affected population of each area of intervention. In addition, through analysis, ARCO identifies with beneficiary’s preventive and mitigation measures with the objective of reducing the threats and the vulnerabilities and increasing the existing capacities. In addition, the community through the community-based protection structures will have full participation in the designing and implementation of the program. Furthermore, the community will have full participation including a decision-making role in the program. AGD approach will be used to ensure the inclusiveness of the program to all of the community in the area. Active and effective participation of affected people both in planning, response, and monitoring processes will be ensured through community discussion. ARCO will promote peace by ensuring that all community groups including Refugees ,IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups and hiring diverse staff. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area.
