Project Summary

Syria remains one of the largest and most complex humanitarian emergencies globally with 13.9 million people in need (PiN) of assistance. According the 2023 the annual Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for the Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) Sector estimated that around 12 million people were facing acute food insecurity and in need of various forms of food, agriculture and livelihood-related assistance in Syria. The worsening economic crisis, agriculture production losses, erosion of purchasing power due to the weakened local currency, insecurity in some locations, coupled with the Russian-Ukrainian conflict, the disruption of food commodity supply chains and production inputs, as well as the large deficit of energy supplies on food systems and agriculture, has seen the number of food insecure people remaining alarming high.
With life-saving emergency agriculture assistance and the recovery of agriculture-based livelihoods remaining very critical, effective coordination through close cooperation with all partners is fundamental in ensuring that food production and agriculture-based livelihoods assistance reaches those in need in a coordinated and timely manner.
The objective of the proposed action is to strengthen the effectiveness and quality of the food security responses (including all hubs) based on evidence, capacity building and strong coordination within the Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) Sector and across sectors.
Project outputs include:
• Output 1: Accessibility of food security and agriculture information for stakeholders response planning improved.
• Output 2: Capacity for national and subnational Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) sector strengthened.
• Output 3: Capacity for subnational coordination of Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) sector built.
This project is expected to contribute to improved planning (evidence-based decision–making) and supporting timely, efficient and effective responses to the needs of affected populations. Furthermore, the project on sector coordination will ensure that duplication is avoided to ensure efficient use of limited available resources. Overall, the proposed project to support the sector will continue to provide an opportunity for FAO to help assure appropriate emergency response in agriculture and promote early recovery in the crop, livestock and irrigation sectors, through mobilizing resources for both the sector as a whole.

3 - Needs

Overall, FSA Sector estimates the PIN 2023 at 15 million in Syria. Due to the complex challenges facing the country and the sector, regular and extensive evaluation for appropriateness and timeliness of the FSS responses, quantitative and qualitative analysis for better understand the prevailing dynamics of the situation, remain extremely critical to comprehensively address risks to the sector in a coordinated manner. It is therefore important to have a robust coordination mechanism capable of facilitating timely and effective response. Identifying and reaching affected populations, avoiding duplication, minimizing delays and providing appropriate support is key not only for an effective humanitarian response, but for the expansion of recovery and resilience actions. FSAS will continue to play a critical role in 202/3, through ensuring a well-coordinated approach, centered on the commitment of partners for coordinated responses through the IASC sector/cluster approach. Moreover, seeks to increase the efficiency and effectiveness of response through: (1)developing a principled, predictable and systematic operational planning process; (2) ensuring greater coherence across the different operational modalities through improved coordination; and (3)strengthening information sharing and monitoring of response. Moreover, the FSA Sector is providing technical guidance to its partners.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Aid workers being targeted due of being aid worker or coming from particular ethnicity/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries’ selection. Mitigation: Inclusion of different categories of affected population based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria. 2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation: Establishing a local selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance; and ensuring effective communication for guidance. 3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation: Establish a community-based selection and monitoring. The project taken into account protection elements, in line with do-no-harm principles, including GBV and PSEA risk mitigation measures. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of Aid workers being they are assigned to this job. Women and youth will be prioritized. The implementation of field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by the Protection Cluster. An accountability to affected population system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place to provide communities with pathways to express their concerns and grievance. This will be used to make adjustments and improvement on the implementation of activities.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans. 2.Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover additional requirements. 3.Risks: Increased control of land and water by party in conflict. Probability: Likely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation: Preventive: continue to gather information from a range of sources; accurate choice of target areas, ensuring that Cluster activities are possible and beneficiaries will undertake the activity; advocacy with local authorities by service providers. If necessary, move to alternative locations previously identified. 4.Risks: Possible climatic shocks. Probability: Possible. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation: Select distribution areas in different agro ecological zones and in irrigated areas to reduce risks and distribute adapted varieties which possess specific desirable traits, including drought tolerance.
