Project Summary

Due to the dire impact of losing WASH services and supplies on human life, BO has put providing water and sanitation facilities among the top priorities of its interventions in crisis. BO designed its 2020 WASH intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs (MSNA) of affected-population in NW Syrian, need severity for WASH assistance, and HNO2020 key messages, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and WASH Cluster. The project aims to restore functionality or keep water infrastructure at the minimum level of operation; and to carry out life-saving/ emergency WASH interventions including, provision of water and sanitation facilities and services, and distribution of NFI kits, as well as improvement of hygienic behaviour and practices of most vulnerable people. The project is consisted of three main components targeting ~100,000 INDV (48250M/ 51750F)
*Repairing and rehabilitation of water systems to enable greater quality control, improve access to safe water, and minimize financial burden on already impoverished families targeting 20000 INDV (9650M/10350F)
Communities in NW Syria are overburden due to high IDP numbers resulting from continues displacement in addition to limited functionality and efficiency of water infrastructure and services. Therefore, BO will rehabilitate 10 drink water wells, including repairing water pipelines and pumps, and replacing/ maintaining broken parts. Also, BO will provide operation support for 90 days to supply water to the most vulnerable people, in meanwhile, will develop a tariff-based system management in coordination with relevant stakeholders (mainly the Local Council in the project area)
*Distribution of essential WASH NFIs and conducting Hygiene promotion activities to improve hygienic behaviour and practices targeting 54000 INDV (26055M/ 27945F), of total, 65% are children under 18 years old and 25% are girls and women in reproductive age.
In crisis, people tend to follow survival instinct which means prioritize avoiding tangible risks with less care to intangible risk such as dangerous impact of incorrect hygiene practices. BO will conduct 6 Hygiene promotion campaigns along with distribution of the 9000 NFIs to improve and encourage beneficiaries to strengthen already well-established hygienic behaviour, knowledge, attitude and practices among the most vulnerable people. The hygiene kits will be customized to address needed items for all gender and age groups (including tooth paste and toothbrush for children, and sanitary pads, feminine disinfectant ...etc)
*Improving access to lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services to reach SPHERE standards, both in terms of the quality of services and gender/protection considerations
With no credible solution looms in the horizon for the conflict in Syria, BO have set up an emergency plan for lifesaving intervention to provide water and sanitation facilities to potentially new displaced people through provision:
-One elevated cement water tank with capacity of 150 cubic meters to improve access to clean and safe water for 20,000 INDV (9650M/ 10350F) near overcrowded and HTR IDPs settlements
-30 small water tanks (2 cubic meters), and 30 latrine blocks (toilets and baths), as well as, water trafficking for 6,000 INDV (1920B, 1980G, 1125W, 975M) for 90 days
Throughout the project, BO will select the target communities based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with WASH Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria. Principles of Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected People are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation

4 - Needs

HNO 2020 and WASH assessments indicate that there are an estimated 4.8M people who require WASH assistance in Aleppo and Idleb GOV, 1.4M of whom are considered in acute need (people at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement). High levels of displacement combined with increase in the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites has contributed to a deterioration of already overstretched and poorly maintained WASH facilities and servicesIn N Idleb and N Aleppo areas high dependency on alternative and often unsafe water sources prevails (42% of the population). This significantly increases the risk of waterborne diseases in these IDP sites. Many communities that are not served through water networks face serious issues regarding water affordability which contributes to poverty and impacts needs in other sectors. Families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 10% of their income on water (could be 20% or 50% in informal settlements). Likewise, continuous drilling of wells in search of water has negatively affected groundwater resources, with a significant depletion of water aquifers observed especially around IDP sites near the Turkish border. Moreover, at least 80% of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional which poses significant public health risks and have harmful cascade effects on communities, especially in overcrowded sitesAmongst PiN, some groups, such as children under 2 years (6,13%) and women in reproductive age (57,9%) as well as PwD, are in need for WASH NFIs, including diapers, sanitary napkins and other hygiene items. Also, IDP and returnee female-headed households have more difficulties to afford most hygiene items in comparison with female-headed households in host communities. The major barrier to access WASH NFIs is the severely reduced purchasing power of families

5 - Implementation of activities

BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct need assessment survey in order to collect up to date data about WASH needs of IDPs, Host Community and Returnees (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e. market assessment or security assessment). Then, BO will select beneficiaries following transparent prosses based on clear vulnerability/ eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable affected-population. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators.
