Project Summary

The project will focus on the protection of women and girls affected by the conflict through the enhanced capacity of protection actors, the establishment of protection services and a wider educational programme targeting communities in the South west and North west regions of Cameroon. The main objective is to improve protection of women and girls affected by the conflict through the enhanced capacity of protection actors, the establishment of protection services and a wider educational programme targeting communities in the South west and North west regions of Cameroon. This main objective will be achieved through three outputs: Enhanced national actors' capacity (police and gendarmerie) for the protection of women and girls affected by the crisis (i) Women and girls affected by the conflict access protection services through gender desks at the police and gendarmerie in Buea and Bamenda (ii) Increased economic support to GBV survivors, public awareness and social mobilization against Sexual gender based violence (iii). The beneficiaries of the program are 22.080 IDPs ( women and girls; men and boys) living within host communities, 5.520 Women in host communities, Women’s groups organized in associations, networks of associations, cooperatives, Women and girl’s survivors of gender-based violence in host communities including IDPs, Families with children

Needs

Following the multisectoral evaluation of OCHA / UNDSS in February 2018, the common GBV assessment conducted by UNFPA and UN Women (April 2018) coupled to informations collected by the situation assessment of MSF, Afrique Solidarité Suisse ASOL, Respect Cameroun (june-august 2018), critical needs and priorities are: Prevention of violence against civilian populations and access to response mechanisms: The multi-sectoral assessment of March 2018 highlighted a situation of large population displacements due to insecurity and violence in the villages of origin. Many displaced persons lost their identity and civil status documents during the displacement. Most of the displaced populations have settled in the forest, in neighboring villages or in cities, and face increased risks of violence, including sexual and gender-based violence, and the risk of arbitrary arrest due to violence. loss of documents and movement limitations, family separation and psychosocial problems. Displaced children face particular vulnerabilities, of which few are currently in school. This exposes them to risky behaviors, such as early pregnancy, drug use, sexually transmitted diseases and exploitation. Access to shelter and essential household items: The Anglophone crisis led to an almost complete paralysis of the two regions of the English-speaking areas of Cameroon, particularly in the South-West. Armed clashes are frequently noted between independentist elements and troops of the Cameroonian regular army taking in stalls civilian populations fleeing areas of high insecurities. Currently, there are more than 150,000 internally displaced persons who have mostly fled to forest areas (80%), while others have moved to urban areas to escape insecurity. The main needs of these people, who have abandoned everything, are: shelter assistance and essential household and productivity items. Access to remote areas: Humanitarian access has been a significant challenge limiting the ability of organisations to deliver a scaled-up emergency response that is comprehensive and effective. Protection needs of women and girls: are huge but difficult to be documented as people are afraid to talk. People are very concerned about their own security and often traumatized by violence they have experienced. Random killings and harassment are serious threats for IDPs in the bush. Many therefore stay inside the village, rather then going out.  Referral pathways (GBV service providers): the referral and counter referral are very weak and not sufficiently functional. There is a need to reinforce the referral pathways included documentation of GBV cases, GBV case management. GBV Coordination Many GBV actors are dealing with GBV Cases but not sufficiently coordinated and the impact of different interventions are still mitigated (GBV Services are not meet GBV survivors expectations and needs) Capacity building of local actors: local civil society organizations have been at the forefront of the response, providing emergency relief to families forced to flee their homes (towns and villages). These actors are trusted within the communities and are the key link in building trust, negotiating access and delivering the response. Capacity building of these organizations to be able to scale up and support this response and for future emergencies should be a key component of the response. This should include training opportunities in humanitarian standards and approaches,
