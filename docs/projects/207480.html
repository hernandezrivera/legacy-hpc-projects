Project Summary

HI began providing services in Cox’s Bazar before the 2017 crisis & has been adapting ever since. Continuing to address complex, acute & multi-layered needs, HI proposes to foster a safe & protective environment with the provision of specialized & community-based services to Rohingya refugees & host community through a twin-track modality: Multi-sectorial (disciplinary) Mobile Units to address mobility or social barriers. Integrated outreach teams (PSS, physiotherapists, protection officers, sports & recreation officers) deliver home-based services focused on education for beneficiaries & their caregivers on safe, effective identification & safe referral internally & externally for therapeutic exercises & other interventions. Currently, HI has seventeen MU which are teams of trained staff providing direct specialized services (including protection, mental health & psychosocial support, physical/functional rehabilitation, & basic health care/SRH) to vulnerable persons & persons with disabilities in a comprehensive approach.
HI will continue to provide individual life-saving protection assistance by safely identifying, assessment & referring individuals being most at-risk or survivors of violence, including women & girls with disabilities. Within its ongoing activities, HI has established strong referral pathways (using Inter-agency forms as per Sector recommendation) with specialized camp protection actors in each of its activity sites. HI developed awareness messages to address gaps identified on the following topics: Human Trafficking &exploitation, PSEA, Inclusion, Children’s Rights &rights of Persons with Disabilities, Violence (including IPV & GBV), &protection risk faced by persons with disabilities. Gender Equality &General Protection.
The project considers the gender, age & disability dimensions of vulnerability with consideration of the principle of “leaving no one behind”. The criteria are also set in a way where women & children with or without disabilities will be given high priority for the specialized services and assistance during the implementation period. Gender disaggregated data collection will be ensured, reported & monitored. HI also actively participates in the PERU teams, which are fundamentally intended for deployment during disasters or emergencies in camp settings. If circumstances dictate, PERU teams can act as an integral component of emergency response teams assigned by other authorities. PSEAH focal in HI is responsible for extensive training of all staff & volunteers using PSEA Network materials & recommendations to ensure an adequate level of practice within the organization. An inclusive Feedback &response Mechanism is in place where feedback is regularly elicited through suggestion boxes, a dedicated hotline for host & refugee communities, community discussions & 1:1 interviews. HI is currently implementing four projects that address the above-mentioned services. HI focused on training humanitarian workers on the inclusion of persons with disabilities in services provision, to increase accessibility & inclusiveness of the humanitarian response.
HI created & supports community groups (in the form of peer support groups) composed of persons with disabilities, to enforce self-resilience & participation/representation to the community. HI has built the capacity to respond to disaster which has been demonstrated in recent fire & flood response.
HI works in partnership with the other actors present at the camp level, to ensure the best response to the needs reported. HI coordinates with both NGOs &government bodies, depending on the areas of intervention. HI through his Compliance & Liaison department is engaging with all relevant authorities (RRRC, DC, UNO, DSS, CiC, NGO AB) to ensure timely coordination & proper communication. HI is part of GO & NGO coordination groups at the DC & UNO Levels.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya families fled violence. But six years later, uncertainty about the future still grips those living in the world’s largest refugee settlement. Now the Rohingya crisis is a protracted crisis. Over 965,467 Rohingya refugees reside in Cox’s Bazar with 52% female, 52% children, 4% elderly, &12% persons with disabilities. As per the PWG-REACH study, 2021, persons with disabilities &specific needs in both camps &host communities face the risks disproportionally compared to the rest of the population, given the fact that intersectionality of factors (gender, age, disability, ethnicity, religion, &other factors) lead to further exclusion, discrimination, hence increase vulnerabilities. Rohingya households reported high levels of insecurity related to limitations on movement, discrimination, harassment, &physical violence prevailing in camps. We also found in the 2023 Joint-Multi Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) report, that the top 3 biggest protection issues in the area faced by households are Crime &violence (48.0%), People joining criminal groups (24.5%) &Restrictions of movement (14.2%). Violence &security incidents inside the Cox’s Bazar Rohingya refugee camps have risen since the beginning of 2022. This has raised protection concerns for Rohingya refugees, including exposure to general &physical insecurity, &child-related protection concerns (child labor 3%, early marriage 2% according to the MSNA 2023), &polygamy, dowry, gender-based violence. Girls represent a large proportion (57%) &are at risk of child marriage, sexual exploitation, abuse, &neglect (ISCG, 2019). IOM report (2021) identified women, adolescent girls &persons with disabilities &caregivers as the most vulnerable groups. People with disabilities are especially vulnerable &require additional protection considerations. Globally, girls &young women with disabilities are ten times more likely to experience gender-based violence than those without disabilities, &girls with intellectual disabilities are particularly vulnerable to sexual violence.As per` HI Jadimura Assessment Report 2020’, the lack of social inclusion of people with disabilities because of stigma has been identified as a possible reason for the increased risk for young people with disabilities of being trafficked, either for sexual or other forced labor. Community participation plays a large role in protection; an individual’s interaction with their surrounding social system affects mental health & well-being while simultaneously providing a support network that acts as a protection mechanism. People with disabilities & older people tend to face barriers to community participation, however, removing an important element of social protection for an already at-risk demographic. In the Rohingya context, children, adolescents, adult women, &older people with disabilities are at higher risk of being excluded from humanitarian assistance than male people with disabilities of working age. Women & girls, particularly those living with disabilities, & Hijras, are vulnerable to physical, psychological & sexual harassment & abuse.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

HI is implementing sustainable, inclusive, and quality humanitarian response within the Cox’s Bazar district. HI ensures a holistic intervention through synergies with other HI projects, external actors & development actions. HI uses the standard referral pathway guidelines for the Rohingya response designed by the Protection Sector to refer to external service providers, including humanitarian actors. Moreover, HI continues to participate as an active member of the Health, Protection, Education and Logistic Sectors; and CPSS, GBVSS, MHPSS working group, and GDPWG to have regular interactions with all sectors. Being an active member, HI ensures effective coordination and synergies with other interventions and actors in the areas supporting to avoid any duplication of activities in the camps and host areas, as well as promoting inclusive humanitarian actions at the sectoral and ISCG levels. Additionally, HI coordinates with development actors in Bangladesh to focus on creating sustainable changes and mainstreaming actions ensuring the rights and inclusion of persons with disabilities.HI is continuously aiming to make its action sustainable and participates in building capacities of the community and local partners to foster community resilience. HI has effective coordination with government authorities regarding integrated approaches, specialized services, and capacity building for individuals and community members, especially persons with disabilities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The program incorporates protection principles throughout all the phases including the design & inception phases. Starting from the design phase HI has carried out protection needs assessment through community consultations analysis of data from ongoing projects & analysis of specific feedback received through accountability mechanisms. Consultation & dialogue with communities including women & girls with & without disabilities have been ongoing in the previous phases of the program & will continue throughout continued implementation to promote active participation of affected populations.Additionally, Staff and volunteers will also be trained on Protection Mainstreaming Principles including data protection, confidentiality, and “Do no Harm” principles. HI Project Staff are in daily contact with HI beneficiaries through routine activities & more importantly at a home-based level. Those activities allow for collecting feedback about services received or any updates regarding the camp situation.This year proposed project also be implemented jointly with the Centre for Disability in Development (CDD).

3.3 - Age & Disability

HI is mainstreaming an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes for interventions in all sectors. Intersections of disability, age, and gender discrimination have a multiplying effect on the barriers faced by children, especially girls, with disabilities and contribute to increased risk factors for vulnerability to poverty, social exclusion, and violence. HI’s commitment to equality and equity of access for women/girls and men/boys in all areas of our work is outlined in our institutional policies (including “Disability, Gender and Age Policy”) and through the adoption of a gender-sensitive approach throughout the project cycle. Practical actions to promote adherence to our institutional policies and values within the current program will include:• Ensure gender balance within our personnel• Ensuring all personnel is trained on disability, gender, and age inclusion and accountable to HI’s Code of Conduct. This includes staff, partners, and stakeholders including consultants, suppliers, etc.• Collecting and analyzing sex, age, and disability disaggregated data for all types of beneficiaries.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

HI places beneficiary participation, feedback, & community empowerment at the core of its interventions, from design to implementation. Meaningful participation is one of the pillars of HI Disability, Gender & Age Institutional policy, as meaningful involvement of boys, girls, women & and men of different age groups, including people with different disabilities, in the design, implementation, monitoring & evaluation of actions or policies affecting them is key in the activity implementation to ensure the relevance of it, & the acceptance by the Rohingyas community which the services are designed for & delivered to.HI maintains a comprehensive process throughout the project to ensure effective engagement & participation of beneficiaries, as well as accountability towards them: the necessary tools, guidance, & delivery of training modules for the project staffs to engage beneficiaries from identification to case closure are developed.The Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM) create communication loops that enable teams to receive feedback from the project participants & to respond in a timely way to their suggestions & concerns. It’s a two-way communications mechanism designed specifically to gather & respond to feedback from the project participants & other community stakeholders. This mechanism includes complaint boxes (in static point) that is accessible, a hotline, an email address, contact of Country PSEA focal point with phone number, all shared to the community to ensure that the access to feedback & complaints mechanism is accessible & inclusive. The diversity of feedback mechanisms is key to ensuring this accessibility to the affected persons, as well as raising awareness about it among the caregivers.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Recognizing the lack of consideration of persons with disabilities in existing disaster risk reduction (DRR) service planning and implementation, HI will deliver specific outputs to improve DRR stakeholders’ understanding of barriers National to DRR information as well as facilitators for ensuring persons with disabilities are included in all disaster response programs. HI will build the capacity of the partners on guidelines for protection to mainstream disability-inclusive DRR. Technical support will be provided to Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU) and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) working group members. Accessibility works will include the removal of environmental barriers currently present in evacuation routes and safety shelters. Awareness sessions will be delivered to persons with disabilities and their family members to ensure they are empowered with knowledge of local emergency response plans, evacuation routes, and safety shelters. HI will carry out direct accessibility works at the community level to remove environmental barriers that limit participation in social activities and/or access to essential services.HI, and its partners will follow preparedness plans to reduce the impact of any natural hazard; HI will coordinate with other actors and local communities to ensure adaptation of operations in case of a disaster.HI integrates environmental considerations into the procurement process to reduce adverse impacts on health conditions and the environment. Considering that, field and base officers are gradually being covered by solar energy and plastic items are given very little preference in procurement.
