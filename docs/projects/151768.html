Project Summary

This project aims at addressing the Shelter/NFI needs of affected population in Donetska and Luhanska Oblasts of Eastern Ukraine. Following interventions articulated further below in the document strictly apply to the goals defined in the respective monitoring framework 2019-2020, i.e.:
- Contribute to adequate transitional solutions related to shelter and NFI needs meeting minimal international and national shelter standards;
- Address essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through in-kind assistance and contingency.

Needs

According to the latest findings reflected in HNO 2019 among approximately 50 000 buildings affected since the beginning of the crisis only a half of the needs was addressed by humanitarian community mostly at GCA. Based on the ongoing verification of data in the Shelter Cluster’s damage database, the Cluster estimates that as of the beginning of 2018, some 5,300 vulnerable households in GCA alone continue to require humanitarian shelter interventions. Humanitarian actors expect to be able to cover some 50-60 per cent of these referrals, with existing funding, leaving outstanding needs for repair some 2,000-2,500 homes in 2019, whilst maintaining a small capacity to respond to new damages. Since there has not been yet developed an effective restitution and compensation mechanism from the government side it proves unwavering need in support by local and international NGO community. Special emphasis should be devoted to the support of public institutions including educational, healthcare institutions, collective (for IDPs) and territorial centres for provision of social services (for elderly people), which largely demonstrate signs of persistent dilapidation of their assets, hence, demonstrating inability to provide sufficient support to the affected population and pre-empt negative consequences related to the conflict given the adverse socio-economic situation in country. It is noteworthy to mention that based on the previous programme interventions and its findings several instances were reported when elderly people were accommodated and nursed at the territory of local churches (Marinskyi raion) or Art Centres (Stanytsia Luhanska). The noted above clearly underpins how much elderly people are exposed to the negative outcomes of conflict activities and in which extent government support is irrelevant to the needs faced on the ground. Preliminary assessments claim that problem related to the heating systems is still considered as one of the most acute. Given the high frequency of electricity outages both triggered by conflict and instances of intentional power cuts imposed by local service providers (especially in Luhanska Oblast), put people in a situation of total uncertainty and despair leaving them without alternative sources to sustain adequate temperature conditions both in private households and public institutions. Also, it is crucial to mention that settlements located in the vicinity to the Line of Contact or just relatively small with fewer numbers of people usually stay off the radar of humanitarian interventions. Since most of the roads at these locations are unpaved they are significantly dependent on the seasonal peculiarities. As such, in winter the roads can be completely snowed under and respectively wash-out in early spring. If not addressed, it will make people to resort to the negative coping strategies often putting at risk their lives and wellbeing. For instance, inability to accommodate and treat with dignity internally displaced population force them to stay at the place of their origin constantly exposing their lives to the violence. The worst scenarios may include instances involving sexual, gender-based violence, looting and arbitrary occupation of private households especially those located closer to the Line of Contact where military presence is especially prevailing. It is girls and women who are predominantly exposed to such types of violence. According to the latest research (Institute for Demography and Social Studies, NAS of Ukraine) 19.4% of women aged 19-49 suffered from physical violence in Ukraine, while another 7.9% - from sexual violence; moreover, almost 70% of victims do not apply for any kind of assistance owing to general unease about this issue, as well as the stigma and trauma associated with it.The project will target the following population groups: older persons; women and girls; people with socio-economic hardship; children.

Activities & outcomes

Output 1. Rehabilitated public institutions (collective centers, territorial centres for provision of social services):- rehabilitation of 3 collective centers/ territorial centres for provision of social services (roofing, windows replacement, rehabilitation of the heating systems);Output 2. Rehabilitated conflict-affected households:- rehabilitation of 200 private households (roofing and windows replacement)Output 3. Provided solid fuel for conflict affected communities (private households, public institutions: hospitals, schools, kindergartens, collective centers, territorial centres for provision of social services, FAPs etc.):- distribution of coal to 500 private HH;- distribution of coal to 30 public institutions;Output 4. Provided back-up source of electricity:- distribution of generators (5KW and 12KW)
