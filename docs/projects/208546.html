Project Summary

Building on the recently concluded funding from the Nigeria Humanitarian Fund (NHF) under the country based pooled funds of United Nations OCHA that concluded in January 2023, the intervention in Gwoza LGA of Borno State focuses on scaling up the prevention and treatment of malnutrition among vulnerable groups, with a specific emphasis on people living with disabilities. The persistent crisis in Northeast Nigeria, especially in Borno state, has left communities grappling with displacement, poverty, and ongoing violence, exacerbating malnutrition rates among children and women. The newly proposed program seeks to address these challenges by empowering communities and strengthening healthcare systems.
The primary purpose of this intervention is to drive a comprehensive approach to prevent and treat malnutrition in Gwoza LGA using the local available solution -Tom brown. The target population encompasses vulnerable groups, particularly people living with disabilities, who face unique challenges exacerbated by the protracted crisis. Building on lessons learned from the NHF-funded project, the program aims to implement tailored nutritional interventions that are sensitive to the needs of individuals with disabilities. Accessible services will be a key focus, ensuring that all community members, regardless of their abilities, can benefit from the nutritional support offered.
This intervention aligns with the broader goal of empowering communities affected by the crisis. By addressing the disruptions in livelihoods, essential services, and infrastructure caused by conflict, the program aims to enhance the overall well-being of the community. Additionally, disability-sensitive awareness campaigns will be integral to the initiative, promoting inclusivity and understanding within the community. The program recognizes the heightened vulnerability of people with disabilities and seeks to create a supportive environment that enables them to access vital nutritional services effectively. Through these targeted efforts, the intervention aspires to contribute significantly to the mitigation of malnutrition and its associated challenges in Gwoza LGA.
During the just-concluded project in Gwoza LGA, Albarka Health Spring Foundation (AHSF) achieved commendable milestones in the prevention and treatment of malnutrition, with a particular focus on individuals living with disabilities. AHSF successfully implemented targeted nutritional interventions, distributing Tom brown grains to vulnerable groups, resulting in improved nutritional outcomes and a notable reduction in acute malnutrition cases. The foundation played a pivotal role in strengthening the local healthcare system, offering training to healthcare workers, establishing or reinforcing nutrition units in healthcare facilities, and ensuring the availability of essential medical supplies for malnutrition management. Furthermore, AHSF actively engaged and empowered local communities through awareness campaigns on nutrition, hygiene, and health-seeking behaviors, fostering increased community understanding and participation. The project's commitment to inclusivity was evident in the implementation of accessible services and disability-sensitive awareness campaigns, promoting a more inclusive and supportive environment for people with disabilities.
However, despite these achievements, the project identified crucial needs that warrant the continuity of interventions in Gwoza LGA through its Active Screening results.
Additionally, the high vulnerability of people with disabilities underscores the necessity for ongoing efforts to ensure their specific nutritional requirements are effectively addressed. The economic consequences of the economic downturn, coupled with climate-related shocks, have further intensified food insecurity in host communities, emphasizing the crucial role of the project's continuity in mitigating these long-term impacts on the nutritional well-being of the population.

1 - Needs

Recent research and findings underscore the persistent challenges faced by communities in Northeast Nigeria, particularly in Borno State, as a result of the prolonged crisis spanning over a decade. The 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan indicates that the crisis has led to extensive displacement, poverty, and heightened vulnerability to violence, with over 8.4 million people in the BAY states requiring assistance. Borno State remains severely affected, hosting more than 80% of the two million internally displaced people (IDP) in the Northeast. The conflict, characterized by attacks from Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and military counter-operations, has not only disrupted livelihoods but has also strained public infrastructure, particularly in healthcare, education, water and sanitation, affecting women and children, including those with disabilities. The crisis continues to result in widespread food and nutrition insecurity, with internally displaced people (IDPs) being the most severely affected. Forced migration for safety has left them reliant on food assistance to meet nutritional needs until they can return and rebuild their lives. Concurrently, food insecurity in host communities has worsened due to the economic consequences of the downturn and climate-related shocks. Recent research indicates an urgent need to address malnutrition, with a particular focus on preventing child deaths resulting from severe acute malnutrition. Immediate, life-saving interventions are imperative, requiring improved access to and utilization of nutrition and basic health services in primary healthcare facilities. This involves ensuring that all communities, including those with disabilities, can easily access essential healthcare services. Furthermore, the research highlights the need to enhance knowledge and practices related to good nutrition and key household practices within communities. Educating caregivers on locally available nutritious foods and promoting proper utilization is crucial. Additionally, encouraging hygiene and sanitation practices in host communities is identified as essential to prevent the spread of diseases, particularly among vulnerable populations. The comprehensive approach outlined in the program is imperative to address these assessed needs effectively, with a focus on accessibility, tailored interventions, and disability-sensitive awareness campaigns.Gwoza LGA is particularly vulnerable, given its proximity to conflict hotspots and the prevalence of violent attacks from Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and military counter-operations. The impact on livelihoods, public infrastructure, and the overall well-being of the population, including women and children, is severe. The research emphasizes the pressing need to address the heightened malnutrition rates among children and women in Gwoza, accentuated by the disruption of essential services, including healthcare and education. Internally displaced people (IDPs) in Gwoza LGA face significant challenges, having lost their sources of income and homes, rendering them reliant on external assistance for their nutritional needs.Accessible services, tailored nutritional interventions, and disability-sensitive awareness campaigns are identified as essential components to address the distinct needs of this vulnerable group in the LGA. Enhancing knowledge and practices related to good nutrition within the Gwoza community, particularly among caregivers, is crucial for ensuring sustainable improvements in nutritional outcomes.The comprehensive approach outlined in the program for Gwoza is strategically designed to address the assessed needs effectively, considering the unique challenges and vulnerabilities present in this specific location.

2 - Activities

The project adopts a multifaceted approach to address malnutrition in Gwoza LGA, leveraging locally available resources and promoting community ownership. Central to this strategy is the production and distribution of nutrient-rich Tom Brown, incorporating indigenous grains and legumes for sustainability. Simultaneously, the initiative includes educational workshops and counseling sessions for caregivers, emphasizing Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices to enhance overall family nutrition.Ensuring inclusive access to healthcare services, the project establishes accessible facilities and conducts disability-sensitive awareness campaigns to reduce stigma and foster inclusion of people living with disabilities.Hygiene and sanitation promotion forms an integral part of the initiative, encompassing the distribution of hygiene kits and community-led sanitation initiatives to prevent the spread of diseases, especially among vulnerable populations. This comprehensive and community-centric approach positions the project as a model for culturally sensitive and sustainable nutrition interventions in Gwoza LGA.Screening of Children between 6-59 months: Use MUAC tape to screen children for malnutrition. Children identified with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) are referred to Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) or Stability Centres (SC) within the community, this is done through collaboration and coordination with other organisation.Identify children with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) for enrolment in the Tom Brown program for an 8-week treatment period, monitoring changes in MUAC measurements. Tom Brown Program for MAM: Enrol children with Moderate Acute Malnutrition in the Tom Brown program. Implement an 8-week treatment plan, closely monitoring changes in MUAC measurements and adjusting interventions as needed.IYCF (Infant and Young Child Feeding) and Protection Awareness: Establish and support groups (mother to mother and father to father) where awareness and IYCF key messages are shared, targeting mothers, caregivers, and community members. Sensitize them on key Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practical approaches to ensure optimal nutrition for children and GBV protection messages as well as pathways to provide complains. Disability Inclusion: Ensure accessibility of screening and treatment services for people with disabilities. Tailor nutritional interventions to address the unique needs of individuals with disabilities. Conduct disability-sensitive awareness campaigns to promote inclusivity and understanding within the community.Cooking Demonstration: Using locally available nutritious food stuffs in the community to teach caregivers and mothers practical ways to prepare health meals. A way of imparting skills and knowledge to care givers on how they can prepare nutritious meals for their young children to prevent them from developing malnutrition.Capacity Building and Training: Train community nutrition mobilizers, and lead mothers on the identification and management of malnutrition, including disability-inclusive practices. Train mothers and caregivers on family MUAC and ways to monitor nutritional changes in the child. Conduct intensive training for CRM personnel training sessions for mothers and caregivers on IYCF practices.Establishment of CRM Desk: Establish CRM desk where complaints from the community will be received and given feedback, this desk will also ensure referral for cases that require are handled properly. Monitoring and Evaluation: Establish a robust monitoring and evaluation system to track the progress of the project. Regularly assess the impact of interventions on malnutrition rates and adjust strategies accordingly. By implementing these activities, the project aims to systematically address malnutrition among vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities, while promoting community awareness and participation in nutritional improvement practices.
