Project Summary

WFP Syria’s interim Country Strategy Plan (iCSP) 2019-20 envision an increase in livelihood programming to support almost 2 million beneficiaries. The project activities provide an overarching chapeau under which a range of interventions will serve to strengthen livelihoods at household/ group-based and community-level. The project will support food insecure and vulnerable households with two activity pillars via Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) and Food Assistance for Training (FFT).
1. Food Assistance for Assets (FFA)
FFA aims to restore and rebuild livelihoods of vulnerable Syrian households and communities through household-level productive assets, income generation activities and rehabilitation of communal infrastructure to enhance agricultural production.
The project is planning to support the targeted households through two types of interventions a) provision of household, group and community-based productive assets; and b) rehabilitation of communal agrarian infrastructure;
Pillar 1a: Household and Group-Based Productive Assets:
Provision of agricultural inputs and technical assistance to households in rural areas to improve and diversify household food consumption and provide income generating opportunities.
Pillar 1b – Rehabilitation of Communal Agrarian Infrastructure
The project aims to rehabilitate and fix irrigation and other agricultural infrastructure in the rural areas. Its’s critical to encourage smallholder famers to return to cultivation, improve agricultural productivity, and revitalize local markets.
2. Human Capital Development/Vocational Skills Training via FFT
FFT aims to enhance human capital and skills development, with a focus on urban women and youth. Active labour market support activities will provide participants with technical and vocational skills training (VST) based on local labour market needs and post-training support to enter the labour market or establish small businesses.
3. Enhancement of food systems,
strengthening local economic recovery and basic service provision, with the aim of stimulating economic employment opportunities for vulnerable communities and providing WFP an opportunity to encourage and/or facilitate the employment of members from vulnerable, food insecure households. Where WFP support is targeted at businesses related to food production and sales, WFP support will directly increase community access to, and consumption of, food commodities, thereby enhancing local food security

4 - Needs

Nine years into the crisis, the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 million people in need; of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity.This year the estimated number of people in need has increased by 8 percent . The number of food insecure people has also increased from 6.5M (2019) to 7.9M (2020), approximately 20 percent according to CFSAM 2019,- Overall cereal production has improved this year in comparison to 2018. However, wheat production estimated to be at 2.2 million tons remains lower than the pre-crisis level of 4.1 million tons (2002-2011) and that of the domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tons. However, at 2 million tons, up from 0.4 million tons in 2018, barley production exceeds pre-crisis levels. About 216,000 hactares of wheat and other crops such as barley, fruit trees ,Vegetables and grazing areas were affected by a number of shocks including floods, fires, frost, hailstorms, high temperatures and plant diseases and pests specifically yellow rust. In Northwest Syria, vaccination of 2.4 million smallholder chickens against Newcastle Disease and of smallholder almost 18,000 dairy cattle against Lumpy Skin Disease has prevented a resurgence of these two devastating livestock epidemics in 2019. FSS stresses the need to combine medium term approaches through enhancing primary production capacity and incomes, especially for livelihoods based agriculture, and providing inputs to improve the agricultural services sector in order to improve local availability of food with short term measures through food assistance and imports which remain key to addressing food insecurity in the country in a holistic manner and hence physical and mental wellbeing as well as the living standards of crisis affected people.

5 - Implementation of activities

WFP Syria’s interim Country Strategy Plan (iCSP) 2019-20 envision an increase in livelihood programming to support almost 2 million beneficiaries. The project will support food insecure and vulnerable households with two activity pillars via Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) and Food Assistance for Training (FFT). Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) aims to restore and rebuild livelihoods of vulnerable Syrian households and communities through household-level productive assets, income generation activities. WFP provides technical inputs, technical training, and food security assistance in the lean season. Rehabilitation of communal infrastructure aims to rehabilitate and fix irrigation and other agricultural infrastructure in the rural areas. WFP provides food insecure direct beneficiaries with food assistance. Human Capital Development/Vocational Skills Training via FFT, this pillar aims to enhance human capital and skills development, with a focus on urban women and youth. Food system Enhancement, this pillar aims at stimulating economic employment opportunities for vulnerable communities and providing WFP an opportunity to encourage and/or facilitate the employment of members from vulnerable, food insecure households. WFP will target food insecure communities that have IDPs, returnees, and/or crisis-affected hosting communities. Targeted beneficiaries should be category B and/or C of beneficiaries’ selection tool. The priority will be given to the women-headed household, families with disable members. WFP will ensure communities participation in the planning, design and implementation of livelihood activities through implementing Seasonal Livelihood Programming and community-based participatory planning tools. The plan is aligned with various Ministries. Operationally, WFP will seek out areas of collaboration or joint programming and support a system-driven response.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Implementing livelihood projects are exposed to two types of risks, man-made risks and natural risks. Man-made risk includes, but not limited to, tension between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, misuse of the assets, and deterioration of security situation. In all cases, the likelihood of these risks are medium however, the impact is very high. Mitigation measurement will be undertaken to ensure accountability to targeted populations and mainstreaming of protection concerns. Community level consultation meetings will develop interventions that reflect the real needs of communities, fall under WFP’s mandate, and can equitably target the most vulnerable community members.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access. Develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternate locations.Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue with advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities; Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Major. Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas. Establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate the implementation of project activities. Risk: Possible climatic shocks during the implementation period (drought in particular). Probability: Possible. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Select distribution areas in different agro ecological zones and in irrigated areas to reduce risks. Distribute adapted varieties, which possess specific desirable traits, including drought tolerance.Risk: deterioration of economic situation and un-functionality of the market; Probability: likely; Impact: sever; mitigation measures: moving food security support from vouchers to in-kind.
