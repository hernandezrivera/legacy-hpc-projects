Project Summary

This education project shall provide access to education services affected children in the project targeted counties of lakes state. its components shall include Capacity development: trainings for teachers on childhood education (ECE), training of parents, teachers and Schools management committees (SMC) as well as Parent Teachers Association (PTAs) on school parents’ roles toward child education and school management. training for parents on MHPSS, protection and PSEA as well as integration of GBV cross cutting activities. The project shall strengthen the capacity of county education departments in cueibet and rumbek east through capacity building and workshops. Provision of School Learning materials: the project shall put into account the availability of the learning materials to the most affected schools in cueibet and rumbek east putting into account distribution of ECD kits, Books, chocks and blackboards. Renovation/Provision of WASH facilities: This project shall also provide a safe WASH facility around severe ranked schools by the State Ministry of education. The project shall raise community awareness, lobby and advocacy to stakeholders on education activities and during international event days such as back to learning campaigns.
The project shall only provide education in emergency (EiE) activities to selected two counties of rumbek east and cueibet which were ranked severely affected by the flooding during the IRNA assessments conducted between August – September 2024. The project shall train at least 15 education actors from two counties, 100 teachers from two counties and at least 5 SMCs and PTA trainings in two counties as well raising awareness on back to learning reaching 30,000 people in Lakes State. 7 latrines shall be renovated/constructed in the affected schools. Project shall also provide teachers incentives of 40$ to at least 40 teachers for the period of six months.
The overall aim of the mentioned activities is to provide a safe, favorable and interesting learning ennivornment for children affected by flooding in rumbek east and cueibet, also supported with counselling and MHPSS services for teachers, parents, SMC, PTA and education support community actors in rumbek east and cueibet counties of Lakes State before the next enrollment/academic year started in Feb/March 2025. The teachers’ incentives shall keep teachers motivated to continue rendering education services in difficult circumstances.

1. Needs

South Sudan’s education indicators and in Cueibet and Rumbek center of Lakes state remain among the worst in the world despite increases in school enrolment over the past few years (IRIN ,2023). Estimated that more than two point eight million primary school aged children mostly from rural areas, are not in school, while the few schools that do exist are not conducive to learning (UNESCO 2024 report). The increase in the demand for education by returnee children, flood affected displaced children compounds the infrastructure issues, enough water facilities, unsafe learning facilities, no salary for teachers, lack of capacity building. The already stretched and under-resourced system now has to contend with additional children, placing a further strain on the limited resources. Gaps between the gross enrolment ratio and the NER point to a large over-age population in primary school. This is because most children enter school late. The South Sudan Development Strategy (SSDS) 2021-2024 outlines key priorities for improving education in the country, including national capacity assessment and surveys, infrastructure and equipment, and teachers' education and training curricula, which is in line with the statement problem. Learning spaces/Learning spaces for emergency affected children must be equipped with emergency teaching and learning materials. As with learning spaces, even in non-emergency contexts in South Sudan, access to teaching and learning materials across the country is poor; in addition, providing safe space for children and youth affected by acute emergencies is only possible if teachers, PTAs are trained to be able to support learners and provide lifesaving information and emergency relevant life-skills and psychosocial support. The impact of this flooding is likely to be especially severe for children in the state considering that the children per class ratio of 50 pupil per according to State Ministry of education standard and with the percentage of children without access to safe Water and sanitation facilities being 55.9 and 69% respectively. This situation is clearly unbearable for boys, girls and even more dire for those with disabilities. Considering the fact that the rainy season is only one and a half months now and with-it seasonal flooding expected in many parts of the state, boys and girls from the IDPs, stranded returnees, and most vulnerable host community families are likely to find it especially difficult to cope without external support. In order to cope they may resort to sending children particularly, girls to fetch firewood/grass for sale or boys to make charcoal or cut poles for sale, actions that not only destroy the environment but also predispose them to possible sex violence. Pupils lacks sanitary pads, which forced them to miss classes during menstruation days. Many of the teachers are not trained to provide emergency education services as well as low and delayed incentives for teachers. This increased the low enrollment to schools due to no/poor teaching. From the Initial inter-agency Rapid Needs Assessment (INRA) Conducted in Cueibet County between 27- 29 September 2024 outlined that many of the schools were severely affected by flooding leaving some infrastructures unconducive for learning by children, affected the WASH facilities. this flooding forced many teachers to close down schools and learning centers and leave for higher areas for safety. hundreds of thousand children dropout and get engaged in other economic activities for survival such as fishing and small-scale business in cueibet county headquarters and fishing settlement areas. in 2024 flooding season in cueibet county 10,599 Households were displaced (Ref: Cueibet IRNA 27-29 September 2024) whereas the data from Rumbek center remains pending from UNWFP. This means that in school children from displaced households were displaced from accessing learning centers.
