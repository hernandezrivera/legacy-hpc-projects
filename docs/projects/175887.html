Project Summary

The project will target the most vulnerable groups (children and Women) in Ikotos County both displaced and host community.
The main aim of the project is to; increase equitable and dignified access to quality malnutrition- specific curative services for children and women, and enhance integrated sustainable preventive measures to halt prevalence of acute malnutrition among children, and women in Ikotos County.
Nutrition service delivery will prioritize provision of comprehensive and timely community management of acute malnutrition at both Nutrition centers at Boma levels and in hard to reach locations without static centers to access service.
The project will as well emphasize on promoting breast feeding and optimal feeding practices among children and women through scaling up of MIYCN counselling services.
With the lessons learnt from compounding effect of COVID-19 pandemic to already existing challenges, several revised/new guidelines and approaches such as family MUAC shall be adhered and implemented in 2021 to ensure continuity of equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services in the context of COVID -19.
Given the multi causative contributing factors to malnutrition, the project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programing with other relevant sectors such as WASH, health, protection and food security through effectives coordination mechanism that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to mult-sectoral basic service which will further reduce prevalence of acute malnutrition and eventually reduce morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable groups (children and women).
Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring that programing of nutrition services approach is community/people centered in such a way that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation to ensure that the intervention is responsive enough to the arrays of the changing needs. Consideration gender and age variation of the vulnerable groups will be prioritize as well for efficiency of the response.

1 - Needs

An estimated 1.9 million people are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021, including 313,391 children (163,913 male and 149,476 female) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1,078,867 children (562,697 male and 516,170 female) with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women with Acute malnutrition (AM-PLW) according to south Sudan Nutrition Cluster, Nutrition HNO/PiN, 2021, (internal). As per current IPC AMN (November 2020-March 2021) Ikotos county is rated among the 24 counties in IPC AMN phase 3.The latest (2019 June) SMART survey AVSI conducted in Ikotos revealed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate for the former County of Ikwoto was 9.2% (6.6 – 12.8 95% C.I.), and the severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate was 2.0% (1.0 – 4.0 95% C.I.). GAM indicates a poor nutrition situation while SAM is classified as critical according to WHO classification.However the situation worsen in 2020 as per the latest FSNMS-R25 survey result of 2020 indicates GAM rate for Ikotos county is at 16.77%. The major contributing to acute malnutrition in Ikotos includes high prevalence of communicable diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum Dietary Diversity: 15%) and poor food consumption score (48%), Elevated levels of food insecurity (Ikotos among the counties rated at IPC AFI Phase 3), COVID-19 related disruptions, and restrictions also compounded to existing food insecurity Covid-19 pandemic as well interrupted smooth nutrition program implementation, e.g. changes in SAM and MAM referral protocols for children have exacerbated lack of access to services.

2 - Project activities

The following will be the activities to prevent malnutrition among the vulnerable groups; Vetting of 35 community nutrition volunteers at village level; Training of 30 health and nutrition workers/35 community volunteers on growth monitoring ;Training 30 health and Nutrition workers/ 35 community volunteers on Integrated Malnutrition prevention package; Timely distribution of Targeted and Blanket supplementary feeds to 990 children (6-59Months) and to 365 PLWs; Training of 30 health and nutrition workers/35 community volunteers on maternal infant and young child counselling; Conduct routine MIYCN counselling to mothers/caretaker of children under five at 15 Static and 3 mobile service access points; Conduct routine Integrated socio-behavioral change communication campaigns (nutrition, hygiene and health promotion) including global breast feeding week campaign; Conduct 15 cooking demonstration to mothers and caretaker of children under five on nutritious dense meal at both nutrition sites and in the community every quarter; facilitate Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns in all the 3 payams;To ensure prompt case identification and quality treatment of acute malnutrition among the vulnerable, the project will ensure active routine/mass screening children U5 and PLW for malnutrition both at nutrition sites and in the community; Employment of nutrition nurses at all the 15 nutrition sites; Training of 30 health and nutrition workers/ 35 community volunteers on integrated management of acute malnutrition; Rehabilitate and equip 15 nutrition sites; Timely, and reliable distribution of therapeutic feeds to 3,648 children with MAM and 1,769 children with SAM and to 2,243 PLW-AM; Provision of monitoring/evaluation tools and national guidelines to all the 15 nutrition sites and to community nutrition volunteers;To ensure effective monitoring AVSI will put in place the following; Conduct routine on job mentor ship and supervision of nutrition workers at nutrition sites by qualified nutrition officers; conduct performance scoring for all nutrition sites on quarterly basis; Facilitate quarterly Joint (SMoH, CHD, UNICEF, WFP and AVSI) support supervision in all the 15 nutrition sites; Facilitation of carry our periodic beneficiary’s and satisfactory assessment in all nutrition sites; conduct quarterly programmatic visits and monitoring of Nutrition sites; Establishment of feedback/complaints mechanisms at all nutrition sites in form suggestion boxes and AAP committees as a means of community engagement; timely and regular nutrition information reports submission from all the nutrition sites into cluster nutrition dashboard on monthly basis; actively support and facilitate 2 national surveys (FSNMS).To enhance equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from other relevant sectors the project will ensure; malaria testing for children admitted in OTP; provision of LLITN to all children admitted in OTP program; provision of supplementary therapeutic feeds to PLWHIV-AIDS/TB and on care in St Theresa mission hospital; Referral of children and women with acute malnutrition for vaccination and ANC services respectively. Distribution of soaps and WASH kits to children admitted at OTP; Distribution of hand washing facilities at all OTP sites; Establish kitchen gardens at all the 15 nutrition centres; Provision of food assistant to mothers/caregiver of children admitted in SC and those with chronic illnesses (HIV//TB) at St Theresa. Referral of mothers to children in OTP/TSFP to GFD. Conduct GBV safety audits at all the 15 nutrition sites; Training of health and nutrition staffs on GBV/protection; Training of 30 health and 35 nutrition staffs on ECD; Distribution of ECD kits to all Nutrition sites.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Nutrition sites will be established equitably in all the boma at PHCU which is easily accessible by the local communities, and in boma without static nutrition services the project will ensure vulnerable groups are reached through mobile services; Reducing waiting time of beneficiaries at nutrition service point by adhering to the effective flow in service provision as per the guideline and ensure every nutrition sites has got adequate staffing (nutrition workers) attending promptly to beneficiaries. In areas where case of violence is high ration provided to beneficiaries will be adequate enough to reduce on frequency of follow up visits at nutrition sites. Engagement of local and traditional leaders during mobilization to provide security in theirs area for their people accessing services and protection of service access points; Women are always more exposed than men, therefore service provider will make sure women are encourage to be accompanied by their husbands while accessing services. The project will ensure initial involvement of women and local women groups representatives in AAP committees and in planning activities implementation; Nutrition facilities will act as a valuable entry point for disseminating information about available GBV services and collecting feedback from women and girls about their needs/safety concerns; Where nutrition volunteers are going door to door especially during the covid -19 pandemic, the project will ensure CNVs are trained/oriented on how to safely respond to a disclosure and that they have the most up-to-date referral pathway. With support and coordination with GBV specialists from available local actors, household nutrition screenings will be used as a modality to proactively disseminate information on available GBV services. Health and nutrition workers/volunteers will be equipped with pocket cards containing relevant contact information; Representations of the GBV referral pathways will be posted in all nutrition sites. AVSI managed to install suggestion boxes in all, the nutrition sites however very few feedback were received majorly because majority of beneficiaries are illiterate (not able to write). Beneficiaries access to information in form communicating complaints and receiving feedback will be enhance through formation of AAP committees at Boma level with representatives from each villages who will be in touch with beneficiaries to receive compliant and feedback. AVSI will meet with the AAP committee to discuss on the complaints and feedback from time to time (Monthly); On quarterly basis there will be a help desks at Nutrition sites attended by one personnel from AVSI at a higher position to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries. AVSI will ensure Local stakeholders are oriented on the program in term of its objective/purpose, targeted beneficiaries and justification of selection criteria such as those with acute malnutrition (children and PLW); Involvement of Local stakeholders and community organs/committees or groups and tradition leaders in selection procedures e.g. vulnerability assessment; Visual presentation of targeted beneficiaries and their entitlement at distribution points/service access points in form of charts, poster; Engagement of community committee/groups or local leaders to participate, supervise and monitor distribution; Distribution points should be centrally and equitably allocated to provide equal chances for all targeted beneficiaries from various location to access the services being provided.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Protection sensitive services will as well be mainstream in the project, the activities will include; facilitation of child stimulation and ECD in all nutrition sites, GBV/PSEA awareness to nutrition workers and beneficiaries, referral of GBV cases to access service at the management sites.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

To improve on production and utilization of nutritious locally available food at household for optimal MIYC feeding the project will collaborate with AVSI food security team to establish demonstration kitchen gardens at nutrition sites, AVSI will also facilitate cooking demonstration to enlighten the mothers, fathers and caregivers on how to prepare nutritious foods harvested from the kitchen gardens and encouraged to replicate the same at home. Mothers and caretakers of children with acute malnutrition will be link to community general food assistant to improve on house hold food security. People with chronic illnesses and mothers of children admitted in SC for management of severe acute malnutrition will be link to institution feeding program to receive food assistance.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Given the fact drivers of malnutrition cuts across multi sectors the project will ensure operational linkages exist with other sectors such as health Food security, WASH and protection through improve coordination and convergence programing. The following activities will be implemented such that the vulnerable targeted groups (malnourished women, children boys and girls) have access to multi sector basic services; Joint health and nutrition education/promotion shall be conducted at all nutrition sites; provision of LLITNS shall be provided to children in OTP programs as a preventive measure of malaria, the project will ensure all children admitted in OTP are tested and treated for malaria, all children identified for medical complication shall be referred for medical care. Malnourished Pregnant mothers accessing treatment at nutrition shall be referred to ANC services. WASH kits shall be provided to children admitted in OTP program and all nutrition sites will be equip with hygiene equipment such as hand washing facilities and the project will ensure sanitation facilities exist in all nutrition sites.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The following approach shall be used; vulnerable groups PLW and children under will be provided with specialized food supplement through blanket supplementary Programs (BSFP) to prevent malnutrition; the project will emphasis maternal infant and young child nutrition counselling among pregnant, and lactating mothers/caregivers to children under five. Awareness on promoting health/ nutrition especially breastfeeding, hygiene shall be conducted. To optimize feeding practice among PLW and children the project activities will include conducting cooking demonstration to mothers and caregivers accessing services at nutrition site. Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies is very vital in reducing GAM prevalence the project will facilitate vitamin A supplementation campaigns and will refer children with identified deficiency for immediate treatment. The project will ensure access to early screening and timely quality treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition. Other key component that will contribute to reduced GAM prevalence is to ensure effective and efficient programing through strengthened program monitoring, ensuring community full participation and commitment and improve coordination and nutrition information management and utilization.
