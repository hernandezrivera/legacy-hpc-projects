Project Summary

Through the proposed project, UNFPA will invest in empowering youth, especially young girls aiming to enhance their resilience, by building their personal and social assets that can promote their positive development and enable them to reach their full potential and contribute to the wellbeing of their families and communities, access to livelihood opportunities and social participation by building the capacity of young people to take the lead and the responsibility to identify and prioritize their needs as well as the community needs; and through building the capacity of young people, more specifically youth female headed households on business startups and provision of seed funding to support them in leading entrepreneurship projects.
Three youth friendly spaces will be established in Dara’a city, Sanamen and Izraa. A set of activities will be provided at the center aiming to promote young people’s wellbeing, leadership, participation and economic empowerment. The project will be implemented in Dara’a Dara’a city, Sanamen and Izraa, the communities selected were based mainly on the 2019 severity scale and on the fact that they are priority return destination and accessible to UNFPA IPs .
The project will follow a gender transformative and gender equality approach, as per existing IASC guidelines . More so, the interventions in this proposal are in line with the “Compact for Young People in Humanitarian Action” launched at the World Humanitarian Summit (2016), UNFPA promotes efforts to put young people at the center of humanitarian action and does so through key actions: 1) On services: Promoting and increasing age- and gender-responsive and inclusive programmes that contribute to the protection, health and development of young women, young men, girls and boys within humanitarian settings 2) On participation: supporting systematic inclusion of, engagement and partnership with youth, in all phases of humanitarian action, through sharing of information and involvement in decision-making processes 3) On capacities: Recognizing and strengthening young people’s capacities and capabilities to be effective humanitarian actors 4) On resources: increasing resources intended to address the needs and priorities of adolescents and youth affected by humanitarian crises, and identify ways to more accurately track and report on the resources allocated to young people in humanitarian contexts.
UNFPA is committed to invest in youth, and emphasizes the rights of adolescents and the need to increase the potential of adolescents and youth to contribute to positive social transformation. UNFPA is mandated to address the needs of young people that are considered life savings for their future well being, it is imperative to develop projects that will contribute to empower young people and keep them away from harmful practices that affect their decisions, health and future.

4 - Needs

While approaching the tenth year of the crises in Syria, the estimated number of people in need of humanitarian assistance reached 11.1 million people including 4.7 million in acute need as of the beginning of 2020 . The number of internally displaced is 6.2 million. Near 1.4 million of people returned to their homes. According to UN estimate half of the population have food insecurity. The protracted crises continued to shed its negative impact on the socio economic conditions in Syria. According to ESCWA estimate more than 80 per cent of the population is living below the poverty line . Millions are deprived of their basic rights including access to proper shelter, food, health services and livelihood opportunities. All that increased the risk of engagement in harmful coping mechanism. The most recent UN population estimate shows that 33 per cent of the total population is between 10 and 24 years representing 6,037,216 million young people, out of which 4.1 million are between the ages of 10 and 19 years .The situation for Syria’s youth population is dire. According to the 2019 ERL severity scale analysis half of the population is estimated to be in acute need of access to basic and social services and to livelihoods and income generating opportunities. 55 percent of Syrians have no jobs and 73 per cent of young Syrians aged 15-24 are unemployed. In the absence of decent job and livelihood opportunities, more and more people living in Syria are resorting to harmful coping mechanisms including gender based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse and early and forced marriage. It is estimated that over 15 percent of the active Syrians are now engaged in the illegal sector and violence economy for a living, such as smuggling and trafficking. The crises has led to a significant change in the role of women in society, many women became the only bread winners in their families. The project will be implemented through partnership with local NGOs.

5 - Implementation of activities

In coordination and collaboration with other UN agencies (UNICEF, UNDP, WFP), UNFPA is working on developing mechanisms and tools that enable expanding the coverage of the supported-Youth related services, provide the actual needed assistance to the beneficiaries and to effectively increase the resilience of young people by providing them with access to opportunities to exercise leadership and participate in recovery, humanitarian action and in sustaining peace. In cooperation with its partners, UNFPA is planning to effectively link the economic empowerment activities to the opportunities in the local market in Dara’a through assessing the status of the market and the existing services and the requirements to strengthen/create suitable environment that is needed to enable the youth people of, successfully, starting up their own businesses. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow the Do No Harm and Do Good approaches . The project emphasizes gender-responsive approach in humanitarian setting. Agreement will be made with the implementing partners (IPs), that the project will have to be initiated ensuring that young people will be selected in a transparent process with the involvement of the concerned authorities from the initial kick of phase of the project till its evaluation. More so, it will also be agreed, that in case of any signs of escalation, both UNFPA and the IPs will be consulting with the local communities on how to proceed further with a convenient response. Based on the results of the consultation, the project activities will be shifted to meet the new requirements. In what concerns the limitation in availability of resources and response capacities, the project will closely involve the local communities, will indeed follow mitigating measures in terms of 1) assessing the needs, 2) doing prioritization and 3) ensuring equal access to those in needs. Such results will be discussed and reviewed by the project committee specially formed for this purpose. Finally, the IP will have to share on a regular basis the achievements of the activities and report on them.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The major risks might be encountered during the project implementation:● Denial of access to the selected project site due to sudden security situation. The implementation will be shifted to another area. ● Interventions might contribute to increasing tensions between host and displaced communities. The project design will take into consideration that participants are selected covering IDP, returnees and hosting community.
