Project Summary

The highly volatile and rapidly deteriorating macroeconomic environment, combined with the poor agricultural season, continue to drive food insecurity in Zimbabwe. This year, the country is experiencing one of its worst food security crises in at least a decade.
The 2018/19 agricultural season was characterized by the late onset of rains across the country and long dry spells. To compound this situation, the country was hit by a devastating cyclone in March 2019, which caused severe damages to crop and infrastructure. Cereal production was estimated at 851,844 MT which is less than half the national requirement of 2.1 million MT. As of 21 November, the Government has stated that more than 800,000 MT maize are required to bridge the country’s grain deficit. Limited availability of foreign exchange to finance imports has created significant concerns for food availability in the country over the coming months with associated price increases further undermining purchasing power and food security for urban and rural households.
To respond to the increased needs WFP intends to scale up assistance to reach 4.1m people (3.9m rural; 200k urban) classified as IPC 3/4 from January to April 2020 during the peak of the lean season and 1.5m (1.3m rural; 200k urban) people in May and June when the harvest will be starting and needs relatively reducing. Further assistance will be provided from August to December 2020: an estimated 1m (800k rural; 200k urban) across August and September and 1.5 m (1.3m rural; 200k urban) between October and December 2020 as projections indicate below average precipitation for the current rainy season and a continued deterioration in macro-economic conditions suggesting that crisis conditions are likely to continue to affect portions of the population throughout the coming year. These figures will be revised based on the results of annual seasonal assessments in June/July 2020.
As part of this heightened response, WFP also intends to scale up coverage to reach all 60 rural districts in the country, including expanding coverage within already supported districts.
WFP stands ready to scale up assistance and is in the process of bringing food commodities into the country using a mix of local, regional and international procurement to meet the commodity requirement.
The contracted transporter capacity is being doubled to reach 40,000 MT per month. In parallel, warehousing capacity is being adjusted in all locations.
In addition, WFP is engaging with 18 cooperating partners and augmenting its own staffing capacity in critical areas. Field Level Agreements are underdevelopment and on track to be ready in January for the scale up. Registrations are ongoing and partners are being trained on PSEA, Protection, SCOPE and other issues.
WFP has a toll-free hotline, an on-site suggestion box and the active, visible presence of staff during the distributions. All complaints would be collected, referred and analysed on a timely basis and feedback provided to the complainant. WFP will adjust programmes based on complaints and feedback provided through these mechanisms.
Ad-hoc and regular monitoring will be conducted to assess any form of exploitation and manipulation of assistance including triangulation of data from the complaints and feedback mechanisms put in place.
WFP and partners will continue to sensitize beneficiaries on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and undertake dedicated trainings at Field Office level with WFP staff and Cooperating Partners.

1 - Activities

• Due to the continuing deterioration in the macro-economic environment, WFP will switch its assistance for the rural population entirely to in-kind from January 2020.• WFP intends to assist rural households with in-kind food rations (7.5 kg of cereals, 1.5 kg of pulses and 0.75kg of vegetable oil) in all 60 rural districts targeting IPC3 and 4 caseloads.• Due to the deterioration in the food security situation WFP plans to assist up to 200,000 people in eight (8) urban and peri-urban domains (Marondera, Norton, Masvingo, Bulawayo/Mzilikazi, Harare South/Hopley, Epworth, Mutare/Sakubva, Rusape) throughout the country. CBT will be maintained as the modality of assistance in the urban context for operational and protection reasons. WFP plans to provide a transfer value of $9 USD/month/person through mobile money. • To mitigate against the potential deterioration of the nutritional status of children in rural areas, WFP plans to provide a protective nutrition-centered ration (3 kg of Super Cereal Plus ration or 1.5 kg of LNS) to children under the age of five in targeted households from January to June 2020, on a monthly basis and reaching up to 561,700 children. • WFP is currently supporting approximately 15,000 refugees in Tongogara Refugee Camp. This already-vulnerable population is also negatively impacted by cash shortages and rising prices. The WFP assistance, on which the population depends, faces regular funding shortages. The planned monthly in-kind ration per person will be comprised of 13.5 kg cereals, 2 kg pulses and 0.75 kg veg oil. A ration of respectively 6kg and 3 kg of CSB++ is provided to children under the age of five and to chronically ill refugees. • Priority is being given to districts with the highest/worst phase classification (i.e., IPC3, Crisis; IPC4, Emergency). Within the selected districts, the most affected wards will be targeted. Then, beneficiaries are targeted through community-based, village level identification. For the LSA scale-up, all targeted beneficiaries are classified in IPC Phase 3 and 4. WFP in collaboration with its partners will integrate protection, and accountability to affected populations (AAP) into its activities where protection concerns might arise and towards contributing to protection- and gender outcomes. Stakeholder meetings will be held to create awareness on humanitarian principles and targeting criteria and entitlements are made public in an accessible and meaningful manner to affected communities and local authorities.
