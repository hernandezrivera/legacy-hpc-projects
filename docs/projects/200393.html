Project Summary

Considering the recent decision declared by the Minister of Interior and Municipalities, a rise in homophobic, lesbophobic, biphobic, and transphobic movements against the LGBTIQ community in Lebanon has been perceived. Both, state and non-state actors currently pose a threat by engaging in harmful acts directed at the LGBTIQ community. This is rooted in the systemic challenges that our society faces, in addition to harmful norms which result in Sexual Gender-based Violence (GBV)(SGBV) against the LGBTIQ community. GBV remains a serious violation of human rights and a life-threatening health and protection concern, where a recent study indicated that 75% of the LGBTIQ community lives in fear and/or at risk of such incidents. Through MOSAIC’s helpline, an increase in requests for support has been also recorded pertaining to psychological, sexual, and socio-economic violence. Threats of verbal (including hate speech) and physical violence are spreading like wildfire across the country, leading to a severe shrinking of safe spaces for the community. Some examples are the recent cancellation of all the LGBTIQ events, and specifically the demonstration to protest the minister’s recent decision due to violence and death threats. With multi-layered discrimination and violence, this community remains a survivor of GBV justified by different cultural, legal, societal, religious, and socioeconomic factors. Recently, and despite the increasing demands for GBV protection, local LGBTIQ organizations have stopped providing services due to the lack of resources - which has significantly put GBV survivors at higher risk.
Our work with GBV survivors involves physical, emotional, or sexual in nature involving rape, physical assault, sexual abuse, intimate partner violence, and honor killings. MOSAIC ensures to follow the GBVIMS protocols along with a very specific LGBTIQ survivor-centered approach.
Through this project, MOSAIC seeks to address the mental needs of LGBTIQ GBV survivors and assist them with relevant cash assistance in responding to their pressing needs arising because of both the health crisis and the economic crisis, with a focus on the following areas:
Mental Health Services
Legal services
Moreover, this potential fund will allow MOSAIC to cover core costs crucial to the sustainability of support and service provision to the LGBTIQ+ community in Lebanon. These core costs entail a contribution to the social worker’s salary, who is a front-liner in the response to the beneficiaries’ needs and is responsible for providing case management services, and directing the beneficiaries to the needed services, whether internally or externally. In addition, this fund will contribute to the yearly rental fees of MOSAIC’s centre where services and activities take place, as well as essential operational costs, that ensure smooth and safe operations for all MOSAIC’s community, including beneficiaries and staff.

Mechanism
The Project seeks to reach out for the most vulnerable individuals of the LGBTIQ+ community residing in Lebanon. Although the implementation will be through our Beirut-based office, our outreach will be directed to all the different Lebanese areas. This is done through our 24/7 helpline and referral system with actors.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

All staff will sign the Code of Conduct declaration, be trained in SEA and have support to enable them to take the mandatory e-learning course on PSEA.Appoint PSEA focal point. Step Strengthen the LGBTIQ community engagement and awareness-raising.Assess and map potential risk areas by sector and discuss actions to reduce the risk of SEA and prevent and sanction it.Design effective coordinated complaint and feedback mechanisms that LGBTIQ communities support and which are sensitive to age gender and diversity and are accessible for persons with disabilities. Integrate SEA risks into protection analysis, programming, and assistance.Report any suspected SEA issues without delay.Victims of SEA to have access to support they need for their safety, to obtain basic material assistance, medical care, psychosocial support, or legal services.RCA will be used throughout the project for risk mitigation. This will be done by evidence-based data collection.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Ensure that all LGBTIQ have equal and non-discriminatory access to protection, assistance, and solutions. Identify the capacities and priorities of LGBTIQ Community and develop protection, assistance and solutions programmes that accord with them.Establish and maintain effective feedback systems using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback.Allocate human and financial resources to ensure that feedback from LGBTIQ Community is systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner.Learn from continuous engagement with LGBTIQ communities and adapt interventions and programmes in response to new knowledge gained.Measure and improve accountability to LGBTIQ Community through assessments of organizational performance on accountability.
