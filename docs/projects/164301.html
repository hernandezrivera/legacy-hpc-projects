Project Summary

The project aims at supporting vulnerable people in Damascus and Rural Damascus with essential health care services. Activities in Damascus will target IDPs and the overburdened community. Damascus is considered as home to thousands of IDPs and function as key referral sites for secondary care, thereby placing heavy loads on the health system. Rural Damascus and other location selected in this project are of high severity scale and in high need for health care services
Activities will be implemented in 6 static centers and 10 mobile teams: static centers will be in Damascus, Kafr Batna, Duma, Harasta, Az-Zabdani, Darayya, while mobile teams will be functioning in Kisweh, babella, Maliha, Jaramana, Arbin, Qudsaiya, Dimas, Ein Elfijeh, Madaya, and Sehnaya,.
Planned activities will be in line with sector objective No. 1 and No. 3
The main activities will be:
Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, and referral through static centers and establishment of medical mobile teams.
Beneficiaries from those services will be disaggregated as follows:
60000 patients (men, women, boys, girls) will benefit from medical consultations
5000 women will benefit from Ante-Natal Care services
5000 persons will benefit from mental health consultations
500 physical rehabilitation sessions will be provided to people in need
500 vaginal deliveries and 200 caesarian deliveries will be supported
1500 patient will be supported with referrals for specialized treatment including secondary operations.
50 health workers and 10 community health workers will be trained on health topics
Al Tamayoz will be responsible of deploying well qualified staff to assure the qualitative implementation, and a good monitoring and evaluation measures will be in place, Reports will be prepared on monthly basis and will be shred with the health sector, project implementation will be monitored through periodical assessment to make sure that designed results and project outcome are on track, assessment are made during activities implementation, Monitoring activities will include : Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place, Conduct filed missions, include monitoring of access to services by sex, age, host/IDP/ Returnees status, and by people with reduced mobility.
Evaluation will be through collecting and analyzing of information that will be delivered to the project manager for the timely action. A complain box will be available in each center and will be opened on daily basis and supervised by the M&E officer who in turn will direct the complains to the general manager for any required action.

4 - Needs

8 years of crisis have heavily disrupted the health system in Syria. 42 administrative districts – home to 14.5 million people – are below emergency standards for hospital beds. Up to 70 % of health workers have left Syria and many others have been injured or killed. Distance, overcrowding and long waiting times are top barriers reported by patients to access health services. Accessibility and cost of transport also impede access to services for persons with disability. Recent assessments indicate the need for improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs, particularly for non-communicable diseases, which are estimated to account for 45 % of all deaths. Mental health and psychosocial support needs are critical. where health structures are damaged or none exist, mobile teams are needed to fill gaps. Referral capacity is needed to overcome gaps in secondary care. Specialized services are needed for physical rehabilitation. HNO health sector chapter draft 4 (20 November2019) According to health sector severity scale and population in need 2020, there are (1009459) people in need for health care services in Damascus, (1025797) in Rural Damascus, (37365) in Duma, (157060) in Harasta, (67422) in Az-Zabdani, and (55144) in Darayya. According to Al Tamayoz database that includes more than 26000 registered families from Damascus and Rural Damascus, 80% of those families are IDPs, most of them are families of women households with hard living conditions. Through AlTamayoz work in Rural Damascus especially in East Ghota from June 2018 till December 2019, and through FGD targeted men,women,boys and girls in other locations: Duma, Zabdani, many different types of health needs were observed. There is lack of free of charge health care services in Rural Damascus. Many families suffer from cold winter, children suffer from respiratory diseases due to the usage of wood as a source of heat. Elderly number is high, with an increased need for chronic diseases medications

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims at supporting vulnerable people in Damascus and Rural Damascus with essential health care services. Activities in Damascus will target IDPs and the overburdened community. The increase of poverty level during the last few months forced IDPs and host community living in Damascus to ignore theirs and their children critical health needs due to the increase in health care services prices and to the prioritization they set for their needs that put food and shelter at the top of the list. Damascus is considered as home to thousands of IDPs and function as key referral sites for secondary care, thereby placing heavy loads on the health system.Rural Damascus and other location selected in this project are of high severity scale and in high need for health care services.
