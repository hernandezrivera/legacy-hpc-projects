Project Summary

Through the proposed project, Oxfam plans to create an official links between the labour suppliers and demanders in the local labour market systems. This will be in terms creating temporary job opportunities for unskilled labour, building the vocational and business skills and ethics, equip skilled people with the needed productive assets to start/re-start micro and small businesses, link people with the job placement opportunities. This links will be done in a way that enables women to access employment opportunities in a dignified and safe ways. Furthermore, Oxfam will support the rehabilitation of productive and market facilities including markets, storage, warehousing, irrigation facilities and processing facilities through labour-intensive schemes when possible. Oxfam will coordinate with different labour market actors like, chambers of industry and commerce, businesses associations, UN and INGOs, traders and the government.

4 - Needs

Although that markets in the Urban settings are functioning well but still the most vulnerable are finding serious difficulties to have access to income-generating opportunities, this is mainly because they don’t have sufficient information and skills to access already limited employment opportunities, and no enough skills, resources and assets for self-employment opportunities. Based on market assessments, Oxfam found that while most of the most vulnerable people identify that the lack of access to income generating opportunities as a critical problem, the labour demanders state that finding skilled and unskilled labour as a critical problem as well, which means that there is critical gaps in linking suppliers and demanders in the labour market system. Besides Oxfam will help the proposed communities by creating emergency job opportunities and ensure equal, dignified and safe participation of women. The employed workers will perform community works like cleaning of the main streets removing debris and waste, minro rehabilitation of the community infrastructure like irrigation canals, walls, street sides, bridging wholes in the roads, awareness on Covid 19 .

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Limited equipment and supplies available in the Syrian market due to instability, sanctions and transactions delays prevent the implementation of project activities. - The USD to SYP Exchange rate fluctuations could result in either big gain at end of project period with no time to appropriate the additional funds, or not enough funds to fulfil commitments and project objectives. - Potential of fraud and aid diversion, amongst Oxfam staff, suppliers and partners - COVID19 spread puts at risk the health and wellbeing of affected communities and of humanitarian workers delivering assistance. - Assumptions: - No interference by conflicting parties in the implementation putting at risk independence, and impartiality. the local authorities will allocate enough production inputs for the targeted businesses (electricity, water and other public services). the community will have the capacity to manage the work in the rehabilitated bakeries sufficiently. The social norms and traditions will enable women to participate in the communities’ decision-making process.Mitigation: Oxfam will perform rapid market assessment to check the availability of the required items and their prices. All Oxfam and partner’s staff will be inducted on Oxfam’s standards on safeguarding and accountability. Very clear accountability mechanism will be put in place prior to the implementation. Oxfam will go for NCEs to absorb the surpluses that it might have as a result to an increase in the exchange rate.
