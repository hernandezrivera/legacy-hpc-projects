Project Summary

Affected people in Cox’s Bazar have equitable access to quality and timely humanitarian assistance that is appropriate and responsive and have increased capacity to prepare and respond to future shocks themselves. Capable Local and National Actors increase their leadership role in protecting the rights of refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, ensuring strong community engagement and empowerment.

Needs

More than 30% of women interviewed feared kidnapping of both boys and girls and more than 30% of men feared trafficking of both boys and girls when asked about the major protection risks in their area for children, according to Settlement and Protection Profiling did by UNHCR in July 2019. Additionally, more than 20% of women and girls cited fear of sexual assault as one of the major risks to their security in the same exercise. Mahjis, CiCs, and the army were listed as the top sources for security listed by both men and women in the same survey. More than 80% of respondents in the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment done in October 2019 indicated they would refer a friend to a Mahji for care and support related to a sexual assault and 90% responded they would refer to a Mahji for serious security issues. This showed the importance of ensuring these authorities improve their protection capacity for the population that relies on them for their security. Further, over 60% of respondents from the Settlement and Protection Profiling cited increased community watch groups as one of the ways to improve their feelings of safety and security where they live, highlighting the importance of the support of partners and communities in advocacy and negotiation for protection. The Multi-sector needs assessment showed that 95% of households used coping strategies due to lack of money to meet basic needs, highlighting the need for support to partners and communities for local and national level advocacy, as well as international advocacy and campaigning for those in power to protect people.

Activities or outputs

Protection is at the centre of Oxfam’s response. The identified protection risks & needs will be addressed by increasing refugees’ self-protection capacity, supporting community-based protection initiatives, facilitating safe & timely access to services, fostering social cohesion, and constant protection monitoring and evidence-based advocacy to raise awareness on protection trends and meet communities’ needs. This aims to 1.Build the self-protection capacity of affected communities, including anti-trafficking, DRR and referral pathways; 2.Facilitate safe, timely access to emergency & protection services, including to GBV; 3.Capacity-building and support to relevant authorities to better fulfil their protection role, including to anti-trafficking, DRR and referral pathways; 4.Carry out & support partners and communities in local and national level advocacy and negotiation for protection; 5.Advocacy and campaigning to convince those in power or with influence to protect people; 6. 1to5 supported by protection monitoring.Single female heads of household participating in cash incentives activity will be a part of a ToT on anti-trafficking and then deliver at least 2 awareness raising sessions in a shelter, targeting female neighbors. Oxfam will target these households specifically cause they are at a higher risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms such as sex work to survive.They will receive training that will improve their knowledge of risk & referral pathways and an receive an incentive.Oxfam adapts human trafficking information dissemination so women will be trained at block level, decreasing the need for them to travel far to participate and enabling them to better outreach people with mobility issues or with cultural freedom of movement constraints as well. The project will also support Rohingya and HC members in building their awareness on GBV and empowering women in leadership. For GBV cases, we work with GBV sector actors and GBV focal points for safe identification and referral. Oxfam will continue to engage with protection monitoring and Protection Monitoring report analysis is based on KIIs (including PwDs), FGDs and questionnaires with women, girls, men & boys. The findings will be shared with key protection stakeholders and the protection sector and will directly inform standalone advocacy initiatives. Oxfam supports, through the protection committee, youth group, and religious leader group in Hnila ward 9, community-based protection and social cohesion events, bringing both communities together to watch dramas centred on protection themes, as well as communal celebrations such as for Eid. Oxfam Advocacy and Policy staff will continue to participate in all Advocacy coordination platforms and lead coordinated initiatives based on protection input and findings of protection monitoring. As for other sectors, Oxfam will continue to provide a quarterly protection monitoring report, addressing specifically Health, Education, WASH, Food Security and Livelihood, Shelter concerns. Oxfam will work to strengthen and support advocacy through the Advocacy WG, the Gender in Humanitarian Emergencies WG, relevant Cash WG, and the Protection Working Group. AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations) Principles will be incorporated into the project through regular meetings with all protection community-based structures and religious leaders/local authorities. Further, the staff works closely with the MEAL team where response to all urgent matters is in 24h to 72h at community level. In accordance with DRR principles, Oxfam does EWER training and ensures up-to-date referral pathways in case of emergency. Regarding PSEA, all Oxfam staff are trained on and actively participate in initiatives of the PSEA network and Oxfam also ensures training of contractors and partners as well. This will improve humanitarian actors capacity to prevent sexual exploitation & abuse.
