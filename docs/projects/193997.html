Project Summary

Through the proposed project, Relief International (RI) intends to reach 222,242 individuals and provide quality emergency lifesaving and recovery services to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities affected by conflict and natural disasters. The project will be implemented in four localities, reaching the following individuals: In North Darfur (El Fashir: 96,935; Mellit: 10,866; Kalemando: 2,591; Saraf Omrah: 22,578; and Al Malha: 8,162), three localities in Blue Nile (Al Rosaries: 3,759; Damazine: 1,783; and Gissan: 8,068), one locality in Al Jazirah (Wad Madani: 12,500), and in Khartoum (55,000). RI plans to reach 141,133 individuals (67,744 males and 73,389 females) in North Darfur, 13,611 individuals (6,533 males and 7,078 females) in Blue Nile, 12,500 individuals (6,000 males and 6,500 females) in Al Jazirah, and 55,000 individuals (26,400 males and 28,600 females) in Khartoum with emergency health response. RI will ensure that its interventions are integrated with other services such as nutrition and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH). Protection and gender-based violence (SGBV) will be mainstreamed into RI's lifesaving emergency health response. Under the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan, RI will align its emergency health interventions with the Health Strategic Objectives. This includes supporting essential public health functions with a focus on strong primary healthcare, strengthening emergency preparedness, response, and all-hazards emergency risk management, and improving access to healthcare and providing timely and quality care for beneficiaries. RI will also focus on managing and treating diseases at outpatient departments (OPD) and inpatient departments, strengthening maternal and child health (MCH) services including sexual reproductive health, antenatal care (ANC), safe deliveries, postnatal care (PNC), expanded program on immunization (EPI), laboratory services, and health education. These services will be provided in 34 primary healthcare facilities. Through the proposed lifesaving emergency health services, RI aims to build the capacity of 570 health workers (255 males and 315 females) through formal and on-the-job trainings. RI will also strengthen the disease surveillance system at the community and health facility levels, and actively engage in preventing and responding to common non-communicable and communicable diseases, including disease outbreak prevention, management, and control. Furthermore, RI will enhance the referral mechanism and ensure that gynecological, obstetrical, surgical, and medical cases have access to secondary healthcare. This will require a functional 24/7 ambulance service and a motivated health workforce and support staff, including ambulance drivers.

1 - Needs

Sudan is grappling with unprecedented humanitarian needs following a military coup, and the transition to a new government remains uncertain. Conflict-induced displacement, driven by inter-communal clashes and political agendas, has surged, posing risks to civilian protection. Criminality and insecurity have also risen, particularly in conflict-affected regions like Darfur. Approximately 15.8 million people, roughly 32% of the population, require humanitarian assistance in 2023. The ongoing conflict has exacerbated an already dire situation. Not only have new areas been affected, but previously underserved regions also face indirect impacts, such as the closure of healthcare facilities, scarcity of commodities and resources, and attacks on medical services. Moreover, over 700,000 people have been displaced within the country, adding pressure on already strained health facilities, particularly in Northern, Gadaref, Kassala, Red Sea, and Gaziea states. Khartoum, with the highest number of people in need (2,104,057), encounters challenges accessing care due to insecurity and extensive infrastructure damage. North Darfur, hosting the majority of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in Sudan (61%), faces similar healthcare challenges, with the fourth-highest number of people in need (674,266). In Blue Nile, long-standing conflict has resulted in mass displacement, increasing the needs of both displaced and host communities. Prior to the conflict, both North Darfur and Blue Nile ranked poorly in terms of healthcare access and affordability. However, during the conflict, Khartoum faced the greatest challenges accessing care, as per the May 2023 national health cluster report. An assessment conducted in January 2023 revealed significant deficiencies in infection prevention and control (IPC) services. Handwashing facilities with water and soap were present in only 25% of Blue Nile's health facilities compared to 65.4% in North Darfur. Waste disposal capabilities were also lacking, with only 25% of Blue Nile's facilities and 50% of North Darfur's facilities equipped for proper waste management. Essential infection prevention supplies were insufficient, contributing to a higher risk of health facility-acquired infections. Access to quality, safe, and patient-centered care remains a challenge in both states. Staffing shortages were evident, with only 50% of Blue Nile's facilities and 38.5% of North Darfur's facilities having all listed staff present during assessments. Privacy for patients during outpatient consultations was limited, and basic outpatient equipment was lacking in most facilities. Reproductive health services faced challenges, with no designated basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC) facilities having all the necessary supplies in Blue Nile, and only 22.2% in North Darfur. Basic equipment and supplies for the expanded program on immunization (EPI) were insufficient, hindering coverage. The infrastructure of health facilities posed barriers to providing safe, patient-centered, and inclusive care, with only a fraction having permanent structures, separate rooms for different services, power sources for nighttime service delivery, and functional vehicles for emergency referrals. Disease outbreaks, including malaria, dengue, and hepatitis E, occur annually, straining the healthcare system and further impeding access to care. Seasonal flooding exacerbates these challenges by disrupting transportation to healthcare facilities. The facility-based disease surveillance system covers only 40% of health facilities, leading to delays in detecting and responding to epidemic-prone diseases. RI’s April 2023 assessment conducted in Wad Madani locality in Al Jazira state indicated that in the main town of Madani, there are 37 HFs of which the SMoH has designated 5 as referral sites for affected communities. Of the 15 visited camps during RI’s assessment in Wad Madani, only two had health services through mobile clinics.

2 - Activities/Outputs

RI will support 34 health facilities in North Darfur (8 HFs in El-Fashir, 5 HFs in Mellit, 5in Saraf Omra, I in Kalemando and 4 HFs in Al-Malha) , Blue Nile states (2 HFs in AL Rosaries, 1HF in Gissan and 2 HFs in Damazine), Al Jazira (4 in Wad Madani) and 2 health facilities in Khartoum in one locality to be determined as soon as security allow . These clinics will provide integrated timely life-saving basic primary health care services in line with the HRP 2023 and the health cluster strategic objectives. These services include general consultations, reproductive health, immunization and EWARS, while mainstreaming GBV interventions through CMR and referrals of GBV survivors and PSEA. In order to strengthen and improve quality of RH services, RI will train midwives (both classroom and on job), procure and distribute clean delivery kits (CDKs) and long lasting insecticide treated mosquito nets(LLINs) to pregnant women. EPI services will be strengthened through supporting EPI campaigns including outreaches with transportation of vaccines and vaccinators, providing trainings and incentives for vaccinators and maintenance of the cold chain. In addition, RI will support staff capacity building and community outreach, integrating health, hygiene promotion and GBV messaging. In coordination with UNFPA, RI will train the local community volunteers in both states on GBV prevention/PSEA protection messaging and survivor identification and referral for both clinical and protection services. In addition, RI will procure, store and distribution of essential medicines and medical supplies including PPEs for infection prevention and control and PEP kits for clinical management of rape (CMR), and will expand and maintain disease surveillance system with early warning component and conduct awareness / orientation sessions for health facility and community based staff.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI aims at promoting equitable access to health services which ensures that the projects reach the most deprived members of the targeted communities. Nearly all the2023 health indicators are gender-dis-aggregated, and it is expected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to mothers and PLWs through provision of reproductive health services. Privacy and confidentiality of patients will be respected and shall be ensured at all times of service delivery. RI will ensure 24/7 functionality of ambulance service and will deploy staff of both genders who will be able to attend to the unique needs of both genders. Skilled village-based midwives will ensure safe home deliveries are conducted. Community Health Workers(CHW) and village health committees (VHC) will identify people with disabilities and/or the elderly who require home visits for treatment. VHCs will have both men and women representatives; the proposed project shall aim at a 50:50 gender representation. RI will take a socio-inclusion approach and shall ensure that people with disabilities (PwD),persons with mental health challenges, those that are in hard-to-reach locations/villages and those with special needs are able to access the needed health services by working on both supply and demand sides of healthcare services. Service providers will be sensitized on rights and needs of PwDs/ those with special needs; and will be trained on inclusive health services provision looking at the dimensions of gender, age, and abilities. Finally, RI will ensure to enforce zero tolerance for discrimination/stigmatization and lack of respect towards PwD among staff. RI will engage with clan and religious leaders to get their support to spread awareness messages in the community. Working with influential community allies to engage men, boys, women and girls is expected to foster community acceptance for the participation and access to health services of deprived individuals and groups, including PwD, women and girls and the elderly. Timing and location for at-risk groups will be considered to facilitate access to assistance. Medical responses to GBV will offer, through professional support, clinical care, and referral, and will be guided by standards of IASC Guidelines for Integrating GBV Interventions in Humanitarian Action. There will be posters in the HFs describing the services offered at the PHCCs and discouraging any form of abuse. RI will establish a special committee, to discuss women reproductive and child health issues and address within the available resources. RI will use seconded SMoH staff work with populations with unique needs. RI will focus on finding bilingual(Tigray/Arabic) people to help with the communication related challenges to provide the services. RI will ensure setting up COVID-19 prevention measures and work with other service providers to establish referral pathways for SGBV and CP survivors, and will ensure follow up of all cases.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI, and shall maintain a protective environment for the beneficiaries including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receive mandatory training on this policy. Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system, putting a mechanism which enables coordination with the inter-agency to facilitate learning of best practices and collaboration with other partners to comprehensive response, including psycho social support to SGBV survivors. RI will strengthen capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedback to inform project designing, improve service delivery, and enhance program recipients participation in each state of the project management cycle. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program design and adjustment, budget allocations, and future programming.
