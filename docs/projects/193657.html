Project Summary

An already impoverished Palestine refugee population in Gaza faces increased vulnerabilities due to on-going blockade with lengthy closures of the border crossings at Erez and Rafah as well as unpredictable commercial crossing at Karem Shalom and access to the sea. It is further compounded with the increase in prices following the Ukraine Crisis on top of the loss of income due to the aftermath of global COVID-19 pandemic, and the recurrent escalation of hostilities in May 2021 and August 2022. UNRWA continues to provide in-kind food assistance to more than 1 million Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip, covering a part of daily caloric needs, yet the stagnated economy continues to be a burden for most of the refugees.
The population has already been facing years of humanitarian crisis, and many people are relying on daily labour and casual work to secure their minimum requirements when and if available. The families with high dependency ratios, including those with children, older persons and persons with disability, and/or families headed by vulnerable groups are particularly at risk, as they are more likely than others to suffer from poverty or other forms of deprivation. Through this project, UNRWA will provide one-off multi-sectorial cash assistance of US$40 per individual to more than 180,000 vulnerable and cash-strapped Palestine refugees identified through multi-dimensional vulnerability criteria including female-headed households, households headed by older person or an adolescent, and persons with disability.
The project is directly addressing the HRP Strategic Outcome 3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued, and contributing to the 2023 FSS Strategic Objective 2: Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs by providing support through cash assistance.

1 - Needs

Over 15 years of blockade with its restrictions on movement, imports/exports and fishing zones continued to be one of the main factors of economic de-development in Gaza. While people continue to struggle to endure the impact of COVID-19, recurrent hostilities, most recently in August 2022, the socio-economic situation in Gaza was further deteriorated due to the war in Ukraine in 2022 globally affecting the prices of essential needs including food. Unemployment continues to be high as small and medium sized businesses strive to survive. Poor infrastructure, electricity outages, outdated Information Communication Technology (ICT) and scarcity of water all contribute to deteriorating socio-economic and overall living conditions in Gaza. The ongoing salary crisis for civil servant of both the PA and the de facto government adds new layers of the population needing immediate assistance (HNO 2023), and 50.5 per cent of households responded to MSNA 2022 reported that their primary income sources are NGO or charity assistance. Within this context, an already impoverished Palestine refugee population, almost two-thirds of Gaza's population, faces increased vulnerabilities. According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza was 44.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2022. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 65 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2022), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 72 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2022). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. According to OCHA’s oPt Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) findings in Gaza, 60.3 per cent of surveyed Gaza households reported a member of their household being unemployed and looking for work at the time of data collection, and the most frequently reported obstacles to any member of their household finding work, at 64.1 per cent is due to the increased competition and not enough jobs. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering most of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs. The MSNA 2022 also found that there was a widespread reported use of negative coping strategies, showing 56 per cent of households in crisis level and 12 per cent in emergency level. Furthermore, 73.2 per cent of surveyed households in Gaza reported that they had received the assistance of some type in the last six months prior to data collection, yet 79 per cent of households also reported their households having recently taken on debt for any reason in the 3 months prior to data collection (MSNA 2022). Today, 81.5 percent of individuals in Gaza, 71 percent of whom are Palestine refugees, live below the national poverty line (UNRWA-PCBS, 2022: Gaza_15 years of blockade | UNRWA).UNRWA started providing a one-off unconditional cash assistance to the most vulnerable Palestine refugees in 2020 as a part of response to the COVID-19 pandemic in order to mitigate additional socioeconomic hardship. The provision of cash assistance continued in the following years to respond to further deteriorating situation in Gaza, supplementing UNRWA’s in-kind food assistance with a focus on the most vulnerable families.

2 - Activities

To help mitigate socio-economic hardship in 2023, UNRWA will provide unconditional one-off multi-sectoral cash assistance to the cash-strapped Palestine refugees in Gaza suffering from worsened socio-economic situation. The assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable families identified through UNRWA’s multi-dimensional vulnerability criteria, including households headed by females, households headed by older persons or adolescents, and persons with disabilities, and the status of the listed beneficiaries will be further verified by UNRWA Relief and Social workers. UNRWA’s Finance Department will arrange the bank transfer based on the verified list of beneficiaries. SMS will be sent to the targeted beneficiaries when the transfer is completed. A phone number will be included in SMS for beneficiaries to contact in case of any issues or concerns. UNRWA aims to provide support to 45,000 vulnerable refugees per quarter with an annual target of 180,000 Palestine refugees, including 41,031 girls, 41,904 boys, 49,355 women and 47,710 men including 5,400 elderly and 7,200 persons with disabilities. While the area is not a part of the selection criteria, estimated number of beneficiaries per area are: 32,103 in the North Area, 49,956 in Gaza, 34,690 in the Middle Area, 34,463 in Khan Younis, and 28,788 in Rafah.UNRWA has a well-established monitoring mechanism with dedicated Monitoring and Evaluation team who regularly conducts post-distribution monitoring for food distribution. In this project, the M&E team will conduct post distribution monitoring survey and assessment through home visits if the situation allows, otherwise the monitoring will be done remotely. The M&E results and recommendations will be carefully analyzed and considered in the following quarter to continue improving the service provision. As a member of PSEA network, UNRWA commits to all PSEA mandatory activities across its programmes, and the interventions included in this proposal are not exception. UNRWA adopts a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. The PSEA focal points are trained to receive complaints in multitude of ways, including a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints, and a centralized confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints are in place. To ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of PSEA mechanism, PSEA awareness raising material will be disseminated to all project recipients. UNRWA regularly engage with HCT oPt PSEA network, and project staff will engage with UNRWA’s representative to this body via training and through project monitoring.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services andoperations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinianrefugees’ rights. The pandemic and the recurrent escalation resulted in an increase in the protection threatsfacing groups with multiple overlapping vulnerabilities, which includes those at risk ofdomestic and intimate partner violence and the older persons. The risk of sexualexploitation and abuse may have also increased, as evidenced in other crisissituations. The Agency is ensuring that protection principles are mainstreamed andintegrated in the response, and that special attention is paid to the needs of the mostvulnerable. Initiatives to raise awareness on and prevent gender-based violence (GBV)have been launched across all fields. Communication with communities will besupported through dedicated hotlines to provide information on available services. TheAgency has also reviewed guidance from the Inter-Agency Standing Committee onProtection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in the context of emergencies,which will inform the response.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project is targeting the most vulnerable Palestine refugees in the prolonged socioeconomic situation regardless of the targets’ area of residences in the Gaza Strip. Thecash assistance will be transferred to the banks, and the beneficiaries will be able tocollect it from the nearest bank for safe access avoiding unnecessary movement. Thecash support reduces the burdens of poverty while relieving strained families andenhancing their sense of dignity and self-reliance. Agency’s multi-dimensionaleligibility criteria system is created to identify the targeted beneficiaries who are mostvulnerable. This project ensures gender equality in its planning, operations andmonitoring.
