Project Summary

This project (women and girls safe spaces) activities are focusing on villages and communities related to Sarmin and Jandairis sub district. The number of population who need protection services within these locations reached more 41849 according to HNO 2019 plan with an estimated rate of IDPs 31%. The main WGSS centers is located in the city of Sarmin and Jandairis, their activities will reach the other targeted locations through one mobile team in each center after coordination and cooperation with COBs within the targeted locations.
This Project contributes to enhancement of high quality GBV services and measures, and works on putting the suitable services in place to prevent and reduce risks of GBV. Throughout this project, activities will try to make up the shortfall in terms of psychological and social support, where a large segment of women and girls within the targeted locations (Sarmin and Jandairis sub district and their villages and communities) are in urgent need to the projects related activities in order to reactivate their role and live with dignity. Sadad Organization will also work on reaching out to community leaders, representatives and local councils to coordinate and target the beneficiaries according to identifies priorities to the most acute needs.
This project consists of four main interventions:
1. Training of GBV staff on psychosocial support, GBV basic concepts, GBV Standard operating procedures(SOPs),Life Skills and First Psychological Aid.
2. Awareness activities:
Awareness raising activities for girls, women on GBV risks inside WGSS centers in Sarmin and Jandairis including (awareness sessions for women and girls for the prevention and mitigation risks of GBV) in addition, the center two mobile teams will provide the same awareness rising activities outside the centers that will target women, girls, boys and men. Awareness raising sessions will be in line with the awareness raising toolkit developed by the GBV SC 3.
3. Provision of GBV specialized services, prevention and GBV response services to women and adolescent girls survivors or at risk of GBV , through the WGSS centers ; case management (within Sarmin and Jandairis their surrounding villages),Focused PSS for GBV survivors (only) in addition to Individual and group counselling / Focused PSS , Develop/update and disseminate referral pathways and Psychological First Aid (PFA).
4. Dignity kit distribution to women of reproductive age (15 - 49 Yrs) with information on GBV risk mitigation.

Needs

In GBV voices report 2019 In Idleb and Aleppo, Assessments have indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe,the number of population who need protection services within these locations reached more 41849 according to HNO 2019 plan with an estimated rate of IDPs 31%. According to SADAD's experiences in these communities through the implementation of the project Supporting Vulnerable Women and adolescent girls' Resilience through Provision of Quality and Integrated GBV services in Sarmine sub district in 2108, we noted that the length of the crisis and deep-rooted social norms, in conjunction with the mounting lawlessness in some areas, are normalizing this violence, resulting in the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, in the market, at distribution sites, etc.) as well as sexual violence, often associated with kidnapping, is a concern raised by consulted women and girls and contributes to psychosocial stress and limits their movements. Additionally, various means of technology—e.g. texting and social media—are used to sexually harass and facilitate early/forced marriages. The restriction on freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately curtails their rights, with widows who have experienced divorce and widowhood and adolescent girls disproportionately impacted. The shame and stigma surrounding sexual violence means that survivors often do not talk about violence when it happens. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family. Girls are reportedly being married younger, with early/forced marriage- sometimes involving girls of 10 or 11 years old - framed as a protective coping strategy. The socio-economic situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, and increased poverty are ultimately leading to women and girls to be at higher risks of all forms of violence including sexual exploitation and abuse. Adolescent girls and young girls can experience sexual harassment and violence in situations such as when working on the land/ farm, in shops, and in instances when affected by child recruitment.Household level gender roles continue to change with negative implications within families and communities.Although women have more responsibility, this does not infer authority. Women have taken on additional roles traditionally associated with men, including as heads of household and principal breadwinners for their families. This contributes to increased pressure and stress for women to provide for their families as they juggle commitments within the home and workplace. Consequently, they are exposed to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at their workplace. Where GBV services exist, women and girls have confirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with women and girls safe spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational and skill training). As a result, SADAD has designed this project aiming to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria.

Implementation of activities

Sadad Organization will prepare a training plan, activities’ implementation plans and a monitoring plan and will share these with project teams and the GBV staff at the start of the project.The main activities of the project are:Conducting a deep needs assessment (focus groups discussions with women girls, men, and local councils) before preparing the WGSSs centers to introduce them to the importance of the center and the importance of its activities and interventions.Output 1 Provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including case management, psychosocial support, reinforce referral pathways and Psychological First Aid (PFA).Activity 1.1 Establishment Women and Girls safe Space Center (WGSS) in the city of Jandairis and strengthening of current model and provide small-scale preparations to Sarmin center.Activity 1.2 Providing 40 survivors (women and girls) reached through WGSS centers or mobile teams with Case Management in each of Jandairis and Sarmin .Activity 1.3 Providing 18 Trainees, (16 females / 2 males) PSS workers, facilitators, caseworkers and social workers, with capacity building training on Gender Based Violence and standard prevention and response activities.Activity 1.4 providing 30 group and 50 individual PSS sessions with both centers and mobile teams Activity.Activity 1.5: Providing 90 survivors (women and girls) reached through WGSS centers or mobile teams with Psychological First Aid (PFA) in each of Jandairis and Sarmin.Activity 1.6 distributing 400 Dignity kits to women of reproductive age (15 - 49 Yrs) with information on GBV risk mitigation.output 2 Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a particular focus on most at risks groups (for ex. adolescent girls and female headed households, and divorced and widowed women and girls).Activity 2.1 Awareness sessions will be provided for women and adolescent girls inside the centers and the subject of these sessions will be selected with women and girls participation in a safe way to avoid causing them harm.2.2 Providing Life Skills training to 150 Women and 170 adolescent girls survivors or at risk of GBV.2.3 Providing 240 women and 120 adolescent girls survivors or at risk of gender-based violence with Vocational Training.-Meeting and discussing with women and girls to choose the suitable life skills and vocational activities and to know the best time for conducting these activitiesThe canters' mobile teams will conduct activities within the targeted locations through: A Organizing awareness campaigns to target women, girls, men and boys including awareness sessions about domestic violence, early recovery, and the importance of back to school and mitigating GBV risks with distributing leaflets and brochuresB- Home visits including awareness sessions about protection risks mitigation targeting women and adolescent girls.Monitoring and Reporting process will begin with the project design to identify what aims to be achieved during the project and how the projects activities and interventions will be implemented. Also, information against indicators is collected through Sadad data management system coordinated by the central MEAL team. The project will be monitored on a regular basis in order to ensure satisfactory monthly progress and accountability to the beneficiaries including collection of monthly data from beneficiaries, monthly technical reports and regular technical supervision missions fielded as required to enhance success. In addition, photos, videos , FGD and signatures by beneficiaries will be provided.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Lack of skilled personnel in case management and prevention activitiesMitigation: Capacity building and technical supervision will be built on job training.Risk: weakness of inter action of the beneficiaries of activity Mitigation: presentation of safety and information security in the center to the beneficiaries Mitigation: Emphasis on the principle of transparency and confidentiality of information followedRisk :Targeted beneficiaries are unaware of the safe center activities, So there will be announcement for these activities in cooperation between the mobile teams and local councils within the targeted villages.Risks: Cultural background and norms could be sometimes as challenges against the achievement of the outputs. Mitigation: SADAD teams will conduct focus group discussion with all beneficiaries to explain that the activities of this project are not against the norms and culture of the target communitiesRisk :Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response servicesMitigation: GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place.Risk :Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging.Mitigation :Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders.Risk :Backlash from family or commuity on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment .Mitigation : Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities.Risk :Persons with disability have difficult accessing GBV services.Mitigation : Ensure all WGSS staff are properly trained on how to identify persons with disability in need of treatment for a timely referral to relevant services.Risk :Facility targeted by airstrikes- especially when PSS services are integrated in clinics in certain areas .Mitigation :Safety measures for low visibility and underground centres .Risk :Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance.Mitigation : Engage with local authorities and clear communication on project objectives.Risk :Lack of quality services and referral expose girls survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization, reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer girls' disclosures and lack of trust in services.Mitigation :Service mapping including only those that can provide girls' centred services .Risk : Lack of commitment in integrating and enforcing risk mitigation measures.Mitigation :Action plans for risk mitigation interventions.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one of the major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conflict in northern Syria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2- Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries. 3- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensified shelling 4- Daily Risk Assessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding the crowding of beneficiaries during activities. B-Low Quality of Programming/ This includes limitations on direct monitoring, statistical data, percentages, samples and tight data related to gender, age or protection issues etc. This is the setting for a decline in program quality unless remote management tools are accurately employed.
