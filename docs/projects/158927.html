Project Summary

This project is a roll over from 2019 HRP project HPSE19-SHL-150603-1. The overarching goal of this project is to better equip communities, prioritizing the most vulnerable IDPs, with the tools and systems they need to manage and respond to the needs related to displacements in urban settings. This project will be carried out in close coordination with IDPs Working Groups, it will support local authorities and communities and it will linki municipal forums to the MoSD and humanitarian actors’ community outreach structure. The project will have two main pillars:
1. Increasing local authorities’ emergency risk management knowledge, including in case of urban displacement
2. Conducting municipal forums in order to build awareness about the existing humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza.
The project will build the capacities of 4,480 beneficiaries including vulnerable households, municipal forums members, CBOs members, MoSD & municipalities’ staff involved in community risk mitigation measures and emergency preparedness. In addition, 200 HH in living in areas surrounding the Designated Emergency Shelters (DES) will be provided with multi-purpose cash to restore their NFIs and purchase hygiene items, in order to be able to accommodate IDPs families in case of any displacement to their areas.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip has witnessed three destructive wars in the past ten years. Five years after the last conflict in 2014, which lasted for 51 days, over 3,000 families, comprising 16,500 individuals, are still displaced based on the latest Shelter Cluster factsheet of August, 2018. After the closure of the Collective Centers (CCs) in June 2015, the IDPs caseload is fully scattered in urban and out of camp settings. In such displacement settings, there is no clear roles and responsibilities defined among humanitarian actors and emergency responders. Gaps in communication and field coordination mechanisms were identified alongside the lack of information from and representation of the vulnerable and displaced population. NRC has invested in filling these gaps through the introduction of Urban Displacement and Outside Camps “UDOC” approach in the Gaza Strip. UDOC is component of the Camp Management (CM) Core Competency aiming to facilitate the access of the affected communities to protection and basic services. The UDOC team established representation and 􀂡eld coordination structures, municipal forums, and commenced the development of the Urban Displacement Response guidelines and tools.These efforts are ongoing as there are continues needs to strengthen the established municipal forums, as well as adapt and mainstream the Urban Displacement Response guidelines and tools Municipal forums are representation structures established through the UDOC intervention. Those forums include representatives encompassing youth, women, community leaders, local CBOs, the municipality, and IDPs themselves. Municipal forums have agreed to and endorsed TORs from the municipality with whom they are working. Municipal forums act as an area based coordination structure that supports thetwo way communication between the affected population and all other stakeholders involved in the humanitarian services. Linking Municipal forums with clusters focal points in different governorates will reflect community’s needs and priorities in clusters coordinated efforts to respond to these needs and priorities. Municipal forums can also support in services mapping in their areas. The latest humanitarian services’ mapping conducted by NRC in Rafah during September 2018 emphasized the lack of knowledge among the potential affected communities about emergency services and mitigation measures. This reality also was also emphasized in Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) conducted in June 2017 with communities at risk of displacement, whereas 67% of the participants in those FDGs stated that the information they hold about how to act in emergency not helpful enough. Furthermore, the latest IDP vulnerability assessment, published in June 2018 by NRC, revealed increased risks of GBV amongst displaced families as stated by 49% of the assessed families. In addition, a similar FGD from September 2018 clearly articulated that vulnerable households are depleting their coping mechanisms by selling furniture and NFI items. If this trend continues, the host community’s ability to receive and accommodate IDPs will be jeopardized. Therefore, community representative structures (municipal forums, CBOs & committees including women & youth) in the displacement prone and receiving areas need to be strengthened through the proposed trainings and cash support to be able to accommodate IDP families and provide adequate referrals. Building the capacities of municipalities and local communities to respond to humanitarian needs during and post emergencies whether inside collective centers or in urban settings is a clear need. Communities and local authorities need to better understand humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza including how to reach the displaced population, services and capacities of the DES and how they can support displaced population in host communities.

2 - Activities

NRC is currently leading the development of tools and guidelines needed to respond to urban displacement, in close cooperation with MoSD and OCHA. To build up on this expertise, NRC proposes to expand coverage through providing tailored training on the Urban Displacement Caseload Management to the staff of the big 5 municipalities in the Gaza Strip and MoSD staff at governorate level. Afterwards, the trained staff of the big five municipalities and MoSD will be linked with municipal forums, neighborhood committees, CBOs and community groups at different areas. These links will be established through a series of workshops attended by the staff and community representatives. These links will enable municipalities and MoSD staff to easily access information about the needs of IDPs in urban settings as they can contact community focal points in certain areas. The focus will be given to areas adjacent to the currently identified DESs as these neighborhoods will be seen as safe refuge to the affected population and will potentially receive higher proportion for the IDPs. Furthermore, due to deteriorating socio-economic conditions in the Gaza Strip, it is expected that host communities would be overwhelmed if they receive IDP families in their premises.Therefore, this intervention will prepare host communities with the tools to provide adequate support to IDPs during emergencies. Though the multi-purpose cash assistance that would be provided to purchase NFI and Hygiene kits, families will be able to restore their resilience and ability to host IDPs. In addition, trainings for local authorities and CBOs will strengthen capacity to support host communities through the provision of awareness sessions and trainings on how to collect and raise vulnerability information during emergencies; finalization of management guidelines for Urban Displaced; and wide dissemination of the developed management toolkit. Moving from this perspective, NRC UDOC is proposing to provide municipal forums and community groups with training on the humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza, including clusters’ coordinated services so that they can identify vulnerable cases and link them to service providers and/or cluster members. These trainings will also jointly target MoSD staff at governorate level in order to link them with their respective communities and CBOs. Activities of knowledge sharing and capacity building will create a roster of community groups that can be easily accessed by the humanitarian actors to seek information about the affected population, or to convey certain messages to the affected communities. UDOC will share this roster with clusters’ members based on their geographical area of work. Trainings will be followed up by awareness sessions to the host communities, where 200 vulnerable households in the potential host communities will be prioritized for multi-purpose cash assistance. NRC’s staff will provide coaching and mentoring to ensure that these capacities are utilized and sustained. Additionally, NRC will support the big five municipalities to design and print guidelines and maps illustrating safe shelters and procedures during emergencies. This will be done under IDPs WG supervision and in collaboration with the shelter cluster. All activities will be implemented under the umbrella of the OCHA-led IDP working group and in support of MoSD

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In order to prioritize safety and dignity of vulnerable families and communities over other considerations, UDOC-Emergency will conduct protection risk assessments (Do no Harm), adopt a conflict and context sensitive approach in dealing with beneficiaries, ensure informed beneficiary consent and acting on instructions of beneficiaries, securing confidentiality and data protection. In addition, NRC will use additional protection technical expertise to supplement its interventions by coordinating and referring cases to specialized protection actors.NRC’s Protection Policy, Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and guidance notes and tools including Core Competency Quality Assessment tools and the Protection Resource Pack all contribute to reinforcing and providing guidance on how to action the protection policy. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners and associates (e.g. contractors, suppliers) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. At the community level, UDOC-Emergency will support multi-sectoral, sustainable, and integrated programming with all NRC’s Core Competencies and with other humanitarian actors, which will allow people to preserve or increase their resources and opportunities, thus making them more resilient, and more likely to find durable solutions. UDOC, Shelter and ICLA will ensure community engagement on different levels of implementation and echoing community feedback. NRC will support OCHA in the assessment and dissemination of information in order to ensure coordinated prevention and response to emergency. NRC will actively and regularly participate in the shelter and protection Cluster, Emergency preparedness team led by OCHA, co-leading with UNRWA communication with communities working group under ICCG and collaborate with other Clusters and actors (e.g. UNRWA) on awareness raising, sensitisation and information dissemination activities to ensure that people have meaningful access to information and feedback mechanisms. NRC Advocacy unit will seek to maintain the humanitarian situation of Gaza on the international agenda in an effort to shape priorities and address the impact of the blockade through an evidence-based approach with the consent of the individuals involved, while upholding the do no harm principle.NRC implements projects considering preserving dignity of beneficiaries, and their preferences, as well as minimizing protection concerns and issues during distribution of assistance.NRC provides awareness raising sessions to community members on protection and gender aspects during displacements to enhance their knowledge and thus access to humanitarian services to meet their basic needs.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NRC programs are designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to-reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, to reduce their vulnerability and build resilience.In addition, NRC UDOC develops the selection criteria based on community’s consultation andin accordance with shelter cluster vulnerability prioritization and scoring guidelines. Specific vulnerabilities such as elderly, children, disabilities etc.. is considered during the beneficiary selection.NRC delivers its assistance guided by humanitarian principles (humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence) and its mission is to protect the rights of displaced and vulnerable persons during crises.
