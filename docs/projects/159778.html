Project Summary

According to the cluster needs analyses 2020, Globally, Somalia scores the highest on the INFORM Global Risk Index due to long-standing political instability, armed conflict, environmental stress, and weak governance structures. Despite political progress and ongoing efforts to strengthen government institutions, the Ministry of Education (MoE) continues to lack the capacity to deliver basic education services for IDPs, children living in areas with ongoing conflict and other groups living under the most challenging circumstances. This results in 1.37 million crisis-affected children (691,295 girls) being in need of education assistance during 2020. The crisis in Somalia has two primary drivers, conflict and natural disasters. According to the Protection Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), 270,000 people were displaced in Somalia between January and August 2019, of which 141,000 were displaced due to conflict and 112,000 due to drought. Of the people displaced this year, an estimated 108.000 (40%) are school-aged children. In total, there are about 2.6 million displaced people in Somalia and an estimated 1 million of these are school-aged children.
Mercy Corps internal assessments also concur with the cluster needs analyses and this project supports the Education cluster 2020 priorities and the MoE area ESSP (2018-2020). The project will address access and retention barriers to education for children affected by crises and emergencies. The project goal is to enhance access to education for children affected by emergencies and crises in Galgadud (Cadado, Cabudwaq, ), Mudug (Galkacyo, ), and in Marodirjeh (Hargeisa) regions. Mercy Corps has in the past successfully implemented Education in Emergencies services and WASH and livelihood projects in most of the regions and has a strong presence and community acceptance. The project targets to provide direct educational support to 3894 displaced and none displaced students in 15 schools within the target regions. This support will not only improve access to education but also ensure a protective learning environment for both girls and boys in the target regions. The project seeks to address the negative effects of hunger through school feeding during school days in the 15 targeted schools. School feeding will retain children in school; boost their ability to concentrate during class lessons.
In addition, the project seeks to support emergency water supply in targeted schools through the installation of water systems. Access to water and sanitation facilities will create a safe, conducive and protective learning environment for children. Similarly, the project will undertake the installation of temporary learning spaces and rehabilitation of school blocks to improve the learning environment. Moreover, the project will put deliberate efforts to integrate protection measures in its intervention strategy. In this regard, the project will seek to address child protection needs in schools through conducting awareness-raising, psychosocial activities in child-friendly spaces and enabling community structures able to prevent and respond to child abuse.
The wellbeing of the child will be enhanced through supporting children with scholastic materials necessary during learning, motivating teachers through training and teacher incentives. The project also aims at strengthening the capacity of the education system to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies, preparedness and response within the education system through teacher training, the establishment of Community Education Committees (CECs) and strengthening regional education cluster systems. In all these interventions, the project will pay particular attention to school-aged children living with disabilities and ensure response interventions are designed to enhance their access and inclusion to education.

Project Activities

According to cloister needs analyses 2020, Somalia still remains among countries with the highest global out of school children rates as a result of a weakened education infrastructures and systems due to decades of neglect, conﬂicts and recurring national disasters such as the drought that culminated in widespread population displacement and the paralysis of social services including Education. Education needs in Somalia are tremendous. Addressing these needs and ensuring all children are enrolled in a safe and protective learning environment with a good quality of teaching requires a long term systemic effort. Mercy Corps will align its strategy with that of the cluster on two strategic approaches including retention of crisis-affected school children and increased access to education for vulnerable displaced children and children in host communities. project focuses on schools as an entry point for life-saving assistance to vulnerable children and is integrating the provision of WASH and protection services at the school level. The project will enhance the focus on the protective role of education and integrate child protection activities at the school level. The project will further pay increased attention to school-aged children living with disabilities and response interventions will be designed to enhance their access and inclusion to education. Where and when possible, the project will prioritize the most sustainable solutions and engage with development actors to bridge the humanitarian-development divide. This includes activities to strengthen the capacity of partners and MoE staff in response planning and coordination. The project response is aligned with the MoE Education Sector Strategic Plans and is aiming to complement the efforts of the government to strengthen the capacity of the education system to respond to the impact of the crisis. To meet the identifies need, the following activities are planned to implement. Classes should take place in daylight hours giving children sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes1 Consult women, men, boys, girls, adolescents, children with disabilities, teachers and parents to understand their needs and preferences for school location, design and education services. 2 Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response 3 Talk to girls and boys separately about the barriers they face and hold discussions with groups of children that could face added difficulties.4 Collect and use data dis aggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming. 5 Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and priorities the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups (refer to Vulnerable Categories guidance note).  4d.
