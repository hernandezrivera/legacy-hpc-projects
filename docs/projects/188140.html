Project Summary

This project (ongoing project (secure) ) was designed in synergy with another ERL partner and basing some of the proposal intervention on the other partner's need assessments with whom you collaborate both at HQ and field level.
To provide Apprenticeship Socio-Economic Recovery opportunities to the affected individuals and families in Idleb ( Bennsh, Idlib, Qah, kefr tkharim) and Aleppo ( Jarablus, Ghandorah, Azaz)
WSA will focus on including PWDs and with the aim of creating a supportive environment for vulnerable groups including women and PWDs through the provision of ERL activities.
From the implementation of the first phase of ERL partner, apprenticeship Socio-Economic recovery program, it was clear the gap in the participation of women and people with PWDs as participants of this type of project was. At the same time as protection and GBV specialized service providers, WSA has always received case where beneficiaries (women and PWDs) had the need of economic empowerment and where not always able to find a relevant actor to refer to provide this service in a protection-sensitive approach.
Project activities will :
Provision of vocational training based on market needs assessment conducted by ERL partner, FGDs, and community participation. The topics covered in those training will avoid the traditional types of vocational training provided by different protection/GBV and ER actors and will include for examples training on soft skills like accounting, project management, photography, and online marketing. PWDs vocational direction intervention to link the PWDs with the appropriate training and this activity will be conducted in WSA’s WGSS. This activity will target 1000 women and man with a minimum of 5 % of women with disabilities.
• Provision of small grants and inclusive livelihood opportunities for women including women with disabilities in WGSS with high priority for GBV survivors and women at risk to achieve economic independence in a total of 480 from the BNFs who participated in vocational training in previous projects including referrals from other GBV case management service providers in the project locations to receive a grant of (800 – 1200 USD) per. Some the 520 beneficiaries will still have the opportunity to be referred to ERL partner, to receive their support in accessing the market based on the knowledge and skills they gained from the vocational training and based on the selection criteria of ERL partner, project that gives the priority for women referred from this project.
* in 2022 WSA will target 600 beneficiaries ( 420 W, 180 M ) for vocational training, them 280 beneficiaries ( 230 W+50 M )will receive small grants.
in 2023 WSA will target 400 beneficiaries ( 280 W, 120 M ) for vocational training, them 280 beneficiaries ( 140 W+60 M )will receive small grants.
WSA has received funds (510,000 USD) from the SCHF for planned activities in 2022 for two projects, (TUR-21/3559/SA2/FS-ER/NGO/20842 ) and (TUR-21/3559/SA2/ER-P/NGO/20730 ).

4 - Needs

Early Recovery and Livelihoods needs continue to rise. Expectations of a further increase in the cost of living, including, reduction of subsidies, and general prices increases as well as volatility in the exchange rate, among others, are projecting onto an additional deterioration in terms of real income and purchasing power across all population groups. the sector foresees the income/expenditure gap to continue increasing in the coming year across all population groups. Those most affected by a significant deterioration have been IDPs out of camps (22 per cent) and residents (20 per cent), while those most affected by deteriorations to some extend have been returnees and IDP in camps. Across all population groups (for 88 per cent of household among IDPs, residents, and returnees), primary reasons limiting the ability to meet basic needs have been the loss/insufficient income and the lack of opportunities to access income sources, reaching a peak for IDPs in camps (97 per cent). Furthermore, for 90 per cent of females and 87 per cent of males, the loss/insufficient income, and the lack of opportunities to access income sources constitute their first concern vis-à-vis physical and mental wellbeing, followed by the unaffordability of food and other essential items. Borrowing, debt increase, and loss of income as the main causes for debt change, present high and extremely comparable trends among IDPs, residents, and returnees, indifferently of the group. . 5 - Implementation of activities

1000 Vulnerable households/HH (70 % FHH)will enhance business skills through attending vocational training. Moreover,480 Vulnerable HHs(out of the selected 1000 HH) will increase their levels of income.The targeted HHs will be supported by business assets through financial (grants/micro-credit) and in-kind capital for both start-up and recovery. Each HHs will receive small grant with value of 1000 USDSince these funds are in the form of grants and provide funding for specific purposes, thus, these funds will be determined based on proposals from selected beneficiaries for funding. In another word, having different proposals can justify the rationale behind the difference in business grantsIn addition, the business skills of targeted HHs will be enhanced in topics related to market and business management. The small business initiatives that will be supported are home sewing, hairdresser, clothing trade, shoe making workshop, library.etc.The exact numbers that will be supported from each small business type be accurately determined in consultation with targeted HHs and based on the results of market assessment. It' worth mentioning that the project will encourage group approach, which will priorities the business that presented by group of entrepreneurs.The value of the proposed grant has been determined based on rapid market assessment conducted by WAS and NKO in the targeted locations, and the coordination will be at the local authority level in NW Syria and ERL-cluster

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will priorities the most vulnerable persons(males and females including IDPs), considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with PWDs or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project. Moreover, the project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups and not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. WSA will provide GBV awareness sessions and thus, protection services and rights' assisting those targeted females will be mainstreaming during this project by WSA team targeting those beneficiaries with awareness sessions about strengthening self-protection mechanisms, rights' . Females will be employed within the team to consider community sensitivity and separation between female and male beneficiaries during distributionsWSA will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach to integrate conflict sensitivity in project activities. The feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them. The mechanism will be easy to use and impartially accessible by all including the marginalized and illiterate people. Ensure that vulnerable groups and individuals are able to access services equally and without any discrimination.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on WSA needs assessment conducted within the targeted locations, WSA will priorities the most vulnerable persons to receive services during this project. However, they may face risks of exploitation by service providers or by another actor in the community and WSA will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Ensure participants know services were to disclose if violence occurs 2. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and how to report 3. Establish good relations with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them. 4. ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all. 5. train all staff and contractors on PSEA CoD. Also, the project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups, not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. Starting with the project's first needs assessments and during the whole project period, WSA will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach to integrate conflict sensitivity wherever the feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them. It will be confirmed that the service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against beneficiaries. Apply the principle of “different needs equal opportunities.”


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A- IEDs/VBIEDs/PBIEDs/ Can be direct or indirect. It is one of the major threats in Syria, Mitigation measures: 1- Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activates. 2- Commitment to all humanitarian principles, especially neutrality and impartiality to avoid Targeting. 3- Continuing coordination with the Security and Safety department during staff movement B- The intervention of Armed groups in humanitarian action/ Its common risk, to obstruct humanitarian action (blackmail) Mitigation measures: 1-The organization's obligation not to provide any information about the donor to any military faction interventions and other related information. 2-WSA policy is clear, do not to make a concession. 3-Coordination with civilian local authorities and Turkish government. C- Fluctuation of Dollar rate and issues of money transfer, Mitigation: 1- WSA is using OWNDA &QUICK BOOKS tools of cash monitoring and follow-up. 2-Sending money to Syria: the money will be transferred to vendors in Syria by Ptt. therefore, all vendors must have accounts in the Ptt. D-Lost of Access: The areas of project are located in relatively safe areas, and WSA has full access and full approval from the related directorates , in case of change of the controlling authorities , the alternative plans will depend on the phase of the project reached , and the action plan implemented and achieved , all beneficiaries will be trained and oriented on all S&S procedures and mitigation measures how to.
