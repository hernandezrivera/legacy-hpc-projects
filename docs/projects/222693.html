Project Summary

This project aims to address the pressing challenges of acute food insecurity that affect over 50% of the population in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Pibor, South Sudan. These regions, plagued by food shortages, climate shocks, and economic instability, have been pushed into IPC Phase 4 or worse, necessitating urgent intervention. By integrating life-saving measures with resilience-building initiatives, the project seeks to empower vulnerable communities, reduce dependence on external aid, and enhance overall food security.
This initiative aims to save lives and strengthen community resilience through emergency food assistance and improved livelihood opportunities. It seeks to address immediate needs while promoting sustainable solutions that bolster local capacities.
To enhance food access, the project will provide conditional cash transfers to 1,500 farmers to access quality seeds and tools, while delivering food assistance and vouchers to 1,500 individuals facing extreme food insecurity.
Efforts to boost agricultural productivity and resilience include the distribution of nutrient-dense vegetable, dry and main season agricultural kits to 60 farmer groups.
The project also emphasizes sustainable livelihoods by training 60 farmer groups in post-harvest management, integrated pest control, and enterprise development. Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLAs) will be facilitated to enhance social cohesion, financial inclusion and strengthen local economies. Support for fisheries through the provision of equipment and technical training, will diversify income sources and improve food security.
The project targets 1,500 farmers comprising of (600 men and 900 women) and 152 fishermen/women (100 men and 52 women). With a total budget of $200,000, funds will be allocated to personnel, procurement, transport, and project support costs, emphasizing agricultural inputs, capacity-building activities, and cash transfers to ensure sustainable food security and livelihoods.
Aligned with South Sudan’s Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) 2025, this project aims to reduce reliance on humanitarian aid, enhance household resilience, and improve food security and livelihood outcomes. Its gender-sensitive and inclusive approaches ensure the engagement and empowerment of all community members.
This intervention is poised to significantly reduce hunger while empowering communities to withstand climatic and economic shocks. By fostering sustainable livelihoods and improving local food production systems, the project will lay a strong foundation for long-term resilience and recovery in the affected regions.

1. Needs

Over 50% of the population in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Pibor is currently in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse, with the situation expected to deteriorate further during the lean season. Across South Sudan, 63,000 people are projected to face IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) conditions between April and July 2025, putting them at significant risk of starvation and death without immediate assistance. Vulnerable returnees further strain local food systems, with 31,000 in IPC Phase 5 and 220,000 in IPC Phase 4, highlighting their critical need for support.Malnutrition rates are alarmingly high. Among children aged 6–59 months, 2.1 million are acutely malnourished, including 650,000 with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.43 million with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Pibor bear a significant portion of this burden, placing children at severe risk of illness and death without urgent intervention. Additionally, 1.1 million pregnant and breastfeeding women are malnourished, endangering both maternal and infant health. Across 53 counties, malnutrition has reached IPC AMN Phase 3 or above, with 39 counties in Phase 4 (Critical). Lean season projections indicate further deterioration in 62 counties, with Baliet County in Upper Nile expected to reach IPC AMN Phase 5 (Extremely Critical).Climate-induced vulnerabilities compound these challenges. Recurring floods destroy crops, displace communities, and increase the prevalence of diseases, significantly worsening food insecurity. Pibor is especially affected, with flooding undermining agricultural productivity and market accessibility. During the lean season, the depletion of food stocks, reliance on high-cost market supplies, and poor infrastructure further exacerbate food insecurity.Agricultural and economic challenges are deeply entrenched. Traditional farming methods, limited access to quality seeds, and insufficient tools continue to hinder agricultural yields. Market reliance, combined with high food prices and economic instability, reduces purchasing power, leaving many households unable to meet their basic food needs. The spillover effects of conflicts, including those from neighboring Sudan, exacerbate food security challenges, particularly in border regions such as Pibor.Poor WASH conditions and limited healthcare access exacerbate malnutrition and disease. With 74% of the population practicing open defecation, waterborne diseases like diarrhea are rampant, further compounding malnutrition risks. The lack of adequate healthcare facilities and services in these areas undermines efforts to manage malnutrition and treat related diseases effectively.Returnees remain highly vulnerable, forming a significant portion of the population in crisis. Over 85.1% of returnees are in IPC Phase 3 or worse, placing immense pressure on local food systems and social services. Their displacement further amplifies the urgency of addressing humanitarian needs in these regions.The combination of acute food insecurity, widespread malnutrition, climate shocks, and inadequate healthcare and WASH infrastructure creates a perfect storm of humanitarian crises in Aweil North, Aweil East, Aweil South, and Pibor. Without timely intervention, mortality rates will increase, especially among children under five and pregnant or breastfeeding women. Vulnerable populations will face heightened risks of starvation and disease, and local food systems will further collapse, deepening reliance on humanitarian aid. This proposal responds to these pressing needs directly and through leveraging synergy and integrated approach, combining emergency aid with resilience-building efforts to save lives, enhance food security, and empower communities for sustainable development.

2. Project activities

• Conditional cash distribution: 1500 farmers from 60 groups receive cash value of 60,000$ for seeds and farm tools.• Distribution of dry season agricultural kits (VK, FK): Distribute dry season Vegetable Seeds (VK); 20 g amaranth, collard, eggplant, onion, tomato, 30 g watermelon, pumpkin, and 50 g okra to each of 60 farmer groups.• Distribution of main season agricultural kits (CK, VK, FK): Distribute main season crop kits (CK) 5 kg maize; o 5 kg sorghum; 3 kg cowpea; vegetable kits (VK) and Fishing Kits (FK) twins, hooks, and cooler box to 12 fishing groups from 12 targeted Payams. • Training of 60 farmer groups on good agronomic practices.i.e post-harvest management, demo plots, Integrated Pest Management (e.g. FAW, Desert locusts, Pesticides etc), ox ploughs.• Establish 60 farmer field demonstration sites• Support to fisheries: Train 12 fishing groups on artisan net making, harvesting, preservation, and marketing.• Seed Fairs (Input Trade Fairs): Conduct 8 seed fairs. 2 fairs per County• Village Savings/Loans Associations : Facilitate and train 60 VSLAs from the 60 farmer groups.• Train 60 farmer groups on enterprise development. Vocational & other enterprise/business-related support
