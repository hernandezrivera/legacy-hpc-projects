Project Summary

In this project, TF will provide critical GBV prevention and mitigation services to vulnerable women, men, girls and boys among returnees, newly IDPs, and non-displaced but affected host communities through right based, participatory and integrated approach. The project will be implemented in hard to reach and priority counties of Cueibet, Lainya, Budi, Yei, Rumbek Centre, Magwi, and Kajokeji Counties where GBV risks are high. TF will implement SASA and EMAP toolkits in its community based GBV prevention and mitigation. Women and Men Groups (Community Based Protection Networks) at Boma Level will receive training on how to implement the two toolkits at local level through engaging one another on the role communities play in causing and preventing GBV. TF staffs are well versed with the tools and will undergo refresher training prior to commencing the intervention. Referrals of GBV cases will be made to service providers through existing referral pathways. Women girl friendly spaces will be established for at risk women and girls to access GBV information and engage in structured activities. Women Girl Friendly Spaces (WGFS) will also be used for distribution of hygiene kits and training adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene management. Women and girls Trainer of Trainers will receive training on Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES) as part of risk mitigation. Due to high level and concentration of protection needs in these locations, TF project staffs will be based in targeted location with the communities to better understand their pressing protection needs and coping mechanisms, help affected communities come up with alternative protection durable solutions, train CBPNs on resilience-enhancing messaging such as Housing Land and Property (HLP). The project prioritizes safety, dignity, non-discrimination, inclusive participation and encouraging complaints and feedback. A key focus for this intervention is AAP through CFM embedded in project activities. CFM will be established upon contact with the affected community and during implementation process to receive, acknowledge and address complaints. Protection is mainstreamed through safety audits, sharing GBV information and referrals, and training women and girls on FES. The project is integrated with other sectors for cost effectiveness and holistic approach in intervention. For example: TF already has FSL Funding covering Yei and Kajokeji counties from SSHF and Diakonie Katastrophilfe lasting until March and December 2020 respectively. GBV Survivors, at risk women and girls will be linked to the intervention for livelihood training, provision of vegetable, crop and fishing kits to boost their capacity to produce own food for home use and selling. Also, TF has secured Shelter and Non-food Items from SSHF for Cueibet and Kajokeji Counties. At risk women and girls will be prioritized for provision of S/NFIs materials such as cooking sets, serving spoons, plastic sheets, mosquito nets and kanga. GBV staffs will participate in Joint Missions with Nutrition partners (ARC) in Kajokeji, GREDO (in Budi) to deliver GBV messaging, distribute dignity kits to women and girls and conduct life skills training at Nutrition outreach and stabilization centres. GBV staffs will work with TF’s, UMCOR, Plan, GREDO, and VSF-Germany FSL teams in Rumbek Centre, Budi, Kajokeji, Magwi, Cueibet and Yei to introduce the concept of FES to women farming groups. In locations at risk of Cholera, Ebola and HIV and AIDS, TF staffs will work closely with health partners (ARC, TERM) and IOM to deliver lifesaving health messages. Protection partners will be engaged during safety audits, HLP messaging, GBV referrals and while implementing Cash Based Intervention (CBI) activities to mitigate tension and potential violence as a result of CBI. In total, 11428 (8650 F) individuals will be reached through the project. Out of the 11428 targeted beneficiaries, 8600 are IDPs, 1828 Vulnerable Host community and 1000 returnees.

1 - Needs

61% of Displaced Persons in South Sudan are women and children, who have fewer coping mechanisms to mitigate GBV risks. Women and girls continue to suffer grave violation of their rights including killing, rape, early child marriage, and abduction. GBV cases continue to be under-reported by survivors due to stigma, shame and fear. According GBV-IMS, approximately 98% of reported GBV incidents in South Sudan affect women and girls. In addition to gender, age and disability increase risks to GBV, with adolescent girls, unaccompanied children, elderly women and persons with disabilities among the populations most at risk. Gender inequality exacerbated by armed conflict, social, cultural and economic factors, low confidence in the rule of law, limited service availability, and reliance on informal justice structures are key root causes and consequences of this human rights and public health issue. The conflict has amplified gender inequalities and sustained traditional gender roles, negative social norms and cultural practices, such as child and forced marriages with serious consequences on girls. According to a Global Initiative Report (2019) on out of school children in South Sudan, displaced families living as IDPs and refugees are increasingly forcing/encouraging their daughters to get married in exchange for cattle and money. This violates the rights of the girls, denies them education and a future they are entitled to. A Joint research (No Simple Solutions-Women, displacement and Durable Solutions in South Sudan) conducted by OXFAM, TITI FOUNDATION, DRC, NRC, Nile Hope, and Care International in Equatoria Region, Lake States, Upper Nile and Jonglei has noted increased fear among displaced families in sending girls to places of origin fearing they will be targeted by armed groups. In the month of June (2019), the UN Secretary General report (Children in Armed Conflict) for South Sudan has documented grave violations on children in Equatoria Region even after the signing of Peace Agreement in September 2019. According to the report, 72 children, including 1 boy, were subjected to sexual violence, 33 of whom were under 15 years of age and 8 of whom were gang-raped during the reporting period. An IRNA report for Kajokeji (June, 2019) noted serious protection concerns. During FGDs with the women and girls, it emerged 7 incidences of rape and attempted rape had occurred in Kajokeji. The number is expected to be higher since most rape cases go unreported due to stigma associated with reporting, weak referral pathway, fear, lack of services to manage rape cases and experienced case and health care workers. This has greatly compromised GBV reporting, access to GBV information and services thus subjecting GBV survivors to greater protection risks, stigma, trauma and possible repeat abuses. Protection risks facing women and girls are also amplified due to lack of trained and equipped community protection structures that are supposed to challenge negative gender norms, prevent, mitigate and respond to SGBV at community level. Women and girls also lack friendly spaces they can utilize to support each other, access GBV information, advocate for GBV services and protection. In contested spaces such as Yei and Kajokeji where presence of armed groups is prevalent, women and girls walking long distances into the forest to fetch firewood have reported increased exposure to gender violence and abuse often perpetrated by armed groups roaming the forests. This when coupled by lack of skills and knowledge on making and use of Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES) has amplified GBV risks. As a result of conflict, markets and supply routes have been disrupted in deeper and hard to reach areas of Magwi, Budi, Kajokeji, Yei, Lainya and Cueibet making it difficult for women to access Hygiene kits forcing them to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as using ash, soil, dirty rags to manage menses with serious negative impact on women and girls health.

2 - Activities

ACTIVITY 1.1: Preliminary Meetings: Preliminary meetings in each target county will be held with Community Leaders and members, Local authority, and representatives from NGOs implementing projects across Rumbek Centre, Budi, Kajokeji, Magwi, Lainya, Yei and Cueibet Counties. The purpose of the meetings being to share information regarding the project, activate Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) ,provide information on CFMs including how to submit complaints and receive feedback, seek community inputs and support. ACTIVITY 1.2: Conduct Safety Audit: TF will conduct safety audits in targeted counties prior to commencing the project to map GBV risks .The findings will be embedded in the project to ensure safety of staffs and beneficiaries during implementation. ACTIVITY 1.3: Formation, training (on EMAP and SASA toolkits) and support for Community Based Protection Mechanisms (CBPNs) each composed of 15 Members (8 Females& 7 Males). CBPNs will receive training on GBV SoPs, women rights and will confidentially conduct community dialogues and awareness on GBV, its harmful effects and prevention and advocating for women rights. ACTIVITY 1.4: Training of case workers: TF will train 60 case workers on PSS support and other support services including referral of GBV cases according to GBV SoPs. ACTIVITY 1.5: Update Referral Pathways: TF will update existing referral pathways comprised of chiefs, case workers, service providers, and service providers. The referral pathway will play an increased role in GBV monitoring, reporting and after-case/follow-up support. ACTIVITY 1.6: Training of women and men on GBV prevention: TF will conduct training on GBV targeting women and men drawn from target counties. The training will focus on GBV issues prevalence at the community level, children rights as well as on GBV advocacy. Trainees will be selected among youth groups, women groups, religious leaders, and chiefs. Select leaders will have an elevated profile and actively participating on protection issues affecting the community. ACTIVITY 1.7: Establish Women Girl Friendly Spaces: TF will establish 6 WGFS to be managed by WGFS Managers. At risk women and girls in WGFSs will engage in PSS, life skills training, beadwork, knitting and peer to peer interactions. ACTIVITY 1:8: Conduct Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) training targeting vulnerable adolescent girls. Training will entail personal hygiene, how to use and properly keep clean/dispose hygiene kits and will utilize WEDC Guide and Wash plus MHM training manuals. For sustainability, TF will train 25 women in each target county on how to make reusable sanitary kits. ACTIVITY 1.9: GBV Awareness: TF will conduct monthly community based awareness and advocacy meetings through the CBPNs. The format of the awareness campaigns will include FGDs with stakeholders, GBV Education in schools, GBV Advocacy meetings with local authorities including chiefs to discuss GBV issues affecting the community, promote women rights and behavioural change, how to mitigate GBV risks, role of the leaders in ensuring basic GBV services are available at the community level, and positive norms to mitigate GBV. ACTIVITY 1.10: TF through experienced and practising GBV lawyers will facilitate monthly legal aid clinic at target location to provide information on avenues GBV survivors can safely pursue to get justice. ACTIVITY 1.11: Needs assessment on dignity kits: TF will conduct an assessment among girls and women to obtain information on specific dignity kits needs and preferred content of the kits. ACTIVITY 1.12: Post Distribution Monitoring: TF will conduct PDM activities on Dignity Kits to assess utilization of the products and to obtain feedback on areas of improvement. ACTIVITY 1.13: Joint development and review of GBV SoPs: TF will participate and contribute to the development / review of GBV SoPs in partnership with SGBV Cluster other actors to guide provision of coherence of quality GBV services.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The guiding principle of the GBV intervention is “Do no harm’’ and it prioritizes the protection, safety, dignity and rights of beneﬁciaries while delivering services and assistance. Trainings will be provided in adequate scale, within safe and easy to affected Returnees, Internally Displaced Persons and vulnerable host communities according to needs and risks devoid of any form of discrimination based on age, gender, ethnic allegiance, or religion. The GBV project ensures maximum inclusion and will seek to build the capacity and assist affected individuals to claim their rights. Also, activities proposed will abide by principle of humanitarian protection by ensuring women, girls and children are protected from all forms of sexual gender based violence and exploitation. Platforms such as open community dialogues, complaint and feedback mechanisms, community consultations, Focus Group Discussions and meetings are embedded in the project to ensure community members views, suggestions, ideas and concerns are received, acknowledged and attended to on time to safeguard their rights and entitlements

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The GBV intervention is designed based on information obtained from returnees, Internally Displaced Persons and Vulnerable host communities during IRNA, Protection Cluster and Multiagency assessments. Assessments and Reports noted are referenced in the GBV project to justify the intervention and were compiled after extensive Interviews, Focus Group Discussions, Observation and household visits to obtain information from affected persons. Titi Foundation has reached a minimum of 98 individuals (53 Females) while designing the GBV projects. Their needs, priorities, concerns and suggestions are embedded in the project activities. As such, the project is a product of assessed, expressed and prioritized needs of the affected communities and will ably meet the speciﬁc needs of the affected populations.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Due to years of conﬂict, Government and Community Structures and Networks collapsed in targeted counties. However, as a coping mechanisms, communities in target locations have come up with their own systems of addressing challenges they face as well as in disaster risk reduction. In locations with or without Governance and community structures, the GBV project assumes participatory approach seeking to involve community members in project activities at every stage to generate project buy in, legitimacy, acceptability, sustainability, accountability and to allow the community members to contribute their talent, ideas and other resources to complement the intervention. Coordinating project activities with the local authorities, community leadership diminishes chances of overlap or duplication of effort. Also, coordination allows the project to beneﬁt from technical knowledge and capacities at the community and government agencies levels. The GBV project staffs will actively participate in meetings, ﬁeld visits and assessments organized by the Local Authorities, Community Leaders and chiefs throughout the period of the intervention. Also, the intervention will seek to develop existing referral pathway for effective referrals of GBV cases Project Staffs may encounter while implementing the project. Increased participation of community members through existing networks in the community allows for strengthening community networks and systems for better response to risks and disasters.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The Proposed GBV intervention is an emergency response informed by pressing needs currently faced by Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and vulnerable host communities. Whereas the aim is to meet immediate GBV needs of the affected communities, the project will seek to engage target communities on durable GBV solutions. For example, increased involvement of community members in the project will generate a pool of community members well versed with women and girl’s rights, need to protect women and girls and modalities of GBV Prevention and Mitigation.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Whereas project locations were immensely affected by the conﬂict that erupted in 2013 and escalated in 2016, they have enjoyed relative calm after the signing of Revitalized Peace Agreement in September 2018. Accessibility to locations and beneﬁciaries is by road and by air with the local authorities allowing unhindered access to beneﬁciaries and delivery of humanitarian aid. Assessments conducted in the locations by the Protection Cluster have noted improved security with no much risk posed to humanitarian agencies and supplies. Noting that security situation is fragile across South Sudan, Titi Foundation will conduct safety audits in target locations to map existing and potential risks and possible impact on project activities. The ﬁndings of the audit report will be embedded in project activities and used to advocate for protection of staffs, beneﬁciaries, project supplies and assets. Further, Titi Foundation will rely on protection cluster, GBV Sub- Cluster and UNOCHA for security updates. Local authorities will be informed of staff’s movement, project activities well in advance in order for them to provide security. To alleviate resource driven conﬂict during project implementation, the GBV project will ensure protection for environment with special attention being placed on avoiding depletion of natural resources i.e. ensuring trees are not cut, avoiding conducting project activities next to watering points, animal migration corridors, and religious and cultural shrines.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The GBV project will seek to equip beneficiaries with skills and knowledge they can utilize to come up with their own protection solutions. In the proposed intervention, women, girls, boys and men will be provided with information on their rights and need to protect their rights. Community Based Protection networks will play a critical role of mitigating children right abuses while advocating for increased participation of the community in protection of children. Titi Foundation will also continue to fund raise to support GBV activities in targeted locations during and after the 12 months’ period through Diakonie Katastrophilfe and other agencies who are already willing to continue supporting its interventions. Finally, by building the resilience of community members, the intervention will strengthen the resilience of households’ thus expanding household coping mechanism.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

61% of displaced persons in South Sudan conﬂict are women and girls. Negative gendered norms, safety concerns and domestic chores can exclude women and girls from accessing humanitarian assistance. To ensure equal participation of women and girls in this project, project consultations and planning meetings will be conducted in locations that women and girls can easily and safely access and during time of day they can attend. Women and girls will be engaged in project monitoring to embed their views and concerns in project activities. To mitigate occurrence of GBV, Titi Foundation will deploy a protection oﬃcer who will provide guidance to project staffs on existing and potential GBV risks to ensure activities do not pose additional risks to women and girls. To mitigate exposure to violence during selection of beneficiaries, TF will conduct consultation with the community members and leaders to provide information on the project including selection criteria, number of beneﬁciaries per location. Also, selection of beneficiary’s exercises will be conducted in collaboration with community leaders, local authorities, beneﬁciaries’ representatives to ensure fairness. All complaints received on the project will be safely, impartially and swiftly acted on with complainants being provided with feedback within the shortest time possible. Prior to commencing project activities, a safety audit will be conducted in each target location to map existing or potential protection concerns posed to beneﬁciaries and project staffs. The Safety Audit report will be utilized by project staffs, community leaders and members in deciding registration, distribution and training sites, where beneﬁciaries can access easily and safely. Project data will be dis aggregated by age and gender to measure level of participation of females.
