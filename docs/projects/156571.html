Project Summary

Building on the expertise and operational capacity of ACTED staff in Syria, the intervention will provide targeted and effective WASH support to those most vulnerable across the country. The action will focus on restoring cost-efficient, sustained and safe access to quality water to populations. Temporary sources of safe water will be installed where no alternatives to water trucking exist and under-performing water and sanitation infrastructure will be rehabilitated. Rehabilitated assets will be handed over to local actors to increase community resilience. Community sensitization on hygiene practices and water usage will be mainstreamed to heighten the impact of the intervention.
Under the WASH sector, ACTED will build on its expertise conducting multi-faceted WASH programming and will combine elements of emergency response while establishing durable solutions. In Syria, ACTED's WASH approach is based on three layers of intervention. First, immediate interventions aim to meet the emergency needs of those most vulnerable through the distribution of WASH NFIs and the establishment of emergency water treatment units and distribution points. Key WASH-related awareness raising is mainstreamed throughout the approach. A second layer of interventions focuses on improving access to sustainable safe water sources through existing infrastructure. The initial emergency response is thus paired with the rehabilitation of WASH assets such as canals, water stations, and boreholes. Additionally, ACTED provides technical and operational support to the authorities managing the rehabilitated sites. A third layer focuses on ensuring the handover of rehabilitated assets to, and establishment of cost-recovery and water safety plans with, local stakeholders to ensure the sustainability of the self-managed infrastructure.
This intervention will focus on emergency response and sustainable resilience, so as to provide the most adapted and cost-efficient assistance to those most in need. Synergies will be drawn between activities and with other ongoing ACTED interventions.
ACTED will deliver an integrated package of WASH assistance in Northeast Syria. Water quantity and quality will be improved to SPHERE standards, with a focus on ensuring durable and cost-efficient access to safe water. A particular effort will be put on offering sustainable alternatives to water trucking where no other sources of safe water are available. In turn, it will allow actors currently conducting water trucking to redirect emergency assistance to other areas. Mainstreamed community sensitization on water conservation, usage, and hygiene will contribute to decreasing Water Born Diseases (WBD) concerns. An effective exit strategy will be ensured by working closely with local stakeholders, building their capacity to operate and maintain rehabilitated infrastructure.

Needs

Based on existing data and assessments, it is estimated that existing needs and vulnerabilities are at risks of being compounded by further waves of displacement in 2019. To identify the pressing needs and current gaps in WASH response in Deir ez-Zor (DeZ) and Ar-Raqqa governorates, ACTED conducted assessments in December 2018 In DeZ governorate, the household surveys, key informant interviews and technical assessments were conducted separately for the three lines (Western Line, Eastern Line and Northern Line) which geographically divide Deir-ez-Zor governorate. The key priority WASH needs for the assessed households in the Eastern (Basira sub-district) and Northern lines (Sur sub-district) were to enhance the quality of water supplied through water trucks, increase the quantity of water through water trucks to communal points, and receive hygiene kits. In the Western Line (Kisreh sub-district), the key priority WASH needs were to provide hygiene kits and water chlorination tabs, and increase the water quantity and quality supplied through the existing network.The Northern line surveyed residents reported being fully dependent on water trucking in the absence of functioning water stations and water network infrastructure, mainly due to the destruction of Rai Sabha station and to the low quality of water from boreholes in the area. Respondents in the Western and Eastern line reported relying on low quality surface water connected to community water networks or used to fill water trucks. Relying on water trucking poses a significant financial burden for vulnerable households who already lack income. Additionally, the percentage of households suffering from Water Borne Diseases (WBD) was found to be critically high (97% in the Northern line, 89% in the Eastern line, and 52% in the Western line). This is directly linked to quality concerns over the water received, which was often cited as having suspended particles and color, and to the reported lack of water treatment equipment/practices at HH-level. In all lines, average water consumption rates were below the minimum recommended rates by the Whole of Syria WASH Atlas (60L/Capita/Day). Households who reported having issues in meeting their water needs declared resorting to negative coping mechanisms such as consuming less water for hygiene purposes and using non-trusted water sources. In Ar-Raqqa governorate, as part of a previous UNICEF-funded intervention, ACTED WASH team based in Ein Issa assessed WASH infrastructure in Northeast Syria, mapping the WASH needs and gaps in access to water supply for vulnerable and conflict-affected populations. Lack of power supply and technical and financial capacities were amongst the key factors restricting communities’ access to the piped water supply and rendering them dependent upon either private water vendors or hazardous surface water sources such as contaminated river water. The assessments resulted in the prioritization of three main sites, based on population vulnerability and the potential impact of an intervention on these communities. Selection criteria included more precisely the anticipated percent increase of drinkable water share per capita, the lack of alternative water supply modalities, and the capacity of the local authorities to take over the operation of the stations post-rehabilitation to guarantee a sustainable impact.

Implementation of activities

ACTED will focus on improving access to clean and safe water for conflict affected population. ACTED coordinates through the WASH Working group, WASH Cluster, and as well bilateral coordination with WASH Partners working on station rehabilitation in Deir Ez Zor and Raqqa (SI, CONCERN, Mercy Corps). Additionally, ACTED coordinates with each Local Authorities (DCC ; RCC ; HAO) and Water Departments to ensure that there is no overlapping targeting. 1. Emergency and quick impact response • Distribution of hygiene kits, water treatment tablets, and clean and safe water storage units in DeZ governorate;• Household-level storage tanks flushing campaign and water trucking safety plan in Kisreh sub-district;•Prepositioning of 3,000 hygiene kits in NES in ACTED’s warehouses in Northeast Syria so as to be able to respond to displacements in Al-Hasakeh, DeZ and Ar-Raqqa in coordination with the other RRM/ERM actors active in the areas;• Installation of an emergency Reverse Osmosis station for immediate and sustainable drinkable water access in Sur (Sur sub-district). This technology providing 60m3 of water per day, is the only sustainable solution in remote areas depending on contaminated underground water.• Installation of a mobile emergency water treatment unit (ETU) in the community of Jdied Eqeidat (Khasham sub-district). The ETU will ensure that the water available from the Euphrates river is fit for consumption both by households and for usage by water trucking humanitarian actors (further benefiting responses in South DeZ), by providing 303 m3 of water per day. To facilitate the latter, ACTED will create water trucking filling point at the site. 2. Rehabilitation of WASH assets - All locations of interventions were identified on the basis of: the severity of needs in the area; the lack of current sustainable safe water sources; lack of planned rehabilitation works by other actors on the infrastructure; the number of vulnerable conflict-affected individuals who will benefit from the rehabilitation; possibility to handover the structure to local authorities after the completion of works. The rehabilitation of the Water treatment stations wil include provision of generator, replacement of submersible pumps, installation of control panel, light structural damaged rehabilitation, installation of chlorine dosing pumps, etc. Rehabilitation aims to achieve full operation and reach initial capacity of the stations.• Rehabilitation of Haluk Water station, WASH facilities in public institutions and establishment/rehabilitation of water quality testing laboratories in Al-Hasakeh governorate;• Rehabilitation of Abu Touta (Karama sub-district), Rajm Onwah (Suluk sub-district) and Al-Tayba water stations (Tell Abiad sub- district);• Rehabilitation of Mhemeda water station (Kisreh sub-district) and 2 conjoined water stations of Sefera (DeZ sub-district);• Rehabilitation of Albuhmad, Hawayej Bumasaa (Kisreh sub-district), and Shaqra (DeZ sub-district) water stations. This will include the implementation of a Water Safety Plan (as describe above) in the targeted locations. The WSP will allow to tackle ‘soft’ hurdles to safe access to water supply and hygiene practices identified in these communities. • Support the SWAT management in Karama (Karama sub-district). 3. Handover to the local authorities • The handover of rehabilitated assets to, and establishment of cost-recovery and water safety plans with, local stakeholders aims at ensuring the sustainability of the self-managed infrastructure;• All rehabilitations will include 3 months of support for Operation and Maintenance to ensure the development and operationalization of a sustainable exit strategy. All activities will be complemented by hygiene promotion, including hygiene promotion sessions of 25 participants and distribution of IEC materials, and mine risk awareness mainstreaming in Northeast Syria.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Non-inclusion of special needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community mobilisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities. Focus Group Discussion are conducted in order to listen to vulnerable people’s preferences and needs in order to respond accordingly. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk: Inadequate intervention to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups Likelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: During the project implementation, ACTED ensures the active participation of women and girls (in separate meetings so that they can freely express their ideas and concerns) in deciding on the location and design of latrines and WASH areas to maximize safety, privacy and dignity. Special needs of women and girls will be taken into account throughout the implementation of the activities, the particular necessities of pregnant and lactating women, female head of household, and elderly or disabled women will be taken into account, and should they be unable to reach the distribution sites, they will be able to obtain the kits by proxy. ACTED ensure that water collection point and distributions site are safe and easily accessible for the most vulnerable groups, including women. Finally, all along the distributions, ACTED will constantly monitor protection and gender issues, to ensure they are adequately identified and addressed. By rehabilitating water stations and installing new water points, ACTED aims at reducing the distance between households shelters and water collection point. This mitigates protection concerns for women and accessible issues for people with disabilities. Additionally, the WASH activities will decrease the risk of water borne diseases, which often affect children more significantly. Components of the hygiene kits/vouchers and hygiene promotion will also focus on women’s personal hygiene, reducing risks for their health. Risk: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV), as well as a PSEA policy. ACTED’s PSEA policy stresses the importance of prevention but also explain ACTED’s mechanisms for report and investigation. Additionally, as part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security and Political:a. Assumption: - ACTED keep the ability to operate directly or remotely in Northeast even if a political change of control occurs during the implementation of the project. b. Associated Risks- Security chaos in areas of intervention, impacting movements and security of teams and beneficiaries - Non-renewal of authorization of implementation for ACTED in case of major political change (i.e. GoS return; creation of a new political OAGs). - Targeted attacks on NGOs workers and facilities due to security chaos or activation of ISIL sleeping cells. c. Mitigation measures - Expansion to any new area is preceded by an in-depth access mission from the ACTED Security team. Formal approval from the Security team for daily movements and daily security updates for relevant staff. Relocation of staff to other offices can be used to carry on activities safely. - Remote procedures have already been set up even though international presence has been continuous since 2017, allowing ACTED to carry on implementation without disruption in case of evacuation. Resource availability:a. Commodities to be procured remain available in Northeast through a competitive process matching quality requirements and prices estimated under the project. - Roads inside Syria remain open to allow the transport of staffs and commodities.- ACTED is able to continue recruiting independently staff with the required level of qualification to carry out the project activities. b. Closure of trade roads and border due to clashes or political change impacting the capacity of ACTED to procure required goods.- Roads to areas of intervention cannot be used due to lack of maintenance or damages due conflict activities or weather conditions. - Tentative of interference from Local Authorities in ACTED’s procurements or recruitment processes. c. Operations in Syria have been regularly impacted by the closure of commercial roads during previous offensives. ACTED has diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned in advance.- Solid procurement and recruitment processes are in place. Community and cohesion dynamics:a. No major change toward NGOs acceptance is observed in Northeast during the project implementation. b. Refusal of ACTED activities by the local authorities and community leaders or violence erupting during activities implementation due to lack of acceptance of the project.c. ACTED has set up a CRM inside Syria, so that stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues and easily express concerns. a. ACTED works as well closely with all actors on the ground in Syria including Local Councils, comins, Municipalities and Technical Departments, and obtains all necessary authorizations needed to conduct the activities.
