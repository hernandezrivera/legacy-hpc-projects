Project Summary

There is a long established need to provide enhanced information management and assessment sup-port the South Sudan WASH Cluster. Throughout 2015 and 2016, REACH, through deployments of staff seconded to the Global Wash Cluster, supported the South Sudan Wash Cluster with IM and Assessment Specialists. These deployments supported immediate IM needs and piloted a baseline assessment tool and methodology. However, the limited duration and tightly constrained scope of these activities, coupled with the return of widespread conflict, resulted in limited uptake of the best practices they established. Given the emergence of conflict in the Equatorias, emergent instability in Great Bahr el Ghazal, and continued displacement in Greater Upper Nile, now is a critical moment in which to provide focused, technical support to the South Sudan WASH Cluster and its partners. The establishment of a functioning knowledge ecosystem, in which actors are using common tools and assessment methodologies to create a common understanding of needs should be seen as priority which will enhance the impact and reach of donor funding, and allow the WASH Cluster and its part-ners to effectively identify needs and prioritize delivery of life saving aid to affected populations.

Needs

Accessibility and security issues within South Sudan have impeded a systematic understanding of WASH needs in many areas of the country, and have created difficulties in establishing a clear and unambiguous system for prioritizing the delivery of aid, thereby limiting the effectiveness of humanitarian planning and limiting the potential impact of donor funding. As this crisis continues to expand, evolve and spill into neighboring countries, it has become increasingly important to fill information gaps to inform a more effective humanitarian response and planning for immediate life-saving WASH activities and contingency planning for durable solutions.REACH will conduct a series of over lapping, integrated activities to support the establishment of a WASH Cluster Knowledge Ecosystem for South Sudan. REACH will directly support WASH Cluster with a dedicated, full time Assessment Officer and a GIS Officer who will work with the Cluster and Partners to analyze and map existing WASH related information to identify information gaps and provide targeting information for REACH led interagency assessments, partner assessment activities and WASH response prioritization. Using the WASH Baseline Toolkit established during 2018, REACH will provide support for partners conducting assessments in order to harmonize WASH assessments around a set of key indicators, by providing a series of WASH partner capacity building workshops that focus on building partner capacity to fill information gaps in the WASH landscape. REACH will provide training to partners on assessment design and execution, mobile data collection, and IM best practices that ultimately results in the execution of 6 county level assessments (locations to be determined by Cluster information needs), and the production of reports and datasets. This actionable information will be used to identify priority areas and/or populations and the key WASH concerns, rank needs across the country to improve priority targeting, and will also help shape what kind type of intervention should be implemented.

Activities

REACH will produce the following outputs under this activity 1 x 12 monthly Partner Assessment and IM Capacitation Workshops (12 Total) 6 County level baseline assessments (location to be guided by cluster) 6 County level assessment reports 6 County level datasets

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

REACH conducts quarterly community feedback sessions, in which information collected is shared back by REACH teams in 10 of our field sites. During these community feedback sessions, data collected for the WASH project is communicated to beneficiaries. REACH further shares all information products online making them available to the wider public.
