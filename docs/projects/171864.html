Project Summary

In 2021, Caritas continues providing life-saving measures for people affected by conflict focusing 0-5 km area along the contact line, GCA. Activities are based on Caritas current project with improved approaches based on cluster recommendations and new evidences caused by Covid-19 pandemic and protracted nature of armed conflict. Project targets the most vulnerable people with critical needs in isolated settlements. Project is based on problem analysis described in HNO and matches clusters objectives and recommendations. The operational capacity os based on 4 implementing partners with humanitarian teams experienced in multi-sectoral assistance via mobile work in high risk area since 2017. Targeting the settlements is provided in coordination with clusters and other humanitarian actors. Registration process provides teams with needed data for selection: criticalness of basic needs, income level and accumulative score of vulnerability. Multi-sectoral nature allows to provide the most appropriate modality of assistance (cash or in-kind) based on vulnerability of people, accessibility to markets, convenience in using home-based in-kind delivery or cash assistance with transportation services. MEAL system ensures evaluation of effectiveness, appropriateness, timeliness, accessibility and sufficiency of relief measures as well as ensures accountability mechanism and Safeguarding measures. Procurement of in-kind relief measures goes according to Caritas procurement procedure in local centers to strengthen capacity of local markets.
The overall goal of the project is to secure basic needs of particularly vulnerable people affected by conflict.

1 - Needs

In its seventh year, the armed conflict in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in eastern Ukraine continues to have a devastating effect on the 5,2 million people living in the conflict-affected areas. Considered a protracted humanitarian crisis, it leaves 3,4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection, and 1,4 million internally displaced. In addition, there is growing concern that the Covid-19 pandemic will further aggravate humanitarian needs, due to the large proportion of vulnerable people, poor healthcare and lack of adequate infrastructure for Covid-19 prevention. The virus has been spreading rapidly in Ukraine, with more than 348,924 laboratory-confirmed cases as of 26 October 2020. However only 2 823 809 test were conducted in whole country. Even under conservative estimations, absolute poverty is expected to rise from 27% to 44%. The situation is particularly grave for the elderly, people living in rural areas along the contact line, IDPs, people with specific needs (PWSN) as well as girls and women, who experience a 50% increased risk of gender-based violence (GBV) and financial difficulties. As women constitute the majority of healthcare and social workers, they also bear the brunt of fighting the Covid-19 pandemic - often under precarious conditions - and face higher risk of infection. With overlapping vulnerabilities and multiple needs, the protracted crisis in Ukraine requires a multisector approach that links the alleviation of immediate basic needs with measures that increase resilience and promote recovery. In particular, the problem analysis of conflict-affected communities has highlighted: 1. Increasing vulnerability and multiple needs in isolated GCA settlements, particularly of elderly people with disabilities: The elderly make up 32% of the conflict-affected population overall, and 41% of the population within 0-5km distance to the contact line. A high-risk group for Covid-19, many of them have overlapping vulnerabilities such as chronic disease, disability and economic hardship and are more prone to isolation and lack access to healthcare. In addition, Covid-19 has exacerbated feelings of isolation (C-UA monitoring findings show that more than half of elderly people receive no support from their local communities, they fear Covid-19 and its stigma) and increased the need for psychosocial support. Women are particularly affected; with a higher life expectancy by 10 years, they make up almost two thirds of the retirement age population in Ukraine. According to the HNO 2020, almost 40% of the conflict-affected population struggle with depression, trauma, anxiety and post-traumatic stress, yet the majority does not know where to seek help. Within a 5-kilometer zone affected by the conflict face particularly high vulnerabilities to the risk of transmission of COVID-19 due to three contextual factors:(i) a high proportion of older people in the region total population, compared with 23 percent across the country), in particular in isolated settlements, (ii) deterioration of the health care system due to the cumulative impact of the armed conflict (ii) the poor the state of the infrastructure required to prevent COVID-19 and – most of them women - who already suffer from insecurity, lack of services, stress and the economic downturn associated with armed conflict Access to appropriate medical services, including emergency medical care in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts remains a difficult task people of all ages, especially for the elderly and the people who live with them disability in hard-to-reach rural settlements near the "line of contact". Factors include high associated costs for medication and travel, absence specialized medical staff, at long distances from commercial and service centers and limited availability of public transport.A REACH-led winter assessment of humanitarian needs in the 5 km zone showed that 37% of households reported insufficient heating.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: At least 80% of food in-kind aid beneficiaries with critical needs in food have improved their food consumption after 3 months assistance. Activity 1: Distribution of 4,200 food packages to 1,400 people affected by the conflict, including the elderly with limited access to markets: immediate access to food by distributing 3 packages of food per person from home delivery to cover food gaps within 3 months. Large families may receive assistance for each family member. Outcome 2: At least 80% of fuel in-kind aid beneficiaries with critical needs in heating have improved their coverage of winterization needs for half a cold season after receiving fuel. Activity 2: Winterization: distribution of 3760 tons of solid fuel for 1880 HHs: Households are provided with immediate access to fuelform 2 tons of sunflower briquettes, sufficient for half of the winter season - or coal / wood inequivalent - to cover gaps in wintering needs. The aid is delivered to the beneficiary's household packaged in bags up to 40 kg with unloading in a convenient for single women and the elderly. Outcome 3: At least 50% of health assistance beneficiaries with critical needs in physical or mental health have improved their health condition 1 moth after receiving full period assistance. 3.1: Allocation of 11,640 medical cash grants and / or pharmacy vouchers to 2,910 beneficiaries with chronic and severe primarily elderly women and large families. Each family member can receive assistance according to the doctor's prescription: Beneficiaries are provided with purchasing power (cash assistance with bank cards) for access to rescue drugs, operations or diagnosis or provided with immediate access to medicines (through vouchers). The cost of one beneficiary is UAH 4,000 as a one-time payment or 4 monthly installments of UAH 1,000 depending on individual needs. Activity 3.2: Provision of 1200 transportation services for 300 beneficiaries in 10 remote settlements of the buffer zone to visit family doctors, and access to secondary and specialized medical care facilities. Activity 3.3: Providing 456 sessions of psychosocial groups to 1040 beneficiaries and providing 3960 individual consultations to 650 beneficiaries so group consultations are mostly given to women and children while men are more inclined to individual consultations and online consultations: Depending on traumas, anxiety level, ability to resilience beneficiaries will receive up to 6 sessions (face-to-face or by phone) or participate in up to 6 group sessions (from 6-12 participants). As soon as a new place is introduced, an initial awareness raising is held and an information session is made available to a large audience. Subsequent sessions are offered more specific and smaller groups tailored to individual needs. Sessions are conducted by trained psychologists who include practical exercises on care of their mental health themselves. Selection of beneficiaries and identification of relevant premises are organized by mobile groups in cooperation with local authorities and activists. Activity 3.4: Providing MHPSS phone consultations on COVID-19 hotlines to community members. Outcome 4: At least 50% of MPC beneficiaries with critical basic needs have increased purchasing power and improved coverage of needs 1 month after they received the last cash payment. Activity 4: Provision of 8000 multi-purpose cash grants up to 2000 people, including women single housewives, large families and those who lost their jobs due to the pandemic and armed conflict: Selected beneficiaries receive 4 multi-purpose monthly cash grants of UAH 1,150 each to cover gaps in their basic needs. The funds are transferred to the head of the family via bank card. Depending on the level of vulnerability, up to 4 family members, including children, can be supported. If cash modality is not appropriate (due to vulnerability or limited access to markets), person will be supported with above in-kind measures.

3 - Indicators & targets

Project M&E system is based on ALNAP criteria for Emergency support. Effectiveness is measured by Outcome indicators mentioned in the previous chapter. Output level, besides qty numbers, is measured in frames of criteria related to sufficiency, timeliness, appropriateness and accessibility. The method is phone surveying of u to 30% randomly selected beneficiaries each half a year. The results have disaggregation by gender, age, vulnerability, status and type of settlement. M&E service is localized in each implementing organization where each local M&E officer provides validation and learning workshops with team.MEAL system is very tailored with targeting and selection approach. Selection is based on income level, criticalness of needs and score of vulnerability evaluated at registration (base-line stage) process. Disability of people is evaluated according to Washington system.Impact: 30% of beneficiaries who have decreased negative coping strategies. The indicator is measured each half a year. It based on methodology elaborated by cash working group. MHPSS beneficiaries are evaluated for improvement of positive coping strategies as well.Objectives: 50% (MHPSS and cash modality) or 80% (in-kind modality) of beneficiaries who have improved securing their besic needs comparing to critical level at the base-line stage. The indicator makes SMART approach for each project component. It is evaluated each half a year.Outputs: 80% of beneficiaries who reported that assistance was sufficient to secure critical needs80% of beneficiaries who reported that assistance was appropriate to secure critical needs80% of beneficiaries who reported that they obtained assistance timely80% of beneficiaries who reported that assistance is delivered in accessible and safe manner# of beneficiaries who obtained food kits (# of distributed food kits)# of HHs who obtained heating material (# of distributed tons of heating materials)# of beneficiaries who obtained medical cash grants (# of distributed medical grants)# of beneficiaries who obtained medical vouchers (# of distributed medical vouchers)# of beneficiaries who obtained transportation to health facilities (# of trips)# of beneficiaries who obtained MHPSS assistance (# of individual sessions; # of group sessions)# of beneficiaries who obtained PSS hot line consultations related to Covid-19 hazards (# of phone consultations)# of beneficiaries who obtained MPC grants assistance (# of MPC grant payments). Caritas has incorporated strong accountability mechanism that provides feedback of beneficiaries not only in program-related area but in Safeguarding complains. Feedback mechanism undertakes: PSS hotlines, PDM and Evaluation surveys, boxes, information leaflets with office hotlines, activists and volunteer feedback, on-line feedback forms of Caritas web resources. Depending on convenience, access and confidentiality, a beneficiary may use relevant type of feedback mechanism and will be redirected to proper person based on request nature.
