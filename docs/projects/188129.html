Project Summary

Providing Shelter and NFI emergency assistance for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community families in conﬂict-affected areas of North East Syria, reaching a total of 59,400 conflict-affected individuals. Through the provision of winterization and NFI assistance to IDPs in informal camps and settlements as well as host community. The project will also deliver private shelter rehabilitation support to 2,436 vulnerable individuals.
NFI, Shelter and Winterization support will comprise three related but distinct components. First, supporting IDPs living in camps, informal settlements and collective centers to survive the harsh winter months. This includes the provision of 2,100 kerosene or diesel heater kits with three months cash for fuel, supporting 12,600 individuals and the distribution of 2,300 cash for winter grants supporting 13,800 individuals. Specific camps and sites targeted include Menbij camps, Abu Kashab and Raqqa informal sites. Second, essential NFI in-kind kits and vouchers will also be distributed to the populations in Deir-Ez Zoir informal sites and other vulnerable informal site populations, targeting 33,000 individuals. Third, we will also undertake shelter activities to support recent returnees and IDPs in Ar Raqqa, Tabqa and Manbij, living in unsafe and inadequate housing. This will be done through undertaking essential repairs and winterization of 400 partially-damaged or unfinished homes as well as ensuring a minimum level of privacy, dignity and access to WASH facilities for each household. This will benefit an estimated 2,436 individuals.

4 - Needs

The 2022 HNO estimates that in 2022, around 5.92 million people will be in need of shelter assistance and 4.91 million in need of NFI support in Syria, an increase of 5% since 2021 despite an improved security situation. It is estimated that of the 6.92 million people displaced, 3.73 million reside in substandard, damaged, and/ or inadequate shelter conditions, raising serious health and protection concerns. These are primarily being driven to move to such locations due to conflict, security concerns, and economic deterioration. In NES there are a large number of IDPs seeking adequate shelter in urban centers, residing in unfinished/damaged buildings. At the same time, many returnees reside in inadequate shelter options with many homes damaged due to years of violent conflict and the inability to undertake essential maintenance on housing due to years of displacement. In Raqqa, half of the population is estimated to be living in damaged or inadequate shelters (HNAP 2021) with 47% of structures damaged, destroyed or demolished (REACH 2021). The number of IDPs living in NES's ten formal camps is above 100,000 while many more IDPs are scattered in urban and rural areas – in informal settlements, collective centers and in urban centers – across NES. As the economy deteriorates and prices rises, the majority of basic NFIs continue become increasingly unaffordable to vulnerable IDP and host families.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project comprises 3 core activities. First, for Sector Objective 1, this project will provide (Sector Activity 1.1) core NFI kits to 2,100 IDP, returnee and vulnerable host community households in need (12,600 individuals). This kit will be provided either in-kind or through a voucher modality. The second activity also under Objective 1 (Sector Activity 1.2), comprises seasonal support as winterization kits (heaters, jerry can, fire extinguisher), cash for heating fuel, and cash for winter grants for three months. This support will be provided to 7,800 vulnerable households (46,800 individuals). Kits will be distributed in November and cash for fuel starting December. Sites targeted for both activities will be Menbij camps, Abu Khashab camp, Raqqa informal sites, and Deir-Ez Zoir informal sites.The third activity, which falls under Sector Objective 2 (Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure) entails rehabilitation of partially damaged and unﬁnished buildings (Sector activity 2.1) in urban areas in Raqqa, Tabqa and Menbij. 400 vulnerable households will be supported through shelter rehabilitation (2,436 individuals). Beneﬁciary selection will be carried out by house-by-house assessments or by referral. Each shelter targeted will be rehabilitated to meet basic SPHERE shelter standards and will be in accordance with the Shelter sector guidance on rehabilitation.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The SNFI cluster Protection Risk Analysis identifies over 50 key protection risks specific to the sector. Based on this, and the NGO's own internal protection analysis, identifies the key risks as follows: inter-community tension as a result of inclusion/exclusion, unclear selection criteria or varied assistance packages by different actors; increased tension between host and IDP communities; and risks particularly associated with distributions. To mitigate the first risk, the NGO adopts SNFI cluster guidance in its vulnerability assessments and its assistance packages to ensure harmonization with other actors. Similarly, the NGO hires local staff to carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive information networks and good community acceptance. This ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness of the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. In terms of tension between host and IDP communities, the NGO conducts extensive consultations with both groups before commencing any activities. The NGO does not target on the basis of status, and thus supports IDP and host communities. In order to mitigate for protection risks for all beneﬁciaries during SNFI distributions, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneﬁciaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female ﬁeld workers.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with supported from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: -Escalation in the ongoing conflict between SDF and Government of Turkey or GoS, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence with extension of attacks and activities to Raqqa and surrounding areas. -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. -Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations. -COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access. Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally. Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. -Flexibly shifting modalities as required depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. -COVID-19 SOPs, activity level SOPs. -Build local capacity and staff.
