Project Summary

The proposed project aims to prevention and response to violence, exploitation and abuse of children including children who are especially vulnerable to the risks, such as those living without family care or unaccompanied, those separated from primary care providers, children living on the streets or in situations of conflict or natural disasters.
The Somalia Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2022 show up to 66 per cent of the population in need of
protection services in Somalia are children who continue to be exposed to protection threats of recruitment, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence. According to the JMCNA 2021 it is estimated that 607,546 children are in extreme need (26 per cent per cent), 1,449,144 in severe need (61 per cent) and 302,440 in stress (13 per cent), and require humanitarian child protection assistance.
Jubaland Development Organization (JDO) anticipate responding in 7 districts on issue affecting children, there by targeting up to 9.5 per cent of the beneficiaries in need identified in the 74 districts covered by HNO 2022.
On Boys and girls including adolescents, children with disabilities, facing life threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress have access to well-coordinated and disability, gender-sensitive quality child protection services by 2022, JDO targets up to 5,400 boys and girls (in ratio of 50% each) through tailored services in line with the CP AoR strategy. The beneficiaries will be cascaded in the 7 location anticipating an average of ration of 600 to 800 beneficiaries per location.
For CP AoR objective on vulnerable and at risk girls and boys in IDP camps and in host communities access to quality child protection prevention and response services, JDO anticipate reaching up to 2700 beneficiaries in the 7 location with community led approach to strengthen mobilization and messaging on key child protection risks, social norms, harmful practices, MRE and related information on CP service and mitigation measures.
JDO will also coordinate with CP AoR to ensure IDP settlements are assessed for safety standards through safety audits, child safety walks and child led participatory mapping exercises targeting up to 9 sites in areas of operation.
The country task for on Monitoring and Reporting of violations against children in 2021, at 1,797 grave
violations affecting 1,392 children (330 girls; 1,062 boys) have been documented, with 631 children (97
per cent boys aged 11-17) recruited and used by parties to the conflict in the first half of 2021 alone. Hence JDO target for 80 to 90 Children Formerly Associated to Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG).
JOD also propose to Family separation which is on ongoing concern, with the 2021 SPMS indicating family separation as the third-highest protection concern reported across South Central. Twenty-two per cent of key informants involved in the SPMS also reported incidents of family separation. The number of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) continues to rise, with more than 11,235 (5,802 girls; 5,230 boys).
Due to nearly every aspect of children’s lives, including their mental health and healthy social support systems, has been disrupted by COVID-19 and risk mitigation measures, ongoing armed conflict and insecurity, and climatic shocks. JDO will also focus intervention on MHPSS to children, care givers including beneficiaries with disabilities to benefit from age, and gender sensitive structured mental health and psychosocial support services. JDO will also ensure to follow the national task-force on case management guidelines with children identified in need.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The risk of exposure of displaced boys and girls to violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements is considered medium considering as families tend to migrate before outbreak of the anticipated violence. boys and girls are usually sent away. However, to mitigate the risks JDO in coordination with CP AoR intend to conduct safety audits in IDP camps and settlements and based on the results advocate with other actors to enhance the safety for boys and girls The high risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors, is due to the general insecurity in the country, to mitigate it, JDO in coordination other actor hope to ensure publicized referral network for various service hence limiting the humanitarian aid pull factor. additionally with covering multiple locations with CP services, the project hopes decentralize access and limiting distance to be covered by the beneficiaries factor.
