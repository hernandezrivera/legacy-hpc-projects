Project Summary

As known, the communities of Jordan Valley are subjected to different Environmental and political harsh circumstances. These circumstances vary from the harsh weather including: droughts and high temperatures in summer, floods and freezing in winter, sensitive hot zones in the annexation plan on the political side.
As WASH cluster focal point in the Jordan Valley, PARC has implemented a full need assessment (Sep 2020) in different socioeconomic aspects throughout the Jordan Valley including WASH vulnerability. In Sep2021, PARC conducted a validation for the previous findings and found that the need is still there.
This Project includes a variety of activities and targeted communities aims to reduce the WASH vulnerability related to drinking water in Jordan Valley. These activities including but not limited to: rehabilitation of a 1000m3 drinking water reservoir, construction of two 250m3 drinking water reservoirs, construction of about 6m water carrier pipelines, distribution of 140 PE water tanks of size 5m3 and many other awareness and capacity building activities related to drinking water.
The project is designed to improve the accessibility for the local residents to the WASH facilities through connecting them directly to safe drinking water sources by constructing new water reservoirs, rehabilitating existing reservoirs and constructing new water carrier lines to make the filling points closer and safer. Moreover it is designed to improve their storage capability through providing them with water storage tanks of 5m3 size, these tanks will improve their resilient in resisting the harsh weather and especially in summer season when the water becomes rare and expensive.
The project will target 13 community in the middle area of Jordan Valley, these communities include: Ibziq, Khirbet Yerza, Hammamat Al Maleh, Marj Na'jeh Herders, Al Jiftlik, Isteih, Al Mashrou' Bedouins, Al Nuwei'ma Al Foqa Bedouins, An Nabi Musa, Deir Hajla and Deir al Qilt.
The project will focus on increasing the ability for 140 HHs to store water by providing them with water tanks of 5m3 capacity, these tanks can be used to store extra amount of drinking water and water for the house use during the droughts and the harsh hot weather especially in summer season. These tanks will be built in a heavy duty way with concrete base to ensure the sustainability and the long term use.
The project will also focus on providing more than 200 HHs of Bedouins in different areas with access to safe, sufficient and affordable water source through the construction of 2 drinking water reservoirs and connecting them to safe source of water. In addition, pipelines will be constructed between the water source and the constructed tanks and between the constructed tanks and the near communities to ensure the availability of water. Moreover, pipelines will be constructed close to other communities providing a safe and close filling points reducing the time and cost for transferring water by tanks.
These activities will be followed and implemented along with sessions and workshops increasing the people's awareness regarding saving water and the best practices in consuming, storage, transfer and many activities related to using water on the HH level.
The project is designed in a participatory and flexible way that ensures taking into consideration the points of view of the beneficiaries not only in the need assessment process but also in the implementation of different activities and in closing the project.
With local Residents of more than 12,000, these communities and especially the Bedouins suffer from irregular and expensive water supply. In most cases and especially in summer, they turn to unsafe and expensive water trucking to satisfy their minimum need of water for drinking and house use. These circumstances resulted in using less than 45 liters per capita which is far less than the water vulnerability line set by the WASH cluster which is 70 liters per capita.

1 - Needs

According to the number released by the Palestinian government, the averages of rainfall are the least in the Jordan Valley areas among the WB and Gaza communities. These low numbers of precipitation (50-100 mm/year) along with the Israeli restrictions on the local residents of these communities on using their ground water by preventing the licenses of excavating wells (All licenses are before 1967) and the limitations on the the amounts of water that can be pumped from the existing old wells. These situations left the Palestinians in Jordan Valley with high vulnerability to water shortage in general and to drinking water shortage in specific. In these communities, part the HHs get their needs of water supply for house-use and drinking through old water distribution networks that are connected to the Israeli water company Mecarot, they usually get the water irregularly and with high prices. Other HHs get their needs of water supply through water trucking, these trucks transfer water to them from a nearby spring, well or filling point, they suffer from the low quality and high price of water and the high cost of transportations (1 m3 approximately from 20-30 Nis according to the distance to the water source and according to the season). The last part of HHs get their water supply through pipes connecting them to a reservoir in their community or a well, those also complain about the quality, price and the availability of water in these reservoirs. Most of the pipes that are being used to deliver water to HHs are not properly insulated nor meeting the acceptable standard published by PWA. Most of Bedouin communities lack the sufficient amount of water, this shortage affects directly women, children and people with disabilities (PWDs). One of their most common problems is the ability of storing water for drinking and house-use especially in harsh weather conditions during the summer season. Women, in approximately all cases, as the responsible of cleaning and hygiene at HH level, suffer the most from the shortage of water supply. Shortage in water supply affects women's personal health and hygiene because they need additional quantities of water as females in order to protect themselves and maintain better health conditions. Children, as the most sensitive/vulnerable group that is affected by the lack of sufficient hygiene and proper hydration suffer from the low quality and quantity of supplied water. PWDs suffer more as they do not get the appropriate care and hygiene even in the normal situation, their suffering is duplicated in such conditions. A community of Bedouins of 40 families are living near Al-Hamra checkpoint does not have any structure related to water supply, they get their needs of water depending on local water tanks transported by trucks from nearby communities of Froush Beit Dajan or Al-jiftlek, the truck usually needs to travel more than 1.5 hours through an extremely bumpy road in each transportation of water. Sometimes, and especially in summer season, when water becomes rare and expensive the cubic meter of medium quality water can cost more than 40 Nis. These unfortunate circumstances led PARC to think of the best way to provide them with sufficient, safe and affordable water supply. PARC believes that construction a 250 m3 metallic reservoir connected by a carrier supply line will significantly reduce their vulnerability by making the filling point very close to their community and will significantly reduce the cost of water and therefore, increase the amounts of water consumption resulting in better hygiene and life conditions. Another Bedouin community of 25 families near Al-Jiftlik, suffers approximately the same conditions, the same methodology will be used to solve the problem there. Marj Na'jeh community have a 1000m3 metallic tank for drinking water in each community, this tank is not being used in the current time due to a major defect in the body and inner core of these tanks. PARC believes that a proper maintenance for this tank along with additional water pipelines to and from these reservoir will make a difference in the water supply in the community, reduce the vulnerability and decrease the dependency on the irregular and expensive water supply by the Israeli company Mecarot. In reference to the latest need assessment conducted by PARC, and as a focal point for WASH cluster in Jericho and Jordan Valley areas. PARC believes that improving the ability of storing water for the local residents and especially for Bedouins will significantly increase their resilience and decrease their vulnerability. Hence, PARC will distribute 140 PE water tanks of size 5m3 to 140 HHs of the most vulnerable in the targeted communities improving their storage ability and ensuring the proper and regular supply for these communities. Finally, PARC believes that the importance of improving the awareness level of the local residents is vital. In fact, it's not less important than the physical activities themselves. The residents of local communities must have a proper awareness sessions regarding the water use, their rights, the current situations ..etc. In addition, their capacities and skills must be improved in managing water, safe use, water conservation, sterilization and hygienic activities through organizing capacity building workshops taking into consideration women needs.

2 - Activities

The Project contains variety of activities related to supply of house-use and drinking water, also, the project will work on increasing the ability of the local residents to store water in 13 targeted communities of Jericho and Jordan Valley especially the middle communities. The activities can be summarized as the following: 1- Construction of 6km of pipelines, these pipelines vary in diameter, length and aim. A- 6 inch, 4 inch and (2 inch for small communities) water carrier pipelines: these pipelines will carry the water from the water source (well, spring) to the constructed 250m3 metallic reservoirs or to the rehabilitated 1000m3 metallic reservoirs. B- 3 inch and 2 inch pipelines: these pipelines will be used to carry the water from reservoirs to the best place in the targeted communities providing them with safe and close filling point for their water trucking, reducing the costs of transportation and ensuring the water availability. C- 4inch, 3 inch and 2 inch pipelines: these will be used to carry the water from the rehabilitated 1000m3 metallic water reservoirs to the HHs in Al-Zubaidat and Marj Na'jeh communities. 2- Construction of two 250m3 metallic drinking water reservoirs. These reservoirs aim to ensure the availability of water near the targeted communities and especially the Bedouin communities that does not have a proper, regular and safe water supply. These tanks are vital for Bedouin communities as they ensure major decrease in the costs of transportation and water prices. They also decrease the needed time to transport water. The construction process will include choosing the best place for the tank, the implementation and the testing and operation process. All works will be ensured to be according to specifications and safety measures. 3- Rehabilitation of a 1000 m3 metallic drinking water reservoirs. This reservoir is existing and it is located in Marj Na'jeh , it was constructed long time ago and is not being used in the current time due to major defect in the metallic reservoir's body and inner core. The reservoir is important to the community as it can be an extra water supply source. It can be used again after proper fixing and maintenance works to decrease the dependency on the irregular and expensive supply by the Israeli company Mecarot. The construction process will include identifying the main defect in the reservoir, fixing and maintenance works and finally operating and testing. 4- Distribution of 140 PE drinking water tanks of 5m3 size. Referring to PARC's latest full need assessment in the Jordan Valley areas. One of the most serious problems that faces the local communities in Jordan Valley is their low ability to store drinking water and especially in summer season. This activity aims to provide at least 140 HHs (mostly Bedouins) with 5m3 PE water tanks to increase their ability to store drinking water and especially in the harsh weather in summer season when water becomes rare and expensive. 5- Organizing ten awareness campaigns and capacity building workshops PARC believes that rising the awareness of the residents of local communities is not less important than the other activities of the project. Hence, PARC and with support of professionals will organize 10 awareness campaigns and capacity building workshops in the targeted communities to improve the skills and abilities of people regarding the related issues of water management, safe use of water, best practices, hygienic activities, sterilization and many other topics. These sessions and trainings will try to cover as much people as possible with special focus on women and PWDs. 6- Formulation of eleven Community Protection Committees (CPCs), prepare them and give them the needed trainings. These CPCs are responsible for assuring the safety and protection procedures in the targeted communities, during and after the implementation of the project. These committees will be trained in details on how to deal with any protection incidents that take place while and after the implementation of the project, including how to contact the responsible sides like OCHA and others. These CPCs are being formed in approximately all PARC's projects and they proved their worth in many cases and places. The previous activities were summarized briefly, full details and breakdowns will be elaborated in the full proposal, including the exact lengths and diameters of pipelines in different location, the exact locations of water reservoirs and the full details of distributing the PE tanks in addition to exact locations and times for the awareness sessions and capacity building trainings. All the pipelines and their fittings that will be used will be according to the Palestinian standards PS.421 and PS.425 by the PWA and in line with the WASH technical manual. Moreover, Metallic reservoirs, PE tanks will be according to the WASH technical manual of June 2021, which PARC and partners participated in it.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a well-articulated feedback and response mechanism (FRM) that is put in action from the design phase until closing. FRM at PARC is ensured with respect to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). The CHS adopted by PARC will ensure to provide a safe/dignified space for targeted population to express their feedback and complaints. In June 2021, PARC updated its accountability policies (information sharing, complaints and feedback and participation) and the Safeguarding policy hand on hand with Accountability specialist from Christian Aid. Accordingly, PARC consulted with local communities to identify the most appropriate channels suitable for them. Hence, a dedicated email (Complaints@pal-arc.org), complaint boxes distributed across the governorates, in addition to the MEAL Officer phone number (Whatts App). These three channels will be utilized to receive feedback and complaints. Then, based on PARC accountability policies the complaint shall be registered into a well-designed complaint form that will be tracked and documented through the MEAL documentation system. Additionally, all project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer within the project MEAL framework. With reference to CHS, complaint mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience will be encouraged to claim their rights. Field staff will announce the availability of the three feedback mechanisms for the local communities and ensure that any complaint or feedback shall be addressed will be taken seriously including any sensitive complaints. Any complaints received shall be protected in accordance to data protection protocols adopted by PARC. Feedback mechanisms will also be promoted and implemented in coordination with local committees (LCs).In regards to safeguarding complaints (SEA), PARC has a well-articulated policy with clear standard data protection procedures and will take any sensitive complaint seriously when proven correct no tolerance policy shall be applied.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No Harm" is seen as a way of ensuring accountability towards the local communities. This approach utilized community participation, information sharing, feedback and response mechanism with respect to Core Humanitarian standards. Which is also in line to environment protection and gender mainstreaming. The designed project adheres to social, political, economic and health context of targeted populations and is based on comprehensive understanding verified through the participatory needs assessment and updated risk analysis. In regards to social context, PARC understands the conservative local Palestinian context and act on that in all project phases taking into consideration the different gender needs such as women’s, PwD, and children have safe access to services. Political context, all proposed interventions will take into consideration the sensitivity of its location in which a low-profile working approach will take place. This will happen by supporting the local community to document the Israeli violations. Additionally, PARC team will report any violation and incidents to official bodies like OCHA. Finally, at economic level the project interventions aid to support local population to empower their production abilities and access to their assets. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analyse potential risks that will be taken into consideration during implementation. PARC team adheres to the developed Standard of Operation SoPs for the implementation of the different activities, field visits, M&E, complaint and feedback mechanism to ensure all safety and security measures are taken against COVID-19. PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholders in the need assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, and community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ensure optimal community participation, outreach, and transparency in service provision. PARC ensures a transparent selection process which avoids social conflict during the implementation. The project is designed based on Human-Rights Based Approaches (HRBA) that includes women, youth and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). HRBA; focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, none discrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human rights instruments are an integral part of PARC work.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at communities. Gender mainstreaming and protection approaches will be integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ensures to engage local community members, VCs and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive understanding of the targeted community’s needs including the participation of women and women led cooperatives and clubs. The findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a Project Local Committee that represents the targeted population and include key actors from the area and representatives of Community Based Organizations which ensures optimal outreach, transparency and ownership to vulnerable community groups. The project Local Committee and PARC team plays a role in ensuring the access to information to all community members through a clear gender sensitive project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and PARC’s social media platforms. PARC team develops a well-defined selection criterion that responds to the needs of the targeted population and ensures accessing the most vulnerable( Children, PwDs, elderly, women headed HH… etc). This will be hand on hand with the project local committee. An open call of applications will also ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ownership, transparency, good governance, gender balance at beneficiary and community level. Weekly meetings will be held between the municipality, project local committee, community members and PARC field officer for each community. PARC accountability system follows a clear mechanism of complaint registration, reporting and solving, is accessible by the municipality and local community members. Regular follow-up of complaints received from communities, VCs, and Municipality are performed with PARC’s M&E unit, including when necessary, addressing OCHA for larger external complaints concerning security and overall occupation related to protection risks. Meaningful access, participation, and empowerment: through the established LCs which ensure optimal outreach to affected and vulnerable target groups. Additionally, PARC will share any human rights-based violations to relevant clusters. Special attention will also be given to people’s access to services under restrictions due to Covid19 outbreak, and online consultations and virtual meetings will be available to targeted beneficiaries to communicate their challenges and voice these needs/rights, which PARC will ensure sharing with relevant stakeholders.
