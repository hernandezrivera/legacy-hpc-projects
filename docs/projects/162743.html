Project Summary

This projects is aiming to prevent and reduce the avoidable mortality and morbidity due to water and vector borne diseases, and hospital acquired infection in Blue Nile, Kassala, Red Sea, Gadarif, North Darfur, South Darfur, West Darfur, Central Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordufan, West kordufan and White Nile. WHO as lead for vector control, will continue supporting MoH and partners to implement integrated vector management campaigns in response to acute outbreaks and increased vector population to mitigate / prevent imminent threats resulted from floods and displacement and keep on monitoring vector abundance and to ensure prevention of morbidity and moralities related to communicable diseases among Internally Displaced Persons and vulnerable population in Blue Nile, Kassala, Red Sea, Gadarif, North Darfur, South Darfur, West Darfur, Central Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordufan, West kordufan and White Nile; a comprehensive package of water quality and safety activities including community based integrated prevention of multiple WASH related diseases and control of vector borne diseases through breaking the mosquito life cycle and confine adult vector density and strengthen hazard control within health facilities.Water quality activities will be implemented by the MoH and WASH partners at state and national level with sustained support from WHO, complementing the UNICEF and WASH partners
intervention. This year, the focus will be at locality level, to promote efficiency by reducing
the cost of sample transport from field to states capital cities and enabling the local
authorities to conduct regular water sampling and testing in prioritized high risk locations. The newly established dashboard will provide different information on bacteriological and sanitary risk categorization and detailed status on the functionality of water sources. This will assist in identifying high risk areas and prediction of potential outbreaks to timely accelerate interventions to mitigate/ prevent the outbreak occurrence. Additionally, the functionality status mapping will provide data to partners in order to maintain, rehabilitate or replace water sources to increase access to sustainable drinking water supply.
The main activities related to this component includes training of environmental health officers/workers (MoH and WES), community volunteers and hygiene promoters on water testing using portable kits; sanitary inspection of water sources; proper water sources maintenance and house-hold chlorination, treatment and storage. The results of the regular monitoring of water sources quality and sanitary conditions will allow timely identification of health threats and implementation of corrective measures through information sharing with all relevant partners. Close information sharing and consultations with
Health sector will facilitate the early warning on identified public health alerts related to water and vector transmission and joint planning of control measures.
WHO comprehensive package of vector control activities includes; training of the environmental health officers, Community Health Workers from MoH/WES in the selected 21 localities/locations on the integrated vector management and entomological surveillance, printing of the IEC material on the common vector, technical and logistic support to vector control campaigns and vector surveillance mission. The implementation will take into account the specific demographics of the IDP camps in targeted states with high population density, and also be directed by health information data on prevalence, vector density and trends of the diseases.

1 - Needs

Access to effective sanitation facilities and the practice of good sanitation and hygiene behaviors are critical for human health, dignity and economic development. The situation in Sudan is complex with a combination of protracted emergencies and economic crisis in rural and urban contexts with access problems to reach the vulnerable people in conflict areas, predominantly in parts of South Kordufan, Blue Nile and Jabel Marra. As per the recent S3M survey; 32.80% of the population of Sudan currently still practices open defecation, only 38.54% have access to improved sanitation (excreta disposal), 60.5% have access to basic drinking water sources and only 7.54% of the population knowing the 5 key hand washing times. During 2019; there were large scale of multiple disease outbreaks, including water and vector borne diseases. Cholera is confirmed in three states, Dengue Fever in six, Rift Valley Fever in five states, Chikungunya in three states, in addition to increased malaria above the epidemic threshold in almost all states. It is estimated that only 62% of schools and 59 % of HFs (HFS 2017 FMOH) have access to basic sanitation, 20% have a place for hand-washing with soap and 85% have garbage around the school. Environmental health management and infection control in health facilities faces many challenges (51% have no water supply) including a lack of allocated budget for maintenance, lack of monitoring and auditing system, although work is on-going to improve the situation. Hospital acquired infection was documented during cholera outbreak in Sinnar and Blue Nile hospitals due to poor WASH status in these health facilities. During 2019 and yet; there were large scale multiple outbreaks including; vector borne diseases; Dengue fever with 3708, Rift Valley fever 358, chikungunya with 214 cases, malaria 2.1 million cases, and expectations of increasing challenges with the progress of climate change. Aedes mosquito is present in Sudan in 17 out of 18 states transmitting different arbovirus diseases and causing a considerable number of deaths, the pattern of these diseases is increasing globally as well as in Sudan due to the effect of climate change, urbanization, water scarcity and poor housing. As women are most affect (61 - 66%) special considerations will be taken to ensure protection of women and elder people. as the vast of cases occurring in the middle age 15 - 45. Older adults with underlying diseases are at higher risk. Vector control is the main strategy for controlling these diseases as no treatment or vaccines are available. Vector related surveillance capacities to predict/warn on potential outbreaks are weak or non-existent in most targeted States and the recent vector borne disease outbreaks showed the high need for more spraying equipment and maintenance or replacement for the old ones. There is increasing resistance to key insecticides and a need to build community capacities to use mechanical means of protection and handle the indoor breeding of mosquito in addition to acceleration of vector control measures using additional effective and environmentally friendly strategies. Water borne diseases including Cholera outbreak (343 cases) typhoid remain a big challenge triggered by use of open sources and water transfer by donkey cart. Only 35% of the targeted localities have some drinking water surveillance system. There is a lack of trained human capacity at Locality level due to rapid turnover as well as limited equipment and supplies (source is WHO/MoH water quality monitoring system 2018 and situation analysis of 2019) Most health facilities do not have strong EH/S&H/WASH systems, with most medical waste being mixed with general waste and disposed of at landfill or near to health facilities exposing the patients, staff and the public to health risks. Where incinerators exist they tend to be traditional single chamber versions. Health care and hazardous wastes pose particular challenges for Sudan.

The results of the regular monitoring of water sources quality and sanitary conditions will allow timely identification of health threats and implementation of corrective measures through information sharing with all relevant partners. The implementation will take into account the specific demographics of the IDP camps in targeted states with high population density, and also be directed by health information data on prevalence, vector density and trends of the diseases.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Distinct attention to the protection of the environment and natural resources is given by WHO during the implementation of the project with medium impact on the environment. The main hazard is the waste generated by the vector control chemical activities. This hazard is reduced with several strategies; promote alternative methods to the chemical management and usage of the insecticide such as the habitat and environmental control, adoption of the gravitraps for adults which contribute to the control of gravid adult mosquito while searching for egg lay positions and reducing contact with the incriminated vectors. However chemical control is still considered as the most important element in the integrated control of vector borne diseases especially during the outbreaks where the vector density exceeded the reference threshold. During such situation WHO has already developed plan for the proper disposal of the chemical generated during the process and accordingly WHO will ensure that the staff are trained and the necessary supplies are made available. Management of the potentially infectious wastes from health activities will be segregated and dealt with in away that prevent environmental contamination and WHO will reduce reliance on waste incineration and install high temperature quality incinerators with treatment units to reduce risks of air pollution.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO will ensure the ease of access by the targeted population will be needs based and without any constraints such as discrimination, or any other constraints. WHO will also enhance the ownership of the community for project activities rasing the awareness of the communities during sessions and trainings to address the social cultural barriers that affect GBV survivors from accessing services and assistance. Activities of risk reduction to ultimately increase the targeted communities’ level of resilience, awareness and capacity when faced with humanitarian hardships and to improve all aspects of protection for children. The project will additionally contribute to achieving sustainable social development through, strengthening local partners on both the national and states level. WHO will also apply the standard measures for the containment of vector control remedies to ensure and prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm.Efforts will be made to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unanticipated or unintentional negative effects on the targeted population, which could potentially expose them to further physical and risks. By design, the project targeted vulnerable communities that requires protection intervention such as IDPs and women population. The project also adheres to WHO PSEA standards.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Vector control measures and community based water safety plan activities are designed, implemented and monitored with the participation of the beneficiary communities, Federal and state Ministries of health and partners to ensure WHO accountability and transparency of the program. Accountability to affected people at all levels is a core value of WHO work. Upon the commencement of this project and following the discussions during the planning phase; WHO will sensitize the community about the project objectives and activities and the importance of community involvement in implementation and feedback. A series of Focus Group Discussions (FGD) will take place during all project phases for different gender and age groups. Joint field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. WHO through its capacity building program is delivering key messages on increasing community ownership and leader ship to ensure efficiency and sustainability of the project, and how this will be achieved through collaboration of different entities and areas where the lead will be taken by communities and the role of WHO and other partners. Because WHO efforts are made to prevent morbidity and moralities resulting form Communicable diseases; immediate feedback is usually shred at the field level with the community on vector surveillance, vector control measures and water quality/safety and sanitary inspection results. This timely feedback is aiming to inform the community on the deficiencies, their impact on health and the responsible body for undertaking corrective measures which some times include the communities them selves.
