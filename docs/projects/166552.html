Project Summary

For an agro-based economy like Zimbabwe, agriculture is the main catalyst for post-COVID-19 recovery. The premise of resilience is about bouncing from a pandemic, local authorities and cities like Harare and others that own urban mass food markets are important pockets of resilience and restoration.
It is important to pay special attention to urgent needs of the food supply system to curb the spread of disease given its vastness and physical contact nature. The food supply system is mostly informal which, according to the International Monetary Fund (IMF) is 60.6%of Zimbabwe’s economy. This means livelihoods of millions of people depend on it. Knowledge Transfer Africa (KTA) estimates that more than 90% share of food produced by smallholders finds its way to consumers through mass markets, traders and vendors. Whilst this system has been declared essential, it is paramount to implement measures to mitigate the spread of the Covid-19 through maintaining safe food markets. Urban market vendors who are predominantly women continue work in hostile environments with limited access to basic infrastructure and services and they trade for long hours (up to 10 hours a day) under unhygienic, uncomfortable and often unsafe conditions. COVID-19 has depleted coping mechanisms, eroded their resilience, and left this group extremely vulnerable as they have no ability to adapt to changes in market conditions and to cope with any external shocks and vulnerabilities.
This proposal aims to provide stabilisation and immediate relief and recovery for the most affected populations through emergency livelihoods and establishment and operationalization of resilient food supply chains that are safe and fully functional during the Covid-19 outbreak and beyond. Embedded within resilient food supply chains are knowledge supply chains comprising statistics and real-time qualitative insights. The end goal is to mitigate against disruptions to national food supply chain system in Zimbabwe in the face of disease-related shocks. The proposed programme intends to support Government’s s urgent efforts towards decentralization/decongestion of mass markets through development of coordination mechanisms (strengthening of online platform and offline mechanisms), as well as refurbishment of market infrastructure with health safety measures in place. This aims to make sure that total close down of these important livelihood stream is averted and that poor households whose lives depend on this are saved from food and income insecurity and further exposure to Covid-19 and other diseases associated with such market systems.
