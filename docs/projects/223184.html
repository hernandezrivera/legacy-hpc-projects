Project Summary

UNICEF will contribute to the inter-agency humanitarian response in 2025 and will also complement UNICEF's development work under its 2023-2026 country programme.
Objective: UNICEF is committed to continuing to focus on critical health unmet needs and remaining inequalities related to maternal, neonatal and under-five child survival.
Project Description: The project focuses on improving access and quality of basic services to address the multiple health deprivations faced by children, adolescents and pregnant women. UNICEF's response incorporates a child-centred approach and is built around community settings, including health centers and surrounding communities. Priority will be given to supporting health systems that reliably provide integrated service packages for children, adolescents and women, focusing on health, nutrition and WASH, also facilitating child protection services and linked to social protection and social well-being initiatives, as well as as well as the educational sector, particularly for health promotion and prevention, improving health literacy, screening and promoting early childhood development and the mental health and well-being of children and their caregivers.
Strategies and implementation: UNICEF will promote different approaches to achieve the goals:
1. Ensure the provision of critical life-saving interventions: a) primary care and high-impact interventions in maternal and child health; b) prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV, as well as care for children and adolescents with HIV; and c) proper implementation of the national immunization plan.
2. Strengthen health systems, including emergency preparedness, response and resilience, and system information.
3. Improving access to quality care tailored to the needs of school-age children and adolescents.
4. Promotion of integrated and multisectoral policies and programs.
The strategy also includes:
Strengthen service delivery, empower communities for health promotion, prevention and social behavior change, build capacity of health service providers, strengthen supply chain systems (essential products and equipment), generate demand and strengthen accountability.
Monitoring and evaluation: To ensure the effectiveness of the project, UNICEF will implement a robust monitoring and evaluation system. This will involve high frequency monitoring of health interventions, evaluations of health professionals after training and monitoring of the distribution and use of supplies, as well as evaluation of the quality of case management based on compliance with standardized protocols and regulations. To get this goal, UNICEF will provide supplies and register forms according to MoH standards and will contribute to retention and training of critical staff, for data processing.
These actions will be implemented in selected urban, peri-urban and remote rural areas of 10 nationally prioritized states, with the exception of HIV interventions and immunization coverage is national, and will reach 596,000 people, including 406,000 children, 165,000 pregnant women and 25,000 health workers.
