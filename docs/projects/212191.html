Project Summary

• Needs.
At HNO 2024 FAS PIN is 15.4 M people. Agricultural production still does not meet the full need. This applies to most crop production, as well as livestock production. The drivers of low production include a continuously deteriorating economic situation, a decline in exchange rate and purchasing power of most agriculture-dependent populations, high prices of agricultural production inputs, weather anomalies, water scarcity, scarcity of energy supplies for various farm and off-farm activities, which impacts on supply chains and transportation costs, leading to an increase in production costs. livestock were impacted by low access to pastures, high prices of feed, disruption of their supply chains the low availability of public veterinary services. Livestock is a major source for livelihood in rural areas, it is also one of the common activities practiced by women. Supporting this sector and people depending on it is very important for income generation, nutrition improvement and women empowerment.
•Intervention, Implementation of activities:
FAO will assist 110,000 breeder HH’s (660,000 people).
Output 1: Livestock productivity for 96,500 HH is improved.
Provide fodder for 30,000 HH (300 kg / HH). Provide veterinary services for 66,500 HH.
Output 2. number of livestock is restored and improved through artificial insemination.
Output 1: Livestock productivity for 96,500 HH improved by:
1. provision of feed: 9,000 tonnes fodder for 30,000 HH
2. emergency animal health support: 2.24 M doses of FMDV, Ecto/Endo parasite treatments for 66,500 HH;
3. Training vet’s on vaccinations and managing Trans Boundary Animal Diseases
4. Training the technicians and herders on GMP and raising awareness on GANaH .
Output 2 number of livestock is restored and improved for 13,500 HH through artificial insemination:
1. Artificial insemination for livestock (2 /HH)
2. provide training for veterinarians on artificial insemination (particularly timing)
3. raising awareness for targeted herders on improved management for livestock to ensure success of artificial insemination
All the activities under each outputs will address specific needs of different gender and age groups.
*Protection Risk Analysis
1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted due being selected by project/risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries’ selection. Mitigation: Different categories of affected population.
2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation: local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria; and effective communication for guidance in local language.
3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation: community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.
