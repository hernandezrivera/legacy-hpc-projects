Project Summary

This project aims to improve access to Primary Health Care with a specific focus on provision of integrated sexual and reproductive health services (SRH) in area C of the West Bank to ensure lifesaving care for especially women and children in the most vulnerable and remote communities. The selected target locations of this project proposal are the same locations that UNFPA/PMRS currently working on however the current project will be completed by Dec 31st 2022. To anticipate and avoid any gap in the service provision in these locations after Dec 2022 this project aims as well to continue the services in these locations.
This will be achieved through the following main activities:
1. Provision of health services that are needed due to the humanitarian crisis by
supporting Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMR) to run primary health care level 2 mobile clinics including provision of SRH services for women, girls, young people including people with disabilities.
2. Strengthening the capacity of SRH providers in the targeted locations on GBV detection and referral, and training on SRH issues including Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) to sustain vital SRH services during emergencies.
3. Enhance accessibility of PHC and SRH services for persons with disabilities.
4. Community outreach on essential SRH i.e. danger signs in pregnancy, family planning, etc. Beside other public health issues, and NCDs with particular focus on breast Cancer BC.

1 - Needs

The West Bank is in a state of chronic crisis driven by the effects of the long-standing Israeli occupation, access restrictions and limitations on movement. This continues to increase the vulnerability and marginalization of vast areas such as those in Area C, Hebron H2 area, and specific population groups within East Jerusalem. These communities face particular obstacles to planning and development, demolition orders that make their lives and livelihoods uncertain and hinder provision of basic services such as the building of permanent health clinics. Specifically, for Area C, an area that represents 60 percent of the West Bank and is home to about 300,000 Palestinians, the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment 2022 revealed that the major constraints to access health care services are due to the unavailability of health facilities within reasonable distance and the high costs of accessing services including transportation costs. In many of these areas, the journey to the nearest permanent health care clinic has lengthened significantly because of unpredictable checkpoint delays and the need to travel circuitous ways around settlements and barriers. This results in basic health care needs of these population groups being overlooked such as sexual and reproductive health (SRH) concerns; including family planning preventing maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality as well as detection of GBV and interventions targeting GBV survivors. Due to the high demand of health care services within these vulnerable areas, the services made available there are less responsive to the specific needs of the women, girls, and men in these areas including the availability of specialized health care staff. The HNO 2023 report estimates that a total of 164,073 Palestinians living in communities located in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2 and the closed area behind the separation wall as people in need, who continue to suffer from limited or no access to primary healthcare. Based on data from the Mobile Clinics Working Group, these areas are therefore in need of alternative modalities of service provision of which mobile clinics offer a vital lifeline by bringing services to the doorsteps of the vulnerable communities and ensure the needed continuum of care. Through UNFPA monitoring and observations during field visits to the mobile clinics, it has been recommended to diversify the health care service delivery modality of the mobile clinics to also include home visits to help reach the most vulnerable groups such as women of high risk pregnancy and persons with disabilities. Beside the alternative modalities of SRH service provision, it is recommended to support the MoH PHC services and the PHC care in general in the proximity to area C which is also facing a lack and inability to sustain the SRH and GBV services due to staff shortage within MoH and the change in priorities during COVID-19 which channeled the majority of resources to combat the epidemic and its consequences. Even when services are made available, there is a decrease in overall demand due to movement restrictions, lack of public transportation, loss of wages and inability to afford private transportation to services. This situation has clearly pointed out the need to improve the coordination among service providers and jointly establish unified referral pathways for emergency situations specially to link the local level better with the national level. This will positively impact the quality, availability, and accessibility of health care services including the SRH services.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Support the continuation of mobile clinics services provision for PMRS in area C of the West Bank to provide L 2 PHC based on the minimum service package of the Mobile Clinic Working Group (MCWG) to ensure universal coverage and the continuum of SRH lifesaving care services for women, girls, and youth by integrating the GBV, and youth services within the PHC. The project will support four mobile clinics run by PMRS to enable these clinics to continue delivering services in the areas where MoH has limited access. The PMRS has extensive experience in providing mobile clinic services in area C and is well known and accepted in these communities. UNFPA and PMRS have established coordination mechanisms to run and monitor the overall implementation of the project, through monthly coordination meetings where both parties share information, challenges, and successes.The project will enhance the role of the midwives by involving midwives as part the core staff of the mobile clinic. Their role will complement other staff functions in providing continuum of lifesaving SRH services not only institute based but also their role will be extended to reach women, and girls with limited access through midwifery home visits led counseling sessions. The midwifery-led counseling home visits will provide counseling for family planning, Antenatal care for pregnant women, and support relieving symptoms of postpartum depression by providing psychosocial support to women in need. Activity 2. Strengthening the capacity of SRH providers in the targeted locations on GBV detection and referral pathways and providing training on SRH concerns including Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) to sustain SRH services during emergencies. The training module will focus on the capacity of the teams to detect and refer to SGBV based on the national GBV referral system and pathways. The training materials and preparation will be designed based on PMRS long experience in conducting such training with UNFPA technical support. Such capacity building activities are usually expanded to involve other interested partners’ mobile clinic staff and these clinics belonging to MoH in nearby locations. This will improve coordination and strengthen referral pathways among SRH stakeholders during emergencies. Activity 3. Enhance accessibility of PHC and SRH services for persons with disabilities: The project will ensure that services will be accessible for persons with disabilities. The project will contribute to the adaptations of small-scale physical and information accessibility for a number of health care facilities under the MoH and those premises that usually host the mobile clinics. In parallel, technical support for MoH health care staff and the PMRS mobile teams will be provided through sensitization sessions and technical training to ensure that they have the ability to interact and provide quality SRH services for persons with disabilities. This will be done in partnership with an organization dealing with persons with disabilities (OPD) who will be consulted on the capacity building process and will be responsible for implementing the training as well as follow up on practices. Activity 4. Community outreach and awareness raising on SRH issues, psychosocial coping mechanisms and contingency plans in case of emergency. Conduct educational sessions for women and pregnant women in coordination and collaboration with all SRH service providers in the targeted areas on high risk pregnancies and post-natal care among other SRH issues. Beside the SRHR topics, the project will provide awareness raising sessions on non-communicable diseases including for cancer (with a special focus on breast cancer), diabetes, and other major topics in this regard. Planned group sessions with adolescent girls in those targeted locations will also be organized to increase awareness on topics identified together with the target group such as menstrual hygiene, early marriage, adolescence health and psychosocial issues etc.

The proposed project components and activities will be integrated into the annual work plan between UNFPA and the implementing partner, PMRS, for which a detailed monitoring and evaluation plan will be established to specify the actions necessary to track progress on project results and ensure quality assurance of the interventions through a predefined set of quantitative and qualitative indicators. 5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The service delivery modality was chosen based on assessments showing the lack of adequate existing services for this particular targeted population in Area C. As MoH is limited in providing primary health care services in this area and health care providers are restricted in maintaining fixed facilities, the modality of Mobile Clinics is considered to be the best way to care for the health needs of these communities. The modality is chosen in agreement with MoH, Health Cluster partners and planned in accordance with the identified needs of the Mobile Clinic Working Group during the HNO and HRP preparations for 2023. To avoid duplication with other partners UNFPA had close coordination with those relevant partners in the mobile clinic working group on the selection locations to make sure that the project will fill the gap and focused on those locations where there no partners working in. Moreover the selected target locations of this project proposal are the same locations that UNFPA/PMRS currently working on however the current project will be completed by Dec 31st 2022. To anticipate and avoid any gap in the service provision in these locations after Dec 2022 this project aims as well to continue the services in these locations.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback from beneficiaries through the various established mechanisms is a central element of community participation in this project and UNFPA and its partner are committed to continuous feedback collection and accountable to the incoming feedback from community members. Therefore, UNFPA, in close collaboration with PMRS and other stakeholders in Area C, will accelerate the work of establishing local advisory committees which are to be represented by a diverse group of local community members with regards to gender, age and disability status. The purpose of these committees are to provide UNFPA and its implementing partner with guidance on needs and priorities in the targeted areas and to discuss feedback on quality and timeliness of services and any other concerns. These committees will increase accountability and transparency with the affected population and boost stronger local participation and sustainability of the services. Furthermore, incoming feedback and data is an important source for UNFPA and its implementing partner for generation of lessons learned and best practices which inform the ongoing activity implementation to ensure that the needs of the beneficiaries are met in a quality and dignified manner. As an example of how community feedback has influenced the Mobile Clinic service delivery, each team now also includes a midwife focusing on home visits to follow up with women in postpartum and with family planning needs as well as to provide counseling to pregnant and lactating women. This modality increases the accessibility for those women who for various reasons have no time, opportunity or wish to leave the house, which act as a barrier to many women in attending the services at the facility.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Awareness of the existence of feedback mechanisms is a prerequisite for beneficiaries making use of them. The feedback and complaint mechanisms will be communicated to the targeted population through various channels to make sure all community members learn about their existence e.g. during the awareness/educational sessions, one-on-one sessions with the PSS staff worker, during UNFPA field visits, posters with the contact information on the email and hotline mechanisms. To make sure that beneficiaries not only know of the feedback mechanism, but also are able to access them, a diverse set of mechanisms have been chosen according to the beneficiaries’ preferred needs and abilities. The hotline is often a preferred mechanism due to its easy accessibility also for those unable or uncomfortable with filing a written feedback or complaint. However, it cannot stand alone as not all community members, especially not women outside the labor market, necessarily have their own phone or a phone with credit. Furthermore, UNFPA field visits will help reach beneficiaries who might not be able to access other feedback mechanisms, where the UNFPA team will meet them in person and provide a space for sharing in privacy and confidentiality. Home visits will be considered for those who are not able to participate in outdoor activities due to severe impairments or other limitations.Other accessibility measures for different disability types will be considered in the communication with persons with disabilities such as sign language interpretation and text info printed in the braille alphabet for those with visual impairments.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No Harm” is a guiding principle for UNFPA in upholding its mandate and commitment to vulnerable and marginalized groups and women in particular. UNFPA adopts a mainstream integration of protection and prevention measures across its operations and programmes, such as PSEA, risk mitigation reviews and ensures that interventions are adapted accordingly. With regards to protection and safety priorities, facilities used by the Mobile Clinics have been selected from a set of criteria identified to enhance the safety and protection and minimize threats and vulnerabilities whenever possible. On a technical medical level, the facilities and personnel abide by Infection Prevention Control IPC guidelines to ensure that patients do not face additional health risks when coming to seek care. In terms of infrastructure, facilities seek to have a waiting area with seating capacity, protection from the sun. Facilities also seek to have at least two separate rooms for private consultations, which are staffed by a female and male doctor respectively to make sure beneficiaries feel dignified and safe when sharing their health concerns. To the extent possible, the villages are visited by the same Mobile Clinic staff from week to week to ensure continuity and build trust with the community members. Trust, privacy and confidentiality are of particular importance to successful interventions in these remote and traditional areas, as community members might face stigma or social control due to health complications or concerns. For this reason, the staff hired for the mobile clinics have to sign, abide and receive training on code of conduct to uphold the rights and dignity of those seeking care. At an organizational level, the selected implementing partner has successfully undergone strict UNFPA partnership selection criteria and vetting which includes assessment of PSEA commitments and policies. In this project, partners and other actors will be trained and mentored to delivernon-judgmental, disability-sensitive and high-quality integrated PHC/SRHR services ensuring confidentiality, privacy and respect for personal choices related to the care of those seeking help.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The very essence of this project is to enable equal and impartial access to primary health care and SRH services. According to needs assessments, the marginalized population in Area C suffer from multiple barriers to basic services which this project aims to overcome by bringing the services to the doorstep of the communities through the Mobile Clinic modality. The project design takes into consideration the specific needs of the affected population through a gender-, age- and disability-sensitive lense since the need for SRH and accessibility to services are different from group to group and area to area. The targeted areas have a high fertility rate and presence of women staying at their homes looking after their kids while husbands work outside the communities. Thus the Mobile Clinics have a special focus on caring for the SRH needs of the targeted women to improve their quality of life. The project is designed to provide equaland impartial accessibility without barriers and discrimination through all planned activities. Impartiality is upheld through striving for large outreach and accessibility by ensuring proper coordination with other operating actors. These comprehensive awareness and educational sessions serve to highlight and promote women, girls, and young people’s right to health care and how to obtain it. Thus communication plays a central role in ensuring accessibility by developing a diversified communication strategy in order to ensure reaching everyone, especially those at risk of exclusion. The project also offers a diversified set of service provision in order to widen the reach and fulfill the needs, through existing health clinics (fixed and mobile) in addition to home visits when applicable and enhance services and interventions through other channels such as phone and virtual platforms. This will address barriers in terms of movement restrictions that prevent people from seeking help. As part of ensuring equitable access, this project has a dedicated focus and budget for people with disabilities in terms of accessible infrastructure adaptations such as ramps, railings etc. as well as information and communication means like braille and sign language interpretation at health facilities to minimize barriers for full participation and inclusion in service provision.
