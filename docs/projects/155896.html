Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates number of crisis-affected population in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria at 11.7 million people (49.8% male and 50.2% female) out of which 4.7 million and 5 million live in areas of major severity of needs (inter-sector severity 3) and acute severity of needs (inter-sector severity 4-6) respectively. The above include 6.2 million protracted Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs); 1.4 million returnees; 324,000 IDPs in hard to reach areas, 1.6 million population movements and the rest overburdened host communities. In addition, 480,000 Palestine refugees are considered part of the people in need. 67% of the 11.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria according to Syria Response Access Dynamic December report (OCHA, 2018). Overall, 9 million of people are estimated to be food insecure and at risk of food insecurity by Food Security Sector.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 40,000 crisis-affected households (equivalent to 240,000 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to enhance resilience of food insecure rural population in areas with high concentration of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in Syria and expected project outputs are:
Output 1: Income generation capacities of crisis-affected households increased.
Output 2: Food production capacities of crisis-affected population increased.
Output 3: Critical Local agriculture infrastructures rehabilitated through labour-intensive modality.
Output 4: Availability of inputs for multi-commodities processing units for fruits and vegetables increased.
Output 5: Capacities for honey production for crisis affected households Increased.
It is expected that the project support will improve food and nutrition security of crisis-affected small-scale farmers and specifically the project will diversity diets through increase income and create employment for the most vulnerable members of the households especially for women, youth and elderly which will lead to improvement of livelihoods, increase local availability of food, increase self-reliance and improve food and nutrition security of smallholder crisis-affected households especially the most vulnerable ones (women, youth and elderly).

Needs

Rural livelihoods continue to be severely affected by the current Syria crisis. Wide spread displacements, massive destruction of productive assets, severe disruptions of input and output markets and frequent impacts of climate related shocks have affected livelihoods of rural crisis-affected households and tested their resilience to the limit. Livelihood assets losses, unemployment, deepening poverty, inflation and steep depreciation of Syrian pounds have further eroded capacities of families to meet basic needs. Household food security and economic situation remains dismal with an estimated 9 million people in need of livelihoods or emergency employment and sustainable income generating opportunities. Agricultural production in Syria were further hampered by a combination of decreasing availability and increased prices of key agricultural inputs. In addition, continuing population displacement due to insecurity have created a shortage of labor force leading into an increase in daily wages beyond financial means of many farmers. With so many adverse factors now stacked against them, increasing numbers of farmers are left with limited options to survive. Support to agriculture livelihoods through income generating activities, establishment and implementation of conditional cash transfers and labor intensive schemes for the rehabilitation of damaged critical agricultural infrastructure in severely affected areas will be key in strengthening the resilience of both IDPs and host communities. The proposed project aims at using a combination of in kind and cash transfer (conditional) modalities to help strengthen the resilience of rural households and improve their livelihoods. This will improve production capacities, address economic vulnerabilities, complement rural household’s income particularly those severely affected by the current crisis and address immediate needs whilst strengthening the absorptive capacity of vulnerable populations. Through conditional cash transfer component, the project aims to provide temporary employment and sources of income for vulnerable households with sufficient labor while undertaking light rehabilitation and reparation of community assets critical to agriculture. The project will seek to utilize intensive unskilled labor in order to reach maximum possible community participation and covering the widest possible are to help vulnerable people meet their basic needs. Strengthening capacities of local institutions to undertake labour-intensive work can contribute to poverty alleviation by generating decent formal and informal employment that are productive and provide them with income for immediate family needs. Women associations (WA) were created in Syria to diversify and increase community and household income by earlier programme. It essential to strengthen the capacity of women associations and any other groups to increase food production and income. The proposed interventions will be implemented through WAs, beekeepers associations and other groups/associations to be selected in close corporation with community leaders in Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Ar Raqqa, Deir ez Zor, Al hasakeh, Homs, Lattakia, Dara, As Sweidda, Quneitra and Idleb governorates especially in those sub-districts which experienced high population displacements recently. The proposed interventions will be implemented under direct oversight of FAO Syria with support from line ministries, directorates, departments and NGOs as may be required.

Implementation of activities

Currently FAO is operating in thirteen governorates – Aleppo, Al Hassakeh, Ar Raqqa, Dar’a, Deir ez Zor, Hama, Homs, Idleb, As Sweida, Quneitra, Rural Damascus, Tartous and Lattakia. In governorates where UN staff presence is not permitted by UN security regulations, FAO operates through government seconded officers, NGOs and service providers who are directly supporting implementation of activities and report to FAO. FAO Representation in Syria will be responsible for the overall implementation of the project backstopped by FAO headquarters with technical support of FAO’s multidisciplinary team in the Regional Office for the Near East and North Africa. FAO works very closely with MAAR to ensure that the project goals are achieved through proper technical support and monitoring. The implementation of the project will be further supported by:• A central coordination committees composed FAO, relevant MAAR directorates, community-based organizations and implementing partners. The committee’s role is to support project implementation and ensure application of the criteria for beneficiaries' selection.• Local teams at village/district level, led by FAO and composed of local community representatives (traditional leader or “Moukhtar”), extension workers and implementing partners respectively will be in charge of selecting project beneficiaries in line with FAO established set of criteria that take into account gender and age sensitive considerations.• FAO Resilience Officers in the targeted governorates will operationally and technically supervise project implementation.The proposed actions will benefit from existing capacity which can be scaled up whenever required. Main activities include: 1) Selection of project area (districts and sub-districts) base on assessments, discussions with relevant authorities and community leaders.2) Identification of locally base implementing partners and service providers in close collaboration with local leaders. 3) Development of detailed beneficiaries' selection criteria in consultation with key partners and stakeholders. The selection criteria will take into considerations gender, age and other vulnerabilities factors.4) Participatory identification of projects to be supported or rehabilitation work to be undertaken in close collaboration with leaders and communities. 5) Identification of the most appropriate mechanism or modalities (e.g. cash transfers, vouchers, in kind input distributions or others) to provide livelihood support to selected households base on local needs.6) Support targeted households in the development of micro projects and business plans. 7) Procurement of inputs and equipment required for implement of identified micro projects.8) Rehabilitation of identified micro-projects which may be agricultural infrastructure such as water harvesting structures or wells through conditional cash transfer.9) Provide technical assistance to beneficiaries, community based organizations (CBOs), Government institutions and implementing partners to ensure both effective and optimum use of cash transfer mechanism.9) Provide capacity building and training to ensure adequate utilization of inputs to be provided .10) Timely and effective monitoring to ensure appropriate feedback. The project will target vulnerable and conflict affected households especially IDPs, returnees, overburdened host communities with special focus given to women headed families, landless vulnerable groups, households with elderly and disabled members. NB: All the activities will address specific needs of different gender and age groups.

Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection; Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population example IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth base on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors; Mitigation measures: establishing a local beneficiaries selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place a clear and precise guidance on beneficiaries selection criteria and processes and ensuring an effective communication in local language for the same.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets: Mitigation measures: establish a community based beneficiaries’ selection and community based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements in line with "do not harm" principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit risk of beneficiaries being targeted as a result of the being selected for the project. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well to limit possible frictions between the two and a special attention will be provided to the returnees to help them in their resettlement and resuming their production activities through protecting and rehabilitating their asset. Moreover, the project will target households who receive food support (about 15% of the total targeted breeders) in order to help them shift from receiving food support into producing their own food or able to afford so more food will be available and accessible to people. An accountability to affected population system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place to provide communities with a mechanism to express their grievances. This will be used to make adjustments and guide improvement on the implementation of activities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and assumptions beyond project control and their mitigation measures:1) Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation; Probability: Likely; Impact: Severe; Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; Advocate with Resident Coordinator (RC)/Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) and other instances to regain access. Develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2) Risks: Major displacements/return that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities; Probability: possible; Impact: insignificant; Mitigation measures: Continue with advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities. Advocacy with other organizations to include new arrival in assistance programmes.3) Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers (SP) have capacities to deliver the project activities within changed legal framework. Diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders).4) Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: major; Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas. Establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate implementation of project activities. 5) Risk: Deterioration of the relations between IDPs and host communities in the period/area concerned; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: Insignificant; Mitigation measures: Ensure assistance is balanced and not directed only to one specific groups. Identification of beneficiaries done in partnership with local communities including IDPs and host communities. Select implementing partners and service providers that are acceptable by local communities and aware of conflict sensitivity.6) Risks: Increased control of land and water by parties to the crisis; Probability: Likely; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from a range of sources. Accurate choice of target areas ensuring that farming is possible and beneficiaries will undertake the activity. Advocacy with local authorities by service providers (SPs). If necessary, move to alternative locations previously identified.7) Risks: Possible climatic shocks during implementation period (droughts/floods in particular). Probability: Possible; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Select project areas in different agro ecological zones spread the risks.
