Project Summary

This project responds to critical needs aimed at enhancing protection of civilians through various programmatic responses and in diverse locations in accordance with the National Action Plan on Protection of civilians . The overall objective of the proposal is to contribute to protection of civilians through enhancing community security; unarmed civilian protection, community policing through community based structures legal protection through legal aid lawyers and strengthening of grassroots committees for protection of civilians.

1 - Needs

Sudan is currently going through political upheavals. Two years post the 2019 revolution, protection needs still remain with glaring gaps that will be amplified by the recent military take over on 25 October which threatens the protection gains made thus far including the implementation plans of the national protection of civilians and the JPA. According to the HNO of 2021, 4.5 m people are projected to be in need of protection services - IDPS, returnees refugees and local communities. Further, as a result of localised violence and factional fighting in 2021, thousands of IDPs, returnees and local population thousands of IDPs, returnees and local population were displaced in Central Darfur, North Darfur, South Darfur and West Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan states. Hundreds of villages/houses were burnt, local markets, households assets and livestock looted, many individuals were killed or physically injured. These events and incidents are expected to affect the implementation of the Juba Peace Agreement, security arrangements and hence protection of civilians which is one the agreement main pillars. Furthermore, protection of civilians, in particular, is one of the main pillars of United Nations Integrated Assistance Mission in Sudan (UNITAMS) mandate. In light of the said events and the current political situation and whereas the primary responsibility for Protection of Civilians lies with the Government of Sudan ( Government of Sudan is currently dissolved ) and the failure of the peace agreement parties to form the 12,000 joint security forces ( agreed upon in JPA) and deploy them for protection of civilians, returnees, IDPs, and host communities in Darfur and else where, it is clear that civilians in Darfur and other hotpot areas will not feel safe if left without adequate protection and subject to potential security risks. Furthermore the JPA, it is envisages that many IDPs and refugees will start to return to areas of origin. However, besides the obvious security risks that might emanate from UNAMID exit, there is a likelihood of remnants of exploded ordinances and various munitions lying uncollected in return areas where fighting took place. Similar situations of returnees with a need to protection is expected in South Kordofan, Blue Nile and the Eastern Sudan. Therefore, there will be need for establishment of grassroots committees for protection of civilians through community community policing methods, at least in several localities in Darfur and the Two Areas, besides training and elevating the capacity of community protection networks. Further, enhancing the role legal aid human and rights organizations are essential in ensuring legal protection of civilians from any real and potential abuse. more over, dispute settlement and conflict resolution is extremely important tool in averting occurrence of inter communal conflicts in Darfur and other hot-spots. Such an intervention is vital for guaranteeing protection of civilians through building trustful relationship between IDPs/ host communities, governmental and grassroots committee members as well as through construction of physical facilities for grassroots committees, where the volunteers (GCV). This approach helps instill public confidence in security forces as well as providing community level protection to citizens. In the context of SCR2524, Unarmed Civilian Protection is assuming increasing importance, especially in the context that is defined by the Juba Peace Agreement and the approach of UCP will help in reducing the negative impacts of the exit of UNAMID Peacekeepers.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1: Enhanced community security and protection of civilians by strengthening community policing, Unarmed Civilian Protection and community security structures; 1.1: Promote community policing activities through recruitment and training of community policing volunteers to provide continuous community level protection 15 most affected hot-spots 1.2 Support community grassroots organizations activities aimed at promotion of peaceful inter communal co-existence among IDPs camps population, host communities and returnees through applying durable solutions approaches in 10 selected hot-spots including in IDP camps and return areas.Output 2: Promotion of unarmed civilian protection through: 2. 1: Deployment of national and international protection monitors embedded and working with local youth in 15 locations across the hots posts and return areas.2 .2 : Awareness raising of the targeted beneficiaries including IDPs, returnees and host communities about their human rights and options under durable solutions approaches ;Output 3: Enhancing legal and human rights protection and strengthening local conflict resolution mechanisms, including on land issues though;3.1: Enhancing capacities of the native administration and community structures on dispute settlement techniques related land-based conflicts in the return areas as well as on prevention of conflicts between farmers and pastoralists in 15 hot-spots and return areas; and 3. 2: Provide legal protection to the civilians in the host spot areas through supporting legal aid organizations, paralegals and other community structures in 15 locations including in IDPs camps and return areas.
