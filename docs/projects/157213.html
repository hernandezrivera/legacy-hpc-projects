Project Summary

North Darfur is one of the most hosting returnees and IDPS population in spite of lacks economic access to food due to limited livelihoods and income sources, limited availability of household food stocks, suffers from reduced dietary diversity and high rates of malnutrition. As Sudan continues to face economic challenges, the food insecurity of IDPs, residents, refugees and returnees is expected to worsen. The intervention project is designed to cover the immediate food insecurity and livelihoods diversification skills, lack of knowledge of improved agriculture practice and wide spread of livestock diseases and management of natural resources. This intervention will targeted 800 HHs (newly displaced persons and extremely vulnerable, returnees and host) in Dar Alsalam locality mainly focusing with administrative unit: Sag Al Naam,Gadad Wagif , Gamiza and Hashaba in North Darfur State. The project implementation will cover improvement of food production and productivity of 800 HHS in term of provision of agricultural inputs (seeds (standard seeds package – core pipeline; assets and capacity building training through information sessions on sustainable agricultural practices, 200 vulnerable households will received restocking assistance of 600 ruminants(3 each), 100 HHs will be train in food processing and agribusiness skills and provide them with tools and assets to help them diversify their incomes, vaccination of 20,000 livestock, 100 female will be train in efficient stoves , . BPWO will work with those vulnerable women to maximize the profit they make from their goods. In the way, BPWO will train them on Agro-processing (fuel-efficient stove, production tool kits. The implementation of the activities will help IDPs and host communities improve food security and ability to restore their food and livelihoods security to meet their immediate food needs with diverse foods through positive and resilient coping mechanisms. All activities under this intervention requested by communities when consulted by BPWO in the assessment stage. BPWO will guarantee equal access for women, girls, boys and men, to benefit from the project and will ensure beneficiaries selection will be based on assessment targeting the highest in need populations with prioritization single female households, BPWO will utilize its internal reporting system, to monitor the project implementation, ensure progress reports, lessons learnt and best practices will be shared on a regular basis with FSL sector lead and other sector partners on the FSL sector meetings at state and federal levels. BPWO M&E Formats will be used to assess the outputs indicator on monthly basis through project/program staff and community volunteers as appropriate along with the results monitoring. The project team will focus on routine activity monitoring to ensure smooth and quality implementation of the activities, throughout the project cycle and recommendations will be implemented in project adjustment. The information obtained will be compiled by the project coordinators at field office to feed into the monthly report to BPWO HQ in Khartoum program staff will conduct regular field visits. The results obtained through monitoring will be presented and discussed with stakeholders in the field, which includes beneficiaries, other partner agencies and in line ministries in a transparent manner. Reports will be submitted to FSL Sector and Ministry of Agriculture

Needs

According to the latest IPC results, 5.67 million people in Sudan are classified as food insecure. Among the food insecure population, the sector partners plan to target 4.39 million of the most vulnerable people that fall under IPC Phase 3 and above. In North Darfur is one of the most hosting returnees and IDPS population in spite of lacks economic access to food due to limited livelihoods and income sources, limited availability of household food stocks, suffers from reduced dietary diversity and high rates of malnutrition. As Sudan continues to face economic challenges, the food insecurity of IDPs, residents, refugees and returnees is expected to worsen. With the upcoming lean season in May, the food insecurity of those most vulnerable is likely to continue to deteriorate and food assistance is crucial. Similarly, timely and adequate agricultural livelihood interventions for small-scale farmers is necessary to ensure farming households are self-reliant and resilient to seasonal food insecurity. The ongoing economic situation, along with the recent lifting of fuel and wheat subsidies, continue to contribute to the devaluation of the local currency and high rates of inflation resulting in an increase in food prices and costs of agricultural production. Although crops benefited from favorable precipitation and harvest in 2018, the high cost of production resulted of high agricultural input prices as well as shortages of fuel and cash affected farming production. Rising prices, compounded by limited access to livelihoods opportunities, have been identified as the main drivers of food insecurity and poor food consumption, which have led to a severe decline in the purchasing power of the most vulnerable communities including returnees, refugees, IDPs, as well as vulnerable host communities and residents

Activities

Activities Activity 1.1.1: To distribute main crop seeds (standard seeds package – core pipeline) to 800 HHs (450 Women and 350 Men) Activity 1.1.2: To distribute hand tools (hand tools package will include: 1 shovel, 1 rake, 1 planting/weeding hoe) to 800 HHs Activity 1.1.3: To conduct 9 information sessions for the selected 800 vulnerable farmers households on improved and sustainable agricultural practices in collaboration with Ministry of Agriculture and other local authorities. The sessions will be conducted during the distribution which will be in patches (per location) to facilitate the distribution procedure, and to ensure the benefit from the sessions. Activity 1.1.4: production and distribution of efficient stoves for 100 female Activity 2.1.1: Distribute 600 small ruminants (sheep) to 200 vulnerable households 3 (including women HHs) goats each to re-stock their herdsActivity 2.1.2: To conduct 2 training workshops to 50 vulnerable HHs on food processing and provide them with startup kits and assetsActivity 2.1.3: Vaccination and treatment of 20,000 livestock head in collaboration with local veterinary authority against local epidemic and endemic diseases owned for 200 targeted HHs (50% will be female headed HHs Activity 2.2.2: establishment of women development center to be served as ongoing programs for women empowerment and leadership, as children friendly space and for community and youth information center

Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental resource management is the management of the interaction and impact of human societies on the environment. It is not, as the phrase might suggest, the management of the environment itself. Environmental resources management aims to ensure that ecosystem services are protected and maintained for future human generations, and also maintain ecosystem integrity through considering ethical, economic, and scientific (ecological) variables. This project intervention tries to identify factors affected by excessive use of natural resources that rise between meeting needs and protecting resources such as trees cut for fuel and constructions. Thus initiating the efficient stove production alongside with formation of natural resources management committees for preservation and protection It is thus linked to environmental protection, sustainability and integrated landscape management.
