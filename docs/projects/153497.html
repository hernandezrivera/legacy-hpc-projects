Project Summary

The proposed emergency food security and livelihoods support project is a dual sector project targeting 4 selected counties of Bor South and Duk in Jonglei state and Aweil North and Aweil West in NBeG state and will run for 12 months from January –December 2019.The main goal of the project will be to increase food production and access by providing lifesaving emergency livelihood support kits. The proposed project will provide training to most vulnerable households to enhance fishing,crop and vegetable production mainly for consumption at the household level. And, given an increase of market day’s events, sale of surplus at local markets in selected payams of Malual North, Akewic, Wad Wiel and Ariath of Aweil North and Aweil West counties of NBeG state and Bor town, Makuach, Anyidi, Ageer, Payuel and Padiet of Bor South and Duk Counties in Jonglei state which will create extra income for these households. Specifically, this project will target vulnerable households in the above mentioned payams. These payams are chosen due to their suitability for fishing and vegetable production.
SAADO will target 15,333 households in this proposed response with emergency fishing,crop and vegetable kits. The locations have been chosen because of the needs prevalent in these counties according to the IPC September 2018 and the existence of active programming by SAADO in the areas. The emergency livelihood kits will be obtained from the FSLC-recommended core pipeline to communities affected by the ongoing crisis in Jonglei and NBeG states.
SAADO will put in place a mechanism for effective and participatory community vulnerability mapping in the selected Bomas and use a comprehensive beneficiary selection criteria developed and discussed and agreed upon with the communities to include Men, Women, Boys and Girls. During community consultations at various levels, all men, women boys and girls will be consulted to fully understand and appreciate the impact of the current crisis on men, women and children that would further inform response. “Do no harm” and AAP principles will be applied throughout the duration of the project, so that any possible negative implications of the project at community level are addressed before they occur through integrating a strong Communicating with Communities (CwC) feedback mechanisms. The project will build upon/complement SAADO long experience in implementing livestock disease prevention and control through vaccination and treatment in Bor South & Duk counties with support from FAO as well as implementing the 1st and 2nd allocations of the SSHF IN Jonglei state. It will also complement the Lives Saving and livelihood protection and nutrition activities carried out by SAADO in Aweil North county through the 2.5 year DFID funded BRACE II project as well as the financial literacy training (FLT) project in Aweil West County and therefore provide integration which is a key focus of the FSL cluster. The project will integrate with other FSL partners’ interventions in the counties especially WVI and PUI in Aweil North and Aweil West and CRS, NRC, and VSF-Germany in Bor South and Duk counties in Jonglei state. The proposed activities are necessary and sufficient to achieve the stated cluster objectives for these interventions based on the relevance of each activity in increasing food production for the most vulnerable households in the targeted communities. The indicators selected are SMART and SAADO has in place a strong monitoring and evaluation system/mechanisms to measure these indicators and to ensure the intended results are achieved. The means of verification, such as monthly reports, distribution lists and regular field visits are strongly credible and are currently used by the organization in its MEAL processes across its field offices in the country.

Needs

Despite the recent signing of the revitalized peace efforts in Addis Ababa, the country continues to be in a state of anxiety as the deal was received with mixed reactions throughout the country. The peace agreement, which was largely moderated with heavy involvement of Sudan and Uganda, had many questioning whether the process was inclusive, according to (South Sudan Humanitarian Bulletins, April-May, 2018; OCHA Humanitarian Briefings 2018). According to the most recent Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) September 2018 analysis, it is expected that 6.1 million1 people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August 2018), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). The condition is exacerbated by the current ongoing economic crisis characterized by hyperinflation and run away food prices including an estimated cereal deficit of 482,000 MT from the 2017 harvest according to the CFSAM report January 2018. In Northern Bahr el Ghazal state, more specifically in Aweil North & Aweil West counties payams of Jaac, Warapai and Malek Miir and/or Kiir Adem the long-term effects of the conflict, coupled with high food prices, erratic rainfall patterns, depleted livelihood options and limited humanitarian access, continue to put severe pressure on households’ food security especially the vulnerable women, girls, and children who constitute 51% of those affected. Moreover, according to the recently declared Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) data (South Sudan IPC September 2018 and projections for IPC_ Jan-March 2019, food insecurity is expected to reach critical levels particularly in NBEG state which is already in crisis phase with current IPC data indicating Aweil North County as having 28,000 individuals in emergency phase 4 and 70,000 individuals being in crisis phase and 126,000 individuals being under stress levels of food insecurity for the period September-October 2018. In addition, the nutrition data from UNICEF South Sudan Sitrep conducted within the last six months in 2018 reported the Greater Bahr el Ghazel region having global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels above the emergency threshold of 15 percent with a GAM prevalence by Weight for Height (WHZ) of more than 17 percent including (GAM level 17.2%SAM level 4.3%) for NBEG state in particular as being in either crisis or emergency phase with high levels of acute malnutrition. The local communities of Aweil North County also experience the systematic looting of their animals by armed actors during the seasonal migration of the Misseriya and Rizigads nomads, which also depletes their livestock assets thereby exacerbating their hunger levels. At times, they resort to fishing and farming to source of livelihood, but limited fishing equipment has resulted in limited catches. The situation in Bor South and Duk Counties of Jonglei state is even more dire according to IPC September 2018 which reports Duk County having (6000 emergency;52,000 crisis;46,000 stressed)and Bor South(24,000 emergency;108,000 crisis;84,000 stressed) and the Food Security Outlook Update for South Sudan (FEWSNET, August 2018),which reports that there is an increased level of cattle raiding and inter-communal violence as well as increased cases of livestock diseases which are on the rise as a result of migratory movement of pastoralists and livestock. The most recent rains were sporadic and planting was negatively affected in many parts of the communities. High levels of GAM rates >21% were present during the last harvesting season, and given the expectation of continued lowering of households’ purchasing power and conflict-related restrictions to normal livelihood activities, many households are expected to face extreme acute food insecurity

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This project will ensure that the affected populations in the targeted areas have a genuine voice during activity design and decision-making process that inform the proposed projects's priorities and modalities. As part of their orientation training and continual development, project personnel will receive instruction on why information sharing is prioritized, how it is operationalized, and what their roles and responsibilities are in making it work. In each target area SAADO will initiate anopen dialogue with the community and key stakeholders. We will ensure that communication and messaging is timely, accurate, appropriate, effective and understood. Project parameters such as site and beneficiary selection will be openly discussed with beneficiaries using a highly transparent methodology that is grounded in community participation. Information sharing will also be a key feature of the proposed project to help in avoiding overlaps between different actors in the selected counties of Bor South & Duk of Jonglei state and Aweil North and Aweil West counties of NBeG State through building collaborations between humanitarian agencies and affected populations.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages will be contextualised to speak to different age groups and gender.The project will achieve this through establishment of Project Management Committees (PMCs)composed of 60% women and 40% men which will enhance community participation, ownership, and satisfaction in SAADO’s proposed intervention. Furthermore,the PMCs will include a membership that represents all subsetsof society including marginalised groups, and will be responsible for implementing a two-way BCA strategies identified in the project’s Beneficiary Communication and Accountability Plan. This will include delivering initial orientation and ongoing information to communities on project activities,rationale behind beneficiary selection, and supporting the management and resolution of complaints throughout project planning and implementation. PMCs will also be involved in supporting the implementation and management of project activities to ensure that these resources do not create a source of competition and dispute, the project will strengthen the capacity of community structures to develop and enforce context-appropriate systems for ensuring theequitable access and utilisation of communal assets and resources. The project will also ensure communities are actively involved in beneficiary selection, will conduct face-to-face information, question and answer sessions, and establish a complaints system to receive and pre-emptively respond to issues with the potential to negatively impact community relationships.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will ensure that beneficiary sites and training centres are safe spaces for both genders and those women with children have access to child spaces where they can play safely while their mothers attend trainings. During meetings, Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) and training sessions or any gatherings on this project, SAADO gender and protection staff will address the people we serve/beneficiaries on issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Where ever such issues are raised SAADO will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part of strengthening the referral pathways.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project’s M&E approach will focus on accountability to beneficiaries, gathering and sharing evidence to inform programming and compliment and progress initiatives from other humanitarian and resilience actors. SAADO will work within a rigorous system that is in line with the overall guidelines for MEAL in Emergencies, along with the Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability (CAMSA) as well as contributing and aligning with the food security & livelihoods and nutrition clusters monitoring mechanisms.Building on a rapid needs assessment, a mini baseline and end line survey will be conducted using quantitative and qualitative methodologies to measure impact and change and to set benchmarks against agreed indicators. Conflict, gender, and protection analyses will be updated on a continual basis to inform program implementation. A clear monitoring and evaluation framework will be set up in discussion with women and men separately and in groups, to be monitored by implementing field teams, which will include monthly monitoring reports with clear action points. We will also ensure that a gender-sensitive community feedback mechanism (for consultation, feedback and complaints) is established to inform and change programming, including the establishment of accountability groups based on community-defined vulnerability criteria. Routine monitoring shall be conducted using a mix of participatory appraisal and quantitative methods throughout the duration of the project, engaging primary agents of change, communities, local stakeholders, and project implementing teams.At field level progress toward project objectives will be closely monitored by the Food security & Livelihood Technical Advisor and MEAL specialist supported by the Director of Programmes. Where possible, beneficiaries and local officials will take the lead on activity monitoring, and report on progress at community learning events.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Briefly describe how

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure protection mainstreaming to members of affected populations by ensuring that females have equal and equitable access to services and that targeted action to advance gender equality and protection is based on a gender and age analysis. All activities and results (output and outcomes) will enhance gender equality and have protection considerations.The project embraces ADAPT & ACTC framework for protection and gender equality programming. For example our project (1) analyses the impact of the crises (violent conflict, shocks and economic) on girls, boys, women and men and what this means in terms of division of labour/tasks, workload and access to humanitarian assistance and protection (2) ensures that girls and boys,women and men participate equally and equitably in the design,implementation, monitoring and evaluation; and that girls, boys, women and men are involved in decision-making positions.
