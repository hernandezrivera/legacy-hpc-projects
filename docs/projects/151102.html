Project Summary

American Refugee Committee's (ARC'S) proposed health project will be implemented in close coordination with the MOH/D/RMOs in each region, as well as WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA and other health cluster partners.
ARC plans to respond in Banadir and Jubaland of South- Central Somalia and Sool, Sanag and Togdeer of Somaliland, where the humanitarian need is critical due to ongoing impacts of drought, displacement and conflict. The intervention will support existing government health centers or MCHs to provide quality primary and reproductive healthcare services to 200,000 individuals as direct beneficiaries while reaching to estimated 520,000 individuals as indirect beneficiaries. Access to essential health services remains a priority for the conflict affected populations in proposed project target locations.
ARC health project will provide emergency and basic primary and reproductive health care services that include treatment of common illnesses, management of injuries, reproductive health care services, maternal and child health care, health education, provision of essential medicines, and control of communicable diseases within the community. Protection will be mainstreamed into all project activities, through a comprehensive package of training of HF staff on identification and referral of severe cases of S/GBV. The intervention will reduce morbidity and mortality among the displaced vulnerable population by primarily focusing on ensuring that target facilities provide an integrated package of primary health care services with emergency referrals, disease surveillance and the inclusion of reproductive and mental healthcare services. ARC will closely coordinate with the MOH and respond in locations where significant gaps in health service delivery have been identified. ARC will ensure the participation of the local communities in decision making throughout the life cycle of the project to further strengthening community acceptance and ownership.
ARC will invest in local health capacity, strengthening the existing physical structures and human resource, ensuring that staff incentives are in line with MOH scale paving the way for transition to locally and nationally owned and managed facilities. Through the lifetime of the project, ARC will work with the MOH to pave the way for a proper handover, and discuss transfer of responsibility slowly or in phases to D/RMOs where and when possible.

Needs

Political instability, natural hazards and lack of development have driven up humanitarian needs in Somalia. ARC conducted needs assessments in multiple locations, including the proposed project locations of Banadir and Jubaland of South-central and Sool, Sanag and Togdeer of Somaliland. ARC acknowledges the importance of conducting regular need assessments due to the constantly changing environment for IDPs, returnees ad host communities. In October 2018, ARC conducted a rapid multi-sector needs assessment in Sool, Sanag and Togdeer identifying a massive need for medical services. Lascaanood hospital which does not operate in full capacity is the only referral point for Sool and Sanag while Burco hospital is the only referral point for Togdeer and Odweyne. The main identified health needs includes; lack of referral mechanism, lack of diseases surveillance system, shortage of qualified heath workers, shortage of essential medicines, lack of key services like BemONC, vaccination and nutrition programme. Most of the Health centers need rehabilitation of toilets, sanitary networks and water supply. In Banadir and Jubaland region, the number of IDPs and returnees arriving has been steadily increasing, predominately due to insecurities placing a massive and overwhelming strain on the already fragile public services and infrastructure. The health needs in these areas are particularly critical, with the increased possibility of a water-borne diseases outbreak. Respiratory tract infection, diarrheal diseases and skin infections are the top cause of morbidity. Women and children were heavily affected by the severe crisis caused by the political instability coupled with reduction in health services that led to compromised access to essential Reproductive healthcare services like Antenatal Care (ANC), Post natal Care (PNC), safe deliveries, and lack of access to vaccinations for children and youth affecting pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and children directly.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed project mainstreams protection by prioritizing safety and dignity of beneficiaries, providing and promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation. In order to ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries.While the health need will likely continue beyond the life of the proposed grant, ARC simultaneously pursues two approaches, in working towards the most sustainable long-term solution while taking into account the near and mid-term realities. 1. ARC continues service provision with DOH HFs. In this case, ARC will seek to diversify funding. At every level of project involvement and management, ARC will take steps to ensure transparency and capacity-building of the medical staff. ARC will ensure the RMOs are engaged throughout the response to create room for smooth handing over so that RMOs are well prepared to ensure coverage of the grant supported healthcare services.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Everyone has a right to healthcare services and ARC program will maintain. ARC acknowledges the need to serve all beneficiaries equally by adhering to the humanitarian principles in its intervention during the project life span and ensured the project served all people in the targeted geographic areas, regardless of gender, age, nationality, IDP status, disability status, religion, ethnicity, political affiliation, economic means or any other categorization.ARC will prevent risks of exclusion based on displacement by ensuring that internally displaced persons benefit from the interventions just like the host communities. The project will target women, men, girls and boys in both IDP camps and host communities so that equity and equality is maintained at all levels. In actualizing this, there are risks that ARC will need to mitigate so that service provision to the most deserving communities is not hindered.ARC will also put in place community consultation and feedback mechanism Kuja Kuja o collect real time community satisfaction feedback (www.kujakuja.com).

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

A protection risk for this project is that of exclusion or denial of assistance, especially for older persons, persons will disabilities, children, unaccompanied youth and women. ARC will ensure the health facilities are accessible to all vulnerable populations by building on the conducted assessments, as well as through regular reporting and monitoring and complaints mechanisms

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Through the adoption of "Do No Harm" approaches, efforts are made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs are implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of Somalia conflict situation. ARC believes that understanding the situation and the context in which we operate is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and to avoid negative consequences in the operation area. As a part of the Protection project, ARC will carry our community capacity building to promote peaceful co-existence and conflict resolution.Insecurity arising from extremist groups (Al Shabaab) and inter-clan clashes is addressed through contextual understanding and working with the community to acquire information about threats. ARC will maintain a high level of acceptance at community level and with key stakeholders and decision makers. ARC will continue monitoring the security situation and trends in the region, analyse risks (their likelihood and impact), review mitigation measures, and integrate the findings in ARC’s Safety and Security Management Plan, and ensure that all staff are trained on and adhere strictly to our security guidelines.
