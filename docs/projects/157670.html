Project Summary

Through this project, UNICEF aims to support the humanitarian actors in providing quality, cost-effective health interventions to 1,208,781 under fifteen years of age children (boys and girls) in humanitarian settings. It is proposed to support provision of an integrated primary health care package (immunization, IMCI, ANC, skilled birth attendance, postnatal and new born care and prevention of mother to child transmission of HIV/AIDS) through
fixed, mobile and outreach services in addition to supporting provision of community based health interventions (integrated community case management for children under five for instance). UNICEF is also going to support procurement and distribution of the essential health supplies (measles and polio vaccines, IMCI kits, MW Kits, ORS, and Zinc supplements) and commodities to cover the needs of the most vulnerable children and
women in the emergency affected areas in addition to the supplies needed for emergency response to the natural disasters and epidemics (LLITNs and AWD kits). UNICEF will also support the implementing partners in building the capacity of the front-line health care providers to ensure provision of high quality child health services. Furthermore, community engagement and health promotion will be supported to strengthen community’s resilience, enhance utilization of the existing health services and promote for
healthy practices.

Needs

For many years, Sudan faces a wide range of natural and man-made disasters leading to many negative impacts on people, social services and the basic infrastructures in most parts of the country. In general, emergencies lead to disruption of health services and exacerbate the long-standing health problems while simultaneously increase the acute morbidity and mortality. The protracted conflict affecting Sudan, has weakened the national health system leading to poor services’ coverage, shortage of essential health supplies and qualified health personnel. Furthermore, emergencies are causing increased rates of poverty and huge displacement waves in most parts of the country. Currently, more than 3.6 million people need humanitarian assistance in Sudan including IDPs, returnees, and refugees most of them are women and children under-five years of age. With deteriorated economic situation, prolonged food in security, poor access to WASH services; the affected children become more vulnerable and at increased risk of diseases’ outbreaks including diarrheal diseases, respiratory tract infections, and Malaria. Collectively; these diseases cause more than 30% of under five mortality in the country. Vaccines preventable diseases are among the major public health threats in emergency settings. A nationwide measles outbreak has happened in Sudan in 2010 during which the worst affected localities were the ones in humanitarian situation. Since beginning of2018; another outbreak started causing more than 5,400 cases all over the country. However, the results of the MICS 2014 show that only 34% of children with diarrhea received ORS for treatment and less than 60% of children with pneumonia received antibiotics. This could be attributed to many factors: geographical/financial inaccessibility to health services, substandard quality of health services (shortage of trained staff or supplies), and poor health seeking behavior. Furthermore, health awareness among conflict affected population is also one of the major challenges. The country was also affected by a large-scale outbreak of acute waterydiarrhea (2016-2018) leading to over 35,000 suspected cases and more than 800 deaths. Recently, a chikungunya virus’s (CHIKV) outbreak started in the eastern zone of Sudan. This high country’s susceptibility to epidemics is generally linked to weak health system coupled with poor emergency preparedness and inefficient response at all levels. This project is seeking to strengthen capacity of the MOH and NGOs to scale up community-based interventions to address communicable diseases among children, respond to outbreaks of infectious diseases, sensitize communities and strengthen prevention, including immunization. Community based services such as ICCM, house visitation for newborn care, community midwifery will be supported to deliver essential and lifesaving services to complement the health facility based interventions. This will significantly reduce excess mortality.

Activities

UNICEF will collaborate with other UN agencies, including WHO, UNHCR and UNFPA in supporting the MOH and NGOs to deliver the planned lifesaving services. With WHO as the sector lead for health, UNICEF will participate actively at national and subnational coordination fora to advocate on its mandate to meet the rights of children. UNICEF will leverage technical capacity and other resources from WHO and UNFPA, include supplies and equipment to address gaps of PHC services in the project areas. UNICEF will collaborate with NGOs through direct project partnerships and technical assistance and coordination through the sector to leverage capacities for implementation of the project and to address gaps. All of UNICEF projects and programmes are gender mainstreamed, customized to the culture of the beneficiary communities as well as aligned with environmental standards. additionally, data will be reported by age and gender where possible. Project will be Specific project activities are:1. Procure and distribute the essential heath supplies (vaccines, ORS, Zinc, IMCI kits, MW kits, LLITNS and AWD kits) and commodities including cold chain and IMCI equipment, 2. Build the capacity of the front-line health care providers (women and men) -at both facility and community levels- to deliver quality life-saving health services (EPI, casemanagement of childhood illnesses, PMTCT, maternal and newborn care), 3. Supportreferral of severe/complicated cases to the secondary level of care, 4. Provide operationalcost for mobile clinics, outreach and mobile immunization teams, 5. Supportpreparedness and timely response to the health emergencies- including communicablediseases’ outbreaks- among the vulnerable population , 6. Support provision of healthpromotion and communication for behavior change/impact activities to the vulnerablepopulation through media, campaigns and house to house visits, 7. Strengthen monitoring and evaluation of the health activities

Impact on environment/natural resources

Due to the susceptibility of Sudan to climate change, UNICEF will discourage cutting of trees for construction purposes as often done. The project will also discourage felling of trees for energy purposes as part of ongoing community sensitization. The climate change is one of the factors increasing population's vulnerability to naturaldisasters in Sudan. Therefore, this project considers supporting health emergencypreparedness, timely response and building community's resilience as one of the mainobjectives. Locaties at high-risk of the natural disasters (mainly floods and drought) arefully considered and targeted by this project. Building the community capacity andsupporting services' provision through community workers- including communitymidwives- are among the activities planned for under this project to serve as mitigationmeasures.
