Project Summary


Since 2018, UNHCR has been implementing home gardening activities for refugees in camps. Over the years, refugees have grown crops, especially vegetables, in their households to enrich the nutritional value of their food intake and to have some additional income. A large portion of the unemployed refugee workforce can be involved in the productive agriculture sector to produce vegetables. Agriculture based support has been also extended to members of targeted host communities. As of October 2022, 8,500 refugee households have received production kits, while 5,600 host community households were supported to increase their agricultural production and access to income generating opportunities. For further addressing the livelihood needs of the Rohingya refugee and the host community, the project will promote climate smart agricultural skills and practices for household food & nutrition security, and self-reliance of the poorest and vulnerable households of both the host and refugee communities. To better achieve household food and nutritional security, the targeted households will get support for food production in their homestead with innovative multi-layer technologies. The activity will cover climate-smart agricultural production in the homestead with low irrigation and climate-sensitive on-farm interventions in the refugee and host communities.
In both communities, 70% of the targeted beneficiaries will be female. The project will work for empowering the women through engaging them in agricultural production and income from surplus production and link them with the markets. Women empowerment and engagement in agriculture will as well help in reducing the risk of being victims of GBV incidents and increase their decision-making participation. The project will integrate and ensure gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, and Protection. The project will coordinate with Field and Site Management team of UNHCR for DRR inclusion and Energy and Environment team for better integration of climate smart agricultural practices and environment protection. The interventions will be promoted for addressing climate-smart agriculture and green growth.
Through the project UNHCR will be able to support the refugees and host communities with:
- Agricultural skills development in climate-smart agricultural technology followed by the inputs, food production and preservation including DRR/DRM tools for protection of the agricultural products.
- Productive assets, regular capacity building and improved technologies provided to vulnerable host community households, including as well green skills trainings creation of green -small or micro enterprises.
To address the Complaint, Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), UNHCR has a separate entity to capture feedback, complaints, and suggestions as part of the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP). The project will adapt learning from the community and improve the intervention. Since the project will be implemneted for both the refugee and host communities and both the communities will be sensitized on importance of peaceful coexistance untill repatriation and where possible, will encourage dialogue between both communities to promote social cohesion.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Refugees in Cox’s Bazar generally have no regular income opportunities and lack the modern technological skills that would allow them to adapt quickly to change in their situation. The fifth round of the Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 5) published in March 2022, revealed that 95% of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance, similar to 2020 (96 percent). These results reflect the slow economic recovery of an already fragile population with no income sources nor livelihood opportunities. Furthermore, the results revealed that half of the Rohingya refugee labor force was not engaged in any type of income-generating activity. Therefore, the promotion of self-reliance and livelihoods opportunities through climate smart improved agriculture and creating income opportunities – including through agricultural ventures using available land – are among UNHCR’s key livelihoods objectives for refugees in Bangladesh. The host communities near the camps and settlements have been affected due to the influx of Rohingyas, as land once used for farming and other activities is now occupied by refugees. The influx has placed a strain on infrastructure, services, and natural resources in these communities. According to REVA 5, overall vulnerability in the host community remained as high as 2020, with 52 % of the population being moderately to highly vulnerable in 2021 compared to 51 % in 2020. The main drivers were the economic contraction and decline in economic activity across most sectors during the COVID-19 lockdown in a population highly dependent on daily wage labor. UNHCR is increasingly working to mitigate the negative impacts and help host communities benefit from the economic opportunities. UNHCR will introduce innovative market-oriented agricultural interventions to improve the resilience of those affected by the refugee influx. The interventions will as well support household food and nutritional security through agricultural production (vegetable gardening, fish and poultry rearing) and link the producer with services and markets.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will comply with the protection principles by ensuring their participation throughout the project cycle. The project will ensure equal participation of diverse groups, including persons with disabilities, and adhering to the principle of "Do No Harm". The project will comply protection mainsteaming principles and approaches. UNHCR uphold the Zero tolerance policy to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual harrassment(SH) and apply same to policy to partner by including it to project partnership agreement.

3.2 - Gender

The project will follow the household approach and contribute to food and nutrition security of the households, but at least 70% of the project beneficiaries will be women. As it is not a regular phenomenon in their culture, assessing the challenges facing women in participating in agricultural livelihood programme will be assessed. Specific measures will be adopted to overcome these challenges, continuously monitoring the extent to which women participate in and benefit from project activities, and adjust the strategy as needed if the target of 70% is not achieved. UNHCR enhances socio-economic response by creating livelihoods opportunities to women, men, adolescents and gender-diverse populaton. Livelihood activities can improve women's autonomy and teachnical knowledge, improve leadership capacity and reduce notable gender power imbalance specially women, girls, person with disability and gender-diverse population.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project will target main beneficiary in the age group of 18-59. The project has an obligation not to include anybody below 18 in productive engagement. UNHCR maintain the child safe gaurding policy and will select beneficiary through baseline survey and proper need assessment. The beneficiary lists will cross check with UNHCR database so that only age above 18 involve in livelihood activities.

3.4 - Diversity

UNHCR activities are aligned with its AGD policy, ensuring that people we serve are at the center of programs. UNHCR undertake core actions in the areas of engagement: AGD-Inclusive Programming; Participation and Inclusion; Communication and Transparency; Feedback and Response; Organizational Learning and Adaptation and Gender Equality and Commitments to Women and Girls. To promote further empowerment of beneficiaries, UNHCR works based on an inclusive approach to promote gender equality and protect gender-diverse population and people with disabilities. Most vulnerable beneficiaries face barriers to access basic services and are often excluded from education and livelihood opportunities. Among the target participants, at least 70% of them will be women to ensure gender parity. It will identify gender diverse popluation, person with disbilities and minorities as well as ensure at least 1% persons with disabilities engagement in the project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will adopt a community-based approach which incorporates the principles of age, gender and diversity mainstreaming in their work with refugees and host communities in order to ensure accountability to affected populations; in other words, through consultation and participation throughout the programme cycle, refugee communities in each camp and host community of the targeted villages should be engaged meaningfully and substantively in all aspects of programmes that affect them and play a leading role in catalysing change and enhancing community resilience. Besides this UNHCR has unic tool free hotline facilities and different complaints response mechanism that ensure meaningful participation of all gender diverse groups.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The overall environmental and climate change impact, the Project will reduce the ecological footprint through economic empowerment, especially for women. The project will integrate the guiding principles of UNHCR’s Environmental Guidelines in alignment with the national legal framework (The Forest Act, 1927; Wildlife (Conservation and Security) Act, 2012) and Energy and Environment Working Group guidelines. All environmental and climate action activities will be implemented under the umbrella of UNHCR within or beyond the project scope in a comprehensive complementary approach. The focus will be to embed the principle of ecological restoration and reduce the risk of climatic hazards.
