Project Summary

The project is aiming to provide integrated protection GBV and livelihood services, for vulnerable girls and women, more focusing on widowed, women with special needs, adolescent girls, in 6 community centers in North-West of Syria, to ensure vulnerable adolescents and young women who are exposed to acute and/or chronic crises are safe, protected and more resilient.
Through the project we will provide, structured psychosocial interventions for women, GBV case management and referral, life skills and mentoring, and vocational training, in order to empower them and increase their resilience.
MAPs consider the intervention in 6 regions, due to the availability of access and according to previous needs assessed through other programs. the six regions are Tal-Abiad, Jarablous, Albab, Azaz, Idleb, and Afrin.
The livelihood component is considered to train women in two ideas in order to improve their positive coping and personal development to self-efficacy and well-being:
1. The professions do not require any qualifications e.g. Crochet and Sewing.
2. The professions require relevant experience e.g. Computer and technological skills.
Clear selection criteria will be prepared to select those who will be part of the livelihood intervention.
270 women will be targeted by Crochet training, each training will consist of 3 months, Women who received Crochet training will be able to produce crochet pieces and MAPs will use the crochet to advocate on certain GBV violations, locally and through MAPs network of activates regionally and internationally, those women will financially be supported in a sustainable way from the income of Crochet selling in the regional and international advocacy campaigns.
women from the 2nd set of training will need basic knowledge in using a computer in order to empower women to take better rules in the society and find a sustainable job, the training will target 1215 girls and women. This training will be followed by an internship with either NGOs offices inside Syria, as volunteers and/or private sector if available, all the training and the internship will be paid with small incentives.
All the project staff will be trained on GBV basics, GBV Case Management, the inclusion of PwDs, protection mainstreaming principles and approaches, protection risks analysis and safeguarding and PSEA.
The project will also provide Community-based protection services to ensure that IDPs and host communities are effectively involved in the project lifecycle from design to implementation to feedback through the already established protected feedback channels to help them report suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns.
Monitoring will be conducted at the end of each intervention by measuring baseline and endline. M&E measuring progress against project indicators. A compliance mechanism is in place to ensure quality and transparency. MAPs will ensure to train staff to use GBV SC M&E Toolkit to be in-ling with inter-agency guidance reports.

4 - Needs

The protection needs are widespread in Syria, Inadequate living conditions, the loss of livelihoods, traditional safety nets, property and assets, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions and the impact of Covid-19 pandemic, compound gender-based violence (GBV) risks and child protection concerns.According to HNO 2021, 13.1 million PIN are in need for protection assistance , 25% of them have disabilities and 75% of them are women and children, where children with and without disabilities comprise 44% of the people in need across Syria.Across Syria, 27% of children in households are displaying signs of mental distress, doubling from 2020, Voices of Syria 2021 reported that GBV remains a major protection risk and pervades the lives of women and girls, particularly domestic and family violence, early/forced marriage and sexual violence and harassment. Protection monitoring reports indicated that GBV responses and prevention activities remains a need in the north of Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be done through 6 community centers, after recruiting the staff, MAPs will train staff on GBV basics, GBV Case Management, the inclusion of PwDs, protection mainstreaming principles and approaches, protection risks analysis and safeguarding, and PSEA.Interventions will target women with a focus on vulnerable groups such as divorced, widows, females with disabilities all centers are prepared to ensure dignified access to women and girls with disabilities Through PSS support they will be able to improve functioning, strengthen positive coping and build social connections. The project will provide the GBV prevention and mitigation including empowerment activities, GBV case management, including PwDs, GBV awareness-raising sessions, Life Skills ActivitiesA set of criteria will be defined to provide vocational training for women according to their capacities. Women who are eligible according to criteria and don't have computer skills will be trained on Chorchet for 3 months, and they'll be able to produce Crochet pieces at the end of the project which will be used for advocacy purpose on GBVs issues. While those who have prior knowledge will receive Computer technological skills, in order to increase their self-confidence, their resilience and empower them to start their own businesses. an internship will be provided for those women to enrich their knowledge. Programs will be designed in collaboration with women and young adolescents in a participatory manner including PWDs. Monitoring will be conducted at the end of each intervention by measuring baseline and endline. M&E measuring progress against project indicators. Compliance mechanism is in place to ensure quality and transparency.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.Risk: Exploitation or abuse of beneficiaries by staff when providing services Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation: Training staff on PSEA, Code of Conduct, and activating the reporting mechanisms. 2. Risk: Mental health is highly stigmatized (High impact, medium likelihood) and poorly understood. Mitigation 1) existing relationship within targeted community and community is consulted 2) program uses wellbeing instead of mental health 3) program targets a wide section of the community and not single out particular groups. Promotion of gender equality and rights of marginalised groups and changing gender roles and responsibilities may excerbate GBV (High impact, medium likelihood). Interventions may cause harm if not properly managed. Mitigations 1) updated gender analysis, 2)consult men, women, girls and boys and marginalised groups on project design, 3) ongoing monitoring of red flags through audits, 4)strengthen feedback and complaints mechanism . 3. Risk Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls from accessing GBV services Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs Monitoring: SOPs and complaint channels are in place Resources: M&E and HR Acceptable level of risk.

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The risk of PSEA is existed mainly between the trainers and trainees, without mentioning the risk between the project administration with either trainers or trainees, the likelihood is medium. the mitigation is the presence of PSEA policy and its mainstreaming in all other policies and administrative procedures. the training of the MEAL staff to deal with this kind of issues in the field, the orientation sessions, and the refreshment training that conducted during the project lifetime, in addition to the strong compliant and feedback mechanism that MAPs will include to be notified ASAP when any PSEA accident happened, including engaging senior-level management to react. and finally the singing of all staff on the code of conduct that has a PSEA clause included and explained for each staff. in addition to making awareness session to local communities and conucils about PSEA and its regulations and strengthening the community-based feedback mechanism through the already established protected feedback channels. collaboration with the PSEA network to ensure the development of PSEA procedures are in line with PSEA strategy.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Cross-border operations are still in effect Risk: Borders are closed due to not extending the cross-border resolution Mitigation: plan ahead and rely on internal resources until restrictions get lifted. Assumption: The community centers are located in relatively safe areas. Risk of armed clashes, arrest and detention, shelling/artillery. Mitigation: 1)MAPs will sure to do a security risk assessment before renting any place for training and will make sure to implement the activities in a safe and secure area. 2)staff to contact the security focal point and alert all staff to be prepared for immediate evacuation/hibernation if possible.Assumption: Covid-19 pandemic, Risk: Ongoing covid-19 pandemic had resulted in delayed delivery of services Mitigation: project adaption to ensure public health measures are observed. This is expected to continue in 2021. MAPs developed SOPs according to WHO standardbreds in order to implement the activities, MAPs will also take into consideration the possible lockdown days during the project plan.MAPs will also plan to move to remote intervention when possible and to mobile team modalities to adopt the expected new regulations.
