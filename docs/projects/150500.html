Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable herding and Bedouin families in Area C living in inadequate shelters due to the challenging environment – including restrictions on movement, settler violence, absence of a Master Plan and lack of building permits. The proposed shelter renovation includes the rehabilitation and insulation of substandard shelters and winterization for vulnerable communities. The project aims to protect herder and Bedouin families from harsh weather conditions, reduce the consequences of the challenging environment leading to forced displacement, and strengthen the resilience of target beneficiaries. Living standards will be improved through rehabilitation of the structurally unsound and inadequate shelters in order to provide adequate protection from adverse weather conditions during the summer and winter and decrease recurrent assistance by providing more durable and sustainable materials.
Secondly, the project will target vulnerable groups in Hebron city’s H2 area, those living in inadequate shelters close to Israeli settlements and military points. These families face a challenging coercive environment which includes restrictions on movement and shelter rehabilitation, settler violence and military attacks. The proposed shelter renovations include the supply and installation of protective materials (fences, gates, steel window nets, yard and alley covers) as well as renovation inputs (treat dampness, improve WASH connections and facilities, treat leaky roofs and walls, replace doors and windows). Thus, the proposed action for this area will strengthen the beneficiaries’ resilience by (a) protecting them from harsh weather conditions, particularly during the winter when they are exposed to ceiling leaks and wind; (b) mitigate the risks associated with Israeli military and settler violence in particular during clashes; and (c) generally improve their living standards by rehabilitating their structurally unsound and inadequate shelters.
The proposed intervention matches the allocation strategy and priority as the project intervention directly supports the HRP 2018 Strategic Objective #2 ‘The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL, and # 3 “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The proposal also addresses the Shelter Cluster objective #2: " Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”.
The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment. Ultimately, both proposed interventions aim to reduce the beneficiaries’ exposure to forced displacement by mitigating the effects of the above-mentioned factors.

Needs

The 2018 HNO identified the protection needs of more than 30,000 Palestinian families living in Area C related to accommodation (threat of demolition, poor living and sanitation standards, health associated risks and lack of privacy). Water infiltration, insufficient ventilation and lack of insulation present a constant health hazard for Area C’s inhabitants, particularly for women, children and the elderly, as they spend the most time in the shelters. The common one room shelter fails to meet privacy and safety minimum standards. In addition, families have limited access to basic health and sanitation facilities. The prolonged conflict restricts future development opportunities. The financial vulnerability of herders has increased due to increasing fodder costs, inflated water prices, restricted access to grazing land and low availability of credit. Consequently, they are gradually being forced out of business, unable to pay off debts and are forced to sell off their animal flocks and assets. As part of its relocation plan for the Bedouin population, the Israeli government has increased the pressure on communities in the eastern Jerusalem Governorate, Masafer Yatta’s Firing Zone 918 and the north Jordan Valley, by issuing eviction orders as well as by demolishing their homes, livelihood structures and community assets. Data from OCHA indicates that the Israeli administration demolished more than 308 structures, affecting 5825 people, in 2018. In July 2018, AAH updated a needs assessment and protection analysis for 80 communities in Area C with a particular focus on community participation and engagement of women, men, girls and boys. The assessment indicated that communities in the E1 zone, Firing Zone 918, Jordan Valley and Road 317 clusters are the most affected by Israeli restriction policies. According to a June 2018 assessment led by the shelter cluster, and in which AAH participated, more than 3,500 households in Area C suffer from bad shelter conditions due to their limited financial capacities and Israeli restrictions on construction and movement. In August 2018, AAH participated in OCHA’s H2 assessment which found that the most vulnerable communities in Area C and H2 allocate their limited resources to the maintenance of their dwellings, as Israeli authorities forbid them from expanding their infrastructures. Based on these reports and using a community based approach, the proposed project prioritizes 26 communities at risk of forced displacement due to (1) dire socio-economic conditions (high unemployment rate, low income, total dependency on herding, limited health care services, limited social capital including education); (2) sensitivity of their location to environmental and anthropic threats (recurrent natural disasters, threat of demolition, eviction or confiscation orders, land confiscation, military and settler violence) and; (3) shelter status. While AAH strives to reduce the beneficiary’s exposure in all ways possible, mitigation measures such as direct beneficiary implementation also imply that said beneficiaries need to have the ability and willingness to engage in the project, as well as understand the risks inherent to it. The project’s design was developed together with the community representatives paying, with particular attention to the needs of women and children. For example, in herding communities, limited access to sanitation facilities forces women to defecate during the night, which increases health risks as well as their exposure to harassment and sexual violence. Taking safety and dignity into account, safe and weather-proofed shelters with sanitation facilities which provide sufficient privacy is an essential improvement for women and children’s wellbeing and protection. Hence, AAH and RCSD conducted a detailed shelter technical assessment in the proposed 26 communities to evaluate the condition of the targeted shelters and recommended the most appropriate improvements.

Activities

The proposed activities aim to protect vulnerable families through an appropriate response to their immediate needs, thus increasing their protection and resilience to manmade and natural shocks and integrating risk reduction and coping strategies to improve the provided emergency assistance. Said activities will respond to the critical identified needs of women, men, girls and boys, to improve their living conditions by upgrading their shelters to the minimum standards. Safe access to better sanitation facilities will improve women’s health and provide increased privacy through internal partitions. The project will improve the households’ capacity to sustain their livelihood, by (1) increasing their resistance to manmade and natural risks; (2) decreasing cases of displacement and family separation as a result of inappropriate shelters and; (3) strengthening their capacity to rehabilitate and upgrade their shelters by supplying them with the needed materials and technical advice. The project will capitalize on AAH’s extensive technical experience in implementing shelter activities and RCSD’s experience in assessing and implementing humanitarian activities in the South West Bank. Activity 1: AAH and RCSD will rehabilitate and/or replace 61 substandard shelters, considering technical, cultural and protection considerations, diverse needs of women, men, boys and girls, the elderly as well as people with disabilities (based on FGDs). In order to do no harm, where the security situation requires it, direct beneficiary implementation will be adopted to avoid exposure and to mitigate protection risks. Technical solutions will be adapted accordingly. Activity 2: Rehabilitation and/or installation of 25 latrines based on initial survey on current hygiene practices, existing facilities, social and cultural habits and analysis of the economic conditions, considering key protection concerns and ensuring inclusive participation. This activity will complement Activity 1, as in some cases HHs receiving shelter upgrades/rehabilitation will require latrine installation as well. Activity 3: Provision of 52 winterization kits and NFIs, including plastic cover sheets, for Bedouin and herder dwellings, to mitigate winter-related hazards; appropriate kitchen kits will also be distributed. Activity 4: Supply and installation of protective measures for 25 shelters in the communities most affected by military attacks and settler violations. These measures will include the supply and installation of fences, protection materials for windows and doors, improving the shelter’s safety, for the particular benefit of women and children. Legal awareness sessions and consultation with relevant agencies, such as NRC, will be conducted. Existing protection committees, Mukhtars and women and men of targeted HHs will be involved in the design, implementation and monitoring of the activities through different meetings and FGDs targeting each group separately. Advocacy campaigns will be prepared to address SWOs/DOs. RCSD staff will be trained on monitoring direct implementation activities, their field officers will work directly with AAH technical staff and the targeted groups to ensure the quality of implementation. The implementation monitoring of the targeted shelters will be divided between AAH and RCSD staff. The latter will also be involved in the final handover of the rehabilitated shelters. This will ensure that RCSD field staff improve their capacities in designing, implementing and monitoring activities in sensitive areas. It will also enable them to establish direct communication with the related community bodies such as the local councils and CBOs.

Indicators

A socio-economic baseline survey will be conducted by RCSD and AAH to identify target families and conduct a gender and age analysis including sex and age disaggregated data. This survey will facilitate the supervision, monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Women, men, girls, boys, elderly people and people with disabilities will be involved in the design, monitoring and evaluation of the activities to ensure full participation of all members of the communities. This will be done through regular follow up in order to collect beneficiary feedback and suggestions for improvement, both at individual level and through FGDs, ensuring safe spaces where women, men, girls and boys are given the opportunity to express their needs and opinions. An end line and KAP survey will be conducted to assess and evaluate the satisfaction and impacts of the activities on each of these categories, including an evaluation on the allocation of additional disposable income derived from savings on annual maintenance of shelters, responsibilities of men and women at household level and improved access to adequate shelter and sanitation services. Project activities will be monitored through (1) working field staff; (2) community representatives; (3) beneficiary lists and donation certificates; (4) baseline and endline reports; (5) donor and cluster monitoring visits; (6) project progress and impact reports. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through a beneficiary feedback mechanism that will be carefully monitored. The protection committees and village councils will support AAH and RCSD to identify and liaise with beneficiaries, provide ongoing field monitoring of the rehabilitated shelters, and advise on the protection concerns as well as the ongoing security situation to determine the optimal timing of field operations. The expected objective verifiable indicators from the project are: - 61 shelters rehabilitated or reconstructed- 52 winterization kits distributed- 25 latrines improved or installed- 25 shelters protective materials supplied and installed- The project outcomes in line with the cluster expected outcomes (2018-2020) - 61 assisted HH (349 individuals: 91 women, 103 men and 155 children) living in upgraded unit in accordance with minimum standards- 426 assisted (123 women, 113 men and 190 children (140 HHs) used NFIs as intended to meet basic household needs- 25 assisted HH improved their access to basic WASH facilities
