Project Summary

In addition to food distribution to vulnerable populations, SI support vulnerable households with means of improving their livelihood, in order to increase their resilience. This will be accomplished through distribution of agriculture inputs (seed and tools) and Livestock (fattening); along with necessary trainings so enable them to earn a living, thereby contributing to resilience of beneficiary households.
Projects will be implemented in MMC, Monguno , Ngala, Askira Uba and Dikwa LGAs
Under the proposed project, SI will respond to the emergency food needs of the vulnerable populations through food vouchers for food assistance, in coordination with the Food Security Sector recommendation. To support the early recovery, SI proposes to improve household own food production through distribution of agriculture inputs such as seeds and tools to reduce reliance on humanitarian aid. Small livestock distribution will also be done to ensure recapitalization for the most vulnerable HHs which could also increase their income if they were to sell the said-products (milk, meat, egg etc.).
Host community members/returnees and IDPs staying with host communities will be targeted in priority for these interventions, as per the FSL sector priorities for 2020
Through the proposed projects in the areas, SI will ensure that adequate Do No Harm analyses are being conducted and that participatory methods in all aspects of the project cycle are implemented. All services to be provided will be designed to ensure safe and easy access for all taking into account vulnerable person with specific needs (women, children, elderly, etc).
SI has developed monitoring tools that include protection mainstreaming indicators and has included questions related to protection mainstreaming in all its surveys, in order to measure that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner.

1 - Needs

Since the conflict between Nigerian Security forces and armed opposition groups (AOGs) escalated in 2010, more than two million individuals have been displaced. Among them, a vast majority are composed of women and children. Most were displaced within Borno State. The humanitarian response is challenged by information gaps including a lack of clarity on the security environment in inaccessible areas, a lack of clarity on the availability and access of services and the varying vulnerabilities of beneficiaries. Following the projections of the Cadre Harmonisé from June to August 2020, about 3.6m individual food and nutrition situation will deteriorate during the lean season, especially in Borno State.. During this period, there will be around 3 million of the population moving into emergency phase (Phase 4). Acute food insecurity in the North east of Nigeria is mainly due to lack of access to farm lands, protracted conflicts leading to increase in the number of IDPs, disruption of market supplies and livelihoods, and limited access to food. This confirms the needs to sustain the ongoing humanitarian assistance and complement it with recovery and resilience interventions in order to promote self-reliance and prevent further deterioration of the food and nutrition security situation.In southern Borno, access to land and markets is better, although the LGAs were reported to have poor food consumption scores. In Dikwa LGAs, the return of IDPs was triggered by renewed accessibility, which has led to the gradual return of agricultural and livelihoods activities, basic services as well as the functionality of local market. However, recent findings show Dikwa LGA with highest % of HHs with poor food consumption among other LGAs: up to 82% of HHs. Recent assessment conducted by SI team showed important gaps remain in Dikwa when it comes to Host Community and IDPs out of informal camps.All LGAs currently targeted by the project are now all in Crisis Phase (IPC3). Market functionality remains constrained by poor transport infrastructure and insecurity. In Monguno, the main source of income of the IDPs has dramatically changed. Indeed, 82% in Monguno were rain fed farmers whereas nowadays, only 23% live from agro-pastoral production. In MMC and Askira, there are demonstrated gaps as very few beneficiaries have been reached, in comparison with the estimated number of individuals affected by food insecurity. In Monguno and Ngala our current livestock activities have proven continuous needs that will require further support. Finally, in Ngala, seeds and tools distributions implemented within our EU project have demonstrated the relevancy of ensuring a continuity in this type of activities in those areas. Women are specifically highly vulnerable to food insecurity, as they have globally less access to resources and assets, less access to education They also are poorer with less access to cash and less access to food. Women are more exposed to violence, especially sexual violence and are more involved in petty trade, begging and sale of natural resources. Vulnerable HH are particularly at risks of food insecurity and will require a specific focus with continuous inclusion and tailored activities. More than 75% of the HH in Borno State relied on one or more of negative coping strategies like begging, sex for food or sale of productive asset. These negative coping mechanisms are affecting more women. SI is currently implementing food security and livelihood project funded by EU, packages include food distribution to vulnerable households during the lean season, distribution of agricultural inputs (seeds and tools), livestock recapitalization and support household income source through provision of Start-up packages for identified Small income generating activities. SI will capitalize on this current experience to analyse lesson learn related to the implementation of these activities in order to increase their impact and efficiency.

2 - Activities

Data collection will be disaggregated by gender and age. Community participation will be a key principle in assessment and project implementation.1. Support most vulnerable Household with food assistance. (900HHs)SI will provide monthly food voucher support with transfer value of 25,000NGN to 900 vulnerable HH (average size of 7, total of 6,300 individuals: 1449 women, 1197 men, 1890 girls and 1764 boys) to improve HH food security and reduce use of negative coping strategies that affects particularly women and children, and their associated risks. In total, 9 rounds of voucher assistance will be implemented January to September 2020. The voucher value has been determined based on the FSS harmonized guidelines and SPHERE standards, providing 70% of the minimum energy needs of 2100 kilo calories/person per day. Specific protection measures will be implemented during distribution (identification of a safe distribution site, ensuring that access is guaranteed to all segments of the community). Before the distribution, beneficiaries will attend awareness sessions on hygiene, food safety and nutrition.2. Support the local agricultural production of targeted beneficiaries (400HHs) The selected 400 HHs (average size of 7, total of 2,800 individuals: 644 women, 532 men, 840 girls and 784 boys) will receive seeds and tools, chosen after beneficiaries’ consultation.The farmers will be classified into 2 groups, in consultation with community based on their preferences and previous framing skills: 1. Main crop farmers - these farmers’ HHs will receive 35kg of different variety of main crop. 2. Vegetable farmers - these farmers’ HH will receive 20kg of different vegetable seeds. Beneficiaries will also receive nutrition sensitive trainings and farmer field school module and will be trained on application of Bio-manures. The beneficiaries will be followed by SI Agricultural team during all the project to provide advices and recommendation. Specifically, SI will aim at targeting the youth for training sessions. 3. Support to small livestock recapitalisation (400HHs)For this activity, SI will target 400 vulnerable HH (average size of 7, total of 2,800 individuals: 644 women, 532 men, 840 girls and 784 boys) that primarily relied on livestock production before the crises. Each HH will receive 3 small ruminants, based on their preferences and choice of production: 2 female goats/sheep and one male for HH who choses reproduction, while 3 male sheep for HH who choses fattening, together with feed to cover half production cycle to prevent early destocking of livestock. Small livestock recapitalization will allow the most vulnerable HHs to increase their access to food thanks to the products derived from the livestock (milk, eggs…), but could also to increase their income. SI will also procure veterinary drugs and equipment to provide HH level veterinary services in a case of disease outbreak or injuries and trainings on key livestock management principles.BENEFICIARY TARGETING AND SELECTION.Beneficiaries will be consulted and engaged through different means, including the organization of gender specific FGD, in order to collect accurately and safely the view of the different groups among the community. Selection of beneficiary will consider the following criteria, among others: Food distribution: Female headed HH, Child headed HH, dependency ratio, people with chronic illness, SAM and MAM children, special needs etc. Livestock distribution: willingness, economic vulnerability (used to score the coping strategy index) and inclusive criteria. Seeds and tool distribution will be based on access to secured agricultural land. SI will also implement a joint verification with other FSS partners working in the geographical location to prevent duplication. SI will also ensure regular and improved coordination with local agricultural and livestock authorities to favor a better acceptance and ensure a proper implementation of project.

The Nexus activities will take place in the LGAs where such activity could be implemented without too strong restrictions. Additional LGAs, such as Askira, could be targeted by these specific activies after demonstrating sufficient land access, basic services and market functionality.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SI will collect the need of the community since the assessment phase through community consultation and participatory approaches. This would be done by using mechanism allowing to accurately collect specific needs of the different groups of the community, considering gender and age specificity, but also other specific vulnerabilities. SI will establish through community participation, a community based complaint management committees which members will comprise of elder statement and women of integrity whom are known and well respected by the community members, this people are usually the point of call as traditional means of settling conflict in the community.Where it is appropriate SI will set up complain and feedback box in the community which can be checked every day or weekly.Where there’re available mobile network, SI will set up a hotline number which community members can call or send sms to lodge in complains and feedbacksAll Solidarites staff have been trained on protection issues by Protection actors and can identify and refer all cases of GBV, child abuse/etc. if needed. SI employees have been trained about PSEA risks. SI is a member of PSEA network in Nigeria.The needs analysis and assessments conducted by SI capture the differences between men and women, and both groups are consulted. For crop kits, the inputs are distributed equally between men and women. Women are the preferred target beneficiaries for the vegetable kits distribution as vegetable growing is mostly implemented by women. Men will be preferred for the main crop kit distribution, as rain fed farming is traditionally carried out by men. Gender and age disaggregated data on programme coverage and impact will be collected, analysed and routinely reported on and programme activities monitored for improvement in self-reliance as well as beneficiary satisfaction for both women and men, girls and boys.SI will use at least 1% of this budget towards protection mainstreaming activities.
