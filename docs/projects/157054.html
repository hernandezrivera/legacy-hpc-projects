Project Summary

NGO X has been supporting the operation of four Water Units serving the conflict-affected population of Idleb Governorate since 2014. An estimated 560,000 individuals rely on the water pumped by 50 water station within the four units for their households’ drinking and hygiene needs. The programme will continue to provide inputs and technical expertise to allow for the continuation of services while strengthening the financial and management capacity of local stakeholders to manage the utility and continue on a path towards sustainability. In addition, NGO X will provide emergency assistance to recently displaced households to allow them to meet their basic hygiene needs.
In September 2018 NGO X managed to get an emergency fund to support additional 15 water stations that provide water to approximately 210,000 individuals in Idleb governorate. As a result, currently, NGO X is supporting 65 water stations by providing water to approximately 770,000 individuals in Idleb governorate, both projects are going to end by 31 March 2019 and it is expected that NGO X is going to receive fund to run the essential project which provide water to 560,000 individuals and NGO X will work to find a fund to continue the emergency project.
Prior to the conflict in Syria, the water and sanitation resources in Syria were under pressure from population growth and urbanization. Syrian water resources faced significant managerial challenges and had been prioritized by the Syrian government for modernization. Water utilities were heavily subsidized and as of 2006 were only covering 55% of their operational costs and were unable to accumulate funds for investment. The sector was further challenged by its complex management structure with different Ministries and sectors having overlapping responsibilities and functions. The protracted conflict has worsened the situation. Unreliable electricity, damaged water infrastructure, the influx of IDPs, threats from militant groups inside Idleb, and households with few financial resources are all challenges facing the water sector.
The NGO X programme’s desired outcome is that community water systems are efficiently and effectively managed and the community is informed, invested and supportive of local institutions and has sustained access to safe drinking water and hygienic conditions.’
Key activities include:
• Providing inputs (fuel, oil, HTH, salaries) to enable the stations to pump water for over 101 villages.
• Intense organisational capacity building to ensure water units and stations have the financial, administrative and management skills necessary to manage the stations in a transparent manner that prioritises accountability to customers, the community and donors.
• Stations develop realistic and achievable cost recovery targets based on financial analysis and communicate the approach to communities.
• Continue using the modality of ‘Infrastructure Stability’ fund that water stations can apply to for upgrading or investing in their stations and water networks. Maintains resources to enable emergency repairs conducted by NGO X.
• Supporting recent arrivals into Idleb with hygiene kits and hygiene promotion.

Needs

According to HNO 2019 report, 2,379,649 people are currently residing in Idleb Governorate and almost half of these identified as internally displaced persons (IDPs) (1,125,233) and a further 115,839 persons returned to Idleb in 2018. The Governorate now hosts the second largest number of IDPs in all of Syria and the burden of displacement appears to be extremely severe: the ratio of IDPs and/or returnees to resident population is 47%. This presents an enormous challenge in terms of providing a WASH response that mitigates for the impact of displacement and the burden placed on host communities. In October 2018, NGO X contracted a third-party monitoring (TPM) firm, CARFAX, to conduct a survey on its water provision programme. A total of 69 community surveys, eight focus group discussion and three local councils meeting were conducted in two villages in NGO X’s reached area. The majority (100% in one village and 90% in the other village) of respondents reported accessing water through the water main network, 25% through water trucking. This indicates that access to the water network is not universal in NGO X’s operational area. From NGO X’s experience, this can be attributed to a number of factors including water losses as a result of damaged infrastructure, new buildings not yet connected to the water network and low water pressure resulting in HHs located far away from water stations, at higher elevations or above the second floor in apartment blocks being unable to access water at household (HH) level. The respondents also indicated that the pumping is not always continuous through the networks, and people use in addition to water trucking the private wells in their area. The WASH HNO 2019 assessment showed an estimated 15.6 million people who require WASH assistance, 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need. The lack of a regular power supply still constitutes primary bottleneck for the WASH sector and explains the sub-standard performance of the water distribution systems in many areas, in particular NSAG (Non-State Armed Group) -controlled. In Northeast Syria, the contamination of water stations with explosive hazards has also been significant challenges, which have contributed to ongoing needs related to the lack of safe and affordable water and raised concerns on protection of WASH staff. The WASH HNO 2019 also has indicated that Idleb Governorate as well as surrounding NSAG controlled-areas have become extremely crowded amidst the significant displacement to and within the area. Increased pressure on already overstretched WASH services has resulted in increased dependency on alternative and often unsafe water sources that significantly increase the risk of waterborne diseases. The continuous drilling of wells in search for water has negatively affected groundwater resources, with a significant depletion of water aquifers observed especially around camps near the Turkish border. The extremely high levels of displacement combined with increase in the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites has presented significant challenges to WASH sector partners, in view of the increased cost and complexity to deliver in IDP site settings than in host communities. Cross-border and cross-line access have a particular importance for WASH sector, especially for people with acute WASH needs as this type of programming constitutes bulk of WASH emergency response. Finally, keys gaps exist in the operations and maintenance of water and sewerage networks in terms of coordinating efforts to remedy the situation, systematising routine works and ensuring quality of works carried out. The protracted conflict has worsened the situation. Unreliable electricity, damaged water and sewerage infrastructure, the influx of IDPs, threats from militant groups inside Idleb, and households with few financial resources are all challenges facing the WASH sector.

Protection Risk Analysis

For WASH NFIs distribution there are the following risks: inequality in distribution, physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion, gender insensitive contents verities of kits with inconsistent items and late Distribution of expired unhealthy items. To mitigate the effects of these risks NGO X will do the following: Promote Establishment of adequate management plan, promote involvement communities in distribution planning and feedback on package for improvement, provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to users, Harmonise and standardise gender friendly hygiene kits among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use and establish and monitor expiry date of supplies prepositioned in warehouse, ensure relevant storage provision and carry out timely disposal of damaged/expired items. For Support to water supply systems there are the following risks: Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors and theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by groups and used in conflict activities. To mitigate the effects of these risks NGO X will do the following: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time, organise joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard, Include community and authorities in targeting, ensure sensitive information is handled with care, MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities and promote cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. For Proper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials there is a risk of possibility of explosion and human casualty and asset destruction and NGO X will mitigate this risk by Ensure proper storage of supplies with clear provision of care and safety consideration.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, supported by continued adherence to NGO X's acceptance strategy and the joint operating procedures (JOPs), while security conditions remain sufficiently stable;- NGO X maintains a level of cooperation with the Turkish authorities who essentially guarantee NGO X safe access to the region;- Sufficient and timely access to required inputs through procurement, reliable supply and available transportation continues, and border crossing remains open;- Strong coordination and collaboration with local authorities and humanitarian actors in the AoO prevails;- NGO X's strategies and mechanisms to prevent armed and sanctioned actors from interference in programmes that violate the JOPs remain effective;- NGO X can continue to support cross border activities by maintaining its offices in Turkey as its primary hub Risks:- Remote control management from Turkey and Jordan presents the risk of international and Syrian staff losing legal status; sudden border closures disrupting operations; other regulatory changes which could affect the way NGO X operates cross-border;- There is a risk of fraud (misappropriation/theft of funds/assets) including banking, cash management, procurement, transport, and programming;- Tools/repair supplies not available: repairs not able to be completed on time due to lack of availability of parts and other supplies- Fuel theft or misuse at water stations- Aid diversion – cross border supply chain- Lack of coordination in WASH sector results in duplication of effort or major gaps Mitigation:- Safety and Security Evacuation Plans (SSEP) and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) in place, and an experienced security team on both sides of the border which will continue to monitor trends, conduct an actor mapping, and share information to enable rapid and appropriate changes to implementation plans;- Ongoing communication with actors operational across the operational area, and continued adherence to Acceptance Strategies built on community and stakeholder understanding of NGO X’s programming; this means working closely with Local Councils to identify shocks and affected HHs, plan and conduct distributions, as well as maintaining open communications with communities;- Advocate for these supplies to be allowed through on the same terms as humanitarian items. Identify potential suppliers at beginning of program, allow adequate time for procurement. At the outset of the programme, NGO X conducts a spare parts supply chain assessment to discern who the suppliers are, where spare parts are coming from and where the gaps lie. NGO X will also analyse previous procurements to identify the spare parts that were difficult to procure inside Syria and look at alternative solutions i.e. procurement in Turkey.- NGO X has a strong reconciliation system that is reviewed by Senior management on a monthly basis.
