Project Summary

The project aims to support the IDPs in North West Syria through the distribution of basic and seasonal NFI kits, in addition, to help people cope with the situation in the area and survive the harshest of conditions both in and off-camps. Also, the project includes multisectoral response to ensure access and availability to basic services, like shelter upgrades and replacement, tent insulation, in addition to roads repairs, rain drainage, and building of latrines, to cover the raising needs for shelter and NFIs in North Rural Aleppo and Western Rural Idlib areas. The project will be targeting both IDPs and host communities to alleviate the pressure on community resources. alongside different gender and age needs including women children elderly and people with special needs will be taken into consideration while designing the project.
The intervention will be done through in-kind modalities preferably bought from the local market ensuring support to the local economy. Allocation of items in areas will be done through needs assessment which will be multi-sectoral in nature to capture all aspects of IDPs’ and their hosts’ conditions, to explore a wide range of interventions and to ensure appropriate intervention. 4 - Needs

According to the NHO 2021 report, the overall number of people in need of humanitarian assistant in Syria is 13.4 million a number that is slightly lower than 2016 and 2017 and is a 21% increase from last year’s. 2.7M of these needing shelter assistance lives in NWS. The shelter PiN has increased 4% between 21 and 20 according to the HNO. The Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute, a significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement, and new IDPs swell already overburdened communities especially in NWS, resorting to living in unsustainable settlements with poor conditions and access to basic services. The majority of the 1200 IDP sites listed in the CCCM database are self-settled, meaning that they lack proper site planning and infrastructure, and inadequate access to shelter and safe water. According to HNAP, The IDP shelter conditions are poor, and a significant of 11% of the population live in tents, and while most IDP households live in finished homes and while it is the most common shelter type across the country, the story is different in NWS were a whopping 28% of IDP HH living in tents. Only 3% of IDP HH reported no issues with their shelter, lack of space, privacy, and cold and damp conditions were the most issues reported. A signiﬁcant proportion of those who are in tents and improvised shelters will need replacement/repairs yearly. Also, 90% of the HH reported diminished access to NFI’s with unaffordability being the main constraint. As nearly as 1M people were displaced in NWS until the spring of 2020, accompanied by the pandemic and the economical deterioration. Poor access to NFIs, especially during winters such as heating fuel, blankets, and winter clothes.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

QC has a clear strategy to mitigate the risks of PSEA, the strategy includes direct and indirect beneficiaries of our programming, adults in wider communities in which we work in. It also includes QC’s staff, partners, suppliers sub-contractors and any other individual engaged by QC's programs. Particular attention is needed for vulnerable groups, including women and children, and those most at risk of discrimination based on disability, gender identity and sexual orientation, age, and religion. Our approach to PSEA is through the following:1- Ensure that all staff, representatives and third parties are aware of the high standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect individuals of SEA. 2- Having an organizational culture based on accountability where there is zero tolerance for PSEA. 3- Have an endorsed reporting mechanism for staff, beneficiaries and partners to feel safe to report violations, and then 4- ensure that reports are handled in a manner that respects due process, and swift and credible investigation for any violations. And actively promote fair investigation and sanctions. 5- mitigating risks in programming by identifying the risks and design the program accordingly. Starting from needs assessments through project design (shelter layouts, kits components) to the implementation to closing phases. 6- Comprehensive operational, HR and mgmt. measures are also required to PSEA. By conducting relative vetting and checks of staff, suppliers ..

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Outbreak of conﬂict prevents access to project locations. Mitigation: Experience from previous operations found that while conﬂict occurred, a cautious approach that plans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Further, the project design ﬂexibly targets at a unit level of “sub-district.” By using this target instead of speciﬁc communities, the project can respond district-wide, wherever non-conﬂict areas with access allow. Risk 2: Current controlling entity or local council no longer supports QC activities or directly interferes with activities threatening humanitarian imperative Mitigation: staff/partners keep in close contact with the various local councils to be able to address any issues at an early stage. QC is transparent throughout all actions and communications with all stakeholders to ensure our status as impartial, neutral, and independent is unquestionable. QC will suspend operations in one location while continuing in another until delivery of aid is assured to be unimpeded. Risk 3: Bad weather conditions. Mitigation: By starting all Shelter activities during spring until the end of summer to avoid bad weather conditions which will result in a delay in implementing the activities, and cut access to targeted locations. Risks: 4 Overcrowding and/or integrating groups that do not have social histories together has the potential to increase risks of exposure to different forms of GBV. Mitigation: Use the GBV speciﬁc assess. questions to understand the different GBV risks present in the collective centers and/ or camps as well as different households' speciﬁc needs to feel safe in/around their shelter structures. Implement GBV risk reduction activities through Specific shelter..
