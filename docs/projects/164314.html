Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide essential primary and secondary health care services through timely assistance to war wounded, victims of violence and conflict-affected people including trauma care, primary health care, life-saving obstetric and reproductive health, as well as psychosocial support in Raqqa and Deir ez Zor governorates through the support of two targeted health facilities. In Raqqa, the project will support the running of a maternity hospital to provide comprehensive reproductive service and paediatric primary health care. In Deir ez Zor, health services access will be provided through the support of Shiheil health facility, in coordination with local authorities. Activities under this project will contribute to address the lack of affordable, quality and safely access to health services in targeted areas. To meet new needs and mitigate new risks since the military events of October 2019, pre-positioning of medicine and equipment for hospitals has been integrated in the intervention strategy to increase the capacity to respond to new needs coming from the security and the context evolution in the future, both within NES and along border-crossings.

4 - Needs

In November 2019, this international Health partner conducted a Technical Assessment in Raqqa and Deir ez Zor (DEZ) governorates, based on a quantitative methodological approach. The assessment targeted for both PHCs and hospitals and it allowed the mapping of the health situation in both Raqqa and DEZ. The assessment targeted 7 public hospitals (4 in Raqqa, 3 in DEZ) and 28 PHC (11 in Raqqa, 17 in DEZ). The assessment of the health service delivery has been coupled with interviews with the directors and administrators of the targeted facilities. The assessment has been conducted over three days in November 2019 and the report has been finalised and disseminated. The assessment focused on four main components: general information related to the type of implemented activities, number of beneficiaries and financial status; human resources; type of medical services provided; and list of medical equipment and drugs. The data analysis reported as key health priorities the following: provision of primary health care, emergency and life-saving medical and surgical assistance as well as SRH. In Raqqa and DEZ, health facilities face major issues such as the increase of catchment areas because of the displacement, and a lack of referral and support services; supply chains for medical goods have been severely disrupted; and the long distances travelled by those seeking medical assistance exposes them to serious protection risks. In November 2019, this Health partner reported that all hospitals assessed are heavily reliant on external support and funding and there is not yet the capacity of the government to take over these facilities to ensure their availability to the public of a quality, free service.

5 - Implementation of activities

This partner will continue to provide essential health services through timely assistance through trauma care, primary health care, life-saving obstetric and reproductive health, as well as psychosocial support. This action will respond to the needs of 2 health facilities (one in Shiheil and one in Raqqa). The action will be structured as follows: (1) Support to heath facilities to provide primary health care and specialised consultations, life-saving emergency and surgical assistance, SRH care including family planning counselling ; (2) GBV, Child Protection PSS and case management referrals for women and children in the supported health facilities. The overall strategy will be guided by upholding humanitarian principles, safe and equal access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries, as well as appropriate considerations for context-specific vulnerabilities. In this, special attention will also be paid to protection mainstreaming by streamlining gender, age and disability considerations in all stages of project cycle management (needs analysis, strategic/operational planning, implementation, monitoring, reporting and evaluation). The Health partner will also uphold its policies and procedures regarding Child Safeguarding and PSEA obligations throughout its programming. This partner will also screen for malnutrition for all children between 6 months and 5 years, using MUAC. This partner treats only MAM cases, and refers SAM cases (which need special milk and special food (RTUF) to another health actors, using a "MUAC form" for screening and a "medical referral form" when any case needs to be referred. The MEAL process covers a number of steps and concepts: a continuous review of project progress, checking planned activities against results, evaluating if corrective action needs to be taken; gathering and analysing information of activities/outputs; evaluate the quality of the intervention and what can be improved.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The key protection risks associated with Health response are in line with those identified by the Protection sector in the Protection Risk Assessments/Analysis Matrix for Health. Risks include but are not limited to: attacks and threats against health facilities; incapacity to respond sufficiently to gender-based violence related cases to provide survivors with the assistance and care required; sexual exploitation and abuse of vulnerable people; harm and stress of healthcare workers put themselves and affected populations at risk; risk of data breaches ; risks associated with the closure of health facilities including data protection violation, looting or diversion of equipment and supplies, and gap in healthcare services; lack of guidance on context sensitivity for healthcare workers; lack of participation and input from affected communities on needs and priorities; and inequitable access to healthcare services across communities, especially for the most vulnerable Mitigation measures will be taken to the best of the Partner’s ability throughout all programming, which include but is not limited to: use all available mechanisms for visibility, advocacy and accountability regarding attacks against health facilities; train all healthcare staff on how to provide care for SGBV survivors, including integration of GBV prevention and response activities within all healthcare departments; apply and train all staff (and potential staff) on PSEA policies and guidelines; implement effective and accessible community complaints and feedback mechanisms; establish clear ‘duty of care’ policies for all staff and implement throughout all health programming; implement strong data protection systems that are backed up; train staff on safe withdrawal and closure procedures for both data and physical equipment or infrastructure; consultation with community (women and girls, people with disabilities and older people); establish strong links with local authorities and humanitarian actors

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risks include but are not limited to: impact of the UN cross-border resolution on humanitarian access, cross-line humanitarian access is further restricted; authorities in areas of intervention do not allow cross-border or internal access; registration delays or refusal by GoS for INGOs in receiving authorisation to work; local authorities’ control and monitoring of humanitarian assistance jeopardizes the ability to respect core humanitarian principles; border closures in neighboring countries inhibit the flow of good or personnel; escalation of conflict; shift of frontlines; potential conscription measures coming into effect that would impact the work and movement of national staff in areas under SDF control. Assumptions for this project include but are not limited to: humanitarian workers and programs are not directly targeted; dormant cells from radical groups preventing access to the intervention area are not activated; the security situation does not deteriorate; cross-border humanitarian access and intervention continue to be permitted and feasible; borders remain open with neighbouring countries; market prices, inflation rates and exchange rates remain stable and accessible. Mitigation measures include but are not limited to: providing support to advocacy efforts by the existing coordination forum at national and international level if cross-border implementation is hindered; continue the existing partnership with national NGOs as well as identifying new potential partners, in tandem with launching the registration process with GoS in Damascus and identifying alternative solutions to completing activities in case of delays in the registration process; maintain strong relationships with local authorities to ensure acceptance, information sharing and strong communication channels between parties; monitor the context closely, continual updating of internal contingency planning; monitor security context closely and ensure continual security plan update
