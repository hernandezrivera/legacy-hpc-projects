Project Summary

The project seeks to improve the maternal health of PLW as well as children between the ages of 6 -59 months in Garbaharey district in Gedo region, Somalia. Through running of health center in Garbaharey the project is able to treat multiple referrals and admission cases, undertake critical reproductive health services, undertake screening and admissions and undertake periodical and critical health programs relating to sanitation, hygiene, breastfeeding. While at it the project also capacitates women and other CHW on critical essential services to be replicated to the targeted communities.
Additional it aims at [providing routine immunizations. The planned antenatal care and post natal care is considered critical due to lack of hospital (in Garbaharey apart from the MCH dealing with nutrition that GEWDO runs. while at it the project will also undertake screening of children, referrals where appropriate and deal with obstetric cases.

Needs

Many IDPs and host communities in Gedo Region especially in Garbaharey and Dolow lack basic adequate basic social services. The relentless past drought, catastrophic floods, prolonged and protracted conflict (instability in South Central Somalia have contributed towards towards food insecurity, malnutrition, lack of social services like education, poor hygiene problems and systematic destruction of assets. With over 1.1 million people displaced, according to UNICEF, and mainly to the South central which is the epicenter of the conflict and millions others undocumented owing to volatile security risks in Somalia the situation can not get any worst. Destruction of the social services in the ensuing conflict and the natural destruction of sources of livelihood mainly livestock and partly farms through droughts and lately floods have left the local people exposed to a myriad of challenges. Add to lack of adequate resources from the local administration and actors and the situation can only get dire. Somali has one of the worst health indicators in the world. According to UNICEF The Under-Five and Maternal Mortality rates in Somalia are amongst the highest in the world; one out of every seven Somali children dies before seeing their fifth birthday (137 deaths/1,000 live births) with a higher number in south and central Somalia. The leading causes of infant and child mortality are illnesses such as pneumonia (24 per cent), diarrhoea (19 per cent), and measles (12 per cent), as well as neonatal disorders (17 per cent). Undernutrition is an underlying factor in over a third of the deaths. One out of every 12 women dies due to pregnancy related causes (Maternal Mortality Rate is 732 deaths of mothers for 100,000 live births – down from 1210 in 1990). The average fertility rate is 6.6 children per woman. Access to maternal health in Garbaharey district is low. This is the first major project providing prenatal and antenatal care, obstetrics and sanitation combined.

Activities

The project activities shall be;(i) Provide adequate medicine and other support activities to the MCH in Garbajarey.(ii) Screening and admission of pre- and post natal cases.(iii) Provision of the necessary maternal information targeting PLW.(iv) Provision of curative services to the women .(v) Capacity building of women on prenatal and postnatal care practices required including breastfeeding and sanitation.(vi) Periodic and routine immunization.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Constant migration and movements of people in Somalia's complex geopolitical environment can not be avoided especially when the displacements are caused by factors beyond the control of GEWDO. However the organization undertakes routine documentation especially for the new arrivals in the IDP camps and host community in which it operates. Where appropriate it promptly includes the new arrivals in its programs provided they demonstrate conditions admissible under the standard screening and admissions criteria set out in the different programs. While at it the organization, cognizant of the complex clan-based anthropological nature of Somalia ensures that whenever its program includes communities displ;ace from other ares and trhe risk of inter-clan tension may be significantly higher, it works with the local community, local authority and stakeholders to thaw this tension.. These policy has helped GEWDO to operate in very complex areas including rural villages of Dollow, Rural Garbahare and the Somalia Elwak towns.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Somalia has very complex gender based exclusions that are embedded in the local culture. lately through its various programs GEWDO has been able to see possible progress which includes even women being accepted as part and parcel of decision making and peace processes. This is done through community wide collaborations and consultations, all the while working with stakeholders to promote women issues. This policy has worked in the past and GEWDO still intends to use it. At internal level the organization does not condone any exclusion based on gender and age and none of its employee or partners does the same. It is a policy that has guided the organization since its formation and is at the core of all its activities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Somalia is still very dangerous for humanitarian work (and also to the local populace)and the risk of conflict in South Central Somalia can not be ignored. Working with the local administration, goodwill from the local community and the relevant security agencies, the organization has been able to avoid major security problems and none of its beneficiaries have been killed or injured unless in a very rare circumstances within the control of the organization.
