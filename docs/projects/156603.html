Project Summary

Support the resilience of residents affected by the crisis especially the households headed by women, by supporting the main agriculture services and reducing the productivity cost for the olive trees and empowering the marginalized families by providing them with a income generating source.
the project will purchase the yield of 2000 farmers have 100,000 olive trees, the expected yield will be 1,000 MT of olives, the production will be squeezed to produce 200 MT of olives oil, 50 % of the oil will be directed to support the next cycle of the project while 100 MT of oil will be distributed to most vulnerable IDPs and host communities HHs.

Needs

According to HNO 2019; An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategiesEight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. Almost 40 percent of the surveyed households report to be an IDP or returnee of whom 74.5 percent reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Among them 478,282 displaced people living in last resort camps are of particular concern as they have limited access to a diversified diet or income opportunities. The relationship between poverty and food security – understood in terms of availability of food to the whole population, and access to products guaranteeing minimum nutrition standards – is inseparable. As per World Bank from 2011 – 2016, the cumulative GDP loss in Syria is estimated at $226 billion out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone . Overall from various sources 83-89 percent of Syrians are Below Poverty Line. As per ESCWA, an estimated 83.4 percent of Syrians live below the upper (moderate) poverty line which is an increase from 28 percent in 2010 . Additionally, the Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) which takes into account health, education and basic needs related indicators showed that the national poverty rate in Syria is at 38.9 percent. In light of the severe impact of the crisis, unemployment has risen to 55 percent and purchasing power has declined to 1/10 of pre-crisis level (if compared to the income in 2010). A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. Although food availability in markets has generally improved compared to the previous years, main constraints with reinvigorating markets remain the high exchange rate of Syrian Pounds to US Dollar, steep transport costs, and low demand due to high unemployment rates and limited purchasing power. As provided in the graph below the price of a monthly Food Ration has reduced if compared to the peak of the crisis, but still remain very high for Syrians. Purchasing food on credit continues to be adopted by every second household and is more common among IDPs, returnees, female headed and rural households. An estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Low Capacity coupled with challenges in recruitment and retention of appropriately skilled staff at all levelsMitigation measure: - Bridging the staff gap- implementing a capacity building and training program for staff at various tiers and implementation responsibilities2. Lack of coordination and synergies with representatives of target communitiesMitigation measure: involve focal persons from the local council to coordinate with the beneficiary elders 3. Identification of beneficiaries and appreciation of project achievements will be weak because they do not hear, read or see enough of project activities and positive impactsMitigation measure: - Encourage field visits by community elders with appointed focal persons to see the results and hear from beneficiaries directly- Develop a communications strategy to guide constituency building through internal and external communications with Project stakeholders- Consider Media communication and mobile applications 4. Grievances and complaints of expected direct beneficiaries are ignored by the implementing partner with all the negative consequences this will haveMitigation measure:- Establish a comprehensive Grievances Redress Mechanism (GRM) with well-defined guidelines for complaint acknowledgement and redressal. The GRM to be publicized externally as part of each project communication's strategy- staff are provided with the necessary training to report unaddressed complaints 5. Insufficient supervision and poor-quality control will cause faults and deficiencies in project deliverablesMitigation measure: periodically carry out spot checks, certify quality and report back to QRC 6. Continuing risk of insecurity and potential escalation in insurgency related violence with direct impacts on project activitiesMitigation measure:- train field staff on MOSS compliance (Minimum Operational Safety Standards)- Use of alternative measures for project supervision including Remote Asset Monitoring to carry out spot checks in the target areas 7. Non-transparent procedures of recruitment and procurement processes may lead to project getting a bad reputationMitigation measure: Establish a wider and transparent dissemination of project procurement and recruitment advertisements 8. Sustainable functioning of distribution or project implementation not well ensuredMitigation measure:- Contribution of identified and planned capacity building by trainings - Encourage communities to provide their feedback on the distribution process or project deliverables- Ensure M&E efficacy- Qualified staff hired
