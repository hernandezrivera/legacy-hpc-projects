The project in line with the education sector response framework in Syria 2021 following two main pillars 1) Access: Increased safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children, 2) Systems: the capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence-based education response strengthened,
The General Objective of the proposed project is to contribute to increasing resilience through improving sustainable and equitable access to basic education services in communities affected by the crisis.
The Specific Objective is to improve the satisfaction of the basic education of the most vulnerable populations targeted the most vulnerable areas based on the last updated severity scale from education sector in Rural Damascus Dier Ezzour, Daraa, Hama, Qunietra and Aleppo (children, girls, women and children with disabilities), through interventions that ensure decent educational conditions and strengthen their resilience in 12 months. We intend to achieve these objectives with: a) light Rehabilitation and adaptation of safe spaces in 4 elementary school in each location in order to promote equitable and inclusive access to children, including children with disabilities; b) Increase of quality teaching methods and equal learning opportunities; c) Strengthening protective education services in the 4 target schools in each location; d) Strengthening of COVID-19 prevention. The direct beneficiaries of the project are 220,151 beneficiary: included school-aged children, children with disabilities marginalized and vulnerable children and girls who will received economic assistance(Cash for Education) to reduce financial barriers to school access, teachers and school staff will be trained on innovative pedagogical methodologies for inclusive education and addressing the protection needs of girls and boys.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO2021,after10years of conflict, it is critical that formal and non-formal education services are approached as a long-term investment by not only children and their families but by aid actors.COVID-19 and worsening economic conditions have worsened the consequences of systemic underinvestment in education serviceDue to the compounding impact of2020situation many students are either behind in their learning finding it difficult to return to school or at risk of dropping out.Across all learning levels, education facilities that can provide inclusive education for the estimated15%of school-aged children with disabilities remain extremely limited. Children who did not attend school are more likely to come from households that perceive themselves as hungry.IDP children were more likely to come from these households. Key reasons children were not going to school include, schools not being available, schooling not being affordable and children working to support the household.Broadly, households responded that the costs associated with sending children to school need to be alleviated; more schools need to be established, school structures need to be made safe and conducive to learning. Teachers need to be invested in both through capacity building and adequate,remuneration.The threats that students face attending schools that are unsafe are exacerbated by COVID-19.It is difficult to ensure physical distancing or ventilation in overcrowded schools, together with limited availability of operational WASH facilities. Accordingly,TdH is planning to intervene in the locations through admin3taking the severity sore into account:Jebel Saman,As-Salamiyeh,DeirezZor,Dar'a,Quneitra,Qudsiya,Haran Al'awameed, Arbin,Madaya

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities, M1: SARC and SCFPA have displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations put onto them. Currently they are already accessing the mentioned locations with funding from various entities for several months. R2: TdH are unable to timely contract technically skilled employee, M2:TdH has solid recruitment/human resources processes and regulations to ensure the best qualiﬁed candidates are hired in a timely manner.R3: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities, M3: TdH already obtained approvals to work in Northern ,Middle and southern area of Syria, in addition, TdH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU,R4: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria, M4: TdH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources.R5: Security risks : TdH and Local partner’s staff are exposed to threats, including kidnapping, detention, intimidation, and serious injury, M5: the security situation has been considered relatively calm but unpredictable and partners are able to conduct their work in relative safety,TdH Security officer and Security Focal points in the project locations are regularly analyse the operating environment and have established safeguards to prevent and respond to risks R6: Due to COVID-19 outbreak in the targeted locations/schools the local authorities applied curfew in all governorates,M6: TdH have a plan B changing the most of the activates methods to be online instead of the physical presence.
