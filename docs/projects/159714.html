Project Summary

The Overall Objective of the proposed intervention is to improve coping mechanisms of vulnerable Palestinian farmers in area C including preventative measures and mitigate the impact of settler and Israeli security forces violence and intimidation to sustain their livelihoods. The two specific objectives are: to enable farmers to resume and maintain their livelihood sources through rehabilitation and support of livelihood productive assets; and to improve the coping ability of vulnerable farmers to withstand protracted threats and intimidation. The project is in line with the country strategic objective three (SOP3): The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The intervention is in line with the Food Security response strategy (HRP 2019), “Protecting vulnerable household livelihoods by restoring their productive capacity, through the rehabilitation and provision of basic assets; the facilitation of access of essential inputs and services to advance the quality and sustainability of local production; the promotion of income-generating activities; and the introduction of more efficient and sustainable technologies (e.g. renewable energy).” The project is specifically in line with the Food Security Sector (FSS) objective no 1: The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected.

1 - Needs

The proposed intervention builds on the current FS intervention implemented by the four partners and will continue to address prevailing needs of acutely vulnerable farming households. According to the 2018 SEFSEC, 10.1% of the Area C population is severely insecure compared to 2.3% in areas A and B; while 15.9% are moderately food insecure compared to 5.3% in areas A and B.. The vulnerability of women is further exacerbated by their limited possession of assets, hindering their ability to support their families including their basic needs. Validated needs for this phase include constrained productive capacities due to continued damage to vulnerable farmer’s agricultural assets resulting from Israeli occupation policies, settler and security forces violence; subjection of lands in nearby settlements to the permit regime, stop work orders and movement restrictions; shortage of water for agriculture and lack of irrigation networks to facilitate access to water; lack of access to agricultural lands due to inadequate agricultural roads, poor farm management, and insufficient feeds; lack of access to resources enabling sustenance of livelihoods, and access to water from cisterns and filling points. The lack of awareness on legal proceedings, available channels and financial resources to address land confiscation and stop work orders are due to the following: a) absence of a centralized administrative body providing legal counseling and response mechanisms to address military orders; b) lack of knowledge about technical steps to prepare integrated and complete case files to challenge military orders; c) presence of several entities managing such cases, causing confusion, lack of accountability and mistrust of people receiving military orders towards service providers; d) continued IHL violations by the GOI against Palestinian land and housing rights; e) loss of large areas of land, especially agricultural land, due to increasing Israeli stop orders over the last five years; f) failure to address hundreds of Israeli military orders due to frustration, ignorance, and hopelessness in challenging them; g) increasing costs of legal aid to defend the right to land and housing. During the current phase the consortium coordinated the selection of localities and types of assistance with the MoA and its Directorates of Agriculture, FSS, FAO, PARC, CRS and other actors. In 2019, the MoA’s Damage Documentation Department (DDD), registered increased incidents of attacks towards agricultural lands and livelihoods assets and is in the process of registering pending verifications to be shared with the consortium. During the previous phase farmers have reported prolonged processes of documenting attacks and entry into the MoA registry. Building on lessons learnt, the intervention proposed will address needs and challenges through a revised criteria aligned to the current 2019 HF project: vulnerable farmers with damaged assets; farmers with socio-economic and protection challenges not registered in the MoA system; and farmers prone to or at risk of settler violence and damages to their agricultural assets. This includes the pool of governorates and localities expanded to seven governorates instead of six; 53 localities instead of 43, focusing on Qalqiliya. The consortium will continue coordinating with relevant actors/sectors to avoid overlap with planned interventions by other aid actors.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will continue providing women and men farmers in Area C with agriculture assets to maintain their livelihoods and enhance their coping mechanisms. The intervention will be implemented in coordination with MoA, FSS Cluster and other actors. Oxfam and partners will mainstream protection and prevention aligned to humanitarian interventions to meet needs of most vulnerable farmers. Oxfam will ensure livelihood resumption, sustainability and improved access to agricultural assets to support farmers in rehabilitating agricultural roads/lands, land preparation, agricultural inputs, gender sensitive livelihoods and conditional cash. The design, implementation and selection of localities and beneficiaries will be coordinated with MoA, line Ministries and relevant organizations. Selection criteria includes farmers neither receiving support in maintaining their livelihoods from MoA nor from other actors. Farmers will be selected from a pool of 53 communities from seven governorates. MoA will provide beneficiary lists based on agreed criteria, while Oxfam will reassess and validate them. MoA’s procedures, guidelines and resources will be considered to maximize benefits, avoid harm and effort duplication. When identifying agriculture livelihood needs, decision makers are represented by HH members (male and female farmers) considering that participants below 18 years, is child labour. Oxfam will lead on overall implementation, while partners (ESDC, MA’AN, LRC) will directly implement activities. ESDC is a member of the SVCG and FSS with expertise in livelihood rehabilitation damaged by settlers. MA’AN, with expertise in agriculture, will rehabilitate livelihood assets. LRC, with legal expertise on documentation, advocacy and referrals will support partners and beneficiaries in coordination with the Jerusalem Legal Aid and Human Rights Centre (JLAC). Proposed outputs and activities build on the 1st HF and 2019 HPC reflecting emerging priorities and coordination with actors during the 1st HF and recent coordination meetings with MoA. Output 1. Livelihoods of 274 beneficiary farmers (27 women and 247 men) are sustained through emergency agricultural assets support: 1.1 Coordinate with MoA, FSS and relevant actors; 1.2. Filter cases with no responses from MoA, OCHA tracker, other actors; 1.3 Refer cases not within FS mandate to relevant clusters; GBV cases to GBV cluster; 1.4 Procure agricultural assets/conditional cash support; 1.5 Monitor delivery and implementation. Output 2: Livelihoods of 1200 beneficiary farmers (1080 men and 120 women) and their families protected through access to and the rehabilitation of agricultural land: 2.1 Hold community engagement sessions with men and women farmers, MoA and Governorate level to identify protection interventions; 2.2: Select agricultural roads in coordination with MoA/relevant authorities; 2.3 Confirm additional interventions as per engagement sessions; 2.4 Procurement for agricultural roads rehabilitation/relevant interventions; 2.5 Rehabilitate 40km of agricultural roads; 2.6 Rehabilitate 520 dunums prioritizing demolished/affected crops, land, assets through inputs and services (seedlings, grains, machinery work, fencing) and rehabilitate greenhouses; 2.7 CFW to 256 farmers through land rehabilitation; 2.6: Technical support to farmers especially women through female agricultural engineers. Output 3: 20 farmers HHs (45 Females and 47 Males) farmers are supported through legal aid and 3.1 Monitor and document settler attacks, lands vandalizing and ISF excessive use of force; 3.2 Refer legal, psychosocial, other needs to relevant clusters; 3.3 Refer affected women to safe service providers; 3.4 Awareness sessions on accessing legal aid/legal processes; 3.5 Technical support (preparation of legal files) to 20 cases of victims of land and housing rights violations; 3.6 financial support to cover legal aid fees to farmers/victims (10) of land violations.

3 - Indicators

Oxfam is committed to genders-sensitive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) across all its programs and projects which ensure equal access to Oxfam project services for males and females from all age groups. Oxfam also developed a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability (CAMSA) which forms the basis of M&E systems for all Oxfam projects. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies will be applied. CAMSA approach guides on how gender-relevant needs, actions, and issues are monitored during implementation and how project reviews and evaluations consider gender sensitive issues. However, all tools, data gathering, data analysis, reporting and recommendations and action points are gender and age disaggregated were required. Throughout the project MEAL will be undertaken in accordance with a comprehensive M&E plan which will track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe, and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. Data collected will be gender sensitive and disaggregated by age and sex for both qualitative and quantitative in nature using different tools such as questionnaires, Key Informative Interviews (KII), Stories of change, and Focus group discussions (FGDs) Prior to the projects inception, OXFAM’s MEAL team will develop an M&E framework to ensure that all indicators are tracked properly and in a timely manner. Oxfam’s MEAL team together with partners will ensure carrying out regular monitoring visits, reporting through focus groups and individual meetings with women, men and beneficiaries at risk of settler and IF violence. Oxfam will work in close coordination with humanitarian assistance providers to communities at risk of losing their livelihood source and forcible transfer in Area C address gaps efficiently. It will also adopt a beneficiary feedback mechanism to inform MoA, FSS and OCHA about referred cases. A complaint mechanism will be put in place in collaboration with a representative of the MoA. Finally, lessons learnt workshops will be organized at the end of the project to document learnings, case studies, successes and challenges. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The system has an effective referral system by which the submitted feedback referred to relevant program manager based on the selected category by the reporter. The accountability mechanism is managed by Oxfam MEAL Officer ensuring that all complaints are managed within 2 weeks. For major programmatic issues, senior management will be also reported. However, safeguarding issues including sexual exploitation and abuse are referred to safeguarding team and the country director and removed from the system for data protection purposes. The ICT system as well as the complaint template have the following categories: Category 1: Positive Feedback, Category 2: Request for Assistance, Category 3: Request for information update, Category 4: Minor Dissatisfaction, Category 5: Major dissatisfaction, Category 6: Safeguarding issues (Fraud, unaccepted Behaviors, sexual exploitation, and Abuse).

8.6.b - If 'Other', please specify

In OPT, Oxfam developed a comprehensive Feedback, Complaint, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) under its Saving Lives Program (including under this project). The mechanism has been activated in September 2018 using mobile case management tool to capture real-time data from the field through the use of Information Communication Technologies (ICT). The FCRM ensures access of all vulnerable groups including women, men, PWD, and elderly people to submit their feedback with safety and privacy. Feedback may be received either online, verbally or through the hotline and feedback boxes

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential protection needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available. Technical assessment will be conducted in a way that takes into consideration the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also keeping the protection of data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on and enhances the roles each member of the household fulfills and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.The implementation of the activities will be associated with risk analysis that identifies risk, risk level, likelihood and mitigation measures that will be taken into consideration in advance. All activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and in full engagement of the communities targeted.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

At the community level, local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the intervention, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any bias in selecting, targeting and service distribution. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through conducting awareness sessions to inform targeted communities about services that can be accessed as well as on legal measures and access channels. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing on the participation of women in the project’s committee as well as further exploring women and people with disabilities needs.
