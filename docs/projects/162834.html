1 - Needs

The education sector estimates that 1.47 million schoolagedchildren (4-16 years) need assistance to access and/or continue their education, mainly in Darfur, South Kordofanand Blue Nile. Approximately 61 per cent of pre-schoolchildren (4-5 years) in conflict-affected states are not inschool because of limited space, teachers and materials.This has short and long-term consequences for the children’sintellectual, social, emotional, language and even physicaldevelopment. Among the 14-16 year age group, 32 per centof children have access to secondary schools. Reasons forthis gap include the long distances required to reach schoolsas there are no boarding facilities, limited household incomeand few secondary schools - there are 19,627 basic schoolsacross the country, and 4,267 secondary schools, includinggovernment and private ones. (HNO-2019)

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is going to address the environmental issues by considering the local environmental context of the targeted area, and a conduct continuous assessment for potential environmental impacts will be carried throughout the project life. The project is also going to do mitigation measures by compensating for negative impacts (if any), and enhance environmental benefits in the project by practicing some measurements, the followings are part of it: • Replace the use of fired bricks when constructing schools with Soil-Stabilized-Blocks (SSB) or Cement-Stabilized-Blocks (CSB).• Locally available material is recommended provided that it is not impacting negatively on the environment.• Include environmental awareness sessions in school, and support solid waste management schemes • Reduce packaging material and discourage use of plastic bags to reduce the amount of waste generated. • Reduce wood consumption in school construction by supplying iron pipes and using environmentally friendly material for constructions and rehabilitation; and promoting alternatives, Consider also alternative energy sources such as solar panels for lighting and pumps.• Enhance tree planting

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As mainstreaming issue, the project will ensure that each activity will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community and targeted schools consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community and schools targeted feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults.Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable and disabled children within the target schools in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. The safety of children, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put children, WV staffs or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While project will prioritize female and child-headed families access education and participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion.. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including teachers, PTAs, women and children.To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staffs will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiary children.
