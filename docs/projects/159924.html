Project Summary

Relief International (RI) will increase access to and utilisation of primary health care services by pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWG) and children under the age of 5 years in Banadir, Hiraan, and Gedo regions of Somalia. RI aims to improve health seeking behaviour, including attitudes towards skilled delivery and hygiene in the target communities.
Delayed, and poor performing Gu rains across Somalia have led to widespread crop failure and reduced livestock productivity. The 2019 Gu harvest in southern Somalia is the lowest output since 1995. Repeated climatic shocks, including more frequent and prolonged dry conditions and episodic flood events, undermine food security, compounded by widespread insecurity, displacement, and poor access to basic services including nutrition, water, and sanitation increase morbidity. Humanitarian access remains a challenge across southern Somalia where main roads connecting Bulo Burte to Jalalqsi remain blockaded and attacks on government buildings in Ghabaharey and Belet Hawa heighten fear of al-Shabaab attacks impede vulnerable populations’ access to lifesaving health and other humanitarian assistance. As localized insecurity and access deteriorated in parts of Banadir, Gedo, and Hiran, prolonged harsh dry conditions imperilled food security and nutrition outcomes, and access to water and sanitation at household level decreased precipitating a marked uptick in the number of consultations at health facilities in the target communities as compared to the same period in 2018. To date, RI have conducted 125,460 primary health consultations; Over 7000 for acute diarrheal diseases (ADD) and 16,364 acute respiratory infections (ARI). Despite a promising start to the Deyr season (October – December), three successive failed harvests in Hiraan and other parts of southern Somalia has undermined household resilience and will require the urgent scale-up of the humanitarian assistance to avert a further deterioration.
Aligned to the health cluster strategic objectives, RI will address the health needs of displaced and rural PLWG and children under the age of 5 in need of immediate and sustained health interventions. RI currently supports 12 static health centres in the target regions that provide a comprehensive package of health and nutrition services. Women and children comprise the majority of patients seen at the health facilities. Data collected from RI’s health centers reveal that in many cases, women and children in need of health and nutrition services travel long distances. In Hiraan, RI found, in July 2019, that 30% of patients travelled more than 10km to reach health facilities. These journeys invariably expose these vulnerable groups to protection risks, as they traverse through unfamiliar and insecure environments. Anchored to static health facilities, RI will deploy mobile health and nutrition teams to serve underserved host and internally displaced communities outside the main urban hubs. These mobile teams will increase access to lifesaving primary health services, including ante- and post-natal care, and integrated management of childhood diseases for those unable to access the static health facilities in Gedo, Hiraan and Banadir. These health interventions will help decrease morbidity rates and overall mortality by simultaneously addressing poor nutrition and WASH status among the target populations.
The health service provision at the supported static facilities with the mobile teams will reach 100,440 PLWG and children, predominantly from IDP and underserved rural and remote households.

Project Activities

RI will implement the following activities in alignment with the health cluster strategic objectives:- Provide primary health care services at static and mobile facilities, including consultations and referrals to vulnerable people in need (men, women, children, host communities, IDPs, returnees and refugees) [target: # of total consultations: 178,000]- Improve access and reproductive and child health services including ANC, PNC, skilled delivery services and child wellness visit, and EPI [target: 120,000 under 5s vaccinated];- Procure and provide essential drugs for the static health facilities and mobile clinics;- Train health facility staff to ensure competency and quality care [target: 120 staff trained];- Conduct community sensitization and awareness on key health messages [target: 100,000 sensitized];- Support measles and polio campaigns with Somali MOH, WHO and health partners [target: 10,000 immunized]- Train health workers on EPI planning, forecasting and tools for immunization Improve early warning and surveillance systems [target: 12 health workers trained]- Train health workers on sentinel reporting and response [target: 12 health workers trained];- Train health workers on AWD identification and response as part of rapid response team Community Engagement [target: 20 trained] - Conduct health promotion and disease prevention awareness campaigns [target: 12 campaigns; 4 per region]- Train community health committees to support management of health facilities and serve as a conduit to better meet community health needs [target: 6 health committees trained];- Train community health workers on integrated community case management of malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea [target: 24 CHWs trained];- Link traditional birth attendants to qualified midwives at the health facility and train TBAs on danger signs of pregnancy Systems Strengthening [target: 12 TBAs linked];- Establish referral pathway from community to PHU, health facility, referral health center, to tertiary health center [target: 12 pathways established]
