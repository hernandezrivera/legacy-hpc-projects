Project Summary

To enable extremely vulnerable households to meet their basic needs based on their own priorities and to mitigate or reduce reliance on harmful coping strategies, DRC will provide unrestricted and unconditional MPCA to extremely vulnerable households in Aleppo, Daraa, Rural Damascus, and Deir-ez-Zor. DRC will leverage different channels to identify potential MPCA recipient households and adopt a multi-step verification process to ensure independent and needs-based targeting. To mitigate risks of exclusion, DRC will collect self-referrals through community outreach and its CFM hotline, while informing the CWG of the planned response and geographical coverage to facilitate inter-agency referrals. With a strengthened focus on integrated response to enhance the effectiveness and impact of sectoral interventions, DRC will prioritize internal referrals of households targeted under other Sectors in close coordination with Shelter, WASH, Protection, and Economic Recovery teams. At the beneficiary registration stage, DRC will carry out a gender-age and protection risk analysis to identify potential risks associated with a cash modality, including intra-household and community power dynamics and safety and accessibility considerations. On a monthly basis, DRC will also conduct joint market monitoring across the target locations to assess market functionality, ensure programming is not causing market distortions due to the sudden influx of cash, and to verify that both the TV and continued use of cash as a response modality remains appropriate.
DRC will utilize the currently agreed MPCA TV with a recommended buffer of 20%, which amounts to a monthly TV to 1.716.000 SYP. Each household will receive MPCA for a period of 6 months, and DRC will also provide transportation costs (50.000 SYP) for each cash cycle to cover beneficiaries’ expenses of travelling to and from the distribution points to remove barriers in accessing assistance and to ensure the MPCA package can be exclusively used to cover basic needs. Given the severity and scale of needs among target households, MPCA will be provided for a period of 6-months via successive distributions, in line with monthly consumption patterns and expenditure cycles, to ensure a minimum period of time to strengthen and stabilize household consumption. However, the number of instalments will remain flexible and adaptable to evolving needs and circumstances of beneficiaries and the broader context, such as new shocks, severe inflation, or displacement. In accordance with sanctions, DRC utilizes a pre-contracted local financial service provider (FSP) for the delivery of cash to beneficiaries, with three-way counting of the cash by DRC, the FSP, and the beneficiary. DRC also has experience negotiating for the opening of temporary branches in close proximity to beneficiaries’ residence (within walking distance) as needed. Continuous, real-time risk assessment and mitigation measures will underpin all distributions, especially mindful of the heightened vulnerability of individuals selected for MPCA. If the protection risk analysis alerts DRC to encashment-related risks, prior to confirming the distribution plan, DRC will verify with beneficiaries that they are able to safely access the distribution point. To the greatest extent possible, MPCA will be sequenced with complementary livelihoods programming that moves towards the longer-term objectives of resilience and self-reliance, and thus provides exit opportunities for humanitarian assistance.
Note: This project is anticipated to be covered through multi-donor funding, rather than a single, standalone project. (see more information on mitigation and criteria in Extended Summary).

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Economic deterioration continues to be among the primary drivers of the crisis, propelling needs severity and new displacement. As of last November, the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) indicated a two-fold increase in the cost of living within the year 2023 alone, and a four-fold rise over a two-year period. While the average income in Syria increased in 2023, the soaring cost of living often outpaced any gains (WFP December 2023 Market Price Watch Bulletin). This discrepancy continues to place significant strain on households, making it increasingly difficult to meet their basic needs. The severe reduction of WFP Food Assistance has already had direct consequences on the food security of the most vulnerable, resulting in a concerning trend of deterioration in household consumption, with IDPs experiencing food insecurity at the highest rate (draft HNO 2024). Rising inflation has also been accompanied by rampant currency depreciation. By December, the Syrian pound depreciated by 81% in three years, with an alarming 94% loss in value over four years (WFP December 2023). The widening income-expenditure gap combined with reduced purchasing power magnifies the financial hardships faced by households, pushing more people to adopt negative coping mechanisms. Risk Mitigation: All costs associated with MPCA response will be carefully budgeted and will be reviewed on a regular basis. Based on the CWG guidelines, the MPCA TV will incorporate a buffer in anticipation of inflation/depreciation. DRC will routinely monitor markets and exchange rates, with PDMs conducted after each MPCA cash cycle further contributing to MEB monitoring. Should market monitoring and PDM data raise concerns on the appropriateness of the MEB, DRC will escalate findings to the CWG and may adjust the TV or frequency of instalments if/as needed. Selection: DRC will use a vulnerability assessment tool, enabling DRC to apply a scoring system based on pre-defined selection criteria. The system is designed to identify and weigh a range of socio-economic vulnerabilities and protection risks, including household demographics, dependency ratio, physical or mental conditions, income and expense gaps, food consumption, and negative coping strategies, while placing priority on those whose position in society puts them at a disadvantage and/or exacerbates vulnerabilities. Households will be classified under 3 categories (not vulnerable, moderately vulnerable, extremely vulnerable), with only those categorized as extremely vulnerable to be prioritized for MPCA with the aim of preventing them from slipping into a situation of irreversible harm. The analysis of information will identify eligible households to receive assistance, with DRC’s MEAL team conducting a final verification exercise in which 20% of households will be selected randomly for spot-checks to confirm that the selection process was conducted accurately and fairly and to mitigate inclusion/exclusion errors.
