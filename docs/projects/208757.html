Project Summary

Novel Alliance for Development Aid (NADA) intend to reach a total of 2,221 individuals (40%women, 30%Girls, 20%men and 10%Boys) including those living with disabilities in camps, and host communities. The project will support advocacy visits to key stakeholders (40% women, 60% men) to community and government stakeholders on promoting HLP rights for women and girls and GBV risk mitigation and response. Also, the capacity of staff, Community groups, and relevant Government agencies (Ministry of Environment and Urban Planning, SEMA, NEMA, BOGIS, YOGIS, Ministry of Women Affairs, FIDA, and other relevant institutions will be built to support effective project implementation. 20,000 (40%women, 30% girls, 20% men, and 10% boys) will be reached with awareness and sensitization on cultural barriers affecting HLP rights against women and girls and GBV risk mitigation. Community-based project committees will be constituted (50% women and 50% men) and trained on GBV/PSEAH risk mitigation, HLP rights, and mediation to support community engagement and a more efficient project implementation.
Through the project, Advocacy visits will be paid to the Ministry of Women Affairs, SEMA, BOGIS, YOGIS, MLG, Ministry of Housing and Urban Development (MHUD), and religious leaders on alternative care arrangements and shelter support to women and girls in need including facilitating access to Land as a result of displacement either by insurgency or farmers headers crises. 60 (40%women, 40%men) individuals from Borno Geo-Information System (BOGIS), Yobe State geo-information system (YOGIS), Ministry of Women Affairs (MWA), SEMA, and community leaders will be trained quarterly on HLP rights and mitigation strategies, community dispute resolution (CDR), and alternative dispute resolution (ADR)
Moreover, NADA will through the community leaders establish/strengthen community-based dialogue and mediation committees to support mediation and dialogue that will foster conflict resolution for sustainable peace and development. Monthly Radio program including the daily airing of jingles in key advocacy messages on HLP rights and GBV/PSEA risk mitigation, key stakeholders will serve as guests during radio talk shows to reach out to the larger population in almost all the LGAs in Borno and Yobe States. Women and girls will be empowered with livelihood skills, 200 women and girls including 70 survivors of SGBV and other GBV-related issues identified and documented through the case management processes with a socio-economic start-up kit for resilience building and reintegration

1 - Needs

According to HRP 2023, out of the 8.3 million people in need and 6 million people targeted, Yobe constituted 2.9 million in need and 1.6 million people targeted to be reached with protection. The assessment, Rapid Overview of Areas of Return (ROAR) carried out in Yobe state with focus on Yunusari LGA by REACH in June 2022 to better understand the need, current displacement patterns and returns in the location; the assessment reveals that there are multiple displacements including re-displacement after attempts to return – consideration about work and the presence of income sources and reclaiming belonging and assets like farmland or livestock left behind in areas of origin are the main drivers of returns. This assessment can be applied to other LGAs with similar conflict situations such as Gujba LGA.The ROAR assessment shows that 30% to 65% of the residents had their property looted or destroyed when they were displaced. In addition to stolen properties, it was reported that a high number (40%) of the people lost their legal documents during the crisis; although some have applied for affidavits, many people still do not have their legal documents. After all, the existence of formal mechanisms such as courts are not fully functional because most of the staffs are not based within the location, as such it takes a longer period to have solutions to any legal matters.The reported increase in violence in northeast Nigeria led the European Union-funded Programme on Managing Conflict in Nigeria (MCN) to conduct a rapid assessment. The result from the research conducted by Amina Salihu and Hadiza Mali Bukar reveals that data from Sexual Assault Referral Centres (SARC) shows that females (especially those below the age of 18 years) experience SGBV more. study into the prevalence of SGBV in Yobe state to understand the causes, types, and trends of sexual and gender-based violence in some communities of Yobe State. The Research titled Housing, land and Property Rights Facing Returnees in Northeast Nigeria conducted by the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) in 2019 reveals women had a very limited ability to independently enjoy property rights in Northeast Nigeria. These restrictions persist in the conflict period, and there is evidence that the conflict may have made the situation worse for some women especially where there is no documentation for HLP rights. This issue is compounded by the absence of key stakeholders, including traditional authorities, who could provide access to rights under the informal systems.Finally, Novel Alliance for Development Aid (NADA) carried out the identification and profiling of project participants in 2023 in 10 different LGAs of Yobe state during which the organization learned that the commodification of women as properties owned by fathers and subsequently transferred to husbands causes violence against women. Women and young girls experience domestic violence, including psychological abuse and denial of the right to housing, land and properties (HLP), most women, enjoyment of housing, and property rights depending on their family relationships.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NADA will ensure that the proposed intervention ensures the safety and dignity of target populations, as well as avoids causing harm. NADA have already mapped out protection risks in across the different locations and will continue to do so during the project period. The protection risks already identified include restriction of movement of IDPs in camps, sexual harassment and violence, exploitation, discrimination of returning refugees in Yobe, abduction of women and girls when fetching firewood, perceptions of young women by their own communities and others, harassment as a result of perception of former affiliation with AOG in the family and gender based. Hence, woman participation but also youth, elderly of persons with disabilities’ engagement will be highly encouraged and supported. NADA will thereafter work in close coordination with these community representatives to identify needs and conduct referrals to appropriate services. Training of project staff and community workers to increase their capacity to recognize and deal with sensitive protection issues.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

This project is designed in consultation with the community stakeholders and based on lessons learnt from our previous implementation in the target LGAs. NADA shall use a people-centred feedback mechanism to gather opinions, and complaints of the community members and beneficiaries on its project to inform decision-making for quality project implementation. However, lessons learned from the approach will be used to form a LGA level project committee that will include all community groups' representative and stakeholders to improve the participation of the affected community in the project implementation process. NADA project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the already existed community-based project structures to discuss the project deliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. NADA will also use a feedback mechanism through focus group discussions to gather perceptions of the project. beneficiaries and community members on the services rendered which will form part of the discussion in the project implementation review meeting with the community-based project committees at the end of the project. NADA will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and have trained PSEA focal person who is solely responsible to receive and respond to any SEA-reported cases. NADA will sensitize the LGA level project committee on PSEA
