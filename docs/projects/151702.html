Needs

Despite the fact that the conflict, to a certain extent, has calmed down, the WASH situation continues to worsen in eastern Ukraine. According to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) published by OCHA, some 3.2 million people are in need of WASH-related assistance in eastern Ukraine – more than half of which are currently located on non-government controlled territory. Fifty-six percent of households reported being dissatisfied with the quality of their drinking water most commonly reported as being due to the chemical composition (72%). Households within 5km of the LoC reported the most daily water shortages (16%). The situation for major water infrastructure remains critical: in an area where 4.6 million people rely on centralized water systems, there were 105 WASH incidents when shelling, shooting or other issues directly affected water infrastructure in the 12 months up to and including August 2018. Water and sanitation systems are more than 70 years old, and have not been repaired adequately since fighting started. Due to the bad state and poor upkeep of WASH infrastructure, the infrastructure itself is not fit to cope with frequent water and electricity cuts and breaks down easily leaving frontline communities without the access to water, sanitation, and heating. PIN seeks to close the gap left by state services which are unable to keep up with frequent water cuts caused by shelling and electricity cuts, and due to antiquated systems. There are around 3500 schools, kindergartens and vocational training colleges in the two conflict affected oblasts, of which 45% reported a need for improvements to substandard latrines, or inadequate water supply (Education cluster 2018). According to PIN estimations, the gender breakdown stands at 54/46 females/males, with this ratio being even higher (65/35) in the remote frontline villages and social institutions supporting the elderly, such as hospitals and hospices. Most critically affected are the elderly and single women living in remote frontline villages. For many elderly, access to hygiene items and water from wells or other alternative source is arduous, particularly for those with decreasing eyesight and/or mobility during the winter season. Many people in the Donetsk and Lugansk Oblast have lost their livelihoods due to the conflict. Paying for water has become an issue for normal people: according to HNO 2018, 55% of households in GCAs and 45% in NGCAs have reduced their expenditure on food and medicines in order to pay increasing utility costs. Overall utilities costs represent 20% of household income in GCA areas and 15% in NGCA. The reduced household budgets and the high price for the basic hygiene items forces families to economize. Therefore, a reduction in hygienic washing practices has been observed throughout the region. Girls and women of reproductive age have specific hygiene needs that in the current situation cannot be adequately met. When these vulnerable groups are located in remote areas without an easy access to a water source, a reduction in hygienic washing practices is anticipated. In 2018 a market study by the ACCESS consortium (PIN is a part of ACCESS), NRC and Save the Children found that some hygiene items were not available in all locations in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, the least available items being adult and infant diapers, only available at 53% and 29% of surveyed locations respectively. Meanwhile the cost of hygiene items increased on average 10% from November 2017 to February 2018. Hygiene items prices in Donetsk city are around 25% higher than the Ukrainian average.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Improved access to sufficient quantity of water • PIN will support the local administrations and utility companies on centralized, decentralized and private hot and cold water supply in public infrastructure and social institutions.• In case of necessity and feasibility, in locations where re-establishment of the existing water supply system is not possible and where water trucking is a constant need due to the damages of the main water supply lines, PIN will organize the development of a new water source, such as a new borehole or pipeline connection.Outcome 2: Improved access to sufficient quality of water• PIN will provide drinking and technical water through water trucking. PIN will strive to provide people residing in the areas of PIN water trucking interventions with the amount of water which is the recommended for the total basic WASH needs according to the Sphere standards. • In the areas with no other possibility, PIN will procure and distribute bottled water on a very short term basis to people who have lost access to water due to the conflict or have difficulties accessing adequate amounts of drinking water.• PIN will also install smaller water tanks in social or in private HH to facilitate the water trucking activities. These tanks will be also used for contingency water storage. Outcome 3: Improved access to adequate sanitation • PIN will support the local administrations and utility companies on centralized, decentralized and private waste water systems in public infrastructure and social institutions.• PIN will support waste management activities in locations where solid waste has over-accumulated and is presenting a serious threat to public health • PIN's sanitation intervention in institutions will focus on improving the sanitary conditions in social institutions hosting/servicing vulnerable populations. • Besides, PIN is going to focus on the rehabilitation of heating systems that can include replacing radiators and pipes as well as replacing heating boilers, pumps tanks. Outcome 4: provide critical WASH related supplies and/or awareness• PIN will organize the distribution of hygiene kits that take into account the gender and age specific needs of the most vulnerable (target population: sick and disabled, single mothers/fathers with children, etc.) – material donation, voucher and cash assistance. A certain quantity of hygiene kits and disinfectants will be kept as contingency stock.• Hygiene promotion targeted at the most vulnerable (children, elderly, pregnant & lactating women) on the topics of appropriate hygiene practices (personal hygiene, waste management, water consumption) to prevent water and sanitation related diseases will be conducted by PIN.• PIN will provide hygiene vouchers as one of the most efficient and appropriate modalities to provide life preserving subsistence assistance. PIN will collect information on age, gender and number of people with specific needs in project targeted communities. PIN will develop a plan together with community members in order to determine the most appropriate way to reach to people with specific needs, like people with limited mobility, elderly people, etc.; identify possibilities of joint actions with relevant humanitarian agencies like Help Age or Handicap International in such cases. Activities in 2019 form part of a vision to continue providing WASH support to the affected area until the end of 2020
