Project Summary

The intervention ensures 31,740 beneficiaries have safe access to basic WASH services. The project intends to provide quality, gender-responsive services including safe water and sanitation facilities. The contribution cluster sectoral objectives will be achieved through the provision of safe water for drinking with climate-resilient structures, conducting water quality analysis, provision of climate-resilient sanitary facilities in emergencies, conducting hygiene/sanitation promotion campaigns, distribution of WASH NFI’s and conducting gender risk analysis (safety audits) to mitigate WASH related GBV. This will contribute to sectoral objective 4, minimizing exposure of vulnerable groups to protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to safe, tailored, timely, and dignified access to appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action. The intervention contributes to WASH Cluster sectoral objective 3, which reduces community members’ exposure to environmental hazards through the construction of climate-resistant WASH infrastructure. This will build resilience and provide sustainable WASH services.
According to the HNO, 2023, an estimated 6.1 million people will need WASH services with only 3% under minimum, 4 % under stress, 56% under severe, 36% under extreme, and 1% under catastrophe. Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South Counties are classified under severe with 100,000 to 200,000 people in need of WASH services each County. Pibor County is categorized under extreme with over 200,000 people, Pariang under Severe with 50,000 to 100,000 people and Aweil West is categorized under minimum stress with 25,000 to 50,000 people in need of WASH services respectively. The vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by high risks from a broad range of hazards: floods, water scarcity, extreme heat, wildfires, a medium earthquake risk, disease outbreaks, and an influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan. These have disrupted socio-economic systems and livelihoods. Recurring multi-faceted socio-economic impacts on people, including severe economic contractions, soaring in prices of basic commodities, and loss of livelihoods particularly in urban areas have increased protection risks and disrupted access to basic services.
The project targets the host communities, IDPs, and Returnees. The total beneficiary figure is 31,740 people. They are disaggregated as follows 6,253 men, 6,348 women, 9,522 boys, and 9,617 girls. The beneficiary figures are inclusive of the persons with disabilities in the communities.

1. Needs

People in South Sudan are still suffering from the effects of the national/sub-national conflicts. The vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by high risks from a broad range of hazards: floods, water scarcity, extreme heat, wildfires, a medium earthquake risk, disease outbreaks and influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan. These have disrupted socio-economic systems and livelihoods. (UNICEF, South Sudan Country Office Humanitarian Situation Report No. 170, Reporting period: 1 January – 30 June 2022, available at https://www.unicef.org/media/124886/file/SouthSudan-Humanitarian-SitRep-June-2022.pdf). Since May 2023, a total of 830,000 returnees and 80,000 refugees have been registered in South Sudan. WFP/RRC Office registered 16,310 returnees from 4,979 households have been registered in NBEG. All these returnees from Sudan in NBEG are integrated into the host communities, exerting pressure on the few existing WASH facilities.Limited access to clean water and sanitation services contributes to the high prevalence of malnutrition, water-borne diseases, poor menstrual and hygiene management, risk of GBV at water points, absenteeism from school, and school dropout. Access to improved sanitation is below 10% in 56 counties with the most extreme percentages found in Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei where open defecation is practiced as a norm (FSNMS Round 28).42% of the population do not have access to an improved water source, 45% walk for more than 30 minutes to water point and 16% HHs have at least 1 jerrycan/bucket for storing water (2022 ISNA Household survey). This is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities, as well as for women and girls who are increasingly exposed to GBV. 20% of people using water facilities reported safety concerns, including violence and discrimination (HNO, 2023). This intervention will provide safe drinking water, promote practice of good hygiene and sanitation, distribute WASH NFI’s contributing to reduction of or exposure to risk of GBV, waterborne diseases like diarrhoea, cholera and Hepatitis E and vector-borne diseases such as malaria, lymphatic filariasis, and schistosomiasis due to flood water. It will help reduce malnutrition cases as water is a factor of food. The climate resilient WASH facilities will ensure that WASH services are accessed throughout the seasons.

2. Project activities

GENDER ANALYSIS IN THE 4 SELECTED COUNTIES OF PROJECT IMPLEMENTATION: This will help to analyze how WASH crisis affects beneficiaries differently (women, girls, boys and men) for appropriate action. This will help in identification of barriers to women participation in WASH and ways of closing the gap. WASH Cluster check list for AAP will be used. SAFETY AUDITS IN EACH OF WASH FACILITIES: Analysis of physical safety of and access to each WASH facility will be conducted to identify associated risks of WASH-related GBV, analysis of community norms and practices related to WASH that may increase the risk of GBV, assess the level of participation and leadership of women, adolescent girls and other at-risk groups in WASH, assess awareness of WASH staff on basic issues related to GBV whether they can provide basic support/directions to GBV survivors, review existing/proposed community outreach material related to WASH to ensure it includes basic information about GBV risk reduction. This will identify gaps in service delivery, gaps in security, access, distance covered, privacy, lighting, segregation, help desk, special needs, and first aid services. The analysis checks for inclusion of Gender-related issues in WASH Programming. REHABILITATION OF BOREHOLES (68 BOREHOLES): 17 boreholes per County. The platforms will be elevated to become climate/flood resilient. REHABILITATION OF MINI WATER YARDS (8 MINI WATER YARDS): 2 mini water yards per County. CONSTRUCTION OF NEW MINI WATER YARDS (4 MINI WATER YARDS): 1 new mini water per County. This will be done in Institutions like Schools, Health centers, Nutrition Centers, and densely populated areas like market centers. WATER QUALITY ANALYSIS FOR ALL WATER POINTS (80): Physico-chemical, chemical, and microbiological parameters will be analyzed and compared to the South Sudan Permissible limits in drinking water.ESTABLISHMENT AND TRAINING OF WATER MANAGEMENT COMMITTEES (80). 640 participants will be trained in operation and maintenance. 8 members per committee.CONSTRUCTION OF TEMPORARY CLIMATE RESILIENT COMMUNITY LATRINE BLOCKS (40): Sex disaggregated 10 blocks of 2 stances each block in each county for IDPs, returnees and host communities in the targeted areas.RENOVATE LATRINE BLOCKS (24): 8 latrine blocks per targeted County. The targeted latrine blocks shall be in Institutions: Schools, health centers/facilities.INSTALL HAND WASHING FACILITIES (64): They will be installed at the renovated and temporary latrine blocks constructed.CONSTRUCT SEX DISAGGREGATED TEMPORARY BATHING SHELTER BLOCKS (40): 2 stances of each bathing shelter for the IDPs, and returnees from Sudan who are integrated with host communities.ESTABLISH AND TRAIN HYGIENE AND SANTATION COMMUNITY MECHANISM (80): Each targeted community will have 10 members trained and mandated to promote hygiene and sanitation. 800 members will be trained as hygiene and sanitation members. CONDUCT AWARENESS IN GOOD HYGIENE AND SANITATION PRACTICE: This will be conducted through awareness sessions done through messages shared on megaphone announcements, house-to-house visitations, and face-to-face interactions. CONDUCT MONTHLY RADIO TALK SHOWS: Radio talkshows will be conducted in all 4 targeted Counties, engaging listeners on good hygiene and sanitation practices, sustainability of services, and GBV mitigation.DISTRIBUTION OF SOAP: Distribution of soap to 10,000 households, in the 120 targeted communities in Aweil East, Aweil South, Aweil North of NBEG, and Pibor of GPAA. 400mgs/person/month. DISTRIBUTION OF COLLAPSIBLE JERRYCANS: Each of the 10,000 households will receive 1 jerrycan.DISTRIBUTION OF PUR AND WATER GUARD: These will be distributed to 10,000 households in response to an emergency.SUPPORT THE EXISTENCE AND FUNCTIONING OF CLUSTER COORDINATION MECHANISM: This helps in the coordination of water, sanitation, and hygiene in humanitarian situations in the targeted locations.
