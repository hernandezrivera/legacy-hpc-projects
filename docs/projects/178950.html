Project Summary

Providing Shelter and NFI emergency assistance for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community families in conﬂict-affected areas of North East Syria, reaching a total of 44,400 conflict-affected individuals. Through the provision of winterization and NFI assistance to IDPs in informal camps and settlements as well as host community. The project will also deliver private shelter rehabilitation support to vulnerable families
NFI, Shelter and Winterization support will comprise three related but distinct components. First, supporting IDPs living in camps, informal settlements and collective centers to survive the harsh winter months with the provision of 3,500 kerosene or diesel heater kits with three months cash for fuel, supporting 21,000 individuals. Second, essential NFI in-kind kits and vouchers will also be distributed to the same population and to other new IDPs or returnees, targeting 21,000 individuals. Third, we will also undertake shelter activities to support recent returnees, particularly in Ar Raqqa, Tabqa and Manbij, living in unsafe and inadequate housing. This will be done through undertaking essential repairs and winterization of 400 partially-damaged or unfinished homes as well as ensuring a minimum level of privacy, dignity and access to WASH facilities for each household. This will benefit an estimated 2,400 individuals.

4 - Needs

The 2021 HNO reports that around 5.88 million people are in need of shelter assistance and 4.69 million in need of NFI support in Syria, with over 1.4 million located in NES in Hasekah, Ar Raqqa, Aleppo and Deir ez Zor governorates. The number of IDPs living in NES's ten formal camps is above 100,000 while many more IDPs are scattered in urban and rural areas – in informal settlements, collective centers and in urban centers – across NES. There also remains a growing housing crisis in some of the largest urban centers in NES, particularly in Ar Raqqa, Tabqa and Manbij. A significant surge in returns and IDPs to Ar Raqqa city over the past few years has led to a sharp increase in rental prices and a shortage of adequate shelter. 41% of communities in Raqqa find current rental prices unaffordable (REACH; January 2021). Many new returnees are forced to occupy partially and severely damaged buildings, raising serious health and protection concerns. Given the high levels of vulnerability of many of these returnees, most are unable to afford the cost of even the most basic shelter rehabilitation works. These shelters are often unsealed and exposed to the elements making them difficult to insulate, and raising serious safety, protection and health concerns. At the same time, as the economy continues to stagnate and prices rises, the majority of basic NFIs have become unaffordable to vulnerable IDP and host families.

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with the Whole of Syria sector objectives, this project comprises 3 core activities. First, for Sector Objective 1 (Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support), this project will provide (Sector Activity 1.1) core NFI kits to 3,500 IDP, returnee and vulnerable host community households in need (21,000 individuals). This kit will be provided either in-kind or through a voucher modality. The second activity also under Objective 1 (Sector Activity 1.2), comprises seasonal support in the form of winterization kits (heaters, jerry can, fire extinguisher) and cash for heating fuel for three months. This support will be provided to 3,500 vulnerable households (21,000 individuals). Kits will be distributed in November and cash for fuel starting December. The third activity, which falls under Sector Objective 2 (Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure) entails rehabilitation of partially damaged and unﬁnished buildings (Sector activity 2.1) in urban areas. 400 vulnerable households will be supported through shelter rehabilitation (2,400 individuals). In collaboration bilaterally, and through the NFI/Shelter working group, with other humanitarian actors working in shelter rehabilitation, will develop a shared beneﬁciary selection process, HLP due diligence, MoUs and other best practices. In terms of beneﬁciary selection within these districts, house-by-house assessments will be conducted and referrals accepted. Each shelter targeted will be rehabilitated to meet basic SPHERE shelter standards and will be in accordance with the Shelter sector guidance on rehabilitation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The SNFI cluster Protection Risk Analysis identifies over 50 key protection risks specific to the sector. Based on this, and the NGO's own internal protection analysis, identifies the key risks as follows: inter-community tension as a result of inclusion/exclusion, unclear selection criteria or varied assistance packages by different actors; increased tension between host and IDP communities; and risks particularly associated with distributions. To mitigate the first risk, the NGO adopts SNFI cluster guidance in its vulnerability assessments and its assistance packages to ensure harmonization with other actors. Similarly, the NGO hires local staff to carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks and good community acceptance. This ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness of the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. In terms of tension between host and IDP communities, the NGO conducts extensive consultations with both groups before commencing any activities. The NGO does not target on the basis of status, and thus supports IDP and host communities. In order to mitigate for protection risks for all beneﬁciaries during SNFI distributions, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneﬁciaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female ﬁeld workers. In camp settings, multiple distribution points are established to help keep the number of people at one point to a minimum and to reduce waiting times.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. • Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. • Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area • COVID-19 SOPs for general IPC in place as well as activity level SOPs.
