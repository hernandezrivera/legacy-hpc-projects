Project Summary

ZOA plans to implement food security, livelihood, emergency support and protection activities in Greater upper Nile and Greater Equatoria states. It aims to cover 8,300 HH in 7 counties in the above mentioned States. The counties are: Bor South, Fangak, Pibor, Lekwangole, Gumuruk, Verthet, Akobo in Greater Upper Nile are and Lainya, Juba in the Eastern Equatoria area and Terekeka . It will be implementing activities under the Food Security and livelihood cluster specific objectives 1 and 2 for the year 2019. The activities are mainly cash based transfers, Seed, tools, Vegetable seeds and fishing kits.

Needs

In South Sudan sixty percent of the population suffer from severe acute food insecurity during the peak of the lean season (June- July) when food shortages are typically at their worst level. These shortages are exacerbated due to the persistent conflict, disruption of livelihood, poor economic conditions and challenges faced in the delivery of humanitarian assistance. More than 6 million people are facing “Crisis” or” Emergency” levels of acute food insecurity (IPC phases 3 and 4). This represents a close to 20 percent increase compared to the same period (June-July) in 2017 . Areas affected by armed conflict during the planting season of April-June are of particular concern, especially where such livelihoods disruption has been recurrent for several years. It will be difficult for households in these areas to realize a harvest, and the possibility of extreme food insecurity will likely remain until at least January 2019.The majority of South Sudanese depend on agriculture-based livelihoods which cannot be maintained once they are displaced, which means that these communities become extremely vulnerable. Failure to keep agriculture production at the same levels is not only driven by conflict-related displacement but also general disruptions to livelihood markets, lack of access to social services and restricted trade due to roads made inaccessible by insecurity and the current rainy season. Immediate action to maintain and save livelihoods will have lasting impact, access to farming, fishing and livestock safeguarding inputs and services have the potential to significantly improve food security.The protracted conflict has forced 4.5 million South Sudanese from their homes as of mid-July 2018 , drastically reducing their capacity to sustain their livelihood. The number of IDPs increased from 189,000 in 2013 to 2 million in July 2018 (out these 202,043 are living in PoC areas ). Refugees in neighbouring countries increased in number from 387,000 in 2014 to 2.5 million in July 2018 and are expected to reach 3.1 million by end of December 2018 due to conflict related displacement and migration in search of food. The value of the South Sudan Pound (SSP) has fallen dramatically against the US dollar. Before the war, one US dollar was worth about five South Sudanese pounds but in September 2018, a dollar was worth about 230 SSP (it even peaked at SSP 320 to 1 US$). ( Food and Security update July 2018 published by FAO, UNICEF and WFP). The nutrition situation remains critical in many areas affected by serve food insecurity with approximately 1.1 million children under five years at risk of acute malnutrition. GAM rates were over 20 percent above WHO’s 15 percent emergency threshold during the March-May period . The conflict is destroying particularly the greater Equatoria and Upper Nile regions as well as westernBahr el-Ghazal and has resulted in a dire crisis, with significant displacement, serious and systematic reported human rights violations and abuses against children as well as adults, including the killing of civilians, arbitrary arrest, detention, torture and other inhumane and degrading treatment, conflict-related sexual violence including rape, and looting and destruction of civilian property. The parties to the conflict are not meeting their obligation to prevent these horrendous crimes and hold perpetrators accountable. It has been reported to the UN Security Council that attacks on civilians are deliberate, not collateral to the conduct of hostilities. ( South Sudan Protection Cluster update)

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

the project takes into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse issues. When the project is discussed in the community it is done in the presence of RRC representatives and local leader team which consists of the traditional Boma or Payam leader, Women, youth and Church leaders w ho are representatives of the people. The leadership team is involved from the planning to the completion of the project to avoid any kind of exploitation by any stakeholder involved in the project. ZOA implements project in partnership with African Development Aid (ADA). The areas that ADA is working are prone to numerous forms of abuse including recruitment into armed groups, gender based violence, neglect, early marriage, abduction, child labour, violence and exploitation among others. Many children are reported to be out of school due to conflict, they lack safe environment for play/socialization; learning; adequate parental care; and are particularly vulnerable to different forms of violence and psychological traumatic disorders, which can often have lifelong effects. They are in need of protection, care, education, urgent family tracing & reunification, and psycho social support services. ADA will be used to address the protection needs in the FSL projects.
