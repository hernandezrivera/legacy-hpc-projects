Project Summary

Hyperinflation, worsened by the Ukraine war, has drastically increased staple goods prices in Syria, outpacing wage growth and pushing 90% of the population below the poverty line. The health sector, largely dependent on outside funding, has seen numerous facility closures since late 2021. A cholera outbreak since September 2022 has reported over 71,463 cases and 23 deaths in NWS. Severe and acute malnutrition among children has risen catastrophically from 1% in 2021 to 1.7% in 2022. Acute maternal malnutrition affects 11% of mothers in NWS.
The need for free, quality healthcare is at an all-time high, especially for vulnerable groups like IDPs and PWDs. Primary needs include essential primary healthcare, emergency care, treatment for NCDs, pediatric consultations, dialysis sessions, medicines, and both general and specialized surgical services.
The dramatic increase in medication prices is due to the small pharmaceutical industry in NWS, which relies on expensive imports and medicines from GoS-controlled areas. This has led to a large increase in medicine prices, causing many patients to reduce treatment. This is particularly concerning for young children and the elderly suffering from NCD. The high price of antibiotics has become a significant barrier for most families. The availability of specialized medical items is even lower. Essential pregnancy-related medication and supplements are increasingly inaccessible for women and girls of reproductive age.
Also, supplements like iron, folate, and calcium, are given high anaemia (25% in children under 5) and malnutrition (11%). Stunting affects 25-28% of children under 5. Heart surgery patients face death without intervention, but at $5,000, most can’t afford it. Dialysis patients depend on free sessions and medicine.
Intervention
The project seeks to provide free-of-charge medicine to 23 health facilities including dialysis centers, cardiology centers, PHC centers, General hospitals, and mobile emergency units. Furthermore, the project aims to provide stipends and running costs to 4 MEUs, 3 PHC centers, 1 blood bank, 1 pediatric/neonatal ICU, 2 dialysis centers, 1 cardiology center, and 1 physiotherapy/rehabilitation/prosthetic center. Furthermore, the project will include the support of 10 referral vehicles, training of 150 health staff, rehabilitation of 8 health facilities, and provision of medical equipment to 10 health facilities.
Protection risks and other risks
In response to health facility violence, emergency plans are prepared for high-risk situations, including staff guidance, equipment relocation, and schedule adjustments. Communication with IR’s safety and security department is established for immediate updates on risks or emergencies. For Gender-Based Violence (GBV) during travel to health facilities, IR trains health workers on GBV case management, increasing personnel at target facilities. IR supports a referral system with designated staff and transport for GBV victims, enhancing access to specialized medical services. To protect from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, all IR employees sign a code of conduct outlining proper behavior and disorderly conduct measures. A whistleblowing policy is clarified to all personnel, ensuring serious and confidential handling of sexual exploitation or abuse complaints. Contact details for reporting such incidents are made available to IR partners and communities.
PSEA:A survey of potential beneficiaries is conducted and analyzed to understand and respond to the targeted community. This ensures participation and rights of all genders and ages, adhering to IRW’s Safeguarding policy, including PSEA. Staff and volunteers are educated on PSEA and safeguarding. 3- Project updates are communicated to local councils and advertised in targeted areas. With referral services, IR ensures the participation of vulnerable groups in the project. Outreach workers, trained in PSEA, inform community members about their rights toward IR.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project will assist the health sector of NWS through various activities all under the strategic objectives of the 2024 HRP namely SO1 and SO3. The assistance will include in-kind and financial support to the health facilities and health staff of NWS. Under SO1, the project will implement service support activities which are included in the HRP. The main activity under the project will be the support of health facilities in providing essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, and referral. Under this activity, stipends to nurses, physicians (of various specializations), pharmacists, and other health professionals will be provided along with running costs for the health facilities. PHC and specialized consultations will be supported as well as natural deliveries. Specialized surgeries such as open-heart surgeries, ENT, and GI surgeries will be provided free of charge at supported health facilities. Furthermore, a physiotherapy center will be supported which will provide physiotherapy sessions and prostheses for amputees. The project will also support health facilities providing antenatal care by ensuring the presence of supported gynecologists/obstetricians and midwives. the following are HeRAMS CODES for the supported health facilities with stipends and running costs to achieve these 2 activities: B0204011 ! Finally, the project will also provide training and rehabilitation support to 150 health staff, 15 community health workers, and facilities in NWS. Training activities will include topics such as; GBV basics, IPC, PM+, medical lab guidelines in waste management, anesthesia guidelines, and health facilities management. Furthermore, the project will increase access to quality health services by establishing functional health facilities. Under this activity, 8 health facilities will be rehabilitated.
