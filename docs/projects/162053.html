Project Summary

"The overall objective of the project is- ""Improved protection and well-being of at-risk children of Rohingya community hosted in Bangladesh"".
The project has 4 result-
1. Develop physical, psychosocial and cognitive aspects of 2760 Rohingya children (4 to 12 years) under 11 CFS.
2. Protection of Rohingya adolescents (440 children from 12+ to 16 years) under 11 CFS from exploitation/abuse/ neglect and life skills education
3. Capacitate and support Rohingya Community to create safe, and protected environment of their children.
4.Identified Rohingya children who are at risk receive individual PSS and Case Management services to meet their unique needs."

Needs

Boys and girls represent 55% of all refugees and the majority of whom need child protection (CP) services. As the protracted refugee crisis enters its third year in 2020, the emergence of new protection risks necessitates a coordinated and harmonized approach by all CP partners to ensure sustainability and effectiveness of child protection systems and services. Child labour remains a key concern. Findings from the August 2019 MSNA show that 3% of 5-17 year old children are reportedly working; with 51% of those working exposed to the worst forms of child labour. Data from the Child Protection Sub-Sector 5W exercise and CPIMS Plus indicate 8, 596 UASC were identified and are deemed to be susceptible to multiple child protection risks. Based on findings from the CP sector’s needs assessment for JRP 2020, it is evident that there is a pressing need to scale up specialized mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) for those most vulnerable and severely affected. Boys and girls, including adolescents, face a wide range of protection risks including violence, neglect, abuse, sexual violence, separation from caregivers, child marriage, child labour, trafficking and psychological distress. Children are also exposed to significant threats within their own families and communities and are therefore in need of critical lifesaving services and support. The gendered dimensions of child protection risks, with its related consequences, persist in the lives of adolescent girls and boys in the camps. Adolescent girls are particularly susceptible to child marriage whilst boys face heightened risk of child labor, exploitation and human trafficking. As documented by various studies on the Rohingya crisis, adolescent girls face difficult camp conditions that contribute to the ongoing risks of GBV including domestic violence and child marriage. Results from UNHCR’s 5th SPP and ISCG’s MSNA conducted in 2019 indicate that approximately 5-10% of Rohingya households engage in child marriage with two-thirds of these households considering it a normal practice. Therefore, there is a need for targeted gender-sensitive CP interventions for adolescent girls and boy that take into account the specific issues they face due to intersecting identities and vulnerabilities. For adolescent girls and boys living in the camps, there are specific risks that arise from the lack of access to education and self-reliance opportunities. Based on findings from ISCG’s MSNA conducted in August 2019, only 13% of boys and 2% of girls aged 15 – 18 attend learning centers. Idleness and helplessness that arise from this situation leave out-of-school children more vulnerable to further protection risks such as exploitative labour, drugs and criminal activities. There is very limited space for play and recreation in the camps. Furthermore, adolescent girls’ movements are restricted due to many factors and this results in them being confined to their sheds, further limiting their access to lifesaving care, support, services, and resources. It also denies them the opportunity to develop support networks that aid in their healing and recovery. Therefore, child-friendly spaces remain an important entry point for children and adolescents to access psychosocial support, child development, life skills and pre-vocational sessions to stimulate their learning, growth and emotional, physical and mental well-being in a protective environment.

Activities or outputs

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has been successfully running 11 Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) since April 2018 in Camp 4 (6), Camp 4 Extension (3) and Camp 20 Extension (2). The following are activities planned for 2020:1. Operation of 11 CFSs to provide safe, secure, stimulating, participatory and supportive environments for children and adolescents. CB is currently implementing the “Essence of Learning” curriculum in its CFS which fosters learning of children affected by crisis. CB will continue to offer different kinds of play and activities to stimulate qualities such as creativity and build skills of problem solving, critical thinking, communication and cooperation – following UNICEF’s Principles for Child Friendly Spaces in Emergencies.2. In 2020, CB will work with its community-based protection program based out of Camp 4 Extension by using its community centre as a safe entry point to pilot youth activities targeted at adolescent girls and boys. Specific sessions on life skills activities, positive masculinities for boys and healthy relationships as well as positive coping for both adolescent boys and girls will be conducted at the community centre as well as the CFSs to ensure wider coverage and sustainability. To ensure accountability to affected populations, CFS staff will conduct regular ‘listening’ sessions to promote a safe and secure feedback mechanisms for both adolescent girls and boys to share their needs and concerns.3. The CFS team consists of PSS workers, case workers and facilitators. They are trained on global guidelines and minimum standards related to CP, GBV, CCSS, Male Survivors, MHPSS, PFA and case management (except facilitators). In 2020, CB will continue to invest in the technical and institutional capacity to further strengthen its child protection programming as well coordination with other Caritas protection colleagues working on GBV and its community-based protection program.4. CB has a national policy for PSEA and Child Safeguarding. Complaints can be collected through the several entry points and are directly processed by the PSEA Focal Point. In 2020, CB will conduct regular trainings for its senior management, sector managers and field staff on PSEA reporting and response. This is in addition to the Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding trainings that will also be organised for all staff. 5. Inter-agency and inter-sectoral coordination: CB participates in various inter-agency and inter-sectoral coordination for a and mechanisms. In Camp 4 Extension, CB is both the Protection Focal Point and Child Protection Focal Point. At field-level, CB attends Child Protection coordination meetings and engage with the CPFP. It will follow Referral pathways established by the Sub Sector and liaise with relevant service providers of other sectors. At CXB, CB will continue participation in the Child Protection Sub-Sector. CB will continue reporting in the 5W and contributing to the SitReps.6. DRR intervention: In addition to prioritising a participatory method to DRR awareness raising, CB will conduct quarterly DRR trainings for its staff as well as community members to increase their understanding of preparedness activities, contingency planning exercises and measures to mitigate family separation and other risks to children in case of an emergency/natural disaster. These sessions will be conducted in collaboration with CB’s Shelter and WASH teams to ensure CFSs remain a safe and secure place for all.
