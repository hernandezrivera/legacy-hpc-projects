Project Summary

As the Syrian crisis entered its eighth year, the deteriorated humanitarian situation throughout Syria is becoming more and more difficult for civilians who are still bearing the burnt of a conflict marked by unparalleled suffering, destruction and disregard for human life, increased level of unemployment, lack of livelihoods or means of income in addition to other humanitarian needs and protection risks.
The main objective of this project is to support resilience of vulnerable farmers HHs and youth and to increase their access to livelihood through creating income generation activities to restore local economic recovery. The project will focus on enhancing the participatory approach within the local communities through engaging them in the process of investing in the available local resources. The project will allow different segments of the targeted community (local population, IDPs, returnees) including vulnerable groups such as male and females youth and women to discuss their concerns and participate in the process of community social and economic recovery and they will have better and equal access to services and means of income to support their resilience and alleviate suffering.
The project will start by rehabilitating an agricultural association office within the targeted area and activate its role as a social and economic local unit for future recovery. After rehabilitation, the door will be open to receive membership applications (at least 500 members (males + females) from local farmers who have access to not more than 1 hectare of arable land; lands’ ownership or lease contracts and those engaged with agriculture such as agronomists). After being members, those farmers will be able to benefit from the association's agricultural services and inputs according to their needs and interest.
The Association will work on providing its members with needed agricultural inputs and/or services according to their needs (Seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, tools and equipment, other related services like land-ploughing). Members who want to benefit from the agricultural services and inputs should be committed to the principles of the association which will take the process of monitoring the process of lands' cultivation, harvesting the products, using the provided services and inputs.
The Agriculture Association management elected committee (2 females+3 males) will also oversees the process of conducting agricultural awareness seminars and lectures for the local farmers.
The second part of the project is related to establishing an office within the agricultural association as a “village micro-credit office”. A micro-credit management committee will be elected (5 members / 2 female + 3 males) and then the membership door will be open (there will be at least 100 members mostly youth participated in the micro-credit association; 35% females + 65 % males / of them 30 % IDPs).
The participated members will be divided into small groups (5 to 10 members in each group) to receive vocational and capacity building training according to their interest (such as but not limited to Food processing and food production, dairy products manufacturing, economic feasibility of the "small projects, growing Mushrooms, house gardening, etc.). After training and being eligible, those trainees will receive a “group-grants”; 3 to 10 members in each group (providing them with needed equipment and tools; at cost of maximum 800$ for each member within the group) to start a small income generation project after signing contracts with the heading committee including title of the funded project, duration, total cost,economic feasibility. These Contracts will be under the group leaders’ and local council’s guarantee and the heading committee will be responsible for the whole process of monitoring and supervision to the approved projects.

Needs

Now in its eighth year , the humanitarian crisis in Syria continues and thus the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming and remain high. Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), indicates some 11.7 million people in Syria require humanitarian aid and assistance, of them, 5 million considered in acute need in addition to the increased number of IDPs to reach 6.8 million people. Although number of people in need has slightly decreased compared to the previous year, still there are an estimated 6.5 million Syrians considered as food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity according to (FAS HRP 2019) bringing the total up to 9 million Syrians who need of urgent life-saving and life-sustaining food and livelihoods assistance.Vulnerable populations across Syria such as people with disabilities, single headed households, females headed HHs and the elderly often face particular challenges physically accessing food, for reasons ranging from a lack of mobility to threats to their physical safety. In many communities, an influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees contribute to socio-economic pressures that also create vulnerabilities for host communities. Food Needs in Syria are driven by many crisis related factors such as lack of financial access, as high food prices, low purchasing power, and lack of means of income or livelihood opportunities which badly affected the economic and living situation of people and led to food insecurity and this has caused heavy reliance on negative coping strategies, a lack of dietary diversity, and significant gaps in food consumption. Moreover, The Agricultural sector, which is also considered as a main source of income for large proportion of people inside Syria is also threatened by factors such as land contamination, depletion of productive assets and savings, increasing debt; and limited economic opportunities and lack of availability of and/or financial means to access essential inputs, and damaged infrastructure with lack of agricultural support offices or units. This has contributed to high levels of poverty across Syria, with an estimated 64-to-84 per cent of the population is living below the poverty line (HRP2019).The targeted area in this project is (Jandairis sub-district) in Afrin – Aleppo. The area is classified 4 to 5 in the severity scale with number of acute people in need reached 18501 in addition to the increased number of IDPs reaching 10820 according to HNO 2019. Sadad has conducted rapid market and agriculture related needs assessment within the targeted area considering different groups of population with focus on gender and age considerations. Findings of the assessment indicated different needs related to different groups of local community and IDPs. All assessed groups especially youth mentioned restrictions and lack of access to services and livelihood as a result of lack of funding sources and the absent of community-based units or committees. Both males and females farmers discussed problems related to lack of agricultural services which were provided to farmers before the crisis like consultation, agricultural inputs and services and the low income of farming households to ensure access to agriculture inputs. This has badly affected agriculture which is considered to be the main source of income for people within the targeted community. In addition, youth groups reported marginalization to their role and inability to enter the labour market as a result of the lack of vocational knowledge and skills in addition to their weak economic situation and lack of fund.Largest group of assessed people reported need to establish such a community-based offices or associations to help more in organizing and supporting their resilience to be able have access to basic services or start their own business and enter the labour market as for youth.

Implementation of activities

Sadad Organization and in cooperation with local council within the targeted area will start by community groups meeting and discussions to introduce different segments of population to the project's importance, activities, objectives, and procedures.At the beginning, the process of rehabilitation and preparing of the agricultural Association and its related vocational training center will be carried on; providing the needed equipment, furniture, tools and supplies.After rehabilitation; there will be a call for membership and start receiving members’ beneficiaries requests for the agriculture association and the vocational training center. Then, the heading committees of both agriculture office and vocational training office will take responsibilities of selecting and registering 500 males and females farmers beneficiaries for the agriculture association and 100 of youth beneficiaries for the vocational training center including the establishment of members database.After that, the process of providing farmers beneficiaries (500 members of the agriculture association) with required agriculture services and inputs will start according to each farmer’s need. Member farmers will fill, when needed, a paper request to include their needed agricultural services and inputs for the cultivation season; not exceeding 200$ for each beneficiary. After identifying the requested services and inputs by benefitting farmers, M&E officers in cooperation with the heading committee will evaluate and check the lands and ensure they are suitable for cultivation and assess the requesting member and then approve the request and move the request paper to the purchasing and logistic committee to start the purchasing and then providing the benefitting farmers with the requested services or inputs. (seeds, fertilizers, pesticide, irrigation tools, fuel, land-plowing service, other related services or inputs). The agriculture association, through its technicians and experts will also provide farmers with continuous consultation and agricultural seminars. At the same time, the vocational training center and its related micro-credit office will start the process of selecting 100 males and females youth beneficiaries and then providing them with vocational training after dividing them into groups according to their training interest (such as but not limited to Food processing and food production, dairy products manufacturing, economic feasibility of the "small projects, growing Mushrooms, house gardening, etc.) the training will be selected in consultation with beneficiaries and based on the labor market assessment.After receiving training and being eligible, Each group of trainees will submit an integrated small-business project. The heading committee will provide technical support and consultation for trainees’ groups when designing their projects and after submission, the heading committee will study the received projects and will decide which projects will receive the fund. The micro-grants will be group-grants; 800$ per member in the group and the group will include up to ten members from the same specialization (received the same kind of training). The micro-credit heading committee in addition to M&E officers will take responsibilities of monitoring the whole activities of starting the small business projects for the trainees’ members and will provide them with technical and needed support.

Protection Risk Analysis

Starting from Needs assessment, Sadad took into consideration the participation of different groups including females and males, youth, PSNs and others in the process of assessing the gaps and identifying the needs. 1. Protection risk 1: Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection . Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: there will be Community based beneficiary selection according to identified and clear selection criteria based on the needs. Also the activities will include different groups of people with no exclude to any group. Monitoring: there will be focus group discussions with beneficiaries and community feedback and complaints mechanisms and strong accountability mechanisms to ensure transparency with all targeted groups. 2: Protection Risk 2: 1. Inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigating Measures: There is inclusion of all groups from the design stage, the Local beneficiary selection committees will include equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities and criteria will be clear and shared with all beneficiaries in addition to Clear/precise guidance to the CPs on the beneficiary selection process and criteria. Monitoring: there will be enhanced accountability and complaints mechanisms committed to the beneficiaries; establishing community feedback and complaints mechanisms, focus group discussions, media monitoring and community based monitoring system including the heading committees within the association and the micro-credit office. 3: When Targeting for IDPs/Returnees for livelihood projects e.g. household level food production there may be Risk of Access to land/asset and Risk to secondary rights holders e.g. female headed households. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: Engage with local authorities to verify status Work alongside other complementary programs on HLP Review appropriateness of the activities visavi the dynamic pertaining land rights. Protection risk 4: risk of sexual exploitation, harassment by the heading committees members to the females women and/or youth benefitting from micro-credit or agriculture association grants. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 2.1: the working staff and the heading committees members will receive trainings on the humanitarian principles and enhancing protection. 2.2: all the working staff will be asked to read and sign and be committed to the code of conduct. 2.3: accountability measures will be enhanced and the feedback and complaints mechanisms will be easily accessed by all beneficiaries especially females beneficiaries. 2.4: Train staff on the Inter-Agency Coordination in Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms. 2.5: the two heading committees will include females members to enhance the process of ensuring protection and the participation of females.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Resources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension. Likelihood: high and this risk can be managed and mitigated. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: There will be a comprehensive needs assessment at the start of the project in cooperation with local councils and when selecting beneficiaries M&E team will check their accordance with the selection and vulnerability criteria and for each member, the heading committee will assess his/her need and M&E officers will ensure this process. 1.2; Sadad will focus on accountability and will develop a high-quality monitoring and feedback mechanisms in this project especially for females beneficiaries to receive their complaints and feedback.Risk2: Escalation of the security situation. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1.1 The security situation is proposed to be safe across the targeting community as the area is located in the Euphrates shield areas, the thing which ensure the impelemtation of the project activities. Risk3: risk of tension between the agriculture association members and the micro-credit office members when selecting the two head committees management members; Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: the heading committees will have equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities as well as females and males members. There will be a clear shared procedures and principles and all members will be committed to these procedures and an election process under the supervision of local councils and representatives of different groups of community based will be conducted to name the management members.Risk4: risk of beneficiaries’ stealing money (grants) or not using it as agreed and planned. Likelihood high. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 4.1: the targeted beneficiaries either farmers or youth benefiting from micro-credit will not receive cash money. The heading committees will assess the need for farmers and buy him the needed inputs or provide him with the service. The micro-credit heading committee will also not provide cash money but they will receive small-income projects by the elegible members, then studying the projects and oversees the process of purchasing the needed equipment, and other related materials and every thing will be documented in cooperation with local council.Risk5: the risk of beneficiaries not restoring the received cost “refund” after the agreed period. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 5.1: members who want to benefit from agricultural association and the micro-credit services must be committed to the principle of service cost-recovery as. There will be a signed contract between the heading committee and the beneficiaries under the supervision of local council and with the guarantee of three other members.
