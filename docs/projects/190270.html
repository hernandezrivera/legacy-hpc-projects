Project Summary

As September 2021, the Ethiopia National Displacement Report 10, highlighted an increase to 4,239,636 displaced individuals nationwide due to several factors, such as conflict (85%), drought (7%) and seasonal displacement (3%). The MYR reported 14.8m of people in need in Ethiopia, without including Tigray. In 2021, the triggers of humanitarian needs in Ethiopia were armed conflict, inter-communal tensions, climatic shocks, desert locusts and disease outbreaks. In 2022, the humanitarian situation for Ethiopia is still characterized by severe shocks, including the conflict in north of the country, drought conditions in southern and southeastern pastoral areas, and persisting inflation (34.5%in January 2022). The preliminary estimation of the Shelter& NFI Cluster see the number of people in need of shelter assistance at 7.85 million, including 4.62 m IDPs, 1.99 m returnees, general population non displaced 1.24 million. Given the severe climate and man-made factors, and taking into consideration the volatility and insecurity of the Ethiopian context, COOPI proposes to cover the needs of the most vulnerable recently displaced, returned, and host communities in the areas where it has strong operational presence in Afar and Ahmara Regions. The situation in the target regions has deteriorated significantly since the spillovers of conflict in Tigray in July 2021, where restrictions to humanitarian access and conflicts have exacerbated the needs of the affected population. After the retreat from Ahmara of the TPLF to Tigray, considering the presence of COOPI in Ataye Town, the project is prioritising border woredas of the neighbouring regions of Afar-Amhara and specifically North Shewa and Oromia Special Zone; on the other hand, considering the active presence of UAGs and intercommunal violence, the project will also target Awi Zone, where COOPI will operate from its office in Chagni Town. In Afar, thanks to the comprehensive operational presence at all Zones, COOPI aims to address both conflict affected population who have endured the effects of occupation, destruction and looting of public infrastructures and those areas affected by intercommunal conflicts (Afari/Somali). The humanitarian context is complex in Afar and Amhara due to the remaining effects of the heightened conflict. The new displacements of population, provoked by new centres of conflict, closure of temporary shelters, or voluntary returns have increased the burden on already fragile systems.
The project aims to contribute to reaching the following Shelter&NFI cluster’s objectives: a. ensuring contextualized access to lifesaving shelter and NFIs for displacement affected population with a focus on the most vulnerable and at-risk population group to safeguard their health security, privacy, and dignity; b. improving protection outcomes by ensuring the conflict/displacement affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions.
The target beneficiaries are conflict affected population located in geographical areas categorized as extreme and severe conditions by combining several Shelter&NFI response options: provision in kind or cash of ESNFI and NFIs, Rental Support/Cash for Rent (mainly in urban areas) and Shelter Repair kits, specific support for households living with a member with disability. Furthermore, theproposal includes cross-cutting activities related to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitationand Abuse (PSEA); the support to referral mechanism to specialized agencies. COOPI will mainstream gender& age throughout the implementation of the project by ensuring the participation of women and men and including gender sensitive selection criteria. In total the project targets 14062 households in Afar and 11530 in Ahmara, estimated as 129222 individuals (22% male; 23% female; 26% boys; 25% girls; 2% elderly men; 2% elderly female.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

COOPI adhere to the humanitarian accountability principles and all COOPI Ethiopia staff is trained of the 5 commitments of the Accountability of the Affected Population. COOPI will make sure that beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable and safe assistance, where and how to obtain it.Following COOPI Standard OperatingProcedures, to ensure informed decisions COOPI staff will involve partners, communities, local authorities, beneficiaries and especially vulnerable groups in the planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating of this project. This includes engaging beneficiaries at the design stage by conducting focus group discussions and individual interviews with both men and women in various age groups, and ensure that assessments include the most vulnerable, including elders and people with disabilities. During the action, beneficiaries at all levels will take a leading role in the formation/consolidation of the committees described in the proposal (selection committees). These committees, jointly with local authorities and project staff, will co-lead in the selection of the recipients and in the monitoring of the action.COOPI will provide opportunities for women, people with disabilities and older people to hold positions of responsibility and be represented on community-based committees including as community focal points for assessment teams. An accessible, well understood and confidential mechanism will be set up for receiving suggestions and complaints, with a clear follow-up system on all reports.COOPI’s feedback and complaint mechanism includes information sharing on:• Project objectives, outcomes and outputs, implementing agency and donor.• Selection criteria and mechanisms to report issues for beneficiaries;• Composition of the ES-NFIs kit/entitlements. The validation and agreement with the main stakeholders on the selection of sites and beneficiaries of distributions will take place through meetings with community members, local authorities and cluster representatives.COOPI Project management team will strengthen local capacities, foster local knowledge and community coping strategies, as well as enhance protection mechanisms providing where possible psycho-social services to the community and beneficiaries.COOPI management team will make sure that field teams are sensitized and trained on the importance of making assessments tools age-friendly, by including questions on the needs and capacities of elderly and vulnerable people.Particular attention will be provided on PSEA. COOPI will provide training on its internal policies to the project staff and main stakeholders. The policy includes the concepts related with PSEA, preventive measures, referral mechanisms as well as feedback and complaints mechanisms. Besides that, COOPI will brief the staff also using the Ethiopia PSEA Network Training Package.COOPI willset up a feedback and complain mechanism to allow beneficiaries’ participation, improving monitoring and accountability reporting and promptly adjust the implementation of the project. Mechanisms to report issues will be done through:•Provision to local stakeholders of main contacts for COOPI staff (phone and email), especially for the SMT program staff • Provision of help-desk contact (help@coopi.org) • Establishment of complaints and feedback box and notice panel at the field (depending on level of literacy of beneficiaries)• Participatory selection of focal points at community level, for oral complaints/feedback.COOPI will record all possible complaints and feedbacks. According to the severity of the issue COOPI SMT will respond, in at least 4 days, involving different staff at different level:• Minor complaints/ request of information: social promoters/wash advisor/emergency lead.•Major complaints: Programme Manager/Emergency Response Lead/CountryAdministrator/Head of Mission/HQ
