Project Summary

Under FAS strategic objectives 2, & 3, Rescate designed an area-based approach in 2 governorates Aleppo & Deir Ez Zour (DEZ). Under the drought like conditions in NES and Rural Aleppo, Rescate project will support light rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructure including irrigation canals, water and irrigation systems. The main and sub canals rehabilitation supports returned farmers to boost their land reclamation especially with the distribution of one season kit inputs for farmers in the 6th farm in Maskaneh & Hatlah in DEZ. Although the land reclamation is costly but it will combat soil salinization to stope land asset deterioration and loss.
Most areas original population has not returned to their homes yet due to the lack of farming facilitating gear, mainly water irrigation, hence they still live in other areas in pursuit of livelihood. This exposes women and girls to grave GBV violations and discrimination.
Rescate approached MAAR & MWR and obtained the priorities in Maskaneh and DEZ especially that the areas are classified as 4th category regarding the rain precipitation this led that the fields are not suitable for farming and planting, therefore reclamation of land will be supported.
In DEZ, the low level of the Euphrates deems pumping as an urgent prerequisite. Rehabilitated pumps are idle due to long electricity outages and generators nonefficiency due to expensive operation/repairs costs, thus; result was nonfunctional pumps that did not serve the purpose. Studies gave weight to irrigation as a priority lifesaving need highlighting that agriculture is the community only source of living. The plan is installing solar power system, cleaning the irrigation canals, maintenance of suction pipes, install and maintenance of suction valves.
In Maskaneh, since the pumping stations are functional, therefore intervention is to rehabilitate the main irrigation canals including damaged concrete covers, which causes leakages in the irrigation water flow, and this leads to cavities due to the Gypseous soil in the area.
The outcomes of the rehabilitation, expected 15-20% of the original inhabitants will return to work in their own lands. With the two-year intervention plan, including land reclamation, and distribution of agricultural inputs for 1 season per the FAS kit composition – including seeds, fertilizer, pesticides and equipment; coupled with related training to returnee farmers with focus on women. This will increase the intervention sustainability & will enhance the inhabitants’ protection & resilience.
Reviving agriculture as a major profession for the population in the 6th farm 2,188 hectares. Crops cotton, maize, sunflower, beetroot, sesame, wheat, barley, and vegetables.
Returnees will support & enhance the other livelihoods like farming, planting, groceries, hardware stores & smith shops. A community based participatory approach will be used as an important strategy to encourage communities to take responsibility to participate in identified agricultural and livelihood
interventions.
Securing sustainable irrigation will secure immediate livelihoods to HHs farmers allowing them to work on cultivating their lands for the next season. Creating jobs for women the core farmers & protecting them from GBV violations. Ensure food security in these areas with outreach to the cities will contribute to control food prices.

4 - Needs

In the target localities HNO 2022 provided Maskaneh status (3 severe, PiN 27,966, Acute PiN 22,732, @ risk PiN 5,233) & DEZ (4 extreme, PiN 197,585 Acute PiN 169,920, @ risk PiN 27, 664) the majority of HHs suffer from disruption of their livelihoods due to asset loss and lack of access to farmlands. Upon return, needs are dire for functional irrigation, combat agricultural land salinization to enable reviving livelihood. Local communities’ vulnerability increased as communities rely heavily on agriculture, the source of food and income. This project will, Under the approach to combining lifesaving and life sustaining assistance with livelihoods activities and promote early recovery and resilience, will respond to the key needs “In Hatla, renewable energy to ensure irrigation, repair of canals, and combat salinization of agricultural soils with reclamation of land in Maskaneh, Rescate response is delivering immediate agricultural assistance & securing sustainable irrigation to provide agriculture-based livelihoods interventions to HHs farmers to work on cultivating their lands in Maskaneh and Hatla and hence protect against further loss of assets and adoption of harmful coping strategies by the targeted householdFAS Aleppo sub-national coordinator and FAO Rep in DEZ encouraged the project due to people’s critical needs for lack of resources to start farming. Oct 2021, 6th Maskaneh farm & Hatla were assigned as urgent priorities to revive livelihoods for returnee population.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project team will consist of programme management and technical staff that are responsible to set a detailed plan based on implementation strategies. Rescate and ST will:- kickoff meeting with stakeholders and potential beneficiaries to discuss the project strategy, and workplan- conduct FDGs to discuss beneficiary selection criteria, followed by community mobilization, thus data collection. - regular meetings with the Farmers Unit FU to be abreast of the project, to optimize support & approval on implementation. - announce beneficiaries’ selection criterion which will be implemented and stressed by the field team in collaboration with stakeholders. - establish a project committee to follow-up with the beneficiaries to ensure sustainability and exit strategy in collaboration with FU.- Procurement procedures to sign agreements with contractors for; 1) reclamation of land in Maskaneh, 2) renewable energy in Hatla, 3) agricultural inputs for the two areas. Rescate will work with local communities to prevent discrimination and ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups.This project responds to the need of ERL and resilience to help vulnerable smallholder crop farmers become more self-reliant through protection & improvement of their livelihoods assets, rehabilitation of irrigation systems and other agriculture related community assets i.e. people’s resilience is boosted and the communities will be self-reliant and live in dignity and normalcy as Farmers on their land

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Activities might create some inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery or service types if one community is served and another is not. Misunderstanding with the community has been identified in rural areas where no protection services exist. Population shall understand the purpose and goal of the activities, it might create a negative effect and prevent the project quality implementation. PSEA & GBV minors are some of the most vulnerable in the war in Syria, especially girls. Tensions linked to stressful situations may lead to domestic violence. 1) Rescate and Partner will ensure consultations with the targeted communities and neighboring ones; 2) create a mapping of services and involve the community in the activities’ design; 3) Undertake explanation to the community and encourage acceptance. 4) Rescate will monitor all population movement and community tensions to be aware of the situation in the field and design the program respecting “Do No Harm”; 5) PSEA & GBV risk, training is provided to suppliers and staff to ensure knowledge and commitment of the Code of Conduct; 6) field staff already communicate on regular basis and will continue to explain to the community about the services available and the activities implemented; 7) community focal points are consulted and can call Rescate team within the frame of the complaint mechanism in the course of the project.8) lack of official IDs, accept 2 official identity dox duly ratified by any official Dpt.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Miscommunication with one or more of the stakeholders may possibly happen, and this may lead to major consequences such as biased selection for the beneficiaries. In this regard, RESCATE will coordinate closely with the ST (as a partner) and other local partners and communities to ensure that the provided service will be delivered to the deserved beneficiaries properly at the planned time by adopting a close and regular approach for communication with the local communities and authorities in alignment with the shelter cluster standards.• Due to some program or technical issues in the project proposal, the project may start as the harsh winter season begins, which may delay some concrete works. Rescate team is planning to start the implementation activities in the spring season to avoid the frost which affect the concrete and to ensure the good implementation practices.• The devaluation of the Syrian pound due to the inflation may cause differences between the prices, in this context, Rescate will reflect the difference in exchange rate on the target of the project.• Regarding the security issues that may occur and access during the implementation of the M&E phases, RESCATE staff will commit to the safe access procedures that will be announced by UNDSS and other concerned partners in this context.
