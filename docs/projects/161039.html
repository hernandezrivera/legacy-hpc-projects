Project Summary

Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes the Strengthening emergency WASH assistance through provision safe water, sanitation and hygiene services in order to Mitigate WASH Gender- Based Violence (GBV), Health and Malnutrition related needs of IDPs, Returnee and Host Communities in, Torit County, Eastern Equatoria, Panyikang Upper Nile and Aweil East County Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal. Impact Health Organization (IHO) needs assessment conducted in Torit County, Aweil East and Panyikang showed that Most Households had no access to latrines, drink and use water from the river with limited treatment, limited access to water collection containers and as well as limited hygiene awareness. In both locations WASH infrastructure at the nutrition centers, health Centres, schools and at the community-level were limited hence there is a need for an urgent response.
Findings from IPC acute food insecurity & acute malnutrition analysis September to December 2019 reported that all counties (Torit County, Eastern Equatoria, Panyikang Upper Nile, Aweil East County Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal, are classified as critical phase (levels 3) of which the Global Acute Malnutrition is expected to raise due to worsening food insecurity is primarily driven by influx of conflict and displacements and expected to rise above minimum levels due to insufficient crop production and disruptions to livelihoods and persistent macroeconomic deterioration.
IHO assessment in 2019 in Aweil East, Panyikang and Aweil East Counties Showed that there was limited access to hygiene promotion messaging and limited access to WASH Supplies i.e Soap, Clean waters etc among household with SAM children and as well poor Sanitation facility infrastructure in the visited OTP sites that needed rehabilitation. In another IHO Assessment report (see report attached) Aweil South County showed that there was general lack of access to safe drinking water as communities relied on unprotected community hand dug well. There was also limited access to sanitation facilities and hygiene awareness. Hand washing and water treatment was not practiced hence reported cases of diarrhea in the community especially in children under five years. In the same regard, The April 2019 nationwide WASH assessment by REACH reported high proportion of settlements in NBEG with poor latrine usage (less than 50% of the assessed population), Aweil East and Aweil South are among the five top counties with very poor/absent latrine/sanitation access. This project will integrate health, nutrition and education WASH related interventions into the response.
The project will target communities with poor access to safe water supply, unsafe sanitation and hygiene practices, areas with poor sanitation coverage and support health, education and nutrition centers with rehabilitation of WASH facilities. Therefore, this project will address the needs WASH-Nut WASH-GBV and WASH-Health needs of 60000 Individuals (19404 Men, 20,196 Female, 9996 Boys, 10404 Girls) of which 30% (Men 11741 Women 12220, Boys 6048 Girls 6295) shall be WASH and Nutrition needs in Aweil East County Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal, 30% T Men 2682, Women 2792 Boys 1382, Girls 1438) shall be WASH and Health (Cholera hotspots and Household members affected by relevant health issues (respiratory, AWD, Cholera, eye infection, etc.) in Torit County, Eastern Equatoria State and 40% (Men 5294, Women 5510, Boys 2727, Girls 2838) WASH and GBV (Women and girls at risk/in need of core GBV services, access to an improved water source, access to a functional and improved sanitation facility in Panyikang, Upper Nile state South Sudan.

1 - Needs

Following the December 2013 crisis another major crisis erupted in July 2016 aggravated the humanitarian situation in South Sudan, producing secondary or third displacement with populations under stress lacking access to basic services and constraining humanitarian access. In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Findings from IPC acute food insecurity & acute malnutrition analysis September to December 2019 reported that all counties (Torit County, Eastern Equatoria, Panyikang Upper Nile, Aweil East County Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal, are classified as critical phase (levels 3).Among the most affected Counties are Torit County, Eastern Equatoria, Panyikang Upper Nile, Aweil East County Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal having figures way off thresholds in the thematic areas of WASH, nutrition, health and food security. The situation has been deteriorating due to conflict in the region. As a result, water point infrastructure such as boreholes, water systems, and latrines and other facilities were destroyed. Impact Health organization assessment in Kudo, Ifwotu, Imurok, Lowoi, Hiyala, Himodonge, Bur and Torit Payam Torit County in October 2018 showed that most household 71.3% do not meet the basic minimum of water requirement of 15ltrs per person per day,88.6% household do not have a single latrine practicing open defecation (OD) and there is limited access to hygiene promotion messaging or training. IHO needs assessment October 2018 conducted 3 Payams of Lul, Kodok and Kodok Town. The western bank continued to have limited access to boreholes and source water primarily from unprotected sources 96% of assessed western bank settlements reported no access to a borehole by September 2018, comparable to 92% in June 2018. As a result, in September, the majority (78%) of assessed western bank settlements fetched water from rivers. Livestock rearing practices also presented obstacles to sourcing safe water in this quarter. The majority 72% of the western bank (September 2018) settlements reported animals drinking from or kept near to water points, including 100% in Panyikang county. Such practices heighten risk of water contamination.IHO assessment finding in Panyikang County showed that majority of the household assessed 90 % had no access to latrine. All respondents 100 % reported lack of access to safe water drinking water and all communities source drinking water from the river with minimal treatment as only 20 % of respondents reported to boil or filtering drinking water. Majority of the respondents 87 % reported their households used less than 15 liters/person/per day. This because 75% of the respondents reported household lacked water containers and Most respondents 60 % reported lack of soap in the house for hand washing as 40% report existence of soap or ash for hand washing. Aweil East County significant WASH needs are identified alongside high rates of malnutrition. Although food security presents a primary concern for the affected population, however access to safe drinking water and insufficient safe hygiene practices poses a great risk of WASH disease outbreak. Most households in Aweil East are reported to use an unprotected water source as the primary source of water. (Reach report, Situation Overview: Greater Bahr el Ghazal, South Sudan April - June 2018). This in line with Assessment by IHO in April 2018 which showed only 3.7% of the responders treat drinking water in Aweil East. In response to the above indicators, IHO plans to undertake Integrated WASH interventions (WASH-NUT, WASH-GBV and WASH-Health so as to meet the needs of the Vulnerable Communities.

2 - Activities

To meet the community WASH needs in the targeted locations, 1. Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations: IHO will rehabilitate non-functional boreholes, distribution of water purification supplies such as PUR, Aqua tab hence promoting timely and equitable access to safe water, 2. Enable vulnerable and affected populations sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services to practice safe: IHO will facilitate construction emergency sanitation facilities and hygiene awareness among IDPs/host communities and returnees. The process will involve working closely with communities to identify their WASH needs in terms of availability to safe water, access to sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion. 3. Undertake hygiene promotion to Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases: IHO undertake, hygiene promotion by training community hygiene promoters who shall conduction household awareness and hygiene campaigns. Hygiene awareness shall be complimented by distribution of WASH NFI to vulnerable households and groups. 4. Mitigate WASH related GBV issues: IHO will also mitigate WASH-related GBV issues outside PoC/settlement most specially among women and girls who are the primary direct users of WASH services, by assessing GBV gaps through safety audits both at water sources and Sanitation facilities and finding will guide interventions to promote safety of women while accessing water and Sanitation infrastructure. GBV awareness will be integral part of the project intervention in IHO will also map GBV actors and create a referral pathway for GBV cases. 5. Reduce Malnutrition among SAM/MAM cases: IHO will undertake WASH/Nutrition interventions both at household and OTP/SC/TSFP sites. These shall include increasing access to safe drinking water through borehole rehabilitation, reduce open defection through increased access to latrines and promote infant young child feed practices to reduce diarrhea diseases through hygiene awareness. In addition, Boreholes rehabilitated shall consist of water traps for animals and irrigation purpose and communities shall be education on use traps to support household farming. 6. Avert and controlling disease outbreaks such as cholera, etc. IHO will promote access to WASH services (access to safe water, and build capacities by training of health workers, WASH focal points, school children etc to appropriate hygiene practices both in institutions and household level.

The awareness will be conducted in the communities on hygiene promotion and water management as well protection of women and girls and the asserted life skills will facilitate behavior change hence sustaining safe behavior.
