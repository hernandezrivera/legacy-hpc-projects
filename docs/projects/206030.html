Project Summary

This project aims to build sustainable WASH services for the resilience of communities in Yambio, Ezo, Mundri West, Aweil East, and Bor South Counties. This intervention contributes to the WASH Cluster strategic Objective 3; which seek to build resilience through durable and long term access to WASH services. The overall goal will be achieved through 6 major Programme outputs that focus on the provision of safe water, the practice of at least 3 key improved hygiene behaviors, sustainable access to sanitary means of excretal disposal, enhanced capacity of community structures to effectively manage and operate the maintenance of WASH facilities, mitigate WASH related gender-based violence, and enhanced capacity of State Directors of Water Supply and Sanitation to effectively plan, supervise and monitor WASH services.
The intervention will provide safe water for 58,500 beneficiaries through the rehabilitation of 85 non-functional boreholes with climate-resilient structures, protection/treatment, and conversion of 20 hand-dug shallow wells to hand pumps and rehabilitation of 3 solar-powered water yards. Conduct Water quality analysis for each of the water points. The operation and maintenance of the Water facilities will be ensured by the re-establishment and training of water management committees for each of the water points with at least one-third female representation.
This intervention will seek to promote the practice of at least 3 key improved/good hygiene and sanitation behaviors through awareness campaigns targeting 58,500 beneficiaries. This will be achieved through monthly radio talk shows, the production and dissemination of IEC materials, and sanitation jingles on radio stations. A Survey in schools on Knowledge, Attitudes, and Practices on the design and use of school sanitation facilities for girls, boys, and teachers will provide baseline information for the gaps. Handwashing facilities will be installed in the targeted schools and health facilities with each institution receiving 2 handwashing facilities disaggregated for boys/males and girls/females. Establish and train school health and hygiene clubs on WASH promotion. Celebrate and commemorate World Water Day and World Hand Hygiene Day 2024 to create awareness about safe water, good hygiene, and sanitation practices.
CLTS activities will be implemented in 38 new villages, reaching 19,000 individuals. It is anticipated that the CLTS interventions will encourage these communities to build 3,100 household latrines. CAP (Community Action Plans) for ODF will be developed and implemented in each triggered village, and Internal and external ODF verification and post-ODF monitoring exercises will be conducted for sustained ODF retention.
The capacity of community structures will be enhanced to effectively manage and operate the maintenance of WASH facilities. This will be achieved through the development of State WASH committee guidelines, re-establishment, and training of 864 committee members from 108 water management committees where each committee will consist of 8 members with at least one-third female representation.

1. Needs

An estimated 6.1 million people’s living standards and their well-being will be impacted in 2024 because of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation (HNO,2023). Lack of WASH services exacerbates the suffering of those in need resulting in water-related diseases and incapacitated copying mechanism. Only 41% of the total population in South Sudan have access to safe water while only 10 % have access to improved sanitation with 61 % of the population practicing open defecation (UNICEF WASH in South Sudan Briefing note, October – December 2022). Across South Sudan, 61% of the HHs reported having access to at least 3 liters of water per person per day, and only 35% reported having access to a safe water under 30 minutes without protection concerns.In Jonglei State, only 39 % of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs, 33 % of households reported traveling for longer than 30 minutes to access water. 49% of households reported relying on surface water sources (HNO, 2023). WES has an average of 23% of people having access to safe water with Ezo less than 20% and Yambio less than 60% of the population. Only 50.5% of the population in Yambio County had access to enough water by quantity. In South Sudan, 80% of households do not access a latrine. In Jonglei and Northern Bahr-El-Ghazal only 10% of households consistently access a latrine, with open defecation the norm, using a latrine is considered a taboo in many parts of the country. In some locations, constructed latrines are hardly used due to societal pressure, with people preferring to walk a great distance even at night to practice open defecation. According to the WASH Cluster 2023 HRP Targets, of the 132,765 people in need in Yambio County, only 53,100 people have access to an improved water source, 4,270 people have access to sanitation facilities, and only 52,100 people reached with hygiene and sanitation promotion messages. Only 26,100 women and girls have access to improved water and sanitation after a safety audit. Of the 69,000 people in need in Ezo County, only 27,600 people have access to an improved water source, and 15,252 people have access to sanitation facilities. Only 27,100 people were reached with hygiene promotion messages. Only 14,700 women and girls have access to improved water/sanitation facilities after a safety audit. (www.response.reliefweb.int/South-Sudan/water-sanitation-hygiene). The lack of WASH facilities, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities, and lack of access to clean drinking water contribute to poor health conditions, with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such as diarrheal diseases, cholera, and Hepatitis E. Repeated shocks have weakened community-based structures and services. Inadequate access to services will continue to push families to adopt negative coping mechanisms, exposing the most vulnerable, particularly children and women, to protection risks, such as violence, exploitation, abuse, recruitment, trafficking, child labor, and early marriage (HNO, 2023). This intervention seeks to establish long-lasting resilient WASH solutions such that communities can bounce back from such shocks without practicing negative coping mechanisms. The signing of the Revitalized Agreement in September 2018 provided optimism for the socio-economic transformation of South Sudan. This intervention will provide access to safe drinking water for 58,500 people, build the capacity of Communities to Plan WASH programs and to effectively manage the operation and maintenance of WASH facilities sustainably, provide sustainable access to sanitary means of excreta disposal and practice of good hygiene behaviours to 19,000 people. Contribute to mitigation of WASH-related gender-based violence, enhance the capacity of the Director of Rural Water Supply and Sanitation to effectively plan, supervise, and monitor.
