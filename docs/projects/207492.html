Project Summary

UNHCR delivers comprehensive protection services in Cox’s Bazar and ensures the centrality of protection through protection and gender mainstreaming, inclusive coordination and leadership of the humanitarian response. In 2024, UNHCR will continue to work with the Government to secure identity and documentation for Rohingya refugees through continuous registration, which will serve as the keystone for protection interventions, assistance delivery and solutions planning. UNHCR will also strengthen its community-based protection approach, which provides the foundation for age, gender and diversity responsive services. UNHCR’s response will deliver integrated protection services including registration and documentation, legal protection, and community-based protection. UNHCR will conduct protection monitoring in all 33 camps to identify protection incidents/trends to support advocacy and address protection gaps, identify and assist refugees facing urgent and serious protection concerns and facilitate multisectoral referrals. UNHCR will strengthen access to justice through the capacity building of law enforcement/justice sector actors, strengthening of legal assistance activities (through its partners) in all 33 camps, including outreach and legal awareness to eliminate barriers for all persons of different ages, genders and specific needs, legal counselling, legal assistance and representation, and mediation support. UNHCR will empower Rohingya refugees by strengthening community capacities for leadership, self-management and engagement in 33 camps, including through elected refugee representatives in four camps. UNHCR will support refugee volunteer networks such as Community Outreach Members (COMs), community groups, religious networks (including imams and female religious teachers) and sports for protection clubs. COMs will identify and support persons with specific needs, and will mobilize communities for protection. Male and female community groups, including youth groups, will deliver community initiatives that foster community self-reliance and social change. Communication with communities will be mainstreamed. Community and information centres will also act as essential hubs to disseminate timely, accurate and life-saving information. UNHCR will strengthen disability inclusion in all its protection interventions as well as provide targeted services for persons with disabilities. Within the current Government policy framework, UNHCR will advocate for greater access to age, gender and diversity (AGD) responsive education and economic inclusion, so that young refugees retain hope and become agents for positive change in the community. UNHCR’s solutions strategy takes into account the Government’s policy that voluntary repatriation is the only feasible solution for Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh, and promotes the conditions needed for voluntary, safe, dignified and sustainable return in Myanmar. UNHCR will work to improve protection conditions and quality of life for refugees in Bangladesh pending solutions, building upon the framework for responsibility sharing provided by the Global Compact on Refugees (GCR). As reinforcing accountable Government leadership of the response will build a foundation for the inclusion of refugees in the national systems in the future, UNHCR will continue to support the capacity development of both Government and humanitarian actors across its operations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Exacerbation of pre-existing protection risks, including specific risks for refugee women and girls and persons with specific needs, a deteriorating security environment, stressed community protection structures and deepened inter/intra-communal tensions continue to be highlighted by the community. The number of reported serious protection incidents significantly increased in 2023, with a 490% increase in abduction cases reported in the first half of 2023 compared to the same period in 2022. The Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) corroborates such findings, with 60% of refugee households expressing concerns about protection risks compared to 33% of host community households. A lack of socio-economic opportunities, unequal gender norms and a culture of impunity foster pervasive violence in refugee households and communities. Joint Government-UNHCR Rohingya verification for the new caseload who had arrived in Bangladesh in 2017 was completed in 2019, while the pre-2017 caseload was finalised in 2023. Residual cases accumulated during the COVID-19 lockdown while processing capacity was reduced in 2022, resulting in a backlog of cases. Continuous registration must remain accessible to ensure that refugees can record and document their status and access assistance and services, while a census-based verification for the new caseload is required for the purpose of reviewing the population in the camps, systematically updating information and reissuing documents which are becoming illegible and are susceptible to tampering. Major gaps remain in the civil registration and documentation of births and deaths, while refugees’ unrecognized legal status hinders access to justice and effective protection, putting them at risk of arbitrary arrest, unlawful detention and leaving them vulnerable to exploitation, abuse and human trafficking. UNHCR provides legal assistance to refugees, who are often reluctant to report crimes in fear of retaliation by criminal groups and due to lengthy formal processes and lack of trust towards law enforcement institutions. Lack of access to the formal civil justice system limits refugees’ ability to assert their legal rights related to housing, land and property matters, as well as family issues, including women’s right to divorce, maintenance and guardianship in the formal justice system. Despite a Women’s and Children’s Tribunal in Cox’s Bazar, limited awareness of recourse mechanisms and cultural norms disproportionally impact women’s and girls’ access to justice, including survivors of violence. Women and girls, persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations continue to face heightened barriers in accessing services and information. Focus group discussions (FGDs) reported that women, especially older women or women with disabilities, were much less likely to have access to information and complaints mechanisms (11/2023). Meaningful participation of women and female youth remains challenging given restrictions on movement and access to communications due to cultural norms (GiHA, Comprehensive Gender Analysis 03/2022). PWDs also face barriers related to mobility in shelters (over 52%), as well as movement within the camps (over 75%) (REACH, May 2021)). This is particularly true among older and female PWDs who face the most significant challenges in accessing services. In total, 67% of female elderly PWDs had reportedly not received any assistive devices despite requiring them, compared to a maximum of 58% of male or female PWD across all age groups, and 55% of female older persons without disabilities (REACH, May 2021). Strategic intervention is therefore necessary to strengthen inclusive age, gender and diversity sensitive responses which support refugees to become drivers of change in their community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNHCR has worked with Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh for over 30 years, providing registration and documentation, legal and community-based protection support and services for all refugee men, women, girls and boys. UNHCR has provided continuous support for registration, documentation and legal protection in all 33 camps from the outset of their establishment. UNHCR’s international protection mandate underpins its continuous commitment as both a first responder and provider of last resort, alongside the Government, to ensure refugees’ access to protection. UNHCR continues to provide a comprehensive community-based protection programme in 33 camps. In each camp, UNHCR prioritizes the assessment of existing community capacities as well as humanitarian actors and capacities in order to ensure complementary and effective interventions. UNHCR leads the Protection Sector and participates as an active member to ensure coordination with other protection stakeholders, and also participates in all camp-level protection coordination structures. UNHCR already provides integrated protection services at community centres, comprising community engagement and empowerment activities, information service centres, gender-based violence (GBV) and psychosocial support, and legal assistance in many camps. In 2024, UNHCR aims to increase the integration of protection services in order to ensure the seamless and accessible provision of information and support to all refugees.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNHCR’s protection programmes are built on an analysis of community power structures and cultural norms, and aim to strengthen existing community capacities whilst ensuring access for and inclusion of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. UNHCR and partners provide access to services through community centres that provide integrated protection interventions in a non-stigmatising manner, and also ensure that outreach activities reach those who cannot or do not feel comfortable attending centres. UNHCR conducts regular community consultations, risk assessments and safety audits which engage men, women, boys and girls, including persons with disabilities, persons of diverse sexual orientations and gender identities and persons with specific needs in order to identify and mitigate risks, and to optimize safety, dignity and equity. UNHCR continuously builds capacity internally and for partners on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), the UNHCR Code of Conduct and other essential protection standards.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNHCR’s recognizes that gender equality is fundamental to the well-being and rights of all people and remains committed to ensuring that gender considerations are integrated throughout its protection activities. UNHCR’s protection programmes ensures that all refugees fully participate in decisions that affect them and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others. As the age, gender and diversity (AGD) policy is fundamentally linked to UNHCR’s rights-based and community-based approaches, UNHCR includes refugees meaningfully in its operational planning and also identifies protection gaps which need to be addressed. All UNHCR partners are trained on child safeguarding, as well as prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and the Code of Conduct. Focus groups are regularly conducted with boys and girls to identify priority activities as well as risks and challenges in accessing programmes. UNHCR and partners engage outreach volunteers to reach out to and include refugees of all ages, with a focus on adolescents, youth, adults, older persons, and persons with disabilities. UNHCR works with male and female youth groups, providing training and resources for young people to identify priorities and address them. UNHCR also delivers recreational activities, awareness and opportunities for engagement with children aged eight years and above, including through sports programmes. UNHCR’s outreach activities ensure that all refugees, regardless of age, gender, and disbility are provided with information about protection services and have the opportunity to access services. UNHCR Community Outreach Members (COMs) are trained to identify and support the most vulnerable groups including older persons, women, children, and persons with disabilities and to provide support to ensure that they are able to access necessary services.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AAP is a core responsibility and central to UNHCR’s mandate. Refugees participate in deciding the priorities for activities implemented by UNHCR and lead their own initiatives through community-based mechanisms throughout the operational management cycle. UNHCR conducts regular community consultations in all camps to identify priority issues and obtain feedback in order to adapt its protection programming as needed. UNHCR also operates community feedback and response centres, complaints boxes, email service, and a toll-free helpline in order to provide information to and elicit feedback and complaints from refugees. UNHCR prioritizes two-way communication, ensuring that individual complaints are responded to and that communities are provided with collective feedback. A fundamental part of UNHCR’s work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct (CoC) and a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and harrassment for its staff.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Protection from sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment is a crucial area for UNHCR. UNHCR is an active member of the PSEA Network, a coordination structure to prevent, mitigate, and harmonize the response against SEA/SH incidents in Bangladesh, having a dedicated group organized for the Rohingya Response and a second one based in Dhaka. All UNHCR Implementing Partners are also members of the Network and follow the Inter-Agency SoP review in 2023. Only in 2023, UNHCR assessed 13 Implementing Partners on their capacities to prevent and respond to SEA/SH, intending to update another 5 assessments in 2024 to guarantee excellent and safe deliverance of services. UNHCR staff is annually trained on CoC and PSEA/SH, as well as all personnel working for UNHCR Implementing Partners, including volunteers. UNHCR delivers an annual refresher to Focal Points from NGOs and INGOs and monitors their performance to cascade the training to the national staff, volunteers, community leaders, and members of all communities, focusing on the safe identification and confidential referral of cases. According to UNHCR Partnership Agreements, all Implementing Partners need to focus on communicating with communities – using non-verbal and accessible language, on the complaint and reporting systems, the importance of reporting, as well as the protection and reputational risks related to SEA incidents. An audio and poster awareness on SEA, created in the Rohingya language, was used to reach volunteers in a short period to convey the most critical information and we intend to keep using this material to enhance community empowerment and create a safer environment in the camps.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR will remain a central player within inter-agency preparedness and response efforts related to disasters such as monsoons and cyclones. UNHCR includes monsoon, cyclone, landslide, fire and other disaster preparedness aspects in all of its communication with communities activities, and also in community initiatives. When disasters occur, UNHCR targets protection services to respond to the needs of those affected, with a specific focus on women, children and persons with specific needs. UNHCR is an active member of the Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) and thus are on standby to be deployed during disasters or emergencies in camp setting to identify urgent protection cases, provide emergency assistance including psychosocial first aid (PFA), map functioning and non-functioning services in the affected areas, and carry out other activities to serve and protect affected refugees. In addition, UNHCR has First Onsite Response Teams (FORT) which are deployed after disaster to ensure minimal presence of staff to monitor and follow up the ongoing emergency response in the most affected camps with partners. FORT conducts immediate onsite assessments which contributed to Sector level data gathering. UNHCR is an active member of the Emergency Protection Working Group at ISCH level and a partner organization of the ISCG Multi-Hazard Response Plan. Moreover, UNHCR built facilities in consideration of environmental and climate concerns, and disaster risk reduction (DRR).
