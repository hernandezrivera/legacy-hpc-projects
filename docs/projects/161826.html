Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the delivery of quality protection services, improving the protection environment and supporting durable solutions for IDPs, IDP returnees, refugee returnees and vulnerable host community members in North East Nigeria. UNHCR interventions will strengthen the protection environment by identifying and minimizing protection risks to which the affected populations are continually exposed and by expanding the breadth and quality of protection response in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa through:
1. Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring, including through community-based mechanisms, and protection by presence;
2. Quality of registration and profiling of Persons of concerns;
3. Enhanced access to legal assistance and legal remedies;
4. Enhanced access to Civil registration, including civil status documentation;
5. Advocacy and capacity building in support of the development of Law and policy, including the domestication of the KC;
6. Promote Self-reliance through access to protection safety-net activities;
7. Support the discussions and the processes for durable solutions

1 - Needs

The protection crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States has been marked by mass human rights violations, brutal attacks, abduction, rampant sexual and gender-based violence, arbitrary arrests and detention, family separation, disappearances, and forced displacement. Widespread abuses, coupled with the break down in civil administration structures, law and order throughout the conflict, has seen the erosion of the protective environment for those in most affected areas. 1.2M in "hard-to-reach" areas are severely at risk and require urgent support. The most vulnerable civilians face heightened protection risks, including female and child-headed households, unaccompanied/separated children, adolescent boys and girls, the elderly, the chronically sick, people with physical and mental disabilities, pregnant disabilities, pregnant and lactating women within IDP camps, host communities and return areas. Recovery efforts continue to be hampered in many areas by lack of basic infrastructures, conflict-driven insecurity resulting in new displacements, limited access, limited availability of basic services and limited presence of the relevant national actors and key humanitarian partners in some locations, all of which exacerbate protection risks for the most vulnerable populations. Threats are often compounded by the high level of congestion among displaced families, lack of basic resources for survival and by the location of vulnerable individuals in isolated, insecure or inhospitable places. Multiple rounds of vulnerability screening, ongoing protection monitoring, and protection assessments show the severity of protection needs for those most affected by conflict and displacement. Insecurity and desperation has resulted in additional challenges for female headed households and widows, increasing their vulnerability to sexual violence and forced/early marriages, leading among others to risk of rejection and stigmatization by their families and community members. High numbers of unaccompanied and separated children, orphans and child-headed households have critical protection needs and many children have had to resort to hawking/begging as means of survival. There has been an increase of instances of attacks, harassment, abduction and GBV when women and girls venture outside sites' security perimeter in order to search for firewood. The challenges for reintegration facing boys who were recruited and used by Boko Haram are critical. The absence of civil authorities in newly accessible areas and civil authorities functions undertaken by military has undermined the humanitarian and civilian character of IDP sites. There is a critical need to continue to scale up efforts to prevent, refer and respond to cases with identified protection risks with effective protection interventions. Strengthened engagement, collaboration and coordination with all stakeholders on durable solutions is highly required. In line with the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy, the protection of affected persons must be at the forefront of decision making, informing strategic response. 1,580,093 formally displaced individuals have returned to their places of origin as of August 2018. This includes 35,701 refugees who returned to Nigeria from Cameroon, Niger and Chad from January to August 2018, many of which have fallen short of international standards on voluntariness, safety and dignity, including the protection against refoulement. IDP returnee and refugee returnees are staying in difficult to access areas which lack basic services, infrastructure and livelihood opportunities, and both face challenges with regards to housing and repossessing their original properties.UNHCR, and the Governments of Cameroon and the Federal Republic of Nigeria signed a Tripartite Agreement for the voluntary repatriation of Nigerian refugees living in Cameroon in March 2017, setting out modalities for the voluntary and facilitated return of Nigerian refugees in safety and dign

2 - Activities

A wide range of activities will be implemented under 5 main axis of intervention namely favorable protection environment, Fair protection processes and documentation, Security from violence and exploitation, durable solutions and Community Empowerment and Self Reliance and eight objectives.Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring and protection by presence: UNHCR will • Strengthen its protection monitoring system and enhance presence in the field to support affected populations with the identification of protection risks and incidents as well as human rights violations faced by their community members through community-based protection monitoring systems (Protection Action Groups); capacitate them to be able to refer/respond to issues, and provide comprehensive protection response including through referrals to other stakeholders;• Continue and strengthen detention centers and facilities monitoring as well as efforts to seek access to military screening centers/detention;• Analyze information on protection risks and incident trends as well as develop and disseminate high quality protection products as a basis for evidence-based advocacy and programming in order to ensure appropriate response by humanitarian actors, as well as to ensure protection mainstreaming within other sectors. Quality of registration and profiling: UNHCR will • Prole and track vulnerable IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees through the rolling vulnerability screening process to allow for delivery of assistance tailored to specific needs, including referrals for specialized services such as child protection, MHPSS, legal protection and medical response by UNHCR and Protection Sector partners.• In collaboration with the competent national institutions, continue the registration of spontaneous refugee returnees to ensure the planning of comprehensive reintegration programs.Access to legal assistance and legal remedies: • Ensure enhanced access to quality legal aid and services, including legal advice, counselling and legal representation on civil and criminal matters. This includes legal assistance on housing, land and property issues among others including through mobile courts.• Conduct advocacy interventions for IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees’ access to national justice systems. • Contribute to the capacity building of relevant actors on ADR mechanisms to support the resolution of minor issues within the community. Civil registration and civil status documentation: UNHCR will scale up its support to national institutions for the issuance of civil status documentation namely birth certificates and certificates of indigene. The number of beneficiaries in 2020 will also be increased. Law and policy developed: Advocacy efforts for the domestication of the Kampala Convention on IDPs through the adoption of a national law for IDP protection in Nigeria will be pursued and intensified. Capacity building activities of relevant national institutions will be conducted to support advocacy as well as extended to humanitarian actors to ensure that assistance is provided in line with international standards and that core protection principles are adhered to by service providers.Durable solutions for returning refugees and IDPs: UNHCR will• Engage in advocacy and efforts to establish the conditions for the pursuit of durable solutions both for IDPs and refugees;• Establish and reinforce existing cross-border coordination mechanisms with offices in neighboring countries of asylum for Nigerian refugees;• Develop and/or strengthen partnerships with development actors to enhance the Humanitarian -Development Nexus;• Provide support to both refugees who repatriate spontaneously, as well as engage on facilitated or promoted voluntary repatriation for refugees in surrounding countries who elect to return; • Provide reception and transit facilities for refugee returnees, equipping the way stations and recepti

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR will strengthen community-based feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanism, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention and other partners, for intervention. Vulnerability screening will be used to identify and target those in need of protection based materials. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. UNHCR’s firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries through Participatory Approach, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict and insurgency.
