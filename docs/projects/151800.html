Needs

Voluntary returns are on-going despite the conflict, violence, insecurity, absence of functioning and effective institutions, impunity, and lack of infrastructure in Somalia. While there is increased effort to promote sustainable return and re-integration, a substantial number of returnees have limited access to basic services, precarious or no physical security and are constantly exposed to myriad protection risks. Possible and increasing military operations by security forces, inter-clan clashes or natural disasters may further worsen the protection status of the return areas. Lack of proper legal, psychosocial and physical support also remain a challenge in most parts of the favourable return areas. Many returnees choose not to return to their areas of origin, preferring to settle in urban areas. Many of these already face pressures on basic services, further compounded by recurrent influxes of IDPs. Many IDPs in protracted displacement remain in situations with no progress towards durable solutions, resulting in systemic marginalization. The protection crisis is also characterized by entrenched but fluid societal divisions ,aggravated by competition for resources, and compounded by chronic poverty, underdeveloped legal and policy frameworks, and hampered governance – all impacting on humanitarian assistance delivery and the right to freedom of movement of civilians and humanitarians, resulting in the risk of exclusion from assistance. Other risks include lack of access to basic services, such as education and health, exclusion from services provided by government, and harassment and attacks from police and host community. Sexual violence also remains a critical protection concern for IDPs. A significant proportion of IDPs are members of minority groups lacking the protection of major clans.

Activities

- Ensuring the HCT Centrality of Protection strategy drafted under the leadership of UNHCR is kept at the heart of all interventions and also advocated through the UNHCR lead/co-lead clusters, to mainstream the priorities of the strategy into all activities.- Supporting implementation of the National Action Plan and the National Development Plan, UNHCR will support the implementation of a displacement focused solutions and durable solutions initiative. UNHCR will support the (re)integration of IDPs and returnees, through an inter-agency approach including the implementation of joint programmes. UNHCR’s focus will remain on protection, including community cohesion and HLP. - UNHCR will continue to lead the Protection and Returns Monitoring Network (PRMN), tracking displacements and rights violations among IDPs, and increase the use of PRMN data, including through improved collaboration with MRM, MARA and other mechanisms. UNHCR will conduct protection monitoring and assessments and will also support the capacity of the national and regional authorities to conduct IDP profiling. - Strengthening Community based activities, including through the construction of community centres that will benefit IDPs, returnees as well as the host community.- Ensure partners and community leadership structures are aware of their obligations to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse. Communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanisms to ensure high standards of accountability and meet the policy of zero tolerance. Support psychosocial, legal, and medical assistance and provision of dignity kits will targeting SGBV survivors. Capacity building and awareness raising sessions will take place for government agencies, partners and the community on SGBV prevention and response.Support will also be provided to IDPs on land tenure and documentation. Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) will be provided to IDPs on housing, land and property rights.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

UNHCR, will work with government and other partners to create bridges to bring displacement on to the development agenda and find permanent solutions through local integration. The most vulnerable IDPs will be identified through collaboration with IDP committees and provided with permanent shelters and support for sustainable livelihoods and basic services. Market surveys will be conducted to determine the sustainability and viability of livelihood opportunities. UNHCR will advocate for the placement of permanent land/shelters in locations where integration with host communities and access to basic services will be improved. To ensure that IDPs are aware of their housing, land and property (HLP) rights, UNHCR will undertake awareness raising on relevant HLP laws and advocacy with government for non-discriminatory application of HLP laws in coordination with partners/clusters. Inclusion of IDPs in the planning process will be crucial for planning peaceful coexistence activities and achieving implementation that enhances integration. Awareness raising amongst host communities and IDPs will take place to promote peaceful coexistence, in coordination with peaceful coexistence activities.The potential for local integration will be realized through the inclusion of IDPs in development plans, prioritization of civil documentation, by way of advocacy to the government, and the implementation of the area specific strategic plans.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The risk of exclusion is significant in IDP settlements. There is a low level of understanding and acceptance of the rights of refugees and IDPs both within the host community and with authorities, as well as prevailing misconceptions of respective roles and responsibilities. Likewise, discrimination against minority clan members is another protection challenge, with IDPs considered to be foreigners and a burden on local resources. There is lack of mutual trust, respect and recognition for the need of social cohesion, inclusion and integration between IDP and host communities. Most IDP settlements are manned by gatekeepers that often act as informal managers. In the absence of the government authorities providing IDPs with assistance and protection, informal managers have often become the primary go to person and provider of support and, to a certain extent, protection. This dependency can lead to exclusion from services that are provided. Returnees are faced with similar risks of exclusion after absence from the country to prolonged periods. UNHCR’s protection activities aim to counter exclusion risks as part of the Centrality of Protection

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

IDPs continue to face a myriad of protection risks, including lack of access to basic services, risk of exploitation and abuse including at the hands of so-called ‘gatekeepers’ and social exclusions. A significant proportion of IDPs are members of minority groups lacking the protection of major clans. GBV remains a pervasive issue, with domestic violence the main form of GBV in IDP sites, though sexual violence and harassment occur when girls and women leave settlements to collect firewood, feed livestock or on the way to and from school, and girls working as house maids report sexual exploitation and abuse. Fears of reprisal lack of confidentiality and a weak police and court system results in a majority of GBV cases going unreported. Young girls and single women are at the greatest risk of GBV, especially in temporary sites of newly displaced population that lack adequate WASH services or community lighting. UNHCR is reaching out to better understand protection challenges and to strengthen the community’s “protection networks” so that they can respond to protection problems and facilitate access to institutions and available services. Building trust with the local authorities, service providers and the international community will require continuous investment, both in areas of displacement and in return areas. The participation of the displaced population needs to be ensured through the establishment of committees coupled with the presence of community mobilisers, including for the provision of information on essential services, available assistance and returning process. Participation of women in community-based committees is rare and needs to be reinforced to guarantee a truly participatory approach and give voice to the whole community. In some areas progress has been made, and UNHCR will further strengthen community structures and engage with all persons of concern through age and gender sensitive approaches.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Displaced women, men, girls and boys in Somalia remain victims of various protection incidents and other human rights violations. Violence is occurring in places of refuge and in areas which are controlled by non-state actors and which are not accessible by humanitarian actors. Continuous displacement and lack of livelihoods has also reduced the capacities of these uprooted communities to access humanitarian and medical aid. • UNHCR will continue to monitor closely all protection incidents occurred within the context of forced displacement in Somalia. By advocating and putting in place response systems, every protection incident should get a referral or direct assistance. Additionally, the information gathered through a thorough monitoring system feeds into the wider system to better analyze main protection incidents in the country and better tailor programming to needs. • Influenced by the HCT’s Centrality of Protection strategy, UNHCR will pilot monitoring in areas which are occupied by armed non-state actors. Especially in South West State and Jubbaland State, individuals will be identified with strong connections in areas which are currently not included in humanitarian programming and assessments. Expected is that these Independent Monitors will feed protection related information to UNHCR and the wider humanitarian community on specific protection needs among these communities.
