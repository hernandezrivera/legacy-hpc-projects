Project Summary

COOPI will implement the project in Kaga (Borno) and Geidam, Machina, Fune, Yunusari and Yusufari LGAs (Yobe), to support vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPS), vulnerable Host Communities and vulnerable returnees to improve food security. The inclusion of all categories (IDPs/Host/Returnees) as targeted beneficiaries will avoid conflicts in the communities as no category of population will be excluded. and the selection will follow vulnerability criteria.
The project will provide assistance to 38,700 individuals meaning around 6,450 households, with food assistance through e-vouchers for a project duration of 12 months. Based on COOPI previous experience on such projects and taking into account the time needed for an effective assistance (including beneficiaries’ selection and closure of the activities) and the will to reach a significant number of beneficiaries, 9 transfers will be conducted during this time frame covering the lean season.
At the same time COOPI will foster resilience of the communities through distribution of agricultural inputs to the most vulnerable households.
Beneficiaries will be trained on climate-resilient agricultural techniques and effective post-harvest storage. COOPI will adapt the approach to the specific agroecosystems of Yobe and Borno State (with a focus on improved soil quality, improved efficiency use of rainwater, and prioritize, in addition to standard rain and soil practices, the elements of use of organic options for fertilizer, and farmer-managed natural forest regeneration and/or tree nurseries).
Beneficiaries will also have greater access to improved seeds and other high-quality inputs via cash-based vouchers. Selected beneficiaries will receive agricultural input cash-based grants for use in buying improved seeds, inputs and tools (where appropriate) in local markets. Arrangements will be made in advance by COOPI with vendors, whose stocks and established presence will be screened and monitored by this proposed project. COOPI will pilot a combination of methods to determine the most effective market intervention. Cash grants will cover the complete cost of improved seeds and high-quality inputs and seek to establish vendors in the marketplace. The outcomes of cash-based action will be to help rebuild input supply markets by partnering with private sector input suppliers and other actors to improve stocking and services to farmers. To restore and build market systems.
The beneficiaries’ selection process will be as following:
1. In order to ensure a relevant targeting of the most vulnerable people, a census of all the population in the targeted area will be done, including household composition and household socio-economic situation.
2. Once this data collected, the selection process will be based on food security criteria, by using indicators such as FCS and CSI.
3. In parallel, in order to ensure a well acceptance of the program and an adaptation to the context of the targeting process, the participants (including IDPs, host communities and community leaders) will be involved through focus groups and committees, in the definition of the socio-economic criteria. The vulnerability criteria will be established in collaboration with the community, through these focus groups, with an emphasis on persons with specific needs. These vulnerability criteria can be: widow, elderly, disabled people, household with no income.
4. Finally, the selection of the most vulnerable households according to the thresholds previously defined will be done. The weighting of the socio economic criteria is done with the community. Past experiences have shown that household headed by widow, disabled and elderly are usually considered as the most vulnerable households by the community.
This community-based approach enables selection of beneficiaries while preventing or limiting frustration from people not included in the final selection.
The vendors are selected through clear criteria: geographic proximity with the communities, capacity and quality of storage, quantity and variety of food items. Vendors will be required to have a bank account and a safe.
The food security component will be linked with COOPI Nutrition activities in order to maximize the outcomes of the project.
COOPI will continue coordinating its activities with national and state authorities, NEMA (National
Emergency Management Agency) and SEMA (State Emergency Management Agency). Regular meetings will be held with local authorities at LGA and state level (including the Local Area coordination meetings) to ensure they are informed about the project activities and progress, implementation methodology, as well as to ensure their involvement in decision making and in the identification of any mitigating actions required. Community Development Officers (CDOs) of each LGA will be directly involved in the project activities. Yobe State Humanitarian Response Committee, as well as Local Government Council (LGA level) will be informed in a timely fashion about the starting of the action and regularly updated on its progress.
Beneficiaries and community leaders involved in previous actions have supported COOPI to identify needs and gaps in the assistance coverage and have been directly involved in the design of the intervention through consultations. Food Management Committees, composed of beneficiary representatives will be engaged before during and after distributions.
The action will adopt a participatory approach involving all the groups of the affected communities,
including community representatives/leadership, in the processes and decisions that affect their lives throughout all the project phases. Beneficiaries' inputs will support the development of an accessible feedback/complaints and response mechanism (CRM) to ensure it is built on their preferences and in full respect of their cultural practices and customs. Special attention is given to the involvement of particularly vulnerable groups and people that may be discriminated and stigmatized based on gender, ethnicity, disability, experienced violence and abuse, ensuring they are not left behind.
Activities are designed to reach a large number of community members/groups: children, adolescents, parents/caregivers that will be supported in providing appropriate care to their children; mothers will take the lead in disseminating nutrition and hygiene messages among themselves and among pregnant women and their community in general; Community Mobilizers will be trained and assisted in providing quality service to the target communities, becoming also a key vehicle to reach out to other community members.
COOPI’s monitoring system will involve all program staff and beneficiaries aiming at: a) assess impact of activities on the lives of beneficiaries, through interviews, focus group discussions, surveys, PDM and observations; b) assess actual program achievements against its planned objectives and indicators; c) identify strengths and weaknesses of individual program activities; d) make changes to programming as needed to increase the quality of services; and e) advocate for unmet needs and gaps. In addition to the Do No Harm and conflict sensitive approach, COOPI will attempt to link this project with on-going protection activities and accountability to affected population-AAP mechanism, including complaint response (set up of complaint desks, complaint boxes, hiring of CRM officers and assistants-half of them will be female).

Needs

The crisis in Nigeria continues to affect the population living in the North-East. According to the HRP 2018 an estimated 7.7 million people, in the most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, are in acute need of protection and assistance. The XXIV round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM - August 2018), estimated the number of IDPs and Returnees to be 3,506,841 (1,926,748 IDPS and 1,580,093 Returnees), 94% of whom have been displaced due to the Boko Haram insurgency in the North East. Food insecurity in North East Nigeria has worsened due to the crisis and conflict in the Region which have led to an alarming number of IDPs, disruption of market supplies and livelihoods, and significantly limiting the populations having access to food. IOM estimated that 60% of the IDPs reside with host communities while only 40% of them reside in camp/camp-like settings. The influx of IDPs into host communities exacerbated a situation of already scarce resources as showed by the last Cadre Harmonisé analysis (March 2018). The CH report shows that food insecurity remains alarming particularly in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States within the two critical CH phases 3-4. According to FEWSNET ’s Market Monitoring Bulletin of May 2018, prices in NE Nigeria are generally higher than other regions as result of insecurity and are expected to remain above average until the revival of main harvest, moreover, populations depend heavily on market purchase, currently since significant improvement in security in Yobe state, many markets are functional with enough supplies. The persisting conflict continues to substantially impact households’ livelihoods in the NE Nigeria. Despite the main seasons harvest coming soon, due to restricted access to land for most households in the NE, harvest will be substantially below average for most of the households. In line with the above context, identified needs by the sector, priorities and objective for 2019, the project seeks to deliver life-saving food assistance to the most vulnerable IDP, returnees and host community households. Although food security outcomes have improved among households receiving food assistance, these improvements are unlikely to be sustained without continued humanitarian food assistance. In parallel, the project aims at fostering resilience of vulnerable communities by providing them with agricultural inputs and training to improve agricultural production.Inclusion and protection will be mainstreamed during all the project implementation phases, from targeting to monitoring and evaluation. Inclusiveness will be addressed within the project activities through direct involvement of all the different segments of the community – including women, girls and other at-risk groups - in targeting, assessing preferences for proposed commodities, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of the CBT programme. Particular attention is given to the analysis of gender-specific food insecurity situation and priority needs and constraints of women and men to access resources. Analysis of the data collected will have a gender perspective, taking into account the division of tasks, access to and control over productive resources and decision-making patterns between women and men at household and community levels. COOPI relies on following key policies to implement its activities:-COOPI, with its existing Protection Policy which outlines COOPI's positioning on Protection and includes theoretical framework on key concepts, international legal framework and main approaches to establishing and developing protection programming-COOPI’s Protection SOPs which describe steps, processes, activities & tools to setting-up a protection program-Child Protection Code of Conduct-COOPI’s adoption of the "UN Protocol on Allegations of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, adopting UNICEF Child Safeguarding and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, UN Protocol on SEA Allegation.
