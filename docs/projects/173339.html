Project Summary

With the fund and technical assistance of AAR Japan, and implemented by NGO Forum for Public Health, two Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) in Alikhali, camp 25 and Jadimura, camp 27, support total number of around 200 boys and girls. CFSs offer child protection service including MHPSS, counseling services, PFA, referral support, non-formal education, awareness raising sessions and group discussion on CP related topics and sports competitions for children in order to promote their psychosocial well-being and to empower children to avoid protection risks in the communities. Community awareness raising on child protection issue are also implemented through awareness sessions and outreach, and parent meetings. If COVID19 pandemic continues, we will run activities on CP and COVID-19 through psychosocial support by MHPSS team over phone, and awareness session with adults including topics such as positive parenting with limited scales and COVID-19 preventive measures taken. During the time engagement of children in CFS needed to be avoided, provision of materials for drawing or toy through parents will be implemented. AAR Japan and NGO Forum are committed to oblige the principle of "do no harm," Child Safeguarding and PSEA policy and COVID19 safety security guidelines and all other government and sector protocols in implementing activities prioritizing the safety and dignity of child. AAR Japan and NGO Forum ensure adequate Complain Response feedback Mechanism through hotline, complain box, and verbal complaint following policy and procedures to provide quality services and ensure accountability.

1 - Identified Needs

Bangladesh hosts about 860,697 (Ref. UNHCR population factsheet as of 31 August 2020) individual Rohingyas from Myanmar who are living 32 congested camps in Cox’s Bazar district. Among them 7,604 and 14,970 individual Rohingyas are living in the camps 25 and 27 in Teknaf upazila respectively. In Teknaf upazila, Rohingyas refugees are living amongst the host community. Rohingya children have limited access to public spaces and thus hindering effective access to basic services including formal education, which continue to impact negatively on the protection environment, contributing to harmful coping mechanisms, including child trafficking for both boys and girls and early marriage. Especially under COVID-19 pandemic, pre-existing gendered risks have been exacerbated. For instance, early, forced and child marriage has been increased due to financial pressure resulting from the containment measures has taken place, and halt in non-formal education ("In the Shadows of the Pandemic", ISCG et al., October 2020) as well as parents are afraid that their daughter can fall victim of a violence or trafficking before marriage. In some camps, Mahji are reported to be involved in early marriage. Thus, awareness raising on such protection risks aiming at children, guardians, religious and community leaders are priorities. Under the impact of COVID19, children are likely to experience the negative impacts on their mental health due to the loss of educational opportunities and the shutting down of safe places ("In the Shadows of the Pandemic", ISCG et al., October 2020), which creates the need for MHPSS and CP support. Thus alternative indoor activities, mental health supports are priorities. In Camps 25 and 27, there are not enough places and opportunities for children to spend during the day safely. All these issues mentioned above have a huge impact on the mental health and psychosocial well-being of the children and creates the need for MHPSS and CP support. The proposed project’s support will offer CFS for children, including MHPSS service, recreational activities, NFE, cultural activity and community capacity building through outreach, awareness session, parent meeting, CBCPCC meeting, positive parenting session for affected Rohingya Refugee Community. All activities will be implemented with aims to reduce protection risk of the children of Rohingya Communities.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Awareness Sessions-To increase the knowledge and to avoid of protection risk such as human trafficking and early marriage, awareness sessions will be conducted with small number of boys, girls and parents at one time with COVID-19 protective measures. In COVID19 pandemic situation, this activity will implement with adults especially on positive parenting and CP risk reduction. 2. MHPSS - MHPSS Support will be provided by PSS Counselors, Clinical psychologist, and skilled volunteers under the supervision of Clinical psychologist through group and individual counseling and over phone including psychosocial first aid, trauma Identification, stress management, anger management, and mediation. This service follows the Sector's relevant principles and procedures. 3. Referral - Maintaining the ‘Do No Harm’ principle internal and external referral will support to meet the beneficiaries actual need. There will be also provision of vehicle support for the referral cases and special care will be taken to maintain the confidentiality of the concerned survivor. 4. NFE - Non-Formal Education (NFE) will be provide to the children such as mathematics and alphabet learning through participatory approach at CFS. In COVID19 pandemic situation, NFE activity will be provided through positive parenting session or providing learning materials by volunteers, which may door to door visit or providing materials to parents at CFS. 5. Cultural activities & sports - This will be provided to develop children's creative, imaginative and social skills through painting, drawing, collage, pasting, finger painting, crafting, posters making, singing, reciting, storytelling, and other recreational activities as well as easy sports competitions. In COVID-19 pandemic, the project will continue support to the children through positive parents and providing drawing materials. 6. Capacity Building - Capacity building of staff and volunteers on protection related issues will be conducted to ensure quality services through training, workshop, on the job coaching and experience sharing etc. Development of the capacity of volunteers will be one of the priorities in the COVID-19 situation and for community engagement. 7. Community Engagement - Monthly CBCPC meeting will held with CiC to maintain close liaison with the concerned government authorities, information sharing on camp situations, CFS services and advocacy the protection mainstreaming. Outdoor awareness sessions focus on Child Protection, Gender and Positive parenting. Several Community watch group will form based on sex, age and power who will support to identify volubility, specific needs and other child protection risk factors in the community and cooperate to resole the problems. 8. Coordinate with Sector and Government: Coordination with Child protection sub-sector, GBV sub-sector, protection working group, district and Upozila administration as well as RRRC office and in the camp level coordination with CiC, other child protection agencies, site management, GoB, protection actors and other stakeholders will maintained with the aim to smoothly achieving the common goal with other actors. 9. Disability Inclusion- The Centers will have ramps for the smooth entrance of Person with disability and will also offer disable friendly WASH facilities in center. The PWD beneficiaries will be referred to the proper service provider with the support of vehicles and ensure the impartial access to assistance and services. 10. Host Community and Social Cohesion- The project will run some social cohesion activities in host communities which are adjacent to Camp 25 & 27 focusing four key components 1) social relationships, 2) connectedness, 3) orientation towards child protection and parenting 4) equity. 11. Recreational Activities for the children of Host Community - Learning, drawing, recreational session/event with appropriate materials and toys will organize for the host community children.
