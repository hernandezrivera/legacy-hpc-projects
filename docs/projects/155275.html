Project Summary

CARE proposes a cash transfer based project to support 10,000 vulnerable hurricane-affected households through a food security intervention to meet their urgent needs while strengthening their livelihoods.
The project overall goal will be achieved through the following strategic objectives:
1. Critical and immediate basic needs and economic recovery of the poor households will be addressed and fostered through cash for work activities
2. Improve community assets (physical, social, human and institutional) for future resilience and economic recovery through community directed public works.
Indicators and targets:
Indicator I -To address critical and immediate basic needs and foster economic recovery Access to basic needs for 10,000 households through cash transfer and cash for work.
Indicator II – A monthly $60 cash-transfer is made to the 10,000 households in the Grand’Anse department
Indicator III – 5,500 household-heads participate in cash for work activities

Needs

A report published by the national food security coordination (CNSA) on August 8th, 2018, reveals that seven (7) among the 10 geographic departments – including the Grand’Anse department – were severely affected by a drought that expects to last until the second quarter of fiscal year 2019. On top of that, since July 2018, political turmoil has contributed to handicap the economic activities of the rural households that depend mainly on day-to-day transactions over the local markets. These dire situations contribute to exacerbate food insecurity among the most vulnerable and poor households – as they have negatively impacted the socio-economic conditions of the most unfortunate. With regards to the Grand’Anse department – which is still in the process of recovering – it is worth reflecting back on what happened a couple of years ago. Hurricane Matthew, which made landfall in Haiti on October 4, 2016, had a devastating impact in the South and Grand Anse departments, which have been the most affected areas of the Southern peninsula by this category 4 hurricane. Mathew inflicted damage and losses in Haiti estimated at the equivalent of 22 percent of GDP (World Bank October 2017). According to the result of the Synthesis of the HNO analysis 982,650 people are in need of urgent food assistance. The loss of livelihoods has resulted in increased vulnerability of the population in the southern peninsula particularly through the inability to produce or purchase adequate and appropriate food. Economic vulnerability and limited capacity/resources to manage food shocks lead to food insecurity and extreme disruption of livelihood assets lead to large consumption gaps in the short term.Based upon the aforementioned elements, CARE proposes an intervention that will enable alleviation to 10,0000 vulnerable households in the department of Grand’Anse. Let us note that CARE’s proposed intervention aligns perfectly with GoH sectoral programs and policies and is based on household identified needs. It will not only support the most vulnerable to meet their basic and urgent needs; but will also contribute to strengthen and/or reinforce livelihoods.

Activities or outputs

The main activities will be revolved around cash transfer to support households affected by the ongoing drought and Hurricane Matthew in Grand Anse that are currently in the process of recovering from the last natural shocks. Initially, CARE proposes a six-month cash-based response in the Grand’Anse department. Monetary assistance has been proven in times of emergency to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of humanitarian assistance. As such, it will be a key element in planning this action to ensure access to vital needs. The use of cash transfers will allow aid to reach targeted beneficiaries directly while preserving their dignity and giving them the freedom of choice to define and prioritize their basic needs (primarily food and water). And it will also help them prepare themselves for the upcoming cyclonic season. As member of the CBTWG, CARE will prioritize the harmonized cash strategy developed with Cash Based Transfer Working Group (CBTWG). The individual monthly cash transfer per beneficiary is set at $60/month as per the cash based project implemented during the response to Matthew. The amount is based on the standards set by the World Food Program (WFP) which represents 80% of the national food basket – as defined by the national food security coordination (CNSA) – and has the same value as the in-kind food support. In addition, CARE will conduct, market analysis and monitoring to assess their ability to provide basic goods and services to the vulnerable population. Then CARE will conduct a ten-day cash for work activities for 5,500 households among those who receive the cash transfer. The standard CFW component will focus on stimulating recovery of the local economy by creating short-term labor opportunities, income flow, support of local business through purchase of materials and encouraging households to purchase food and non-food items according to their needs. CFW will also help rehabilitate damaged communities and community assets, like water supply systems, fields, schools, and roads. Non-physical social work activities like hygiene and health promotion will also make up CFW activities to ensure all eligible beneficiaries are able to participate. Under the proposed intervention, cash-for-work activities will be scheduled so that participants have sufficient time to prioritize their agricultural activities (for example, cash-for-work work days scheduled every other day or every three days). Households will be selected based on the following criteria: households affected by drought and hurricane Matthew, household with 2 children under 5 years old; someone with reduced mobility; pregnant or nursing woman; female or teenage head of household; isolated heads of households, including a member who disappeared during the cyclone; loss of livelihood, whose house has been partially destroyed The project overall goal will be achieved through the following strategic objectives: 1.
