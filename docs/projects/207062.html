Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to emergency water supply, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services for conflict affected and vulnerable host communities in the South West region of Cameroon. Recurring violence continue to fuel instability in the North West and South West leading to large-scale displacements, aggravating WASH vulnerabilities.
As of February 2023, close to 70,000 persons are estimated to be internally displaced while almost 90,000 have returned to their habitual place of residence, as per the multi-sectoral needs assessment (IOM MSNA, February 2023). The use of unsafe drinking water sources is considered the most likely cause of repeated cholera outbreaks, according to an August 2023 OCHA report. Limited access to clean water and poor sanitation services have accelerated the spread of water-borne diseases like cholera, which remains highly contagious and potentially fatal.
This project will target 35,000 conflict-affected people with sustainable and gender-sensitive WASH services. It will prioritize reestablishing damaged facilities and extending supply systems in IDP/returnee locations and strategic urban areas across Ndian, Meme and Kupe-Manenguba Divisions. Focus will be on providing equitable services to women and girls and persons living with disabilities.
Key activities will include:
 Rehabilitating damaged water points and extending supply systems with new distribution connections to increase availability and access to safe drinking water. This includes in harnessing of springs, rehabilitating water catchments and or extending existing community piped water supply system, such as construction of stand taps in areas with high population density, which provide adequate quantity of portable water. This will help eliminate dependence on contaminated sources and cut disease transmission.
 Distributing critical WASH Kits like soaps, menstrual hygiene supplies, and water purification tablets. These basics supplies improve health and hygiene practices while reducing the risk of poor hygiene-related morbidity and mortality. Provision of Menstrual Hygiene Management ( MHM) Kits to Women and Girls of menstrual age will be imperative in order to prevent them from engaging in negative coping mechanism to provide for their needs.
 Constructing emergency communal latrines and handwashing facilities for displaced groups to largely reduce open defecation. This is crucial to interrupt disease spread and ensure safety and dignity, especially for women and girls in rural areas and clustered urban neigborhoods.
 Conducting hygiene promotion and capacity building to sustain services beyond the project's lifetime. This aims to spur behavioral change and community ownership for long-term impact.
The recent needs assessment conducted across conflict-affected communities of the South West highlighted alarming gaps in water, sanitation and hygiene services faced by both displaced and host communities. Therefore the project will have an important impact on the safety, health and wellbeing of women and girls in the target communities. Improved access to water and sanitation facilities is vital to ensure dignity, privacy and security for females. The provision of menstrual hygiene items is also key to enabling adolescent girls and women to manage their periods in a healthy way. Overall, enhanced WASH services will cut water-borne disease rates and decrease preventable illnesses and deaths. This will benefit the wider population, spurring productivity and human capital gains.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The humanitarian needs among the population in Cameroon will continue to escalate following displacement of populations due to floods, socio political crisis, inter-community conflicts, poverty, and cholera outbreaks. (HRP 2023) as it the case with communities in Konye, Ndian and Kupe-Manenguba. Based on multisector needs assessments carried out in the Far North, the North-West, South-West, Littoral and West regions, coupled with the monitoring of access to water, hygiene and sanitation services, it is estimated that 1.9 million people in Cameroon will need humanitarian assistance in water, sanitation and hygiene (HNO 2023) which necessitate the implementation of this project.Access to safe drinking water was found to be a critical challenge, as limited access to clean water and poor sanitation services are among the factors that have contributed to the rapid spread of diseases with only a fraction of the population able to secure adequate quantities of save drinking water. Priority should be given to actions aimed at improving people's access to safe drinking water, proper sanitation, and hygiene (OCHA August 2023 Situation Report) as One-fifth of rural communities in Meme, Ndian and Kupe-Manenguba rely solely on unimproved sources like rivers and streams, escalating the risk of water-borne diseases. In urban areas, there are shortages owing to increased population due to displacements from unsafe areas. According to Caritas Kumba field reports, supply infrastructure damage, insufficient water points to meet needs and long distances were identified as factors restricting clean water access, which disproportionately affects vulnerable groups. The recurrent cholera outbreaks testify to the urgent need for improved water infrastructure, and eliminating dependence on unsafe sources.The sanitation situation was equally found to be dire, with limited access to clean, private toilet facilities especially in rural communities and crowded areas in urban towns. Though many use latrines for defecation, available facilities often do not ensure privacy or disability access. This drives open defecation, which worsens public health issues, especially for women and girls. Constructing adequate, separate sanitation facilities is vital to end this practice, as the inaction of humanitarian funding for the WASH Sector may lead to a national cholera outbreak, and increased displacement of affected population (HRP 2023)Severely inadequate hygiene practices also pose risks, as hygiene item ownership is low across the board. Few people have relevant items to maintain proper hygiene. Menstrual hygiene remains a key concern, with the high cost of supplies deterring purchase by women and girls, which has further been aggravated by the rise of basic household commodities. This underscores the need to distribute basic hygiene kits.Finally, while infrastructure and supplies will provide immediate WASH access, entrenched behavioral change is pivotal for long-term impact on health. As such, awareness creation via hygiene promotion and community capacity building were highlighted as critical to enable continuation of services beyond the project lifetime.The integrated WASH activities of this project directly address these assessed deficiencies in water access, sanitation, hygiene practices and awareness by building inclusive infrastructure, distributing dignity kits and driving community mobilization. Prioritizing the needs of groups like women and persons with disabilities will promote an equitable, humanitarian response.
