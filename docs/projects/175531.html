Project Summary

The ongoing instabilities in most parts of South Sudan, combined with effects of floods and lack of food security and livelihood opportunities result in the displacement of communities and the economic downturn and have put a further strain on already limited WASH services.
PAH is a very experienced organization with broad portfolio of successfully implemented projects in various sectors. PAH will use is strongest expertise in WASH to provide assistance to vulnerable communities across the country by providing accessing to water, improving and sanitation both as single approach as well as in multi-sectoral responses PAH always seeks the synergies with other actions, as WASH sector covers the needs crucial for other aspects of life too.
PAH will mainly target people facing high level of WASH vulnerability, those in IPC levels 3, 4 and 5, displaced or returnees and host communities with over-stretched resources. PAH will focus on rehabilitation and building water sources in institutions and those prioritized by community members. Sanitation will be provided on institutional level. PAH provides hygiene promotion, combined with protection, health, nutrition elements, with cross-sectoral topics adjusted to the needs of the groups. This capacity building is always based on community members chosen to be promoters, water user committees and other engaged people within the community. PAH holds itself accountable to affected population and ensures its ownership of project results.
Recognizing the cross-cutting nature of WASH, PAH combine the described activities with other sectors by providing dignity kits and NFIs, PAH works to improve protection status of women and girls. While improving access to water PAH takes care of irrigation needs for farmers and water for livestock, which contributes to improvement of food production and contain elements of diseases prevention. By improving sanitation and water access in nutrition sites and schools, PAH contributes to work in those sectors.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planing the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.

1 - Needs

The ongoing instabilities in most parts of the country cause the displacement of communities and the economic downturn, both have put a further strain on already limited WASH services. Conflicts (like in Tonj or Pibor) and extremely high levels of violence cause displacements, loose of assets and livelihoods, that result in high reliance on humanitarian assistance. Another trigger for rising food insecurity was increased inflation across the country due to Covid-19 restrictions in transport. Large-scale flooding for two consecutive years resulted in low yields and deterioration of food security, access to water and hygiene. Floods in 2020 had affected over one million people in 47 counties with an estimated 485,000 people being displaced from their homes. Given the congestion of flood-displaced populations in high ground areas and heavy contamination of the safe water sources in Jonglei and Pibor Administrative Area, preventive oral cholera vaccination campaigns were planned for Bor and Pibor targeting respectively 72,750 individuals and 93,250 individuals aged one year and above. The first round of the oral cholera vaccination campaign was implemented in Bor from 16th to 29th December 2020 while the campaign in Pibor was slated to start on 15 January 2021. Cholera alerts were called in areas close to the border with Ethiopia - in Longochuk, Maiwut, Nasir, Ulang, Akobo and Pochalla. In Pibor the measles outbreak was observed in the last quarter of 2020. The prevalence of open defecation is over 40 per cent in IDP and returnee settlements across all ten states, with over eighty per cent of IDPs and returnees in Warrap, Unity, Jonglei and Upper Nile living in settlements with evidence of it. The same applies to IDPs only in Eastern Equatoria, Lakes and Western Equatoria, and to returnees only in Northern Bahr El Ghazal. As mass displacement across the country affects the majority of the population, the WASH situation assessments gave alarming results. 42.4 per cent of IDPs and 48 per cent of returnees live in settlements where the main water source is further than 20 minutes away on foot (one way), while 32.7 per cent of IDPs and 35.8 per cent of returnees live in settlements reporting water unfit for human drinking. 20.3 per cent of IDPs, or 286,634 individuals, and 27.9 per cent of returnees, or 383,957 individuals, live in settlements where people feel unsafe when they go to collect water. In IPC projection for December 2020 to March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Pibor County, in Pibor Administrative Area. Later in 2021, the situation is projected to worsen - 60% of the population are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. An estimated 2.47 million (20,5% of the population) are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. 31,000 people are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people in Akobo County in Jonglei State, 7,000 people in Aweil South County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, and 13,000 people in Tonj North County in Warrap State. The efforts to improve food security and nutrition have to be accompanied with comprehensive WASH services to ensure a healthy and safe environment for food production, storage, processing and intake. According to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix, 89.7% of IDPs and 90.6% of returnees in host-community settings live in settlements without gender-separated latrines, compared to 30.8 per cent of IDPs living in camps and camp-like settings. Toilets and bathrooms that can be locked from the inside are not available to 90.8 per cent of IDPs and 87.3 per cent of returnees in host-community settings. In comparison, 70.5 per cent of IDPs in camps or camp-like settings do not have access to locked latrines.

2 - Project activities

1. Rehabilitation of existing water points Consideration will be given to water points at institutions (nutrition sites and schools). In grazing areas water points will be designed in a manner to ensure safety for people and animals. During rehabilitation in flood prone areas, the aprons of the water points will be raised to avoid inflow of contaminated water from floods. In areas with high food insecurity PAH will assess the irrigation practices and possibilities and may give instructions and encourage people for sustainable and safe solutions for watering the crops. This will provide synergy between WASH and food access. PAH will target water points at the community level. 2. Establishment of new water points. New water points development may be considered in case of communities, whose water points are overcrowded, there is no water point, however the community is willing to stay in this place. New water points will also be established in permanent/semi-permanent IDP location depending on the need and population. The water points may be developed/upgraded to solar powered water yards. For both new and rehabilitated points the water user committees will be trained and equipped to ensure sustainability and maintenance cost will be instituted. Pump mechanics will be trained locally to ensure urgent repairs in case of break downs.. The water points will be raised to avoid inflow of contaminated water from floods.. 3. Provision of solar lamps, dignity/hygiene kits and soap will be ensured for those most at risk of GBV. PAH will provide vouchers for soap which will cover three months’ supply. Where possible, the cash based intervention modality will be implemented. Depending on availability, items will be procured from the local markets in the project locations. The market and risks assessment will be conducted to later decide if vouchers modality can be used. 4. Community awareness on hygiene, sanitation, domestic hygiene, COVID19 mitigation etc. will be delivered to the community at the HH level, including monitoring of implementing the measures, with special focus on mother and care-taker of severely malnourished child. For sustainability of water treatment, local approaches like boiling, SODIS, filtration using clean cloths which can be locally available and use of local coagulants such as muringa will be encouraged PAH will provide protection training and awareness raising to community hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committees’ members, women groups and others.. PAH will work closely with FSL partners and/or combine the project with FSL activities, if possible. 5. Distribution of WASH NFIs. PAH, relying on core pipeline and local market for cash voucher assistance will distribute core WASH NFIs. ensuring meaningful access - separate queues for elders, disable and pregnant women. Carriers will be considered for people with limited mobility. PAH will ensure the distribution will be safe in terms of Covid-19 protection. 6. PAH will consider construction of new or improving existing sanitation facilities at local institutions, at crowded settings. Fixing the structures with proper doors and inside locks will enable privacy and mitigate risk of GBV.. For a sustainable sanitation approach in both IDP, host community and returnee settings, PAH will form and train sanitation committees who will be selected from across the villages. The provision of the sanitation kits will be accompanied with awareness on the importance of latrine use, the dangers of open defecation etc. In institutions, PAH will establish gender and disability segregated sanitation facilities. The sanitation facilities will be handed over to the institutions management and monitored to ensure sustainability. 7. PAH will conduct WASH assessments with protection analysis using risk equation. in cooperation with clusters and NGOs to provide platform for gap analysis and needs.

3 - Project indicators

MEAL plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. PAH MEAL team will support the development of common data collection methodologies and take part in joint needs assessments to ensure common standards. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings are incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. PAH will utilise sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be:# of water points rehabilitated at nutrition sites and schools; # of institutions with functioning, safe and dignified sanitation facilities; # of people (disaggregated by sex and age) received WASH related NFI, including dignity kits; # of people trained and sensitized thanks to hygiene promotion or protection combined with other cross-sectoral topics. Despite of quantitative indicators, PAH will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. PAH conducts periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, including clusters.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by PAH officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project - volunteers.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, PAH will try to adjust mechanism paths. In case of beneficiaries with disabilities, with limited mobility, PAH will visit households with the help of the hygiene promoters to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, community hygiene promoters, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time PAH and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. As the GBV cases are often unreported, PAH officers will engage the caseworkers from the community and will be present in the community as often as possible to build trust and encourage effective psychosocial support. TOR for all activities will be presented to the authorities, plans are discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based hygiene promoters, water user commitees' members, translators, enumerators. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, PAH staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge. To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback is kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. The implementing programme team, under supervision of Senior Management, if necessary, conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. For example, eligibility criteria for identifying beneficiaries for WASH NFI distribution will be used and the following categories of beneficiaries will be considered: people in areas of IPC 4 and 5 and WASH vulnerability 4 and 5; female-headed households, elderly-headed households, child-headed households, unaccompanied children, people with less or no community link, people living with disabilities, elderly and people with chronic sicknesses, pregnant and lactating women with little support, disease-affected population, children and women, responsible for fetching water. During the registration for distribution, PAH will endure door to door registration to ensure that even those who are not able to come to the registration points are registered including their vulnerability status. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. PAH will ensure that decisions pertaining location of WASH facilities are made in consultation with women and girls. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up During registration/distribution, PAH do registration from household to household and will ensure that order is maintained by involving community leaders, crowed controllers and creating separate ques for men and women, children and different categories of people. The protectionProtection risk assessment will be conducted before deciding the distribution site to ensure that distribution sites are safe and accessible to the beneficiaries. PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear age gender and disability (AGD) data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. PAH will achieve it through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, the host community and returnee population. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation PAH can avoid aid diversion, ensure ownership of project results and involvement of the community in monitoring and control of the results. PAH will coordinate closely with partners of different sectors or will put efforts to join multi-sectoral responses to ensure synergies and comprehensive responses. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, including logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored continuously. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information, between others about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, local regulations that may try to include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards. Protection assessments and analysis capturing information on access, safety, dignity, participation and power dynamics will be the key methodology where needs are analysed, prioritized with clear recommendations from the beneficiaries

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

WASH services, as planned in the proposed project has some, however not a direct and significant influence on the provision of protection services. Mainly, thanks to community mobilization, PAH officers, water user committees, hygiene promoters can spread the protection messaging and encourage seeking for help. PAH can provide a referral if there is protection partner in the area.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The household safe, hygienic environment and safely prepared food with clean water, sanitation and healthy daily practices in terms of hand-washing may significantly prevent malnutrition in the family. In the case of domestic food production, storage and sales on the local market, access to water, soap and the proper hygienic practices are crucial for safety of the food.Moreover, if the safe water access is ensured, women can limit the time used for collecting water and take care of livelihood activities instead.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The access to water, sanitation and hygiene is one of the basic services, allowing dignified and safe life. As described in the proposal, PAH ensures the equitable access and inclusion of all community groups, by various means. While implementing WASH activities, PAH works strongly to build future resilience and basis for the long-term development of the communities, minimizing exclusion, empowering the vulnerable groups and encouraging ownership of the project results.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The malnutrition of children can have many reasons and influencing factors. One of them is the access to safe, clean water, safe food and hygiene, that prevents the spread of diseases both for children and care-givers, including lactating mothers. The household safe, hygienic environment and safely prepared food with clean water, sanitation and healthy daily practices in terms of hand-washing may significantly prevent malnutrition and enable correct grow of infants and children. It also supports the treatment in case of family members provided with supplementary feeding and diseases.
