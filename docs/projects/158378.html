Project Summary

This emergency response project will provide cash assistance to 374 households of the communities of Ogossagou Peulh, Ogossagou Dogon, Guiwagou and Wélingara for a three month period to enable surviving households of the Ogossagou massacre and surrounding affected communities to cover their basic needs and survive the onset of rainy season. The massacre of Ogossagou took place on 23 March 2019, and saw 161 people killed, 410 houses burnt, 80 granaries destroyed and large amounts of village livestock carried away. 374 households (2,244 people) were seriously affected including IDPs, returnees, village, neighbor and host communities) and are living in dire conditions. Needs include immediate need for shelter and NFIs, health and nutrition, food security, rebuilding of livelihoods, protection, and education. The project will allow the most affected households cover their basic needs while waiting for specialized agencies to intervene, subject to a detailed needs assessment and available funding.
The project will also benefit 186 female-headed households, and in some cases households of up to 14 people due to the skewed demographics and victims of the massacre. The immediate survivors and surrounding population are in an extremely vulnerable situation, subject to worsening security conditions, ongoing exodus of people in need not able to access relief as well as complete obliteration of livelihood means and income-generating activities.

Needs

DCA and its partners conducted a Needs Assessment in Ogossagou and Guiyagou and Djamnati. The teams met and discussed with the survivors, using direct observation, discussions with authorities and partners, focus-group discussions and individual interviews with groups of women, youth (girls and boys) and men. Immediate needs where identified within:• Shelter and NFIs: immediate reconstruction at the start of rainy season• WASH: insufficient water access points for communities, as well as constant risk of contamination.• Food security/nutrition: In May, population shows signs of malnutrition, and distribution of food rations are not sufficient nor long-term sustainable to build resilience.• Health: Security impedes both health workers and population movement, with devastating effects on vulnerable population, especially children, elderly and pregnant women.• Livelihoods: economic activities halted or weakened, especially young men at risk of negative coping mechanisms with the further potential of fueling the conflict.This project will address the immediate needs of affected households through the provision of cash to households through mobile transfers to provide immediate emergency compensation for the loss of livelihoods, housing and household items, as well as provide meagre financial assets to use for immediate needs.

Activities

This emergency response project will support and complement immediate activities related to food security and nutrition (WFP and authorities) through cash-based support to households in need through mobile transfer. Each head of household has been equipped with a mobile phone and sim card and will throughout the lifetime of the project receive three cash transfers of FCFA 40,000 each. The project will undertake a market and baseline study, set-up of beneficiary committee for selection of beneficiary households, procurement of mobile phones and sim cards for 374 head of households, three cash transfers, coordination with other emergency response projects, community liaison and support with referrals to health and protection needs.
