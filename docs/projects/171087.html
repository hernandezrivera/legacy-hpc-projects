Project Summary

Proposed lifesaving and life sustaining interventions include provision of Unconditional / Multipurpose, conditional (Knowledge transfer focusing on improved nutrition and COVID control), asset building through cash for work initiatives, improved agricultural production (cereals and vegetables) and provision of fodder to pastoralist and agro-pastoralist particularly in desert locust affected communities. These initiatives will not only help beneﬁciaries to deal with the negative coping strategies on immediate basis rather will also help communities to protect their productive assets and recover from shocks through improved asset building, enhanced capacities and strengthened linkages. Livelihoods and Food Security will continue to engage with other actors including humanitarian agencies working in the areas, public / private sector institutions and local authorities to promote cash programming for synergized support.
NRC has already signed framework agreements in place with the Telecom companies in each of the operational areas for electronic transfers. This has been learnt that electronic transfers are swift, safe and ensure upholding dignity of the beneﬁciaries.
Needs assessments will be conducted in the disaster hit areas to devise and implement lifesaving (UCT / MPCA and or conditional transfers) and life sustaining initiatives. These programs will not only help communities to manage immediate needs rather will also help affected communities in dealing with the adopted negative coping strategies and will enable them to protect their assets and improve knowledge base and assets efficiency.
Over the period of times, NRC has learnt through experience and adopted localization approach in operational areas that complementing integrated programs funded by various donors help to deal with multiple needs of the communities. Therefore, identiﬁed and registered HHs Where appropriate will beneﬁt from a companion program for agriculture production, diversiﬁed livelihoods and awareness raising around good nutrition sensitive intervention. These companion programs will ensure that poorest HHs have improved capacities and are able to access food and livelihoods options available in the market. Small farmers will be provided with agricultural inputs for improved agricultural production and access to nutritious horticultural crops. NRC will make sure that seasonal calendar is adopted during devising interventions so that maximum community members could participate and beneﬁt. NRC will also be devising and implementing cash for work programs wherever appropriate. CFW initiatives will be identiﬁed in close collaboration of communities and local authorities; usually these initiatives include establishment or rehabilitation of irrigation channel, water harvesting structures, protection walls and or repair / establishment of farm to market roads. initiative will not only provide labor opportunities to the casual laborers rather will also help beneficiaries to sustain shock, protect and conserve natural resources asset base. Neediest villages will be identiﬁed in close collaboration with cluster and other key stake holders in the area. Furthermore, there are anticipations that there will be another attack of Desert locust. Therefore, NRC plans to provide fodder to pastoralist and agro – pastoralist communities on the move so that they are able to manage their livestock during the acute phase of the DS attack.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfer enables individuals, households and business communities to buy what they need, when they need it, to invest in businesses and or pay off debts. This has been observed over the period of times that cash transfer has been swift, cost effective and reached intended beneficiaries without any compromise on security and or exposing them to protection risks. At the same time, cash and loans help to reinvigorate micro and small businesses within program targeted areas and nearby markets. This not only stimulates markets rather ensures increased availability of goods and services to the whole population, including those who don’t get cash assistance. Unlike traditional distributions, the benefits are multiple and multi-dimensional, benefiting the people, public and private sectors. Cash transfers have also been shown to increase people’s food security and consumption due to increased purchasing of food using the cash transfer. Cash transfers have been seen to have advantages over food aid specifically due to the inefficiency of delivering food and the opportunity for beneficiaries to actually purchase the food they enjoy eating, and have been shown to be successful in assisting those who are most vulnerable in the community. The humanitarian sector must not forget the lessons learned from previous crises: where we repeatedly called for the need for integrated multi-sector responses that pull together immediate, medium and long term needs for more impactful programming. Cash should therefore be provided in a more complementary manner, using approaches that use, build and support existing systems; strengthening and preparing them for future crisis, and enabling resilience to develop within those systems. The mobile money system makes the cash transfer program incredibly efficient in transferring money to beneficiaries upholding their dignity and protection.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Poor, vulnerable and displacement affected households adopt negative coping strategies and are left without access to sufficient nutritious food or clean water, and are therefore at risk of asset depletion, destitution, disease and malnutrition. NRC has already planned market needs assessments to provide insights into market functions, demand and supply conditions including labour market as well as changes in essential food commodity prices in the wake of the COVID-19 outbreak, recent locust invasions and other adverse climatic conditions in various parts of Somalia.

Mitigation Measures

Protection of civilians: In a rural context, communities or groups are either forced intotemporary displacement because of climatic shocks or conflict, or might join urbancenters in search of better access to humanitarian aid (pull factor), access to servicesand economic opportunities. During slow onset and long-tail crisis such as therecurrent drought and conflict, the same communities may partially displace toneighboring towns. Since all the Shelter interventions will be channeled through clusterapproach and involvement of relevant local authorities, thus the impact of armedconflict and violence will be mitigated through proper coordination, advocacy atnational and international level, media and referral mechanisms. Exclusion andmarginalization: The Federal Government of Somalia has committed to women’sempowerment and eradicating violence against women, recognizing that withoutinstitutionalizing the equal rights of women, the country will never be able to stabilizeand develop in a sustainable way. Somali women and girls continue to bear anunequal burden of the hardships occasioned by poverty, conflict and clan-basedculture, which promotes strict male hierarchy and authority. This is further exacerbatedby cultural limitations on the role and status of women in Somali society. Women withdisabilities are often marginalized on account of their disability and their gender. Theymay be more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion, and often have limited social,political and economic opportunities and lack of access to basic services. Women withdisabilities may also be at greater risk of sexual and physical violence and abuse. Girlswith disabilities often experience discrimination, for example in education and familylife. This project will ensure engagement of people, especially women with disabilities,and the elderly at the co management level and will ensure that programsinterventions do not generate negative side-effects. The beneficiary selection will beconducted in a participatory approach with the community leaders and the localauthorities that will identify the most vulnerable people. Women and Child headedhouseholds will be given special priority. Exclusion and marginalization: Targetedassistance will be provided to the most vulnerable groups including women, girls andboy. Beneficiaries will be selected through a community-based approach, which willidentify the most vulnerable based on certain criteria as female or child-headedhouseholds, elderly, disabled, large families and the chronically ill. The beneficiaryselection will be conducted in a participatory approach with the community leadersand the local authorities that will identify the most vulnerable people. Women, girls,boys and Child headed households will be given special priority. Protectionmainstreaming: Protection mainstreaming will be done in all shelter interventions. Forthe recipients of the transitional shelters, at least communal title deed will be providedto avoid any eviction. Local authorities and settlement leaders will be involved in theprocess of title deeds, who will serve as witness in case of any dispute.
