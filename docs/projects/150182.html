Needs

For more than eleven years of blockade on the Gaza Strip and the frequent outbreaks of hostilities the basic infrastructure, service delivery, livelihoods were harmfully affected and livelihoods and coping mechanisms almost eroded. Lack of electricity (currently 5hrs/day) has severely affected health services and other community services and facilities as well as the agricultural production. The increased power shortages have further undermined economic activities, particularly the manufacturing and agriculture sectors, with some 30,000 out of 80,000 dunums planted with seasonal vegetable crops at risk due to irregular irrigation. Dairy cattle farmers and poultry breeders do face significant losses (OCHA, August 2017). The lack of financial capacity represents a significant barrier to small farmers in resuming their livelihood, and they are unable to afford for the costs of agricultural (seeds, fertilizers, irrigation nets, tools, etc.). The findings of a Household Expenditure and Consumption Survey, released by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) in May, show a significant increase in poverty rates in the Gaza Strip: from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent by the end of 2017, equivalent to around 1.01 million people, including more than 400,000 children. This means that poverty increased by more than 14 percentage points in a period of six years (OCHA June 2018). Further, the restrictions on movement and access imposed by Israeli authorities, especially in the ARAs continue to undermine the livelihoods of tens of thousands of Palestinians in Gaza, including fishermen, farmers and herders, moreover, entry into newly accessible areas remains precarious. Agriculture dependent communities in the Access Restricted Areas (ARA) are particularly vulnerable to food insecurity with imminent needs to secure their livelihood and lessen the impact of the restrictions, in addition stabilization of livelihoods has been dramatically slow and has increased dependency on short term assistance and high costs associated in resuming production of food. In the agricultural sector, women's participation is about 13.1% (compared to 7.8% for men) although many times unaccounted for and unpaid. However, their role and production capacities is often not recognised or supported. Women have traditionally played an essential and active role in small-scale, urban agriculture. Seemingly, many women enter the agriculture sector because there is no other choice (for work/for survival). Moreover, women’s access to agricultural input is much limited as it is usually the ‘men’s job’ to communicate with the outside world. Officially, 11% of the Palestinian households are headed by women (widows and divorcees). According to the World Food Programme, “One third, of households headed by women are food insecure, compared to a quarter of households headed by men.” Therefore, the project will prioritize female-headed households. In order for the beneficiaries to have impact on the planning of the activities, to analyse the needs and to give inputs to the project design a participatory approach will be applied and participation of women in the decision making processes and design will be specifically addressed in this project.

Activities

The Project output is: “The agricultural production capacity of 680 small-scale farmers/ households in the Gaza Strip areas affected by the last war is restored”. This intervention address both the North and the Gaza governorates, and targets small-scale farmers and female-headed households who are unable to resume agriculture work, due to lack of access and affordability of the key agricultural production inputs. The project is designed to increase the target groups’ resilience through providing the means to the farmers to resume their livelihood. The strategy focuses on developing the households’ productive assets that would improve household food self-sufficiency and potential income. The resulting income would enable households to address immediate and essential food and nutrition needs, hence decreasing their food insecurity. The program will provide agricultural inputs to the targeted beneficiaries to be able produce in the cropping season. This will lead to improve production, increase the average income from agriculture while enhancing the purchasing power for the most vulnerable population affected by food shortages. During the implementation of the project activities, beneficiaries’ safety and dignity will be fully considered. Besides, NPA will avoid causing harm or any unintended negative effects of the proposed intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to risks. Further, NPA will tackle any potential barriers which would affect beneficiaries’ access to project services. Furthermore, NPA partners will set up an accountability system to address beneficiaries’ concerns and complaints. NPA will ensure active community involvement in the design, delivery, monitoring and evaluation of the project. Moreover, NPA and local partners will consult with local community leaders and Community Based Organizations as well as project beneficiaries including women and female-headed households. NPA will coordinate with other International NGOs to ensure that the project does not overlap with other interventions and will cross check with MoA and the FSS as well as other international actors. Local partners will be responsible for the coordination with local authorities and organizations working in the same field. Activities, which will be implemented by NPA partners, shall be fully coordinated with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA). Activity 1: distribution of Input voucher scheme 1.1: distribute 1360 input vouchers for 680 small-scale farmers and rural women to support livelihood recovery and restoration of productive assets. The small-scale farmer will benefit from vouchers for exchange of agriculture inputs (improved seeds, seedlings, fertilizers, irrigation tools, farm tools etc.). Activity 2: Input voucher redemption: Voucher can be redeemed for a pre identi ed list of agricultural inputs, including seedlings and crop seeds; tools such as, hoes, shovels; organic fertilizer, pipes and tanks for irrigation networks; and metal fencing. 2.1: 680 small-scale farmers and female-headed households redeem vouchers for essential seeds and farm tools (seed and fertilizer, etc.)

Indicators

Activity 1: Indicator/Target 1: 680 small-scale farmers and female-headed households received input vouchers to support their livelihoods recovery and restoration of productive assets; Indicator/Target 2: 70% of beneficiaries that report satisfaction with input voucher scheme. Activity 2: Indicator/Target 1: 680 small-scale farmers and female-headed households redeem vouchers for essential seeds and farm tools (seed and fertilizer, etc.); Indicator/Target 2: 680 dunums made productive or with improved production capacity Indicator/Target 3: 1360 voucher redeemed by 680 beneficiaries (560 male, 120 female) Indicator/Target 4: 95% of vouchers redeemed for essential seeds, tools and other agricultural inputs; Indicator/Target 5: 70% of small-scale farmers and female-headed households are able to benefit from the current planting season; and Indicator/Target 6: 60% of beneficiaries report satisfaction with seed and agricultural input selection and quality. In the first stage of the project implementation, NPA partners will conduct a baseline survey to map the situation before project activities commence and provide the data for indicators to monitor change. The monitoring process is designed to continuously assess the appropriateness of the project interventions, propose corrective measures and support learning and evaluation. A monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning (MEAL) plan will be designed at the preparation phase of the project. NPA's field staff will be engaged in household visits, community mobilisation and orientation sessions, shop monitoring, and beneficiary redemption monitoring. Progress against agreed upon benchmarks and milestones would be measured and reported on in the partners monthly progress reports, submitted to the NPA. Final progress, financial and audit reports describe the activities implemented, their objectives, participants, results obtained (outputs and outcomes), challenges encountered and how these were managed, lessons learnt and recommendations will be prepared and delivered to the donor. The Project monitoring plan includes gender specific indicators and will identify which project benefits specifically accrue to women. All quantitative data to be collected will be disaggregated by gender and age. Monitoring will be at two levels, including adequate monitoring tools: 1. Monitoring implementation in the field: day to day monitoring of activities is the responsibility of partner field staff. This will include shop monitoring, prices monitoring, beneficiary redemption monitoring, transfer of payments, and overall compliance. 2. Monitoring Food Security indicators, socioeconomic indicators and general project impact on the people participating is carried out through partner field staff and is analyzed jointly with NPA. A Baseline, Post Distributions Monitoring, and an end-line survey will take place covering the key project outputs.
