Project Summary

The conflict in northeastern Nigeria continues to undermine social stability and economic development due to the scale of displacement disrupted livelihoods and diminished agricultural production due to the protracted nature of the crisis. This has a consequential impact on food security and nutrition situation. The conflict has also further affected the health system with less than 30 per cent of health facilities remaining functional in Borno State, where malaria is endemic and diarrhoea and other diseases affect the population regularly and are often life-threatening.
Based on the NE Nigeria Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 8 (Preliminary report) in Oct 2019, the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) remains at serious levels in several domains, particularly in Borno and Yobe states. Based on WHZ the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition in Borno was 8.1 (7.0-9.4 CI) while 11.5 (9.6-13.7 CI) in Yobe. Whereas the prevalence of SAM in Borno was 0.9 (0.6-1.4 CI) and Yobe 1.5 (1.0-2.2 CI). Some of the drivers of malnutrition were poor dietary diversity, low rates of exclusive breastfeeding (particularly in Yobe), the incidence of common childhood illnesses (fever, diarrhoea, cough etc.) and poor water, sanitation and hygiene practices. The high influx of IDPs and the high malnutrition rates among newly arrived children as they have been deprived of humanitarian assistance for several years may also contribute. The nutrition situation is likely to worsen due to poor hygiene and sanitation conditions, especially in host communities disease outbreaks such as measles, and the influx of newly displaced populations.

1 - Needs

As mentioned in the above, the observed high level of GAM among newly arrived children from hard to reach areas has also been seen through the periodic MUAC assessment conducted systematically by the nutrition sector partner. ACF is providing Community-based and inpatient Nutrition and health services for SAM, CMAM, IYCF treatment as well as contribution to emergency humanitarian efforts to build nutrition security.

2 - Activities

To achieve the proposed project objective below activities will be carried out i) Conduct MUAC screening and referral of SAM cases, ii) treatment of SAM with or without medical complications, iii) Support training of key health and nutrition providers in CMAM including both inpatient and outpatient care, iv) Support joint nutrition situation monitoring and analysis, v) Conduct Nutrition SMART survey in collaboration with the SMoH nutrition focal points and vi) the Nutrition sector coordination team. The project will also conduct CMAM program coverage assessment in the targeted areas and support the Nutrition sector plan and focus through the active participation in the sector coordination meeting, implement IYCF interventions through the use of Mother support groups and Mother Care Groups approaches and support the implementation of the micronutrient supplementation.
