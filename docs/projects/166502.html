Project Summary

IRC proposes to help entrepreneurs and small businesses located in the FN of Cameroon who have been affected by the socio-economic impacts of COVID-19 to restart their economic activities and meet their households’ basic needs.
Based on IRC’s assessments in our proposed areas of intervention, the types of trades/businesses that these entrepreneurs and small businesses are most commonly engaged in include catering, baking, food sales, agriculture, animal husbandry, sewing, carpentry, car mechanics, and office work . To support these entrepreneurs address their economic needs in the face of COVID-19, the IRC provide them with tried-and-tested training and coaching that will enable them to better adapt their activities in response to the pandemic; and ensure they focus on the delivery of essential goods and services for the local economy – for which there is strong market demand, despite the current climate.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon is facing many of the serious crises that are impacting the wider area around the Lake Chad Basin: (1) conflict, affecting the Far North (FN) of the country, where the IRC is proposing its response in partnership with Standard Chartered; (2) endemic poverty that existing even before COVID-19; and (3) the onset of the pandemic itself has exposed how poorly covered the basic needs were of the local population by the small number of humanitarian actors acting there,The FN region has for some time been marked by attacks by non-state armed groups, resulting in the forced displacement of populations and the destruction of livelihoods. This threat is combined with chronic vulnerabilities that greatly reduce the ability of communities in Cameroon to meet their basic needs, particularly among the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls. The FN counts 321,886 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), 123,489 returnees, and 48,769 refugees outside its refugee camps. Chronic insecurity and instability has also negatively affected markets (e.g. supply, price increases, scarcity of certain foodstuffs). The region is characterised by endemic poverty: 74% of the population has lived below the poverty line before the humanitarian crisis begun in the FN; that number has only increased since the current crisis. There are water shortages, and IRC is currently working with partners in the region to alleviate this challenge through provision of new water points. The 2020 Cameroon Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) identifies 1.1 million people estimated to be in severe acute food insecurity. The closure of borders with Nigeria and Chad, destruction of infrastructure and loss of inputs, forced displacement, flooding combined with desertification have devastated the local economy. Before COVID, the economy was based mainly on agriculture, livestock and fishing; local agricultural production is estimated to have decreased by two thirds post-crisis since 2014 Finally, COVID-19 has further exposed these serious vulnerabilities across this part of Cameroon. Since its first COVID-19 case was diagnosed on 5 March 2020, over 20,000 have now been confirmed at the time of writing. The shock of the pandemic on Cameroon's pre-existing vulnerabilities has forced the government to take drastic measures: air, land and sea borders have been closed, as have workplaces, and individuals have been subjected to movement restrictions which have in turn affected both the economy and individuals’ mental health. the IRC will achieve the objectives listed below. Activities proposed to deliver these objectives are listed underneath. In undertaking this programme, we are guided throughout by the lessons learned and achievements of similar work we have already implemented in this location over the past four years. Objective 1: Small businesses negatively affected by COVID-19 remain operational• Identification of 230 clients negatively impacted by COVID-19. • Training on entrepreneurship skills and ensuring ‘COVID-secure’ business operation. • Business recovery grants for 230 entrepreneurs. Objective 2: Entrepreneurs have access to gender-sensitive savings and loans resources Savings groups and gender discussions.
