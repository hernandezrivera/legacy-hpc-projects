Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect the livelihoods, improve the resilience and
productive capacity of communities in middle and southern eastern slopes in Area C in the West Bank (WB). The objective is drawn from the Country Strategic Objective: SO3: “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. This is also in line with FSS guidance for Immediate assistance and livelihood support for farmers in Area C of the West Bank. This is also in line with FSS objective ob1: “Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced”, and the activity: “Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced”. The proposed activities will focus to improve the access to the essential resources (water, grazing land, etc) and provide inputs to increase the production capacities of the targeted farmers in the selected areas. As part of CARE strategy, and given the nature of the protracted crisis, CARE work closely with and through AIDA toward coordinated advocacy, for accountability for IHL violations in Area C.
The project targeted areas are located in most vulnerable areas (Area C, Bedouin communities) in the West Bank where the majority of the livestock population exist.

1 - Needs

Palestine’s context has remained largely unchanged from the past year, with the main challenges originating from the protracted conflict, economic stagnation, high unemployment, gender inequalities and high rates of poverty and food insecurity. In the WB, the prolonged period of slow economic growth, restrictions on movement, trade, investment and access to land and water resources, together with higher food prices, continue to erode the fragile livelihoods of poor Palestinians. According to the HNO 2020, In the WB, over 350,000 people (62% are refugees) living in 63 communities in Area C are at heightened risk of forcible transfer, due to a coercive environment generated by Israeli policies and practices, which create pressure on residents to leave their communities. These practices, which include the demolition or threat of demolition of homes, and livelihood assets, plans to relocate communities to urban townships, to further restrict access to natural resources, and are often implemented in connection with the establishment and expansion of Israeli colonies as well as settlers’ violence. Food insecurity (9.2%) are still entrenched and localized in the West Bank’s southern governorates. More than 60% of Bedouin and herding communities in Area C of the WB are food insecure while 26% of the households (HHs) in Area C are food insecure (WFP 2018). East Jerusalem and South of West Bank are the most affected areas with regard to demolitions, as more than 70% of this area was impacted by restrictions and demolitions in 2019. This area holds the bulk of agricultural and grazing land. Demolitions of agriculture structures, confiscations of assets, forcible transfers, movement restrictions, and colonies expansion, all contribute to livelihood instability and deterioration of the socio-economic environment. The overall guiding principle for CARE’s theory of change is adopting an impact-driven approach, acknowledging that our impact groups require emergency and early recovery supports, however, CARE will utilize interventions that address both immediate needs, as well as the underlying drivers of those needs. Based on CARE various reflections & learning from the gender transformative programming in the opt, female negative experiences of the protracted fragile context are compounded by social norms which inhibit their access to food, inputs, finance and livelihoods’ services, as well as lower control over income and household decision making (CARE, validated 2019). The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of targeted communities, based on a number of key sources, including comprehensive assessments conducted for similar project implemented by CARE /partner (AYADI Project), various and comprehensive need assessments conducted by CARE & PARC in Southern, Middle and Northern WB, HNO 2020/FSS), meetings with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Gender Transformative Insights (CARE WBG 2019), Vegetables & Sheep/Goat dairy assessment (CARE 2018), a series of consultation workshops/ FG were held by with representatives of farmers/ herders, women's CBOs & local councils in targeted locations to revalidate their needs ; with taking into consideration the gender and age groups’ needs. All of the activities below reflect the needs realized at the field level, and which also are in line with what has been identified from HNO 2020. Most of the interviewed framers/herders indicated high constraints in accessing to irrigation water due to damaged/ deteriorated carriers, as well as high needs to rehabilitate greenhouses, water cisterns & pools, agricultural roads, rehabilitating Livestock infrastructure/inputs & capacity building. The lack of possibilities to rebuild these agricultural/ livestock infrastructures leaves households unable to provide neither for their own consumption nor for income generation.

2 - Activities

The project was designed in coordination with other actors mainly MoA and with cooperation between CARE/ PARC & MoA, FSS, and other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication, and maximize the number of beneficiaries who are in need. The activities will be implemented in partnership with PARC, who will be responsible for the technical implementation at field level. CARE will be responsible for the overall quality management, technical support in certain areas like gender and protection and will act as the main contact for donors & stakeholders. The project will target female and male small-scale farming /herding HHS/communities who are living in middle and southeastern slopes of WB Area C. It directly responds to the special needs of female/male small-scale farming & herding /Bedouin families and communities through providing initial production inputs; rehabilitating their individual and/or community-wide livelihood assets & infrastructure (Water networks, agricultural road, etc.); providing them with necessary supplies/tools, and with existing basic knowledge needed to preserve & sustain their farming/herding activities. Prior to any project implementation, the organization will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities & beneficiaries with line ministries offices, & with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities. The project team will finalize, validate the selection criteria per activity to ensure transparency. The criteria will look into socioeconomic status/ vulnerability, access, small-scale farmers, women-headed households, availability of water, availability of agricultural services, previous benefit t from previous projects. Activities for O1: Small-scale male/female livestock herders in Area C (eastern slopes (ES) of the WB- Ramallah, Jerusalem, Hebron and Bethlehem) to protect their livelihood & improve their resilience: • Provide required inputs for protecting, sustaining & increasing livestock holders’ resilience (climate tolerant seeds-(ES) Ramallah, Jerusalem, Bethlehem), farm management inputs (dividers, rehabilitating shelters/barns/farms, (ES) of Jerusalem and Bethlehem), in addition to inputs for to improve quality (e.g. inputs/ tools for better hygiene of milking & dairy processing • Improve access to required water & road infrastructure by providing and/or rehabilitating water cisterns, water harvesting systems, and water carriers in (Jerusalem, Jericho, Hebron); and rehabilitating agricultural roads (Bethlehem, Hebron). • Awareness raising/extension visits on best practices & techniques (hygiene, recording systems, diseases prevention, etc.) to be conducted by the project staff and in full coordination with MoA before & during supplying inputs and trainings. Farmers expect training & extension services to guide their farm/stock management and production practices. 3 - Indicators

Indicators for O1: • 100 barns rehabilitated serving at least 4,500 sheep/goats in favor of 100 HHs (HH headed by women represent 10%) • 30 cisterns with 3600 cubic meter water storage capacities constructed/rehabilitated; serving 150 dunums in favor of 120 HHs (women represent 10%).• 8 Km of water carriers and 2 water distribution points constructed/ rehabilitated in Hebron and Jericho ( 4 Km and 2 distribution point ensuring access of herders who own at least 3,200 heads of sheep/goats, & irrigating 320 dunums of lands in favor of 80 HHs (women represent 5%) and 4 km of water carriers constructed/ rehabilitated to serve & irrigate 320 dunums of open fields (Vegetables) in favor of 80 HHs (women represent at least 5%). • 18 Km agricultural roads rehabilitated/opened (6.5 km; serving 165 herders (7,300 sheep/goats) & enabling their access to 1,500 dunums (women represent at least 5%) and 11.5 Km enabling their access to 2,300 dunums (open fields vegetables) in favor of 314 HHs (women represent at least 5%). • 600 women headed HHS (small-scale female breeders) provided with necessary hygiene milking collection containers; for better quality & shelf life of milk (women represent 100%). • 150 women headed HHs (small-scale breeders) provided by necessary hygiene small HH dairy processing kits & infrastructure support in order to make their processing units more convenient & hygienic for women, for processing dairy products with high quality & to get rid of the dairy wastes without affecting the environment (women represent 100%). • 250 small-scale breeders are provided with high yield/drought tolerant seeds (37.5 tons) for fodder production covering 2,500 dunums, (women represent at least 15%) • 1,698 herders (approx. 49.7% female) produce higher quantity & quality dairy products and apply better linked to existing marketing channels. • 3,125 temporary working days created in favor of 283 male skilled/unskilled laborers. Indicators for O2: • 6 pools (earth, concrete, metallic) with total storage capacity of 3,600 cubic meters are constructed/ rehabilitated serving 300 dunums of agricultural lands in favor of 30 HHs (women represent at least 10%) • 30 dunums greenhouses provided with a cover sheet, internal irrigation networks, and organic fertilizers in favor of 30 HHs (women represent at least 16%). • Productivity & accessibility of 4,150 dunums of marginal agricultural land improved, in favor of 577 HHs (women represent at least 7%). • 5,145 temporary working days created in favor of 454 male skilled/unskilled laborers.
