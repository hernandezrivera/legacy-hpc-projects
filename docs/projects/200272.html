Project Summary

The project aims to reinforce an effective protection environment and to reduce the risk of GBV for girls, boys, men, and women from Lebanese, PRL, and Migrant communities. The project will build on GBV prevention and response mechanisms currently in place, as it will continue to provide a multi-sectoral response to GBV and upscaled prevention services through improving community engagement. CIL with its local partner will provide a comprehensive package of services for GBV survivors through the implementation of case management, recurrent protection cash assistance, focused and non-focused PSS,
awareness-raising activities, PSEA, and distribution of dignity kits.
This package will be combined with community outreach, which will involve stronger community engagement and will ensure timely access to services. Beneficiaries with specific needs, survivors of GBV, or women and girls at risk of GBV will be identified to benefit from project services. Identification of beneficiaries will happen through: outreach, external referrals, internal referrals, self-referrals through the helpline, and referrals from partners. A combination of targeting methods will be used in order to improve access and inclusion. Specific vulnerability criteria of targeted beneficiaries with specific protection risks will be put in place in order to ensure the identification of “most at risk” individuals, especially for the selection of beneficiaries under the emergency cash assistance activity, an example of prioritized most at risk or most vulnerable groups: women head of household, women and girls with limited or no access to SRH services, women with medical needs, elderly women, PWSN, etc,.
The project aims to achieve two main outcomes: 1) To reduce the risk of GBV through prevention services and strengthening community engagement; 2)To mitigate the impact of GBV through improving access to response services and activating referral pathways. The project will also promote accountability through strengthened coordination, community participation, improved access to information about PSEA, and prevention and response through service provision. Project services will be provided through a combined approach of static (in partner(s) community centers) and mobile services in different areas: Beirut, Mount Lebanon, North and Akkar.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Objective 1: Activity 1.1 - Non-focused psychosocial support for women and girls, men and boys; aims to foster resilience and encourage the adoption of positive coping mechanisms. Activity 1.2 distribution of dignity kits - Local partner to distribute dignity kits in accordance with UNFPA guidelines. Distribution will be accompanied by key messages about SRH & GBV-related issues. Activity 1.3 awareness raising activities at the community level, covering different topics such as GBV key concepts and related protective and risk factors, PSEA, SRH, and information about available services. Activity 1.4 community outreach; through outreach volunteers will act as an entry point to identify survivors of GBV and refer them to case management services. Outreach volunteers will receive training on safe identification and referral.Objective 2: Activity 2.1: focused PSS; on building self-confidence and self-esteem, encouraging decision-making, building peer support networks, and providing essential information on GBV prevention and response. Activity 2.2: provision of case management services; individual counseling and specialized services through activating referral pathways. Activity 2.3: Recurrent protection cash assistance; will be provided in complementarity to case management services in a tailored manner to vulnerable GBV survivors to cover immediate and urgent needs. CARE works with local partners (LECORVAW, RDFL,...) who will be onboarded as soon as the funding is available.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

CIL will ensure that all staff including partner staff and outreach volunteers are trained and ready to respond to PSEA incidents in a safe and confidential way. Also, SEA risk mitigation measures have been established and incorporated into the emergency response while SEA key messages and reporting mechanisms are mainstreamed in the proposed activities. Safety audits will be conducted to assess and identify any potentialrisks. CIL will also be able to integrate protection and social inclusion through the below considerations: (i) Project will promote the participation of women, girls, and persons with disabilities; (ii) CIL Incorporates a “do no harm” approach in order to mitigate the unintended negative outcomes, e.g. perpetuating social exclusion, or escalating GBV. IEC material and PSEA risk assessment analysis developed under CARE on-going project will be used as well.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

CIL is committed to the universally recognized Core Humanitarian Standards, which include a commitment to safe and responsive mechanisms to handle complaints. Furthermore, an effective Feedback Accountability Mechanism (FAM) is central to CIL’s duty of care to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). To safeguard such accountability, the project will ensure consultation at the community level at the design, testing, and implementation phases. This will be done through CIL’s community-based design methods and proven techniques (i.e. conducting info sessions on AAP, and ensuring complaint boxes are present during the different trainings). Discussions with community members will take place to ensure data is relevant and needed, applying a do-no-harm approach throughout the intervention. CIL will facilitate consultations at the community level with People in Need, local authorities, and key community members, including volunteers to better understand the needs of targeted communities.
