Project Summary

While many components of the Syrian society suffer from the repercussions of the crisis, the category of persons with disabilities (PWDs) and their families is among the most affected. Despite the efforts of multiple governmental and non-governmental organizations to respond to the needs of persons with disabilities, available resources –whether human or financial – are unable to cover the needs during the current time and the near future. In addition to these challenges, there are cases of persons with severe disabilities who severely suffer from lack of access to services – whether basic or medically rehabilitative – not to mention economic and social support services, considered currently a difficult goal to attain under the current situation.
Moreover, the cases of persons with severe disabilities present one of the greatest challenges as they suffer from higher mortality rate and deeply depressed socioeconomic situation.
Within the situation of limited resources and capacities to respond to the needs of persons with severe disabilities, cash-based intervention represents an effective mean to fill the gap and provide important support to persons with severe disabilities and their families.
The suggested project aims to achieve the following main objectives:
1. Improve the protection of persons with severe disabilities through individual targeted protection interventions (conditional cash assistance).
2. Strengthening the resilience of the families of persons with severe disabilities by preserving the financial, material and human assets within the family.
Based on the amount requested, the project could target approximately 500 PWDs.
The criteria for targeting beneficiaries is mainly based on the severity of the disability they live with, in addition to the socioeconomic vulnerability of the household.
UNDP has experienced implementing similar project in the governorate of Aleppo where it targeted 500 persons with severe disabilities namely spinal cord injuries with cash assistance for 6 months in the amount of 155$ per month. The cash assistance was conditioned to following a rehabilitation treatment Programme designed by specialist individually for each beneficiary. The project was also coupled with a graduation model to support one household member to secure job opportunity to further support the resilience of the whole households of the persons the severe disabilities. UNDP has benefitted from this experience and collected lessons learnt that fed in the developed model. This model of the project is able to be replicated in other governorates especially the ones most affected by the war

3 - Needs

While many components of the Syrian society suffered from the current crises, persons with disabilities (PWDs) and their families are among the most affected and marginalized. PWDs experience higher morbidity rate and deeply depressed socioeconomic situation. There are no exact data reporting the exact number of persons with disabilities in Syria. It is estimated that 27% of the population above 12 years old now in Syria live with a level with disabilities of which 30% is linked to permanent disabilities. It is also reported that among these numbers, 5% of the permanent disability cases are for example spinal cord injuries which is one of the most severe cases of disabilities and carries a high level of morbidity and mortality not to mention the negative socioeconomic impact on the households, communities and health system. In a previous assessment conducted by UNDP Syria on the cases of severe disability like spinal cord injuries it was also estimated that there would be 500 PWDs in governorates which were affected by the war reflecting on the number of the population in those governorates. In addition, there are several other cases of impairment caused by the war and or diseases or congenital that also fall under the category of severe disabilities that creates a burden on the resilience of households and communities.

4 - Implementation of activities

The Disability Inclusion Programme manager will provide technical support and oversight and will lead the overall implementation of the project in close collaboration with NGOs, MOSAL, and local authorities. The project structure will consist of programme management and technical teams. UNDP field staff will share their expertise in community mobilization, beneficiary selection, awareness raising activities. UNDP will make use of its partnerships with financial institutions in Syria for cash disbursement. UNDP will hold a kick off meeting to discuss the overall project strategy, logframe analysis, workplan, and to agree on the roles and responsibilities concerning project implementation. UNDP will meet with local authorities (Governor Office, MoSAL, Area Managers, and national authorities to review the project, secure ongoing support and approve implementation. UNDP will setup a solid, transparent selection criterion of beneficiaries which will be implemented and monitored by the UNDP team in collaboration with stakeholders. Although there may be variances in the vulnerability criteria in different communities, UNDP will work with local communities to prevent discrimination and ensure inclusion of most vulnerable PWDs. The project team will provide regular monitoring (field monitoring visits, remote monitoring, and indicator data tracking mechanisms to provide a regular assessment for appropriate course corrective actions. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Beneficiaries directly targeted in the projects are persons with severe disability. It has been noticed that women with disability are more likely to endure violence than those without disability. For that, it is critical to ensure that the risks faced by persons with disabilities are effectively addressed, the elements of protection mainstreaming will be considered during design and implementation. The protection and safety of the project beneficiaries will be promoted through individual data collection will be done discretely for each beneficiary, a dedicated team will work on analyzing the data which might help in detecting any GBV cases and\or discrimination acts based on gender and disability. Women will be prioritized in the selection process. The teams working on the project under UNDP will have female and male members to handle sensitive cases specially for women and children. Also, awareness sessions will be provided for all beneficiaries to raise their knowledge around persons with disability rights and the privileges the Syrian government provides for them. PSS for PWDs with referring paths for persons in need of more in depth consultations. Finally, UNDP will activate complaint policy and ensure that the beneficiaries could raise their complaint and get feedback indicating that their complaint has been taken seriously, different way will be implemented to activate this system like direct tel NO and complaint box.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNDP is committed to mainstreaming protection in all implemented projects and conforms to the IASC understanding of “protection”. Staff are trained on humanitarian law and the UNDP organizational policies including child protection, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of the project implementation, UNDP will promote and strengthen the capacities of functional community groups such as the NGOs team members, local government officials, and other actors. ThThe planned activities are tailored considering the rights and best interests of victims, hence will be in line with UNDP’s principle of “do no harm” and non-discrimination for all persons connected to SEA and will be delivered in a manner that seeks to uphold rights and wellbeing. Moreover, UNDP staff and community groups will continuously monitor the context on the ground and report any protection concerns identified by community members (IDPs and host communities) as appropriate. Furthermore, UNDP has offices located in the respective governorates with staff and present within the community; therefore, UNDP is well positioned to receive any report of imminent or ongoing exploitation and threats faced by persons in vulnerable situations. In the event that any abuse or misconduct is reported, UNDP will take appropriate action, and share specific concerns with appropriate actors to make either referrals or adjustments in interventions to improve beneficiary safety.
