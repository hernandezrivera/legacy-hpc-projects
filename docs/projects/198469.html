Project Summary

This project seeks to prioritize the provision of life-saving healthcare services and contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improved access to basic lifesaving Primary Healthcare, mobile medical services, and referral services for vulnerable populations in the IDPs and host communities in Askira/Uba and Hawul LGAs, Borno State.
The project will support the capacity development of the healthcare workers through on-the-job training, and continuous mentorship to ensure they have adequate and relevant knowledge and skills to provide quality services to the affected population. Also, the project will strengthen community healthcare access through operationalizing of mobile medical health teams that will reach the affected population with outpatient services, provide health education on hygiene and sanitation practices, maternal and child health services, COVID-19 prevention, prevention of epidemic-prone diseases, prevention of sexual and gender-based violence, PSEA and other relevant topics in the two LGAs in communities such as Chul,Rumirgo, Uba, Lassa, Wamido, Uda, Ovu IDP CAMP, Womdio IDP camp, Askira town, Marama , Kwajaffa, Tashan Alade, Ghuma, Sakwa, Bulungwi ,Yimirshika, and Shaffa Host Community)
The project will support and strengthen the last-mile distribution of essential lifesaving drugs and medical supplies for IDPs, Host Communities, and Returnees (including persons with disabilities) with critical needs in line with the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan.

1 - Needs

Displacement in Northeast Nigeria continues to be driven by the active presence of NSAGs as an estimated 2.2 million people are internally displaced (IOM 2022). Violent armed confrontations have frequently led to significant displacement and greater needs as estimated 5 million people are in dire need of humanitarian health assistance across the BAY states. In addition, the increasing influx of IDPs, due to the BSG-led camp closures in Maiduguri, continues to strain the already overstretched resources, leaving people without adequate food, limited health services, dignity kits and NFIs. Also, access to health services is also hampered by high medical costs as reported by IDPs (75%), returnees (61%) and non-displaced households (80%). (HRP 2022) Furthermore, the Health Sector has identified some key concerns including shrinking access and humanitarian space; a spike in the conflict in some areas with resultant impacts on populations and critical facilities; limited essential services; near-absence of functional governance especially at LGA level; and the ongoing relocation or resettlement of IDPs to their LGAs of origin, also it was noted that the state government has promised that all camps will be closed in the year 2023, most locations such as Askira/Uba and Hawul LGAs will be flooded with returnees and new influx which will overburden the already not enough healthcare resources. It is also known that Hawul have 21,616 IDPs and Askira/Uba has 10,314 IDPs. Among the key risks are severe malnutrition, the outbreak of cholera and vaccine-preventable diseases like measles and cVDPV2. In Epi week 31 of EWARS, 158 of the Malaria reported cases were from Askira/Uba and 7 confirmed cases of COVID-19 from Hawul. The Northeast region still has the highest maternal mortality rates in Nigeria, estimated at 1,549 per 100,000 live births. Approximately 28% of IDPs (370,000), 34% of people in host communities (400,000), and 32% of returnees (170,000) do not have sufficient access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene. In-patient and out-patient healthcare services are challenged due to the lack of adequate human resources for health, most health facilities lack the standard number of nurses, midwives and CHEWs, that will help maintain and run the facilities appropriately. Thus, the project seeks to improve community healthcare responses by strengthening the primary health care system to enhance health services to the most vulnerable among the conflict-affected population in Askira/Uba and Hawul LGAs.

2 - Activities

The project will increase the availability of essential lifesaving health services in order to meet the current health needs of 10,000 vulnerable populations especially women, children and adolescent girls in Askira/Uba and Hawul. The project will also enhance effective quality health care for the in-patients and out-patients, treatment of minor ailments, strengthen early warning for disease outbreaks, reproductive and maternal health services, counselling and psychosocial support, health education, community dialogues and sensitization, provision of essential medical drugs and supplies in the two LGAs. Programme Activities include: 1. Capacity Building for 16 Healthcare Workers on Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) and Community-Based Health and First Aid (CBHFA) who will implement coordinated activities that will expand access to comprehensive health services for in-patients and out-patients, prevent reproductive health-related morbidity and mortality, and promote positive health-seeking behaviour change processes by linking health demands and possible services. Also, AHI will work with Doctors that will provide general consultations and specialized services; Nurses/Midwives will provide SRH Services: ANC care, delivery, and post-natal care and the CHEWs will assist in community mobilization and health facility referrals for women and girls. The primary healthcare facilities will be supported to provide routine immunization services and campaigns such as ASAQ for children < 5, Vitamin A distribution and de-worming with albendazole via health facilities and mobile health clinics targeting displaced people, and other vulnerable groups in the LGAs 2. Support 6 primary health facilities with the procurement and distribution of last mile medical drugs and consumables such as prescriptive drugs, micronutrient supplements, syringes, dressing materials, equipment (BP machines, thermometers, weighing scales, stethoscopes etc), data capture and reporting tools to enable the effective provision of services by the facilities and the mobile Health Team 3. Set up Community Mobile Health Teams from trained Health Workers to support healthcare services and facilitate community referrals, immunization campaigns and follow-up. These activities will improve health services for 5,000 beneficiaries across the Two LGAs. The CHEWs will assist the Nurses/Midwives with immunization campaigns, epidemic prevention and awareness and mobilizing the community to seek and access appropriate care. 4. Organize awareness-raising activities, Risk Communication and Community Engagement on good health practices and nutrition, Hygiene promotion, priority diseases and SRH issues. SBCC sessions will be organized in the community to foster behavioural change as well as promote gender sensitization and access to appropriate care. 5. Enhance Community Participation and Accountability through an efficient beneficiary feedback mechanism utilizing amongst others Community Stakeholders Meetings that will avail opportunities to share project updates and get feedback to improve the project

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

AHI will work with protection mainstreaming principles of prioritizing safety and dignity and avoid causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, participation and empowerment. In this regard, the project will actively promote adherence to Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) that describe the roles, responsibilities, guiding principles, and procedures for prevention of and response to any form of gender-based violence affecting the community. All the health workers will be sensitized about PSEA and Do No Harm, and sign a Do No Harm Pledge. AHI will ensure that the health workers apply culturally sensitive approaches, as well as, understand the structural and cultural factors that contribute to women’s and girls’ vulnerability to GBV and SRH risks. In line with this, the project will ensure the application of the humanitarian guiding principles of “do no harm”, confidentiality, safety, nondiscrimination and impartiality in service provision such that priority is given to those who most need it, regardless of their affiliation, in every implementation phase. In recognition of the fact that the project is targeting the most vulnerable populations and its main beneficiaries are adolescent girls, women of childbearing age and persons living with disabilities, the service providers will include both male and female health workers to facilitate acceptability for women and girls to benefit from services. To maximize the positive impacts of access to services we will:1. Recognize the position of men as key decision makers, and ensure that men receive information about the importance of services being offered through effective and relevant channels, such as community dialogues. We will also undertake advocacy and public education activities that foster joint decision-making at the household level and encourages trust in women’s ability to make decisions about the need to access services 2. Acknowledge that young women, especially pregnant adolescents, often experience discrimination at the hands of health workers and this affects/ delays health seeking, therefore hindering their access to antenatal care (ANC) and other SRH services. Thus, we will support the improvement of interpersonal communication skills among health workers to equip them to accept, counsel and support pregnant adolescents. Similarly, we will provide older household members and husbands with information on how to support younger women through pregnancy for better health outcomes, in order to improve healthcare access among pregnant youths. Finally, we will ensure that the project’s feedback and complaints mechanisms are appropriate and robust enough to communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from, with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promoting beneficiary empowerment and assisting in detecting misconduct. The Feedback Reporting System we emplace will also document and track the incidence and resolution of feedback/complaints from the persons of concern

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Timely feedback to beneficiaries’ complaints: Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. AHI will actively seek the views of affected populations using a community response mechanism (CAPs) to improve policy and practice in programming. There will also be key informant interviews and focus group discussions held with beneficiaries to garner feedback on project implementation and awareness will be raised within the communities on these feedback channels. We will also ensure that the multiple feedback and complaints mechanisms are appropriate and robust enough to communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from, with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promoting beneficiary empowerment and assisting in detecting misconduct. Integral to this, our Feedback Reporting System document and track the incidence and resolution of feedback/complaints from the persons of concern. We will also proactively demonstrate that we have listened by regularly giving details of the changes we make and explaining why, and when certain actions cannot be taken, where any. Deliberate attention will be paid to closing the loop through preferred and trusted channels.
