Project Summary

This WASH project is designed to implement interventions for the Jebel Boma community through the mobilization of community and selection of 10 hygiene promoters (HPs) through the help of local community leaders and train them to carry out awareness raising sessions and conduct campaigns routes of disease transmission, safe water chain, community mobilization, safe and unsafe hygiene practices; conduct House-to-house hygiene messaging and visits to educate people on key hygiene messages and awareness on risky behaviors; Formation or refresher training of women groups and engaging them as ambassadors and as good examples of safe hygiene practices in the community and forms 6 women groups with 10 individuals targeted with the training on safe water chain, routes of contamination, hygiene in food handling; Display information, education and communication (IEC) materials in strategic locations such as busy streets, markets, water points etc. to help send hygiene messages such as safe water chain, hygienic hand-washing practices, safe sanitation, routes of contamination; Carry out hand-washing (HW) demonstrations in the primary schools, markets, and other public places where people gather to promote hygienic hand-washing practices at critical times; Organize and conduct 6 jerry-cans cleanup campaigns at water points (1 per boma).This will help prevent contamination of water due to dirty containers. During the campaigns, the team will target 1,000 individuals for each campaign on mobilization of the communities, provision of information on safe water chain, common cleanup with the water point users with the provision of soap; Menstrual hygiene management sessions with girls will be conducted in the community to raise awareness on hygienic MHM practices with 4 sessions on Menstrual Hygiene Education. 1 session will be done for women and girls at community level, second sessions will be done during MHM kits distribution with the support of female Community HPs; Training of 30 community leaders, church leaders, youth leaders and opinion leaders to oversee and promote proper use and maintenance of the water points and provide leadership to the water users committee and the entire committee in maintaining the water points; Distribution of menstrual hygiene products (pads, panties and soap) to girls of menstrual age with focus on schools; training of 10 pump mechanics on repair, operation and maintenance of boreholes. APD will work with relevant county authorities and institutions to mobilize persons to be trained; Repair and rehabilitation of 10 water points and the activities include: change of pipes, rods, cylinder heads, pumps and other worn out parts of the hand pumps will be replaced, fencing and platform rehabilitation; Carrying out of water quality analysis both at repaired/rehabilitated water points and at HH level using H2S viral tests, using bottle scopes, for water testing at water points and HH level. This will help in determining the chlorination process for the water points and designing water treatment messages and treatment methods; Disinfection and chlorination of contaminated boreholes, based on the results from the water quality analysis to ensure provision of safe water for drinking and HH; Training of 70 water committee members for repaired/ rehabilitated water points with 7 members at each borehole to oversee and promote proper use and maintenance of the water points; Provision of cleaning tools to health facilities and schools (hard/soft brush, bucket, liquid soap); conducting general clean up campaigns on waste management in the market areas; Training of community on CLTS to enable community construct their own latrines using local materials.

1 - Needs

On April 2019, the violence conflict in Jebel Boma occurred between the Jie and the Murle( Indigenous tribes) community causing displacements of people to neighboring villages and across the state of Kapoeta. The violence resulted to multiple cases of WASH though the com unities initially since the 2013 and 2016 conflict has never received any WASH services in the area. APD will target Returnees, IDPs, and host community in Jebel Boma, Pibor County, with a package of emergency WASH activities in an effort to ensure continued access to minimum, life-saving services. The acknowledgement that poor WASH services are key driver to poor health and malnutrition, since women and girls are the primary direct users of WASH services, the project will contribute to GBV mitigation among the target population. The project will implement life-saving integrated WASH and GBV activities targeting 10,000 beneficiaries suffering from inadequate access to safe drinking water and poor hygiene conditions in the areas of Itti, Nauyapuru, Nyatt, Marwo, Labarab and Korardep with a project designed to improve access to safe water at nutrition centers, schools, health centers, as well as raise awareness on safe sanitation and hygiene practices in order to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases.As the region has a high malnutrition level with IPC 4 classification. Our WASH response will integrate nutrition, education, FSL and health partners into the response. The project will target communities with poor access to safe water supply, unsafe sanitation and hygiene practices, areas with poor sanitation coverage and support health, education and nutrition centers with rehabilitation of WASH facilities.The situation has further been deteriorating with the ongoing conflict in the region. As a result, water point infrastructure such as boreholes, water systems, and latrines and other facilities were destroyed by armed actors. Another factor to the deteriorating situation is limited humanitarian access. Hot spots have been identified by the humanitarian community; these areas are characterized by high GAM rates, hunger gaps, insecurity and significant movement of returnees back to their original homeland from Ethiopia. Malnutrition and food insecurity makes communities more prone to water-related diseases, especially children under the age of 5-years. This is of special importance during the dry season that is starting from November. Integration with other sectors will ensure decreasing prevalence of diseases and malnutrition. Water supply and latrine facilities improvement at health, nutrition and education institutions will contribute to reduction of disease transmission, and hence make health, nutrition and education centers safe for users. Key hygiene and health messaging at these institutions will be conducted in coordination with partners, using the integrated tool of the three clusters of health, nutrition and WASH. With the involvement of our donor and APD M&E department, the indicators will track cooperation with communities through existing beneficiary feedback mechanism, to address GBV and protection threats in WASH, and ensuring services provided meet the needs of the beneficiaries. Clear beneficiary selection criteria for WASH NFIs distribution will be put in place and clearly presented to the community.In this project, APD will ensure the mainstreaming of protection and accountability to affected populations, focusing on to the most vulnerable groups. APD will ensure that services are delivered in a gender-sensitive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, the elderly, people living with disabilities.

2 - Activities

APD will implement the following activities: Training of 50 pump mechanics on repair, operation and maintenance of boreholes with the inclusion and participation of women as pump mechanics will be encouraged, with a target of at least 25%; Repair and rehabilitation of 10 water points through main repair/ rehabilitation activities include: change of pipes, rods, cylinder heads, pumps and other worn out parts of the hand pumps will be replaced, fencing and platform rehabilitation; Carrying out of water quality analysis both at repaired/rehabilitated water points and at household level; Disinfection of contaminated boreholes, based on the results from the water quality analysis; Training of 70 water committee members for repaired/rehabilitated water points with 7 members at each borehole to oversee and promote proper use and maintenance of the water points, 65% of majority will be women; Mobilization of community and selection of 10 community hygiene promoters (CHPs) through the help of local community leaders with an aims to engage women as 50% of CHPs; Training of 10 CHPs to carry out awareness raising sessions and conduct campaigns; House-to-house hygiene messaging and visits to educate people on key hygiene messages and awareness on risky behaviors; Formation or refresher training of women groups and engaging them as ambassadors and as good examples of safe hygiene practices in the community. Six women groups with 10 individuals will be targeted with the training, carried out by CHPs; Display information, education and communication (IEC) materials in strategic locations such as busy streets, markets, water points etc. to help send hygiene messages. Carrying out hand-washing (HW) demonstrations in the four primary schools, markets, and other public places where people gather to promote hygienic hand-washing practices at critical times; Organizing and conducting 6 jerry can cleanup campaigns at water points (1 per boma). This will help prevent contamination of water due to dirty containers. In order to promote safe water chain and safe hygiene practices, the team will conduct one jerrycan cleanup campaigns in each boma targeting 1,000 individuals for each campaign; Menstrual hygiene management sessions with girls will be conducted in the community to raise awareness on hygienic MHM practices targeting 2000 Individuals, with 4 sessions on Menstrual Hygiene Education. 1 session will be done for women and girls at community level, second sessions will be done during MHM kits distribution; Training of 30 local community leaders, church leaders, youth leaders and opinion leaders to oversee and promote proper use and maintenance of the water points; Distribution of menstrual hygiene products (pads, panties and soap) to girls of menstrual age with focus on school girls and vulnerable households; Provision of cleaning tools to four health facilities and two schools (hard/soft brush, bucket, liquid soap); Conducting 4 general clean up campaigns on waste management in the market areas; Training of community on CLTS to enable community construct their own latrines using local materials.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. APD has high standards of conducting assessments and distributions, always concerning rights of beneficiaries and in full accountability. Before the activities are conducted, the beneficiaries are mobilized and made aware of the project activities, of their rights and selection criteria, what they will get and what is not in the project. Local authorities, chiefs and the entire community are enlightened of the selection criteria and how the registration is done. Beneficiaries will be asked to sign on the forms as a confirmation for receiving assistance. Distributions and other activities are conducted with high sensitivity to the special needs of women, children, elderly and disabled. APD is aware of cultural norms and local law, acts always within the law of South Sudan, while humanitarian standards and international human rights are our highest guidepost. "Do no harm" principle is fully respected. All beneficiaries and affected population are informed about complaint mechanisms, there are multiple ways of asking questions, introducing queries and complaints, which are always considered and answered by our staff. Personal data of beneficiaries are protected according to European standards (GDPR). APD professional Monitoring Team pays special attention during post-intervention monitoring to check the respect of rights of beneficiaries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. Vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (women and children, lactating and pregnant mothers, household led by children, mothers and children who fled family alone) are the primary beneficiaries of this project. APD will involve affected people right from the assessment in the process of needs identification and activities will be designed to ensure meaningful access to service provided based on the needs of the displaced people to ensure that the services are provided to the targeted beneficiaries. APD will also use people from within the affected people deliver assistance and implement the project activities, for example, hygiene promoters, pump mechanic, water user committee will be selected from among the IDPs, locations for facilities and distribution sites will be decided together with the affected people to ensure meaningful access. APD activities will target both IDPs and vulnerable host community whose resources are stretched with the influx of IDPs for where there is a needs for WASH NFIs distribution. Water sources will be repaired to benefit both IDPs and the host community. WASH activities in Institution will target both IDPs and host community. The people with special needs/disabilities are more vulnerable to violence due to their reduced capacity and often missed out during the distribution due lack of access and the older people pose a brunt of conflict because of their fragility and inability to provide for themselves. The disabled and old people will be allowed to participate and involved throughout the project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. This project is linked to community and government system. APD will work in close relationship with local communities focusing on traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at the local level with local leaders and community leaders like the Boma chiefs, County Authority and Payam Administrators among others ensuring proper information sharing. During the assessment, APD will involve the community in identifying their needs and coming up with recommendations. Intervention terms of references will be shared with the community leaders and the general community like women groups in FGDs to ensure the community take part in decision making and implementation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. APD proposed project is aligned with the national objectives and recognized the urgent needs to meet the social protection of the specific vulnerable populations such as IDPs, Returnees based on the UNHCR 2019-2020 tracking system. The Returnees will participate in the ongoing peace building and promotion of social cohesion between the Returnees, and the host communities and national reconciliation efforts in the country. This project objectives are taken from the WASH cluster strategic objectives intended to provide timely/sustainable lifesaving, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene interventions to population under 2019 IPC 3, 4 and 5 in difficult areas.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The project focus on beneficiaries’ safety through protection risk analysis survey, safety audit tools exercise and access negotiation supported by UNOCHA. Our field security focal person will be used to analyze security situation. There will be security updates from field before any intervention and will be shared through humanitarian hub, with close coordination with UNDSS to receive and report security incidences. APD has experience in the proposed locations of intervention, has good capacity in the management of activities in such an environment. We conducted protection assessment and risk analysis, stakeholder mapping and needs verification to understand context analysis, protection risks and vulnerability level. We found that the protection environment for our targeted populations is safe and stable for intervention of this proposed project. Since 2018 to date, we witnessed the high inflow of Returnees from Ethiopia to their original homelands and present of IDPs in the proposed location. Our WASH monitoring team will make sure the high concerns of protection of the targeted population is mainstreamed into this project to ensure safety and stability and ‘do-no-harm principle’ so that any assistance provided to the vulnerable populations pose no more risks or create any conflict rather provide assistance to conflict affected populations and encourage sense of togetherness and coexist as one Society. In all our activities, we will ensure the safety of our beneficiaries is observed, their rights promoted and protected, enhance safety and dignity without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. This project aims at enhancing the resilience of the community by reducing future risks for targeted communities. APD will involves various stakeholders throughout the project cycle. These includes the community leaders, and government officials in building sustainable relationships between the people and their environments. The selection of local pump mechanics and formation of water management committees will be done through the help of the community leaders and members/beneficiaries. We will have a joint trips and activities more especially with the water user committees to enhance self-management, pump mechanic to build the capacity to maintain and repair water points, handing over of the hand pump repair toolkits to local leaders, and community hygiene promoters to keep conducting social and behavior change communication sessions. This will instill sense of ownership and participation without conflict. We will mainstreams our peace building program into this project and encourage peaceful coexistence between the IDPs, Returnees and the host communities. Upon the project completion, APD will hand over the project to the community.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups are not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. For example, eligibility criteria for identifying beneficiaries for WASH NFI distribution will be used targeting the female-headed households, elderly-headed households, child-headed households, unaccompanied children, people with less or no community link, people living with disabilities, elderly and people with chronic sicknesses, pregnant and lactating women with little support, people at community institutions, disease-affected population, children and women, responsible for fetching water. During the registration for distribution, APD will endure door to door registration to ensure that even those who are not able to come to the registration points are registered including their vulnerability status. Women and girls are not included in the consultation and decision making process will be consulted separately on their needs and ensure that decision pertaining to location of WASH facilities are made in consultation with women and girls. Separate FGDs will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff so that they can open up during registration/distribution, registration from household to household and will ensure that order is maintained by involving community leaders, crowd controllers and creating separate ques for men and women, children and different categories of people. The protection risk assessment will be conducted before deciding the distribution site to ensure that distribution sites are safe and accessible to the beneficiaries. Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents such GBV and child protection will be strengthened by working closely with the protection partners like GBV and CP sub-clusters for services referral.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be put in place to attend to complaints or grievances of stakeholders ( local leaders, project suppliers, direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, group of persons, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the our program, activities, staff, and project suppliers) at all stages of the project. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to our program staff, as well as invited to share their experiences and suggestions with APD’s monitoring team or program staff. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee, enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback directly with APD staff, through complaint desks, community leaders, etc. information shared will be used for improving actions/activities, advocacy for further services from relevant actors in situation where APD is unable to respond to the needs including referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention. Positive feedback will be used for documenting success stories. For people living with disability like those who are immobile, APD team will consider visiting households with the help of the hygiene promoters to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, community hygiene promoters, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedback since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Feedback mechanism is part of our accountability to AAP commitment.
