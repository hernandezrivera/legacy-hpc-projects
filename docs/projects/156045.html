Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 418,000 out of 445,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2019. However, UNRWA will be working towards targeting its assistance towards the most vulnerable Palestine refugees. A vulnerability assessment was conducted in 2018 to ensure delivery of humanitarian assistance towards the most vulnerable.
The project includes two main components which are cash assistance for NFI and distribution of in-kind NFIs. Under the cash component, UNRWA is planning to adopt a two-tier approach for humanitarian NFIs assistance in 2019. The focus will be on four main groups that can be identified through UNRWA’s registration system. These categories are: families headed by a person older than 60 years; families headed by a person with disability, and people with disabilities; families headed by a female; and orphans. UNRWA estimates that 126,000 Palestine Refugees fall under the identified categories and will accordingly provide them with cash assistance for NFIs amounting to USD 10.5 per person per month. Meanwhile, UNRWA will provide the remaining 292,000 Palestine Refugees in need of assistance with cash assistance for NFIs equivalent to USD 5.25 per person per month to help them meet their basic needs.
Under the in-kind NFIs distribution, UNRWA in 2019 will focus its response on areas where access is restricted, acute emergency setting, and according to the needs in newly accessible areas. In addition, UNRWA will stop large scale distributions undertaken since 2012 to all IDPs and will focus on creating a contingency stock for 5000 families in need, including all main NFIs distributed by UNRWA namely blankets, mattresses, tarpaulin, clothes, kitchen sets, jerry cans, baby kits, cleaning sets etc.

Needs

Eight years of conflict in Syria continue to have dramatic consequences for the people living in the country, and Palestine refugees in particular. Violence, economic ‎disruption and a contracting job market as well as a decrease in provision of public services and social security programmes have created and compounded ‎vulnerabilities within the population. In the absence of a durable political settlement, intense and widespread hostilities have resulted in deaths and injuries, continued ‎internal displacement, large-scale migration to other countries, lost livelihoods, mounting humanitarian needs and access restrictions hindering the emergency response, ‎including in UNRWA areas of operations.‎In 2018, UNRWA estimates that 445,000 Palestine refugees remain in the country out of 560,000 individuals registered with the Agency in Syria. Displacement, loss of ‎assets and livelihood and high levels of inflation have contributed to the increased vulnerability of the Palestine refugee population. Many families have lost their main ‎breadwinner to involvement in, or repercussions of the conflict, whether through death, incapacitation, detention or departure to another country in order to seek protection ‎and asylum. Almost 60 per cent of Palestine refugees have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict and, according to UNRWA estimations, more ‎than 180,000 have seen their homes destroyed or severely damaged due to hostilities. Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal (Aleppo) camps, previously home to more than 30 ‎per cent of the Palestine refugee population in Syria, are almost completely destroyed. UNRWA vulnerability assessment, 2018[3], estimated that 90 per cent of Palestine ‎refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than USD 2 person/day) prior to receiving UNRWA cash assistance. As a result, it is expected that a large ‎majority of the Palestine refugee population will continue to rely on UNRWA humanitarian assistance to meet their most basic needs in the years to come. ‎In 2019, cash assistance will remain a priority for UNRWA in Syria. Although vulnerability remains high across Palestine refugees in Syria, funding challenges are limiting ‎the Agency’s ability to continue to provide the same levels of humanitarian assistance as in past years. In order to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestine refugees ‎continue to meet their basic needs, and to ensure an efficient use of scarce resources, a targeted approach will be introduced in 2019. Groups characterized as most ‎vulnerable, including female headed households (FHH), persons with disabilities, households headed by older persons and unaccompanied minors (orphans) will receive a ‎higher social transfer value, while lower levels of emergency assistance will continue for all those refugee families that do not fall in the most vulnerable categories. ‎

Implementation of activities

The project has two main components that will be implemented as illustrated below:‎A)‎ Cash assistance for NFIs: UNRWA has been providing life-saving humanitarian assistances to Palestinian Refugees since the beginning of the crisis in Syria. ‎The cash assistance programme remains the most critical form of UNRWA emergency support to Palestine refugees in Syria, helping them to meet part of their ‎essential humanitarian and survival needs that provides flexibility and dignity of choice. The cash assistance programme is considered as a flagship programme ‎of UNRWA’s humanitarian operations. It remains the primary form of assistance for 418,000 conflict-affected and vulnerable Palestine refugees living in Syria. ‎Currently, UNRWA distributes cash through three partners (Al Baraka Bank, BBSF Bank and Al Haram Company) in addition to its own cash distribution centre ‎located at filed office. UNRWA works with the three partners to ensure smooth implementation of cash assistance activities within, the capacity of each partner, ‎the location of the outlet and the address of the beneficiaries. As part of the planning phase, UNRWA generates a list of all eligible Palestine refugees and ‎provide the list of beneficiaries to the partner ahead of the starting date. Once the local currency is availed in the outlets and following intensive coordination with ‎the partners, UNRWA sends SMS to each beneficiary communicating the amount, location and time. UNRWA assigns dedicated technical UNRWA staff to each ‎of the cash outlets, allowing the Agency to ensure strict compliance with financial rules and regulations for distribution of cash and also for verification.‎B)‎ In-kind NFIs distribution: UNRWA distributes in-kind NFIs in areas where access is restricted, acute emergency setting, and according to the needs ‎in newly accessible areas. In addition, UNRWA will focus on creating a contingency stock for 5,000 ‎families in need, including all main NFIs distributed by UNRWA namely blankets, mattresses, tarpaulin, clothes, kitchen sets, jerry cans, baby kits, cleaning sets ‎etc. in-kind NFIs are distributed through mobile teams and in the areas affected by the acute emergency.‎‎ ‎

Protection Risk Analysis

-‎ Beneficiaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from distribution points where they receive non-food items (NFI). ‎o Likelihood: medium ‎o Impact: medium‎-‎ Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, particularly at locations with high number of beneficiaries. ‎o Likelihood: mediumo Impact: medium‎-‎ Explosive remnants of war (ERW) are recognized as a protection risk for Palestine refugees across the country, including when accessing UNWRA services. ‎o Likelihood: low ‎o Impact: highMitigating measures: ‎‎-‎ In order to minimize problems in accessing distribution points, UNRWA allows flexibility within the families as to who collects the assistance and a delegation ‎system allows individuals of families to nominate a trusted person to collect the assistance on their behalf. ‎‎-‎ UNRWA also provides lists of what beneficiaries received to facilitate their travel while carrying in-kind assistance and sends text messages to beneficiaries to ‎advise them on changes to services in order to minimize unnecessary movements. ‎‎-‎ UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. ‎‎-‎ UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming ensuring principles such as safety and dignity and access are incorporated into distributions‎-‎ UNRWA has prioritized awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to ERW.‎UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a regular basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its ‎compliance to the main protection principles.‎

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Improved access with localized conflict in certain areas of the country: Overall, humanitarian access is expected to improve with fewer Palestine refugees in hard-to-reach areas (13,500 as of end October 2018). At the same time, hostilities may continue in certain areas of the country. Most Palestine Refugees in Syria will remain displaced: It is anticipated that the levels of displacement will remain high among Palestine refugees, as the housing stock in Yarmouk unofficial camp suffered significant destruction during the conflict, most recently in May 2018. The majority of the 160,000 former residents of Yarmouk are therefore expected to remain in displacement, often paying high rents, which further increases their vulnerability. A similar situation is expected in Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps which have sustained major destruction; these three camps used to provide housing for more than 30 per cent of Palestine refugees in Syria.Increase in spontaneous returns as more areas become accessible: As new areas become accessible to civilians, as observed for Sbeineh and Khan Eshieh camps in 2018, additional small scale spontaneous returns are anticipated, provided that safe access is granted for civilians and basic public services (water, electricity) are restored by the authorities. While a small number of spontaneous returns of PRS (Palestine refugees from Syria) from outside Syria were observed in 2017 and 2018 (about 2,000) and are expected to continue, most returns are expected to involve Palestine refugees displaced inside Syria.UNRWA continue to raise sufficient funds to sustain its operations in Syria: UNRWA will continue to mobilize resources and to advocate for the needs of Palestine refugees among donors, partners, and other relevant stakeholders. UNRWA will mitigate the risk of limited funding by prioritizing key projects and interventions, and focusing on the needs of the most vulnerable Distribution of in-kind NFIs might be delayed due to either security reasons or delay in securing required approvals: in case of any security risks, distribution will be suspended and security measures will be taken to ensure safety of UNRWA staff involved; work will be resumed once security and safety conditions are restored. Risk: Influx in the return of the refugees from the neighbouring countriesAssumption: It is expected that the security situation would be improved in Syria In 2019 which will attract more refugees to return than estimated. The plan for the distribution of NFI will be impacted by the number of the returneesMitigation Measure: Number of returnees will be monitored closely and a database will be prepared to address the needs of the returnees for NFI.Risk: Funding shortfall might be affecting the distribution of NFIAssumption: Following the Increasing trend of the UNRWA’s funding shortfall over the past years UNRWA it might happen for the year 2019Mitigation: UNRWA will target its distribution for the most vulnerable refugee
