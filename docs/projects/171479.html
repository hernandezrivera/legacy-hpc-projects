Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to strengthen community-based preparedness and emergency response in the Middle Area, Khan Younis and Rafah governorates.
The project is heavily informed by annual multi-sectorial assessments in Gaza, including in the Middle Area, Khan Younis and Rafah governorates, ACTED’s continued presence in the 3 governorates, including through several recent and ongoing shelter rehabilitation, community preparedness and NFI prepositioning projects, extensive consultations with key stakeholders, recommendations from the MoSD and Shelter & NFI Cluster in Gaza and incorporates a number of key lessons learnt from previous interventions.
The proposed intervention is fully aligned with the 2021 HNO in which it is stated that maintaining an adequate stock level of NFIs is considered an absolute necessity in light of the escalating risks in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. As such, the project will meet the increasing need for the provision of the necessary NFIs and disinfection materials to quarantine/isolation centers and the families in home quarantine/ isolation.
The project also supports the achievement of 2021 HRP for the Shelter & NFI Cluster, most notably by helping to ensure sufficient NFI stocks to respond to the needs of IDPs at the onset of a large-scale conflict in Gaza while being able to respond to emerging needs resulting from natural disasters in addition to covering the needs at COVID 19 quarantine and isolation centres as well as the people at home quarantine. Specifically, the project will address Objective 3, namely "the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, the protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued." It will do this by increasing the capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner.
Specifically, ACTED will implement a multi-pronged approach through the following means:
In order to continue increasing communities’ preparedness and emergency response capacity, ACTED will reinforce one existing Community Emergency Response Network (CERN) to cover the Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah governorates, which are historically the most affected by mass movements of population during emergencies, and where communities have limited knowledge of emergency mechanisms in place.
ACTED will also increase its stockpiling capacity and geographic coverage by further prepositioning NFI kits in 3 warehouses, thus ensuring preparedness for future man-made and natural disasters while enabling immediate response to urgent COVID-19 needs as they arise.
In total, the project will benefit 13204 individuals. 1 CERN, composed of 3 Community-Based Organizations (CBOs), of 2 members each, 3 Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) comprised of 6 individuals each, and 70 volunteers (94 individuals in total), will be reinforced by refresher trainings on community preparedness and emergency response; 300 host households (1710 individuals) will be assessed to identify and address needs for future bouts of hosting IDPs; and 5200 NFI sets, expected to cover emergency needs for at least 2000 households, representing 11400 individuals, will be prepositioned in 3 existing warehouses.
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with a specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, the disabled and the elderly. Gender principles will be mainstreamed throughout the entire action and CBPCs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as gender-based violence (GBV) with a particular focus given to women and children. Finally, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be core to the proposed Action by giving account to, taking account of and being held to account by affected populations.

1 - Needs

Gaza’s fragile humanitarian situation caused by over 10 years of blockade has significantly deteriorated with the succession of wars in 2008, 2012 and 2014, the Great March of Return in 2018, and most recently the outbreak of COVID-19. In this context, there is an increasing need for the provision of the necessary NFIs and disinfection materials to quarantine/isolation centers and the families at home quarantine/ isolation. As outlined in the HNO 2021, maintaining an adequate stock level of NFIs is considered an absolute necessity in light of the escalating risks in the Gaza Strip, with instability potentially leading to a new devastating escalation and associated displacement estimated at up to 600,000. As per the IACP of 2020, at least one-third of IDPs may reside with host families, an initial rush to schools is anticipated, as is a gradual move to informal shelters. With the deteriorating political context in Palestine, humanitarian partners instigated prepositioning and capacity-building activities in early 2019 to strengthen community-based preparedness and emergency response efforts in Gaza. However, the COVID-19 outbreak led to a fast utilization of emergency NFI kits to support quarantine facilities. As per the Shelter Cluster Contingency Plan, current levels of NFI stocks are very low and need replenishing to ensure humanitarian actors’ capacity to respond to future emergencies. During the COVID-19 crisis so far, ACTED alone stocked out over 7,200 items to support quarantine facilities, representing 33% of its available stocks. This situation is expected to worsen with the impending return of 2,000 Palestinians from Egypt. The Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) estimates that in the coming weeks, over 70,000 households, representing 356,310 individuals, will necessitate emergency NFI assistance as a result of COVID-19, including support to quarantined individuals and to returnees through hygiene and cleaning kits. A such, the IACP of 2020 states that stockpiling is needed for additional 60,000 NFIs at a minimum. Since 2016, ACTED conducts annual multi-sectorial assessments in Gaza, including in the Middle Area, Khan Younis and Rafah governorates. These assessments, in addition to ACTED’s continued presence in the 3 governorates, including through several recent and ongoing shelter rehabilitation, community preparedness and NFI prepositioning projects, enables ACTED to collect up-to-date data on households’ profiles, concerns and needs, and shows increasing vulnerabilities in a context of exacerbated political tensions and COVID-19 pandemic, in particular for the most vulnerable groups i.e. women, children, people with disabilities, elderly people, and people located in Access Restricted Areas or affected by quarantines and movements restrictions. A Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) assessment led in 2017 with 2014 IDPs showed a concerning lack of community preparedness, with 80% of respondents being unaware of the location of the closest DES to their homes, and almost 60% not knowing whom to contact to access humanitarian services in emergencies. Respondents in the Middle Area and Khan Younis governorates showed the lowest levels of such knowledge. During and after escalations of violence, IDP households, and in particular vulnerable groups i.e. women, children and people with disabilities, remain exposed to a range of protection threats including gender-based violence (GBV). A 2014 UNFPA survey showed that, during the 2014 war, only 16-20% of NFI assistance related to personal and hygiene needs were safely available for women and girls. Lack of safe and adequate access to emergency assistance, especially during the first days of emergency and displacement, negatively impacts vulnerable groups of IDPs, in particular women and children. Host-community households also suffer from additional economical and psychosocial burden with the hosting of IDPs, especially when IDPs and host families do not share family relationships nor equivalent social backgrounds. Non-food items will be packaged and pre-positioned in warehouses based on standard specifications and contents. The quantity and specification of non-food items in a household package will be considered with reference to specific needs such as the number of people in a typical household, their age, sex and the presence of people with specific protection needs, as well as the items’ cultural acceptability. The packing of the sets will be done based on the possibility of adding other items or sets according to needs of beneficiaries or complementarity of interventions among various actors or activities

2 - Activities

A1: Reinforcement and capacity-building of a Community-based Emergency Response Network (CERN) to cover the Middle Area, Khan Younis and Rafah governorates: ACTED will reinforce an existing CERN to respond to emergency needs arising from rapid and mass population displacements due to man-made or natural disasters. The coordination mechanism within the CERN will be as follows: ACTED will supervise 3 CBOs in Rafah, Middle Area and Khan Younis; the CBOs manage 1 CBPC each in their respective governorate; the CBPCs operate through a network of volunteers. ACTED will trigger emergency responses following coordination with the Shelter cluster. Upon receiving the alert, the CERN will be responsible for ensuring the timely and quality implementation of the emergency response, including coordination with relevant stakeholders (ACTED, OCHA, affected populations), conducting rapid needs assessments and data collection (within 3 days of the shock), and organizing, delivering and monitoring NFI distributions. The volunteer network will support the CERN in community outreach and distributions. ACTED will provide the 94 CERN members (of which at least 30% will be female) with 3-day refresher training. ACTED will organize a simulation exercise to assess CERN capacity to conduct rapid needs assessments, coordinate emergency response and deploy rapid distributions.A2: Mapping of host communities’ needs and capacities: The CERN supported by their volunteer networks, will identify at least 300 HHs who historically served as host families during previous escalations, and have a maintained capacity to host IDPs in case of future emergency (at least 100 HHs per governorate). The CERN will carry out 10 information sessions with the identified 300 HHs, to build their capacity ahead of future emergencies. ACTED and the CERN will conduct a survey with the 300 HHs to assess their hosting capacity and identify gaps, concerns, and needs that could prevent them from hosting IDPs or hinder the safety and dignity of IDPs and/or host families. The CERN will conduct trainings with the host communities to respond to their identified needs and concerns.A3: Improvement of 3 warehouses in Rafah, Khan Younis and Middle Area: To increase stockpiling capacity and geographic coverage of prepositioned items, ACTED will:- Renew contracts with CBOs for the 3 warehouses set up under previous HF-funded projects;- Increase storage capacity in 2 of these warehouses;- Provide refresher training on warehouse management for the CBOs in charge of the 3 warehouses.The renewal of existing contracts and increased storage capacity for existing warehouses will ensure the prepositioning of emergency NFIs for a period of 24 months after project’s end.A4: Purchase and storage of NFI kits: Based on Shelter Cluster guidelines and past experience in prepositioning NFI kits, ACTED and MA’AN identified 4 NFI sets. Each kit comprises 1 sealing-off set, 1 bedding set, 1 hygiene kit and 1 cleaning set. ACTED will replenish stocks in the 2 existing warehouses and start stockpiling in warehouses based on needs and depletion levels, by purchasing and storing 1500 sealing off sets, 700 bedding sets, 2000 hygiene kits and 1000 cleaning kits (5200 in total).It is expected that over 2000 HHs (comprised of 11400 individuals) will actually benefit from the kits, as all HHs will be provided with sets based on their actual needs rather than receiving a standardized kit. MA’AN and CERN members will adjust the contents of NFI sets to be distributed to each IDP and host HH, based on assessed vulnerabilities and specific needs (i.e. children, women, female-headed HHs, elderly members). As per signed MoUs, CBOs will be expected to closely monitor and regularly report on the NFI prepositioning status in each warehouse during the project and up to 24 months after project end. Finally, ACTED will make sure to integrate PSEA activities throughout the intervention, in line with the PSEA network recommendations and according to IASC principles and guidance. Coordination activities will include ACTED’s direct engagement with the HCT oPt PSEA network as well as collaboration with operational partners to establish or strengthen inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to affected populations in all priority response locations. Similarly, PSEA capacity building activities will include the requirement for project staff to attend PSEA network, as well as the provision of training on PSEA, referral pathways and existing support services to the 94 CERN members. In doing so, ACTED will ensure respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law), whilst also designing activities with the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries in mind. The action will seek to strengthen AAP by emphasizing the development and implementation of effective and quality programming that enhances access to protection while also recognizing the dignity, capacity, and abilities of beneficiaries, most notably through the active involvement of CERN members in the response. This will also help deliver a culture of accountability in the intervention by integrating AAP best practice, including PSEA.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. Specifically, ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) is independent from the Program Department and will be in charge of reinforcing the projects’ accountability throughout the project cycle management by regularly assessing project implementation. AMEU is an essential part of ACTED’s project management structure, providing a mechanism to crosscheck and give recommendations on the work of the project team and partners. AMEU leads assessment of baseline situations of beneficiaries through data collection, monitors ongoing implementation through regular field visits and consultations with beneficiaries, and evaluates the outcome of the activities. This methodology ensures the AMEU uses an outcome monitoring approach to analyse the effect of the action on target groups. ACTED will measure the following indicators: # of community members have improved capacity for emergency response; % of female members of the Community-based Emergency Response Network (CERN); # of host households assessed, trained and/or assisted with NFI; % of CERN member report their satisfaction on the training and the simulation; % of host HHs reported that the raise awareness sessions were useful and covered their needs identified during the mapping exercise; # of individuals that shelter agencies are able to support immediately at the onset of an emergency; # of standard/customized Shelter NFI kits propositioned at agency’s warehouses and # of warehouses contracted, equipped and secured to be allocated for NFIs stockpiling. ACTED’s project management team will use the Project Management Framework (PMF) tool to plan and monitor implementation to assess progress and highlight blocking points. The action will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to provide feedback and suggestions directly to ACTED staff through a phone line, post, email, and complaint boxes in relevant locations, as well as directly to partner’s staff members. The CRM should ensure that beneficiaries can easily and without fear of repercussion communicate cases of abuse of power by financial institutions, community leaders and/or other key actors involved in the cash-transfer program and/or aspects of program and/or transfer modality that inadvertently have negative implications. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Financial monitoring will be undertaken by all project partners and then consolidated by ACTED, with external audits of the action taking place on an annual basis. ACTED staff will request from MA’AN monthly reporting on activities on top of contractual obligations to ensure all indicators are on track and register the beneficiaries’ satisfaction and any difficulties encountered. This monthly reporting will minimise the risk of delays in the activities’ implementation, that any change in the implementation of the project is notified and approved by oPt HF and finally that all indicators are on track and if needed corrective measures are taken to reach the target. Finally, it will enable ACTED to provide comprehensive and clear interim and final reports to oPt HF.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, whatsapp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED’s work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that co-applicants learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. Upon CERN creation, ACTED will provide the 94 CERN members with a 10-day training including Basic Life support, Psychological First Aid, Gender-Based Violence, violence towards children, Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), referral pathways and existing support services; ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis. Finally, all activities will be conducted in strict adherence of COVID-19 mitigation measures including social distancing and personal protective equipment (PPE) to minimise the risk of COVID-19 transmission among beneficiaries, staff and stakeholder alike. For example, the simulation exercise will be conducted in a large open space, with masks, gloves and respecting all protective measures including social distancing.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. ACTED will ensure impartial access to assistance through a strictly needs-based approach. After all, different needs lead to different dimensions of risks and resources for various groups, thus resulting in the necessity of a more comprehensive analysis. ACTED AMEU will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to needs, bearing in mind differing power dynamics. For instance, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted through a survey conducted by ACTED and the CERN of the 300 HHs to assess their hosting capacity and identify gaps, concerns, and needs that could prevent them from hosting IDPs or hinder the safety and dignity of IDPs and/or host families. ACTED will conduct the survey through gender and age-sensitive Focus Groups Discussions, in order to collect perceptions and concerns of different groups (i.e. men, women, children, people with disabilities, elderly people). Individual interviews will also be conducted with at least 1 male and 1 female member of each household, in order to collect their differentiated perceptions and concerns on the hosting process. ACTED will ensure that female staff lead women’s focus group discussions and interviews, to ensure women feel safe to raise any concern they may have regarding the hosting process. Detailed data disaggregated by gender, age and diversity will be collected to ensure a gender mainstreaming approach and measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups. ACTED’s Protection Officer will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM.
