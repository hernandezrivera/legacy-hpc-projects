Project Summary

WFP is implementing a nutrition response frame with a stronger focus on the prevention of malnutrition. The program focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with a focus on the first 1000 days of life. The program targets all children aged 6-23 months including those children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition-MAM) in the most food insecure departments presenting the highest GAM rates in the Far North, North, Adamawa, and East region, while in the Northwest and southwest region the program targets children aged 6-23 months in food insecure household. The program activities include 1. Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment; 2.Promotion of infant and young child feeding practices 3.Distribution of specialized nutritious food to children aged 6-23months 4.Social and behavioral change communication in favor of good nutrition and health practices5.Promotion of the use of the blanket feeding platform or provision of complementary services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning, etc. WFP in 2023 intends to assist about 44000 nutritionally vulnerable persons in the Far North region

1 - Besoins / Needs

In 2023, it is estimated that more than 289,500 people, mainly girls and boys under the age of 5 and pregnant or lactating girls and women (PLW), will be vulnerable in the Far North. It is estimated that a minimum of 126,700 people will suffer from GAM in 2023, including 3,700 pregnant and lactating women and 123,000 girls and boys under the age of 5.The results of the latest nutrition survey (with the SMART method) conducted in February 2021 by UNICEF and the Government of Cameroon showed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 5.9 percent with 0.2 percent of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), at the regional level. The Far North had one of the highest prevalences of chronic malnutrition with 36.4 percent. In addition, a small-scale SMART survey conducted in December 2020 in four health areas highly impacted by population displacement (Bodo, Afadé, Kalafarka et Maltam), showed a GAM rate of 11.1 percent and SAM rate of 1.8 percent, tending to support the hypothesis of Sector partners that areas with increased vulnerability factors (such as IDP concentration, low access to health centers and WASH facilities) present higher acute malnutrition rates. In the Far North, more than 70 percent of the total IDPs are living in host communities. Over 37 percent of them are children under 5 and the average number of children per household is 5.8. Together with the host population, they are amongst the most exposed to the lack of access to drinking water, poor access to health care, poor hygiene, epidemics, and the consequences of poor crop harvests, which can lead to rapid deterioration of their nutritional status. In addition, to the LCB-crisis-related displacement, intercommunity conflict broke out in 2021 in the Logone Birni district and spread to the rest of the Logone et Chari and Mayo-Danay divisions with implications in Mayo-Sava and Diamaré divisions. As of 4 January 2022, 32,901 people were internally displaced according to the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM), among whom 10,000 children under 5 years and 5,500 women of childbearing age. The first screenings performed in the Domayo-Djarma IDP location identified 10 children with wasting. Severe floods have also recently affected nearly 500,000 people in areas already fragilized by the security crisis, among them, 95,000 children under the age of 5 years are put even more at risk of malnutrition. The indicators related to infant and young child feeding practices are among the poorest in the country, with an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 50 percent, a timely introduction rate for complementary foods
