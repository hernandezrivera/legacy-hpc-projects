Needs

UNHAS is committed to continuing the provision of safe, reliable, efficient and effective air transport services to the humanitarian and development communities in Sudan. Close to 5.6 million people required humanitarian assistance in 2017, compared to 4.6 million in 2016. It is projected that the level of need will remain high in 2018, unless the situation in South Sudan changes drastically. The insecurity, vast distances, and poor road infrastructure networks within Sudan severely limit access to the affected population. Since 2004, the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) has been the main source through which NGOs, UN agencies and the donor community access hard-to-reach locations to implement and monitor humanitarian and development projects

Activities

Put together UNHAS flight schedule based on access needs while ensuring deep field connection to/from Khartoum are maintained, whenever practicable. Flight frequency for localities served will also vary depending on the caseload vis/vis UNHAS capacity and air asset fleet composition. The fleet capacity is based on target location and humanitarian/development community needs taking into account service reliability, effectiveness and efficiency to allow agility in security relocations and access to new locations without compromising the continuity of ongoing projects. To ensure a continuous risk analysis and mitigation, UNHAS works closely with UNANMID, WFP, UNDSS and NGO security forum. UNHAS will share flight timings and any other relevant information to ensure safe extraction. UNHAS engages Users in determining flight schedule amendments (adding or removing locations) through monthly User Group meetings held in the three Darfur sates and Khartoum. Change in service delivery and/or policy is through quarterly UNHAS steering Committee meeting that includes Donors, the UN and NGOs. Transport 120 metric tons of light cargo such as medical supplies and information and communications technology (ICT) equipment.
