Project Summary

South Sudan faces an increased risk of explosive hazards where over 18 million m² of land is suspected of being contaminated with landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW). The highest levels of contamination are mainly located within the Jonglei state (Canal/Pigi) and Eastern Equatorial (Magwi) which has the highest returnees from Uganda.
Explosive ordnances inhibit civilians from collecting water or firewood, cultivating land, attending school, and receiving health care, among other essential services. They further prevent humanitarian organizations from accessing vulnerable communities or providing lifesaving assistance in conflict-affected areas.
The population census records 96097 people in Canal/Pigi with 4445 KM square area and 169826 in Magwi in 2008 with 5202km square. This totals to 265923 people who are exposed to death or injuries by explosive ordinance. IMSMA reports March 2022 shows that a total of more than 20 UXOs were identified and 15 destroyed in Magwi. For this reason, CINA’s EORE project will target host communities, IDPs and returnees from Uganda and Kenya.
EORE activities seeks to reduce the risk of injury from mines and Explosive Ordnance by raising awareness and promoting behavioral change, including public information dissemination, education and training at community level. It will be the role of EORE Educators to move from community to community, schools, churches, door to door etc sensitizing and raising the awareness on safety and prevention messages about the EO. CINA EORE project in Canal/Pigi and Magwi will employ preventive measures to deal with injuries which come as lack of knowledge especially to the returnees and IDPs who may not be well conversant with the local marking signs. CINA will reach 45,125 people (8,500 Women, 7,125 Men, 15,000 girls, 14,500 boys) including 6,750 people with disability to be reached with EORE messaging in Jonglei state (Canal/Pigi) and Eastern Equatoria (Magwi county). These will include the elderly, IDPs, returnees, host community who have a lot of potential in socio-economic activities for improving their livelihoods.
This project will ensure safety of all beneficiaries and encourage them to provide feedback on the relevant of EORE materials, messages and other concerns about EORE activities as means of AAP and coordinate with MA SC all activities. CINA will deploy EORE team in both Canal/Pigi and Magwi Counties. From experience, this approach is more sustainable because CINA normally strives to work with local structures as much as possible. In Magwi County, CINA EORE Team One who were already engaged in implementing the UNICEF EORE activities will be reinstated to continue with the EORE . In Canal/Pigi, CINA EORE Mobile Team Three which has been responding in Ayod (where more than 8 UXOs have already been identified awaiting clearance) will be deployed to conduct EORE interventions there.

1 - Needs

The Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) data set Magwi June 2022 reported about 20 UXOs identified in the area. The population of 8419 (1385 Men, 2435 Women, 2176 Boys, 2423 Girls) who received EORE at still at risk. Humanitarian actors implementing EORE activities recognize that estimating the true extent of landmines and Explosive Ordnance (EO) contamination within South Sudan is a real challenge specifically within Canal/Pigi and Magwi counties counties. There are existing testimonies for example in Magwi County where CINA has been implementing EORE activities through SSHF and UNICEF funds. Despite several years of interventions by different humanitarian actors in regards to EORE activities in South Sudan, many people including host community, IDPs and Returnees are still engaged in practicing risky behaviours that continue to cause injuries and death of civilians. This is evident also by the fact that although a significant number of UXO incidents are reported to EORE survey123 software, the exact number of EO casualties in the Country is still unknown and is considered to be more higher than the number of the cases reported. It has been realized that explosive hazards prohibit civilians from a safe and secure environment in which they can access life-saving services, collect water, firewood or other daily subsistence needs such as cultivation of land and/or fishing. This is more practical in deep field where landmines and unexploded ordinance (UXO) inhibit the ability of women, men, girls, and boys to meet their basic needs and access opportunities to survive (HNO 2022). The targeted beneficiaries need more EORE messages to be in the know of the dangers encompassing their lives when they approach unknown substances/UXOs that can be harmful to their lives or to those who live among them in the same locality. Such unknown objects may include anti-personnel mines, anti-tank mines, unexploded ordinances among others. CINA has experienced EORE Teams who have been trained through funds by UNICEF and SSHF. These competent staff will be used as well as the existing EORE banners printed by UNICEF to scale up education in schools, markets and other public places where there are systematic gathering on weekly basis. This project will conduct need assessments to inform risk education for Canal/Pigi and Magwi Counties.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1. Conduct awareness raising sessions to people (including those with disabilities) on key EORE messages in Counties of Canal/Pigi and Magwi County. Every EORE Session in school and community will take approximately 45 mins each. Due to large number of children in school, arrangements will be made with teachers so that it is done per class meaning in one day; there will be two sessions in a particular school. The schools normally accept 3 days visit in every week and the attendance per class is usually between 20-30 as per COVID 19 guidelines . However, the introduction sessions might have the whole school parade benefit from public information dissemination for more pupils to be informed on EORE messages. The team will use the EOR printed banners provided by UNMAS since they are generally accepted internationally. Activity 2. Carry out one (1) EORE awareness sessions for humanitarian actors from GBV, child protection, health, FSL working groups/actors to to improve coordination in service delivery for mine/ERW survivors and PWDs in each county. Having an integrated approach across all sectors will help CINA actors receive information related to EO fast and help in effective decision making as well as protection mainstreaming. Equipping every sector with key EORE messages will help the humanitarian actors take care of their own safety together with others as they respond in their project locations. Activity 3. Conduct community-based EORE training to caregivers of mine/ERWs victims, community leaders, local authority leader, women and youth groups reps etc by the second quarter in both Canal/Pigi and Magwi Counties. These topics to be covered will include but not limited to education on dangers of EO, learning about local markings around suspected UXOs, how and who to report any spotted mine/ERW, what to do when one comes across an UXO, informing others about which areas are safe/unsafe, information on small arms and light weapons (SALW), etc. The team will basically use the EORE printed banners that were provided by UNMAS since they are of the international standard in size, colour and quality. Activity 4. Convene one-day training to teachers/PTAs/SMCs on EORE messages to impart knowledge to the children at school and their families in both locations. Members of Parents and Teachers Association (PTAs), School Management Committees (SMCs) who will be trained on key EORE messages will act as EORE Mentors in schools, homes and places of residence and in their communities in both counties. Activity 5. Provide training to adolescent peer educators on EORE messaging to support in delivery of risk education safety messages in schools and community. Most of the at-risk populations are often children and women. Training and support to peer educators will not only help in disseminating EORE messages to large number of people but will help in project sustainability. CINA will therefore train adolescent peer educators on RE per targeted location for a period of 3 days on the key EORE messages. They will then be tasked to replicate the same messages in schools and community. Activity 6. Establish/strengthen and train Disabled People's Organizations to advocate for the rights of mine/ERWs survivors, PWDs among other minority groups. After establishment/strengthening of DPOs, training of the DPOs members will help in the sustainability of the project after the project closure. The DPO members will be empowered to act as agents of change and keep informing the communities of the dangers of mine/ERWs and importance of risk education to the community members, IDPs and Returnees. In each county, there will be one DPO. Activity 7. Provide training for CINA EORE team, accreditation, internal quality assurance and IMSMA reporting including on EORE survey 123 software.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CINA works to ensure women, girls, men and boys with disabilities have access to vital services and have the opportunity to apply their skills and capacities to benefit themselves, their families and communities. CINA also strive to identify the issues they face and develop long-term solutions

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CINA EORE teams will have a two-way communication between the affected population and the Team members. The affected population will inform the EORE team about locations of spotted mines/UXOs and as CINA EORE team also educate them on messages that change behavior. During CINA monitoring and evaluation activities, the project will be able to have an interactive communication with all stakeholders and views analyzed and shared. The EORE teams will encourage beneficiaries to give suggestions on areas of improvement or to report any malpractices. EORE Teams will be empowered to maximize transparency and ensuring that complaints and concerns are addressed, feedback accessible and participation is inclusive because different people can be affected differently

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During implementation of activities, banners with PSEA messages and telephone number will be displayed during EORE sessions in the community, and beneficiaries will be sensitized about importance of reporting any suspected PSEA case using the PSEA form or telephone call. Feedback will be relayed back through CINA PSEA focal person or the Project Manager. CINA commits to do all the needful for preventing programmes having any negative effects such as SEA byCINA employee and relate personnel against beneficiaries and members of the community. CINA takes seriously all the complaints and concerns about sexual exploitation and abuse and it initiates rigors investigation of complaints that indicates a possible violation of this policy, CINA Code of Conduct and HR policy.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The EORE project will ensure that beneficiaries get information about the project through use of information awareness sessions, use of Disabled People's Organizations, Peer Educators and Community Liaison. Community Complaint and Feedback boxes will be displayed at the sites during EORE sessions and members sensitized to drop in their complaints or feedback using a form. Atelephone number will be displayed on EORE banners where community members can call and report their complaints/feedback. In addition, feedback will be collected during M&E activities.
