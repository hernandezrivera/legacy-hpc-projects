Project Summary

The impact of the crisis on the infrastructure and human resources in health sector have seriously increased health needs and affected the access of people in need to health care services. This project aims at providing comprehensive health care services to affected people ( men, women, boys, girls ) from different communities (IDPs, returnees and host community) in Damascus and Rural Damascus. Activities in Damascus will target IDPs and the overburdened community. Damascus is considered as home to thousands of IDPs and functions as key referral sites for secondary care, thereby placing heavy loads on the health system. Rural Damascus is an area where returnees movement is high and where health facilities are insufficient. This project will support provision of health care services through 4 static centers in Damascus, Azzabadani, Kafarbatna, and Harasta, and through 6 medical mobile teams that will covers Babbila, Ein elfijeh, Madaya, Arbin, Sehnaya, and Qudsaya. Targeted locations are of severity scale 3, and 4 where AlTamayoz has access and needed approval and previous presence in some of them.
Planned activities will be in line with Health sector objective No. 1 and No. 3 that will include:
Provision of medical consultations to 70000 patients ((men, women, boys, girls)
Provision of trauma consultations to 1000 patients
Support mental health consultations, target is 5000
Support the provision of 1000 physical rehabilitation sessions
Support 500 vaginal deliveries and 200 caesarian deliveries
Support 1500 patients with referral for specialized treatment including secondary operations
Support 4000 women with Ante-Natal Care services
Support 500 health workers with medical masks and gloves (selected health workers are those deployed for this project in 10 locations in addition to health workers at 10 other NGOs which provide health care services in Damascus and Rural Damascus and are not supported by other UN agencies)
Support 50 health workers with protective gowns
Support the delivery of 50000 treatment courses
Train 100 health workers on mhGAP, PFA, and other Health topics
The selected locations for this project are also selected for other 2 projects under protection and education, a well-organized referral pathway will be in place that will enable a comprehensive package of services for people in need especially women who are at risk of GBV or GBV survivors, children with certain protection risks. Project will also assure coordination with other actors in the targeted locations for any further assistance (legal, nutritional,….).
The project will take into consideration people with disabilities of different ages through providing suitable and easy access in the facility, and deploying trained staff for support, in addition to using the mobile team modality. Special care will be provided to the other vulnerable groups (children under 5, elderly, and women of reproductive age).
Al Tamayoz will be responsible of deploying well qualified staff to assure the qualitative implementation, and a good monitoring and evaluation measures will be in place, Reports will be prepared on monthly basis and will be shared with the health sector, project implementation will be monitored through periodical assessment to make sure that results and project outcome are on track, assessment are made during activities implementation, Monitoring activities will include : consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place, Conduct filed visits, include monitoring of access to services by sex, age, host/IDP/ Returnees status, and by people with disability. Evaluation will be through collecting and analyzing of information that will be delivered to the project manager for the timely action. A complain box will be available in each center and will be opened on daily basis and supervised by the M&E officer who in turn will direct the complains to the general manager for any required action.

4 - Needs

“As the Syria crisis approaches its tenth year, the health system remains heavily disrupted. With just 58%of hospitals and 53%of PHCs fully functional, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system. In 2020, households and health workers experienced increased stress, placing additional demand on mental health services. Due to cumulative effects of concurrent crises, cost of health services; lack of medicines, supplies or health staff; and fear of exposure to COVID-19 have replaced distance, over-crowding and long wait times as the main self-reported barriers to health access with persons with disabilities disproportionately affected. Disrupted medication supply chains, particularly for psychotropic drugs and NCD medicines were previously identified as key gap in the health response and contributor child mortality” HNO draft 2021. As per Syrian morbidity and mortality summary No. 7: Between 23 March and 13 April 2021 there were (54308) COVID-19 cases confirmed by rt-PCR, 37.4% of total number (20331) were reported from Damascus including (1385) related deaths. the positivity rate of the total tests reached 26.1%in RD and 21.6 % in Damascus. According to health sector severity scale and population in need Jan 2021, there are (1,005,126) PiN for health care services in Damascus,(185,668) in babella,(21512.7) in Kafrbatna,(11161) in Arbin,(245688) in qudsaya,(93621) in Harasta,(16280) in Azzabadani, (19148) in Einelfijeh,(17294) in Madaya and (33770) in Sehnaya. As from AlTamayoz observations in Rural Damascus (Azzabadani) where health care services were provided under grant from WHO during the second half of 2020, the only public hospital is still used as a quarantine center.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims at supporting vulnerable people in Damascus and Rural Damascus with comprehensive health care services. Activities will be implemented though 4 static centres and 6 mobile teams: static centres will be in Damascus, Kafr Batna, Harasta, Az-Zabdani, AlTamayoz already has operational points in those areas which serve as points to provide several services and AlTAmayoz aims at expanding the support to people in need to include primary and secondary health services. Mobile teams will be functioning in babella, Arbin, Qudsaiya, Ein Elfijeh, Madaya, and Sehnaya. The main activities will be: Provision of essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, MHPSS and referral.Establishing medical mobile units, and will include some activities related to COVID-19 response.Detailed activities are: Provision of medical consultations to 70000 patients ((men, women, boys, girls)Provision of trauma consultations to 1000 patients Support mental health consultations, target is 5000 Support the provision of 1000 physical rehabilitation sessions Support 500 vaginal deliveries and 200 caesarian deliveriesSupport 1500 patients with referral for specialized treatment including secondary operationsSupport 4000 women with Ante-Natal Care servicesSupport 500 health workers with medical masks and gloves Support 50 health workers with protective gownsSupport the delivery of 50000 treatment coursesTrain 100 health workers on mhGAP, PFA, and other Health topicsAlTamayoz will be responsible of deploying well qualified staff to assure the qualitative implementation, and a good monitoring and evaluation measures will be in place (as mentioned in Summary part)

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: women and girls are at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence.Likelihood: high, Impact: highMitigating measures: Women will have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package available at all health facilities under this project. Train at least one health staff in each center, especially those who are likely to come in contact with GBV survivors during the provision of health services, on providing trauma-sensitive, survivor-centered first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. All staff should receive training on communicating with persons with diverse disabilities to enable women and girls with disabilities to be identified and receive support on an equal basis Monitoring: Monitor service mapping and referral pathway development to case management to ensure inclusion of services that can provide safe and confidential lifesaving response.
