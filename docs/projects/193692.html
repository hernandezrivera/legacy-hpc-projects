Project Summary

Timely life-saving Shelter and NFI assistance is essential to prevent physical hazard and ensure safety, security, and dignity of IDPs and vulnerable residents. The purpose of the project is to contribute to improving protection, safety & security, and dignity of new IDPs through provision of life-saving assistance, and extremely vulnerable people as well as PWSNs living in protracted displacement with more sustainable, emergency-recovery type of interventions. The project targets localities in Khartoum, Al Jazeera, East and South Darfur states that experienced several displacements in 2023 and are still hotspot areas. The project basically targets two groups; first new crises-affected people will be provided with emergency shelter making materials & NFI assistance. Second, people living in protracted displacement will be supported with replenishment of S/NFI and shelter upgrading solutions. Based on the experience, in this project we planned to support 97,698 people (about 16,583HHs) by ES/NFIs.
To achieve the above purposes Alight will monitor situations; preparing hotspot areas and identifying affected & vulnerable communities, and raising awareness of the communities on hazards. We regularly coordinate with HAC and UNHCR to exchange information on existing humanitarian situations. Whenever responses are required (suppose conditions worsen or new humanitarian conditions emerge such as displacement of people due to conflict or extreme hazard (drought/flood)) then Alight will trigger joint need assessment (with state level stakeholders), conduct joint verification exercises and response plans. Any response prioritization will be done through cluster conventional processes. Activities that are in line with cluster objectives and relevant to the locality culture and traditions are carefully selected and will be implemented to deliver effective responses. Wherever and whenever possible Cash Base intervention (CBI) (up to 20% of total budget) will be applied to help households fulfill their S/NFI needs. Alight will engage local authorities, COR, HAC, UNHCR and other key partners and jointly monitor and Supervise distributions and conduct Post distribution evaluation and document learnings.
To make this project a success, Alight uses its leverage of cumulated knowledge and experience. During implementation, Alight will work with the communities and local administrations, mobilize its potential suppliers and transporters, which will help to timely responses. Also, in case NFI gaps occur in the market Alight will utilize the Core Pipeline to respond to emergencies. As much as possible uniformity of responses will be respected by applying standardized NFI packages and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) developed by the ES/NFI cluster. Wherever necessary to deliver holistic response, Alight will combine WASH and protection responses with the S/NFI distribution.
As much as possible Alight will respect and take care of the environment, culture, traditions and local businesses. We will use environmentally friendly and culturally appropriate shelter materials, most of the procurements will be locally sourced, which will help to activate local economies. Alight also provides on-the-job training in building techniques of shelters that will be useful for developing self-reliance and resilience.
To improve accountability, transparency in programming, and adherence to “Do no harm principles,” Alight will strengthen the capacity of staff (men, women, and youth) and local national NGOs through training in the complete package of PSET for emergency response. Additionally, Alight will implement the project by considering protection and accountability to the community and to the donor. Our MEAL system will gather important data and produce information. Communities will be provided with information about our complaints and feedback systems and Alight document complaints, refer and take corrective measures.

1 - Needs

According to Shelter & NFI Cluster, Severity and PiN Calculation for revised 2023 HNO (severity level 4, &5), it is projected that about 5.7 million people in Sudan needs Shelter and NFI in 2023. Recent Conflict affected, protracted political and economic crises coupled with tribal conflict and frequent natural emergencies eroded the community's coping mechanism and made them vulnerable for disasters. These Highly vulnerable populations need life-saving assistance and protection as per national and international standards. Additionally, due to climate change's effect, the intensity of drought and flood increased affecting hundreds of thousands of people in the project areas. Most affected population groups are IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations battling extreme poverty who do not afford to buy Shelter and NFI services. In addition, people in protracted displacement, over 5.7 million (as per revised HNO2023) also need resources to maintain or transition their shelter to semi-permanent shelter and fulfill their NFI needs. Generally, resilience building is required to enhance coping capacities, to reduce the impact of disasters, and lessen chronic vulnerability by means of DRR and early recovery approaches. At state level there is need to improve the coordination mechanisms between actors, UNHCR and the state governments to address issues of chronic vulnerability and provide durable solutions. Moreover, reviewing frameworks for emergency preparedness and response and state level development of strategy including but not limited to capacity building, development of Information management tools, contingency planning are required to strengthen the government planning and response capacity. After April 15, 2023 conflict-related displacements affected several people in all localities of Khartoum, South Darfur, Al Jazeera and other states. Emergency S/NFI needs are eminent. The population living in protracted displacement continues to reside in non-durable and unsafe dwelling structures. Even though some of them wanted to maintain and improve or upgrade their shelters to semi-permanent structure, financial constraints limit their ability to do so, while availability of shelter materials and skills necessary to construct/repair shelters are not a gap. At same time, in many places, though displaced communities are vulnerable need assistance t the hosting community are also living in staggering needy conditions. IDPs will be unprepared for departure and will need assistance through the provision of basic NFIs. While experience showed the majority of displaced people seek shelter in camps, whilst the remaining others seek refuge with host communities, either with family or friends or in rented accommodation. In the targeted project areas, it is predicted that majority of IDPs will probably move into hotspots’ locality safe areas as in previous IOM migration route shows. Also, it's common that IDPs will bring their livestock as it is their most valuable asset and source of livelihood. Even if Sudanese have good hosting culture, hostilities between IDPs and host population due to security and resource factors may arise (hostilities expected to be heightened after emergency phase end). As much as resources allow, projects should consider such scenarios and carefully respond to both communities. While new displacements are expected due to natural and human-induced factors, returns to original places would also occur and the communities who voluntarily return need provision of appropriate shelter repair kits and basic household NFIs.Also, response needs to be tailored to ensure every segment of the affected communities including PWSNs involved in all stages of the program cycle through consultation and two-way feedback mechanisms and get adequate humanitarian assistance.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ALIGHT will use environmentally friendly approaches such as use of conventional S/NFI materials and locally available materials for construction and maintenance activities. ALIGHT uses local culturally appropriate materials for shelter construction such as mud bricks, grass, bamboo sticks and these are easily renewable and have less environmental impact. Also, Alight includes sensitization activities on environmental degradation and climate change impact, communities will get awareness through trained community volunteers and encouraged to plant indigenous trees. At start of rainy season to reduce the impact of malaria, Alight will conduct dedicated malaria prevention campaigns and environmental interventions such as drainage improvement and collection and safe disposal of solid wastes and filling potholes and draining pools. These activities will be conducted by community participation. Alight will follow the GoS environmental assessment before intervention. Infrastructure locations will be carefully selected in order to avoid water sources and environmental contamination. Our environmental health activities that include solid waste collection, transport and disposal and de-sludging and disposal of latrines will be carried out in consultation with the states WES and according to the regulation of the country. For this project, Alight will not use any chemicals that have negative effects on nature and the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Alight’s design of services ensures their accessibility across the gender, age and diversity (GAD) categories and vulnerability scenarios, with special provisions for at high-risk categories of beneficiaries such as female headed households, young girls, older and severely ill and people with disabilities. Alight ensures all persons with special needs, and all persons assessed to be a higher protection risk are prioritized. We shall ensure their active participation through routine analysis of protection risks, individual and group assessments, and, where possible, encourage their participation and representation in all affairs of their communities. This will help Alight to develop project designs that are adapted to mitigate the protection risks of the users based on improving Safety, Access and Dignity (SAD) of the users of services. Alight will work with other members of the community to strengthen the social protection for people with specific needs through mobilizing community support. Alight is committed to the principle of `do no harm' in humanitarian actions. The interventions are designed in a way to mainstream the four main protection principles in project interventions; ie avoiding exposing people to further harm; ensuring access to impartial assistance; and protecting people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion while assisting people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuse. The project services will be accessible to avoid exposing the beneficiaries, particularly women, to any risks. Necessary measures will be taken to preserve the privacy and dignity of the target beneficiaries. To ensure dignity and rights of beneficiaries to health and nutrition services, the project will ensure that the provided interventions are acceptable to the community and they take part in it through involvement of community leaders and community volunteers. Services are provided with impartiality regardless of sex, gender, ethnicity or religious affiliation.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Alight’s commitment to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) focuses on implementing systems and methods that reveal client experience of organizational services so that we can align a response and re-establish trust between Alight and the IDPs, host communities and refugees who are our clients. Thus, Alight believes that trust works in both directions and therefore it must be built on the foundation of honesty and mutual accountability. It is this belief that defines our work in enhancing greater involvement of these clients in designing, developing and implementing programs, defining their quality outcomes, and solving problems alongside them. These principles hold us accountable to improving the quality, relevance and impact of our work in the interest of beneficiaries. With deep and well-established links with the communities in the target localities, we have built capacity of community structures that support day-to-day planning and implementation of activities that include community nutrition screening, community water quality monitoring, community clean up campaigns, community household visits, and health education. Through these relationships, we have ongoing conversations with these community members about their ideas, concerns, and needs. These ongoing discussions help shape our program designs.To further demonstrate our commitment to AAP, we will continue ensuring strong accountability in our humanitarian responses toward affected communities and individuals directly not only through the hitherto mentioned involvement, but also by encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. This will be done via periodic focus group discussions (FGDs), community forums, community meetings, community awareness and outreach campaigns, branding and marking, and ongoing feedback/complaint response mechanism (F-CRM) throughout this project. Alight has also started producing briefs and a monthly newsletter which will be translated into Arabic and that will be accessible to targeted communities.
