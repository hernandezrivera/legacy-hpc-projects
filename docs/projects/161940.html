Project Summary

NGO Forum in partnership with NCA and AAR will respond to the WASH needs where refugees are living amongst the host community focusing in Teknaf areas. The security context in the target area has recently deteriorated, with increasing tensions between host communities and refugees, exacerbated by environmental disasters, lack of access to income-generating activities and pressure on local resources, such as water and agricultural land. NGO Forum/NCA will consult with the refugees and host communities in order to prioritise activities that will integrate the needs of the communities. The Teknaf area suffers each year from water scarcity during the dry season and, because the Master Water Network planning process will not begin before 2020, therefore the proposed project will include contingency capacity for water trucking to cover drinking-water needs. NGO Forum/NCA/AAR will maintain and improve water and sanitation infrastructures and services to support rights holders in coping with natural disasters. The project will continue with hygiene promotion activities and distribute Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) items based on latest REACH survey identified women preferences, and explore the use of the voucher system in conducive market environment. As an integrated programming approach, NGO Forum/NCA/AAR will train and sensitise the WASH team, especially the community volunteers, on SGBV topics enabling them to identify cases and do referrals. Specific gender concern in the WASH program: Within the humanitarian community itself, site planning and WASH infrastructure planning/design processes have mainly been dominated by male architects and engineers, often with gendered assumptions about the user. In general, latrine provision in the initial stages of the response was largely determined through an engineering lens or from the perspective of quantitively reaching minimum standards, and did not always incorporate women and girls’ needs, gender disparities and socio-cultural constructs. The proposed project will aim to strongly focus on the consultation aspect during the initial deisgn phase of a gender segregated latrine construction, to address the major issues identified in prior WASH and gender specific research, namely the sharing facilities, lack of sex-segregation, distance, privacy, dignity, and difficulties to manage menstruation. The expected impact of the gender focused WASH activities will be the improved qualitative WASH indicators and a reduction in the percentage of households (women and men) facing problems accessing latrines and bathing facilities, and a reduction of percentage of women and men reporting feeling unsafe while accessing latrine facilities, as well as an overall increase in the percentage satisfaction of WASH services provided.

Objectives

"1. To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs. 2. To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable sanitation (including householdwaste) facilities to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times. 3. To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviours through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities."

Needs

"Bangladesh hosts about 913,988 (Ref. UNHCR population factsheet as of 30 September 2019) individual Rohingyas from Myanmar who are living 32 congested camps in Cox’s Bazar district. Among them 41,007 and 14,269 individual Rohingyas are living in the camps 26 and 27 respectively in Teknaf upazila. In Teknaf upazila, Rohingyas refugees are living amongst the host community. The security context in the area recently deteriorated with increasing tensions between host communities and refugees, exacerbated by environmental disasters, lack of access to income generating activities and by the pressure on local resources such as water and agriculture lands availability. The WASH response capacity is challenged by these factors such as limited access of space, hilly topography leading partners to consider rented lands to ensure the provision of quality services. In addition, the area is currently particularly affected by AWD , cumulating 75 of the 88 cases reported from the 1st of January until the 23rd of October. Among those, 25 were confirmed in camp 26 and 27 and 9 suspected, reaching both Rohingyas and host communities. Access to safe water in Teknaf camps and in host communities remains a critical need in the WASH response. Due to the geological formation, Teknaf area is suffering each year from water scarcity during the dry season. During this period, 42% of the population (41,487 persons ) receives less than 15 litres of water per day. People in camp 27 are highly impacted by the water scarcity throughout the year where only 31% of the drinking water needs are covered . Current water infrastructures collecting water from different sources (rainwater, surface water, aquifer) require upgrade and maintenance to increase their efficiency while the WASH sector and partners together with the authorities of Bangladesh will start to work in 2020 on the water network master plan. Gender specific concern: When looking at the sub-camps level, the current gap regarding the number of gender-segregated latrines is about 25% in camps 26 and 27 representing 14,000 people in need, while the gap to be covered in terms of gender-segregated bathing spaces is 88% in camp 26 and 39% in camp 27 i.e. 28550 people. In addition, priorities must be given to the protection of vulnerable groups as 37% of children and adolescents and 36% of female individuals in camp 26 reported feeling unsafe while accessing or using latrines due to lack of segregation, lack of security at night and unsafe infrastructure in general. Solid waste management (SWM) remain an important issue both in camp and host community, favouring health hazard as demonstrated with the presence of dengue cases. Only 40% of the respondents are using a SWM system, while the rest of the solid waste is thrown into the drainage, the open, or is buried. Following the monsoon season, and the sector guidelines, sanitation infrastructures require improvement and maintenance in order to ensure good sanitary conditions in the camps and host communities and limit the risk of WASH related diseases. Less than half of the population (47%) knows and practices critical handwashing, 35% of the interviewees in camp 26 and 27 dispose the < 5 children’s faeces in the open revealing a great need for improving their hygiene behavior avoiding diseases. Since the International Organization for Migration (IOM) stopped the Hygiene top-up kits pipeline in March 2019, humanitarian actors have struggled to meet basic hygiene needs. Due to weaknesses of the community engagement activities in the overall response, communities lack empowerment and ownership, which often causes problems with operation and maintenance of the facilities."

Activities or outputs

"In close coordination with the DPHE, site management, and CiCs, with the overall emphasis on WASH resilience, user/community engagement, safe, all-inclusive access, and consultation of womena and girls, the proposed project will incoporate the following list of activities. 1) Water supply: Rainwater harvesting, O&M of existing facilities and upgrading to solar water pumping, rehabilitation of surface water storage. Monitoring of the aquifers and ensuring water quality. 2) Sanitation: O&M of existing facilities, construction of latrines (with gender sensitive approach and consultation, as identified in prior research and workshop recommendations from Oxfam research on social architecture: [a] Design adaptations to existing facilities with the installation of solar lights in all latrines and wash facilities, door locks and bins for MHM, shelves and ropes for hanging clothing. [b] Other aspects of MHM in design, including washing, drying and hygiene, to ensure waste water is fully managed for each facility, and water available close to bathing facilities. [c] Make improvements for privacy and dignity with screens, develop cleaning mechanisms with communities, and better access routes from households) and handwashing stations, and bathing space. Construction and maintenance of biogas facilities, solide waste management and recycling. Awareness raising on approriate separation of waste and reducing at the household level 3) Hygiene Promotion activities: Improving messeaging focusing on latest REACH survey identified gaps in knowledge and practice. RANAS approach to enable better barrier identification and promote safe practice and health seeking behavior. MHM and hygiene kit distribution based on latest assessed needs and recommended actions from REACH MHM surveys, with gender and age disaggregated data and corresponding relevant recommended actions. Explore E-vouchers on hygiene or MHM kit if market condition and government policy allows. 4) Maintenance and opreration of existing WASH facilities (latrines, bathing cubicles) in the host community in Hnila union of Teknaf upazila including school latrine in selected school, adjacent to the selected camp 25 of Hnila union. All the WASH facilities maintained are gender-segregated and checked poctograms are propery put. For the safety and security, proper lighting, door locks will also be monitored and maintained in functional condition. CRM has been introduced and will be continuously operated in the project so that beneficiaries and people in host community can report complaint and feedback through hotline, maintainance comittee or in person. Also, training on PSEA will be provided to all staffs and CRM guidline which integrate PSEA policy has been introduced. Monitoring will be conducted periodicaly and repair and maintainance will be taken in consideration of the different needs of beneficiaries including female, male, girls, boys, adolescent, persons and children with disabilities and the elderly. 5)Hygiene awareness session and awareness session on MHM for school children (boys and girls) to promote hygiene practices and change behaviours among school children in Nhila union. According to the report of Innovations for Poverty Action, 27.2% of women in the host community mentioned that they throw their used sanitary napkins into the latrine, which can be harmful to the sanitation system as it may clog latrines. Also, 70.8% of women in host community use cloth to manage blood flow during menstrual period, which is higher rate than in camp, which is 58.8%. As knowledge on MHM is crucial to maintain health and welbeing of adlescent girls, AAR Japan and NGO Forum will conduct MHM awareness session in schools targeting adolescent girls.Activities 1-3 will be implemented in coorporaion with NCA while 4 and 5 with AAR Japan."
