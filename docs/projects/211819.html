Project Summary

In the context of 13 years of successive conflict and protracted humanitarian crises, Syri’s health systems continue to suffer and struggle, acute emergencies of natural and man-made disasters, recurrent disease outbreaks, and climatic shocks on top of the prolonged and complex crises further impacted and increased morbidities and mortalities of the affected communities.
Escalating deteriorating socio-economic conditions, poor infrastructure/WASH conditions, power shortage, food insecurity, malnutrition, and water crises have all also affected and worsened the health sector situation and further contributed to recurring disease outbreaks with subsequent negative health outcomes.
According to the Sector PiN - HNO, Aleppo and Idleb contain among the highest people in need of health assistance, whereby around 7.2 million are in need of health assistance in the four governorates as follows (Aleppo 3,198,455 – Idleb 2,470,487 - Homs 830,102 - Deir-ez-Zor 741,866).
Furthermore, through SSSD’s database from the health points and through the evaluation carried out by SSSD’s teams, the following was concluded:
The most common health complaints observed in the areas of intervention by the doctors at the health points were cardiovascular (mainly arterial hypertension, diabetes), and GU/RH followed by respiratory, gastrointestinal and musculoskeletal.
A large number of areas lack the minimum medical and health requirements (lack of medical personnel and necessary equipment, distance from surrounding health centers, and a number of health facilities being out of service).
Also, based on interviews with stakeholders conducted in mid-November, the team was told that a significant number of people are in need of health services, but cannot get the latter due to difficulties in transportation, as well as customs that do not allow women to go outside alone.
Implementation of activities
2024 Furthermore, SSSD aims to provide primary healthcare services to people in need through 4 mobile medical units in4 governorates, namely Homs (Al-Qusayr), Idleb (Sanjar), Aleppo (Haritan) and Deir-ez-Zor (Deir-ez-Zor city) for people in need of health assistance.
The starting point would be from SSSD’s health points, whereby the mobile medical units will go to hard-to-reach areas, where beneficiaries cannot access health facilities due to difficulties in transportation, furthermore, primary healthcare services will be provided, including consultations, medicinal treatments, health screening, RH services, ECHO testing, health and GBV awareness-raising, through 4 mobile medical units (1 mobile clinic in each governorate), targeting 28,500 beneficiaries from consultations and preliminary screening,16,000 beneficiaries from medicines_ including NCD treatments, and 43,200 beneficiaries from health and GBV awareness sessions.
Each mobile medical unit will have one internist, one gynecologist, one nurse, one midwife, one health educator, one GBV educator, 2 facilitators and one team leader. As for the visits, they will be conducted 5 days per week (from Sun to Th), from 9 am till 3 pm.
Also, throughout the project, the M&E team will conduct monthly visits to the areas of interventions, do spot checks, and make phone calls to ask about the beneficiaries' satisfaction of the services provided.
The sector's performance outputs and outcomes will be evaluated against targets set in the log frame.
The reports will capture sex, age and disability disaggregation for service delivery indicators and monitor delivery to ensure close alignment between delivery of assistance and populations in greatest need.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NeedsThe fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population.As per the 2024 HNO, over 14.9 million people in Syria are estimated to be in need of life-saving primary and secondary care health assistance in 2024, including 5.1 million displaced persons, 2.3 million children under 5 years (including 532,595 live births expected), 4.1 million women of reproductive age, more than 700,000 older people, and 2.4 million persons with disabilities, as well as those with early onset Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) account for 49.8% of mortality in Syria.Access to and functionality of basic health services remains the main challenge affecting all population groups in Syria. Close to 40% of primary and secondary care health facilities serving over 4.8 million people in need of life-saving health services are either partially functioning or not functional.Over 150 sub-districts with 2 million people in need remain “underserved” by the health sector, mainly due to funding gaps and a shortage of skilled healthcare workers.Around 26% – 38% of residents have reported difficulty in accessing health facilities in less than one hour's distance, particularly in Aleppo.Districts classified as severe (3) & and extreme (4) in the health sector severity have increased from 89 and 113 in 2023 to 122 and 118 in 2024, respectively.A devastating 7.8 magnitude earthquake hit Northern Syria in early 2023, with a devastating impact on the health system, 67 health facilities were damaged by the earthquakes, of which six were destroyed.In contrast, some of health centers suffered significant damage and had to go out of service, in addition, 133 primary health care centers and 18 hospitals in some governorates were partially damaged, health sector damages and losses due to the earthquake have been estimated at 312.3 million USD, while short- and medium-term recovery requirements total 935.5 million.The February earthquake not only damaged health facilities but also fragile medicine equipment and devices such as C-arms, MRIs, and CT scans, many of which were already operating well beyond their normal lifespan due to a lack of eligible funding for replacement of major equipment.Furthermore, many machines are non-functional due to unilateral coercive measures which prevent the importation of spare parts and expertise needed for repairs. During the earthquake, these factors impacted the timely and effective delivery of life-saving trauma and surgical care.According to the 2024 for people in need of health assistance, Homs Al-Qusayr falls within a severity scale of 3, while the other areas that were chosen to fall within a severity scale of 4 Idleb (Sanjar), Aleppo (Haritan), and Deir-ez-Zor (Deir-ez-Zor city).
