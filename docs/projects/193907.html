Project Summary

TCC&WW-GVC are aiming to reach children in the most vulnerable communities in the WB,especially in AreaC and H2,by carrying out a combination of soft and hard component activities that will contribute to increasing equitable safe access to inclusive and quality education in 24 communities in AreaC/H2,in line with the mandates,strategies,and expertise of TCC and WW-GVC,in order to contribute to the overall safety and dignity of the children and their families.This proposal is fully in line with the strategic objective2 of the Education Cluster,SDG4 and CRC art.28 and 29,which aim to guarantee accessibility to quality safe educational services for children living in AreaC and H2 communities.TCC and WWGVC are active members of the Education cluster and are committed to promote quality education in Palestine,supporting the MoE and UNRWA in providing adequate education services,such as rehabilitation of education infrastructure for schools and KGs facilities including but not limited to playgrounds,laboratories,and classrooms,as well as offering capacity building interventions and trainings for students,teachers,principals,and supervisors.Palestinian students are facing double risk of dropping out of school the first resulted from the consequences of COVID19 outbreak in early 2020 which has significant shock on the education system,and resulted the shifting towards e-learning,and the other continuous risk is the Israeli occupation and control over the communities in AreaC and H2.As a matter of fact,in addition to destroying Palestinian homes and infrastructures,Israeli armed forces are frequently present outside schools and,together with settlers,they regularly attack schools and students on their way to school,adolescent boys result more vulnerable to be subjected to violence,as they are more likely to be targeted by Israeli forces at checkpoints for stops and searches.Therefore,eliminating the need to travel to school would also have specific implications both on gender and age level,especially for girls the situation is more complicated,because parents are more afraid to send their daughters to school and to cross the checkpoints.Referring to the Education cluster status reports,INGOs reports,a study published by NRC,and a recent study conducted by TCC in 2021 on the learning loss for students from 1st to 4th grades, there has been a steep decline in the well-being of students and teachers,and a learning loss with regard to the academic and social achievements of students across Palestinian territories.The findings from TCC study highlighted, school-related vulnerability is more affected by the learning loss resulted from the school closure and the shift to the e-learning, and girls are most likely to be affected by this. Based on a pre-test, prepared according to the students’ level 1st-4th grades,it will be provided to the children,to assess their academic level,then teachers will be able to determine the interventions based on the edu-tainment manual.This means that students will learn through Edu-tainment method that makes them more actively engaged in the learning process,and increase their achievements Also,the issue of travel to and from the school is an important determinant of students’ wellbeing.Therefore,eliminating the need to travel to and from school,alongside the provision of safe learning environments,may serve to improve the well-being of these young people.The proposed activities will target 24communities in the WB through direct rehabilitation of 19 existing school structures in need for rehabilitation,and 5KGs either internally (Interior repairs,furnishing,WASH facilities) or externally (addition of classrooms,play area).Moreover,as part of soft activities,students attending schools will be targeted in extracurricular activities,ongoing remedial classes,in addition to trainings for teachers.The actions will benefit10,688 students,teachers,parents,living in the 24 community.Disaggregated in the indicators part

1 - Needs

This proposal addresses many issues in Area C and H2 contexts like,school closure and catch up classes, security concerns,and distance to school.According to the MSNA findings this year,72.9%in AreaC and 83.5%in H2 of households reporting a need for catch-up classes due to school closures due to COVID-19;Also,24.8%in AreaC and 18.4%in H2 of households that reported children feeling unsafe or very unsafe when traveling to/from and studying in schools,due to several reasons such as,crossing roads,firing tear gas on schools or students, violence or harassment by settlers,and military entry to schools.Moreover,WW-GVC through their Community Protection Approach (CPA) collected quantitative and qualitative data related to Education sector in 172 communities, aiming to systematize protection related information on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities, with a community engagement method,which was last updated in January 2022. Additionally,TCC and WW-GVC implemented a need assessment in Sep 2022 in collaboration with MoE for a number of targeted communities in Education Sector supported by several field visits.According to WW-GVC data,the vulnerability in Education sector for the targeted communities is reflected through the high protection index, that ranges between 50% -90%, which signals an urging need for targeting Education sector as an immediate action.This situation is the result of the combination of several factors,namely: lack of schools and KGs inside the community,or within a reasonable distance for children to be able to access them on feet; the risks faced by the students to reach their schools during their round-trip;the status of enrollment and the high rates of dropouts related to the long distance to reach schools.According to the research study led by TCC in 2021,the first need that emerged from the study was updating the interventions in emergency plans in order to bridge the gap in early childhood stage, and thus reintegrate them into schools,so that they are prepared for the school,especially for children of the years 2019-2020 and 2020-2021,the second need reported is to assess the capabilities and competencies of female workers and educators in kindergartens,and re-examine the needs of their competencies,so that to ensure that they have scientific degrees that qualify them to work with children in the early stages of childhood. Moreover, it is necessary to define more specialized capacity-building programs related to the problems that affect the children, in order to intervene with children in times of emergency, as well as adapting programs to the educational loss. The third need addresses re-examination of the kindergarten sites and infrastructure in order to provide a healthy and appropriate environment for children in kindergartens. Many government kindergartens depend on the support of local communities, and most of them lack the appropriate conditions for education, especially protection methods, educational games and advanced tools. More specifically,four schools in four different communities in Hebron,Bethlehem,Tubas,and four KGs in four different communities in Qalqilya,Bethlehem,and Tubas are in need of construction,as students at all educational levels are forced to walk on feet for very long distances in order to reach the nearest schools which,in some cases, can be as far as 12kms away,which takes hours per round trip.Additionally, the bad condition of the internal road in some areas increases the protection risks faced by children who walk carrying their heavy bags under extreme weather conditions, along with the natural hazards,as well as the risk of harassment from settlers/IF during their trip;especially when there is a security escalation or high presence of settlers/IF or Israeli officials.Some students cope with the long distance by living with their other families and relatives in the village where the schools are located,while going back to their shelter in the community by the end of every week,which causes family separation due to sending part of the family away.Other students (especially girls),negatively cope with the lack of educational services through school drop-out, which is found to be increasing in Bedouins communities.In addition to upgrading of-construction of new classrooms for schools-in two communities (Biet Za’ta and Dhaher al Malih).In regards to the internal environment of the existing schools in the targeted communities,the joint assessment carried out by TCC and WeWorld-GVC with close coordination with MoE in August/September 2022 during the proposal elaboration phase showed that 16 schools and 3 KGs in Area C/H2.These communities’ schools and KGs are in a critical condition and need urgent rehabilitation:the schools’ interior environment is very poor as it lacks proper lightening and ventilation;the classrooms suffer from water leakage,and the humid atmosphere in the school imposes health concerns on both children and teachers; additionally,there is lack of laboratories or multipurpose rooms for any educational activity (including online courses),and they are facilities that also lack of adaptability to meet the needs of children with disabilities.As for the external environment,10 schools and 3 KGs are lacking healthy sanitation units for students,and no play area for the children. Moreover, the lack of space leads the children to express their energy through violence, as stated by the schools’ principles.As for the upper environment/ capacities:teachers and the school cadre in general are in need for specialized training to build their capacities on new methods, strategies,and techniques to integrate them in planning and managing their classes be it physically or virtually.The integration of technology in education is needed more after shifting towards e-learning,and to ensure equality in accessing education for all it is necessary to equip teachers,students and to adopt the curricula and the school environment to these changes

2 - Activities

According to the quantitative and qualitative data from EC that highlights the high vulnerability in Education sector, the proposed modalities, remedial and environmentbuilding actions was based on the needs identified by the Education Cluster and WWGVC’s Protection Response Plans, the proposed activities are complementary and longterm oriented, with the aim to restore and support children while they suffer from the subsequent effects of an unsafe access to education, as well as to promote safe environment which will prevent or limit current and future gaps related to a meaningful and inclusive access to education for all. During the design of the project, the different needs of boys, girls, men and women and PwDs identified through the field assessments that followed the recommendations of the MoE were generally related to their needs to guarantee a safe and protected environment for access to quality education, where children do not feel threated or intimidated. Based on that, the actions will entail general integrated activities that will contribute to the Education Objectives as follows: Education Objective 2– Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children Outcome 1: Enhanced capacities and educational environment in 24 Educational centers. Output 1: Strengthened learning modalities and quality education services for school teachers. Proposed Activities: 1. Conduct a specialized training Academy for teachers (School teachers and KGs teachers) on the new learning methods and strategies, and using technology in designing, managing and evaluating the classes. 2. Design and implement 2 school initiatives using the new methods of learning. 3. Develop a manual on innovative strategies and learning methods that could be used in emergencies. 4. Provide schools with technological kits. Aiming at integrating students more effectively in the e-learning, these kits will be provided to schools that contains one of the following (tablets, or laptops, or projector, or white boards, or connecting the school to internet, this would be depending on the need of each school after doing a need assessment) Outcome2: Enhanced educational environment in 24 Educational centers. Output 2: Soft and hard rehabilitations and educational kits are provided in 24 educational centres. Proposed Activities: Provision of essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that school environments are appropriate and safe for students and teachers. 3. Upgrading of two schools (new classrooms) in two communities in Hebron (Biet Za’ta) and Jenin (Dhaher al Malih). 4. Rehabilitation of 16 schools in Hebron (Umm Al Kheir, As Simiya, Ar Rifa'iyya) Bethlehem (Al Jab'a) Ramallah (Ras al Tin) Jericho (Marj Ghazal, Al Muarajat East, Marj Naja , An Nuwei'ma Al-Fauqa) Tubas (Kherbit Atuf, Bardala) Jenin (Ti’innik) Nablus (Kherbit Tana) Salfit (Marda, Kefil Haris) Qalqilia (Arab ar Ramadin Al Janubi) and rehabilitation of three KGs in two communities in Hebron (Tel Rumedeh) Bethlehem (Jab’a, Khirbet an Nahla). Output 3: Improved numeracy, reading and writing skills among out of school aged- students aiming at reintegrating them into the education system. Proposed Activities: 1. Selection of 100 children from the targeted grades (1st to 4th) from each targeted education communities based on TCC’s diagnose tool (CARD) in corporation with the MoE. 2. Provide remedial classes – 3 classes per day and compensation classes for children over the period of 4 months in different subjects with a focus on Math, Arabic, and English: 4 groups of students (25 children in each divided into Math, Arabic and English in addition to counselling groups)- 3 hours per day from 12:30- 3:30 after school hours, 3 days per week. 3. Develop the existed manual on the edu-tainment methodology, to be used in the remedial classes, as it focuses on using arts, sports, drama, in the learning process. 4. Provide 18 hours training for the new hired teachers on the developed manual of edu-tainment and the use of the edu-tainment methodology and activities, 5. Provision of school based -PSS activities for the targeted students and school staff, this will include recreational activities on Saturdays for the students, open days and extracurricular activities. The activities will be designed and implemented based on an AGD approach, assuring the active participation of women, men, girls and boys, and PwDs, in analyzing their needs and tailoring accordingly the implementation of the activities, through interviews and focus group discussions (FGDs). PSEA messages and calls for feedback will be integrated into the programming. Technical assessments will be conducted before the intervention to design appropriate interventions that match their needs in compliance with Education Cluster SOPs for PSEA (based on Risk Assessments). Staff will include awareness activities on PSEA, including complaint mechanisms (including how to report SEA-related complaints).

3 - Indicators

The purpose of this action is to address the communities’ humanitarian and protection needs while improving their access to education and contributing to safety and dignity. TCC and WeWorld-GVC will coordinate the progress of activities and the monitoring of the indicators with the MoE, which will strengthen the involvement and accountability of MoE and its directorates in all project activities. Moreover, this will create interactive relationships or build/improve channels of communication/collaboration between the project beneficiaries and the MoE, which will also contribute in guaranteeing the sustainability of project interventions. Specifically, the action will lead to the achievement of the specific objective following these indicators: 1320 of female and male vulnerable students and school staff have supported to access learning. 15% of parents engaged/participated in school activities. 3928 of female and male students and teachers benefiting from emergency school rehabilitation. 2831 of female and male students benefiting from remedial education services and learning support. 159 of out of school children supported by education services aiming at reintegrating them in the education system. The project partners will appoint one Monitoring & Evaluation focal point each for the project. The delivery and quality control will be ensured through regular field visits to the sites. Staff will monitor progress on agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. These measures will take into account beneficiaries’ participation and reporting throughout implementation and monitoring. The achievement of the indicators will be measured through an in-depth baseline technical assessment. An evaluation will be conducted to measure the impact and benefit related to the activities’ implementation for the different target group (including teachers, students and their caregivers) with a gender-age- diversity sensitive approach. The evaluation will include quantitative data collection (post specific survey) and qualitative analysis through separated focus groups (women/girls and men/boys, including PwDs) focusing on access to education/ safety/ dignity and integrity. MEAL officers jointly with the social team in WW-GVC and TCC will be responsible in collecting and analyzing data in assessment and monitoring phases. Both direct beneficiaries and community members will have access to an anonymized Complaint Response Mechanism jointly managed by TCC and WW-GVC throughout all the duration and beyond the time of the intervention. The action responds to educational needs and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of personal safety, dignity and protection. The designed activities will assure: an improvement of the safety of all students and teachers, with specific attention to girls and boys who are under threat of drop out or have history of dropping out; good learning environment and subsequent improved health conditions and more resistance to COVID-19 and other diseases for all school attendees especially CwDs during cold and hot seasons; increased security and safety of all students during their ways to schools.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Complaint response mechanism is part of WW-GVC and TCC accountability and commitment strategies to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneﬁciaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints or suggestions in a secured way and receive a response in a timely manner. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

TCC and WeWorld-GVC provide assistance in a way which endeavors to minimize exposure to additional hazards and mitigate as many risks as possible. Needs and risks are balanced through the Community Protection Approach where community is involved in a thorough assessment to understand the vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the programming reflects preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs in a participative way. Considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PwD. The eﬃciency is encouraging all local actors and community involvement on the overall steps of the suggested intervention and prevent unintended negative impacts. Through this inclusive and comparative risk analysis organizations increase the accountability allowing themselves to quickly respond to any unintentional negative impacts created by the intervention. The proposal elaborates a remedial action to hazards that caused a change in educational needs and affects the ability of girls and boys to attend school. Which has an impact on the levels of literacy, numeracy and wellbeing of the affected population. TCC and WW-GVC aim to reduce the vulnerability of the beneficiaries caused by the systematic settler and IF abuse violence creating an increase of protection risks. The core action aims to create a safe, secure and inclusiveness environment in schools and realize supporting actions that focus on the need for physical protection, resilience building and psychosocial-emotional protection. The Do No Harm principle is assured during all the project cycle, guaranteeing that every project- phases and steps are endorsed by the beneficiaries themselves or their families, to prevent -in so doing- any potential negative consequence. The assessment lead by WW-GVC in Area C and the needs assessment carried out for the project writing focus on the understanding of the needs of the communities, specifically girls and boys. Particular attention is given to input/feedback provided by vulnerable groups such as women, whose participation is strongly encouraged to any decision-making process, and girls and boys in line with article 12 of the Convention on the Right of the Child. Specific attention will be also given to the lessons learned coming from previous similar projects of both partners. The elaboration of each single activity will take into account the different needs and investigate how girls and boys are differently affected, as well as PSEA SoP. In the implementation phase, monitoring mechanism will guarantee direct interaction with the beneficiaries to make sure that their concerns and needs are heard and to guarantee their right to participate and be agents of change. Moreover, WeWorld-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint mechanism system, allowing beneficiaries and affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will then -if needed- influence management decisions and concrete implementation. Safety and dignity have been taken into consideration in the design of tailored activities to address the different needs and risks that girls, women, boys and men are currently facing.COVID-19 protection measures (wearing face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WWGVC and TCC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project follows a sustainable humanitarian approach aiming to provide remedial and environment-building actions, ensuring equity, sustainability and impartial access to education, in light of the ICRC’s Protection ‘Egg’ Framework. Impartiality principle is carried throughout the action on the basis of needs, exclusively, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress and making no distinction on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class or political opinion. Impartiality principle will be recognized throughout the actions' entire phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring. This is illustrated by the fact that assessments have been conducted through the same tools and methods in every school targeted, collecting the data directly from the duty-bearers (MoE) as a way of accountability and transparency. On another note, schools targeted for needs assessments have been collected through trustable sources, as public data from OCHA or the Ministry of Education, avoiding random targeting. During the design phase, several schools have been assessed by TCC and WeWorld-GVC staff in coordination with the Ministry of Education, to identify the different needs and potential activities and beneficiaries. The identification of the beneficiaries and activities followed the criteria of higher need and higher vulnerability. The proposal targets girls and boys and KG children, besides the educational staff, as they are considered as vulnerable groups due to the high exposure to Israelis attacks while accessing to education services and schools, they are generally not safe as are exposed to the threat of tear gas and sound bombs thrown by the IF, and the risk of inspections in cases of security escalation. Children with disabilities will be also targeted by the project and their specific needs will be analysed and addressed. During the previous needs assessment, no cases of severe disability have been identified. However, to guarantee the right to education for all and sustainability in the long-term, the activities will be implemented with an inclusive approach and will be tailored to children with disabilities and/or specific needs. Furthermore, the distribution of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) cards in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedback and complaints about the implemented project.
