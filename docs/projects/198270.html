Project Summary

In response to the humanitarian crisis in north-eastern Nigeria, SHADE opened its office in Maiduguri in late 2021. Today, SHADE has 15 national staff based in Borno State. SHADE current intervention strategy in Northeast Nigeria is in line with the 2019 -2022 Humanitarian Response Strategy’s second strategic objective, which aims to "enhance timely, unhindered and equitable access to multi-sectoral assistance and protection interventions through principled humanitarian action.” All of SHADE projects aim to ensure equitable and safe access to critical and lifesaving assistance, promoting the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. With this funding SHADE will expand its scope in providing the needed humanitarian assistance in site management and advocacy etc, benefitting a wide range of Persons of concern that don’t have access to support and assistance. This project seeks to provide a holistic CCCM response in line with the Shelter/NFI & CCCM sector approach to strengthen and improve the living conditions of the displaced persons through camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) and Out-of-Camp Area Based Approach. The project, which will be implemented under a 12 months’ period, will cover a total of 12,168 individuals affected by the ongoing conflict and different waves of displacements with a CCCM response. Additionally, the project aims to bridge the gap between the people of concern and service providers to ensure quality, dignified and equal access to humanitarian assistance as well as protection mainstreaming within the camps and Out-of-camp settings. It will also coordinate the several humanitarian partners within the camps and out-of-camp in collaboration with the State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA) and National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) officials and local authorities to harmonize service delivery and avoid duplication of services by operating partners. As CCCM partners, the project will further carry out site facilitation, camp management and its structures, support to environmental hazards mitigation as well as provide a channel for complaints mechanism where the beneficiaries also receive feedback to their queries and a measure to strengthen communication with communities in accordance the CCCM sector strategy. The beneficiaries of this project will be new arrival IDPs, returnees and host communities, in application of an approach fully compliant with the Sector strategy. SHADE will integrate a community participation approach for all beneficiaries, to acquire more community ownership and empowerment in the project using local materials and personnel. The application of the community approach would imply maximum engagements of communities through the existing structures to enhance accountability to affected populations while ensuring AGD mainstreaming in all project locations. SHADE understands Protection as a Key pillar to SHADE Interventions and as such considers holistic protection mainstreaming in all the CCCM activities to increase project Impact.
To implement the proposed intervention, SHADE as a national NGO and a member of the CCCM/Shelter/NFIs will draw on extensive experience in implementing CCCM program, across multiple IDP sites in Borno State, SHADE is currently managing CCCM programs in Damboa LGAs, benefitting over 4,000 IDPs, which is presently self-funded, and on strong relationships built with, partners and beneficiary communities in Damboa, SHADE will integrate best practices and lessons learned for adaptive and evidence-based programming. The organization will continue to capitalize on these experiences, in order to guide the proposed intervention.

2 - Activities

Based on activities that will be implemented, as per below details, SHADE will seek to contribute to the improvement of persons of concerns protection, through more effective coordination of humanitarian interventions in the targeted camps and strengthening of communication with communities in these sites.1. Site governance and community participationTo foster camp/site governance, SHADE will establish and maintain all-inclusive committees and build on years of previous experiences by providing all committees and local authorities including SEMA, NEMA, community volunteers and committees/leadership in camps/camp-like and in Out of Camp settings with basic training on CCCM roles and responsibility. In addition, SHADE will carry out community mobilization through sensitization and awareness creation on leadership and selection of leaders and joint community mobilization events.2. Coordination of servicesAs a camp management agency, SHADE will ensure the coordination of service provision at camp site level. Monthly and ad hoc CCCM meetings will be organized and chaired by SHADE to identify needs, share information, and organize the response by avoiding duplication of services. To ensure successful coordination, SHADE will engage with all stakeholders involved in the humanitarian response, including key national authorities such as SEMA and NEMA, community structures. Meeting minutes will be prepared and shared with partners, and attendance sheets will be signed up for accountability purpose. SHADE will further organize quarterly community leaders meeting in all sites including the out of campsites across project locations in Damboa LGA3. Management and maintenance of camp site/camp infrastructureCamp living conditions are improved through site maintenance and improvement works in addition to the implementation of critical risk mitigation strategies and emergency assistance support. Site maintenance and improvement works in IDP campsites in Damboa LGA. To promote safe and dignified living conditions in the target IDP camp sites, SHADE Camp and Settlement Technician will use data generated from site profiling exercises to identify urgent site maintenance needs. Findings will be analyzed and prioritized through joint consultations with humanitarian stakeholders and beneficiary communities, including NEMA/SEMA, Sector Leads, local leaders, and community members.4. AdvocacyWhen a case which needs additional support is identified, CCCM team members will refer the community member to specialized service providers through a referral form. The tool is designed to include a case code, informed consent section, required personal data, information about the reason of referral, a section for referring agency and reception agency to be signed up for acceptance. The protection officer will be in charge to refer protection cases and ensure that follow up have been made. Data protection mechanisms will be in place to ensure proper data management and respect confidentiality.4.Establishment of information, coordination and communication centresSHADE will construct and manage 2 information & Coordination centers in Garjan and Abatchari communities in out-of-camp settings. This is piloted under the out-of-camp approach intervention which target IDPs living within the host communities either with host families, in structures that are not categorized as camps/camp-like setting or are renting premises in towns or market centers. The Information, Coordination and Communication Center (ICC) will be used as a key component to Out of Camp Area based Approach to create linkage between IDPs, refugee returnees and host communities.
