Project Summary

In recent years, Cameroon has been affected by crises in several neighboring countries, which has led to insecurity and left Cameroonian extremely vulnerable. The Eastern region of Cameroon hosts more than 154,067 Central African refugees , including 39,439 girls under age of 18, with 86.02% of them living in the sub-division of Garoua-Boulai, Kette, Batouri and Kentzou,. This influx of refugees has placed significant pressure on already limited resources and basic social services in the host areas, exacerbating pre-existing vulnerabilities, particularly affecting women and children. The gradual decline in humanitarian aid and the insufficient level of funding for development projects further negatively impact refugees. Refugees and host communities therefore find themselves with substantial humanitarian needs, such as basic services for nutrition, health, education, and protection. Providing these services in the affected communities requires a combined response effort.
In this climate of insecurity and limited access to services, women and children are the most at-risk of experiencing, and being exposed to violence. Violence against women and girls is common, especially in displacement and humanitarian settings, and poses a threat to women’s own health and well-being as well as to the social cohesion of communities. According to the Demographic and Health Survey 2018, 07.7% of girls against 02.9% of boys aged 15-19 are sexual violence survivors. From January 2021 to September 2022 in the sub-division of Garoua-Boulai, Kette, Batouri and Kentzou, 225 GBV cases were registered, 24% were against children aged under 18. 16% were rapes and 10% forced marriage (IMC UK activities reports). It is important to remember when examining numbers around GBV and violence against children that these types of crimes and offenses are often under-reported, and the true number of incidents may be higher.
Regarding child marriage, a Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey from 2014 reveals that 11% of girls are married before they are 15 years old, and 36% of girls are married before reaching 18 years of age. The same survey shows that 47% of children aged 5-17 are involved in child labor, with slightly fewer girls than boys involved in these practices (44% female vs. 50% male).. This phenomenon is more pronounced in rural areas, with 61% of surveyed children reporting they were involved in child labor practices, compared to only 30% of children in urban areas.. Child labor practices harm the development of the child, and sometimes put them in dangerous situations and conditions. From IMC UK 2021 activity reports, 126 cases of children at-risk of violence, 65% fo whom were girls, were identified in the sub-division of Garoua-Boulai, Kette, Batouri and Kentzou. Of these protection cases, 43% were involved in worst forms of labour, with more than half being girls.
The project will address the following objectives:
• To strengthen the capacities of local and community organizations to prevent and respond to GBV and violence against children, especially child marriage;
• To ensure the mitigation of the risks of child marriage through the socio-economic empowerment girls at-risk, especially those who do not attend school;
• To explore opportunities to establish public-private partnership to mobilize resources to fight against child marriage.
The project aims to address violence against women and girls and child protection concerns through a community-based approach, including fostering strong partnerships with local municipal governments and with other relevant humanitarian partners. IMC UK will engage communities in the target municipalities through community-based organizations (CBOs), traditional and religious leaders, health centers or posts, schools, and other key influential members of the communities to foster community buy-in and participation in the project.
