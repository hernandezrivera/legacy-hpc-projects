Project Summary

The project supports for enhanced preparations for possible future escalations in hostilities through the prepositioning and warehousing of the lifesaving NFIs. It supports to strengthen the preparedness capacity of the hosting communities to provide for adequate and gender sensitive response to the needs of IDPs. Set of hosting communities will be targeted in Gaza City and the Northern Governorate including Beer Al-Naja, Mashro beit lahya, and Al-Yarmok Areas. The project applies community based preparedness and emergency response mechanisms. MA’AN capitalizes on its community based emergency response structure to support the IDPs in hosting communities and others in the PA-Led designated emergency shelters to access their needs of life saving NFIs. The project includes the prepositioning and warehousing of the lifesaving NFIs relying on the CBOs who are part of MA’AN community based emergency response structure. The NFIs stockpiles will serve mainly the hosting communities in emergencies and will be ready as a backup to support the IDPs of the governmental designated shelters. Community capacity building and awareness raising activities will be provided to the targeted hosting communities addressing the skills and knowledge needed to provide for adequate support to displaced families.
The objective of this action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the Shelter Cluster 2020 HRP, objective 3: “Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased".

1 - Needs

Following MA’AN efforts to enhance preparations for possible future escalations in hostilities through the prepositioning and warehousing of the lifesaving NFIs targeting the localities of Southern Rimal, Beach Camp, Sheikh Radwan, Al Nasser Area, Jabalia Al Nazleh, and Western Zaitoon Area, MA’AN has been planning to proceed addressing the preparedness gaps in Gaza City and the Northern Governorate. In order to inform MA’AN plans, and in view of the findings of the report "Mapping of Humanitarian Services at Community Level in the Gaza Strip" produced by NRC in April 2019, MA'AN had conducted an assessment to address the physical stockpiling of emergency NFIs and arrangements in-place to procure NFIs immediately, capacity of potential host families to be able to support IDPs during emergencies, and capacity of local partners (CBOs) to support IDPs and affected people during and post-emergency situations. The assessment targeted the Northern Governorate (Beer Al-Naja, Mashro beit lahya), and Gaza City (Al-Yarmouk Area). These areas were identified in the NRC study as neighborhoods where IDPs are expected to flee to at some point during an emergency as suffering from overcrowded housing conditions, deteriorated infrastructure, and other concerns. The assessment included consultations with key stakeholders such as the hosting communities and IDPs. Three focus group discussions were conducted involving 21 men, 11 women, and 3 people with disabilities. This included IDPs and hosting families. The discussions were to learn about their experience in the last war and risks they faced to access Non-food items and its impact on safety and protection. The discussions addressed the different needs of IDPs, with particular focus on elderlies, children, women, and to examine the needs of the hosting communities.Significant concerns were revealed which entails immediate relevant actions. Overall, it was found that hosting communities have experienced difficulties to access non-food items due to the lack of accessible and safe NFI warehouses, which undermined their safety and protection. The assessment addressed the capacities of the potential partner CBOs in terms of their experience in emergency response, the existence of contingency plans, the CBOs staff capacity, the availability of adequate storage areas/warehouses, and the CBOs interest to act as first responders in emergencies. The assessed CBOs have warehouses adjacent to official DESs. The information of these CBOs and the hosting communities they serve is as follows:1. 2 - Activities

The project supports for enhanced preparations for possible future escalations in hostilities through the prepositioning and warehousing of the lifesaving NFIs. It supports to strengthen the preparedness capacity of the hosting communities to provide for adequate and gender-sensitive response to the needs of IDPs. Set of hosting communities will be targeted in Gaza City and the Northern Governorate including Beer Al-Naja, Mashro beit lahya, and Al-Yarmok Areas. The project applies community-based preparedness and emergency response mechanisms while capitalizing on MA’AN community-based emergency response structure. The project addresses the preparedness for large scale conflict. However, in cases of small scale escalation of hostilities, or natural disasters, and in coordination with the shelter cluster, a set of measures can be considered to support the affected population to access their needs of NFIs. MA'AN's emergency response system is flexible to address such emergencies.The project main activities include:1. Procure and preposition NFI and emergency shelter kits at the partner CBOs warehouses (Sealing Off Kit, Bedding Set, Dignity Kits, and Washing Set). The sealing off kit will be for the use of the hosting families in order to provide for the adaptation of the hosting space addressing the concerns of the overcrowding conditions, GBV and child protection concerns, and to provide for more protective and safe hosting space addressing the house vulnerabilities to the weather conditions (winter-related risks). No distribution of NFIs will be conducted during the implementation until there is an emergency situation that entails NFIs distribution. The Bedding Set, Washing Set, and dignity kits will be for the use of the displaced families to their emergency needs while helping women and girls maintain their dignity.2. Conduct an assessment for the families who experienced hosting IDPs in the last escalation of hostilities 2014. The assessment should address the hosting families’ needs to provide for adequate and safe hosting. This should inform the selection of the 300 hosting families. Part of the reported needs will be addressed during the emergencies (i.e. the distribution of sealing off kits and the training), and other needs will be reported to the cluster for referral.3. Develop contingency plans for the partner CBOs to support for accountable and responsive shelter aid delivery 4. Develop a stockpiles management plan 5. Provide training to the CBOs team to ensure adequate stocks management practices, 6. Provide training to the CBOs team and volunteers on (1) the protection principles and how to mainstream while addressing the needs of IDPs and hosting communities, and (2) how to assess the needs of the IDPs and the hosting communities in emergencies7. Apply safety and security measures to ensure the safekeeping of stocks8. Adapt the CBOs warehouses to ensure adequate and safe stockpiles keeping9. Conduct set of awareness activities in targeted hosting communities to enhance the community preparedness capacities, addressing (1) child protection and GBV (how to address, how to deal with, and how to prevent), (2) the mapping of the GBV and child protection services providers, (3) the existing national emergency preparedness and response systems, (4) the self-recovery through community implementation methodology, expanding their ability to implement future low-budget maintenance of shelters; and to adapt the available hosting space for IDPs needs addressing overcrowding, and privacy concerns while mainstream the protection and GBV related concerns. (5) orientation about protection measures such as explosive remnants of war (ERW), and first aid skills.10. Conduct training for emergency first responders “community members” on the mainstreaming of the humanitarian standards and protection principles in emergency responses, with particular focus on GBV and child protection related concerns.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

In the last two years, MA’AN has been working on developing an accountable, accessible, and gender-sensitive complaint system. The system offers different means to address complaints or feedback in writing, verbally, via phone or SMS, directly to the responsible person/field monitors from MA'AN, and verbally and directly to MA’AN offices. The system includes particular procedures to address gender-sensitive complaints. The system has been managed online involving different layers of MA’AN governance structure. MA’AN has been working on adapting the system to include special procedures during emergencies to get it more accessible for IDPs and hosting communities. This project will include introducing the system to the partners CBOs, volunteers, and targeted communities ensuring well orientation about the system and how to access in emergencies. The communities’ feedback on the system will be considered for further adaptation to ensure accountability and accessibility.During the project implementation, a feedback mechanism will be applied to ensure the involvement of the targeted communities in the project management and decision making. Orientation workshops will be conducted at the beginning of the project to discuss the project activities and get further feedback from the targeted communities about the implementation methodology. A set of focus group discussions will be conducted during implementation to get regular feedback from the targeted communities about the implementation process and the applied measures of accountability.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

As part of its work on enhancing a gender-sensitive emergency response system, MA'AN has an updated contingency plan and emergency SOPs that include policies, procedures and work practices sensitive to gender issues and child protection concerns. MA'AN's humanitarian team and all emergency structures including the partner CBOs, community-based emergency response teams, and volunteers are well-trained on these policies and practices and are capacitated with the knowledge and resources to carry out a gender-sensitive humanitarian response and address all gender issues and IHL violations during an emergency event properly. The CBOs and volunteers who are involved in managing the project activities will be well oriented about the MA’AN contingency plan and related emergency SOPs. In addition, they will receive training on the protection principles and how to mainstream while addressing the needs of IDPs and Hosting communities. During the project, the emergency first responders “community members” will receive training on the mainstreaming of the humanitarian standards and protection principles in emergency responses, with particular focus on GBV and child protection related concerns. The training and orientation for CBOs, volunteers, and emergency first responders will ensure a particular focus on GBV detection and referral and will ensure linkage to child protection referral networks. From another perspective, this project seeks to enhance the protection of IDPs in the hosting communities in Gaza and North Gaza governorates through providing NFI kits that respond effectively to the emergency needs of both IDPs and the hosting families. The identified NFI kits in this project take into consideration the concerns and specific needs of all IDPs, particularly women and children who are more likely to be exposed to GBV during an emergency event. In addition, it includes NFI kits for the use of the hosting families to enhance their preparedness for safe and protective hosting conditions through providing for the adaptation of the hosting spaces, addressing the concerns of the overcrowding conditions, GBV and child protection concerns.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In coordination with the partner CBOs, a set of sessions will be conducted in the targeted communities to ensure that they are well-oriented about the project scope and the intended support. The sessions will include orientation about MA’AN complaint system and mechanisms. The project includes to develop contingency plans for the partner CBOs and building the capacity of the CBOs teams addressing clear mechanisms to ensure equal and impartial access to aid. A set of accountability mechanisms will be ensured in place. In coordination with the partner CBOs, a set of sessions will be conducted in the targeted communities to ensure that they are well-oriented about the project scope and the intended support. The sessions will include orientation about MA’AN complaint system and mechanisms. Accountability to the targeted communities will be encouraged and ensured through a gender-sensitive complaint and feedback mechanism that involves direct and regular communication between MA'AN and the final beneficiaries to allow affected persons to communicate their concerns and complaints on regular basis. Specific attention will be given to the complaints and feedback mechanism in order to increase the ownership of beneficiaries over the project, engage them in addressing the expected risks and challenges, and give them sufficient spaces to participate in the decision making and project evaluation process of the project.
