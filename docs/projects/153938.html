Project Summary

The Iraq Internally Displaced Persons Information Centre (IIC) is an inter agency accountability mechanism that allows affected populations to access information on existing and planned humanitarian assistance and convey feedback on and complaints about the humanitarian response in Iraq.
The objective of this project is to ensure that feedback from callers identifies gaps in assistance, permits ongoing information exchange, influences how information is shared, and shapes how humanitarian assistance is organised and delivered.
Accountability to Affected People (taking account, giving account and being held to account) lies at the heart of the Iraq IIC.
In 2019 the project will target IDPs in and outside camps and returnees, supporting- together with the other humanitarian actors- the transition to durable solution, but also supporting protection through provision of information and referrals.

Needs

December 2017 marked the end of major military operations in Iraq against the so-called Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL). While more than 4 million returns have been recorded as of September 2018, almost 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) remain, of whom 71% reside outside of formal camps. Moreover, secondary displacement and new arrivals to formal camps signal the tenuous nature of some returns. Therefore, although recovery efforts in Iraq are underway, understanding the multifaceted and intersecting needs of all affected groups is critical to supporting durable returns, while maintaining services for those in protacted displacement and addressing the unique vulnerabilities of populations who remained non-displaced during active conflict (Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment -MCNA). In this scenario of post-conflict transition, findings from the humanitarian needs overview (HNO) continue to identify gaps. Humanitarian partners are moving towards a ‘needs-based’ response, able to target the areas more in need as well as the most vulnerable population. Return is expected to dominate the humanitarian response in 2019, priority areas include Anbar, Ninewa, Diyala, Salah al Din, and Kirkuk.Information and communication are critical forms of aid and key prerequisites for communities to participate in their own recovery. Access to information is a human right and people affected by conflict and disaster should have access to an ongoing, reliable flow of credible information on the disaster and associated relief effort. While communicating with affected populations has never been more essential in Iraq, the simple provision of information is not enough. Two way communication and feedback is critical to ensuring communication is effective, and efficiently circulates the urgent needs and priorities of affected populations. Two way communication systems deliver effective aid in the form of information, facilitating the recovery of communities in emergency contexts. Communities can be empowered by providing information to aid agencies, which in turn can be used to advocate and deliver solutions to genuine problems. In August 2014, an inter agency team carried out a rapid assessment with displaced populations and host communities to understand their information needs and access to communication channels. To address findings of an information vacuum surrounding the humanitarian response that isolated affected populations from the response, the assessment recommended the establishment of a two way communication centre to provide IDPs with timely information and to refer complaints, feedback and urgent needs to the humanitarian community. In July 2015, the Iraq IIC opened its lines as part of a pilot programme. Since then it has handled more than 69,906 calls.The Iraq IIC is the only accountability and two way communication mechanism that represents the entire humanitarian response in Iraq. Key gaps that the centre response to are:• Limited information about humanitarian assistance, services and outreach activities, especially in hard to reach areas;• Lack of information and knowledge for women and girls who face restrictions on movement and access to services;• Absence of a joint inter agency complaints, referral and reporting mechanism;• Limited two way communication between displaced populations and aid agencies; and;• Restricted access to feedback that affects humanitarian actors’ ability to understand the needs of affected populations in real time and adjust programmes and services accordingly.

Activities

To better respond to the needs outlined, the project will provide a mechanism to assist affected populations with life saving information and facilitate referrals to services through the provision of:• A nationwide toll free number that facilitates direct communication between affected people and the humanitarian agencies that serve them;• Alternative community engagement methods to reach women and vulnerable members of society, including those requiring specialised support services;• A safe, functional, dignified and confidential complaints referral mechanisms through which affected communities can hold agencies accountable and the project can close the loop on complaints. The Iraq IIC is the centralised complaints mechanism for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Iraq Taskforce and is a key member of the Communicating with Communities Working Group;• Feedback mechanisms, enabling aid agencies to take account, give account and be held to account; • Training to Iraq IIC Operators in protection, child protection, sexual exploitation and abuse, and gender based violence; and,• Publicly available dashboards that inform partners of needs and gaps on the ground as reported by affected communities across the country.During 2019, the Iraq IIC will: • Maintain operations seven days per week;• Work with partners to augment training of operators, with a particular focus on protection issues including gender based violence and child protection;• Disseminate a multi pronged, multi staged, multi platform countrywide outreach campaign, including gender specific material;• Employ alternative community engagement methods to reach women, girls, and other vulnerable member of the community. Outreach efforts will include in the field awareness sessions with affected populations and humanitarian actors in partnership with locally relevant humanitarian actors;• Tap into cluster specific referral pathways and support services while simultaneously consolidating cross sectorial analysis and information to inform a functional understanding of the situation on the ground by performing monitoring and evaluation services for agencies, including post distribution surveys and assessments of hard to reach and accessible areas, and by producing regular datasets to visualise community led information and feedback streams;• Share in an operational level package with organisations and clusters on a daily, weekly, monthly and ad hoc basis through a variety of reporting streams to contribute to the pool of evidenced based data to help improve the relevance of programming;• Coordinate with Clusters, bilateral partners, as well as the Communicating with Communities Working Group, Returns Working Group, the Assessment Working Group, and the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Network;• Coordinate closely with partners and clusters to ensure the timely exchange of two way information, especially in hard to reach and priority areas;• Disaggregate data by age and sex; and,• Conduct post call surveys to monitor caller satisfaction with the Iraq IIC's services.

Protection Risk Analysis

As a consequence of the protracted crisis in the there are several vulnerable categories of population in need of humanitarian support: displaced families, residents in host communities, people who have stayed in their homes in retaken areas, or families planning to return or have returned to their areas of origin.The Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA) conducted by REACH showed that, as per September 2018, 23 % of households are in need of protection assistance. In addition, the assesment shows that they may face simultaneous needs in multiple sectors. Roughly one-third of all returnee households were found to be in need of humanitarian assistance in three or more sectors. Specific gender vulnerabilities are to be expected. Women, man, girls and boys may continue be victim of gender-based violence.Women households, elder people and people with disabilities are considered specifically wulnerable, as they may encounter more difficulties in accessing livelihood opportunties.Women may lack of access to reproductive health services.Children are at risk of explotation, they can be forced to work and be outside of the learning environment.In July 2014, an inter-agency needs assessment into the communication and information needs of displaced populations recommended the establishment of a two-way communication centre to provide displaced populations with timely information and to refer complaints, feedback and urgent needs to the humanitarian community.Subsequent formal and informal review of communication and information requirements among affected populations – conducted by agencies such as UNHCR, ACTED through various means including focus group discussions, surveys throughout 2016 – continue to support the view that the humanitarian response must further strengthen accountability mechanisms. A review of the total number of calls handled by the call centre in February 2017, shows that 60% were requests for information, 30% requests for assistance, and 4% were complaints and feedback calls.

Mitigation Measures

As a confidential and cost-free access point for PoCs across Iraq, including from hard-to-reach areas, the Iraq IIC regularly receives and forwards reports of serious protection concerns, access issues and human rights violations to the Humanitarian Country Team. Once verified this documentation feeds into high-level human rights advocacy. The Iraq IIC is also the primary mechanism for referral of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), strongly endorsed by the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Iraq Taskforce. Through leverage of established referral pathways, operators share information on how to access specialised support services (health, legal and psychosocial) for survivors of abuse, gender-based violence (GBV) and SEA. As the largest inter-agency accountability project of its kind in the UN system, the Iraq IIC serves as the primary humanitarian complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) in Iraq, and aims to complement existing accountability mechanisms by promoting partner CFMs (hotlines, helpdesks) whenever available. The Iraq IIC performs regular analysis of requests, feedback and complaints to support decision-making at the program level in ensuring humanitarian services are in alignment with the needs of the affected population and are not in violation of its core principles. The Iraq IIC enhances the capacity of staff on the integration of protection principles in every aspect of the project cycle, including day-to-day work. The project will be informed by the ‘do no harm’ principles, adhere to humanitarian principles and will promote the application of international standards promoting protection and resilience of Iraq’s most vulnerable. The Iraq IIC adheres to strict confidentiality principles and operators have been trained in creating a safe and dignified environment for IDPs to talk openly about their concerns without fear of retribution. Data collection and sharing adheres to strict security protocols.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Accountability to affected population is the foundation of the Iraq IIC, a key, and unique, accountability mechanism and two-way communication channel in Iraq. A collective accountability mechanism, it is the world's largest inter-agency accountability mechanism of its kind. The Inter-Agency Standing Committee Operational Peer Review (May 2015) identified the Iraq IIC as a key accountability to affected population (AAP) mechanism and a crucial link in achieving a comprehensive AAP approach within the humanitarian response in Iraq. The Iraq IIC was the only project referenced in the 2016 Iraq Humanitarian Response Plan's section on “Building a Better Response”. In the 2017 Humanitarian Response Plan, the Iraq IIC and was named as a primary mechanism for accountability for affected people in Iraq. The Two-way communication systems deliver effective and critical aid in the form ofinformation and communication, key prerequisites for communities to participate in their own recovery.Communities can be empowered by having channels for providing information to aid agencies, which in turn can be used to advocate and deliver appropriate solutions to evidence-based problems. This information exchange reduces confusion, mistrust and frustration among affected populations, emboldening resilience especially among at-risk members of society (women, children, elderly, and disabled persons). By sharing real-time information and trend analysis, the Iraq IIC builds resilience among humanitarian responders by promoting evidenced-based preparedness and programming.Additionally, efforts to promote AAP and the requirement of CFMs in the implementation of humanitarian programs ensures that collectively, the humanitarian community is able to manage both shocks to the response from evolutions in the emergency as well as stresses on programming due to the evolution of beneficiary needs.
