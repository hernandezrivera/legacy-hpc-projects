Project Summary

The project was built in a standard manner to achieve a supportive integration of children in northern Syria in terms of health, psychological, where the project will be as follow:
Health:
Through Children Specialized Hospital in Sarmada, where the hospital will provide specialized services by a professional staff to reduce the death and diseases rate among children in the region.
They will also provide medical and ambulatory screening services for children from birth until 14 years of age. Intensive care and laboratory services, pharmacy, radiology and routine vaccine for children and mothers of childbearing age and other nursing services are available free of charge 24 hours a day. The Hospital have the following sections:
- General pediatric clinic.
- Specialized pediatric clinics.
- A 3-bed emergency section.
- A 25-bed ward including a section devoted to the isolation of communicable diseases.
- A section of incubators comprising 12 incubators, two intensive treatment devices (submarine) and two normal treatment devices.
- Intensive care unit includes two incubators, three beds and three breathing device.
- pharmacy for free medicine.
- Free laboratory.
- X ray and ecosystem clinic.
- Psychological support team.
- EPI routine vaccine clinic.
- Medical warehouses for the hospitals.
Protection:
The project will strengthen the protection of children and mothers by providing psychological support and case management for children and their families and raising awareness about the basic rights of these groups. The psychological support team will also study some specific cases in addition to preparing some information materials to activate advocacy for these vulnerable groups.
According to HRP 2018, humanitarian needs in Syria have increased since the start of the crisis, with 13.1 million people needing humanitarian assistance, including 6.1 million internally displaced persons and more than 5.3 million children in need of assistance. In addition, the dramatic increase in prices and the lack of basic resources have exacerbated the humanitarian situation, especially as people have lost their source of income. It is estimated that only 50% of hospitals are fully operational due to repeated attacks and the injury of staff and facilities. The ongoing humanitarian crises in Syria have created massive waves of internal displacement, most of which have moved into northern Syria.
As a result, the population of the northern regions has doubled because of the ongoing conflict, which places great strain on services which really depleted, resulting in a near collapse of the health system. The increasing influx of displaced people in recent months has added to pressure on health facilities and limited access to health services, which has contributed to a marked reduction in health care.

Needs

according to HNO draft report 2019, 2.2 million children between 0.4 and 2.9 million children between 5-17 are in need all over syria.Dana sub-district in Idleb is one of the 4 most crowded sub-districts in Syria with an approximately 361,545 persons in need for healthcare assistance according to HNO 2018, with dozens of camps on the Syrian-Turkish border like Atmeh camp which includes about 73,000 IDps (21% of total IDPs in Dana) and many other camps that were set up for IDPs near Dana close to the border, and the sub-district have a number of 344,700 IDPs constituting about 70% of its population (487,900) according to REACH August 2018 situation overview report. In August 2018 REACH conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment in Idleb and surrounding areas, this assessment covers 362 out of approximately 700 opposition-held communities in the region through community-level Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and aims to address critical information gaps and to serve as a baseline informing humanitarian actors of the needs and conditions of populations in the region. This situation overview provides an outline of findings from this assessment and includes the following sectors: demography, protection, FSL, health, WASH, education, shelter and NFI, and sectoral priorities. The assessment in Health sector showed that households in 92% of assessed communities were reported to have faced barriers to accessing healthcare in the month prior to data collection. In 247 (68%) assessed communities, KIs reported a lack of available health facilities, while in 209 (58%) assessed communities, a lack of medicines and medical items was reported, KIs in 118 (33%) communities reported that no permanent health facilities were available in the community in the month prior to data collection. In 2018 Dana district received tens of thousands of displaced people from the EG, Homs and Daraa through the evacuation waves toward Northern of Syria and that play a great role in increasing pressure on vulnerable health services in the area. Sham Children's hospital had 54,018 beneficiaries in 2018 with more that 145,000 services provided to them, and IHD confirms that this hospital is the referral hospital in case of child health in northern of Syria.

Implementation of activities

Provide a clinical consultation for approximately 7,800 children and newborns.Providing emergency services to 4,500 children.Providing hospitalization for approximately 2,220 children and newborns in hospitalization department, isolation room and intensive care department.Provide 31,500 support services through the laboratory, radiology department and pharmacy for the patients.Provide routine vaccine service for children and women of reproductive age for 10,000 beneficiaries.Conduct a malnutrition survey covers about 4,200 cases.Provide case management sessions for about 300 persons and psychological support for about 1,500 children.
