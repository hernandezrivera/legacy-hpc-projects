Project Summary

The proposed Action Against Hunger (AAH) response to be implemented in 2023 across the Gaza Strip are aligned with Strategic Objectives 2 and 3 as well as the WASH Cluster Objectives 2 and 3.
AAH’s intervention aim to support around 586,179 individuals representing 143499 boys, 137871 girls, 143949 male adults, 137869 female adults, 11021 male elders and 12425 female elders including people with disabilities and specific needs) to gain safe access to the rehabilitated WASH facilities in public facilities (3 health care facilities (HCF) and 9 schools). The intervention will also target unconnected communities (Alawdah and Tal Hawa in Gaza city, Alsatar in Khan Yunis city and Alzanah in Bani Suhila) that rely on cesspits to discharge their wastewater, and will connect them to adequate wastewater drainage system. Furthermore, AAH will construct stormwater drainage systems in Beit Lahia and Gaza to protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys from being in contact with flooded stormwater or wastewater during the winter season due to the accumulation of stormwater in the cesspits flooding in the streets and in hospitals and schools. In addition, the intervention will support WASH service providers to carry out electro-mechanical repair works for 5 wastewater pump stations and 6 water wells to ensure the continuous operation of these facilities
The interventions will be complemented with public hygiene promotion and awareness campaigns including but not limited to community orientation sessions and initiatives, hardcopies and digitalized IEC materials to disseminate key messages about proper use of the WASH facilities to enhance the hygiene good knowledge and practices, distribution of personal and dignified hygiene kit in female schools, and peer to peer activities within the targeted schools.
By improving access to sanitation facilities in public places and communication with communities through the hygiene awareness campaign, AAH aims to to improve the communities’ hygiene practices, provide a safe environment and reduce public health risks, and enhance the ownership and active participation of communities, in addition to improving communities access and trust to use AAH FCM channels for feedback and complaints.
The proposed intervention is guided by AAH Gender and Protection policy and AAH staff endeavor to streamline safety and dignity in all humanitarian interventions. Accountability and participation will be ensured through interviews and focus group discussions and through applying AAH’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.

1 - Needs

21% of Gaza’s population suffer from stagnant sewage and/or accumulation of mixed stormwater with sewage (MSNA 2022) due to lack of wastewater and stormwater networks, and rely on poorly managed or homemade cesspits, or undersized random pipelines or discharging their greywater directly to the streets creating health and environmental hazards for the surrounding communities.In addition, around 85% of households employing negative coping mechanisms are due to shortage of water and sanitation services especially during emergencies due to limited capacity of the service provider to operate and maintain the WASH facilities.Such conditions are the result of limited financial and technical capacities of the service providers, and Israeli restrictions on the entry of materials leaving WASH service providers struggling to fully restore and maintain the functionality of WASH services in the Gaza Strip. The households’ economic situation and high unemployment rates (46.6% during the Q1-2022, PCBS) are negatively impacted by the political situation and households cannot afford to frequently evacuate the cesspits (several times a month with a cost varying from 20-75 ILS/trip) according to the WASH cluster vulnerability map of Dec 2021, leading to not prioritizing it by most vulnerable households. Four localities in Gaza, Bani Suhila, and Khan Yunis within the WASH Cluster vulnerability mapping were identified by AAH in coordination with CMWU and area focal points as areas that include around 2746 households (representing vulnerable men, women, boys and girls) not connected to nearby wastewater networks. Furthermore, these households are unemployed, with 54% under the poverty line and dependent on aid, and high rate of complaints are received by those municipalities about the inability of vulnerable households to manage the cesspits or rehabilitate the collapsed one- putting their families, especially children, at risk and continues suffering from severe stormwater/wastewater floods during winters. Moreover, 2 localities in Beit Lahia and Gaza were identified based on the revised flood zones map and prioritization (AAH-2022) as suffering from frequent stormwater floods, putting around 8,977 households at risk of waterborne diseases through cross-contamination of a fragile water supply infrastructure, closures of main roads and preventing children from attending schools. AAH with the support of service providers identified 11 facilities (6 wells and 5 wastewater pump stations) in need of electro-mechanical support to ensure continuous water and sanitation serviceThis situation imposes more pressure and burdens on women who are responsible for water management in their houses and health care of the family members, and on children, elderly and PwDs, who are the most vulnerable to health effects from the lack of adequate WASH services and experience difficulties during the floods.Moreover, WASH facilities in public schools and health centers suffer from poor WASH conditions, as they are impacted by all negative internal and external factors. According to the Education cluster, 63% of public schools run by the Ministry of Education already operate on a double- or triple-shift system, resulting in excessive crowding in schools and insufficient and poorly maintained WASH facilities and un-adapted for children with disabilities. Poor latrines status with the overcrowded schools (60 students/latrine that does not meet the standards; 30 students/latrine), undermines the students’ hygiene practices. According to AAH assessments in 2021, only 25% of students reported their ability to practice safe use of sanitation facilities in their schools. which raises the need for urgent rehabilitation of WASH facilities (MoE assessment of schools’ infrastructure needs in 2019) to ensure safe and healthy learning environments. and hygiene promotion and behaviour changes activities to reduce the vandalism practices among students towards the schools’ assets.As a result, AAH in coordination with MoE identified 9 schools to rehabilitate the WASH facilities and to allow 7185 students and 340 teachers to safely access and use such facilities. The intervention will be supported with hygiene promotion activities addressing proper hand washing, personal hygiene practices, latrines proper use, enhancement of menstrual hygiene management (MHM).While for HCFs, AAH assessments during 2021-2022 clearly show the MoH’s limited capacity and financial resources that hinder their ability to maintain the existing facility, reduce the deterioration of WASH facilities, or ensure privacy and safety for staff and patients that increased the difficulty of the stay or the working environment for staff. Moreover, weak community behaviour and lack of ownership had led to inefficient WASH facilities. Such conditions have an impact on the continuity of WASH services, the ability to provide health services especially during emergencies and limit the application of the Infection prevention and control measures in these facilities.Accordingly, AAH will continue rehabilitating the WASH facilities in the European Gaza Hospital and an additional 2 HCFs of Level 4 with awareness campaigns, disseminating key messages for medical staff, cleaners and users to ensure safe use of the HCFs by around 96,679 individuals.

Activity 1: Access to sanitation and hygieneActivity 1.1: The installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection networks and HH connections. To connect households in the Gaza Governorate (Alawdah and Tal Hawa) and Khan Yunis Governorate (Alsatar in Khan Yunis and Alzanah in Bani Suhila) and close about 1300 cesspits. The assessment will use an inclusive participatory approach to identify key needs, priorities and gabs in hygiene knowledge and practices and feed into the design of hygiene promotion activities and the content. Community awareness campaigns will be designed based on the findings, and conducted to improve the knowledge and practices, adopting the Communication for Development (C4D) approach to reinforce the massages related to good personal hygiene practices, menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and safe drainage and management of wastewater systems. These initiatives may include clean-up campaigns, a community garden, marathon, wall painting, and interactive awareness workshops. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

8.6.c - How will feedback be used? * The Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) is managed by the AAH’s MEAL unit. Following the FCM standard operating procedures it will be introduced to the targeted communities through information materials like brochures and information leaflets inside the communities where WASH interventions are taking place, to provide communities and beneficiaries with different channels to access AAH, prior, during and after implementation. The FCM will be active from the beginning of the project through an active hotline number managed by MEAL staff. Complaints, feedback or suggestions are recorded on a protected sheet, and MEAL staff uses the agreed FCM procedures to handle the complaint based on its level and sensitivity. AAH management and the program team jointly discuss the complaint (depending on its nature and sensitivity) and formulate responses on a case-by-case basis. On average, within 2 weeks the response usually is provided to the complainant or community members by contacting him/her back through phone or emails. If the response includes actions these will be considered by the program team, they will fill in the learning log sheet with actions planned to allow the MEAL team to follow up the execution of these actions. On a semi-annual basis, a FCM report will be developed and shared with AAH management and program team to document learning captured from FCM findings, suggestions and feedback.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Throughout the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming and Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. AAH has mechanisms in place to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct and our held accountable if they do not respect it. AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Tip Sheets, along with learnings from various interventions and external evaluations. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. Risks assessments and analysis of the protection context are undertaken for all the projects, and even more so when implemented with at risk groups (e.g. minors) or in areas where risk of doing harm is exacerbated (e.g. Area C in the West Bank or inside health facilities). In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH also continuously works on understanding gender roles and how its interventions might impact them without aggravating existing power relations between genders. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, which focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, and Independence. During the course of the project, AAH will ensure all projects are easily accessible to those most at need, avoiding risks to the beneficiaries. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. At a global level, AAH relies on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistleblowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE ́ towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented to report these behaviours to headquarters. Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated by providing compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and in 2019, AAH conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
