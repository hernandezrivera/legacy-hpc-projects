Project Summary

In 2025, Tdh will enhance its WASH response by strengthening the management and maintenance of existing WASH services through cost-effective solutions, community engagement, and gender-responsive, rights-based, and inclusive services that cater to adolescents, youth, and the elderly in Camp 26, and the host communities, including the new E Block. WASH activities will promote knowledge and awareness to encourage proper personal hygiene and appropriate use of WASH facilities. Tdh will also address the need for adequate and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene. All activities will follow the do-no-harm principle, prioritizing the safety and dignity of women and girls.
Tdh will continue its sensitization and prevention efforts before and during natural disasters, including disaster risk reduction and addressing AWD, dengue outbreaks, etc., and will also provide support in the event of man-made disasters, such as fires. Throughout the project development, a WASH operations and maintenance committee, along with key stakeholders, was engaged, and community feedback and response mechanisms will be maintained during the implementation period.
Tdh’s WASH activities will address gaps and needs at various community levels, considering age (children aged 5-17, adults aged 18-59, and elderly aged 60+), gender, and diversity (including support for other-gender families within the project areas). Tdh will continue to operate and maintain the Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) plant which alredy estabilished, handling desludging, treatment, and disposal, along with waste management activities such as collection, transportation, segregation, and disposal, as well as organic composting and plastic recycling. Tdh-operated FSM and waste management services will continue to receive, treat, and dispose of sludge and waste, while recycling plastic waste from Camp 26 and 27, the two Tdh-operated Primary Health Care Centers (PHCs), the host communities, and Tdh’s Multi-Purpose Community Centers.
The two PHCs operated by Tdh already have incinerators; however, given the high volume of contaminated waste generated at these centers that cannot be treated on-site, all waste—after sterilizing the plastic components of infectious waste—will be processed at the SWM Plant, which is equipped with crusher machines, ash pits, sharp pits, and incinerators following appropriate standards. Tdh will continue to operate and maintain WASH facilities in the PHCs, camps, and host communities, as well as construct, rehabilitate, or decommission facilities (e.g., latrines with solar lights, bathing cubicles, rainwater harvesting systems, incinerators, women’s hygiene centers, and connecting drains) based on assessed needs. Water supply to the recently installed desalination plant in Camp 26 (F-Block) will continue. Given the water scarcity in Teknaf, this plant will help ensure better access to water for beneficiaries in Camps 26 and surrounding host community, alleviating water scarcity challenges to some extent. Tdh will conduct ongoing management and improvement of all facilities to ensure effective Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) and disinfection.
Awareness-raising and WASH-related messages will be disseminated to both refugees and host communities through household sessions, meetings, campaigns, day celebrations, and the development and distribution of IEC/BCC materials. The Tdh WASH and outreach teams will cover topics such as waste management, sanitation, handwashing, AWD/dengue prevention, and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) among adolescents and women. These efforts will be supported by the distribution of NFI/WASH kits during emergencies, as part of Tdh’s contingency plan, including handwashing devices, hygiene kits, and MHM kits at the household level. Emergency latrines will also be provided to ensure adequate WASH support for beneficiaries during any disaster.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In Camps 26 and 27, the 2023 J-MSNA findings highlight ongoing gaps in WASH services, with service gaps of 34% in Camp 26 and 49% in Camp 27. While 97% of Rohingya refugees in Camp 26 and 90% in Camp 27 reported access to improved sanitation facilities, these are often shared latrines serving 7-8 households, which does not meet sector standards. Additionally, there is an average gap of 6.5% in sanitation facilities, along with significant shortcomings in handwashing facilities, which are essential for personal hygiene. In both camps, 88% of refugees reported access to drinking water, leaving a 12% gap. In host communities, households reported 77% access to handwashing facilities, with a 23% gap remaining. They reported 94% access to improved sanitation facilities, but a 6% gap persists, and only 55% reported access to water, leaving a 45% gap. The Tdh-PRM-funded project from 2020 to 2024 has addressed some of these issues through the operation and maintenance of WASH facilities, construction of new facilities (including a desalination plant), and distribution of WASH items and NFIs. While continuing solid waste management, fecal sludge management, and desalination services, improvements have been made to manage LDPE, and a plastic recycling machine has been installed. As a result of distributing household bins and awareness messages, households have begun to segregate their waste, promoting better environmental health. Due to high population density, latrines fill up quickly, and inadequate desludging services can lead to overflowing pits that contaminate nearby water sources. Block cleaning services are necessary, as proper wastewater disposal from households and WASH facilities is often insufficient. Therefore, traditional toilets should be avoided, and septic tanks should be constructed in low-lying areas where needed. According to the 2023 J-MSNA findings, ongoing WASH needs in Camps 26 and 27, as well as surrounding host communities, include insufficient access to handwashing, sanitation, and water supply services. The findings indicate that 97% of sanitation facilities are accessible in Camp 26 and 90% in Camp 27, with 88% water access reported in both camps. However, challenges persist, particularly in hygiene, and fecal sludge management facilities need to enhance efficiency and coverage. There is also a need to harmonize solid waste management systems and engage WASH agencies and communities in sustainable practices, ensuring female participation in these efforts. After seven years of WASH activities in Cox’s Bazar, agencies must focus on improving existing facilities to enhance durability, quality, privacy, and accessibility while addressing gender, disability, and age factors. Constructing new latrines following unified designs (including solar lighting) will help address coverage gaps, along with upgrading FSM facilities through full-chain treatment processes. Another priority is to strengthen solid waste management systems, alongside assessing factors that motivate waste segregation. The HP strategy should be followed, focusing on participatory approaches for FDMNs to foster changes in high-risk behaviors. Community mobilization will be strengthened to ensure the involvement of women and girls in the design, feedback mechanisms, and implementation of WASH services. As Teknaf is a water-scarce area in Bangladesh, there is a pressing need to develop water resource management systems and appropriate solid waste management that contribute to environmental preservation, along with innovative interventions to save water.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Tdh adopts age- and disability-sensitive approaches in its WASH interventions to promote inclusive access and participation for all beneficiaries. Tdh’s activities address the specific needs of children (5-17 years), adults (18-59 years), and elderly individuals (60+ years), as well as persons with disabilities across Camp 26 & 27, and the host communities. WASH facilities are designed and maintained to be accessible, with modifications to support individuals with physical disabilities, ensuring that the infrastructure upholds inclusive standards and promotes dignity and autonomy for all users.Regular WASH activities—such as hygiene promotion sessions, meetings, campaigns, and NFI distribution—actively involve people of all ages, genders (including third gender), and those with disabilities in decision-making processes. The project ensures accessible WASH facilities by incorporating necessary accommodations during construction. Safeguarding measures are strictly followed throughout implementation, adhering to the do-no-harm and CHS. To reduce open defecation, children under five are provided with plastic potties to encourage proper toilet habits. Identified protection concerns are promptly reported to the protection team, and solar lights are installed near latrines to GBV risks and ensure safe, secure latrine access at night.Tdh integrates age and disability-responsive messaging in hygiene promotion sessions, campaigns, and meetings, empowering participants to maintain personal hygiene and engage in safe WASH practices. Inclusive and accessible hygiene facilities, such as latrines with solar lighting and adaptable cubicles, promote safe, equitable use, particularly benefiting elderly individuals, people with disabilities, and women and girls, thereby reducing their vulnerability to protection risks.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure that beneficiaries are included in the program's design and implementation process, the ISAC AAP framework identifies three key steps: taking account, giving account, and being held to account. Tdh will conduct regular community meetings and interactive sessions, including consultation sessions, short interviews, and exit interviews, to update community members on how their feedback has influenced program decisions and actions. During these sessions, Tdh will provide detailed information about previous feedback and the resulting changes implemented. These gatherings will foster two-way communication, allowing community members to ask questions and offer additional input. To ensure continuous improvement of feedback mechanisms, their effectiveness will be evaluated every six months, and new channels will be explored as necessary. Periodic feedback reports, summarizing the feedback received, the actions taken, and the outcomes achieved, will be shared with the community through these meetings. This approach will promote transparency, accountability, and active community engagement in the program.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will prioritize community engagement to raise awareness of rights and reporting mechanisms, integrating sensitization on GBV, child protection, and PSEA into WASH campaigns. Gender-segregated facilities and solar lights will enhance security, reducing the risk of SEA. Any PSEA concerns will be addressed following the organizational policy, in coordination with relevant agencies. To prevent SEA, all staff, volunteers, guards, and cleaners will receive PSEA orientation and CoC training. A robust reporting mechanism will ensure proper handling of SEA cases, in line with Tdh’s zero-tolerance policy. As co-chair of the PSEA network, Tdh supports awareness efforts and participates in meetings. Tdh’s WASH operations committee and key stakeholders will maintain accountability, with a feedback mechanism that empowers vulnerable groups, including women, girls, and persons with disabilities, to report abuse safely. All services will prioritize the safety, dignity, and privacy of beneficiaries. The project will offer gender-responsive and inclusive WASH services, addressing the needs of adolescents, youth, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. Awareness-raising on MHM will empower women and mitigate exploitation risks. Tdh will apply the do-no-harm principle in all activities and ensure staff are regularly trained on PSEA to uphold ethical standards. By embedding PSEA throughout project design, implementation, and monitoring, Tdh will protect the safety and dignity of beneficiaries, particularly women and girls, in line with the 2025 JRP guidelines.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project incorporates Climate Action through strategies addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), Anticipatory Actions, and climate change adaptation. Key elements include:Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) & Disaster Risk Management (DRM): Tdh promotes disaster preparedness and prevention, including tackling waterborne diseases like AWD and dengue, and supporting responses to fires and natural disasters. These efforts build community resilience to climate-induced risks.Anticipatory Actions: The project includes contingency plans for emergencies, such as constructing latrines and distributing hygiene kits to help communities respond to climate events.Environmental and Climate Change Adaptation: The project focuses on sustainable water and waste management practices, including operating the FSM plant in Camp 27 for waste treatment and recycling. The desalination plant also addresses water scarcity exacerbated by climate change, improving access to clean water.The project reduces vulnerability by addressing environmental risks, providing portable toilets, and maintaining WASH facilities. It promotes awareness of disaster preparedness and climate change, ensuring long-term resilience. Additionally, emergency health support and coordination with stakeholders strengthen the community’s capacity to respond to disasters, enhancing overall well-being.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project has embedded EPR (Emergency Preparedness and Response) measures, including a business continuity plan, emergency response standard operating procedures, and contingency funds. It aims to enhance disaster risk reduction strategies for floods, landslides, dengue, and AWD outbreaks by managing waste within the community to minimize flooding risks and providing portable toilets for basic sanitation needs during crises. In the event of WASH-related outbreaks, the project is designed to deliver timely emergency assistance and maintain WASH facilities to ensure their functionality and safety. Routine disinfection measures will safeguard public health. The project also focuses on community resilience by disseminating awareness messages about disaster preparedness and climate change adaptation, empowering residents to take proactive measures. Emergency health support will be coordinated with camp authorities and stakeholders, ensuring that affected populations receive necessary medical care and resources.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project actively engages youth aged 15-24 through a range of WASH activities aimed at enhancing their knowledge and awareness of personal hygiene and the maintenance of WASH facilities. Youth will be involved in various activities, such as participating in awareness-raising sessions on hygiene, waste management, handwashing, and disease prevention, including raising awareness about Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), dengue outbreaks, and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). They will also be included in community engagement efforts related to the operation and maintenance of WASH facilities, giving them opportunities to contribute to local water, sanitation, and hygiene solutions. Additionally, youth will play an active role in disaster preparedness and risk reduction efforts, particularly in sensitization and prevention activities for natural and man-made disasters. The project aims to build resilience among youth, empowering them with vital knowledge, and ensuring their involvement in the sustainability of WASH services within their communities. The project will promote community engagement by involving youth in sensitization and prevention measures related to hygiene, disaster risk reduction, and health issues such as AWD and dengue. Awareness-raising activities will cover topics such as waste management, sanitation, handwashing, and menstrual hygiene management, targeting both refugees and host communities. Furthermore, Tdh will distribute WASH-related kits and emergency supplies, providing youth with practical resources to support their involvement in community health initiatives. Through these efforts, the project not only addresses immediate WASH needs but also empowers youth to take an active role in promoting hygiene and health within their communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

By providing knowledge about personal hygiene and facility upkeep, the project empowers refugees to take ownership of their health. Establishing WASH operations and maintenance committees encourages community participation and feedback. The project also addresses water scarcity through a desalination plant and waste management initiatives like plastic recycling and composting, enhancing sanitation and creating economic opportunities. By integrating disaster risk reduction measures and emergency support, Tdh helps refugees build resilience against future challenges, fostering self-reliance within the community.

The project activities in the host community align with development initiatives and priorities in Cox’s Bazar district. Tdh's WASH response focuses on improving the management and maintenance of WASH services through cost-effective, inclusive solutions that cater to all age groups. This supports the district’s development goals by enhancing access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene. The project incorporates disaster risk reduction measures, addressing both natural and man-made disasters, which is crucial for community resilience. Additionally, Tdh operates FSM plant and waste management initiatives that directly contribute to environmental sustainability and public health, aligning with district priorities for improved waste management. Community engagement through awareness-raising sessions ensures that both refugees and host community members are actively involved, fostering a collaborative approach to development and enhancing overall community well-being.
