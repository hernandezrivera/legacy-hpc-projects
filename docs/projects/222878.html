Project Summary

This project focus to create disaster resilience, durable, safe, secure, dignified, environmental friendly and promote social cohesion. The overall program will include key policies on PSEA, child protection, safeguarding, and complaint and feedback mechanisms. Total target in camps is 15021 HHs (75,725 Individuals) including Camp 13 9442 HHs (47528 individuals), Camp 19 5,579 HHs (28197 individuals) as well as in the host community 2100 HHs (11630 Individuals) in Haldia Palong, Ratna Palong and Raja Palong union. The activities are proposed based on the identified needs and gaps in the shelter performance assessment 2024, ISNA 2024 report and internal monitoring and assessment report by Caritas. The Joint Response Plan (JRP-2025) project including Access and safety for 1250 HHs by constructing Bridge, pathway and stairs. As a shelter focal agency in camp 13 and 19 project will cover HHs through shelter construction training and CFRM desk will be available in caritas working areas. Under Disaster risk management community awareness plan for 5000 HHs, Drainage construction /repair /maintenance and cleaning will cover 600 HHs, fire safety will ensure in cocking space for 6600 HHs, emergency shelter materials will be prepared for 300 HHs, Rapid damage verification for 300 HHs and Tie down support for 15021HHs. This project arrange NFI for 350 HHs, SUM for 6600 HHs and new MTS for 300 HHs. In host community 600 HHs will be cover through access and safety, NFI for 600 HHs and from the shelter support will cover 900HHs. Project will use continuation of treated bamboo support from IOM, in the camp and research-based pilot on alternative shelter materials solutions including construction materials waste management. Families who received shelter support in 2024 will continue to receive regular shelter assistance in 2025 based on their shelter condition and needs. Emergency NFI support, Rapid Damage Verification (RDV)-related shelter materials, relocation shelter construction, and emergency Tie-Down Kits (TDK) will be sourced from the IOM pipeline and distributed with technical support from Caritas. Catchment shelter focal points will be assigned to provide emergency support, as well as shelter and protection-related referral services in the targeted camps 13 and 19. Additionally, age diversity and extremely vulnerable individuals and including elderly persons will receive porter and construction support for the targeted shelter and NFI activities. All targeted interventions will actively involve all gender inclusion and age diversity within community members. Special attention will be given to engaging women, youth and elderly individuals for lightweight daily labor tasks. Caritas will ensure technical support for the design and implementation of accessibility features for age diversity, in coordination with the ADWG and the Protection sector. Additionally, Caritas will collaborate with the Energy & Environment Network on climate action strategies, particularly for activities like solar lamps, solar street lights and research into environmentally friendly alternative shelter materials. Effective coordination with relevant sectors and humanitarian actors—including CiC, RRRC, CIMO, the DC Office, Site Management, the UNO Office, and Union Parishad—will be maintained. This will include active participation in meetings, regular reporting, and ensuring effective collaboration across all levels. Caritas have practices to evaluate project after each intervention. Dedicated MEAL department will conduct PDM and real time monitoring in camp and as well as host community. PDM report will incorporate with project designing and ensue accountability to the affected population.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The overall vulnerability situation in refugee camps has reached an all-time high since the beginning of the refugee crisis. Shelter performance standard assessment 2024 shows 71% of the beneficiary received shelter support last year, while 29% did not. 42% of shelters reported no rainwater intrusion, while 58% indicated that rainwater did enter. Also, 85% of shelters failed to meet the Desired Performance Standards for treated bamboo, and 70% showed visible signs of infestation. ISNA’24 report found 24.61% have issue in cooking space and not functional among them 40% of households indicate insufficient cooking space, while 19% noted an unmet need for cooking-related NFIs. Report also shows over 30% reported lighting issues and expressed a need for solar lamps or repair of lamps to ensure nighttime safety. The ISNA’24 report also highlights ongoing deficiencies in NFIs for affected populations, with a voucher or cash approach recommended to provide beneficiaries with choice of flexibility.Community engagement and participation require strengthening, as many reports poor experiences when raising issues, with limited involvement in decision-making, especially among women, youth, and individuals with disabilities. According to ISNA'24, 31% of households report not receiving follow-ups on complaints. Shelter activities in 2025 will ensure inclusion for women, men, girls, boys, LGBTI, and persons with disabilities, incorporating their feedback and boosting women’s cash-for-work and decision making and participation engagement beyond the current 53%. Refugees are still exposed to natural hazards, and in 2024, waterlogging, landslides and heavy monsoons were major concerns. The camps’ hilly terrain site improvements at the household level are one of critical need including drainage, retaining walls, pathways, stairs, and expected use of durable materials. Many households still have inadequate access to a minimum variety of food. Despite the many improvements through the relief efforts so far and also the ongoing project, the condition of the shelters in many sections of the camp remains a concern. The shelters built so far require regular maintenance and replacement of dilapidated materials. The J-MSNA published in August 2023 revealed that 72% of households reported damage or noticeable issues in their enclosures, with the most common problems being leaks during rain (39%), wall damage (35%), and minor roof damage (35%). A significant majority (97%) indicated needs for non-food items (NFIs), particularly blankets (45%), kitchen sets (53%), and mosquito nets (58%). Only a small percentage of materials can be reused. The socio-economic challenges of poverty, illiteracy, limited freedom of movement and low income opportunities continue to be a concern in terms of medium-term food security. The difficult living conditions in the camps affect the physical and psychological well-being of refugees, especially children, women and persons with disabilities. Appropriate protection of refugees from abuse and exploitation remains inadequate. In addition, there are seasonal environmental impacts, especially in the cyclone and monsoon seasons, during which floods and landslides regularly occur in the camp.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming will be integrated throughout the project cycle, ensuring participation, accountability, meaningful access, and the safety, privacy, and security of affected populations. Targeted households (HHs) will be selected based on specific criteria, including protection needs assessments, referrals from the protection sector, and concerns raised through the Community Feedback and Referral Mechanism (CFRM). These households will be prioritized for support. Confidentiality of data will be maintained in line with the protection principles of humanitarian response. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) teams will coordinate with the PSEA network, Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS), Gender-Based Violence Sub-Sector (GBVSS), and the protection sector to ensure timely guidance and adherence to relevant protocols. The PSEA, child protection, and safeguarding focal points within the CB team will ensure all guidelines and policies are followed by staff. Transparency and accountability to beneficiaries will be prioritized throughout the project. Special attention will be given to the needs of vulnerable groups, including Elderly, Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs), Female-Headed Households (FHHs), Households with Special Needs, and LGBTQ+ individuals. For those households requiring accessibility support, modifications such as stairs with handrails, ramps, and partition walls will be provided as needed.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Caritas Bangladesh seeks the active participation of all members of the refugee and host community in the design of project activities. Particularly vulnerable and/or vulnerable households, will be prioritized in the distribution of materials or supplies and supported in transport and construction through labor. CB will ensure that no child labor is engaged in any activities conducted in the camps or host communities. Child rights will be promoted in all project activities, and the safety and security of children will be prioritized, especially during construction and repair works. CB will provide additional labor or porter support to women with children, pregnant women, and others in need, ensuring they are included in all activities such as meetings, training, assessments, implementation, and decision-making processes. Elderly individuals will also be prioritized throughout the project. The Protection team of CB will work in close coordination with Site Management to ensure the establishment of safe spaces for any unaccompanied children discovered during construction or other project activities. Additionally, during distribution events and training sessions, CB will provide child-friendly spaces to ensure the safety and well-being of children. During technical assistance and material selection, the CB shelter team will take extra precautions to ensure that no child is exposed to potential harm. All staff and volunteers will undergo training in child safeguarding, child protection, PSEA, and the Code of Conduct to minimize risks and ensure adherence to ethical standards.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will ensure accountability to affected populations and all other stakeholders involved in program implementation. The project will adhere to the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), and the program plan will be shared with the community before the start of any project activities. The Project Inception Meeting will serve as a key platform to share project information with stakeholders. CB follows a community-based approach in its shelter/NFI activities, engaging the community in the entire process—ranging from needs assessments and project planning to implementation, monitoring, and ensuring the sustainability of the project. CB will utilize an internal and camp-level Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) to collect feedback and complaints from the community, which will be addressed in a timely and effective manner throughout the project cycle. Complaints and feedback will be gathered through various channels such as feedback boxes, hotline numbers, and the CFRM help desk. These will be reviewed by the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) team, the CFRM focal point, and safeguarding focal points, in collaboration with key community representatives. The results and resolutions will then be communicated back to the relevant community members (both Rohingya and Host communities). In the case of referrals from Site Management or the Protection team, CB's dedicated CFRM and Safeguarding team will verify and address these referrals. Once verified, the outcomes will be shared with the Site Management and Protection teams, who will then communicate the results to the beneficiaries. Both camp-level and sector-level coordination will be a priority throughout the project cycle. Additionally, all community members will be made fully aware of the CFRM process, and the CB MEAL team will ensure its effective implementation, promoting transparency and active participation.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has established a robust PSEA policy, which applies to all stakeholders, including beneficiaries, staff, volunteers, vendors, donors, and partners. PSEA considerations are fully integrated into every project undertaken by CB, with a strong commitment to mainstreaming PSEA principles across all activities. To ensure comprehensive understanding and compliance, CB will conduct regular training and refresher orientation sessions on PSEA for all project participants. Throughout the project cycle, CB will ensure the visibility of PSEA awareness materials such as posters, flyers, and videos at key locations, including distribution points, training centers, and office compounds. This will reinforce key PSEA messages and promote awareness. Separate training rooms and distribution points will be designated to ensure PSEA principles are communicated effectively. In project areas, CB will establish clear and accessible communication mechanisms, including Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM), to facilitate open channels for complaints and feedback. During any intervention or training, there will be mandatory sessions on PSEA referral and complaint mechanisms, ensuring participants are aware of how to report any issues. Additionally, CB prioritizes risk management and PSEA by appointing dedicated PSEA focal points within the project. When handling complaints, CB is committed to maintaining confidentiality, ensuring accessibility, and respecting cultural sensitivity. In the event of a SEA (Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) complaint, CB will take immediate and appropriate action in accordance with the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for SEA Complaint Referral in the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh. Through these comprehensive measures, Caritas Bangladesh strives to create a safe and respectful environment within all its projects, actively working to prevent and address any incidents of PSEA.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. Caritas Bangladesh (CB)’s shelter interventions are deeply integrated with Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) strategies. To ensure effective DRR and DRM, CB has identified several key activities designed to mitigate disaster risks and improve community resilience. These include Household-level Site Improvement (SI), Tie Down Kits (TDK) with technical support, Emergency NFI distribution, Porter support for Elderly and Vulnerable Households (EVHHs), Pre-positioning of emergency supplies for Cyclones, Monsoons, and Fires, Rapid Damage Verification (RDV) and shelter materials distribution, Research and pilot activities on environmentally friendly alternative shelter materials. Orientation for targeted families and local leaders on DRR/DRM, which will be integrated into all relevant training and orientation sessions. In response to climate change mitigation, CB is committed to addressing environmental concerns through these activities, prioritizing actions that contribute to reducing the impacts of climate change. Community committees in each block or sub-block will be engaged and consulted throughout the entire project cycle. This includes key stages such as risk assessments, site selection, implementation, and monitoring. CB will share technical knowledge related to disaster risk reduction with these committees to ensure they are well-equipped to manage risks in their areas. The CB technical team will provide technical support at the community level, when necessary, and conduct follow-up sessions with the community committees to ensure these groups are effectively supporting community-led activities related to DRR/DRM at the block or sub-block level.
