Project Summary

The project aims to improve the living conditions of vulnerable communities in the villages of Karako, Korona, ATTBougou Tabacoro, and Dialakorodji Zirabakoro, located in the circles of Kati, and Ouelessebougou.
The intervention aims to address the needs of vulnerable communities in these villages affected by severe lack of access to safe drinking water, exposing populations to waterborne diseases and protection issues.
This will result in the installation of improved water points and strengthening water governance to ensure sustainable supply of safe and clean drinking water to the targeted communities.
The project also aims to enhance livelihood opportunities for vulnerable women through vegetable production and mobilization of local savings via women's VSLAs, enabling them to diversify their income sources and thereby contributing to their empowerment.
The expected outcomes of this intervention are as follows:
Outcome 1: Improved use of safe WASH services for 5837 people living in Karako, Korona, ATTbougou Tabacoro and Dialakorodji Zirabakoro villages.
Outcome 2: Improved livelihoods opportunities for women in vulnerable households in the target villages.

Please complete/provide a Context Analysis (CA), covering in particular economic, social, political, and environmental aspects. Do you have a standalone CA document? [MF: D1.b, IH: 5.1.4]NoOver the past decade, Mali, a Sahelian country with a population of 22,395,489 inhabitants in 2023 (RGPH 5) has faced a complex crisis that derives from a volatile security situation exacerbated by structural vulnerability factors, socio-economic challenges as well as climate change. The gender distribution of this population is 50.3 per cent for men and 48.7 per cent for women. 49.4% of this population is under 18 years old. The average life expectancy at birth (for both sexes) is 52.6 years, which is below the global average of 71 years. Mali is ranked 186th out of 191 countries on the basis of the UNDP Human Development Index (HDI) 2021. The poverty line was estimated in 2020 at 179,327 XOF and the poverty index at the national level was 45.5% in 2022 according to the HRP 2023.The provision of basic social services in Mali is very weak, and regional disparities in poverty persist, with much higher rates in rural areas. The state's capacity to provide adequate access to basic social services, particularly in rural areas, has been limited by a number of factors, including poor governance and structural problems. According to the report by Mali's National Statistics Institute (INSTAT) on household consumption, poverty and well-being in 2023, 19.9% of rural households are more than 15 km from a source of drinking water, and 23.1% of these households reach it after 1 hour. In the Koulikoro region, 51.9% of households travel between 0-4 km to access drinking water, 39.3% between 5 and 15 km and 8% more than 15 km.In terms of time taken to access drinking water, at national level, 100% of households travel between 0-4 km and less than 30 minutes to access water. On the other hand, in the Koulikoro region, 28.9% of households take less than 30 minutes to access drinking water, 66.4% take between 30 and 59 minutes, and 4.8% take 60 minutes or more. These statistics show that there is a huge need for drinking water in this region. As a result, people are using water sources that are unsuitable for human consumption, leading to water-borne diseases such as dysentery, typhoid fever, diarrhoea, and skin and eye diseases. And because people's incomes are so low, they are unable to meet the costs of treating illnesses caused by drinking unsafe water. Women are the most vulnerable groups affected by low incomes. In rural areas, they represent 75% of the agricultural workforce, but benefit less from the spin-offs of this agricultural activity because of the socio-cultural constraints that limit their access to land. Agriculture is a key sector for lifting women out of poverty, but the increasing degradation of land and natural resources caused by climate change and their lack of training in innovative production techniques make women more vulnerable.Added to this is the lack of financial resources, which prevents them from carrying out IGAs to diversify their income and improve their livelihoods. The combined effects of people's limited access to basic social infrastructure and the diversification of livelihoods are having an impact on vulnerable populations in the communes of Bougoula, Dialakorodji, Kalabancoro and Ouelessebougou in the following sectors:Access to drinking water is an acute problem in the villages of Karako (commune of Bougoula), Korona (commune of Ouelessebougou), Dialakorodji Zirabakoro (commune of Dialakorodji) and ATTbougou Tabacoro (commune of Kalabancoro), as confirmed by the results of the needs assessment carried out in these localities by IR in June 2024. 83.75% of households surveyed said they did not have access to a source of drinking water, including Korona (80%), Karako (85%), Dialakorodji Zirabakoro (90%) and ATTbougou Tabacoro (80).These households, which are in urgent need, use other alternatives to fill the gap. For example, 86.25% of households surveyed said they used unhealthy water sources to meet their drinking water needs. The use of unsafe water sources is the cause of water-borne diseases such as diarrhoea, dermatitis, bilharzia and blindness. The main reasons why people use unhealthy water sources are due to the lack of drinking water sources and their remoteness.91.25% of households surveyed said there was not enough water in the household. These same households say that they travel a distance of between 500 metres and 1 km for the nearest source of drinking water, 6.25% say that it is less than 50 metres away and only 2.50% have a source of drinking water at home. 87.5% of those surveyed said that it took more than 15 minutes to get to the nearest source of drinking water. Unfortunately, the domestic water supply means that the most vulnerable groups, particularly women and girls, have to travel long distances, which exposes them to problems of protection and gender-based violence, according to 43.75% of households surveyed.In addition, 38.75% of households surveyed said that the lack of water points led to disagreements between the women involved in supplying water to households.Difficulties in accessing drinking water can be explained by a number of factors, including the inadequacy and remoteness of water points, the lack of community organisation for the sustainable management of water points, which results in the existence of non-functioning water points, etc.
