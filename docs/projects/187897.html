Project Summary

RI will have a direct implementation and partnership approach to meet reproductive health and protection needs of targeted communities in NWS. Over a 12-month period, RI will look to support partners with RH kits provided by UNFPA to meet the immediate needs of beneficiaries in NWS.
Simultaneously, RI will increase access to SRH and protection services through the support of 2 health facilities and 1 Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) through adjacent/integrated protection spaces providing protection and risk mitigation activities. This will support both confidential and protective environments for referrals. Health and protection activities will be integrated through the health staff visiting the WGSS to deliver SRH awareness and the presence of a social assistant in HF to provide initial care and support to survivors and facilitate referrals to the WGSS.
This program will enhance the overall quality of health service delivery through robust monitoring and supervision to 2 Women and Children Hospitals, which are Nour Hospital in Idleb Governorate in Taftanaz sub-district, in addition to Abin Samaan hospital in Aleppo governorate AlAtareb subdistrict. The intervention aims to provide safe access to integrated quality RH and GBV prevention and response.

4 - Needs

Syria has now witnessed a decade of conflict that has trapped civilians in a pervasive protection crisis where people’s rights to safety, security, and well-being are regularly violated. Within the affected population of NWS, protection issues disproportionately affect groups such as women, children, especially adolescent boys and girls, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other marginalized or socially excluded people and groups (HNO 2021). They also face additional barriers to access the limited available services.RI is acutely aware of and recognizes the importance of rapid and adaptive service provision. Therefore, RI will work in NWS to focus on an integrated health and protection program to ensure the four key results of UNFPA’s focus are met, centralized around the themes of; increased immediate access to relevant services, ensuring protection needs are met with dignified means, and a protective environment is ensured through strong community engagement and the local capacities of organizations is elanced.It is clearly documented that disruptions in assistance, particularly specialized protection services, lead to enhanced distress and increased risks of GBV and other protection issues, undo the progress made and can reset or worsen the severity of the associated needs (HNO 2021). Overall, the project will aim to increase access for conflict-affected individuals to integrated protection and sexual and reproductive health services in NWS.

5 - Implementation of activities

RI will implement the project in Idleb Governorate, Taftanaz sub-district in Al Nour Hospital (G0700016!Nour Maternity & pediatric Hospital), with integrated protection links through a nearby WGSS, and Abin Samaan hospital (B0200213! Abin Samaan Medical Complex) located in AlAtareb sub-dictrict in Aleppo governorate, integrating GBV and PSS services through protection and GBV staff based in the hospital. Technical support sessions on RH from RI health team will be provided to HF’s SRH key staff in addition to the monitoring of supported HFs by RI field health officers using recommended monitoring tools and checklists. Technical trainings on SRH protocols training will be delivered while training on SRH and protection referral will be assessed. Integration between GBV, PSS, and health services through the inclusion of a social assistant in the health facility to strengthen referrals of GBV cases. Specialized maternal and sexual health services will be delivered in the HFs to include antenatal and postnatal care, family planning, vaginal deliveries, c- section, CMR (Clinical management of rape survivors), comprehensive abortion treatment and care, and syndromic management of sexually transmitted infections.RI will provide health facilities with SRH kits donated by UNFPA using a distribution plan prepared by SRH TWG.RI actively coordinates with health directorates, local authorities, WHO, UNFPA, NGO forum, access working group, health,nutrition,protection clusters.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

RI staff or partners perpetrate abuse against participants (in violation of PSEA and Child Protection policies) and/ or staff or programs expose participants to harm. Established feedback/complaints mechanism and following up on the feedback/complaints using feedback tracker. • HR orientation to all new staff and refresher orientation to existing staff on HR policies with mandatory signing on CoC and HR policies (this includes clear definition of PSEA and how to report it). • Regular staff orientation on Incident reporting mechanisms. • IMT follows on a timely approach all complaints according to severity rate. • Deploying a PSEA focal point to represent RI PSEA network, WoS Safeguarding Focal Point appointed to support strengthening safeguarding needs.• Regular communications on updated/revised PSEA policies/revised to the staff and trainings on updated policies are delivered in a regular basis. • Ensure that protection mainstreaming is a core component of RI programming, programs are based on key protection principles: accountability, meaningful access, and avoiding causing harm. • Develop awareness-raising materials for staff, contractors, and clients on PSEA topics.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The registration of INGOs remains unstable, therefore if RI’s registration status were to be revoked in Turkey, we would aim to maintain Syria operations through remote management from outside Turkey. Should the border access be blocked, RI would transition to increase procurement in Syria and improve control measures to assess commodities' quality, especially pharmaceuticals, procured inside Syria. If static HFs are rendered inoperable from airstrikes or any other security incident. RI will suspend, relocate the HFs as appropriate. In case of loss of access. RI will leverage existing community feedback mechanisms to best understand sensitivities in delivering SRH based activities and modify awareness-raising if necessary, to reduce any community backlash. Through RIs staffing model of using indirect staff from the community and community mobilizers who deliver outreach. RI will maintain a high level of transparency with all local communities and groups about humanitarian principles and work to avoid any challenges. To prevent fraud and corruption at the supported HFs, RI has developed a robust, context-specific fraud mitigation plan and continues to reassess and improve operations based on lessons learned. Actions will be taken to promote social distancing to prevent large gatherings of people. Each health facility will have triage space for early recognition of any suspected cases and keep the health facilities free of COVID-19.
