Project Summary

This project will deliver lifesaving emergency health services including maternal, neonatal and child health and contribute to disease prevention and strengthening national and regional emergency preparedness and response capacities in the three zones of Somalia for an estimated 968,388 out of 3 million crisis affected population in need of healthcare. Through deployment of trained health workers including midwives and community health workers in facilities and mobile teams, primary health care services including treatment of common childhood illnesses, as well as emergency obstetric and neonatal care for pregnant and lactating women will be provided. The proposed action complements UNICEF's core interventions in Somalia and the intervention focuses on an integrated, multi-sectoral response to drought, displacement and conflict, providing life-saving services. To maximize impact, this project will provide evidence-based curative, promotional and preventive services and will enable complementarity with nutrition including priorities such as deworming, breastfeeding, complementary feeding, vitamin deficiencies, & micronutrient supplementation. In collaboration with Health Cluster, UNICEF will support the appropriate management of children with SAM and cholera co-morbidity admitted in AWD/Cholera treatment centres. Scaling up the WASH component as part of an integrated and coordinated response, will focus on disease outbreaks, especially cholera. Thus, the integrated action focuses on increasing response in areas of highest need and lowest levels of predictable access in Somalia. UNICEF supported health facilities will be equipped with essential medicines and equipment and trained staff will provide quality services. Emphasis will be laid on Maternal and Child Health, including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care and Expanded Programme on Immunization to IDPs and most vulnerable communities and attention will be paid on application of treatment guidelines as per the Essential Package of Health Services and Primary Health Care. Support to outreach immunization for hard to-reach areas will be promoted to ensure equity and reaching a wider catchment. UNICEF will deliver comprehensive reproductive health care to women of reproductive age, men and adolescent boys among the target beneficiaries and promote health seeking behaviour including adoption of preventive practices through campaigns, health and hygiene education sessions for men and ado. UNICEF will also integrate surveillance and emergency preparedness ensuring adequate stocks for epidemic outbreaks of AWD/Cholera and measles are prepositioned. The project will also contribute to strengthening early warning disease surveillance through weekly submission of Communicable and Disease Surveillance reports from all the functional supported health facilities. Referral system for patients seeking secondary healthcare services will be strengthened. The project will also refer GBV victims to health facilities for comprehensive care and psychosocial support.

Mitigation Measures

Community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations. Lack of static facilities that can provide comprehensive package of health services in IDP settings due to poor shelter is a risk factor denying them timely response and needed referral services. Poor sanitation and hygiene within the IDP camps causes the easy spread of communicable diseases. To address these risks, UNICEF will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive package of health services within the IDP camps by providing health services within the IDP camps. Mobile teams will be supported to provide services to IDPs and referral mechanism to secondary healthcare outside IDP camps. In order to avoid community tensions, host communities will be provided with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. Both displaced and host communities will be consulted about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. In cooperation with protection actors, reporting mechanisms will be established to report security incidents concerning GBV cases and preventing of displaced populations from accessing services. IDP communities will be involved in identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, cross􀂡re or violence. To overcome these risks of exclusion based on gender and age, the implementation will be based on a conducted needs assessment that has desegregated data by sex and age. For example, 302,064 females, 243,600 males, and 194,880 children under-fives (107,184 female and 87,696 male) will be supported. Location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services will be accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. Aged and disabled groups will be supported by ensuring facilities they access have railings and handlebars. Where possible, transportation and enhanced referral will be provided so that they can access services. Health facilities will have lights outside and on the access route to the facility and health services will take into consideration cultural and religious practices and will consider separate waiting areas for males and females. Facilities will be encouraged to have proportionate number of female health staff to address cultural preferences and sensitivities. To ensure equity, mobile teams will be used to reach rural communities avoiding their exclusion. Health staff and health committees will be gender balanced and will have representation from all groups within the community. The protracted conflict in Somalia is characterized by its longevity, intractability and mutability. Lack of respect for IHL means lack of respect for human rights and causes immense human suffering. The conflict in Somalia causes widespread displacement,multiplying the cumulative impact on basic services and livelihoods over time, and the sharp increase in “war poor” populations. Destruction of facilities in conflict is real and this deprives communities’ critical services. UNICEF will mitigate the impact and theconsequences of armed conflict through the promotion of rights-based approach to service provision, providing access and services to all affected people without discrimination. Prepositioning essential supplies in strategic hubs for immediate response, providing outreach and mobile health services to displaced communities, providing referral services for critical cases. Rehabilitation of facilities will be given consideration so that damaged facilities can be operationalized to provide services. Strengthened monitoring and risk assessment will ensure data informed intervention and promotion of the rights of the most vulnerable groups.
