Project Summary

Bur Hakaba is the largest district amongst the five districts of the Bay region in the south-west, Somalia. It is located Baidoa to the west, Wanlawein to the east, Dinsor to the south-west, Jalalaqsi to the north-east and Qorioley to the south. The main livelihoods in the district are southern inland pastoral (camel, sheep, and goats) and the agro-pastoral (camel, cattle, sorghum).
Bur Hakaba is estimated to approximate a number of 160,000 people living in urban and rural villages specifically 26 main villages and 387 sub-villages with varied inhabitants of decedents mostly indigenous marginalized and vulnerable community strongly dependents on crop farming and livestock raising. Inhabitants are mainly sorghum producers.
The causes of Malnutrition in Burhakaba include inadequate knowledge of appropriate nutrition practices, particularly for infant and young child feeding, water shortage, unsafe hygiene and sanitation practices, inappropriate behavioral patterns, and limited access to health and nutrition services. The other factor such as low measurement of MUAC, food insecurity, poor community attitude and behavioral change towards the childcare clearly indicates towards Buurhakaba to implement IMAM program.
Following successive rain failures that cumulated over years, the Buurhakaba district of Bay region is experiencing high rates of malnutrition and food insecurity and remains severely critical.
According to the FSNU - The FSNAU forecasts worsening food security and nutrition situation in the second quarter of 2019 in Bakool, Bay, Middle, and Lower Shabelle. Poor nutrition may deteriorate further with delayed rain and the impending drought. The FSNAU projects the GAM in Burr-Hakaba district at 18.1% and SAM at 2.4%. The Nutrition Cluster estimates caseloads of about 1,500 SAM and 12,000 MAM annually in Burr-Hakaba district with an estimated population of about 160,000.
Populations in both rural and urban including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are currently food insecure. Food security and Nutrition data indicate a likelihood of further deterioration of nutrition situation among rural, urban and IDP populations of Burhakaba district.
Burhakaba is currently facing humanitarian challenges in Agro pastoral areas due to failed Deyr rain. The failed Deyr rain in the Somali region this year has seriously affected water, pasture in most villages. The fact that the Deyr rainy season is immediately followed by the long Jilaal/dry season further compounds the problem due to the likelihood that food shortages will be more severe and of longer duration and the large influx of Returnees people from Yemen, Saudi Arabia, and Mogadishu arrived in Burhakaba in the last 2 Years. Although the overall food security prospect in all Livelihood areas of the district is already poor, the situation will be further aggravated unless a timely emergency intervention is implemented. A timely intervention, in addition to directly reducing malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality, will minimize asset reduction and unsustainable household coping strategies that will have longer-term negative impacts on households.
The overall objective of this project is to contribute to a reduction of excess morbidity and mortality caused by malnutrition amongst children less than 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) over a period of 24 months in Burhakaba.
BTSC proposes to work in Burhakaba where it is currently implementing an emergency nutrition project with UNICEF funding 1 Fixed OTP, 1 SC in Burhakaba Town and 2 mobile Outreach services in Dhowey and Goof villages. 3 TSFP through OTP EAC (1 in Burhakaba town, and 2 mobile as per OTP)
The project will also provide micronutrient support by distributing multiple micronutrients (MNPs) and Vitamin A supplements through static and mobile clinics. Efforts will also be made to link households with children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) conditions.

BTSC will collect gender-disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to emergency health activities. BTSC will ensure that all segments of the affected population including women have equal access to emergency health assistance in the targeted support to advance gender equality is based on gender analysis. BTSC shall apply gender-segregated community consultation meetings enabling women and girls to address their specific concerns, prioritize their needs and appropriately inform the development of proposed activities, measures are also put in place to maintain gender balance in terms of staff recruitment. Equitable and gender-sensitive participation and decision-making approaches will be adopted throughout the project, including women’s participation in the community nutrition committees and access to gender-specific health and nutrition information. Community consultations through focus-group discussions will take place during the project implementation, and stakeholders will be involved in decision-making processes to tailor the project to the needs of different gender and age groups. Furthermore, data will be disaggregated by age and gender in all recording and reporting.
