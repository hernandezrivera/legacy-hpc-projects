Project Summary

Existing WASH infrastructure in Pariang, Mayendit and Manyo counties is unable to provide adequate WASH services (water, safe disposal of faeces and hygiene promotion) due to the population increase, risking increased outbreaks of water borne diseases including acute watery diarrhea, cholera and Covid-19. Inadequate WASH is a major cause of diseases in Pariang, Manyo and Mayendit counties and improving sanitation through hygiene promotion is known to have a significant beneficial impact on health both in households and overall community. In Pariang, Manyo and Mayendit counties there are a number of IDPs and host communities without good sanitation due to the fact that it was not there from the very beginning or it was destroyed by the many conflicts and flooding. There is need for maintenance of hygienic conditions and hygiene promotion, through improved availability of latrines, safe water source facilities, waste disposal, bathing shelters, to raise awareness on water and sanitation related health/hygiene issues, especially to raise awareness of water quality in order to successfully distribute Home base care kits, ( buckets, soap and water purifiers -water flocks sachets/Aquatabs) MHM kits to the community, and to reduce the risk of cholera outbreak, Covid-19 and other sanitation related diseases. The project aims to rehabilitate 80 non-functioning boreholes and 6 mini-water yard in several Payams of Pariang, Mayendit and Manyo Counties so that the IDPs, the returnees and un-displaced population can access water at an average of 750 people (women, men, boys and girls) per borehole which is still below the UNHCR and SPHERE standards quality requirement but is far better than the current average usage of 1,250 per borehole and use of alternate unsafe sources according REACH 2019 assessment report. Some of the population has been forced to move to IDP camps with very limited WASH infrastructure resulting to increased open defecation, use of unsafe water sources water pools, seasonal rivers and swamp waters. A total of 40,000 people (women, men, boys and girls) will benefit through provision of access to safe adequate water by rehabilitation and repair of boreholes and mini-water yards, and improve sanitation through construction of gender sensitive latrines and hygiene promotion awareness creation messaging. UNIDOR has been able to demonstrate that hygiene and sanitation awareness coupled with the installation of improved water facilities can trigger an improvement in attitudes towards hygiene and sanitation among the communities in Pariang, Mayendit, Koch, Leer and Panynjiar Counties in partnership with SSHF, UNICEF, IOM and Christian Aid. UNIDOR in its programing does conduct GBV safety audit before the construction and rehabilitation of water points, institutional latrines, creating awareness about hygiene and sanitation in advance of the implementation of distribution of WASH NFIs and construction of WASH infrastructure. This approach often guarantees a more enthusiastic participation of women, girls and boys, which leads to a higher level of acceptance and ownership by a larger group of community members, and supports greater sustainability of the program by reinforcing the link between water, sanitation and hygiene. This project aims at; to enable the community access safe and adequate drinking water, to promote construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities e.g. boreholes and mini-water yards, institutional and shared family latrines, dish racks, bathing shelter, domestic waste disposal pits etc., and to conduct hygiene promotion to influence behavior change from harmful practices that encourage spread of diseases including Covid-19, by involving women, men, boys and girls in the community through community hygiene promotion approaches, water and sanitation management committees, through involving all the stakeholders in the populations that includes people living with disabilities and the elderly.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to be affected by conflict and flood disasters in some areas and the threat of conflict in others. In Pariang, Manyo and Mayendit counties, Unity and Upper Nile State, this situation, coupled with high levels of poverty, high returns of refugees, destroyed infrastructure, food insecurity and flooding, has caused mass displacement of people and many people (women, men, boys, girls) are residing in IDP camps and others crossed borders as refugees to the neighboring countries of Sudan, Ethiopia, Uganda and Kenya. Whereas some are returning due to harsh living conditions in the camps. With the outbreak of COVID 19, these vulnerable IDPs and returnees continue to suffer more burden due to inadequate and disproportional WASH infrastructure considering the SPHERE, WHO and UNHCR guidelines. This humanitarian and political crisis has negatively affected the already poor, to access basic water, sanitation and hygiene services. According to HNO 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Almost 20 years of civil war has destroyed much of South Sudan’s water delivery infrastructure, leaving more than 30% of the population with no access to safe and adequate water and only 13% with adequate sanitation facilities. Households that are able to access a borehole or tap stand in under 30 minutes, without facing protection concerns, is very low, at 34 per cent coverage. This is a condition which affects the safety of women and girls disproportionately, as they are called upon uniquely to source water for the family. November 2020 – March 2021 IPC AMN analysis cites Upper Nile, Unity & Lakes state with counties at AMN 4 at 31% ,17% &3% respectively attributed to high prevalence of disease (36%) poor quality &diversity of food, poor access to health, nutrition services. October – November 2020 IPC analysis cites Mayendit and among the 34 counties classified at Emergency IPC 4 acute food insecurity. This lack of food, water, sanitation, and hygiene poses a serious threat to public health, particularly among children. Addressing up provision of safe adequate drinking Water, Hygiene and Sanitation needs of vulnerable, flooding and conflicts disaster affected population of Unity and Upper Nile State is paramount due to the fact that the population using water points are more than the standard required because the water points are few. The overuse contributes to breakdown of the pumps and reduction in water availability. Due to the fact that the current water supply situation is below standards in quality and quantity of 500 people (women, men, boys and girls) per borehole and approximately 1,250 people (women, men, boys and girls) served by one hand pump, resulting in a limited number of boreholes and water supply schemes which also results in overuse, which contributes to breakdown of the pumps and reduction in safe water availability. This leads not only to low water consumption (under 10 L/person/day) but also increases the burden of women and girls’ caretakers who solely walk for long distances fetching water exposing them to some forms of GBV namely abduction of young girls and boys, forceful marriages, killing and rape of young girls and women. According to HNO 2020/2021, 34% of surveyed households travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water indicating the increased GBV risk. Due to the fact that houses and sketchy sanitation infrastructures were destroyed within the communities by the many conflict and flooding, the current hygiene and sanitation structures merely exist exposing the residents to open defecation posing an extreme public health problem especially water borne diseases e.g., Acute watery diarrhea and cholera.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNIDOR staff implementing the project are trained on PSEA and child safeguarding policy. There are appointed focal persons situated in all field project sites to monitor the implementation and compliance of the policies. The focal points are introduced to local authorities and who in turn are made known to the community. All the employees of UNIDOR and any other person conducting any service for UNIDOR are taken through the Code of Conduct and they are to append their signatures in agreement. The inception meetings and Focus group discussions, stakeholders are well informed that the services provided are free and they fully deserve them, and any staff trying to exploit the beneficiaries should be reported to RRC or UNIDOR management. Selection criteria for MHM kits and Home Base Care kits is agreed upon through meetings and FGDs. This ensures that deserving beneficiaries receive the services and items.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNIDOR will conduct interviews, FGDs and separate discussions with gender and age disaggregated groups including random one-on-one interviews with selected beneficiary household head of families and persons living with disabilities, women and girls, are centered on four major areas, i.e., relevance and direct benefit, the overall result in the community e.g. is there reduction in sanitation related diseases, is there safe and adequate water, are people practicing hand washing etc., what of the quality of the items given( Home Base Care kits, MHM kits) and sustainability of the project even when UNIDOR exits.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This project will directly involve and fully engage the project direct beneficiaries in Pariang, Mayendit and Manyo Counties throughout the intervention cycle. Inception meetings and organized Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) will be utilized for effective and efficient social mobilization of girls, boys, men and women including the individuals living with disabilities and the elderly to participate fully in separate consultations so that their views and concerns are reflected and addressed. The consultations will be conducted based on their gender and age groups to allow for maximum participation. A female staff from UNIDOR and or a female staff from GBV partner on the ground will consult women and girls separately by their respective age group for confident building to express their views on the best location for locating of water or gender sensitive sanitation facilities before any construction or rehabilitation takes place to mitigate the protection concerns associated with WASH-GBV activities. Distribution of MHM kits to girls and women will as well be discussed in the same FGDs to ensure that the most vulnerable benefit due to limited resources, time and modalities that are secure and inclusive will be considered through consultations with them together with the female teachers of their schools or community members. The project staff will consult individuals living with disabilities together and separately during joint supervision/monitoring site visits and FGDs, based on gender dis-aggregated to express their expectations as well as their capacities to ensure their feedback is captured, documented/shared and addressed. Both men and women from beneficiary communities will be trained together in separate trainings as water management committees, hand pump mechanics, community hygiene promotion, and engaged in the various WASH activities like management of water sources, rehabilitation/repair works of communal water points or latrines, hygiene promotion and COVID-19 awareness creation campaigns, etc. There are many ways in place for ensuring feedback from the most affected vulnerable community members: WASH management committees (WMCs), Community Health/Hygiene Promoters (CHPs), Handpump Mechanics who are routinely engaged with affected community members and their leaders, will be oriented and given specific context information on how to identify and reach out to the most vulnerable household heads/members, including individuals living with disabilities as they will be the direct target of the project. The CHPs, who will carry out routine hygiene promotion awareness campaigns through households-to-households visits will play greater role in receiving feedback or complaint from the most vulnerable affected beneficiary community members. UNIDOR has designed monitoring and evaluation tools used by M&E and project staff for conducting interviews, and discussions with various affected beneficiaries who have been directly or indirectly involved in the project. The findings and lessons learned from these interviews, discussions and information from other sources will be documented in form of reports and human impact stories, to be used by UNIDOR, funding partners and other stakeholders including local authorities and other partners. These interviews, FGDs and separate discussions with gender and age disaggregated groups including random one-on-one interviews with selected beneficiary household leaders/members and persons living with disabilities, are centered on four major areas, for example: relevance, impact / outcome, value for money and sustainability aspects of the project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

UNIDOR will develop together with the community members selection criteria for direct beneficiaries for WASH NFIs, this will help prevent any external interference when distributing items since some local leaders have been known to select their relatives as priority beneficiaries. This will be conducted through Focus Group Discussions with different groups in the community – namely Women, adolescent girls, people living with disabilities, elderly, boys -. The quantities will be established and will vary as per the group of persons selected and registered. During distribution of WASH NFIs, the beneficiaries will not be subjected to long distances looking for services, the services will be taken to them even for far and hard to reach Payams. WASH facilities will ensure that siting and construction and rehabilitation follow strict protection measures after consulting the beneficiaries through Focus Group Discussions with the specific groups. Latrines and water points will be made safe through clearing of bushes around them and more importantly ensure reduction of time and distance of women and girls accessing water points. Timing for distribution of WASH NFIs – Home base care kits, MHM kits – will be done during morning hours so us to ensure beneficiaries reach home with their items during the day. UNIDOR will ensure Confidentiality by appointing focal points to address suspected and any GBV and SEA cases and keeping of confidential information. Regular trainings on PSEA and GBV to all staff will be conducted. Confidential and private meetings and meeting places will be established within the project locations to address GBV and SEA cases.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Safe and adequate drinking water, good Sanitation and good Hygiene is known to reduce malnutrition and poor growth in children by preventing diarrheal and worms’ diseases. According to WHO about 50% of malnutrition in children is caused by inadequate WASH infrastructure. When children are malnourished their resistance to diseases is weakened and they are susceptible and can easily die from diarrheal diseases and other sickness. This project envisages increasing safe water, improve on the environmental hygiene through construction of both institutional and family latrines and conduct hygiene promotion. Household to household hygiene visits will focus on the homestead environment with children and advise action will follow the observation while emphasizing hand washing as prevention measures to fighting diarrheal diseases. Mothers visiting OTP/TSFP centers will receive hygiene promotion messaging before receiving the food treatment. The mothers will also be supported with Home Base Care kits to ensure availability of safe and adequate drinking water.
