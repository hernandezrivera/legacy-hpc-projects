Project Summary

International Medical Corps (IMC UK) will provide integrated lifesaving health, nutrition, GBV, and FSL services to conflict-affected host communities and displaced populations in Canal/Pigi, Panyjar, Malakal, Fashoda, Manyo, and Panyikang Counties of Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile states. This project aims to prevent malnutrition through Maternal-Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) activities, focusing on the first 1,000 days of a child's life. The preventive MIYCN package will build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations. Key activities include kitchen gardening, cooking demonstrations, caregiver counseling for children aged 0-23 months, establishing support groups, providing VASD supplementation, and training frontline nutrition workers.
The project will also promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices. To address morbidity and mortality related to acute malnutrition among vulnerable children and women, IMC will enhance equitable access to quality treatment for acute malnutrition through CMAM services. The response will include managing wasting by treating severe acute malnutrition without medical complications at OTP sites, severe cases with medical complications at SC sites, and moderate acute malnutrition for children under five and PLW at TSFP sites. IMC UK proposes to deliver a comprehensive nutrition package through 60 OTP/TSFP static and outreach/mobile facilities and 3 SC sites across the project locations.
IMC UK will integrate nutrition services into the health system and simplify early detection, referral, and treatment of acute malnutrition. Families and communities will be empowered through initiatives like Family-MUAC, support groups for mothers and fathers, and CNVs/BHWs as key channels for delivering multi-sectoral services at the community, household, and facility levels. To strengthen coordination, knowledge transfer, and sustainability, IMC will collaborate with community structures, CHD, and local NGOs. These efforts will enhance localization and build the capacity of community and CHD staff at the county level, supporting the transition of nutrition services from implementing partners.
IMC UK will monitor the nutrition status of the population and the response through routine program data, quarterly MUAC screenings, and population-based SMART Surveys. With its Nutrition Surveillance Team, IMC will conduct assessments in its operational areas and priority counties, providing robust data to inform effective responses to nutrition needs.
IMC will adopt a people-centered approach that mainstreams Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). By strengthening community systems and collaborating with FSL, WASH, Health, Education, and Protection partners, IMC will ensure children and women access a safe, equitable, and comprehensive package of essential services through nutrition sites. As the leading nutrition and GBV partner in Malakal, Fashoda, Manyo, Panyikang, Panyjar, and Pigi/Canal Counties, IMC is uniquely positioned to integrate health, nutrition, and GBV interventions. This approach will reduce challenges related to coordination, monitoring, and evaluation.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. According to the 2024, integrated food security classification (IPC) projection, an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. According to the lean season (April - June 2025), seasonal deterioration in acute malnutrition is expected. Compared to the current period, the situation is projected to deteriorate in 62 out of 80 counties and remain similar in 16 counties. According to the 2024 FSNMS and SMART survey’s findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition remains very high in all IMC operational areas. The measles outbreak continues to affect children, and flooding, cholera outbreak, displacement, and morbidity-related issues continue to havoc the already dire situation. The influx of returnees due to the ongoing Sudan crisis reiterates the need to enhance and scale up the integrated services. As of October 873,000, people have crossed into South Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. Panyijar, Malakal, Fashoda, Manyo, Panyikang, and Canal/Pigi Counties continue to face numerous challenges impacting the malnutrition status of children. Findings of the SMART survey conducted by IMC in November 2023 in Panyjar, estimated GAM prevalence at 18.5 % (14.8 - 23.0 95% C.I.) % (15.7 - 25.6 95% C.I.). The increase in malnutrition is likely attributed the notably high childhood illnesses, sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices, low maternal healthcare practices, household food insecurity, and poor water, sanitation, and hygiene practices. The SMART survey conducted by IMC in Malakal in Oct 2023, revealed the GAM 20.2 % (16.4 - 24.7 95% C.I) and SAM as 5.2 % (3.2 - 8.5 95% C.I.). Similarly, the SMART survey conducted in Canal/Pigi in March 2023 by IMC revealed GAM prevalence as 17.8 % (14.0 - 22.3 95% C. I) and SAM prevalence 3.8 % (2.7 - 5.4). The crude death rate was below the emergency threshold at 0.60 (0.30-1.18) deaths per 10,000 persons per day while the under-five death rate was 0.55 (0.18-1.66) deaths per 10,000 under-fives per day. As per the 2023 FSNMS survey’s findings, GAM prevalence for Fashoda was estimated as 32.8% and SAM as 8%, Manyo 27.5%, SAM as 5% and Panyikang 13.4% and SAM as 2.7% which is very high above the emergency threshold of WHO.Analysis shows that the key drivers of malnutrition include high food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions, with over 50% of the population lacking access to safe water or sanitation; high child morbidity rates with an estimated 67 % suffering from diarrhea, malaria or upper respiratory infections; gender-based violence concerns; and poor child feeding and caring practices with only 5% children getting the minimum acceptable diet for them to grow and develop well. The persistent inter-communal conflicts, widespread flooding, and the current Sudan crisis has also exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition in the targeted counties, hence, immediate integrated response is needed to avert a possible further catastrophe.
