Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to continue providing services for children and adolescents through access to quality inclusive and age appropriate learning and development opportunities in a safe and protective environment inside the camps and host communities under Ukhia and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh through maintaining and upgrading Learning Centers (LCs), establishing new Community Based Learning Facilities (CBLF),Peer Education Program (PEP), Distance Education (DE) and Cross Sectoral Facilities (known as Girl Friendly Spaces) for early learning and basic education for children and adolescents, enhancing knowledge of parents and caregivers on how to support their children’s learning, development, and social well-being, and improving the quality of teaching through continuous teachers professional development supports. Inclusive aspects of programming including gender-responsive, child-sensitive, psychosocial support, disability-friendly as well as integration between sectors including child protection and health will be mainstreamed in all activities to ensure that different needs of girls and boys will be responded to, and they will equitably benefit from the interventions. Special attention will be paid in capacity building of staff on key teaching-learning and Social Emotional Learning (SEL) issues. All the learning facilities will be equipped with gender segregated WASH points while physical infrastructure, materials and facilities will be strengthened to ensure access and equity for children living with disabilities including Menstrual Health Management (MHM) services. Being a sector co-lead, SC will continue to play a key role in advocacy, increased quality and sustainability of education services for the displaced Rohingya and adjacent host community. To combat sudden COVID 19 resurgence, SC plans to continue the caregiver-led education modalities as well as exploring alternative pathways to accelerated and remedial learning and advocate for continuation of education as an essential service and the permission of technology based interventions in the camps so that the technology/remote learning activities can be ensured. Through consuting with District Primary Education Office and Upazila Office, SC will capacitate the lower performing government primary schools to ensure quality education within the learners.

1 - Identified Needs

Delivering quality education for Rohingya children is hugely challenging due to a number of constraints of technical, contextual, COVID-19 and political nature. The lack of space in the camps prevents the establishment of more inclusive learning facilities to provide education services to those who still have no access to them. 40% of households reported that children aged 3-24 not having been enrolled in learning facilities pre-COVID period whereas 44% reported children not having regularly accessed home-based learning. Dropout rate is supposedly to be increased as 35% of Rohingya household and 25% of Bangladeshi household reported that children aged 3-24 and 6-18 will not be sent back to learning facilities once they will re-open (JSMNA 2021). Lack of learning materials and guidance from teachers are key challenges in learning continuity. Findings of Save the Children’s Needs Assessment (January 2021) highlighted the need for alternative modes for Rohingya children to be able to continue learning during COVID-19 school closures. There is need to maintain current learning facilities and ensure current programming continues and expands with the scale up of Myanmar Curriculum. Geographically, camps are at risk of natural disasters like heavy rainfall, floods, landslides and cyclones. A set of community-based DRR measures and awareness with children and communities is required to integrate into EiE to reduce their vulnerability to disasters. There should be a contingency plan for repairing and maintaining of the learning facilities/structures every year. Adolescent girls are much more disadvantaged in their access to education due to social norms, which restricts their mobility after puberty, safety concerns and widespread occurrence of child marriage and teenage pregnancies. In Save the Children’s FGDs (2021) parents report that they are sometimes are not interested in sending their girls to schools due to the risk that girls are getting involved in relationships with boys at schools. There is a need to address social norms and work in partnership with the community to advocate for girls’ education. There is a need to assess the ways in which learning materials and approaches can be more inclusive and suited to the needs of the learners in order to strengthening learning outcomes. At present, it is difficult for children with disabilities to access learning centers. No special arrangement or support is provided to children with disabilities (CwD). In addition to the physical inaccessibility of the learning centers, CwDs also do not receive the appropriate equipment and materials for learning. The recruitment of qualified Rohingya learning facilitators remains a challenge. Cox’s Bazar is one of the worst performing districts in Bangladesh, and host communities from Ukhia and Teknaf are ever lower performing among the sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar (DDP 2020). Nationalization of registered schools by the government requires support to ensure quality education for children (UNDP 2019). There is scarcity of teachers in government primary schools, hence supporting through para teachers, capacity building and learning materials to the lower performing schools is needed to strengthen the delivery of quality learning within the host communities. A study (2021) by Cox's Bazar CSO-NGO Forum (CCNF), most of the funding (69%) goes to UN agencies, followed by INGOs (20%) and the Red Cross (7%) and national organizations receive only four percent. When international bodies partner up with local organizations, it is not really an equal partnership but contractual. They are not involved in the decision-making and their work largely remain unacknowledged in aid reports. There is a need to support local partners for creating sustained local capacity (technical and operational) for humanitarian work in the future.
