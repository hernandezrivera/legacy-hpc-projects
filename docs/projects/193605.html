Project Summary

In 2023, UNRWA will seek to provide unconditional and non-restricted cash assistance to 3,198 abject poor Palestine refugee households (approx. 20,509 individuals) in the West Bank through the e-card modality, to support them with covering their basic food needs and restoring their coping capacities. This project contributes to:
• Strategic Objective 3 “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”, as well as to
• Food Security Cluster Objective “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”.
Distribution will be complemented by consultations with beneficiaries and awareness raising on PSEA and complaint mechanisms.

Implementing Partners

The United Nations Relief and Work Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) has one of the most significant humanitarian operational presences in the West Bank (WB), incl. East Jerusalem (EJ) both geographically and in terms of sectors of intervention. Next to the Palestinian Authority, UNRWA is the biggest service provider in the West Bank. In the West Bank, the Agency serves 1,111,064 Palestine refugees and other registered persons, of whom 891,384 are registered refugees (UNRWA, Registered Population Dashboard, June 2022). This amounts to one-third of the population in the West Bank, of whom more than half a million are women and girls. UNRWA was established by the General Assembly in 1949 and is mandated to assist and protect 5.7 million registered Palestine refugees. As the main service provider for Palestine refugees in the region, UNRWA delivers primary education, health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement, microfinance, and emergency assistance. The Agency has extensive experience providing emergency assistance to Palestine refugees during humanitarian crises to save lives, preserve human dignity, and prevent and respond to the impacts of such crises. In the West Bank, the Agency's programmes seek to reduce the impact of the protection crisis stemming from over 50 years of the Israeli occupation by addressing the critical needs of the most affected individuals. In terms of response, UNRWA provides critical services to those exposed to protection threats, including through psycho-social counselling, medical assistance, cash-based interventions, and the provision of dedicated programming to specific vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

In the WB, incl. EJ, protracted occupation, economic stagnation and high poverty rates, restricted access to resources, continue to pose serious challenges to food security and nutrition. According to the World Bank (Sep 2022), in 2022, the Palestinian economy continued its rebound from the COVID-19 shocks. However, the pandemic has not only reinforced previous vulnerabilities but also led to the emergence of a substantial number of newly poor predominantly located in the West Bank (World Bank, May 2022): before the pandemic 60.7% of the population lived in the WB, but only 28.7% of the poor lived there, while after the pandemic the percentage has increased to 64.8%. Female-headed households (HH) make up around 10.6% of the newly poor, and HH residing in refugee camps about 10.5%. The negative implications of the war in Ukraine persists as downside risks. From Jan to Apr 2022, the food component of the Palestinian Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose to its highest point in at least the past six years. HH could face average total increases of up to 80 percent in the cost of bread, flour, and vegetable oil between Jan 2022 and Jan 2023 (World Bank, Sep 2022). These price shocks will further erode the purchasing power of HHs, particularly the most vulnerable including refugee, female and PwD-headed HHs, and disrupt their access to food and other essentials. According to the 2020 SEFSec, food insecurity for refugees in the West Bank remains high at 13.60%, and rises to 16.4% for refugees inside camps, compared to non-refugees which has dropped to 7.4% in 2020. 38.3% of refugee headed HHs were food secure, compared to 54.8% non-refugee headed. The deterioration of the security situation in the WB, incl. EJ, contributes to further compound existing vulnerabilities, particularly of refugee HHs living in locations where there is more significant and frequent escalation of tensions. Access and movement restrictions put in place, including halting issuance of work permits to residents of specific areas, have a significant impact on the socio-economic conditions of many Palestinians. As per the 2022 MSNA, 12.4% of refugees inside camps reported restrictions physically accessing work due to checkpoints, permits, compared to 8 per cent non-refugees. 26% of registered refugee HHs reported that a member of their HH is unemployed and looking for work at the time of data collection, with 30% being refugee HHs living inside camps. Also, 25% of registered refugee HHs reported difficulties meeting essential food needs, with 31% being refugee HHs living inside camps, compared to 19% non-refugee HHs. 35% of refugee HHs have also reported to employ crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies, compared to 27% non-refugee HHs. Women and girls in particular are turning to negative coping mechanism, due to pre-existing vulnerabilities, and in response to GBV prevalence and severity. Females are also considered vulnerable as a result to several factors including availability of job opportunity and cultural norms inhibiting participation on the labor market: the labor force participation rate of men reached 71% in Q1 2022, while participation rate among female was 19% (Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, 2022). According to the 2022 MSNA, more than 11% of refugee HHs reported at least one individual with a disability. HHs that include persons with disabilities are more likely to experience food insecurity due to fewer work opportunities, higher need for health services, and extra time required for care work. When the person with disabilities is the primary income earner of the family, the chances of falling into food insecurity are even higher. In 2021, UNRWA Emergency Cash Assistance caseload, activated following the discontinuation of most of food security interventions as a result of 2018 critical funding shortfall, was re-assed by UNRWA, using the social study form to re-identify their eligibility. The poverty level of the HHs was then determined using a proxy means testing formula, harmonized with the MoSD. In addition to consumption levels, this formula takes into consideration other aspects of vulnerability, such as the presence of chronic diseases, housing characteristics and accessibility to utilities, as well as HH composition, including number of elderly and persons with disabilities, and female-headed HHs. The recent assessment has showed that the 3,198 families targeted in the framework of the project are still living in abject poverty and in need for assistance. Therefore, UNRWA plans to continue this important programme in 2023.The vulnerability of the target population to protection risks following rights violations and the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees requires UNRWA to ensure that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries, are accessible and known to the target population, including for more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Consultations undertaken by the PSEA Network for the oPt confirm the need for all organizations to strengthen SEA policies and processes. The MSNA 2022 data found that 8.5% of refugee HHs reporting awareness of how to access and use complaint or reporting mechanisms (compared to only 1.7% of non-refugee HH). While this is a significant improvement and an indication that UNRWA’s efforts to raise awareness on how to access and use complaint or reporting mechanisms are producing results, there is still a need to strengthen these mechanisms. Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single headed households, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers and elderly women.

2 - Activities

In 2023, UNRWA will seek to provide unconditional and non-restricted cash assistance to 3,198 abject poor Palestine refugee households (20.509 individuals) in the WB through e-card modality. • Beneficiary households will receive one bank debit card linked to an account with the Bank of Palestine (BoP). • On a quarterly basis, the account of each household is credited with NIS 125 (approx. US$ 39) per person registered in the household, based on a list provided by UNRWA detailing the name of the family, the amount to be transferred and the geographical location. • Beneficiary households are notified via text message when the fund is deposited and can access the funds via ATMs, points of sale machines, or directly through a bank teller in any of the Bank’s branches across the WB. The latest post-distribution monitoring indicates that all of 16% of the beneficiaries that could not easily access ATMs were able to delegate someone to receive the credit on their behalf. • BoP monitors the process and reports back to UNRWA after each quarterly transfer, as well as raises to UNRWA’ attention any technical issues to be solved on a weekly basis. • Throughout the implementation period, UNRWA social workers will continuously maintain contact with beneficiaries, including through regular field visits, including related to the regular work of UNRWA. Targeted population can also engage with SWs through these channels and provide feedback that than feeds into the project implementation cycle. • Beneficiaries will also be able to provide their feedback and get help addressing any challenges they face through the troubleshooting system and hotline established for them, to address issues and complications faced while collecting the entitlements, or to share any feedback related to the assistance received. • A post-distribution monitoring tool will be used twice during the implementation period to monitor the implementation and targets set through for assistance provided through the E-card modality. The tool makes sure to be used on samples representing vulnerable groups, including women, persons with disabilities, and the elderly. The tool monitorsThe PDM also gives the opportunity to test target population’s preferences on type of assistance and implementation modality, as obtain inputs on how service delivery can be enhanced. During the previous monitoring conducted in Q4 2021, most respondents stated that obtaining cash through this modality has been easy while more than 98% agreed that the E-card gives them flexibility about how and when to use the assistance. The monitoring tools also showed that, generally, female members of the household are the main decision-makers in terms of deciding how to use the assistance. • To close the information gaps on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns UNRWA plans to conduct surveys, interviews and focus group across the West Bank in 2023 to inform and strengthen UNRWA SEA channels and processes based on the consultation and participation of affected groups. To ensure the execution of PSEA, WB Field Office has developed an internal system to receive and respond to SEA complaints in accordance with the oPt PSEA SOPs on Inter Agency Referrals, UNRWA guidance and internal SOPs on Case Management for the WBFO that support frontline staff to safely identify and refer protection cases within the agency. Through 2022, all the UNRWA relief workers and management team working on emergency cash assistance received a PSEA-dedicated training. Therefore, all UNRWA frontline staff who interact with beneficiaries from vulnerable groups, including social workers, are considered priorities for this training initiative. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA WBFO works closely with the PSEA Network and will raise issues requiring technical support and/or external referral through this forum. Within this project, UNRWA will conduct a minimum of three focus group discussions (one in each area) to discuss the establishment of community-based complaint mechanisms (CBCMs), awareness activities and community mobilization interventions on PSEA including how to report SEA-related complaints. Randomly chosen beneficiaries of different gender, age, and vulnerability type will be selected to gather voices of all target beneficiaries, and the result of this discussions will be reflected to improve the existing complaint mechanism. This project will disseminate PSEA awareness raising materials including information on rights and safe complaint channels at all 19 camps and with all 31 social workers. Age and gender-sensitive materials have been developed by UNRWA targeting both staff and beneficiaries and will be disseminated on general rights of the beneficiaries, available complaint channels (including specific consultations on SEA complaints) and how to access survivor-centered assistance. UNRWA is currently conducting an evaluation of its emergency assistance support to Palestine Refugees between 2016 and 2021. In addition to focus groups and interviews with staff and beneficiaries the evaluation team will seek beneficiary feedback through a survey conducted in-person with 340 households in the West Bank. Data collectors will also use this opportunity to share material informing beneficiaries how to raise any concern or provide feedback about the survey and provide details about the confidential mechanisms available for individuals wishing to complain or report suspected wrongdoing.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The E-card modality, which provides cash to vulnerable refugees through bank debit cards contributes to empower beneficiaries, providing more freedom of choice and enabling greater flexibility in household spending. The E-card modality does not require in-person transfer of aid and minimizes the risk of humanitarian workers’ abuse of power over beneficiaries.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action and unintended negative consequences are minimized. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications, and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases are identified and referred to specialized support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultation hotline is available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritization of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities, or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. Ona biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection alignment review, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, Including the principle of Do No Harm. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically, the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. For the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritizes the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, eld visits, communications, and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA utilizes proxy means testing formula to determine the poverty level of the household, which considers not only the consumption levels but also other aspects of vulnerability, such as the presence of chronic diseases, housing characteristics and accessibility to utilities, as well as household composition (e.g. number of elderly and persons with disabilities, and female-headed households). UNRWA ensures that vulnerable individuals can access assistance by external referrals. UNRWA has developed dedicated case management SOPs and internal/external referral pathways, to ensure that the comprehensive needs are met. In various areas of operations, UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in target communities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project targets all abject poor refugee households in the West Bank without prejudice and includes all genders, all age groups, female-headed households, and persons with disabilities. In case a beneficiary household faces difficulty in accessing ATMs, they are able to delegate someone to receive the cash assistance on their behalf, securing their access to aid. The latest post-distribution monitoring indicates that all of 16% of the beneficiaries who could not easily access ATMs were able to delegate someone to receive the credit on their behalf.
