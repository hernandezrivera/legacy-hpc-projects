Project Summary

The year 2021 was marked by numerous protection incidents
affecting children in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga in the Far North. In
addition, significant flooding, as well as measures to mitigate the risks of the spread of
the Covid-19 pandemic, have exacerbated pre-existing child protection problems such as
GBV. Significant population movements continue to be recorded adding to the pre-existing
difficulties of access to basic social services for children as well as the lack of
opportunities for adolescents and youth to develop their potential in a region where 64%
of the current displaced population are children.
This project will support formal and community-based CP systems, placing particular
emphasis on (i) capacity building of adolescent girls and boys as agents of change for
conflict and violence prevention, peacebuilding, and the promotion of intergenerational
and interreligious dialogue, (ii) capacity building of deconcentrated CP structures in a
humanitarian/development Nexus approach focusing on case management, referral,
psychosocial support, and family/community reintegration of vulnerable children girls
and boys and (iii) increased coordination at all levels, under the leadership of the Regional
Delegation of Social Affairs (RDSA).
The key result of the project which is in line with the objectives of the protection sector, is
that conflict-affected children and caregivers are protected from violence, abuse
and exploitation in armed conflict settings and live in a protective environment in the
targeted localities of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Kanyi and Mayo Danay Divisions in the Far-North Region of Cameroon.
The project builds on previously established dynamics and community structures and will
provide PSS to children and caregivers. PSS will be provided both through a oneon-one approach (through social workers or other trained child protection actors) and
through community-based approaches, such as CFSs, adolescent clubs, and
intergenerational dialogue.
Implementing partners will continue to identify unaccompanied and separated children
(UASC). Based on previous trends and on partners capacities, the project plans to assist
UASC, ensuring and providing those children
with psychosocial support, alternative care arrangements, reunification with their families
of origin and reinsertion in their communities. All will benefit from close individual follow up.
To address both social norms but also to ensure smooth community reinsertion, the
project will work at strengthening community mobilization and will organize community
activities around prevention of GBViE including activities to abandon child marriage, to
promote intergenerational dialogue and social cohesion. Based on the projections, a total
of 70,000 persons (women, men, girls, boys) are planned to be reached through
community mobilization activities. Partnership with religious and traditional leaders, and
chiefery including female stakeholders will be an important channel to display messages
and address complex issues. In complement, the work on youth empowerment and
participation will enable to merge with the other members of the communities and
promote prevention and protection of children from violence and exploitation.
The issue of conflict-affected children without a birth certificates was confirmed to still be
a major issue.
The project aims at supporting conflict-affected children in obtaining a birth
certificate.
60 former CAAFAG are expected to be identified, will receive PSS, and will be supported
UNICEF will continue to closely work with civil society implementing partners, providing
them with capacity building, supporting the setting up of SOPs for case management and
referral. UNICEF will also strengthen the coordination mechanism led by RDSA.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The year 2021 was marked by a number of protection incidentsaffecting children in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga in the Far North. Inaddition, significant flooding, as well as measures to mitigate the risks of the spread ofthe Covid-19 pandemic, have exacerbated pre-existing CP problems such as GBV.Significant population movements continue to be recorded adding to the pre-existingdifficulties of access to basic social services for children as well as the lack ofopportunities for adolescents and youth to develop their potential in a region where 64%of the current displaced population are children. Due to insecurity and populationmovements, many children have been forced to flee their villages, sometimes beingwitnesses or victims of violence; involving high level of stress and trauma and significantnegative consequences for their cognitive and emotional development. Family separations due to sudden population displacements continue to occur. Appropriate alternative care is not always available or only to alimited extent. Abductions as well as the use and recruitment of children by NSAGs are amajor problem faced by children and young people in the Far North. Former CAAFAG face real difficulties inreintegrating into their communities of origin despite intergenerational dialogue initiatives.Most young girls who managed to escape from NSAGs often come out pregnant, or evenaccompanied by children, as a result of SV they suffered. In addition, the absence of aformal handover protocol makes it complicated to ensure that some of the keyinternational child rights standards be respected, most importantly the rapid transfer ofCAAFAG to civilian authorities. Adolescent girls aged 10-17 are victims of sexualharassment and violence, as well as kidnapping and child marriage. They are aparticularly vulnerable group because of the combined effect of their age and of gender based discrimination. These various issues have been aggravated by COVID-19prevention measures such as lock down and school closure, leading young girls to beconfronted with less protective living environment. Main GBV incidents faced by girls andadolescent girls are 40% of rape cases, 21% of child marriage et 12% of physical assaults.Incidents affecting boys and adolescent boys include mainly psychological violence(43%), followed by rape, sexual assaults, physical abuse and denial of resources.Although there is a critical lack of data on children with disabilities, this category is atparticular risk of violence, abuse and exploitation in the absence of adequate socialservices and community support. Children who do not have a birth certificate, includingthose who have lost them during their flights, are confronted with major difficulties inaccessing education and sometimes even social services. Even though this problemaffects girls and boys alike, girls suffer even more negatively because of social normsthat are unfavorable to them. The absence of a birth certificate for a girl is critical asknowing the girl's age allows better protection and access to appropriate and adequatepsychosocial and medical services. It exposes them to the practice of child marriage,which is an endemic practice in the region. Child protection actors are also concernedabout the impact of improvised explosive devices on children.
