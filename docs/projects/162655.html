Project Summary

Shelter surveys and needs assessments will be conducted to provide better analysis of the
needs of individuals and households. Transitional shelters will be provided to the most vulnerable communities, IDPs and Returnees. IOM will expand the provision of shelter assistance and skills building training packages including training on durable and local construction techniques and environmental awareness messaging to reduce disaster risks. The provision of environmentally friendly locally produced shelters will provide beneﬁciaries with protection from the elements such as floods, windstorms etc. By enhancing the skills of the community and enabling target beneﬁciaries trained in construction techniques to assist others in constructing shelters the selected beneficiaries could possibly generate a household income. The training sessions will be conducted with the support from local authorities targeting beneﬁciaries, communities and their leaders.

1 - Needs

According to the International Organization for Migration's Displacement Tracking Matrix data, since 2015, approximately 552,152 people have voluntarily returned to different states of Darfur and South Kordofan. In 2019, 80,596 returnees were reported in areas of East Darfur, West Darfur, South Darfur and North Darfur. There are around 300,000 Sudanese refugees living in eleven refugee camps in eastern Chad for the past 15 years. Joint Assessment missions conducted by the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) and IOM to the locations estimated that approximately 164,000 of these individuals have returned. Among these, 1,191 individuals are veriﬁed with refugee documentation from Chad, though not yet de-registered in Chad. In 2020, the UN jointly with the Government of Sudan plans to verify and register the returnees across areas in Darfur. The challenging environment, including marginalization and harassment of IDPs, and Sudanese refugees in the neighboring countries (mainly in Chad) without the prospect of durable solutions presents an obstacle to stability, recovery and reconstruction. Therefore, the population experiencing protracted displacement in Sudan continued to require an early recovery response to make them more self-reliant and resilient. The recent cessation of hostilities and the following declaration by Revolutionary Front combined with the arms collection campaigns and arms carrying ban across Darfur have created an atmosphere of hope for displaced population that has create an opportunity for large-scale returns from camps to areas of origin/choice. The vulnerable returnee population will need the support of the humanitarian community to obtain basic services and durable solution including building transitional shelters to sustain their return.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Output 1: Surveys and needs assessments provide better analysis of the shelter needs of individuals. Output 2: Target beneficiaries have the skills and knowledge to construct transitional shelters and mitigate disaster risks. Output 3: Transitional Shelters are available to the most vulnerable communities Activities:1. Conduct needs assessments to identify and prioritize the most vulnerable protracted IDP and returnees within each locality. During the assessment and project design process, consultation with the community including gender sensitive groupings, will be conducted to facilitate input from underrepresented groups where they may otherwise be reluctant to speak. It is expected that the shelters will benefit approximately 10,000 individuals.2. Conduct shelter survey to identify shelter designs / modules that will be both culturally acceptable and climatically appropriate for each locality with the intention of constructing approximately 2,000 shelters.3. Conduct risk assessments to ensure that transitional shelters are built on risk free zones. 4. Coordinate with local authorities, community leaders and communities to formulate land review committees. Ensure that committees will be composed of at least 55% female and 45% are male participants from IDP, returnee and host communities. 5. Procure and purchase locally sourced and environmentally friendly materials for the construction of transitional shelters. 6. Conduct shelter construction training sessions targeting protracted IDP and returnee communities, community-based organizations (CBOs) and local partners. Training will include durable construction techniques to ensure that structures will be capable of withstanding disaster related risks such as heavy rains, strong winds, dust storms (haboobs) etc. The safe construction training includes compressed stabilized soil blocks (SSB) technique, SSB machine usage, roof ﬁxing through the production of fortiﬁed grass mats, proper drainage system around structures, as well as ensuring proper spaces between shelters.

5 - Environment Marker

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

IOM will integrate environmental considerations into all programmes, and it will follow applicable international requirements. IOM will procure and purchase locally sourced and environmentally friendly materials such as SSB and grass mats for the construction of transitional shelters to ensure that environmental impact is limited. The shelter construction will include proper drainage system around structures to prevent flood related risks, and ensure safe distance between shelter structures to prevent fire related risks and damage to the surrounding environment.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM will ensure to prioritize (1) Safety and security issues ensuring that shelters are built in areas where there is access to other basic services and possible land tenure / ownership issues are discussed between local authorities and among the community members before shelters are constructed. (2) Ensure that all community members including boys, girls, men and women and persons with special needs are assessed and those who are found to be most vulnerable are prioritized to receive transitional shelters based.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in homes) through aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IOM will consult with local authorities and community members on the design and locations for transitional / permanent shelters. People with disabilities and special needs will receive additional support for construction of their shelters and have priority. IOM will ensure that there is a community-based feedback mechanism to take into account community complaints and suggestions and ensure that community members participate in the training and have ownership throughout the process. In addition, IOM has developed a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) format that will track the implementation of project activities and solicit feedback from ES/NFI beneficiaries face to face through visiting random households. PDM activities will involve on interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on ES/NFIs quality, appropriateness, and utilization. The system has also been developed to assess the short-term effects of ES/NFIs in improving living conditions of IDPs/returnees. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in Khartoum ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings To achieve this objective, the PDM plan has been developed and outlines data collection methods (Household interviews, Focus Group Discussion and observation), data sources, time frame, responsibilities, and dissemination procedure. Methodologies used will include a desk review prior to the field exercise being conducting.
