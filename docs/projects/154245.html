Project Summary

Since Al Qayyarah city was recaptured from ISIL in August of 2016, displaced families have begun to return to their areas of origin to rebuild their livelihoods and communities (933546 returnees in Ninewa Governorate as of Sep 30th, Return Overview, RWG Iraq). Nevertheless, the humanitarian situation is particularly alarming out of camps where social services are inadequate, few actors are intervening while local population presents high level of physical, psychosocial and social vulnerability. The project aims to meet the educational and protection needs of 15000 children, in the district of Mosul, sub district of Qayyarah across 12 months. At the core of this intervention, an integrated approach between Education and Child Protection sector. Access to education for children (3-17 years old) in 25 selected schools , school rehabilitation (10 schools), recreational activities, psychosocial counselling in safe Child Centered Spaces, provision of school kits, referral and liaison with specialized services (where required), awareness campaigns on explosive hazards with students, teachers and parents. In parallel, the intervention aims at activating social networks, Parents Teacher Associations (PTA) /School Management Committees (SMC), mobilizing families and community members around protection mechanisms, including prevention measures and monitoring systems, through Community Protection Workers (CPW) that will be trained and regularly assisted by COOPI project team.
Regarding the Education sector, COOPI will work on 10 of the 27 reopened schools in North Sinjar, focusing on the main problem assessed which is the lack of teachers. Salaries will be provided to teachers (at the moment most of them are volunteers) from the targeted schools for 1 year, as well as trainings on informal education , PSS/DRR activities and pedagogy. Moreover COOPI light rehabilitate schools buildings and their wash facilities and provide learning and recreational materials to the schools and to the students (textbooks, boards, stationery, sport equipments..). Finally, to tackle the problem of the distance of the schools, transportation solutions for students will be implemented. Awareness campaign will be organised to promote education and prevent child labour, soldiering and early marriage.

Needs

Violence and consequent displacement had overstretch the already existing limited school facilities,combined with the destruction that occurred during the conflict, will not be sufficient to support the newreturnees. Following the "JOINT EDUCATION NEEDS ASSESSMENT IN QAYYARAH UNICEF EDU-DOE Nenewa DoE Qayyarah –WEO 2017", in August 2018 COOPI reassessed 114 schools : 104 fully functioning; 1 partially functioning and 9 not functioning (used by armed actors; damaged and with explosives in and around the school)- 91 % of the schools in Qayyarah are currently open and operational- 31 schools were used by ISIL (military position ; storage of weapons; teaching and training of children)- 92 % of the schools were closed when the area was under the control of ISIL.- 28 schools with reported explosive hazards in vicinity of schools- 76% of the schools are overcrowded Total number of the students currently in 114 schools in Qayyarah is: 43,750 children (22,340 Boys; 21410 Girls). The total number of teachers is 570 but additional 305 teachers would be required for a ratio 1 teachers to 50 students.Major needs identified: 1. School supplies (91%); 2. School rehabilitation/WASH facilities(85%); 3. School extension/additional classrooms(59%); 4. Recruitment of new teachers (53%)5. Training of teachers(33%)As a result of the above mentioned violence destruction of buildings and infrastructures and limited availability of school supplies are likely to prevent children and youth to access education inthe immediate period as well as for several years, which bears risks of interrupting progress in education and potentially lead entire generations to miss out on an education and future perspectives.A CP assessment conducted by COOPI among 400 children in 12 selected schools and 300 households in the area in August 2018, shows that children are exposed to heightened risk of protection concerns as physical abuse, exploitation, including labor .Hawking and begging was identified as a protection risks for children by 80 percent of the households. Respondents were asked how many children in their household were involved in hawking or begging. Nearly 32 percent of the children within the surveyed households were reported engaged in hawking or begging.Children have been subjected to grave violations with resulting psycho-social distress (60%), exposure to sexual and gender-based violence (10%), separation from or loss of family members (34%), destruction of property (58%), looting of food and livestock and forced displacement (75%). In marginalized returnees communities, such as those targeted by the proposed intervention, lack of basic education structures and safe spaces to play and socialized contribute to expose children to protection risks, undermining also their social and emotional well-being and their ability to develop positive coping strategies. The 2104 ISIS attacks on the Yazidi population in Sinjar (Nineveh province) forced the displacement of approximately 350,000 people. Initially tens of thousands of Yazidis fled to the top of the Sinjar Mountain, where some decided to stay in informal refugee camps.The North and the top of the Sinjar mountain were liberated in December 2014, only a few months after ISIS took over, and have since then been under the Kurdistan administration, until 16 October 2017, when Iraq authorities took over. While security was guaranteed, most of basic services, due to a lackof infrastructure, staff and planning. The area south of the mountain was liberated only in June 2017, after three years of ISIS rule. Security issues made this area inaccessible to the population until September 2017 but the devastation of the south region has caused a second displacement of IDPS, in informal settings, especially in the north area of Mount Sinjar (Sinuni Sub-district) in the collectivities places or in rural villages creating social tension. As reported in the 2018 report by Nadia’s Initiative and Yazda, “In the aftermath of the genocide”, conflict has taken a heavy toll on education and livelihood in Sinjar. Many children remain out of school, teachers are in short supply, and buildings still show signs of conflict. Rehabilitating the educational network has been challenging but continues to be important for healing children and promoting future stability. With most of the teaching force displaced to Kurdistan, the pool of qualified teachers in Sinjar is weak. Volunteer teachers are taking their place but require trainings and certifications to ensure quality learning. Additionally, the turnover of volunteer teachers remains high without guarantees of salaries. Moreover, according to data for this report, 67% of those surveyed stated that a school was not available within a safe walking distance of their homes.

Activities

CP Activities: - Support children at risks and survivors through case management service- Strengthen community based structures to support prevention of and responses to child protection issues- Build capacity of girls, boys, women and men members of Community Based structures related to CP- Provide sustained, structured psychosocial support (PSS) or resilience activities to children- Provide skills and knowledge for parents and caregivers to better care and protect their children- Conduct awareness raising activities on child protection issues, focusing on prevention and behavior changes- teachers and social workers under MoE(CP Integration in Education) Education Activities:– Installation and/or rehabilitation of accessible classrooms in schools - Provision of furniture, adequate teaching and learning materials for children and teachers – Establishment and training of a Community Education Committee for each school supported- Provision of 3 days training for teachers on classroom management, code of conduct and pedagogy.– Provision of a 3-days training for teachers on gender-sensitive teaching strategies– Organization of weekly Peer-to-peer conflict mediation sessions – Salary for teachers from schools for 1 year; - Provision of transportation for the students living far the schools - Community awareness campaigns as means to promote education and prevent child labour, soldiering and early marriages

Protection Risk Analysis

Increased insecurity in the area, new outbreaks of conflicts or severe tensions between returnees and host communities (discrimination, affiliation accusations) prevent humanitarian access to the select facilities.

Mitigation Measures

Security assessments and weekly check with INSO and local authorities will monitor constantly the security issues and provide eventual contingency plans

How is your project accountable to affected population

According with COOPI Child Protection policy and Protection mainstreaming approach communities andbeneficiaries (including children) are involved in the planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating ofeducational programs so they can make informed decisions participate in making suggestions forimproving access and quality of education.Since the assessment phase across all locations the programme staff has and will engage withbeneficiaries through community dialogue sessions and they will be encouraged to participate fully andprovide feedback through accountability mechanisms, monitoring and evaluations systems.Special attention will be put in the hiring process by employing from at-risk groups in education staff,leadership and training positions.School management committees will be encouraged to be democratic and inclusive and religious or community leaders will be involved in school governance to remove ideological or religious motives tooppose or attack schools.Creation of Student and Parents Associations will be encouraged and participants will be trained inmainstreaming protection.A Complaints and Feedback mechanism will be established in all target areas to ensure accountability and allow beneficiaries to engage fully in the programme. Any issues raised will be addressed and if necessaryany programme improvement or adjustments will be made. The feedback mechanism complain will beconfidential and gender sensitive and will include a mechanisms for reporting instances of abuse andexploitation. The mechanism will be promoted through awareness on their existence and functioning, topromote beneficiary empowerment and assisting in the early detection of problems such as targeting,misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse, food diversion and fraud. Ad hoc intervention willtimely respond to the feedback of the beneficiaries to mitigate eventual negative consequences.
