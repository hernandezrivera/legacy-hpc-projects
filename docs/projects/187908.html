Project Summary

Maram has designed this proposed to provide Shelter and NFI assistance for the most affected people in NW Syria to upgrade their living conditions through:
- Distribution of essential NFI items 3000 NFI kits.
- Site improvement through land preparation, drainage system, and lighting poles including the tents leveling /Gravelling for 3,000 shelter units.
- Rehabilitation damaged houses for 500 houses.
1- Distribution of essential NFI items: MF plans to distribute 3,000 NFI kits to target 15,000 IDPs with different ages and vulnerable cases PWN (23 % men, 25 % women, 26 % Boys, and 27% girls ) taking into consideration Gender and age diversity through NFI kits distribution in Idleb and Aleppo area, where Maram Foundation has full access to the mentioned area and is implementing Emergency Response right now in it.
2- Site improvement through land preparation, drainage system, and lighting poles, in addition, to providing tent leveling/ Gravelling activity:
The activity aims at upgrading the living conditions of the targeted IDP sites by improving their infrastructure, this will be achieved by implementing graveling rehabilitation activity for the roads and tents of the targeted sites, establishing ramps for elderly people and PwDs and establishing drainage systems inside the sites to avoid any damage to the tents and property of the IDPs during rainy seasons and thus protect the camps from the consequences of the floods.
Maram Foundation will provide graveling for the roads and ground insulation for the family tents for the affected camps in Idleb and Aleppo after Maram teams conduct a detailed rapid needs assessment.
The total target is thus 15,000 IDPs.
3- Rehabilitation damaged houses: MF will implement this activity to target both the host community and IDPs through shelter rehabilitation activities and rehabilitate shelter for 500 households (HHs), which include 2500 individuals living in 500 damaged houses in Aleppo and Idleb governorate. MF will provide them with shelter repair and rehabilitation activities. This project includes 59% host communities and 41% IDPs.
All activities within the projects will be according to the Shelter cluster objectives and will be designed according to the SPHERE standards.
Furthermore, the planned intervention will mainstream protection principles especially “Do no harm” throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls.
During this project, Maram will consult with the community and individual IDPs taking into account the speciﬁc needs of each group. Maram Foundation also will involve the women and girls through Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) during the community outreach plan.
Summary, this project aims to reach 32,500 individuals targeting most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls and prioritize family members with high vulnerabilities according to the beneficiary selection criteria (female/single/child-headed households, IDP status, no source of income and support, people with disabilities, elderly,...etc.).
Maram Foundation always seeks to high-level coordination with all parties involved in any project (SNFI cluster, Donors, local authorities, other NGOs, and other related parties) to avoid any duplication. So for the activities included in this project and during the designing phase, Maram will coordinate with all the relevant parties and select the locations according to the gaps, which means that there will not be any duplication during implementing the proposed project activities.

4 - Needs

Based on the HRP2021 report, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian assistance, including 6 million in acute need, due to a convergence of factors arising from a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods, and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition, and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. Rapid and immediate lifesaving services remain a critical priority for many IDPs residing in the camps, encouraged MF team to conduct a Rapid Needs Assessment to identify the Shelter needs.The reported percentages in the RNA conducted by Maram reflect the poorly available services of shelter as well as the infrastructure upgrades to tented settlements, particularly in NWs Syria.MF seeks to save lives, uphold rights and promote access to safe and more privacy shelter through the provision of SNFI assistance in particular in 2022-2023, including distributing NFI items, providing ground insulation of their tents in addition to improving many of IDPs sites that affected from flooding during winter seasons through sub-roads rehabilitation /Gravelling, rain drainage system, and lighting poles, in addition, to rehabilitate the houses for those who live in a damaged house where there is no privacy and not protected from the external living conditions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

For distribution NFI: Risk 1: Beneﬁciaries might be exposed to risks if attending badly planned, organized and conducted distributions in connection with poor safety and security planning Mitigation: 1. Carry out thorough safety assessments on the distribution areas 2. Consider speciﬁc risks to women and best avoid and mitigate risk 3. Communicate intensively with national and local authorities, stakeholders and community leaders 4. Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons Risk 2: Increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneﬁciary selection criteria leading to a feeling of discrimination Mitigating: a. Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. b. Ensure beneﬁciary selection criteria are uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and are clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. d. Establish complaint mechanism. - For rehabilitation houses, tent insulation and Improving the IDPs sites: Risk 1: of eviction from camps/transit sites/spontaneous due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneﬁciaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation, Mitigating: a. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster b. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed Risk 2: Increased community tension: as mentioned above.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MF will Implement training on PSEA to be mandatory for all personnel. Periodically, with the support of Field Managers, Focal Points organize a training session for recruited staff, ideally within three months of recruitment. MF will incorporate an attitudes and beliefs’ session in the training as many respondents highlighted thisas the key obstacle to reporting. Maram team will ensure that the affected communities are informed on: I. The definition of sexual exploitation and abuse and about Complaints mechanism and information-sharing efforts and scaled up where possible II. The standards of conduct for humanitarian workers. III. Humanitarian workers have a right to humanitarian assistance without being subjected to SEA IV. Where to report Complaints &SEA incidents (all possible options/channels). V. What services are available, including complaint confidentiality assessments, and how to access them. VI. What to expect after making a complaint, including potential referrals, timeframes, and the roles, responsibilities, and any limitations of actors involved. VII. What steps the CBCM (Community-Based Complaints Mechanism) and the concerned agency will take to ensure safety and confidentiality
