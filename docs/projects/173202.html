Project Summary

The project will be for 12-month in Zalingei in Central Darfur targeting 40,407 (including 231 PWD) direct beneficiaries and 15,541 indirect beneficiaries who have been affected by conflict, seeking behavior and wash, hygiene and sanitation services. The sites of the project include Undrow, Abatta, Tamar, Kotirgi, Waranga, Urokom, Kalgo and Dankouj which were prone to problems of food insecurity, poverty and environmental degradation. The proposed project aligns with the needs identified in multi-agency needs assessment leading up to HRP 2021, with an overall goal to address the immediate and short-term humanitarian needs as displacement, IDP influx and returnees continues to settle. IRW aims to have a direct impact of improve livelihoods and living conditions, as well as strengthen state departments systems and communities to have resilient systems by improving WASH services, enhance access to safe and adequate water for domestic use, provide needs based, timely and effective humanitarian response by increasing protection and SGBV prevention and mitigation services for conflict affected individuals to address the high prevalence of sexual and gender-based violence through WASH services. The overall project goal is to empower vulnerable conflict affected IDPs, Returnees and host community through life saving integrated WASH and FSL Interventions, increased access to and utilization of improved use of safe, gender-sensitive WASH services through increased equitable and affordable access to safe and clean water for drinking and domestic use; improved access to dignified and gender-sensitive hygiene and sanitation facilities; and increased access to gender sensitive hygiene promotion materials, including MHM, meeting women and girls’ unique needs; and improved community capacities and increased sustainable WASH and food security and livelihoods opportunities. The exit strategy is embedded in the project cycle and IRW community engagement and participation strategy where the beneficiaries are fully involved in the identification and prioritization of their needs, and have made commitments to sustain the facilities and infrastructures that will be put in place, capacity building interventions and use of and mentorship of government departments will further ensure continuity of services through the government long after project completion.
In Blue Nile, the proposed project will cover thematic areas of WASH services to conflict affected IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Al Kurmuk, Damazine, Rosaries and Geissan localities. The project will be for 12-month targeting 22,190 direct beneficiaries. It is aiming at providing the IDPs and affected vulnerable communities with basic services and improved food security at the HH level, provide gender and culturally sensitive WASH and protection services while ensuring sustainable environmental practices. The proposed project will complement and link to existing IRW projects and will include rehabilitation of WASH facilities, supporting Community Based Protection Networks and Units, including schools hygiene and sanitation facilities, providing small holder farmers with seeds and tools, capacity building of community members and healthcare staff on hygiene and sanitation while incorporating Covid-19 awareness and prevention. The intervention will thus contribute to improved and increased access to basic services to the affected vulnerable men, women, boys and girls while creating resilience and sustainability. IRW will coordinate the activities with other partners and respective government departments including UN agencies such as UNICEF, WFP, UNOCHA, WHO and UNHCR. It will be direct implementation strategy while IRW will also involve its implementing partners in community mobilization and engagement.

1 - Needs

In Blue Nile, the vulnerabilities affecting WASH and food security and livelihoods of poor population are combination of extreme events and other cropping challenges experienced last season, such as environmental hazards affecting WASH services, crop destruction by animals, floods/dry spells, and high land rental coupled with high inflation rate impacting high food and non-food prices due to control measures of COVID-19. The government capacity to deliver basic services is far behind the international standards e.g. SPHERE standards. Limited access to safe water, large number of the populations including IDPs do not have access to safe drinking water. There is low capacity to manage water facilities at the community level and a shortage of spare parts are key reasons resulting in non-functioning water sources. Lack of access to safe sanitation services. Long term conflicts and lack of regular borehole maintenance have seriously damaged the water infrastructure. Low community awareness on hygiene and environmental sanitation. According to UNOCHA: Water, Sanitation and Hygiene WASH Cluster Status update in 8 Oct 2020, In Sudan, more than 63 per cent of the population have no access to basic sanitation, 23 per cent do not have access to a handwashing facility with soap and water and 40 per cent do not have access to basic drinking water services.Based on needs assessments conducted by IRW And 2021 HNO, 30,127 individuals, are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in Zalingei out of 301,268 population. These estimates may differ as there is no registration of IDPs outside camps. In Zalingei where most of the IDPs reside, 69% out of 112,632 inhabitants are IDPs. According to most recent returnee populations from HAC, Zalingei is currently hosting 54,335 (21,762M and 32,573F) returnees. Despite sustained humanitarian interventions and assistance, Sudan continues to struggle with conflicts, poverty, food insecurity and difficult economic situations that have contributed to malnutrition, escalation of preventable diseases and deteriorating livelihoods. OCHA HRP, HNO 2020 and planning data for HNO/HRP 2021 has shown increase in number of people in need of humanitarian assistance. Covid-19 has further intensified the already soaring human suffering of high malnutrition, escalation of preventable diseases and deteriorating livelihoods and well-being. The increased number of IDPs and returnees in Zalingei has put an increased pressure on existing facilitie more in the newly identified and accessible hard to reach areas, where WASH interventions will be implemented. According to IRW KAP Survey open defecation is still rampant as many lack access to latrines. IRW is planning to ensure safe latrine accessibility, specifically, women, children and PWD (IRW, Dec 2019). The average Household (HH) water consumption is 41.2L/household/day. Furthermore, for an average HH size of 6 persons is 6.2L/per person/per day, and this is below the recommended SPHERE minimum standards. A reasonable percentage of the community (53.6%) has latrines, 20.1% practice defecation in communal latrines. There is a considerable number of HHs about 36.9% prefer to use open defecation. Regarding water facilities management/ treatment is the responsibility of water committee at water source level (men and women) while the vast majority who fetch/ store water are women and girls. In case that the water points are not close from the HHs women and girls might face risks (GBV and Health implication) through the road while fetching Water. According to Gender Analysis Report the priority need among women and girls is MHM as 90% of women interviewed struggle to meet these needs due to poverty. Women and girls face vulnerabilities when it comes to using latrines. Latrines are often not gender segregated and are in insecure locations, resulting in women and girls using them at early or late hours of the day.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Through WASH interventions, BN and CD, will work to achieve WASH sector objectives, in related to improved use of safe, gender-sensitive WASH services among returnees and vulnerable communities in both States estimated by 62,597 target populations in total (22,190 individuals in BN and 40,407 persons in CD). This will be achieved through three main outputs: Under output 1: Increased equitable and affordable access to safe and clean water for drinking and domestic use, CD aims to rehabilitate 20 hand pumps, construct one new mini water yard equipped with solar and diesel generator power for emergency and construct 2 new hand pumps. CD will target 4,169 vulnerable families, selected based on vulnerability criteria and distribute water jericans for water collection and transportation. The intervention will form gender-balanced water committees with representation of all beneficiary types including women and disables. The committees will be trained on operation and maintenance of the rehabilitated water systems and new water facilities and as well support the trainees with the necessary tool kits. In addition to this CD will conduct water monitoring, tests and chlorination for 100 water sources at HH level. The project in BN aims to drill and install 10 new hand pumps, rehabilitate 20 broken hand pumps and rehabilitate one water yard. BN will support and provide operation costs for 20 water sources as well as will conduct water quality test and monitoring for water sources. BN intervention is planning to build the capacities of target communities, through delivering trainings to formed water user committees on operating the water systems to ensure sustainable sources. Under output 2: Improved access to dignified and gender-sensitive hygiene and sanitation facilities conforming to cultural norms, CD plan to conduct 50 culturally sensitive hygiene and sanitation awareness sessions including Covid-19 and SGBV and 20 cleaning campaigns. CD will rehabilitate 5 school latrines, 5 PHC latrines and construct 2 new school latrines, 2 new PHC latrines and 5 new community latrines that are gender and culturally acceptable. These will be followed by distribution of soap to 4,169 HHs. BN aims to provide and distribute soap to 3,000 persons, construct 20 hand washing facilities, provide personal hygiene materials for 1,000 female (reusable sanitary pads) and conduct 20 hygiene campaigns. Through the intervention, BN will also support the communities with 10 vector control spray campaigns both draining and standing, deliver hygiene promotion messages through outpatient therapeutic programmes and stabilization centers. The activities in BN will extend to support the communities in disposing 10 tons of solid waste garbage. The project plan to train the community on proper disposal methods and management. Under output 3: Increased access to gender sensitive hygiene promotion materials, including MHM, meeting women and girls’ unique needs, CD will distribute MHN and dignity kits to women and adolescent girls, while BN will support women with sanitary kits.CD will introduce FSL under output 4: Improved gender balanced Employability, alternative livelihoods and Support for IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host communities by providing high quality certified seeds, extensions services and trainings of agricultural agents on extension techniques. CD will support the communities with agricultural tools, train 500 nutrition sensitive homestead HH, 250 women to benefit from crop and vegetable seeds on home gardening. CD will train 100 farmers on improved methods for animal husbandry, conduct 1 TOT training on animal feed production, and will conduct 2 extension trainings for farmers to bene􀂦t from crop and cash crop seeds to improve sustainable farming techniques. The project will also address the precarious situation of shortage of agricultural inputs, limited appropriate farming knowledge and inadequate environmental awareness.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IRW and IRS do prioritize protection (including Child Protection and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA) and safeguarding against any form of abuse of its beneficiaries (direct and indirect) and staff. IRS through its Gender, Protection and Inclusion focal persons both at Country and field offices ensures that all project interventions and community engagements are in line with specific needs of men, women, girls and boys including PwD, chronically ill and the elderly and that staff and project interventions are sensitive to vulnerabilities. Furthermore, IRS will put into place modalities to minimize risk and address concerns and incidents appropriately when they arise. Safeguarding measures will be integrated into all areas of the organization. IRS has a child safe guarding policy that is adhered to by all staff and all that are contracted by IRS to implement activities on its behalf. The IRS child safeguarding policy provides an overarching commitment to preventing harm to children. IRS will ensure that its beneficiaries and the vulnerable are free from abuse and harm. The community and beneficiaries will be empowered by encouraging them to make their own decisions and provide informed consent. Preventing the risk of abuse or neglect, and stop it from occurring will be practiced and adhered to at all times of project implementation. The focal persons in the field monitors protection and safe guarding on a daily basis across all project interventions, uphold the principle of ‘do no harm’ and minimum standards on protection in humanitarian assistance is respected. IRSS recognizes the need to address and design all activities with the core aim to reduce risks of S/GBV, which is widespread in the country. IRS holds its staff accountable and any form of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, violations against protection, inclusion and safeguarding policies is professionally investigated by the focal persons with the same cascaded to the Country Office (GPI Focal Point and Country Management Team) for further investigation and actions while observing the IRW HR policies and the full respect of the principles of confidentiality and the right to be heard during the whole process. The focal persons both in country and field office have been trained on aspects of receiving and dissemination of complaints and safeguarding information with the aim of ensuring that all stakeholders and particularly beneficiaries are aware of their rights. This will also be achieved by ensuring that all IRS staff adhere to the 9 CHS at all times and that beneficiaries are aware of gender equality and GBV through awareness sessions.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IRS has an established Beneficiary Complains and Response Mechanism (CRM) in place in the target project locations that promotes openness and community participatory initiatives and also includes engaging Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) to channel their complaints to IRS and enables beneficiaries to provide feedback both positive and negative and also to raise complains against IRS staff and or against the nature of project activities being implemented by IRS. The CRM channels through which the community will channel their complaints, concerns and safeguarding related complaints at all times without victimization. Safe guarding related complaints are specifically handled by the Protection Focal Point and Program Manager who escalates the same to the Country Director for further action and direction. The Community Feedback Boxes are strategically placed outside IRS premises as well as at community level where community member can drop complains and feedback as the boxes are checked on a weekly basis by the MEAL Officer. In addition, complains phone number and email is provided in posters placed in all project sites to enable community members report complains and provide feedback without victimization. To ensure that proper records are kept on this, IRS has focal persons in the project sites who document the feedback and complaints and actions taken to address the same and or to mitigate reoccurrence. This are documented in the Complaints and Response Mechanism Registers that are shared with the Country Office for further actions on a monthly basis to enable the senior management to follow on the feedbacks and complains and provide support in addressing and or mitigating the same. Complaints including safe guarding complains that are of high magnitude and impact on the beneficiaries and the organization are escalated to the IRW Global level for further action. IRS has a very strict culture of non-discrimination and or punishment against those providing or raising complains against specific staff and or project activity implementation. IRS will be accountable to the community members themselves through quarterly locality-level discussions with communities and one-on-one interactions with individual stakeholders. The meetings are two levelled the communities plan for their own meetings where complains and feedback are raised with the second level involving IRS and community members where IRS organizes the meet and listens to any concerns, complains and feedback or suggestions they have, address and mitigate problems and incorporate suggestions and feedback into the project design as much as is feasible.
