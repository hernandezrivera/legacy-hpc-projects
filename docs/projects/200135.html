Project Summary

Throughout this project, PUI will support pregnant and lactating women (Lebanese, PRL, migrants) by cascading sound dietary behaviors and practices to prevent malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies. This project was designed based on PUI’s technical expertise and long-term presence and acceptance in the area of intervention.
Monitoring activities will be carried out throughout the life of the project in order to guarantee the compliance of the activities with agreed objectives and targets. A detailed monitoring and evaluation plan (M&E) will be developed at the beginning of the project by PUI’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) unit together with the technical teams and programme staff, in line with the proposed indicators defined in the intervention logical framework. Tools and monitoring activities detailed in the M&E plan will provide regular feedback on the progress towards the proposed results and allow for swift adjustment if needed. A dedicated MEAL Activities Schedule will also be prepared and followed up by the MEAL Manager.
Data collection and storage tools will grant the highest level of data protection and confidentiality, allowing a systematic disaggregation per sex, age, and disability and providing automatic reporting features. While PUI MEAL team will ensure the design, development, and maintenance of internal Indicator Tracking Tables and Databases, a wider integration with the MoPH Health Information System (Phenix) will also be pursued, allowing an additional layer of data triangulation between different sources and thus enhancing the overall data quality and accuracy.
Programme monitoring will combine different methodologies, based on qualitative and quantitative approaches, including field visits and surveys occurring on a regular basis. Their rollout will be ensured under the supervision of the MEAL Manager, which will be in charge of the design of the tools, the management of the MEAL Officers in the bases, and of the data analysis and report writing and dissemination. In particular:
Patient Satisfaction and Post Intervention Surveys will be conducted at least twice during the project implementation period in order to gather feedback from pregnant women on their perceptions about the quality and the outcomes of the services received, their feeling about topics such as safety and accessibility as well as the patient-centered approach supported by PUI. While based on a quantitative approach, these surveys might also include qualitative data collection methodologies such as Focus Groups Discussion and in-depth interviews, depending on the context.
Post Distribution Monitoring exercises will be regularly carried out in order to monitor cash and NFIs distribution, focusing on the outcome and process-related research questions, including direct feedback from beneficiaries about the items distributed and, potentially, complaints to be addressed.
In specific circumstances preventing direct access to the project areas, such as COVID-19 related movement restrictions decided by the GOL or civil unrest, measures will be taken to shift these activities to remote modalities (phone calls, reduced sample sizes, online FGDs, etc.)
Dedicated capitalisation activities, to be conducted at least every six months, will include Lessons Learnt workshops and/or project reviews, to be facilitated by the MEAL unit and involving technical and field teams. Such activities will be carried out against specific ToRs and will focus on detailed learning agendas and evaluation questions; best practices, implementation challenges, and ways forward will be identified and discussed; capitalization reports will be issued and disseminated to all relevant stakeholders.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

PUI is proposing this project in response to ERP's nutrition objectives, namely: 1) protect, promote and support the uptake of recommended maternal, infant and young child feeding and care practices among vulnerable adolescents, pregnant/lactating women and care takers of children aged below 2 years; 2) to scale up programmatic solutions to support the prevention, identification and treatment of acute malnutrition and micro-nutrient deficiencies among vulnerable pregnant and lactating women and U5 children.The corresponding activities of this project are the following:1-Provision of cash for transportation for identified IYCF cases who will be referred to the PHCCs to benefit from micronutrients supplements.2-Provision of counseling about IYCF as well as malnutrition screening for the targeted beneficiaries within the catchment areas of the PHCCs.3- Provision of vitamin D supplement for the supported PHCCs of PUI under different grants.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The intervention adopts a protection mainstreaming approach to addressing protection issues of prevention against sexual exploitation and abuse [PSEA], and integrate protection, gender and social inclusion, through identification of risks to meaningful access, accountability and participation. The mitigation measures include all involved parties’ acknowledgment of PUI’s policies which includes but limited to PSEA Policy, Gender Policy, Safeguarding Policy, Child Protection Policy, Operational Do No Harm Manual, Code of conduct, etc.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To ensure that projects implemented by PUI in Lebanon are responsive to the population’s needs within targeted areas, PUI involves the affected populations in all stages of the project cycle. During implementation, PUI will follow the recently revised FCRM Standard operating Procedures to gather feedback from beneficiaries, using multiple channels. All beneficiaries’ feedback/complaints are addressed and used to improve program implementation, provide timely response, and enhance transparency. Informed consents will be taken from any person of concern and strict confidentiality rules will be followed. The programming is tailored based on primary data directly collected from communities and continuous consultation which allow PUI to design and adapt the intervention to best suit each HH’s needs. Beneficiaries are informed of all available feedback mechanisms throughout project implementation.
