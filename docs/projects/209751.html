Project Summary

In 2024, UNRWA estimates that 155,500 of the most vulnerable Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance. Many among them struggle to secure basic shelter and NFI need, and will be supported by UNRWA through this project as part of the Humanitarian response Plan, while adhering to the shelter Sector technical guidance.
The Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2024 underscores the profound impact of enduring political, economic, and humanitarian crises on Palestine refugees. An alarming October 2023 post-distribution monitoring survey revealed distressing trends: 92% borrowing food or seeking help, 64% reducing daily meals due to financial constraints, and 40% adopting emergency livelihood strategies, highlighting the diminishing ability to cope with increasing poverty.
The vulnerability of Palestine refugees was exacerbated by the February 2023 earthquakes. UNRWA records indicate a doubled number accessing Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) from February to June 2023, indicative of elevated trauma levels. The earthquakes displaced 1,511 households, adding to the 40% already in protracted displacement due to the Syrian conflict.
Between January and October 2023, 1,059 Palestine refugees returned to Syria, while others returned to camps, with a significant number returning to Yarmouk and Ein el Tal camps. However, challenges persist due to the high cost of living outside the camps. As of September 2023, around 2,000 families had returned to Yarmouk, 170 to Ein el Tal, and 750 to Deraa camp.
The comprehensive intervention comprises four key components:
a) Winterization: Providing 155,500 of the most vulnerable Palestine refugees with core NFIs for winterization in various locations, including blankets, mattresses, mats, and tarpaulin sheets. The unit cost of the winterization kit is approximately $51.12 per beneficiary.
b) In-kind distribution of NFIs: Targeting 500 families (1,690 beneficiaries) facing special hardship cases, eligibility dependent on assessments conducted by UNRWA social workers. The unit cost is approximately $78.35 per beneficiary, covering blankets, mattresses, kitchen sets, jerry cans, mats, tarpaulin sheets, solar lamps, plastic buckets, and wood stoves. Locations and eligibility depend on assessments to be conducted by UNRWA social workers.
c) Contingency stock of NFIs for 2,000 families (6,765 beneficiaries) for potential shocks. Planning depends on the scale and nature of any potential crisis.
d) Cash assistance for major shelter repairs: Targeting 653 families (corresponding to 2220 refugees considering the average family size - 3.4 members - retrieved from the UNRWA registration and information system) in Ain Al Tal , Deraa and Yarmouk camps with an average transfer value of 3,500 USD /HH. It is worth mentioning that the actual transfer values that will be disbursed to the beneficiaries will depend on the income level of the agency, market conditions, and needs assessments by the agency and any future revisions of the values by the sector. Distribution will occur through two financial service providers.

PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN Clear Check recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations
