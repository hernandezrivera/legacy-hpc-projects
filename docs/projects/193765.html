Project Summary

The overall objective of this project to target areas most impacted by the increasing electricity deficit in the Gaza Strip to protect current levels of food and agricultural production, livelihoods and food security by providing vulnerable agricultural producers in the Gaza Strip with a consistent, clean and renewable source of energy to guarantee sufficient electricity for essential food production. The intervention pays particular attention to the impact of the electricity deficit on female-headed households and the workload of women and youth. The project will contribute to achieving Strategic Objective 3 of the HRP 2023 and the FSS SO1 “the resilience of vulnerable households is protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced”, as it invests in solar energy sources to ensure that targeted women and men farmers have a consistent electricity supply when required to cope with frequent energy cuts as part of the protracted crisis.
As an emergency intervention, this project aims to minimize damages and losses to domestic crop production in the current season and to enable normal levels of cultivation in subsequent seasons. In doing so, this project will protect agriculture-based livelihoods, food security and domestic food production that are at risk given the chronic electricity deficit and will enable the continuation of normal levels of cultivation if the electricity supply to the Gaza Strip is further reduced. The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable women and men farmers whose livelihoods are endangered. The selection of farmers is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. FAO will also ensure the implementation of Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles by implementing the built-in process for reporting such incidents.
At least 450 households (2 520 individuals including 615 women, 626 men, 1 209 children, 70 elderly) will benefit directly from the project in the governorates of Khan Yunis, Rafah, Middle Area, Gaza and North Gaza, where the impact of the electricity crisis is most acute/significant.

1 - Needs

Since 2007, the Gaza Strip (GS) faced chronic drawbacks due to long-standing power supply deficits, with the 2017 average of 404 megawatts falling to 243 in 2022. Currently, the GS has 12 hours of blackouts per day on average. This deficit has undermined the already fragile living and economic conditions for over a decade – worsened by recurring hostilities that affect access to basic services and reduce the profitability and sustainability of herding and farming households’ (HHs) agricultural activities. The MoA estimates that the August 2022 escalations hit agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock and poultry farms, resulting in $1m of damages and losses. The agricultural sector is heavily affected due to its high energy demand for irrigation wells, ponds, cold storage, food production and poultry hatcheries, etc. for which value chain facilities require energy 24/7. In addition, the impacts of the Russia-Ukraine crisis, which contributed to soaring and unpredictable costs and availability of staple foods and key production inputs further threaten to destabilize the agricultural sector. In 2022, 60% of farming and herding HHs reported decreased incomes, 17% reported resorting to life savings, 7% sold HH assets and 4% sold livelihood productive assets to cope. Alarmingly, these HHs reported spending up to 56% of total expenditures on food (58% for female-headed HHs), a level that categorizes these HHs as the most vulnerable segments of the population (MSNA, 2022). As the power crisis continues, many farmers may no longer afford the higher irrigation costs, leading to crop damage and losses from negative coping mechanisms like irregular irrigation (OCHA OPt). The MoA estimates the cost of 1 m3 of irrigation water increased by up to 40% since April 2017, raising fears that irrigation water costs will increase further if the electricity supply decreases further. Moreover, according to the MoA, up to 60% of GS agricultural land is at risk of being set aside due to lack of electricity needed to pump irrigation water. Women and men farmers, women cooperatives and associations must choose between substantially reducing the scale of agricultural production or investing in costly diesel generators to pump the needed amount of irrigation water. Some smaller producers abandoned their activities, leaving them vulnerable to a poverty trap fueled by food and nutrition insecurity. Service providers suffered from unstable connections with the supply chain. Consumers buy damaged produce and processed food products at higher costs. The need for solar electrification to protect the critical production capacities of farmers and herders in the Gaza Strip has been assessed through a consultative process engaging all related actors in early 2022. This resulted in the formulation of “The Participated Road Map of the Solar Energy Needs of the Agriculture in the Gaza Strip” with a special focus on addressing the needs of the most vulnerable in order to protect current levels of food and agricultural production, livelihoods and food security in the Gaza Strip. According to the HNO, 84% of Palestinians are expected to need humanitarian assistance for food insecurity in 2023, 77% of whom live in the GS. These findings suggest that the low levels of food security have been driven by a combination of adverse factors further aggravated by energy shortages: more HHs become poorer, less resilient and more food deprived. This chronic situation has severely affected vulnerable groups like women, children and older people particularly since 56% of female households in the Gaza Strip are classified as severe and moderately food insecure (MSNA 2022). As the main caretakers, women are burdened with performing all house and family-related duties within the few hours of electricity provided, inflicting additional pressure on women and particularly female-headed HHs who perform work duties inside and outside the house. In addition, small-scale women processors who work at the household level produce approximately 90% of dairy products which rely heavily on electricity for production. This means that when there is no/limited electricity, the burden on women is greater, and limits their production options as women will have to spend anywhere from two to three hours per day to shake the milk manually in order to process it into dairy products. Older people are vulnerable to the limited power supply as movement becomes more challenging when power is cut. When possible, female-headed HHs and other vulnerable groups (e.g. people with disabilities and the elderly) will be prioritized in the selection. Prioritization of vulnerability factors including HHs’ characteristics, dependency ratio, income, assets, and other vulnerabilities will be further detailed in the preparation phase. At the project’s inception community mobilization workshops will be held to raise the awareness of the targeted communities including the various population groups.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will systematically consider Accountability to Affected Population in general and PSEA standards in particular. As part of the project activities, farming communities will be informed about the project objectives, intended support, targeted group and selection criteria through information sheets that will be made available to all community members including elderly, women and children. The information sheets will include information regarding FAO grievance mechanism and channels of communications including for submitting PSEA related complaints. The actual selection of beneficiaries will consider concrete criteria (some of which were mentioned above as part of the vulnerability factors) to ensure prioritizing those who are in most need as well as the value and sustainability of the support for the resilience of the production activities and livelihoods of the families. This will also be in coordination with the relevant MoA directorates and line ministries. The intervention will deliver the following outcome, outputs and activities: Outcome: At least 730 vulnerable food-producing households have their food production capacities, income sources and food security protected Output 1: Agricultural producers are supported with solar energy systems to ensure sufficient irrigation of crops and protection of farmer’s livelihoods. Based on the horsepower of the water pump, different beneficiaries will have different PV systems designed. Activity 1.1: Productive capacities of 20 medium-sized irrigation wells restored by equipping them with solar-powered pumping systems (28 kw per well). This will include signing agreements with the groups of farmers who own the wells to provide irrigation water to neighboring women and men farmers at a reduced price to be determined and monitored in cooperation with the MoA. Each well rehabilitated will benefit approximately 15 female and male farming households for a total of 300 farming households (including 409 women, 417 men, 806 children, 47 elderly). Location: Rafah (x6); Khan Yunis (x8); Middle Area (x2); North Gaza (x2) and Gaza city (x2). Activity 1.2: Productive capacities of 200 water harvesting ponds restored through the installation of solar energy units (3 kw per pond). Location: Khan Yunis: Al Sattar (x25); Abasan al Kabira and Aljadida (x25); Bani Suhela (x20) and Qizan Al Najar (x40); Rafah: Shoka (x20); Muraj (x30); Mawasi, Rafah and Khan Yunis: (x20), and Mosabih (x20). Activity 1.3: Conducting essential awareness campaigns and training for women and men on irrigation management and solar energy maintenance in coordination with the MoA. Location: Rafah; Khan Yunis; Middle Area; North Gaza and Gaza city. Output 2: Poultry and dairy farms are supported with solar energy units to maintain normal levels of egg and milk production, and to mitigate the effects of climate change. Activity 2.1: Supporting 200 poultry farms across the Gaza Strip with solar energy systems. This will maintain essential lighting for egg production and operate water sprinkler networks during heat waves and air extraction fans in summer (0.66 kw per farm). Location: North Gaza (35), Gaza (35), Middle Area (45), Khan Yunis (55) and Rafah (30). Activity 2.2: Supporting 30 dairy farms across the Gaza Strip with solar energy systems for the operation of portable milking machines (2.66 kw per farm). Location: North Gaza (8), Gaza (7), Middle Area (5), Khan Yunis (5) and Rafah (5). At the inception phase of the project implementation, community mobilization workshops will be held to raise the awareness of the targeted communities including the various population groups around PSEA-related issues and available communication networks. At the conclusion of the activities, a post-delivery monitoring survey will be implemented to seek communities’ feedback on the awareness and the responsiveness of the FAO grievance mechanism, including for PSEA related complaints.

3 - Indicators

Results will be measured by change in income level and survey of energy availability of beneficiaries. Each indicator will be gender, age, and disability disaggregated where possible. FAO will ensure that the interventions are delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. Project activities and achievements will be monitored consistently to ensure continued alignment with the FSS response plan and the FAO Country Programme Framework 2018-2022. Gender-disaggregated data will be collected on all activities. Participation of women will be closely monitored and tailored adjustments will be made to ensure effective participation of women. Post-delivery monitoring will be conducted by interviewing a sample of female and male beneficiaries in order to monitor use of the inputs distributed, prospects for sustainability of the intervention as well as overall satisfaction of beneficiaries as part of FAO’s accountability to affected populations. A Grievance Redress Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow-up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until final decision and the subsequent follow-up. The procedure includes several well-defined steps tied to specific timeframes, including preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution, and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post-delivery monitoring as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant. All project activities and results will be coordinated with the FSS to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned shall be shared with all FSS stakeholders. In addition, FAO will maintain coordination with the protection cluster and the Humanitarian Country Team in order to ensure alignment with and support of advocacy initiatives.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable women and men food-producing households whose livelihoods are endangered. The selection of farmers and herder's is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries at risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. FAO will also ensure the implementation of PSEA principles and will have a built-in process for reporting such incidents. The proposed intervention is meant to protect endangered agricultural livelihoods of vulnerable female communities in the Gaza Strip by providing vulnerable agricultural producers with a consistent, clean and renewable source of energy to guarantee sufficient electricity for essential food production, hence the core of the intervention aims at protecting beneficiaries. Priority whenever possible, will be given to vulnerable marginalized groups including women, people with disabilities, and older people. FAO also abides by the principle of Do No Harm; and respects a low profile during the implementation of activities to avoid any harm coming to beneficiaries. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from a further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.
