Project Summary

WFP aims to contribute to a reduction in acute and chronic malnutrition rates, and an improvement in nutritional status among refugees and local Bangladeshi communities in Cox’s Bazar through the provision of curative and preventive nutrition services for children below 5 years and pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW). WFP will implement its project interventions across 45 integrated nutrition facilities (INFs) in 33 camps, and 55 nutrition sites in the local Bangladeshi community in partnership with UNICEF, UNHCR and cooperating partners under the coordination of the Cox's Bazar nutrition sector. WFP will provide targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for moderate acutely malnourished children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (PLW) to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition in both the refugee camps and in the host community. A 200g daily ration of Super Cereal Plus (Wheat Soya Blend Plus -WSB++) & 100g daily ration of Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) will be provided for children 6-59 months in the host community and camps respectively; and 250g of Super Cereal (Wheat Soya Blend -WSB+) and 16.66g of oil will be provided to PBW under TSFP.
An enhanced prevention of malnutrition package will be put in place to mitigate the increase in prevalence of acute and chronic malnutrition and its associated risks for mortality and morbidity and promote child development. In the camps, this includes provision of the blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP) for younger children 6-23 months and PBWs, and a nutrition sensitive e-voucher programme that allows older children (24-59 months) access to fresh fruits and vegetables aimed at enhancing their dietary diversity and promote growth monitoring and uptake of other complementary services at the INF. These children will receive a US$ 3 redeemable voucher. Social Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) activities and utilization of tools that positively influence behaviour, knowledge, attitudes, and practice around nutrition will be conducted in both the refugee and host communities.
Active case finding through community screening, referral and follow up of malnourished children under five and pregnant and breastfeeding women will be done. Additionally, growth monitoring and promotion for children 6-59 months will be part of the project interventions.
The underlying causes of malnutrition will be addressed through integration of nutrition sensitive approaches in ongoing general food assistance, school feeding, self-reliance, and livelihood interventions.
WFP will promote the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies particularly anaemia through the promotion of dietary diversification.
WFP will conduct capacity strengthening for partners and stakeholders to improve capacity for implementation of nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive programming. WFP will actively engaged at nutrition sector and support programme intervention as member of SAG, Assessment, IYCF & CMAM technical working group.
WFP will support quantitative and qualitative nutrition assessments to ensure that nutrition programmes are tailormade according to the needs of the targeted populations. In addition, WFP will also conduct a study in Ukhiya & Teknaf local community on alternative feeding options for acute malnutrition treatment in collaboration with icddr,b .
Project interventions will be adapted to ensure an inclusive approach to promote participation of women, men, older people, adolescent, people who have chronic health conditions and persons with disabilities. Additionally, protection mechanisms will be put in place including complaints and feedback mechanisms and help desks to ensure accountability to affected populations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Despite levels of acute malnutrition reducing to below the emergency threshold since the onset of the influx, the nutrition situation remains precarious. According to the 2022 Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) conducted for the Rohingya refugee camps, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) by weight for height (WHZ) is at 12.3 percent, which is categorized as high based on the WHO/UNICEF classification of malnutrition and the >10 percent UNHCR standards for refugee camps. Chronic malnutrition remains very high according to WHO thresholds with stunting at 41 percent and underweight at 36.7 percent. Anaemia rates among children and women of reproductive age stands at 50.5 percent and 40.3 percent respectively, indicating a problem of public health significance. Th nutrition situation in the surrounding Bangladeshi community is also concerning. Based on the Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART 2021) and following WHO/UNICEF malnutrition classification, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate in Teknaf and Ukhiya sub-districts is at 9.9 percent and 9.8 percent, respectively. The GAM rates among local people in Kutubdia and Pekua are at 14.9 percent and 13.0 percent respectively, categorized as serious, and critical in Moheshkhali at 16.0 percent. Chronic malnutrition is also in the serious category for Bangladeshi households across all sub-districts, with the lowest prevalence in Pekua (21.6 percent) and the highest in Moheshkhali at 29.8 percent. The underweight rate also falls into the serious category across all sub-districts, except for Moheshkhali where it is critical at 32.1 percent. Acute malnutrition among pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW) is also poor in Moheshkhali (3.2 percent), Kutubdia (5.6 percent), and Pekua (4.0 percent).In these areas – camps and local communities, prevalence for anaemia is considered a public health concern, coupled with sub-optimal IYCF practices, inadequate dietary diversity, poor care practices, high disease morbidity, low birth spacing, and inadequate WASH practices. These have been cited as key underlying factors contributing to the high prevalence rates of malnutrition in the Cox’s Bazar operation.Due to global fund constraint affecting the humanitarian sector, food assistance monthly entitlement was reduced from USD 12 /person/month in the Month of February to USD 10/person/month on 1 March 2023, and further reduced to USD 8/person/month on 1 June 2023. As per the last joint monitoring report published in September 2023 by the nutrition sector, more children are being admitted into treatment programmes for both severe and moderate acute malnutrition compared to the previous year, by 11 percent and 4 percent, respectively. Treatment duration has become longer by 4 to 7 days while overall cure rate has decreased by 3.1 percent (from 90 to 87 percent). Even before the ration cuts, insufficient food intake and malnutrition were widespread with high levels of undernutrition. This nutritional fragility is a worrying backdrop to the ration cuts. Therefore, despite considerable expansion of nutrition treatment centres, food assistance, WASH facilities, and health services, the current situation constitute serious levels of malnutrition in need of ongoing nutritional support.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In 2019 WFP, UNICEF and UNHCR under the coordination umbrella of the nutrition sector initiated a rationalization process. By January 2020, the Nutrition sector had successfully implemented a rationalization exercise to bring four Nutrition services (OTP, TSFP, IYCF and BSFP) together. In 2023, WFP, UNICEF & UNHCR with support from nutrition sector conducted staff rationalization exercise to ensure cost efficiency and harmonized human resources in each integrated nutrition sites. These services are now delivered through 45 Integrated Nutrition Facilities (INFs) in the 33 camps in Cox’s Bazar. All these 45 facilities are co-funded by UNICEF, UNHCR and WFP. In 2024 moderate acute malnutrition treatment will be conducted through 55 INFs within the local Bangladeshi communities in Teknaf & Ukhiya. In 2024, WFP will work with cooperating partners to strengthen the capacity of the government root level health staffs on nutrition service delivery and reporting.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

WFP ensures dignity, safety, security, and protection of all beneficiaries including nutrition programme from any kinds of harm by addressing protection risks and incorporating appropriate mitigation measures into WFP’s programme. The Protection and AAP policy describes strategies to ensure protection principle into WFP’s interventions. WFP ensures the collection of beneficiary data contributes to accountability, inclusion, good programming, assurance and improved coordination with partners, especially when introducing new technologies and services. WFP adopts a people-centred approach that emphasizes the identification of data protection risks and mitigation measures for beneficiaries as part of its context analysis, operational programme design and programme delivery, including the promotion of inclusion and digital literacy. The use of technology when supporting the collection of personal data must be informed by protection considerations to prevent potential discrimination or exclusion. When updating and sharing information on different services, WFP works with Interagency groups, cluster and field level protection focal points for case referrals to ensure that the beneficiaries’ protection needs are met. WFP’s strong collaboration with the Protection Working Group for the integration of protection concerns in all areas of humanitarian operation and wider advocacy support ensures the rights of crisis affected population.

3.3 - Age & Disability

WFP nutrition programme focuses on young and older children aged 0-59 months and pregnant and breastfeeding women affected by acute malnutrition or at risk of it. WFP works internally with different sectors like General Assistance, Livelihoods, Social Safety Net, and School feeding to ensure that other age groups in need of humanitarian assistance are met.WFP's Disability Inclusion Road Map provides strategic guidance for the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all programmatic activities, with a strong focus on upholding their human rights. Through our resilience programs, person with disabilities receive sign language and skill training, empowering them with valuable life skills and enhancing their livelihood opportunities. Nutrition intervention will ensure disability inclusion through disability friendly nutrition site along with prioritization on service delivery. Referral of identified beneficiaries with disability will be referred to appropriate service. In summary, WFP remains steadfast in its commitment to advancing gender equality, promoting inclusivity, and empowering individuals in the communities we serve, leaving no one behind in our pursuit of a world free from hunger and inequality.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

WFP has Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) guidelines and has recently developed a community engagement strategy for AAP (2021-2026), using a human rights approach to humanitarian and development action that considers AAP as the central focus of its operations. As such, WFP recognizes the need for transparent and continuous two-way communication between the organization and the people it serves in order to capture, record, and relay the expressed risks, needs, capacities and aspirations of communities. Actions and approaches which have enabled communities to participate meaningfully in key decisions throughout the WFP programme cycle include assessment and analysis, planning and design, resource mobilization, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. In addition, beneficiaries are well-informed about their rights and entitlements, and have access to complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM) on WFP programmes. WFP ensures that their cooperating partners demonstrate their capacity to integrate AAP aspects into the design and implementation of their activities.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

WFP mainstream Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through its programme, policy implementation and cooperating partner. WFP gave high priority to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) by adopting the Secretary General Bulletin, incorporating PSEA core principles into agreements with retailers, cooperating partners, and vendors. This involved establishing safe and confidential reporting mechanisms, conducting awareness campaigns, and ensuring staff and partners adhered to these standards. WFP implemented a detailed Annual PSEA action plan and allocated a budget for PSEA capacity development, assessed cooperating partners' capacity using assessment tools, provided capacity development for staff and cooperating partners, emphasized ethical behaviour, incorporated protection principles into standard behavioral practices, and captured best practices for wider use in program operations to prevent abuse and exploitation. To ensure the voices of affected individuals were heard, WFP established confidential and accessible Community-based Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) for reporting concerns or incidents of abuse, reinforcing their commitment to taking appropriate actions in response.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, WFP is committed and will ensure measures to reduce impact of nutrition intervention on environmental degradation. WFP will ensure environmental awareness among nutrition programme staffs and volunteers. Nutrition partner will collect empty sachet of Specialized nutritious food (SNF’s) products such as Super Cereal, Ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF), Ready-to-Use Supplementary Food (RUSF). Collected packaging wastage will be then upcycled into items to be used for agricultural practice through sewing and crafting under WFPs resilience activities. WFP will also ensure the emergency preparedness and response plan for nutrition activities to ensure business continuity and service delivery based on need during any kind of emergency.
