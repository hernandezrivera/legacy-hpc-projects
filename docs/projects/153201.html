Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to prevent and manage acute malnutrition in children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers through implementation of community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) activities in Rumbek Center and Pibor counties. This project will focus on the management of children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition and pregnant and lactating mothers with acute malnutrition and will be implemented using the national CMAM guideline. Children of SAM with medical complication will be referred to nearby stabilization center (SC). To ensure quality and good coverage of the program, CNVs and MtMSG will do continuous screening, referral and follow up cases at community level. Furthermore, preventive activities will be conducted at nutrition sites and at community level and areas of focus will be awareness creation on maternal infant and young child feeding, identification and prevention of malnutrition, sanitation and hygiene, cooking demonstration and kitchen gardening. As part of treatment protocol, all children (if there is enough supply) with SAM will be screened and treated for malaria according to the national protocol. Furthermore, preventive kits (jerry cans, Mosquito nets, soaps) shall be provided to mothers /care takers of children under treatment for in OTP/TSFP sites as these children are at higher risk of getting infected and also a way to promote personal hygiene.
All children who will be discharged cured from OTPs will be enrolled to TSFP programs for full nutritional recovery. When there is de-worming and vitamin A supplementation campaign, this project will support to ensure children under five in the county get it in collaboration with SMOH/CHD and the health partner in the county. Furthermore, children who may deteriorate while in TSFP programme will be enrolled to the OTP programme. PI will implement OTP and TSFP programme together in Rumbek Center but only TSFP programme in Pibor county. In addition to the above mentioned interventions, PI will implement BSFP in five sites of Pibor county.
Currently, PI is implementing OTP programme in seven OTP sites (2 OTPs in Kapoeta East, and 2 OTPs in Rumbek East, 3 OTPs in Cueibet) and 23 TSFPs (13 TSFPs in Rumbek East, 5 TSFPs in Rumbek Center, 4 TSFPs in Awerial and 8 TSFPs in Pibor, 1 TSFP in Wulu county) and 5 BSFP sites (all in in Pibor but ended in August).

Needs

Significant number of people in South Sudan are at risk of malnutrition especially those in crisis-affected areas with infants, young children and pregnant and lactating women facing the biggest challenge to meet their basic nutrition requirements all year round. Based on the September 2018 IPC analysis, it is expected that 6.1 million people (59% of the total population2) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity3 at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Humanitarian assistance remains critical to improving the emergency deteriorating further into catastrophe level. The burden of acute malnutrition continues amongst the population and therefore, preventive and curative malnutrition program is a priority to prevent increased mortality rates especially amongst children. Nonetheless, other factors like wash and poor hygiene, poor childcare practices, Infant and young child feeding practices, poor access to primary health services accompanied with low levels of community knowledge, driven by low adult literacy rates particularly among women fuels the vicious cycle of disease and malnutrition. Access to health and nutrition services is needed to prevent under nutrition and morbidity in children. The nutrition status of children< 5 years in Lakes and Jonglie states is an issue of much concern. According to Sept 2018 IPC analysis, 73% of Lakes population are in Crisis, Emergency & Catastrophe which is the highest of all the states. From October to December 2018, Lakes state is predicted to have 57.60% population in Crisis, Emergency & Catastrophe phases. Moreover, according to FSNMS survey round 20 in 2017, Rumbek Centre had a GAM rate of 21.7% (SAM- 5.9% and MAM – 15.8%) which is above WHO threshold level of critical that requires extensive nutrition response. Besides, there are several factors that contribute to the enhanced risk of children’s nutrition status. Of these malaria, intra and inter communal conflicts and revenge killings which cause human and livestock displacement in the whole state resulting in looting of assets, and burning of houses and disruption of major livelihood activities are some of the causes in Lakes. Moreover, roads that supply Lakes with food items are extremely bad, due to heavy rains and insecurity. High market prices and hyperinflation has have reduced the real incomes of wage laborers considerably. The situation of Pibor county is even worse than many other places of South Sudan. Malnutrition situation in Pibor County persisted high as showed by the recent SMART survey, i.e. GAM rate of 20.7% which is above WHO emergency threshold. In addition, Pibor was among the 11 counties which were predicted to fall into Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) if there is no multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance at scale following the January 2018 IPC. Plan International is trying to alleviate the impact of humanitarian crisis through implementation of OTP and TSFP in Rumbek Center and TSFP and BSFP in Pibor county. The programmes will be implemented in integration with local government and other partners working on nutrition and other sectors in the counties.
