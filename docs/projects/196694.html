Project Summary

through this project, Violet is planning to distribute regular and Emergency MPCs for the most vulnerable groups in and out of camps. through cash and voucher distribution, Violet aims to provide humanitarian assistance in a flexible and dignified approach. prior to the cash or voucher distribution, Violet will conduct the Rapid needs and Market assessment to verify the feasibility of cash/ voucher distribution, and beneficiaries' preferences, but also to avoid the harm that may occur as a result of the project actions.
Violet is planning to conduct the following activities:
- One-off emergency distribution of MPC (150 USD) for 10,000 households affected by the displacements and forced evacuations.
- 3 rounds of emergency MPC distribution (150 USD) for 2050 households who are at risk or inhabiting the camps and affected by natural disasters such as floods, heavy rainfalls, heavy snowfalls, or man-made disasters
- Distribution of cash for protection (100 USD) for 1000 households (5000 individuals) for 6 months.
- Distribution of regular MPC (100 USD) for 8 distribution rounds to 2000 vulnerable HHs without access to GFA to meet a variety of basic needs and reduce reliance on negative coping strategies
So far, there is no secured fund for the project at hand.

3 - Needs

The security situation in NWS remains highly dynamic and prone to escalation. Armed incidents and clashes are regularly reported around frontline areas and cause civilian casualties. According to HNO 23, # of PIN in Syria has increased from 14.6M to 15.3M since 2021. The impact of the economic crisis is manifesting itself in reduced access to and consumption of quality diets at the HHs level. With food reported as the top unmet need, families across Syria are continuously changing daily habits to maintain some level of food consumption. as for winterization, 2.5 M people in NWS are in need of winter assistance. Currently, the majority of the population cannot afford essential items due to high inflation and a collapsing economy. Millions are living in perilous conditions, including those who are living in dire conditions in camps with limited to no access to heating. Harsh winter conditions affect the most vulnerable, and access to basic NFIs is needed to ensure that health, well-being, and protection concerns are met. The crippling economy characterized by high inflation, currency depreciation and increase in price commodities remain of the biggest drivers of needs. It drives more people towards poverty, makes them more reliant on humanitarian assistance and increases the cost of response. It is creating a situation of in which some population movements in Syria are triggered by the search for better access to basic services and livelihood opportunities.

4 - Implementation of activities

through this project Violet aims to respond to the most vulnerable groups by distributing MPCs. the MPC is identified to be a flexible and dignified approach. prior tp the MPC distribution, Violet will ensure the markets are functioning and the necessary items are available to avoid the harm may occur as a result of the project actions. this will be done through conducting rapid needs and Market assessments in the targeted areas to check the feasibility of the modality. during the project implementation, Violet will ensure the COVID-19 preventive measures are applied and respected to avoid the spread of COVID-19. to this end, Violet will apply the SOPs developed by the Organization and train the staff on these preventive measures. Moreover, Violet will ensure effective CRM mechanism to provide equal access to the different groups to raise feedback and suggestion. further, Violet will activate the referral mechanism and refer special cases internally between the Organization's projects and externally with other organizations To ensure the coordinated response, Violet will coordinate its activities in both Turkey (through the cluster and CWG) and Syria level with the humanitarian actors to avoid the duplication Violet will ensure the readiness to immediately respond following the shocks by signing a frame work agreement with a Hawala agents. after the cash distribution, Violet will conduct the PDM to measure the beneficiaries satisfaction on the modality.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excludedLikelihood: MediumImpact: High M: Before implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services.2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from assistance or distribution. Likelihood: LowImpact: HighM: Information sharing and distribution points are suitable for PWDs, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance (transportation/delivery) for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to and from or in overcrowded distributions Likelihood: MediumImpact: High M:Selection of suitable distribution (time, location, accessibility), referral of special cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, operate with multiple distribution sites.4) At-risk group do not receive needed services Likelihood: MediumImpact: MediumM: Coordination with relevant sectors to avoid duplications, collaborate with local protection team to ensure equitable access to services, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, systematically use Washington Group Questions as tool to include disability in selection, consider intersectionality


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks: 1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the services Mitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staff Mitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded distribution sites and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation or delivery, participation of protection team in the distribution, if needed. Violet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitoring


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- inability to respond to the IDPs within the first phase of their displacementM: VO has an IDPs tracking initiative by setting communication channels with all LCs in the whole community which usually hosts IDPs, then once an emergency occurs, the LC will inform VO focal points to respond immediately 2: COVID-19 transmission as a result of the actionM: adjusting the activities & applying the preventive measures including sterilizing the Distribution sites, wearing masks during the distribution, screening the BNFs temperature before getting into the distribution site & supplying the distribution point with adequate hygiene solutions, and minimizing the number of BNF at the distribution site & setting different distribution sites at once to avoid the overcrowding 3: aid diversion and fees collection in return for Humanitarian assistanceM: VO will ensure the best Financial service provider is selected based on a transparent and competitive procurement process. before signing the contract, VO will vet the selected supplier on various software. VO will widely announce that humanitarian assistance is free of charge. additionally, VO will activate the CRM mechanism so the BNFs can report complaints regarding fees collection
