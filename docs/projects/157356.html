Project Summary

This project aims to address emergency protection needs of South Sudanese refugees. This protection intervention targets a total number of 70,525 South Sudanese refugees living in an open areas in Khartoum and North Darfur and in camp in White Nile States through integrated approach which will address protection , Child Protection and GBV risks, concerns and needs . The response will include; establish/strengthen and support community-based protection networks, protection ,CP and GBV awareness raising, and outreach (monthly session) in addition to establishment and strengthen referral pathways, through monthly coordination meeting, round table discussion with different stakeholders and mapping of existing services and services providers Community – based Psycho-social support also will be delivered through public campaigns and focus group discussion. Documentation and identification of PSNs will be a core activity under this project through direct assistance and referral to other service providers.
The community based approach will help to ensure the effectiveness of the delivered services as well as value for money .The project will lay foundation for resilience and sustainability through supporting the capacity of community protection networks in order to rollout the project activities with focus on community sensitization and awareness. The community leaders, local authorities, including Ministry of social welfare and COR will be closely involved, consulted and engaged in the project design and implementation. Smooth exit strategy will be elaborated to ensure the sustainability of the delivered services .

Needs

Sudan currently hosts over 406,995 South Sudanese refugees who have arrived in Sudan since December 2013, with over a 160,082 of whom arrived in the first half of 2017. Additionally, there are an estimated 350,000 South Sudanese refugees who remained in Sudan following the Independence of the Republic of South Sudan from Sudan in 2011. White Nile State currently hosts over 173,659 South Sudanese refugees who have arrived in Sudan since December 2013. Despite of signing of peace agreement in South Sudan, localized fighting, political instability and malnutrition are expected to prevent South Sudanese population from returning back.This remains an emergency, with women and children making up about 82 % of the refugee population. As refugees struggle to meet their basic needs in an increasingly difficult economic environment, with limited access to educational, trainings, livelihoods, services and assistance, women and children are at heightened risk of harassment, exploitation, neglect and abuse. As such, SGBV remains a very sensitive issue, further compounded by limited knowledge, information and understanding available on the matter. Some of the existing cultural and traditional practices are also known to encourage occurrences of SGBV.There is still little information available regarding the situation of PSNs in the camps but there are indications of individual cases that may require protection and assistance.On October NADA participated in joint needs assessment conducted by UNHCR ,OCHA,COR , HAC, Almanar, UMCOR, and CIS, in Khartoum state in addition to anther one in ND, which highlighted a crucial need for Individual Registration (IR), updated and detailed population data (at an individual level) is required to strengthen protection profiling and the identification of persons with specific needs, ensuring effective protection planning and targeted responses, particularly in the areas of child protection, education and SGBV. Consideration must be paid to women’s economic and overall protection needs based on the fact of high per cent of registered households are female-headed. Children are in great need to be enrolled in safe learning spaces and to be provided with urgent nutrition interventions. Nada organization conducted a quick survey through focus group discussion in open area- Khartoum locality and same findings were acknowledged. Additionally serious gaps in education, health & WASH services are reflected. Nada’s team stand on the hygiene and sanitation condition; it is very poor and if further deteriorated can cause disease outbreaks. The unsafe resident and big population in limited area affected the security and protection issues.The protection situation especially among children is disastrous.Communities expressed need to awareness raising sessions on human & child rights, personnel Hygiene,Reproductive health and SGBV.Moreover, livelihoods and self-reliance of refugees should be addressed in broader context of durable solutions.

Activities

- Identification of South Sudanese Refugees with special needs including unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs) in Jabal Auila Umbada, Alliet , Khuor Waral,Kashafa,Ridase1,Risase2 and UmSangor.-Strengthen the referral system for UASC, SGBV survivors , through mapping of services, coordination meeting with service providers, and signing of MoUs with relevant authorities.-Facilitate access to health, PSS, education and legal services through referral pathway-Establishment and training of 15 Community Based Protection Networks (50 members 50% women) -Organizing awareness raising campaigns /sessions on general protection issues, child protection and SGBV targets 20,000 persons (7,000 men, 13,000 women and children) in Jabal Auila, Al lait, and White Nile.-Organize 4 round table discussions for governmental officials - Protection awareness monthly sessions targets 1,260 persons-Establishment of referral pathways for SGBV cases and provision of and psycho social support (PSS) for vulnerable 100 SGBV survivors (50 persons 70% women) in Khartoum, White Nile-- Identification and support of 500 PSNs through vulnerability assessment, and provision of social protection support (referral, direct assistance), Referral services will be provided to 300, while direct support will be provided to 100 GBV survivors as well as through distribution of mobility aid and assistance devices and dignified clothes to anther 100 PSNs-Formulation and training of community complaint and Feedback mechanism: identifying and training of Nine community committees, each committee consists of 5 members form refugees and host community (2 in Khartoum,2 in North Darfur, and 5 in White Nile- Operating of 6 Multi-Purpose community centers in North Darfur to be used as a protective environment for refugees to host their protection initiatives, especially for women
