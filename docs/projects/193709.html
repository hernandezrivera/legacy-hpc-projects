Project Summary

The Rehabilitation of Substandard Shelters for 150 Vulnerable Families in Gaza program responds to the needs identified by the Shelter Cluster Proposed Objectives and the Needs and Priorities for HRP 2023 and will renovate substandard shelters (fully or partially) for 150 vulnerable families in Gaza to meet their shelter basic needs and minimum standards. The renovations will include adapting shelters to meet the needs of persons with disabilities (PWDs) and improving the living space of vulnerable groups. The project responds to the Shelter/NFIs Cluster’s Objective n. 2: “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity.” This is linked to HRP Strategic Objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL” .
The project’s overall objective is to ensure that 150 of the most vulnerable households (HHs), which Anera identified in current and previous needs assessments, are supported to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity. These HHs live in substandard housing conditions in Gaza without essential protection against the harsh climate and are exposed to humanitarian and protection concerns.
Anera will work with Unlimited Friend Association for Social Development (UFA), which will support the selection of beneficiaries through a detailed technical assessment and supervise rehabilitation works for vulnerable beneficiary HHs. They will assign data collectors to validate the selected beneficiaries based on vulnerability criteria approved by the Shelter Cluster and MoSD. Anera will finalize and approved the selection of beneficiaries on the standard shelter cluster criteria in coordination with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH).
Anera will select contractors to conduct the rehabilitation works through a transparent bidding process according to Anera’s procurement standards. Repair works typically include basic repairs of toilets and kitchens, repairs of roofs and replacement of asbestos ceilings, leakage and isolation works, and basic renovations of children’s bedrooms (lighting, windows, tiles, ventilation, and isolation). Anera will support HHs with members with disabilities with accessible facilities. In terms of PSEA related activities, Anera will provide a series of targeted PSEA sessions by Anera's PSEA focal point to project personnel, external collaborators (engineers, consulting firms, etc.) ahead of community engagement as well as during the implementation of the project. Anera will also provide PSEA sessions to 150 women/men distributed into 10 groups.
Anera succeeded to secure funding for the rehabilitation of substandard shelters for 50 vulnerable families in southern Gaza to be implemented in 18 months.

1 - Needs

Since 2007, Gaza has faced a blockade, the challenges of which are exacerbated by military operations that have devastated much of its infrastructure and forced 80% of its people to depend on international assistance. Gaza is caught in a vicious circle where interventions are typically restricted to immediate, urgent humanitarian and rehabilitation activities, with little resources left for sustainable recovery and long-term development needs. In 2021, unemployment remained high, with over half the workforce unemployed and 83% of workers receiving less than the minimum wage. As a result, poverty deepened. According to the World Bank, 59% of Gaza residents, half of whom are children, live in poverty. Meanwhile, food insecurity increased from 50% to 53% in Gaza. The overall socioeconomic conditions in Gaza, driven by the COVID-19 pandemic, political instability, increasing tensions, and a dire development situation, resulted in significant deterioration of living conditions. Even before the latest escalations, the unemployment rate in Gaza was 48 percent in the first quarter of 2021, poverty rates were above 50 percent, and approximately 80 percent of Palestinian HHs received some form of social assistance.Based on the HNO 2023, identified by the number and location, there are 21,500 existing housing units across Gaza that are classified as substandard and do not meet the minimum requirements of living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities, and privacy, requiring different degrees of rehabilitation. According to the 2021 Multisectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), 8.9% of the assessed HHs are at risk of eviction. In 28.9% of these cases, it is due to disputed ownership. Lack of affordability of tenants to cover rental costs accounts for 27.2% of cases, and inadequate shelter conditions account for 17.2% of cases. Additionally, the MSNA wash analysis revealed that 12% of Gazan HHs reported being affected by flooding in the three years before the MSNA data collection. Of these HHs, 41.2% reported that their shelter is impacted by flooding, and 87.1% reported that floods disrupt their daily activities. Moreover, the MSNA 2022 assessment indicated that 59.8% of the assessed HHs in the Gaza Strip reported existing shelter damage, defects, or issues at their shelters, such as cracks in some walls in 42.7% of cases, opening or cracks in the roof in 30.3% of cases, broken or cracked windows in 17.3%, lack of or bad conditions of kitchens in 12.7%, and lack of or bad conditions of bathrooms in 9.2%. Vulnerable HHs lack the resources to repair their houses, which leaves them subject to protection, safety, and health risks. Women and girls living in substandard, overcrowded, unsafe shelters with lack of privacy and limited access to hygiene facilities are highly exposed to the risk of gender-based violence (GBV). These conditions are likely to increase protection related concerns and consequent negative coping mechanisms such as early marriages and greater exposure to GBV-related risks, among others. Shelter interventions should consider gender and age factors in the design of the interventions to ensure that they respond to the needs of women, girls and children in terms of privacy, safety and hygiene concerns. 2 - Activities

2.1. Beneficiary selection and final validation of beneficiaries: Anera will implement the rehabilitation activities in partnership with UFA and in coordination with relevant government bodies, including the MoSD and the MoPWH. Beneficiary selection conducted by UFA, and finalized by Anera, will take into account the causes of substandard housing, such as poverty, vulnerability, and the recent hostilities. Through Anera’s work in shelter rehabilitation in the last two years, the team developed a list of hundreds of potential beneficiaries identified in coordination with partners. This includes the list of beneficiaries identified for a similar HH renovation project completed in 2022 and other interventions in Gaza including food assistance and WASH projects. Accordingly, Anera developed a detailed database identifying the most vulnerable areas and beneficiaries that contain or live in unfit and substandard housing units. Anera’s team, through its local partner, UFA, and in cooperation with the Shelter Cluster, the MoSD, and the MoPWH, will conduct damage assessments of identified HHs through field visits during the first two months of the project period to identify the exact rehabilitation needs. Anera will select beneficiaries in coordination with the the Shelter Cluster, the MoSD, and the MoPWH, based on the following criteria: Vulnerable families with annual income below the minimum wage. Family size of at least 5-6 persons. Priority given to female-headed HHs, families with PWDs, families with orphans, and/or families with pregnant or lactating female members. Priority given to families whose heads lost their jobs as a result of COVID-19 or the recent hostilities, such as those who work on daily basis wages or others who lost their source of income. Families whose houses were damaged due to flooding or as a result of the recent hostilities. Anera’s team will review potential beneficiaries and cross-check the names with the relevant key players in the shelter interventions to ensure that they are not receiving duplicate assistance from other parties. Anera’s selection process includes addressing any challenges, dividers, and connectors in the community and planning activities to address these challenges or to ensure that they do not cause harm on the community level. 2.2. Procurement: Once the project team selects beneficiaries and conducts field visits for damage assessments, Anera will start the competitive tendering process for rehabilitation works and materials needed for restoration activities. Anera’s team in partnership with UFA will identify the bill of quantities (BOQ) and technical specifications for procured material, which will be verified with the relevant partners. The team will select the contractors based on a transparent bidding process in line with Anera's procurement policy. 2.3. Training on PSEA: In terms of PSEA related activities, Anera will provide a series of targeted PSEA sessions managed by Anera's PSEA focal point to project personnel, external collaborators (engineers, consulting firms, etc.) ahead of community engagement as well as during the implementation of the project. Anera will also provide PSEA sessions to 150 women/men distributed into 10 groups. UFA will hire the PSEA trainers in consultation with Anera's focal point and will mobilize the arrangements of the sessions. Data collectors will try to identify potential PSEA and GBV needs to ensure that they are clearly addressed in these sessions. 2.4. Rehabilitation Works: This will include basic repairs works for toilets and kitchens, repairs of roofs and replacement of asbestos ceilings, leakage and isolation works, and basic renovations of children’s bedrooms (lighting, windows, tiles, and ventilation). Anera will support HHs with members with disabilities with accessible facilities. The project will also respond to the specific needs of the women and girls in the targeted families in terms of necessary repair works to ensure their safety, privacy, health, and hygiene. Anera ensures that all of its activities are guided by protection mainstreaming to protect the safety, dignity, and rights of the communities it serves and the beneficiary HHs. All Anera staff, including project staff, have been trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA to ensure gender-sensitivity, protection, and detection of abuse or gender violence. Anera and its partner, UFA, will provide targeted communities with information about the projects and provide them with safety guidelines during the implementation of construction activities. All Anera staff, including project staff, have been trained on PSEA to ensure gender-sensitivity, protection, and detection of abuse or gender violence.2.5. Monitoring and Evaluation: Anera ensures the quality and quantity of repair works to each beneficiary HH through follow-up and assessment visits to ensure that HHs have received the needed repair works to support their living conditions and dignity. During the first month of the project, Anera will prepare a monitoring matrix, where the project team will identify indicators at every level of the results chain and means of verification, as well as the frequency and responsibility of measuring indicators. 2.6. Handover: The beneficiaries will sign the handing over certificates after the contractor completes the repair works with the approval of Anera and the local partner. Anera will share the certificates with the Shelter Cluster and relevant ministries. 2.7. Evaluation: Anera will prepare a pre and post questionnaire to measure the needs according to the Shelter's standards, socio-economic conditions of the HHs, and eligibility of HHs against the cluster standards. The post questionnaire will also measure the quality of repair works and satisfaction of beneficiaries with the quality of work and the improvement in their living conditions. The results will be shared with Shelter Cluster focal points.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Anera puts in place complaint mechanisms for beneficiaries to provide feedback and address concerns throughout the project period. Anera informs its partners and beneficiaries of the available mechanisms and how to access them in the initial phase of the project. Complaint mechanisms will be made available to beneficiaries through three methods that include: 1. direct line (text or call) to the local partner organization which identified them for the project, 2. Direct line to the Project Coordinator at Anera, and 3. Locked complaint box at partner organizations and Anera where beneficiaries can submit complaints anonymously and confidentially. The first two methods are the most useful for registering a complaint or asking questions as they are immediate and can be dealt with in real time, whereas the third method of written complaints will be responded to within 48 hours of receiving them. Anera’s Whistleblower Policy provides further protections for beneficiaries, staff, and contractors who express concerns in need of being addressed. Anera will oversee the collection and analysis for each complaint. Additionally, all feedback and complaints are considered to be confidential. In order to submit feedback or complaints, individuals are encouraged to contact Anera’s outside counsel, as posted on our website (https://www.anera.org/whistleblower-policy/).

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Anera will conduct additional needs assessments in the implementation phase through surveys and home visits to assess the needs and capacity of each beneficiary HH and provide tailored assistance to support them in restoring their activities. Beneficiaries will continue to provide feedback throughout the installation period and Anera’s monitoring visits, which Anera uses to improve the project implementation process and ensure that it achieves its desired objectives and impact on the lives of beneficiaries.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Anera’s Accountability to Affected Populations (Operational Framework) summarizes the key concepts for making programming at the field level more accountable to affected populations. Anera is committed to strengthening its accountability to affected populations by committing to the following principles: program design and implementation are implemented in collaboration and with the support of the local community; conflict and gender sensitivity; the implementation of child protection policy; and producers and systems to address fraud and corruption risks. Prior to implementing any program, Anera works with local communities and families to discuss the challenges and needs in the community. Anera hires its staff from the local community where it operates and has strong networks that help to provide information, identify participants, and inform program design and methodology. Anera’s team selects its program participants and beneficiaries in consultation with community members and leaders. In addition, it selects many programs, especially infrastructure projects, in partnership with the local government and relevant ministries. Anera uses a mapping process to identify the most vulnerable communities lacking access to needed services. This mapping process pays special attention to women, youth, and marginalized/special needs members of the community as a main element in project development, planning and design, implementation, and function. Anera puts in place complaint mechanisms for all beneficiaries to provide feedback and address concerns throughout the project period.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Anera’s “Do No Harm” policies are woven throughout all of its program designs based on its principles of Accountability to Affected Populations document. For this project, Anera will ensure that beneficiaries, staff, and partners will be protected from contracting COVID-19 by taking all safety precautions and measures throughout all project phases. Anera also prioritizes the protection of all personal information of beneficiaries. For this project, Anera ensures that all of its activities are guided by protection mainstreaming to protect the safety, dignity, and rights of the communities it serves and monitors access and accountability from the project planning phase to implementation and monitoring through regular field visits and assessments. This includes ensuring adequate representation of vulnerable groups in the project design and monitoring. All Anera staff, including project staff, have been trained on PSEA to ensure gender-sensitivity, protection, and detection of abuse or gender violence. Anera operates a hotline for complaints and issues from beneficiaries, stakeholders, partner staff, or other concerned parties, which are dealt with as quickly as possible by Anera staff and escalated for further response when necessary. The principles of “Do No Harm” are included in the project as follows: 1. Anera’s needs assessment during the design phase includes addressing any challenges, dividers, and connectors in the community and planning activities to address these challenges or to ensure that they do not cause harm on the community level. Anera also conducts a risk analysis prior to the implementation of any project to identify mitigation methods and limit any potential risks on beneficiaries. 2. Anera’s local team and its close coordination of activities with local partners, local authorities, and international bodies and organizations ensures better understanding and consideration of local needs and increases the depth of interventions’ impacts. 3. Anera and its partner, UFA, will provide targeted communities with information about the projects and their impact on the community and provide them with safety guidelines during the implementation of construction activities. 3. The data of all beneficiaries involved in the activities in the targeted communities is protected and kept confidential. From the design phase, Anera works with local partners to identify the most vulnerable areas in need of interventions to increase their resilience and prevent them from the risks of seasonal weather conditions.
