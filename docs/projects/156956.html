Project Summary

The main objective of the proposed project is to protect the health of vulnerable and war affected people the south and north east of Syria by providing WASH assistance to vulnerable IDPs, returnees, host communities and school aged children in areas with high rates of displacement and large-scale destruction. The consequences of the conflict have been particularly severe and have converted many parts of the governorates into hard-to-reach areas, where people suffer from extreme needs, due to the enormous destruction, the lack of public services and the inability to access local markets. To support displaced families and also focus on war affected children, the project will contribute to increase their access to sufficient quantities of portable water for people’s and student’s daily consumption, to ensure their access to safe and dignified sanitation facilities, disseminate hygiene knowledge and create a clean environment. Therefore, the intervention will include the provision of water through water trucking and the rehabilitation of water and sewage network, the rehabilitation of existing WASH-facilities in schools, hygiene awareness trainings for girls and boys in schools and garbage collection. Those measures will serve to cover the daily water consumption, improve sanitation habits and reduce the risk of waterborne and other diseases.

Needs

The Syrian crisis has become the worst humanitarian crisis in modern history. Eight years of conflict and violence have caused extreme suffering, devastated many cities and regions in the country and destroyed large parts of the basic infrastructure. Almost 70% of the population lives in extreme poverty. 13.1 Million People in Syria require humanitarian assistance. The hardship in the northeast region of the country, is overwhelming and more severe than in all other Syrian governorates. The extreme devastation caused by numerous battles, inhabitants being cut off from national markets, limited services and infrastructure and the ongoing threat of armed confrontations and attacks has led inhabitants being in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. Similar conditions have caused approximately 600.000 people in need of humanitarian assistance in Daraa, 470.000 people in Ar-Raqqa, 530.000 people in Deir-ez-Zor, 1.2 Million People in Rural Damascus and 72.000 People in Quneitira. Regarding the water, sanitation and hygiene situation in Syria, tremendous needs are reported in many areas of the country. 6.1 Million People are in acute need of WASH assistance in Syria. Up to 35% of the population is relying on unsafe water sources to meet daily water supply needs. Access to basic water supply remains very limited and drinking water is mainly operated through private water trucking, which forces families to spend up to 15-20% of their incomes to secure access to a minimum amount of clean water. To support those families and relieve them from this enormous financial pressure, all WASH actors in Syria acknowledge the need for more sustainable solutions. Reports have also been received of typhoid outbreak and of severe cases of bloody diarrhea in Deir-ez-Zor, due to the consumption of unsafe water (c.f. OCHA (2018): Syria Crisis: Northeast Syria. Situation Report No. 27 (15 July 2018 – 31 August 2018). The water quality issues in 90% of all communities in Deir-ez-Zor and in wide parts of Daraa include bad smell, taste, and color. However, those are the alternative sources people have to resort to when water trucking services are too expensive or simply don’t reach their village (c.f. REACH (2018) Northeast Syria: Area-Based Assessment of Deir-ez-Zor Governorate. November 2018). Displaced families and returnees have limited access to safe and continuous water resources which makes it extremely hard for them to sustain basic hygiene practices. The needs for water supply in various districts across both governorates are massive and require an efficient emergency water trucking to protect the people’s health and reduce the risk of waterborne diseases. In addition to lack of water supply in different areas across the governorates, conflict affected communities in hard to reach areas remain with no connection to public garbage disposal services, therefore garbage collection campaigns are needed in these areas to help create a clean environment and reduce the risk of epidemic outbreaks. A large numberof schools have suffered severe destruction and currently are not able to provide appropriate WASH-services to their students (c.f. OCHA (2018) Humanitarian response Plan 2018. Syrian Arab Republic). Conflict affected and displaced school-aged girls and boys are in need of adequate WASH in their schools to ensure their access to minimal amount of water for their daily consumption, in addition to the need of educating them on how to maintain good hygiene routines and protect their health.

Implementation of activities

RSRP will secure a constant water supply through water trucking in communities with no access to public WASH-services, and to ensure access to safe and consumable water RSRP will rehabilitate water networks where renovation works are necessary, in addition to the rehabilitation of sewage networks to ensure its safe disposal, prevent contamination and contagious waterborne diseases in areas where sewage isn’t appropriate treated and removed. Garbage collection campaigns will be executed in extremely hard-to-reach communities and areas with high severity in needs with no access to public garbage-disposal services. Garbage cans will be provided and installed in the neighborhoods, garbage-collection trucks will be provided for an extensive garbage-collection operation and local community members will be hired to participate in these campaigns. RSRP will rehabilitate WASH facilities in 50 targeted schools in areas across the aforementioned governorates where the needs are urgent and lack of access to clean sanitation facilities in schools is creating barriers between children and their education. The works will comprise the restoration of the roof, walls, floors, windows and doors in the sanitation facility. Toilets, sinks, water taps, and doors will be fixed or replaced. Furthermore, civil works will be implemented to fix pipes and electrical wiring to guarantee access to the water supply and access to electricity. Student in the targeted schools will also receive a one-time hygiene awareness training. Therefore, WASH staff will hold theoretical and practical trainings with the students in each classroom and teach them the basics of a good hygiene routine and the correct use of hygiene products. In addition, female employees will hold menstruation management trainings for girls to instruct them in basic personal hygiene routines while dealing with their menstruation. Rebuild Syria Reconstruction Program (RSRP) will plan, manage, implement and monitor the Wash-interventions in close coordination with the local community, local authorities (MoWR and MoE) and the WASH cluster, to identify needs, determine the appropriate implementation plan with respect to the local cultural norms and traditions and to avoid any duplications with other implementing partners working in the area. RSRP already has organized and conducted many field visits to the area and held many interviews and meetings with key personnel, especially in Deir-ez-Zor, to recognize and assess the needs of the affected people across the governorate. In addition to assessments and field visits, staff members of the organization have conducted several focus groups sessions and meetings with the people in the mentioned areas which have helped in building profound trust and encouraged people to further communicate their needs and collaborate with the organization when delivering assistance. Based on the gained knowledge Rebuild Syria Reconstruction Program has developed this project-proposal. The accountability to the affected population will be guaranteed throughout the entire project cycle and will clearly be included in the planning and implementation process through the establishment of a complaints mechanism and the regular evaluation of feedback. The monitoring and evaluation process, conducted by experienced local and international staff, will significantly contribute to the project’s compliance with the 4 core humanitarian principles: Humanity, Neutrality, Impartiality and Independence. All the above-mentioned measures and processes will be translated in assessments, surveys, monitoring and evaluation reports and GPS embedded photos that will be shared with donors, partners and relevant clusters to participate in the humanitarian coordination process.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Reduced access for women and girls to the facilities (toilets represent GBV risk) and trainings due to culturally inappropriate design (lack of gender segregated facilities and trainings). – HIGH Mitigation Measure 1: Ensure the alignment of all facilities to SPHERE standards. Install gender specific sanitation facilities; install doors and locks on each facility, and every toilet cabin for privacy. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects.Use of male and female hygiene officers to conduct the trainings. Monitor the correct design and implementation during all project stages. Monitoring and Evaluation: Conduct regular monitoring and post rehabilitation monitoring to ensure equal access to sanitation facilities and include gender and age disaggregation within the projects targets.Required Resources: Monitoring and evaluation team, WASH project team. Protection Risk 2: Reduced access for disabled people/children to the assistance. – HIGH Mitigation Measure 2: Identification and inclusion of disabled persons and their specific needs into the project planning and implementation. Installation of handicapped accessible facilities.Monitoring and Evaluation: Conduct post rehabilitation monitoring to ensure equal access to sanitation facilities in schools.Required Resources: Monitoring and evaluation team, WASH project team. Protection Risk 3: Unequal distribution of services. Other vulnerable communities are excluded. – MEDIUMMitigation Measure 3: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Include community and authorities in targeting.Monitoring and Evaluation: Include in monitoring formats. Ensure 3rd party monitoring considers analysis of inadvertent benefits to opposed groups and consequences.Required Resources: Monitoring and evaluation team, WASH project team. Protection Risk 4: Misconduct by humanitarian workers while realizing the trainings. – LOW Mitigation Measure 4: Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection and develop strategies to address the risks of GBV during all project-stages. Selection of competent and professional hygiene officers and signing of a code of conduct by all humanitarian workers. Establishment of an effective complaint’s mechanism.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Access limitations could prevent the humanitarian actors to provide assistance to the affected population in a timely manner. – LOWMitigation Measure 1: Constant monitoring of the security situation on the ground and capacity to mobilize all resources into another project area if required. Risk 2: Threat to the safety of RSRP field-staff through confrontations between armed groups in the project region. – MED Mitigation Measure 2: Application of RSRP-internal Standard Operation Procedures (SOP) and daily evaluation of the security situation. Risk 3: Tension between included and excluded students and school-staff might lead to conflict between target groups and complaints against the humanitarian partner. – HIGHMitigation measure 3: Clear and comprehensible communication of the selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries. Risk 4: Significant population growth in the target areas leading to insufficient assistance. – HIGH Mitigation measure 4: Consider feedback from monitoring reports and situational overviews of the area and coordinate regular coordination meetings with local authorities for further and more detailed up-dates. In case of a change in the situation in DZ and Daraa, an open and direct dialogue with donors and other humanitarian actors should be initiated to increase capacities in the region. Risk 5: Delayed, denied or revoked GOS approvals which prevent delivering the assistance in a timely manner or prevent implementation of the project.Likelihood: HighMitigating Measures 5: Transparency with local authorities in the areas of intervention, in addition to strong communication and collaboration prior to project implementation with local partner. Risk 6: Unavailability of materials on the local markets due to closure of the border for commercial shipments or not renewal of SC resolution. - MEDIUMMitigation Measure 6: Detailed market analysis in the area of intervention and development of contingency plan. Risk 7: Depletion of the market and inflation of the prices. - MEDIUMMitigation Measure 7: Price monitoring and market assessments done by the RSRP finance and logistics department.
