Project Summary

Women for Justice and Equality (WOJE), proposed project will provide comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response interventions to the conflict affected communities in Bentiu and Malakal Counties in Upper Nile state. The proposal will contribute to the overall GBV cluster target of 546,000 persons reaching to 3,022 beneficiaries in Bentiu (400 boys; 252 men; 1,360 women, 1,010 girls) of Unity State and 2,150 beneficiaries in Malakal (220 boys, 932 girls, 148 men and 850 women) of Upper Nile State. Women for Justice and Equality WOJE, proposes to target 45% of this population as IDPs, 35% Returnees, and 20% as Host Communities during the implementation of comprehensive lifesaving SGBV prevention and response activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors. The target population will equally focus on the phase 1 response to returnees from Sudan following the fresh waves of conflict in Sudan.
The proposed project will aim to implement a comprehensive approach to GBV prevention, response, and risk mitigation. It will focus on delivering quality and timely age-appropriate case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors. Additionally, the project will extend cash assistance to the most vulnerable women and girls visiting Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) who face financial challenges. This support is intended to facilitate their access to multi-sectoral GBV services, prioritizing survivors in need of clinical management of rape (CMR) and those affected by Intimate Partner Violence (IPV). The initiative aims to ensure continuous access to health facilities, legal aid, and safe spaces providing psychosocial support.
The project's proposed activities will also include Strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group, GBV awareness and risk mitigation measures, Strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions, Strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of risk mitigation, GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support, through the provision of need based capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers and strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways. The activities in the established WGFS will be conducted using the guidelines from the ministry of Gender Child and Social Welfare. Women for Justice and Equality, WOJE also intends to strengthen gender-based violence prevention through EMAP and community care programs to reduce GBV risks on men, women, boys and girls.
According HNO, 9.4 million people will be in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan in 2023. Of these, 24% are women, 54% are children and 15% are people with disabilities. According to HNO, out of the 2.7 million people who need protection assistance related to gender-based violence (GBV), 33% are in a serious situation and 22% are at the extreme level while 14% are in catastrophic levels of severity
The targeted locations have been selected because of the prevalent needs stated in the HNO 2023 and appearing in category 4 in the severity mapping. WOJE, Women For Justice and Equality, wide experience of implementing GBV, Peace Building in Eastern Equatorial, through Safer world and in Partnership with Hope Restoration South Sudan through a consortium, WOJE plan to establish in Unity and Upper Nile to management of two (1) WGFS in Bulukut Returnee Center, Provide GBV violence risk mitigation in Schools in Malakal and implementation of the GBV Prevention and Response project in Bentiu.
WOJE will coordinate this proposed response with the GBV sub cluster to ensure that the proposed activities are not duplicated and will work with health partners such as IMC Malakal, Cordaid Bentiu, MSF, and Medair to refer survivors for Post Rape services

1. Needs

South Sudan accounts for the second highest prevalence case of GBV in the region; this was revealed during a validation workshop held on March 22, 2023, on the findings of a nationwide GBV prevalence survey in South Sudan.According to the WHO in 2018 study, shows South Sudan is the second after Uganda reported for high Intimate Partner violence (IPV) (41%, physical and/or sexual). With the support of UNFPA, the current study conducted by the Sudd Institute shows that the prevalence of IPV among married women aged 15-49 is rising (standing at 49.6%, physical and/or sexual). According to the study, substantial proportions of women (aged 15-64) in South Sudan experience GBV either in form of physical (34.0%) or sexual (13.5%) violence in their lifetime.Conflicts, climatic shocks, disease, and high maternal mortality have severely affected the people of South Sudan. The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that 9.4 million people will be in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan in 2023. Of these, 24% are women, 54% are children and 15% are people with disabilities. According to HNO, out of the 2.8 million people who need protection assistance related to gender-based violence (GBV), 33% are in a serious situation and 22% are at the extreme level while 14% are in catastrophic levels of severity.The GBViMS statistics (2023) reports that there has been an increase in GBV cases reported in the second half of 2023 compared to the same period in 2022. During the same period 97 per cent of reported cases in South Sudan affected women and girls, and 21 per cent of survivors were children, of which 79 per cent were adolescent girls. Physical violence, commonly by an intimate partner or someone known by the survivor, continues to be the most common form of GBV, accounting for 42 per cent of reported cases. According to the assessment Report conducted by Nonviolence Peaceforce, NP in September- October 2023, over 281,129 individuals are crossing into South Sudan with 82% arriving in Upper Nile state as of 22 September 2023. Upper Nile state is populated by a mix of ethnic groups and is divided into twelve counties: Ulang, Nasir, Maiwut, Longochuk, and Maban, which are generally considered Nuer lands; Panyikang, Fashoda, Manyo and Malakal, which are generally considered Shilluk lands; and Baliet, Melut and Renk generally considered Dinka lands. This desperate situation has increased the GBV needs in the proposed counties which remain severe as violence and violations persist as a reality of daily life for girls, women and children. The increased presence of mixed populations such as returnees and IDPs has exerted pressure on the few services available in these locations thereby exacerbating protection risks and increasing the need for GBV prevention and response interventions. According to Fangak IRNA report September 2022. The GBViMS statistics (2022) further indicate that 97% of the reported GBV incidents survivors were females (women and girls) with intimate partners accounting for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Access to GBV services for the targeted locations remain a major challenge owing to the limited availability of GBV prevention and response services. The prevalence of gender-based violence (GBV) in Bentiu and Malakal is deeply entrenched in cultural beliefs, attitudes, and perceptions that perpetuate an unequal power dynamic between men and women. These cultural factors contribute significantly to the normalization of violence against women and girls within the agro-pastoral communities. Moreover, survivors face a considerable barrier in accessing available services due to the pervasive fear of retaliation through revenge killings. Women For Justice and Equality, WOJE will aims to address and transform these cultural norms and beliefs to mitigate the risk factors associated with GBV in Bentiu and Malakal, fostering a safer and more equitable.
