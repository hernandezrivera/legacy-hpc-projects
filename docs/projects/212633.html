Project Summary

As of December 2023, 4.8 million IDPs reside in NWS, and more than 4.1 milion people in need. Access to essentials like safe water, food, medicine, healthcare, and livelihoods is limited. According to REACH Needs Assessment 2023, food and livelihood were reported as the top unmet needs by internally displaced persons (IDPs) and resident/host community members, with 43 per cent of households lacking income and work opportunities. Households in NWS more often reported that unemployment was a challenge in meeting basic needs.
Food insecure households are also highly likely to adopt food-related coping strategies, including reducing the number of meals and the size of portions. Additionally, the ever-increasing prices of food and other necessities further compounds the vulnerability of households. The Cash Working Group (CWG) has reported that the price of the food component of the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) had increased by 40 per cent (NWS CWG REACH market monitoring, Feb 2024).
IOM plans to implement the following activities:
1) Deliver emergency MPC to the most vulnerable to cover their most urgent needs immediately following a shock; and
2) Distribute regular MPC*to the most vulnerable households to meet a variety of basic needs and reduce reliance on negative coping strategies.
Beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerability criteria from CWG. Based on the CWG’s recommendations, IOM will ensure through third party monitoirng (TPM) that the IPs follow 'No Harm' principles during the distributions. A rapid market capacity check shall be undertaken by the IP prior to each distribution to attest those targeted markets can absorb the demand for goods and replenish supplies. Three to four weeks after the distribution, PDM activities will be coordinated between the partner’s M&E unit and IOM’s TPM.
IOM will use PTT to first transfer resources from Turkey to PTT offices in NWS, and then utilize a Financial Service Provider (FSP) to transfer the money to the chosen areas. Following CWG recommendations, cash assistance is provided in USD as standard. When USD notes are insufficient in quantity or quality, beneficiaries have the option to select the currency in which the remaining amount can be received.
The distribution of cash assistance shall be organized in full by IPs with the announcement to beneficiaries, organization of the premises, registration, data control, distribution, accounting, and security staff. The role of the FSP’s transfer agent will be only to hand the money to the selected beneficiaries under supervision of the IP upon verification of identity by the IP.
All IP staff are required to have undertaken training on PSEA and signed a PSEA Code of Conduct. Details of IOM’s AAP mechanism are communicated to participants, as well as the Interagency PSEA hotline where appropriate. Gender segregated lines and gender-mixed distribution teams are always used by IPs to further mitigate SEA risks. As IOM is the leading agency for PSEA, all programmes are required to follow the network’s recommendations and incorporate them into each project.
In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will be in regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents and conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations. IOM also has a dedicated access unit that monitors and facilitates principled access for partners within Syria, and IOM will continue to participate in OCHA’s access working group. To mitigate the impact of inflation on cash recipients, IOM will conduct strong and constant market monitoring and will distribute USD to prevent currency devaluation. Additionally, there is the risk that beneficiaries will use cash for unintended purposes which IOM will mitigate by tailoring cash to SMEB needs and by raising awareness of the intended use of cash to beneficiaries during distributions.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Following the Northwest Syria Cash and Voucher Assistance Risk and Mitigation Matrix, the following risks and mitigation measures were identified for this project: 1) Smaller USD denominations - such as 5, 10, 20, and 50 USD banknotes - are unavailable or are of poor quality. The risk has a high probability of occurring and in order to mitigate IOM will give adequate notice to the FSP so that the FSP has enough time to gather banknotes in the required denominations. In line with the CWG recommendations, IOM will discuss with the selected IPs provide the TRY equivalent of smaller USD denominations to beneficiaries when the latter are not available. 2) Another risk that might be resulted in the delayed implementation and increase the costs is that FSPs increase the cost of services to account for new safety and security measures. To mitigate this risk in the beginning of the project implementation IOM will sign the agreement with the FTS to fix the service fee through the project. 3) In order to avoid any social tensions as a result of uncoordinated CVA programme targeting, IOM will share information develop a clear SoPs that will be shared with the IP staff with the instructions to inform immediately about any CVA programme. IOM will coordinate closely project activities with the CWG to avoid any duplications in the assistance provided.
