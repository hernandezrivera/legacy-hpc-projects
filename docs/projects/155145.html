Needs

Forcibly displaced Myanmar nationals living in congested and poor shelter condition in cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, specifically focusing intervention in Camp 8E.The Need and Population Monitoring Round 12 report indicates an average area of 18.76 sq.m per person, significantly lower than the sphere standard recommended for site planning of 45 sq.m per person. Most shelters upgraded in June 2018, before the hurricane season, do not adhere to the covered living space of 3.5 sq. m per person.The shelter materials and disaster risk reduction training provided to the displaced population in 2018 are insufficient for protection against adverse weather conditions. Recent monsoon season had weakened the shelters. Bamboo has deteriorated due to beetle infestation, resulting in a situation in which shelters can easily be broken into. Several such cases resulting in thefts were reported. Female key informants also emphasized the discomfort in existing shelters due to humidity, especially during the hottest portions of the day. Families’ main concerns regarding their current shelters were related to strength of shelters, quality of materials, lack of private cooking and bathing spaces, and lack of ventilation and privacy.It was reported by Shelter Cluster in Auguat that:• More than 78% of HH assessed had purchased additional bamboo, tarpaulin, and rope.• 62% of shelters visited during technical visits had no ventilation.40% of shelters visited during technical visits had an individual bathing facility inside the shelter or connected to it.

Activities or outputs

- Mobilization, awareness, training of selected community members, involving women, to work as committee site and shelter safety watch group- Registration of the selected population in the camp for transitional shelter participation, training, distribution of materials, and construciton supervisions- Technical training provided to community selected carpenters and key household members on transitional shelter construction and disaster risk reduction- Procurement of durable shelter materials. This includes: (1) Guiding and supporting some local metal fabricators in the surrounding host community to produce metal foundation elements to supply the demand of the displaced community; (2) organizing women group in the discplaced community to fabricate brick clay stoves to match each shelters; (3) train and provide work in the displaced community, involving women, to produce bamboo wall mats to support vulnerable families in building their transitional shelters- Distribution of key essential structural materials (foundation units, and treated borak bamboo) for the transitional shelter construction- Construction and supervision of transtional shelters- Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring survey 2 months after construction
