Project Summary

This proposed project is targeting Rohingya refugee and host communities in the affected areas of Ukhiya and Teknaf to ensure lifesaving GBV services and prevention activities to mitigate the GBV. The interventions will ensure the survivors centered approach to offer comprehensive GBV case management services, integrated SRH-GBV services, GBV prevention activities, PSS group activities, individual PSS support, MHPSS support for GBV survivors and women and girls at risk. Adolescent specific curriculum will be implemented to build life skill of the adolescent girls. GBV prevention messages, activities, lifesaving information, referral services to other service points for GBV and NON GBV survivors, structured skill development programme for women and girls through women lead Community centers, engaging men and boys through WLCC to GBV prevention programme, social mobilization activities through SASA methodology will be ensured in 20 locations of host and refugee setting. GBV response programme will be ensured through women friendly spaces, and WLCC will be ensuring women empowerment through curricula based. This project will have a focus on developing community-based protection groups, empowering community to provide a safe community for their women and girls. The project will target to include high risk populations and marginalized groups into all the activities of GBV prevention and response. This year the project will expand into 16 new locations of host and refugee setting, therefore, a total of 39 locations the GBV response and prevention programmes will be implemented, where 10 WLCC offers training, and skill development programmes, engaging men and boys family mebers of trainees in GBV prevention programme through the centers. Knowledge on disaster management, strengthening the facilities/service points to ensure the service delivery throughout disaster, activating emergency response unit (PERU, MMT) will be ensured.
The proposed project will be coordinating with GBVSS , Child Protection Sectors, Livelihood sectors, site management, Sexual and Reproductive Health Working groups, GBViMS, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) working groups, Communication With Communities Working Groups, Gender in Humanitarian Assistance (GiHA) etc to ensure staff capacity, services, avoiding duplication, ensure referral pathway on a timely manner.
In the overall project phase, data will be collected in a safe and ethical manner, Kobo software will be utilized for regular data collection, while GBVIMS and GBvIMS primero will be used for GBV data gathering. Client satisfaction surveys will be conducted with GBV survivors through GBV case management services, while non GBV survivors and trainees of the women lead community centers will also be part of this survey to understand about the satisfaction level of the services they receive. Regular safety audits, Community watch Group Meeting, Women Support groups interactive meeting with Camp Coordination groups will be conducted, to ensure community taking the lead of their own issues. Skill assessment of the GBV case management staffs, Life skill facilitators and of the staffs who will be implementing SASA together will be done. Standard tools to be used to assess MHPSS services and activities.
UNFPA will continue to pursue internal and external measures to protect beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by members of the humanitarian community. Internally, UNFPA shall continue training all staff, Externally, sensitizes partners and others working in the humanitarian community about PSEA. UNFPA will strengthen prevention and response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by ensuring availability of functional PSEA reporting and response mechanisms for UNFPA and its Partners.

Needs

GBV continues to be a constant threat to women and girls in refugee camps and in host communities The reported types of GBV incidents include rape, physical assault, sexual violence, forced marriage, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. According to the GBVIMS data, domestic violence constitutes 76% of total reported GBV cases (GBVIMS report of June 2019). Considering that GBV is generally under-reported, the recorded cases represent only a small fraction of the overall caseload. The JRP midterm review shows only 26% of GBV service entry points integrated health activities, and 29% of survivors who reported rape were referred and received medical care within 72 hrs. According to the IASC pyramid for MHPSS in emergency settings, the majority of the mental health and psychosocial services available in Cox’s Bazar are located at the community level. In the non-specialized interventions, there is a significant gap in the provision of specialized services that are targeting risk groups as youth and mainstreaming psychosocial considerations in the provision of basic services. Increased integration of GBV, SRHR, and MHPSS services is needed, along with community awareness on these services and the importance of 72-hour treatment. 2018 Women’s Refugee Commission report and recent assessments shows Inadequate or un-available services for Rohingya male survivors of sexual violence have also been identified a crucial gap in the response, building upon the. Likewise, GBV service gaps have been identified for groups with other diversity of need including persons with disabilities and LGBTI groups such as the Hijra (third gender) community. FGDs with persons with disabilities and the Hijra community have indicated a need for targeted approaches around accessing health, MHPSS and other life-saving GBV services.Recently assessed refugee households in the camps, 57% were unable to identify any of the GBV service points, while 85% mentioned ‘Majihis’ as their preferred point of referral for cases of sexual violence (ISCG MSNA August 2019). These findings raise concerns given that the Mahji system is highly male-dominated and often criticized for being exploitative. As such, upscaling awareness-raising on GBV services and safe referral points and intensifying behavioral change programs to address socio-cultural barriers remains a priority. lower percentage of community representatives engaged in activities promoting gender equality shows 12% of camps with community representation inclusive of female leaders according to JRP midterm review. Therefore, activities to promote community engagement , specially female in leadership will bring accountability, and there is a need of it. Host community have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to serious protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited access to livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services, and existing bottlenecks in the formal and informal justice system limit access to legal remedies. There are lack of opportunities and capacity building activities around existing skills among the community, especially for women and girls. Women's empowerment and life-skills training, linked with awareness raising campaigns engaging and sensitizing men on the benefits of allowing women to participate, are highly needed. Increased overcrowding and limited privacy across all refugee sites are elevating safety and security risks for women and girls. It while in many of FGD Many Rohingya refugee family units are female-headed (up to one in five), exhibiting even greater vulnerability. The burden of care women assume for children and others can make it difficult for them to take proper care of themselves, women often neglect their own needs as they care for their families and neighbors. There is a need for safe women-centered healing spaces that are accessible to all women and girls, especially including the most vulnerable.

Activities or outputs

1. Ensure continuity of the 23 UNFPA-supported WFS and finalize the setup of sixteen (16) more WFS. 2. Quality assurance of all lifesaving services delivered through the WFS, including case management, clinical management of rape, family planning services and counselling, STI prevention and response, Mental health and psychosocial support, access to services through functional referral pathways. 3. Ensure GBV mainstreaming in all UNFPA-supported Health Facilities, including direct GBV service linkage, outreach and information dissemination about available services for women and girls, and safe and ethical referrals of GBV survivors by health staff. 4. Continue training GBV service providers on GBV case management, GBVIMS, Caring of Child Survivors (specially to work with adolescent girls) and psychosocial support services, engaging men and boys, Ensure specialized MHPSS capacity building to provide specialized MHPSS services to women and girls, and Men and Boys. Ensure mentoring , coaching and supervision for case management service provision. Continue training for Social mobilizers on GBV, SRH, Facilitation, communication skills and on SASA methodology. Continue training of the life skill facilitators on facilitation, communication, GBV, SRH, literacy, and language skills. Train health personnel on GBV guiding principles and referral pathways. Train community volunteers to expand their support for operations in WFS and Health Facilities on GBV prevention, response and referral pathways. Train Community Activists to expand support for GBV prevention activities and connect women and girls with WFS and WLCCs (Women lead Community centers). Engage in capacity development opportunities for national governmental institutions. Engage national media partners in developing stories that cover women, girls, and survivors with dignity. Ensure staff capacity for inclusion of people with disability and LGBT into GBV prevention and response activities. Ensure staff capacity on ethical and safe data collection, to ensure accountability to the participants of the GBV porgramme. 5. Expand engagement with communities, including men and boys, for GBV prevention and response. 6. Distribute Dignity Kits and activate an emergency response unit following natural disasters on affected communities. 7. Operationalize the existing 10 UNFPA-supported Women Led Community Centres (WLCC), providing life-skills and empowerment training through a structured curriculum for women and girls. Integrate psychosocial support into all WLCC services including men and boys engagement. 8. Operate the current GBVSS , GBVIMS activities, GBV mainstreaming in other sectors, ensure Primero is implemented for the partners, ensure advocacy and mainstreaming GBV through GBVSS partners, enhancing engagement with other sectors and government for integration of GBV prevention, mitigation and response programming.
