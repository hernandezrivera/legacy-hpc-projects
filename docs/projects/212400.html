Project Summary

Needs : Across Syria, over 6,8 million persons are expected to be in need of shelter assistance in 2024, thus a 20 per cent increase since 2023, as per the 2024 HNO. After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023After the earthquake 160,000 buildings collapsed, destroying homes and water, sanitation and hygiene infrastructures. This left many people extremely vulnerable. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) in March, 1.7 million people are still living in informal settlements, mainly in tents, in extremely difficult living conditions. In NWS, nearly 1.7 million people live across over 1,300 displacement sites spread across numerous camp clusters in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, highly vulnerable to adverse weather, including snowstorms, strong winds, and floods. In January 2023, snowstorms and floods affected a lot of displacements sites in NWS, resulting in damage to nearly thousands of tents and the complete destruction of about a thousand tents. Shelter destruction and limited livelihoods to conduct necessary repairs put already vulnerable populations at risk, especially in winter. Increasing shelter adequacy issues, such as limited electricity/lighting, poor insulation from heat/cold and lack of privacy is exposing households to harsh weather conditions with a high risk of loss of life, particularly during winter.
Activities : The proposed project aims at addressing emergency and more sustainable shelter needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northwest Syria.
The intervention will target a total of 9,570 beneficiaries under shelter activities. Acted will target 6,570 beneficiaries in camps and 3,000 out of camps. The project will target 7,770 beneficiaries in NES and 1800 in NWS. To note, some beneficiaries will receive more than one type of assistance between NFI and Shelter sectors.
In order reduce the need of tent maintenance and regular replacement in the context of decreasing humanitarian funding, Acted will pilot semi-sustainable shelter structures for 120 households (HH) (720 individuals) in Serakaniye camp (A1.1). The activity will be carried out through a contracted supplier. Simultaneously, Acted will provide light rehabilitation of shelters for 200 HHs (1200 individuals) in out of camp setting to IDPs and host communities, benefitting 1,200 individuals in Kisreh sub-district (A1.2). The rehabilitation work are anticipated to include (but not limited to): repair or installation of windows, doors, repairs, or installation of walls, plastering works, water tank with stand and a ceramic filter installation A contracted supplier will conduct the light rehabilitation works, targeting vulnerable households with non-structural damage to the shelters, requiring winterization, and improved protection.
Also, in an effort to improve access to dignified shelters for IDPs, Acted will provide support on the light rehabilitation of 300 earthquake-affected houses in Harim district, through contracted suppliers, benefitting estimated 1,800 individuals.
Acted extended emergency shelter assistance to reduce safety risks of residents, by conducting insulation upgrades for 5,850 individuals (975 HHs) in Abu Khashab camp.
PSEA : All Acted staff receive induction and regular refresher training on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect Acted Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. Acted staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents.
