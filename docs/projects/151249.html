Project Summary

Somalia faces a protracted humanitarian crisis, with more than 4.6 million people acutely food insecure, 2.6 million of them are internally displaced people (Fsnau September 2018). Decades of conflict have destroyed infrastructure, on which lives and livelihoods depend, such as water, soil, land and forest resources, making Somalis increasingly vulnerable to recurrent climatic shocks. Restoring the farmland productive capacity of Somalis to better prepare for, respond to and recover from shocks is a precursor to improving food security and nutrition, as well as reducing future humanitarian needs. Agro-pastorals and riverine farmers are central to the equation. This project is therefore aimed at enhancing the capacity of the rainfed and riverine farmers to rebuild their productive assets and reconstruct their capacity to absorb the recurrent climatic shocks. Direct target beneficiaries of project are 4500 households of agro-pastoral and 3500 riverine communities in Jalalaqsi district and will be provided farm inputs to 4500 HHs of agropastoral (15kg sorghum +5kg cowpea) and 3500 HHs of riverine (20kg maize+5kg cowpea) farmers . it will also provided vegetable seeds and tuber crops to 1500 HHs of riverine farmers ( 20grm tomato, 20gram pepper, 20grm onion, 20gr of kale and 40-50 vine of sweet potatos) as well as training of 2000 farmers on composting, water harvesting techniques and minimal tillage.

Needs

Somalia faces a protracted humanitarian crisis, with more than 4.6 million people acutely food insecure, 2.6 of these are internally displaced people (Fsnau September 2018). Decades of conflict have destroyed infrastructure, on which lives, and livelihoods depend, such as water, soil, land and forest resources, making Somalis increasingly vulnerable to recurrent climatic shocks. Restoring the farmland productive capacity of Somalis to better prepare for, respond to and recover from shocks is a precursor to improving food security and nutrition, as well as reducing future humanitarian needs. In the project area of Jalalaqsi district, food security and nutrition among agro-pastoral and revirine communities are critical and recent GAM and SAM estimates were 15.7 and 3.3 respectively. The poor nutrition situation in jalalaqsi is mainly attributed to poor household food security in addition to a lack of access to adequate health and humanitarian interventions due to blockage of insurgents. This project is therefore aimed at enhancing the capacity of the rainfed and riverine farmers to rebuild their productive assets and reconstruct their capacity to absorb the recurrent climatic shocks. The project provides farm inputs, animal drawn tools, water pumps, and extension services to the affected farmers. It also provides training on soil fertility amendments and good farming practices for ensuring sustainable food production in agro-pastoral and riverine livelihood zones of Jalalaqsi district. Target beneficiaries are 4500 households of agro-pastoral and 3500 riverine communities in Jalalaqsi district; the project intends to target at least 60% of women in the project areas, however, the project will support indirectly to pastoral communities and Livestock within the target communities will have access to crop residues and pasture of restored ecosystem. SOADO will address on the underlying causes of food insecurity and restoring farmland productivity of target areas

Activities

1. Restore productive capacity of agropastoral and riverine community through provision of farm inputs to 4500 HHs of agropastoral (15kg sorghum +5kg cowpea) and 3500 HHs of riverine (20kg maize+5kg cowpea) farmers in the project target areas2. Diversify livelihood and income from agriculture production to reduce vulnerability to shocks, with particular focus on women and youth (vegetable seeds and tuber crops) and target 1500 HHs of riverine farmers ( 20grm tomato, 20gram pepper, 20grm onion, 20gr of kale and 40-50 vine of sweet potatoes)3. Improve productive capacity of farmland ecosystem through soil fertility management training to the target farmers on composting & water harvesting techniques and minimal tillage for targeting 2000HHs and train them on Good agricultural practices and composting techniques for soil improvement. 4. provision of single water pumps to 250 HHs in riverine and animal drawn tools to 500HHs in rainfed areas of the project target areas.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project incorporates protection principles and promotes meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid; it also ensures that protective impact of aid programming is maximized. The target community is agropastoral and riverine residing along the shabelle riverbank, and Government and Amison troops control the target area and the risk of armed violence is minimized.Within this framework the security situation in Jalalaqsi remains stable and predictable. The road between Mogadishu and Jalalaqsi is open and accessible now and supply can reach safely to the Jalalaqsi district and other project target areas villages. For risks related to the security situation, SOADO has developed a Contingency Planning Process which contains an analysis of the risk management process.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project incorporates protection principles and promotes meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid; it also ensures that protective impact of aid programming is maximized The project directly addresses Do no Harm principles by ensuring extensive communication with the community (including leaders, women, youth, and children) to determine the needs that can best be supported by SOADO in the implementation period. Additionally, issues such as the hiring of staff, the procurement of materials, and the distribution of farm input and other items will be looked at in a manner that directly address the context and maintains the Do no Harm principles by not discriminating against any specific populations/clan or group and by being aware of the current situation of the project target areas.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project incorporates protection principles and promotes meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid; it also ensures that protective impact of aid programming is maximized The project directly addresses Do no Harm principles by ensuring extensive communication with the community (including leaders, women, youth, and children) to determine the needs that can best be supported by SOADO in the implementation period. The project will also increase access to farm input services by increasing number of visits by mobile teams to remote communities, so that the vulnerable women and children are not exposed to dangers, including sexual violation as they travel long distances in search of assistance
