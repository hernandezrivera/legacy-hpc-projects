Project Summary

Access to Nutrition, health, WASH and other social services is limited for all populations in Somalia, including IDPs and Host communities. There is a critical shortage of qualified Nutrition or health care workers and other personnel involved in basic service provision. In this context, IDPs and Vulnerable host community hardly have any access to already limited services. To supplement existing services and ensure that the vulnerable communities have access to basic services, DMO, with support from its partners (UNICEF/WVI/WFP), provides Nutrition and health services to IDPs and vulnerable host communities. In addition to Nutrition and health, DMO also provides additional assistance to persons with specific needs through supplementary feeding. Due to the overall situation in Somalia, durable solutions are very limited. Most of the IDPs do not wish to return back to their region/District of origin due to prevalent security risks and fear of persecution. Besides, there is no national framework to facilitate the legal integration of IDPs. In the areas of return, access to basic services remains a significant challenge. Vulnerable returnee families are often lacking basic household items and shelter. Access to food is also a challenge, despite the existence of coping mechanism in the community of origin and the fact that splitting families (with some of them still remaining in the IDPs camp in major towns may in fact mitigate adverse consequences. Nutrition and Health services are significantly poor (if compared to the standards in the IDPs camps), or totally lacking. . This is confirmed by assessments conducted in Mid-2018. These also highlighted lack of employment opportunities. Many IDPs have acquired skills, but the opportunities for gainful employment in the major towns are lacking. Interestingly, women find it easier to find informal work, as cleaners etc., than men. Access to healthcare, Nutrition, livelihoods, shelter and social services is limited for all populations in Somalia including all persons of concern but in particular for IDPs, as confirmed by participatory assessments undertaken in March 2018 in Baidoa IDP settlements. There’s a critical shortage of basic services and most IDPs continue to have limited or no access to quality health care, Nutrition, permanent land and housing, basic domestic items, food and safe drinking water. Many spend what little income they earn on water trucking. Most IDPs families also require support in the form of basic seasonal non-food items, especially during rainy seasons. Essential and lifesaving medical services are insufficient and overstretched, including critical public health, nutrition and water services, increasing the risk of a public health emergency. Delivery of life saving assistance has been irregular due to insecurity, road inaccessibility, and interruptions of the supply chain.
Response with emergency Health, nutrition and shelter to the displaced persons in Baidoa According to the clusters partners in Baidoa, an estimated 32,000 IDP households need emergency nutrition, health and shelter assistance.
This project would be able to respond to those households that are affected by drought, Conflict and displacement from district all over South west state as well as the new arrivals who come to the district with very malnourished children from various locations outside the towns.
The recent assessment conducted by CCCM clusters with the support of the nutrition and health clusters as well as CCCM cluster members has verified 371 IDPs from previously 377 IDPs thus the need is more like the previous which makes this program very crucial.
DMO since its inception has been helping international organization especially those concerned on areas of mother and child welfare developments through partnerships in program implementation as well as technical and information sharing.
It has been Implementing health and nutrition projects with UNICEF/WVI/WFP especially emphasis in areas like Immunization, systematic treatment(OTP), and promotion breast feeding practices, food demonstration/Fortification and nutrient statue measuring Baidoa district areas and it currently added its coverage areas Berdale, Diinsor and Burhakaba in which we have opened offices.
Deeg-roor Medical Organization in the last 9 months reported the number of patients consulted to be 60968; 10221 PNC 11,205 ANC visits with 492 deliveries by SBA, 18925 U5, Over5 20617; a total of 3,956 children we immunized of which 955 <1 children were immunized for measles; A total 68,456 of < 5 children were screened for malnutrition; of which 56,169 were admitted of having MAM; and 3046 were admitted of having SAM. The project aims to provide routine and campaign immunization to under 5 children and Women of child bearing age; Provision of ANC/PNC care to PLW. Treatment and control of communicable diseases targeting (boys, girls, PLW, WCBA and men); Provision of basic lifesaving medical services to sick patients including AWD/Cholera patients; Treatment of uncomplicated malnourished cases; Identify and admit patients with severe medical complications; Promote the adoption of good hygiene and sanitation practices to the affected communities; Breast feeding promotion and support of IYCF; Identify and assist in the training of community volunteers on health promotion and education; Organize community sensitization and mobilization sessions at facility and outreach centres; Support distribution of hygiene kits if kits are made available; Provision of disinfected water, Treatment of MAM and SAM without medical complication
This project would focus on the drought affected communities (i.e. boys, girls, men and women) in Bay region. The project will focus and give more attention to pregnant women, women of child bearing age and children under five years of age. Special attention will be given AWD/Cholera treatment centres and strengthen communicable disease surveillance system in all health facilities and health mobile teams.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Deeg-Roor Medical Organization (DMO) would collect gender-disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to Nutrition, Health and WASH activities. Deeg-Roor Medical Organization (DMO) will ensure that all segments of the affected population including women have equal access to nutrition, Health and WASH assistance and that targeted support to advance gender equality is based on a gender analysis. The nutrition, Health and WASH program will meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men to generate positive and sustainable outcomes.Deeg-Roor Medical Organization (DMO) shall apply gender segregated community consultation meetings enabling women and girls to address their specific concerns, prioritize their needs and appropriately inform the development of proposed activities. Deeg-Roor Medical Organization (DMO) would endeavor to provide equal chance, inclusiveness and participation of minority and majority community members as well as disabled groups in the district/s and be able to be transparent enough in terms of employment, capacity building, empowerment and resource sharing hence anticipate to foster comprehensive community engagement in the target locations.DMO will use a new approach that will engage communities emphasizing on inclusiveness of marginalized groups and individuals, effective participation, gender sensitivity, empowerment and sustainability. Selection criteria for committee members will reinforce these principles by requiring a minimum representation of (40%) in women as well as marginalized groups, such as IDPs and minority clans. In accordance and compliance with (SPHERE) core standard people cantered interventions, DMO will seek to empower and develop action cantered activities in the project to include women in the communities in the four phases of project cycle which are designing, planning, implementation and evaluation, Women marginalization is rampant in the Somalia and target community. Women are not well represented in decision making, employment and receipt of aid and other important issues which affect their daily life extending to the children.At the end of the project, a strong cadre of community-based structure will be built for service delivery. Community based participatory approach will be employed through strengthening community capacity by capacity building own staff, MoH staff, establishing and training CBWs and CHWs. DMO shall gradually designate CBWs, CHWs to continue the delivery of basic services e.g. screening, including preventive, protective and promotive actions, and their roles and responsibilities will be transformed, from direct implementation to technical support, through continuous support on knowledge transfer and capacity development activities, monitoring and coordination within the existing structures.This participatory and community-cantered approach will ensure community ownership and sustainability. Furthermore, to enhance information sharing and coordination.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

DMO has developed a risk policy and risk register which are now helping the organization mitigate risks that are faced by our organization as well as the target population.Some of the risk that are faced by the target population is displacement without an alternative option that’s mainly caused be settling in private land which the owner may claim for it. We are mitigating this by advising the IDPs and the Host community to request the owner to give them a tenure of not less than 5 years at least or more There is risk of service geographically shrinking due to the abduction of humanitarian staff on the out skirt of Baidoa towards the end of 2017 and beginning of 2018 which has reduced rural response but with security beefed-up by the government it’s now possible to conduct service outside the town in the rural areas occupied by the government forces but still organizations to remain vigilant and this might deny the beneficiaries service they are entitled at the original thus forced to displace to where service are available. They also face the risk of project facing out due to the emergency nature of the current project in Somalia with little resilience which some times leds to the negation of the gains made during the project and this is mitigated by advocating for projects before the running project is faced out
