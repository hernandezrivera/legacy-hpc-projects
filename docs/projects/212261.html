Project Summary

Syria Relief aims to reduce and alleviate the effects of protection risks by strengthening the community-based protection strategy by providing General Protection, Child Protection, and GBV services to the most affected and heightened risk population groups in NWS in 13 sub-districts in which SR is already operating in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, including Idleb, Dana, Kafr Takharim, Maaret Tamsrin, Bab Al-Hawa, Jisr Ash-Shugur, Bennsh, Ariha, Azaz, Afrin, Atareb, Daret Azza, and Jandairis. Also, SR plans to reach affected population in other heightened risk areas and the proposed locations could be Salqin, Armanaz, Sarmin, Ehsem, Mare’ and Jarablus, considering that the severity scale in these locations range between 4 and 5. Syria Relief SR will use already established and equipped centers and establish new centers, including Child Friendly Spaces (CFSs), Help Desks, and Women Girls Safe Space (WGSS) accessible for PwDs and elder in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. These centers will ensure safe spaces and privacy where men, women, girls, and boys from IDPs, host communities, and returnees can access the services based on their needs. SR will activate the role of gender-mixed mobile units to reach out to the most affected households and individuals that face challenges in accessing the centers such as older persons, pregnant and/or lactating women, PwDs, UASC, homeless, minorities, and persons with chronic illnesses including mental health conditions. The services will include specialized and non-specialized case management services in-kind or cash/CVA for most in need, IPA, awareness raising and legal counselling sessions, Tipping Point sessions, girls’ club, young mothers’ sessions, MHPSS sessions including key messages, positive parenting skills, awareness raising about GP, GBV and CP concerns based on community consultation, life skills sessions, structured PSS and recreational sessions for male and female children, safety audits, community initiatives, and referrals to specialized and non-specialized services. Training about HLP/CSD will be delivered to concerned humanitarian workers to ensure they are well-capacitated as well as Family Tracing and Reunification (FCR) training to protection actors in coordination with CP sub-cluster. SR will invest to enhance the capacity of protection staff and community committees to strengthen community-based protection approach and conduct relevant initiatives about Protection, GBV and CP risks and concerns. The proposed activities will be provided either pure protection activities or in integration with other sectors including Shelter, Livelihood, Health, SRH, WASH and Education. The Protection interventions will focus on enhancing the complementarity between Protection services and other aforementioned sectors to target the most affected individuals, households, and community level. This is to ensure that IPDs in the camps, communities or returnees benefit from the integrated services in the project location or area of intervention.
The Project Team will sign SR PSEA and CSG Policies SR has developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to all SR staff and Protection beneficiaries. SR PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk, and vulnerabilities, etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specific steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response(how and what SR will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and deal with CSG cases of child abuse, exploitation, or violence. All community centers and mobile units’ staff will sign SR CSG and PSEA Policies to ensure a safe environment for all Project beneficiaries.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Need Assessment:After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs. According to HNO 2024, 16.7 million people are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Northwest Syria faces fear and uncertainty due to ongoing insecurity. Explosive ordinance, limited services, and risks for families drive fear among 82% of respondents. Amid increased violence and insecurity, the most prominent protection risks include exploitation (67%), family separation (40%), and reduced access to services (39%).Displacement impacts vulnerable populations. Persons with disabilities (75%), destitute older persons (60%), and children with disabilities (65%) need targeted support. (Protection cluster Escalation report- July 2023)Based on the SR needs assessment in June 2023, 60% of interviewees declared that the loss of civil documents increased after the earthquake, 92% of interviewees confirmed that they saw children showing signs of distress. 91% of interviewees declared that loss of privacy is the most expected fears within the community, 73% stated being harassed, 51% stated that no dedicated bathroom blocks, 20% stated kidnapping. According to the Voices from Syria 2023, Women and girls continue to face persistent levels of GBV in their everyday lives. As in previous years, women and girls in Syria are reporting increased risks of GBV including harassment, intimate partner violence, child and forced marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, and technology facilitated GBV. More than a decade of conflict has helped cultivate an environment conducive to violence against women and girls, undoing years of progress on gender equality and women’s rights. Protection Risk Analysis:Assumptions: Relative proximity to frontlines for some areas, potential military escalation. Mitigation: coordination with Security dept that is following the context closely on regular basis. Immediate reporting of any incidents or concerns in order to get the relevant recommendations. contingency plans to evacuation, emergency procedures.Training on access to heightened risk areaAssumptions: Regarding the implementation activities for adolescents, there is a risk that girls may not be able to participate in the activity due to some social norms and traditions, and to mitigate this risk, coordinate with the community key figures and work on building trust with the communities. Assumption: There could be sometimes some trends in NWS against protection projects activities for possible misunderstanding their objectives and allegations about agendas aiming to corrupt communities. Mitigation: Raising communities awareness. Adapting activities to local culture and norms.
