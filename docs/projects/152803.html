Project Summary

The project aims to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene
for IDPs/host communities and returnees living in the hard to reach areas of South
Sudan. It will target newly displaced people, returnees and host communities affected by
conflict. The project will focus on providing emergency WASH services to the displaced
persons including most vulnerable host communities and also support the areas of
returns. Focus will be on the repair and construction of Water points, training hand pumps
mechanics, provision of hygiene and dignity kits and water treatment materials as well as
hygiene promotion and messaging to increase awareness among the affected population
and reduce the incidence water born diseases. The affected population will also be
supported in the construction of emergency latrines specifically targeting the most
vulnerable and newly displaced people. The project will be delivered through a rapid
response mechanism in project locations selected in line with the cluster prioritizing
process.
The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2019 HRP's SO 1 and SO2. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and within 14 days for full-fledged delivery of WASH services and other activities per location. The project will also support returnees in settlement as a part of their local integration or returns to their areas of origin. This will be done in an integrated way with other projects being implemented by NRC so that the returnees don’t only receive WASH support but also a source of Food security and Livelihood through the food security projects in the planned locations. Additionally, the project will be implemented together with provision for education in emergencies and Legal Counseling will be made for the returnees where NRC is already implementing such projects e.g. by providing WASH services in schools or communities or return.

The WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EP&R) WASH actors where implementation is based on the multi-sector assessments and team involving WASH, Shelter, Food security and emergency Education with protection as a cross cutting theme. In some of the locations, the WASH projects link up with other emergency projects to ensure the beneficiaries receive a comprehensive response package. The project will be implemented in coordination with other players in the sector especially UNICEF for information sharing and use of the cluster core pipeline supplies when needed as well as other WASH cluster members for joint assessments and response when appropriate. The project will also be coordinated with the WASH and Logistics clusters especially in prioritization of locations and delivery of supplies.

Needs

Access to safe drinking water: Shallow/hand-dug wells, or surface water (rivers or streams) are the main sources of water in South Sudan. Access to safe drinking water continues to be a challenge, and water-borne diseases have spread due to lack of clean water and poor sanitation. It is estimated that only 7% of the South Sudanese people have access to improved sanitation and only 41% t to safe drinking water . The spread of the conflict to new areas in 2017 aggravated the situation. as boreholes were damaged or made dysfunctional due to lack of repairs. In Jonglei State, the monthly REACH assessment reports shows that while boreholes are the main source of water, their operation and maintenance and contamination by flood water during the rainy season are the main issues. Similarly, in Western Bahr el Ghazal State boreholes and protected wells are the main source of water while they are contaminated by floods during the rainy season aggravating the shortage of safe drinking water in the season. . Due to distance to the few existing limited infrastructure, access to safe water during dry season drops drastically. With increased population in some areas due to displacement and reduced amount of water during dry season, it is expected that water induced conflicts will arise at the water collection points. In South Sudan lack of access to safe drinking water and adequate sanitation pose protection risks for women, with the search to find a safe place to defecate and the trek to collect water, women are exposed to the threat of violence and harassment. For the third year in a row, the Government declared a cholera epidemic in 2016 and, as of 1 December, more than 3,100 cholera cases, including 44 deaths, had been reported across nine counties. Communities along the River Nile have been worst affected and account for 91 per cent of reported cholera cases in 2016. The cholera situation is continued in 2017. Unsafe excreta disposal: Only 16% of the urban population and 4% of the rural population in South Sudan have access to adequate sanitation facilities, which is one of the lowest statistical coverage in the world. 50% and 79% of South Sudanese practice open defecation in cities and rural areas, respectively . Poor sanitation is further reflected by the monthly REACH reports as in Jonglei 62%, in West Bahrul Ghazal 59% and in Unity 95% people do not use latrines. Open defecation is therefore common and is one of the biggest cause of diarrhoea, due to the faecal-oral transmission of diarrhoea-causing organisms. Lack of awareness regarding safe hygiene practices: A higher percentage of the affected population often have very low ability to effectively utilise improved water and sanitation facilities to prevent diseases. An assessment carried out in Mingkaman IDP centre found out that 37% of the samples taken at household level had no Free Residual Chlorine, although these households collected water from treated sources. It was noted that this was a result of using dirt water containers for collection and storage . Poor hygiene and sanitation conditions , as well as a lack of access to safe water, have resulted in an ongoing cholera outbreak. Active cholera transmission was continued and reported in Duk, Ayod, Fangak and Pigi counties & in counties distant from Nile including alerts were reported in Akobo as well as Uror. There is a dire need to raise awareness through promotional activities and training to enable effectiveness of water and sanitation improvements. Community capacity building for the management of water sources: There is a need for capacity building and support to communities to empower them to take responsibility over their water facilities and actively participate in their Operation and Maintenance (O&M) in ways that are flexible and incorporate measures which deal with gender inequality and barriers to access.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes, the project will provide beneficiaries with information about the assistance to be provided. In the beginning of the interventions in the target communities NRC, will share the the intended plan of interventions with the local authorities and with the local Communities. Before the selection of beneficiaries, communities and authorities will be informed about the selection criteria, target population and the support they will receive from the project. Communities will also be informed about the funding, implementation and reporting modalities of the project.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All the IEC material will be revised as per the gender and age-groups covering children and elders for hygiene promotion targeting each age group.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

NRC takes prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse very seriously. All NRC staff engaged in the project will be trained in PSEA; further, staff will be trained in PFA and identification of potential survivors of SEA, and receive training and mentoring in how to respond appropriately and with empathy. All communications with affected populations will stress their right to receive services without coercion or exploitation. NRC will work with communities to develop confidential, appropriate complaints and feedback mechanisms, and respond to any complaints promptly and transparently, to the extent that we are still able to assume both the survivor and perpetrator of their safety. NRC will strive to recruit gender-balanced teams and take every effort to ensure that vulnerable women are able to work directly with competent woman staff.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

NRC believes that all projects must be informed by and reflect the voices of the communities in which we work. To that end, NRC conducted several needs assessments, PDMs in areas that will be targeted by the project in both static and mobile locations. Assessment teams conducted focus group discussions and Key Informant Interviews. Purposive and convenience sampling was used, depending on the location, timing of the assessment, and capacity of the team. Persons that were targeted for inclusion included people with disabilities, older persons, recently displaced people, and female-headed households

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Complaint response mechanism will be handled by protection officers in the field by setting up a complaint desk in the waiting area. NRC normally does and will engage communities in the needs assessment by employing them as part of assessment team. Communities will also participate during the distributions and identification of beneficiaries. Communities also participate in focus group discussions, orientation and feed back mechanisms. The project will offer several complains and feedback mechanisms, depending on the needs of the community. In initial assessments, NRC will consult with targeted persons about the best approach, safety and strive to be responsible and flexible in our implementation.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Awareness and basic project information material will be translated in to local language for the better understanding of beneficiaries. A community leader will always be involved, complaints will always handled and immediate feedback given to beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will fill the complain forms and will be provided with help if required for record purpose. Feedback mechanism will also help in decisions for improvement to serve the communities in more effective manner . In line with NRC’s global commitment to ensure that all aspects of humanitarian intervention – including monitoring and evaluation – are informed by and reflect the voices of the communities with which we work, NRC will ensure that monitoring and evaluation activities are inclusive and accessible. Whether utilising IPA case closure and evaluation forms, PDM tools, or more general community evaluation FGDs and KIIs, NRC teams will seek to engage a range of community members from both sexes, all relevant ages, and across a range of diversity characteristics. In assessment stages and throughout implementation, NRC teams will discuss with communities the ideal mechanisms for monitoring and evaluation to ensure inclusive approaches. In hard to reach areas, or those with emergency responses that might mitigate the kinds of monitoring and evaluation approaches possible in more static locations, NRC will take particular care to discuss with communities safe, realistic options for monitoring and evaluation and means of improving accountability to affected populations.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

NRC conducted protection needs assessment in several locations targeted by the project, both those intended to be served by static and mobile activities. Primary and secondary data collected during the assessments informed a protection risk analysis that particularly considered the needs of women, recently displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection threats will be addressed through engaging with the local authorities and beneficiaries to find out the best safe way to deliver support without putting the beneficiaries at risk of harm to and from the service centres. One mitigating measure is that men must be explicitly targeted and significantly engaged. In all target locations, a majority of respondents reported that men enjoy sole control over assets and decision-making in households. At the same time, respondents across gender and location reported high rates of GBV. By proactively engaging men, there is likelihood of averting harm to women and girls.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

NRC will evaluate three pillars of meaningful access – ability to reach, receive, and benefit from services – during assessment, throughout implementation, and during monitoring and evaluation stages. Questionnaires utilised at individual, household, community level will ask about actual and perceived barriers to access, and project teams will make all possible adjustments to intervention modalities to mitigate challenges as the arise. Especially in static or recurrent mobile locations, NRC will seek to verify access concerns with service providers and maintain and up-to-date, quality-based service maps.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

NRC prioritises the safety of beneficiaries in all activities. Thus, all assessment and monitoring tools take questions of community and individual safety as mandatory. Likewise, all M&E tools will ask about improvements in safety generally, and related to the intervention. Beneficiaries will be asked, before, during, and after an intervention what steps would contribute to feelings of safety, and if they felt that NRC was failing to account for safety in meaningful ways. Beyond perceptions of safety, protection monitors will be asked to report on specific instances of rights violations, both related to NRC interventions and those otherwise occurring in targeted communities. NRC will also include an internal incident reporting mechanism for staff to monitor our own observations regarding beneficiary safety.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

As with issues of safety, NRC prioritises the dignity of beneficiaries in all activities. Thus, all assessment and M&E tools take questions of community and individual dignity as mandatory. Beneficiaries will be asked, before, during, and after an intervention what steps would contribute to feelings dignity and self-worth, and if they felt that NRC was undercutting feelings of dignity in meaningful ways.
