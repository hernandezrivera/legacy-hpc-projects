Project Summary

To ensure that children and communities in the targeted health districts have improved access to basic health care services and commodities, and primary health care services have continued provision of services with COVID-19 related activities integrated. This will entail a package of child survival services as required for displaced children and pregnant women/ caregivers. Provide health care to people who have suffered trauma caused by firearms or white weapon; Support epidemics prevention in all affected areas; health workers trained to raise awareness of good practices to reduce the risk of epidemics; provide GBV survivors health care; supports community outreach and health personnel for measles immunization of children aged 6 months- 15 with vitamin A distribution; and distribution of maternal and new-born kits to pregnant and lactating women/caregivers. Our health responses will also Support Strengthening of Mental health and psychosocial support interventions and integrated C4D capacities
Trained CHWs will provide community basic health services at the delivery points in the community in remote and insecure areas targeted by the program. To prevent the spread of COVID-19 and ensure the safety of the program staff and beneficiaries in the community and health facility, SUDAHSER will support training of community health workers and health care providers on standard precautions including hand hygiene and respiratory protection, rational, correct and systematic use of PPE, safe waste management, proper laundry management, environmental cleaning to ensure that IPC measures are followed. The project location is Oku and Fundong district

1 - Besoins / Needs

The North-West and South-West regions represent 16% of the total population of Cameroon with about 4,000,000 inhabitants. An estimated 573,000 Internally Displaced People targeted are in need in the SW and NW regions. The most vulnerable population are women, pregnant women, newborn babies, children under five, young children both boys and girls. 80% of the IDPs live in the forest and in hard to reach areas in deplorable conditions and have no access to health care services and the 20% living in urban and peri-urban areas have very as well a limited access to health care. Health is one of the main concerns of IDPs. Capacities of health facilities have not been strengthened (Human and financial resources) to receive IDPs. A number of health facilities, have been destroyed, others closed down. Some community pharmacies have been robbed and emptied. Many health workers in both regions have fled away. Community health workers (CHW) are no more functional in insecure health districts; some health districts do not yet have CHW because not yet introduced by the Ministry of health. In the government response plan, health is one of the major components. The different rapid assessment reports conducted have identified the following key health concerns for children in the South West & North West Regions • Children under five and pregnant women may not have access to health facilities to continue as planned their routine immunization. Promiscuity, overcrowding and progressively increased number of unvaccinated children may lead to measles and other vaccine preventable disease epidemics. Mothers and their newborns will be exposed to tetanus because of inadequate vaccination or delivery settings. Some women may deliver in the forest without any medical assistance, assisted by traditional birth attendants or family members in unhygienic conditions also putting at risk the life of their newborns; • Pregnant women have limited access to antenatal, birth and post-natal care. After delivery, the newborn may be deprived of basic essential newborn care: warmth, cord care, exclusive breastfeeding increasing the risk of neonatal death; • The limited access to clean and potable water, hygiene and sanitation, IDPs are exposed to water borne diseases, diarrhea being the number one, acute respiratory tract infections mostly in the under-fives and skin diseases; • Living in the very precarious conditions in the forest characterized by almost inexistent shelter, no protection against mosquito bites, in an endemic malaria zone will lead to an increased incidence of malaria cases especially in the most vulnerable groups: pregnant women and the under-fives; High impact intervention like Intermittent Preventive Treatment (IPT) for malaria prevention in pregnancy, and the use of long-Lasting Insecticide Treated Mosquito Nets (LLINs) are altered; • Accessing health facilities in case of illness or delivery remain a great challenge due to insecurity, lack of transportation means, road blockage, etc. Those who manage to a health facility after trekking for long distances arrive in very precarious conditions; • Rapid assessment reveals data registration and reporting issues. Data on IDPs is not distinguished (e.g. in consultation registers of health facilities not possible to know the number of IDPs who consulted).
