Project Summary

Despite the improvement in the proportion of facility-based deliveries at 82% and access to four antenatal care visits at 78% , the number of preventable maternal mortalities is still unacceptably high with an average of 7 maternal deaths reported every month. Of these deaths, 76% occur at health facilities, 44% during pregnancy and labour while over 70% have attended at least 3 antenatal care visits. This project will focus on strengthening strategies and interventions to improve the access to quality maternal health care for women and girls to reduce the number of preventable maternal and perinatal mortality and morbidity occurring in the Rohingya response. UNFPA as the coordinating agency of the sexual reproductive health working group (SRH WG) will strengthen the coordination of over 42 SRH partners to improve the quality of SRH services. The SRH WG will continue to ensure collaborative inter-sectoral coordination with the health sector, Gender based violence (GBV) sub sector, community health workers (CHW) working group, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) working group and Government stakeholders. This project will strengthen the maternal and perinatal surveillance and response (MPMSR) and the SRH information management systems to identify and address the preventable causes of maternal and perinatal deaths and work with the relevant stakeholders to improve the quality of care provided to women and girls during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period. UNFPA will guarantee the availability of family planning and other reproductive health commodities and supplies through enhanced supply chain management to satisfy the unmet needs of refugees and surrounding host communities. Operationalization of the family planning (FP) strategy for Cox’s Bazar through support to facility and community-based provision of voluntary family planning methods as well as FP evidence generation will be a major focus. In addition, the continued mentorship and skilling of the midwifery workforce in the management of emergency obstetric and newborn emergencies, improved emergency obstetric and newborn ambulance referrals and access to SRH services, improved quality of antenatal and post-natal services as well as quality facility-based deliveries will be of high priority. UNFPA will focus on provision of and access to SRH services targeting adolescents and youths as well other minority groups like the persons with disabilities. UNFPA will continue to support the provision of basic emergency Obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC) services at 21 health facilities and 1 CEmONC facility across 11 camps and surrounding host communities with support to the Upazilla health complexes as well as Sadar hospital. UNFPA will provide integrated SRH/GBV/MHPSS/ASRH services at health facility and community levels working with CHWs for community engagement, pregnancy mapping, EDD and high-risk pregnant mothers mapping and referral, social mobilization, and other awareness-raising interventions as well as traditional birth attendants (TBAs) as facilitators of improved SRH services uptake. The TBAs will be incentivized to refer and escort pregnant mothers for facility-based deliveries and provide nonclinical labour support as trusted community members to pregnant mothers while at the health facility in the camps with low facility-based deliveries according to the CHW WG data. UNFPA will enhance its preparedness to ensure that during natural disasters and disease outbreaks, the minimum initial services package (MISP) for reproductive health are integrated within the health response plans and emergency response teams, emergency response teams are trained on MISP as well as preposition of emergency reproductive health kits. The capacity of health practitioners, community health workers and other stakeholders providing adolescent health to deliver integrated, client-centered, respectful high-quality services will be enhanced.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Based on the ISCG population projections for 2024, there is an estimated 362,195 women of reproductive age of whom 233,658 are Rohingya refugees and 128,153 are from the host community who need SRH services. Despite the fact that there are over 40 health facilities providing emergency obstetric and newborn care at basic and comprehensive levels, there is still need to strengthen the capacity of these health facilities as well as the health workforce in the management of obstetric and newborn care emergencies in order to improve the obstetric outcomes of the Rohingya refugees as well as the surrounding host community. Over the years of the Rohingya response, the proportion of facility-based deliveries has since improved to the current 82% yet the average absolute number of mothers delivering at home is still high at 400 home deliveries per month. The average number of monthly facility deliveries is 2,700 of which about 2.5% end as still births. An average of 5.5% (average of 115 C/S per month) deliveries are by caesarean section. Antenatal care 4th visit coverage at delivery has improved to 78% yet 70% of the maternal mortalities have received 3 or more antenatal care visits during that pregnancy. The number of preventable maternal mortalities is still unacceptably high with an average of 7 maternal deaths reported and over 60 perinatal mortalities per month of which 76% occur at health facilities while 44% during pregnancy and labour. Despite the improvement in the uptake of voluntary modern family planning methods, the number of first-time acceptors is relatively low and the proportion of those using long acting, but reversible family planning methods is still low between 2- 4%. In addition, there still exists several barriers to uptake of voluntary family planning as well as other sexual reproductive health services especially for the unmarried women and adolescent girls. Due to the social-cultural barriers to sexual and reproductive health care services, efforts to create and improve community sensitization, awareness, mobilization, and referral from community to health facilities for SRH services is key. As teenage pregnancy is a major risk factor to poor obstetric complications and outcomes for both mother and baby, efforts to address child marriage and poor health seeking behaviors as well as poor access to SRH services by adolescents at community level are vital. In the spirit of leaving no one behind, it is crucial to have initiatives to meet the SRH needs of the vulnerable populations, such as adolescents and persons living with disabilities. Cox’s Bazar is generally prone to acute natural disasters like fire, floods, cyclones and monsoons and sexual reproductive health needs for women and girls are always forgotten and there is need to ensure that integration of the minimum initial services package into the emergency response plans and teams is done.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNFPA project aligns to the health sector rationalization process and takes an integrated approach to provide the delivery of comprehensive and integrated SRHR-GBV and adolescent and youth-friendly sexual and reproductive health care to refugees and surrounding host communities. UNFPA will also strengthen the quality of SRH services across all health facilities and partners as per the health sector rationalization recommendation. As the coordinating agency for the SRH working group, UNFPA will coordinate and collaborate with all health partners, stakeholders, other sectors, and Cox's Bazar-level government authorities. In addition, UNFPA and all of its implementing partners will maintain a well-coordinated and amicable relationship with the government, including camp-level CiCs, and will work with other partners and stakeholders, engaging in all camp-level coordination platforms. As UNFPA continues to receive multiyear financing from some of its donors, implementing partners will be assisted to maintain continuity, predictability, and sustainability of services.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNFPA, as the coordinating agency for the GBV sub sector and a lead agency working on GBV prevention and response, continuously analyzes the different needs, roles, relationships, and experiences of women, girls, men and boys in the assessment, planning, implementation, and review of the assistance (including protection). UNFPA mainstreams GBV in all its programmes of which SRH is one and fosters GBV/SRH integration. UNFPA believes that the promotion of gender equality in programming not only increases the effectiveness of humanitarian action but also upholds our moral and legal obligations to protect the dignity and rights of all people including persons with disabilities to ensure dignified impartial access by operating according to the principle of non-discrimination and bolstering our mandates to serve the most vulnerable individuals who are at risk of being left behind.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNFPA will ensure its commitment of reaching the most vulnerable population by enhancing the safety and dignity of the targeted populations including children and adolescent girls and boys as well as persons living with disabilities and older persons. UNFPA as a GBV and SRHR agency works to protect persons, including young people, from all forms of violence, abuse, and exploitation including gender based violence and harmful practices including child marriage. Along with providing key information and awareness sessions, the programmes will also support the beneficiaries to understand and identify the referral services related to SRHR, AYSRH, GBV and MHPSS available in the community. As a result, this will enable them to seek services when needed. In accordance with that, UNFPA will ensure the inclusion of all age and gender groups to make the programme inclusive and non-biased. UNFPA services target all persons in need without discrimination including women of reproductive age, adolescent girls and boys, adult men and women as well as key populations such as sex workers and survivors of gender based violence. UNFPA will continue reinforcing partnerships with the organizations working with the Gender Diverse Populations (GDP) and capacity development on SRH/GBV, AYSRH and PSEA. The ethnic, key populations and religious minorities will continue to be in special focus and be involved in the SRH services delivery and access.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNFPA along with its implementing partners (IP) ensure a people centered approach to achieve better health outcomes and embrace our collective commitment toward the accountability to affected populations (AAP) framework as it ensures the Rohingya, and host communities provide invaluable influence, meaningful participation and decision making in the design, implementation and monitoring of program and services. UNFPA will ensure that the people's voices are listened to especially through the regular community engagement strategies including conducting courtyard sessions targeting different members of the community like men, adolescent boys, adolescent girls and women. Client Satisfaction Surveys and suggestion boxes will be in place to solicit, hear, and act upon the voices and priorities of affected people and establish the level of client satisfaction with the quality of services being delivered at UNFPA supported facilities including health facilities and WFS.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNFPA has zero tolerance for sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA) and sexual harassment (SH) and actively advocates for this principle across the organization, IPs, vendors, service providers & beneficiaries. The organization is committed to preventing SEA and providing immediate support to victims. UNFPA works with other organizations to create a workplace free from SEA. Since 2017, UNFPA has been a member of ISCG PSEA network in Cox's Bazar and appointed PSEA Focal Points for the organization.Training on PSEA is a top priority for UNFPA staff when they join the organization, including on staff obligations to report any wrongdoing and survivor-centered support. UNFPA's Office of Audit & Investigation Services (OAIS) ensures that staff can easily report misconduct through an online platform/hotline. PSEA focal points are available to receive complaints & report them back to the OAIS. All personnel involved with UNFPA, including volunteers, contractors, and vendors, receive training on PSEA. The organization has a retaliation policy in place to protect survivors, victims, and whistleblowers from any form of retaliation. Under this project, UNFPA aims to continue building the capacity of their staff and related personnel on SEA and related issues. UNFPA will also strengthen PSEA Champions to prevent SEA and raise awareness on reporting mechanisms among the Rohingya population and host communities. UNFPA will conduct community-based awareness modules and use audiovisual & pictorial resources in awareness sessions to ensure communities are aware of SEA & reporting mechanisms.The organization will work to strengthen reporting channels and ensure conducive reporting mechanisms are available in each UNFPA-supported facility. Whenever needed, UNFPA will conduct capacity assessments and support the implementation of capacity strengthening plans to ensure IPs are maintaining the required PSEA and safeguarding mechanism in coordination with ISCG PSEA Network.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. As the coordinators of the SRH WG on behalf of the health sector, UNFPA aligns to the health sector emergency and response plan which brings together all the health sector and SRH working group partners with collective efforts to enhance the preparedness and response readiness actions to minimize the direct and indirect impacts of natural hazards including cyclones and monsoons. Through the SRH working group, UNFPA will ensure that emergency response teams have midwives to respond to SRH/GBV related emergencies, there is adequate SRH contingency stock through prepositioning of emergency reproductive health kits and commodities, and training of emergency response teams on the minimum initial services package for reproductive health in emergencies. In addition, SRH partners will be encouraged to ensure the reinforcement of the SRH infrastructure to withstand the negative weather effects and other disasters like cyclones, given the increasing impacts of climate change on the country.
