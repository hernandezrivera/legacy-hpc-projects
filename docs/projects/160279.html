Project Summary

In South Sudan the food security situation is currently fragile due to multiple triggers such as insecurity, floods and drought. This situation is evidenced by 58 Counties at present being classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 and above (GAM prevalence above 9.9%). The Counties in Unity and Lakes States are in critical levels of acute malnutrition (IPC September 2019). Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) in response proposes Livelihood support to Vulnerable Host Communities, IDPs and Returnees. The intervention targets 6,000 Vulnerable Households with Seed kits, tools, fish kits, training, Small Grants for Petty Trade and Loan Schemes in the Host Communities, IDPs and Returnee Communities in Panyijiar, Rubkona, Rumbek Center and Yirol East Counties in Unity and Lakes States. The Project aims to reduce dependence on food assistance and to support and strengthen households ability to absorb Climatic shocks that have been detrimental to crop yields as well as the Economic crisis that has equally reduced livelihood opportunities in significant parts of South Sudan.
HACO also intends to integrate the FSL, WASH and Protection support to the Communities in the project sites to help them better cope with the multiple shocks that have beset them.
Although the Food security and livelihood situation in South Sudan is Critical, It is hoped that the Country will soon be on the path to recovery, if all the necessary steps are taken to precipitate change . It is therefore prudent to work towards the resilience of the citizens to better adapt to the change. The provision of Agricultural, Livelihood inputs and training will in effect do well to place the returnees, IDPs and Vulnerable hosts on the path to recovery in the year of return of former refugees and IDPs in 2020. Additionally the provision of alternative livelihood startup kits for petty businesses and Village Savings and Loans Schemes (VSLAs) will provide the much needed support to Youths and Women's Groups especially in the targeted Urban and Peri Urban Sites in Unity and lakes States.

1 - Needs

South Sudan’s conflict has forced over 4.1 million people to flee their homes in search of safety, nearly 1.83 million IDPs and nearly 2.33 million refugees outside the country (IPC August 2019). Unity and Lakes States were severely affected by the insecurity in south Sudan caused by Civil Conflict and Cattle rustling. This has had the effect of causing multiple displacements of the Citizens resulting in the loss of livelihoods and loss of farm land and time that would have been used for Cultivation. All these have compounded the food security situation in greater Unity and Lakes States. In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). In the projection period of September to December 2019, the food security situation is expected to improve as seasonal harvests become available. During this period, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food. In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity (FEWS NET September 2019). This is likely to be the case due to crop losses, continuation of very high food prices, and increased food demand from rising numbers of returnees are expected to constrain food access. This Proposal in response intends to intervene by targeted distribution of crop kits, agricultural tools, fishing kits, vegetable kits and training of farmers through farmer field schools on the best agronomic practices, post-harvest handling, integrated pest management (IPM), fishing, fish handling techniques, preservation and marketing. Young Adults and Women will also be targeted for the distribution of Small grants for setting up Petty businesses and setting up VSLA Schemes. This project intends to enable the targeted communities take up alternative livelihoods (Petty businesses), increase food production and to save lives of the affected population of Yirol East, Rumbek Center, Rubkona and Panyijiar Counties of Unity and Lakes States.

2 - Activities

To increase food production and provide targeted households alternative livelihood choices, HACO proposes the following activities:1. Distribution of Agricultural tools, Crop and vegetable kits. Particularly Maize and Sorghum staple crops to 6,000 Households in Yirol East, Rumbek Center, Rubkona and Panyijiar Counties. 2. The capacity training of 20 County Agricultural/Fishery extension workers. Identification, training and engagement of 200 Model extension farmers in best agronomic practices, post-harvest handling, Integrated Pest Management (IPM), sales and Marketing. Inclusive will also be the Training of fisher men on best and sustainable Fishing practices, Fish handling, preservation and storage as well as in sales and marketing. The establishment of 40 demonstration sites for Fisheries and Crops where the farmer field schools will be conducted. 3. The distribution of Business startup kits targeting Young Adults(100) and Women (100) for setting up petty business of their choice, like beauty shops, Tea Shops and Mini Groceries. HACO FSL Project staff will be trained on gender mainstreaming, Accountability to affected Population and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. 4. Training of the Group members in Savings, Managing Savings, Group management (Group Dynamics) and Entrepreneurship Skills necessary to equip the VSLA members for Management purposes. The Set up of 12 Village Savings and Loans Schemes Managed by the Group members engaging in petty businesses.5. Post Distribution Mentoring and Monitoring of Beneficiaries and lastly Monitoring and Evaluation of the Project.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiary rights will be legally recognized. This will be done by awareness raising sessions on human rights with the project beneficiaries. They will be informed of the availability, access of services and referral pathways to follow in case of abuse. The reporting system will offer a safety net for the protection of beneficiaries against abuse such as Sexual exploitation and abuse.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project implementation will be aligned to the perspectives, rights and needs of different target population groups such as IDPs, returnees and the vulnerable host community. Consultations with the community to gather information on their views, needs and specific vulnerabilities of the target beneficiaries will be done. The consultative process will include the IDPs, returnees and host communities to ensure that decisions taken by HACO will be aligned to the interests of the IDPs, returnees and Vulnerable host Community.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The Proposed Project is linked to Community and Government Systems for Sustainability Purposes. Project Activities will be carried out in collaboration with Community leaders such as headmen and farmer groups and County Agriculture and Fisheries officers. The County Agricultural and Fishery Extension workers will be capacity built and later help anchor the project activities in the targeted communities. This will help to sustain the project results and will provide a foundation for long term Interventions.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is aligned with National Objectives such as Social protection of IDPs, National Refugee Return Framework which provide for durable solutions to internally displaced persons who will no longer have specific assistance and protection needs that are linked to their displacement and can enjoy their human rights without discrimination on account of their displacement. This project will enable the Sustainable reintegration of IDPs, returnees at their place of origin. It will also provide for IDPs Sustainable local integration in areas where internally displaced persons are taking refuge. Further more populations and communities that re-integrate IDPs and whose needs may be comparable to the needs of the displaced have also been targeted.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The Protection Environment has improved but still fragile due to pockets of insecurity, drought and Economic Crisis. This Livelihood Support Project will facilitate IDPs and returnees to enjoy without discrimination Long-term safety, security and freedom of movement in being actively engaged in Project activities. The Project will further provide them with a foundation for adequate standard of living, especially access to adequate food, Access to employment and livelihoods which will build their confidence; propelling them to Participate in public affairs at all levels on an equal basis with the resident population.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The Project provides for Sustainability and handover of project activities in the sense that the County Agriculture and Fisheries extension workers will be actively involved from the inception of the project for ownership. The County Agriculture and Fisheries extension workers will be capacity built and will be actively involved in the implementation of the project; especially in training farmers in best agronomic practices, fishing, fish handling and preservation as well as marketing. The model farmers will also act as community volunteers and will be the ones managing the farmer field schools with the county Agricultural and fishery officers when the project is completed. The VSLAs will also be managed by the Women and Youth Groups carrying out petty businesses in the targeted locations.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The above mentioned risks will be mitigated in the following ways: There will be the training of HACO staff on PSEA. There will also be rights awareness raising sessions for project beneficiaries. Community leaders and leaders of associations such as women group leaders, youth associations will be involved in beneficiary prioritization to reduce cases of disfavoring some potential beneficiaries based on their vulnerabilities such as gender, age or tribe. The beneficiaries will also be clearly told their rights and that no one should violate their rights. The beneficiaries will be fully protected from sexual exploitation and abuse from HACO staff who will have signed the PSEA as part of their Contract obligation. HACO will also follow the humanitarian principle of DO NO HARM to the project beneficiaries. This will help to reduce cases where some of our staff may wish to use their position to take advantage of beneficiaries. .

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Project will have a complaints and feedback mechanism through community leaders, Leaders of Youth Association, Leaders of Women associations, Other relevant groups and HACO. The project implementing team will also have an open door policy for beneficiaries to give their feedback through FGDs and open dialogue with the community. FSL committees will be formed to take care of issues of concern during project implementation. It will be composed of representatives from all the Project stake holders at county level and will be a representative/inclusive body taking care of age, gender and inclusion. The role of the committee will be to receive and register complaints raised by beneficiaries and give feedback whenever possible. Field teams shall set aside a desk and a book where all the complaints and feedback are recorded. The committee will also be trained on case handling procedures. There will be awareness creation to all the beneficiaries to inform them on existence of complaints and feedback desk, clearly informing them where and who to register their complaints and who to give them feedback.
