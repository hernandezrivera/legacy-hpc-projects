Project Summary

According to the detailed site assessment finding and site verification assessments conducted by the CCCM cluster, more than 85 per cent of sites are informal settlements settled on private land with the majority of sites located in urban areas. 284 The lack of access to land coupled with a weak land tenure system, multiplicity of land ownership, land disputes, weak judicial systems to seek redress, and a lack of proper national land policy further exacerbates the vulnerability of IDPs.285 This has led to frequent incidents of forceful evictions pushing IDPs into worsened situations requiring both emergency and prolonged assistance. IDP returns are extremely limited as many displaced families have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services.286. Lower Shabelle and Middel Shabelle have had more than 43% of the IDPs overcrowd and influx reported across Somalia.
In Lower Shabelle, according to Joint CCCM, Protection and Shelter Assessment done by AYUUB, HINA and Juba Foundation in KM50 IDPs identified six IDPs namely: Balafley; Gadaan, Farah walalkey, )Buur fuule, and waraha dhawada host more than 4500 HHs of IDPs ( 2700 people), 90% of them were evicted from another areas and living in overcrowded IDPs sites and lacking all camp coordination mechanisms.
In Middle Shabelle, AYUUB Assessment on Shelter, CCCM, Protection and WASH marked Sheikh Omar, Jiliyaale, Tawakal, Airportka and Liiliqato IDPs sites in Jowhar among the most overcrowded sites with poor construction and camp management and need for Improving the living conditions and protection of IDPs at site level through establishing camp coordination structures at the site-level to ensure equitable access to services and assistance for all members of the community.
The displaced people in the two regions are living in informal sites are in greatest need of CCCM services to improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs at the site level.
According CCCM Cluster monthly service monitoring and complaint feedback mechanism (CFM) trend analysis indicate similar service gaps and issues raised.287 Most IDP sites recorded needs mostly in Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) support, Shelter and WASH assistance, but not excluding other sectors such as health and education which also show considerable service and assistance needs.288 While as the service monitoring cuts across IDP sites and highlights the needs of all IDP populations, CCCM cluster reports indicate that the majority of those most acutely affected are vulnerable groups; women, elderly, refugee returnees, minority groups and people living with disability (PLWD), putting further stress on that demography of displaced families and limiting their ability to cope.
Through this proposed response, AYUUB seeks to address identified frontline humanitarian needs for IDPs by establishing camp coordination structures at the site-level that improve living conditions and protection of IDPs and ensure equitable access to services and assistance for all members of community. AYUUB will target 64,000 people of conflict , drought, floods and Covid19 affected population in Marka, Barawer, Afgooye and Wanla Weyn ( Lower Shabelle) and Jowhar and Balcad ( Middle Shabelle) of Somalia.
AYUUB intends to scale up the CCCM program to contribute towards strengthening camp coordination and camp management to improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs at the site level by ensuring equitable access to services and assistance of all persons in need. All the project activities will be carried out to maximize community participation and enhance the accountability of humanitarian actors to the affected population. AYUUB will coordinate with relevant responders and mobilized towards providing sectorial assistance to those identified as very vulnerable at the site level. Together with the established site governance, AYUUB will carry out service monitoring activities aimed at highlighting service gaps.

1. - Projects activities

To accomplish the objectives of this proposal, AYUUB will Establish/update service mapping of partners operating in sites - Mapping exercise will be conducted to find out the existing resources/infrastructures in the targeted settlements to identify gaps and inform responses, which will be shared with CCCM cluster and will be used by different humanitarian partners for data collection and their response analysis. This will enhance the strategic thinking of all stakeholders in terms of needs and gaps in a particular settlement. Through these mapping exercises, it will be easy for all stakeholders to plan their activities related to a particular settlement. Secondly AYUUB will maintain CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterparts, and conduct coordination meetings during the entire project period, which will include partners working in the same area. The purpose of these coordination meetings is to avoid any duplication, sharing information, and referral mechanisms. Further to that AYUUB will undertake Site Verification exercise covering all sites in the target locations in Lower and Middle Shabelle regions, to do that, AYUUB will conduct it with support from other humanitarian partners and the local authorities to provide multi-sector information on access and availability to basic services to ascertain gaps in service provision and monitor services being provided, the verification will be conducted by AYUUB s multi-functional mobile teams, this will help to produce detailed information on the population of each site, its perimeter, access and services available in sites. NoFYL will use this data and contribute to its dissemination to inform humanitarian response at each level. The site verification will also inform the selection process of the target camps. To promote the camp management, AYUUB will support the involvement of the community through the local structures, and the representation of IDP leaders and vulnerable groups in Site activities. AYUUB will put the People with Disability in the core of all activities, allowing them to be part in the CCCM committees that will be formed for the sites, captured in the training and capacitation activities, mobilization and all other activities that the project will deliver. AYUUB will also work to establish effective and representative Site governance and for the promotion of IDPs’ participation in decisionmaking and the daily life of the Site, including the development of Site rules. AYUUB perceives in order to accomplish well-functioning Site environments, the IDP must have rights to participate all decisions either indirect and indirect along the humanitarian partners and other stakeholders. In addition to gender representation, minority groups, people with special needs will be assured equal representation. Community leaders and representatives that are selected will be given clear functions and written, agreed-upon roles and responsibilities that will be utilized as a possible entry point for other humanitarian partner’s activities that require community consultation. In addition that AYUUB will do Settlement planning training for the existing governance structures and focal points for sites this will target to equip them with skills that will facilitate participatory management and maintenance of public spaces. To evaluate the service and address the beneficiary’s complaints, AYUUB will operationalize Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms within target locations- this will enable the affected population to have access the opportunity to raise concerns or complaints related to any sector within the site. Complaints are collected and registered by trained staff at the AYUUB information desks. The importance is placed on confidentiality particularly in the instance of complaints arising with potential Protection concerns. To accomplish the durability of the sites infrastructures.
