Project Summary

The program will strengthen the overall protective environment and resilience of the most vulnerable internally displaced people and host community members in Northwest Syria. The NGO will provide integrated protection services, including specialized gender-based violence and child protection case management, advanced and focused, and/or those who are survivors or at risk of gender-based violence and abuse – especially women and children. The program will target at least 26,025 beneficiaries. Attention will be given to appropriate entry points such as existing women’s groups and support networks. Areas of operation are selected in coordination with Clusters and health directorates.
Case management services will be tailored to beneficiaries’ individual needs, taking into consideration family and caregivers. Psychosocial support will help beneficiaries to improve their coping mechanisms, manage stress and find solutions to their challenges. Group work will provide space for beneficiaries with similar issues, problems or circumstances to come together for mutual support. Outreach services will spread information on positive mental health and protection risks, as well as assist in reaching the most vulnerable in communities and making appropriate referrals to services. Training will target both community members and front-line service providers to increase their capacity to help and support others. Awareness-raising activities aim to decrease stigma around accessing services through encouraging people to be more aware of protection risks faced.
Protection services will be integrated with health and physical rehabilitation services under other programs in order to improve access, ensure strong referral mechanisms and identify those most at-risk. Activities will be accessible by all, psychosocial support, community-outreach, group work, awareness-raising activities, training for service providers and community members on mental health and protection topics, and integrated mental health services for the most vulnerable people, including people with disabilities, people facing mental conditions, psychological distress and trauma, also will target children and women assessed to be at high risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect or violence, children found to be associated with armed forces and armed groups, unaccompanied or separated children, persons with mental disorders or psychological distress, the elderly, chronically ill and people with disabilities.
All activities will be based upon the principles of the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, Minimum Standards for Child Protection, and Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action. The NGO will work to improve the availability of holistic response for the most vulnerable by conducting training targeting a range of humanitarian actors in order to strengthen response with a survivor-centred approach.

4 - Needs

GBV disproportionately affects lives of women and girls in Syria (HNO, 2019), increasing due to armed conflict, displacement, the breakdown of law and erosion of social structures and norms. Data collected during current programs indicate, psychological abuse is the most prevalent type of GBV case (at 51.5%), followed by physical assault (21.5%); denial of resources/opportunities and services (15%); early marriage (9%) and sexual violence (3%). Needs assessments by this NGO indicates limits to safe access to services for women and girls for reasons such as lack of permission from families and lack of safety and freedom of movement. 74.1% of respondents from January 2020 RNA reported no safe spaces for women, adolescent’s girls and girls. Findings of the child protection HNO 2020, indicate grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited into armed hostility, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence. Cases supported between October and December 2019, showed the top three CP issues were physical abuse (50%), severe neglect (36%) and psychological distress (13%). Children with developmental disorders/delays are among the most vulnerable. There is a need for different levels of protection and PSS services for children and parents/care-givers with adequate support in order to strengthen resilience at the individual and household level. Moreover general protection needs remain elevated, with an estimated 27,635 people in need of PSS services through health and community based services. WHO estimates that one in five Syrians suffers from mild to moderate mental health and psychological conditions, at risk of developing and psychosocial disability and are at increased risk of protection concerns. People with disabilities and those living with chronic health conditions are also a higher risk of protection concerns and of developing mental disorders, psychological distress and substance misuse.

5 - Implementation of activities

Case Management: Integrated GBV, Child Protection and Mental Health Case Management will be provided by Psychosocial Workers and Case Managers with close supervision by team leaders and Technical Support from MHPSS Technical Supervisor and Protection Manager. Case management services will be integrated into PHCs and physical rehabilitation centres for access, confidentiality, with strong referral mechanisms to services such as Clinical Management of Rape and specialised Mental Health. Community-based Awareness Activities and Outreach through group PSS sessions targeting the wider community will be facilitated to respond to collective trauma, lack of social cohesion, shared stressors and difficult living conditions, aiming to promote resiliency, limit psychological effects of conflict, reduce protection risks and stigma and provide an avenue for support. Structured psychosocial activities for children between 6 to 13 years of age, youth empowerment program for youth 14-17 years consisting of various psychosocial support activities, life skills, and skill-building activities will be implemented at safe spaces and through community activities. Structured parenting skills sessions for caregivers will aim to support and strengthen existing parenting abilities and promote the development of new competencies during the current conflict. Capacity Building training to service providers, frontline staff, and community members will build capacity, raise awareness, improve referral mechanisms and strengthen coordination. Trainings will include topics such as GBV and Child Protection Guiding Principles, PFA, PFA for Children, Problem Management, Self Help and Coping Skills. Activities will be monitored and evaluated using beneficiary satisfaction survey, access to service assessments, individual client functioning and well being assessments, training tests, focus group discussions with community members and close program supervision.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions• NGO continues to have approvals from local authorities to operate in targeted locations• NGO maintains access to targeted locations• Security situation remains stable• Non-interference by groups in control of program locations Risks• Security situation is volatile where destabilization negatively impacts staff and beneficiaries• Delays in procurement and delivery of essential pharmaceuticals and medical consumables and equipment essential for health facility rehabilitation and operations; leading to delay in start-up of services for beneficiaries or cause of stock outs and shortage of medicines• Program locations are taken over by GoT, GoS or allied forces; leading to restrictions in implementation of activities without specific approvals (in the case of GoT) or through cross border operations (GoS) Mitigation Measures• The physical security of NGO facilities to be regularly assessed and upgraded as needed. Close security monitoring. Security training for staff which to limit/lessen exposure. Support to evacuation/withdrawal of local staff to alternative locations to continue managing program or revise targeting. The NGO will consider supporting affected staff through Duty of Care, including financial compensation, support for the treatment of injured staff and compensation for families of staff that may be killed as a result of conflict and severance pay if staff can no longer continue• The NGO uses joint efforts of procurement and legal staff to clarify details of hosting countries regulations. The NGO applies for appropriate certifications, licenses and permits to import and/or use bonded transportation and warehouses for humanitarian assistanceThe NGO will explore options to continue support through local partner NGOs registered in Turkey in the case of GoT takeover to maintain support. The NGO will withdraw support in case of GoS takeover to protect the safety of the staff as cross border operations are deemed illegal by GoS.
