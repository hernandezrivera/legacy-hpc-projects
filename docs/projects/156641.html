Project Summary

MWL proposed project will contribute in supporting students affected by the crisis to get access to formal and non-formal education addressing the EiE standards,and raising its quality in a TLS( which is an existing school where we will have two shifts intervention,morning shift"Formal Ed" and afternoon shift"F & Non-F Edu") that will provide safe environment of education in Afrin community.The project targets(1500 Students/600G & 900B)including 30 children with special needs (12G 18B).That will be achieved through: 1-Rehabilitation of school and its WASH to become more interactive place for students,taking gender into consideration by separating the latrines (Males & females) and establishing a private WCs for CWD,and equipping the schools with safety and security tools 2-Training the teachers and administrative staff on CP core,PSS, classroom management,modern teaching methods 3-Provision stationary, teaching & learning materials, textbooks and Student's kit 4-Using the Interim Gov curriculum for the formal part of the project in the 2 semesters during the year ,and Providing literacy and numeracy skills ,remedial & catch-up classes using Curriculum B for the informal part of it. 5-Conducting ASER to assess learning 6- Establishing 2nd shift to providing formal / non formal education in the school to raise its capacity to absorb new IDPs Boys and Girls 7-Establishing CFS inside the schools in line with INEEE & CPMS that will provide structured PSS, sensitization that target students, caregiver, community key members, and referral to specialized services 8- providing incentives for teachers and administrative staff in one shift " the 2nd is supported by ED 9-Providing the school's two shift with winterization facilities.10-Establishing PTAs to follow up with students education improvement and issues.
MWL Proposed Project will target 2370 Person (115 M, 145 W, 1115 B, 995 G).

Needs

The changes in control in central and south Syria in early and mid-2018 as well as increasing hostilities in northwest Syria in September 2018 caused new waves of civilian displacement into Rural Damascus, Idleb and Aleppo. Existing education capacity in these areas was already limited prior to recent displacement influx. Schools in IDP/returnee receiving communities have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students while in most camps education services are deeply insufficient or non-existent. (HRP 2019)According to the information that we received, local councils have a concerns some the Children of Afrin`s schools absent due to harsh cold weather last year, with inability to provide fuel for heating for schools, schools’ administration forced to return back the children to their homes daily due to Severe cold , just to protect the Children from cold diseases, that’s leads the Education directorate concern from additional dropping out of schools that leads to expose them to different kinds of violence (child labor, exploitation, early marriage, recruitment , etc.)During designing this project in November 2018, MWL has conducted rapid assessments in the proposed areas, these assessments looked at the population profile in general, as well as took a more in-depth look at the current state of education process. High level of psycho social distress among children (B&G) and youth (M&F) due to traumatic experiences, and violence were the key findings. Some deaths due to ERW-related causes were also reported. The selected communities have large number of IDPs from Eastern Ghouta As the GoS and allies advanced on Eastern Ghouta in February and March 2018, children and their families lived under ground for weeks without seeing sunlight. They lived with hunger because of the siege as food prices increased due to scarcity the educational process there stopped from Dec 2017.Bed-wetting, aggressive behaviors panic also key findings within IDPs.As per discussions with Hatay Education directorate that shown the weakness of education services in the selected area" The education system is overburdened and overstretched to the extent that schools, overcrowded and under-resourced. The key impediments for children accessing to education are including safety and security along roads to and from schools and alternative learning spaces; Lack of teaching and learning materials; poor quality teaching; damage of education infrastructure. The need of schools in those communities: 5026 desk,694 white board ,931 heater and 227980 liter of fuel for heating.Where Afrin District including 224 schools that provision education services for (37914 Student) through (1381) teacher and administrators, 21 schools of 224 are out of service, 321 classrooms of 1392 are out of service that need rehabilitation to increase capacity to receive the new IDPs children. 40% of evacuated student from Eastern Ghouta didn't attend schools from December 2017, and their families have not enough resources to push them back to school.These initial assessments were conducted through key informant interviews with individuals related directly to communities (Afrin City, and Raju, Bulbul and Shaikh Al Hadid sub districts) the total population: 223012 Person including (43963 Men, 52803 Women, 61359 Boys, 64887 Girls). Implementation of activities

The field staff will be composed of programmatic field officers, logistics officers, finance officers and M&E officers. All officers will interface directly with their counterparts in the MWL management but will be managed administratively on the field by the team leader on the field. This is to ensure that the team on the ground are communicating consistently, yet technical guidance is provided at the management level. The team leader will be responsible for providing programmatic reporting directly to the project manager, provide updates on the performance of the team, as well as represent MWL within the local community. The MWL team leader will conduct consultations with the various beneficiary groups including the local council, school management, children, youth and parents so as to ensure community feedback is integrated into the program design and all stakeholders in the project are on the same page.The main MWL departments that will be involved with their roles and responsibilities related to implementation will be as the following, Programme staff is responsible to design, implement and close the project, day-to-day management of the entire intervention, and oversees correct implementation according to project’s objectives. in collaboration and support of Financial & M&E departments. Financial staff is responsible for Follow-up the project expenditure based on project activities, budget and cash flow check and archive the invoices and purchase orders and issued timely reports.The M&E staff is responsible for the day-to-day monitoring of the project implementation, measuring progress against project indicators, monitoring the procurement process, identify the compliance of suppliers, conducting the post-distribution evaluation, and working closely together with the filed programme team on monitoring and assessment.The Staff will be adhering to MWL Policies, Guidelines and will share weekly Programmatic report & Monthly Financial report, and the M&E Filed staff will conduct field regular visits and share the reports with M&E officer and Programme Manager.MWL compliance mechanism to ensure the quality of work, transparency and satisfied of Beneficiaries.MWL will ensure the coordination with the local councils and Education Directorates, technical team will follow-up the regular procedures until the end of project, and M&E field team will do a comparison between the actual achievements and the planned and prepare appropriate feedback for project manager.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Protection risk analysis and mitigating measuresThe education assessment indicated that a key barrier to education access is lack of safety and security. The Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism on Grave Violation against Children’s (SCR 1612, 2005) verified 56 attacks against education and education personnel in the first half of 2018. Compared to 2017, the number of verified attacks on schools for only the first half of 2018 is more than 65% of the total number of the previous year, showing an increased trend. As populations move into increasingly unexploded ordnance (UXO) contaminated areas, the presence of UXOs prevents children from going to school or makes it dangerous for children and school personnel to do so. All forms of attacks on schools or learning spaces remain a significant protection risk. Efforts are needed to ensure that deconfliction is done and is effective. As the population in north west Syria (NWS) and north east Syria (NES) are pushed into increasingly contaminated areas, more cross-sectoral and cross-cutting engagements are needed to sensitize the population on prevention and reporting. The threat of military offensive also necessitates adequately resourced and practiced safety and security planning for schools, including awareness sessions on UXOs for both children and adults, deconfliction and school safety and evacuation plans. Changes in the security situation can also lead to increased risks including displacement and restrictions of movement for the population and local partners. Poverty and displacement push vulnerable families to negative coping mechanisms including child labor and child marriage. The vulnerability of out of school children in such circumstances puts children at substantially higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations.Mitigating measures• Partners will take into account risks to children, students, teachers and education personnel when planning and designing their projects and work towards minimizing risks through consultative process with Syrian national and local authorities as well as communities.• Key messages for back-to-learning (BTL) campaigns will take into account the concerns of parents and other community members.• Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for their staff and the projects beneficiaries have sufficient resources to enact those plans. Commit partners to ensure a minimum duty of care to their staff including a right of refusal.• Ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security, including mine risk education and deconfliction.• Ensure relevant authorities/groups are aware of/ engaged with the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation.• Ensure flexible context-informed approaches and mechanisms to deliver the project outputs/outcomes, so that the projects activities remain relevant and can be implemented in a changing context.
