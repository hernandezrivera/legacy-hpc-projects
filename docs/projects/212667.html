Project Summary

This project supports children in Idleb(Harim, Idelp) and Aleppo(Afrin, AlBab, Azaz) from the host community and IDPs, which includes the most vulnerable groups13,000children(6,500 boys, 6,500 girls) from7 to15 age,IDP children,and children with disabilities to provide equitable access to quality learning opportunities for girls and boys in both primary and secondary school level in formal education in 25 schools and formal education with the summer program in 9 learning spaces and 23 schools
The project’s activities revolve around the following components:
Component1:Formal Education Support
The project's first component focuses on providing formal education support to 11,000 children aged 5 to 17, encompassing 5,500 girls, 5,500 boys, and approximately 4% Children with Disabilities (CwD). This support is delivered through activities conducted in 25 formal schools in Northwest Syria, targeting various aspects to enhance educational access, quality, and safety, SiG curriculum will be implemented.
Central to this component are awareness sessions and preventive measures aimed at addressing cholera, a significant public health concern in the region. These sessions not only educate parents and local communities on the importance of education but also raise awareness about preventive measures against epidemic outbreaks. Additionally, back-to-learning (BTL) campaigns are conducted to sensitize stakeholders about the significance of schooling and safety protocols within educational environments. offered by the project, fostering community engagement
Furthermore, structured PSS and Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) programs are integrated into daily sessions, utilizing War Child's "I DEAL" framework to address students' emotional well-being and resilience.Community involvement is facilitated through the activation of Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs), empowering them to contribute to the monitoring and improvement of project activities. Capacity-building initiatives targeting 600 teachers and staff members aim to enhance the overall quality of education delivery.
A key aspect of this component is the inclusion of children with disabilities (CwD),facilitated through in-school integration activities led by CwD-inclusion officers. Additionally, the establishment of Accountability to Affected Populations/Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (AAP/PSEA) reporting channels in all 25 schools
Component 2: Non-Formal Education Program
The project's second component focuses on addressing the educational needs of out-of-school children through a comprehensive NFE program. This initiative targets 2,000 children, comprising 1,000 boys and 600 girls in 9 learning spaces, who have either missed out on formal schooling or require remedial support to integrate successfully into the education system
Designed as an intensive remedial learning program, the NFE initiative aims to equip participants with foundational knowledge and skills within a relatively short timeframe. =Emphasis is placed on core subjects such as language and mathematics,supplemented by modules covering hygiene,environmental awareness, life skills, sports, and cultural activities
Prior to program commencement, a placement test is administered to assess participants' educational levels and tailor instructional strategies accordingly. Collaborative efforts with relevant authorities at selected schools facilitate the seamless transition of beneficiaries from informal to formal educational settings
The four-month duration of the catch-up/remedial learning program provides ample time for participants to bridge educational gaps and attain proficiency levels necessary for successful academic integration
Component3:Winterization
The project addresses the challenge of ensuring access to education services during the harsh winter months of the academic year 2024-2025.With a focus on 25 formal schools accommodating 11,000 children and 9 learning spaces catering to 2,000 children

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The devastating earthquake on February 6, 2023, and the recent escalation of conflicts in NWS have caused significant suffering in the region and further worsened the high levels of needs and vulnerabilities across various humanitarian sectors. Education is among the sectors significantly impacted by these catastrophic events. There has been a 4 % increase in the education sector PIN, with 6.9M Syrians needing urgent humanitarian education assistance, primarily children, with 7% having disabilities. About 1.9 million are in northwest Syria. Over 800,000 children in the region are out of school, with 155,000 never attending schoolPrevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) By ONSUR’s Child Safety Code of Conduct, PSEA, Child Protection, and Gender Policies. To implement this commitment:• Sensitization sessions will be conducted for supported school staff on PSEA • Through collaboration with the GBV sub-cluster, we will enhance field staff's capacity to handle PSEA and GBV cases. • All the supported formal schools will establish effective AAP/PSEA reporting channels as per UN Guidelines. • Sensitization efforts will ensure students, parents, and teachers are informed about these reporting channels. Risk Management: Assumptions 1. Targeted sub-districts are safe with no military aerial bombardment 2.Accessibility for project activities is available without armed group intervention 3.Cooperation from Local Councils and Education members during the project period 4.Easy availability of required services and goods locally Risk 1:Security Changes in security conditions, displacement, and mobility issues MitigationA:ONSUR monitors and establishes emergency plans, including site relocation and alternative service delivery MitigationB:Addresses internal displacement by linking families with other schools and coordinating with local authorities Risk2:Local Authorities' Interference Mitigation: Maintaining a neutral stance,coordinating with local authorities,and providing constant updates on project activities to address potential interference - Planned Monitoring & Reporting ONSUR’s monitoring and evaluation methodology is based onthe collection, management, and analysis of data that are further used in periodic and ongoing assessment of the project’s relevance, performance, efficiency, and impact in the context of its stated objectives. A database will be established at the outset of the project to record each beneficiary’s baseline information as well as attendance, exam progress scores and other qualitative information. Data on the outcome and outputs, as well as data for managing activities and input,s will be collected taking into account the following issues: Tracking of monthly teacher and student attendance Regular feedback from parent-teacher associations, Formal and informal chats, interviews & interactions with the students and teachers, Regular check visits by the staff to the targeted schools, Beneficiary complaint forms and feedback
