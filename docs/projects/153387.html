Project Summary

Dispute the revitalized peace agreement signed 12/9/2018 in Addis Ethiopia to resolved the conflict of South Sudan ,the populations of IDP have expressed very little hope to return to their home state, due to ongoing displacement of civilian in many parts of the region and related to poor accessibility of humanitarian services to support the most indeed.
Currently Melut hosting over 30,000 internal Displaced people with an estimated population of over 24,063 host community nearby IDP populations, HLSS with support of CCCM national clusters have remain concerned in providing humanitarian support that to ensure equal and needs based access to improve quality services for populations affected by displacement, and targeting the vulnerable population with priority on addressing protection gaps and building resilience where women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialized integrated services.
HLSS shall ensure equal and need based access to improve quality services for the populations affected by displacement in Melut IDP collective centres and shall maintain the already exiting CCCM mechanisms put in place and currently that support and strengthen site management with increased coordination among the service providers within the five collective centres with effort to streamline humanitarian service delivery in Melut county and shall ensure there is active participation of the local authority and community leadership. Key personnel including Site manager, Community service officers and information officer shall be deployed to actively engage with all stakeholders that able IDP populations to access key services through ongoing advocacy through the partners with a special emphasis on people with specific needs and also provide training on protection mainstreaming to the partners and HLSS staffs on ground.
Governance structure of the camp, HLSS shall continue to support the community leadership and shall further strengthen the already built relationship between the host communities and IDPs through strengthening the leadership capacities of IDPs and the host communities. In turn technical support shall be provided women, men, girls and boys and people with special needs (PSNs).
HLSS shall continue with community engagement and training of the participants that illustrate the commitment and improve level of knowledge following training on CCCM protection mainstreaming and humanitarian response therefore, key activities HLSS shall consider all the committees participation are very important especial women, boys, girls and PSNs all the five (05) collective centres shall conduct Monthly meetings to ensure information are share freely and join decision are taken for implementation of the humanitarian service within the five collective centres for effective services delivery to the entire population of IDP.
HLSS shall continue to ensure essential site infrastructure and utilities such as water points, water drainage systems during rainy season are managed and maintained and shared pit latrines are well maintained to minimize spread of communicable diseases within the settlements. HLSS hopes to support at-risk groups and person with specific needs (PSNs) to sustain their capacity to access some basic humanitarian assistance and look in to their wellbeing in all the collective centres of IDP populations.

Needs

Since the December 2013 crisis in South Sudan IDPs have continue to seek refuge in several part of the country including the PoC sites, collective centres, and settlement, the population of IDP citing insecurity as the main driver of the decision to remain in the displacement camp. As results of continue displacement Melut continue to host over 30,000 IDPs in five collective centres Dingthoma I & II, New Paloch Primary school, Khor-Adar and Melak, lack of basic services to populations at the site remain the key challenges and this situation undermines the human dignity and safety of the affected displaced population who are often deprived of livelihoods and security. The population continues to be exposed on protection risks particularly vulnerable groups such as children, people with disabilities and old people and they suffer the most intense consequences of sustained displacement violence and lack of access to basic services remain at high demands throughout, the first round allocation of SSHF 2018 project implementation by HLSS in Melut IDP collective centers and clearly indicated that needs to support the IDPs through CCCM attention for continue effective and efficient services coordination and mainstreaming protection in all the services by other humanitarian agencies that to create sense of service and promote co-existence among the IDP population remain in high demands. Beneficiaries needs continue to being increasing through data from the site complaint and feedback mechanisms as well as the report from Bi-weekly and block monitoring from all the sites ( Dingthoma 1 & 2, New Paluch Primary school IDP, Khoradar and Malek) the lack of protection services made it difficult after withdrawal of DRC in 2016 from Melut IDP collective centres, however the humanitarian situation in some parts of Upper Nile region continues to experience a gradual improvement in access to basic services like health care, Protection, WASH, Education and FSL, and consequentially leads to a cycle of strained humanitarian service provision which require humanitarian response and to bridge the existing gaps within the IDP populations of concern. Coordination of services with other humanitarian agencies remain key in camp like setting, hence HLSS shall involve all the camp leaders and host community to participation in humanitarian coordination that aim to provide good leadership and strengthen the camp governance structures, therefore basing on what has been achieved through the first and second SSHF round allocation of fund have created since of responsibilities and leadership in governance structures but there still needs for the continuity of the support to the IDP collective centres as a number of challenges were identified such as protection concerns as there are no protection agents on ground to support emergency referrals, provision of the WASH assistance and assistant to PSN and strengthening of the community governance structure on the ground for effective services monitoring and implementation support to the population of IDPs. Thus Health Link South Sudan proposing to streamline protection activities within the already existing activities. And there continues to be site care and maintenance challenges thus the continuity of this project shall support a lot further with the coordination for advocacy for the complains raised by the IDPs to the implementing partners continues to be an issues that need to be managed by CCCM management committee on ground.
