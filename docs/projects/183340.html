Project Summary

The proposed integrated and equity-focused intervention will provide WASH kits for 1,700 (Current LHF Standard Allocation Fund beneficiaries) in North Lebanon, (Al Batroun, Al Koura, Minnieh-Dennieh, Bsharri, Tripoli, Zgharta) between August 2021 and April 2022. CARE International in Lebanon (CIL) and its local partner, RMF (Rene Mouawad Foundation).
In this proposed intervention and in line with CIL’s partnership approach of implementing through partners, CIL shall be responsible for the implementation of the work plan, building and refining of RMF’s capacities, procuring Wash Kits, preparing the ( Information Education and Communication) IEC materials, disseminating the crosscutting messages and awareness sessions, validating the vulnerability assessments scoring, and selecting the eligible beneficiaries, as well as field monitoring of the distribution and leading the PDM process whilst RMF will be responsible for the outreach activities, identification of beneficiaries (through internal and external referrals provided by CIL) and organizing field and door-to-door distributions of the Wash Kits.
The 1,700 beneficiaries of the LHF Standard Allocation are 350 Lebanese households (4HHM) and 100 Migrant households (3HHM) (As recommended by the FSS project selection criteria)

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

CIL works closely with local and national authorities, as well as affected communities to ensure that protection and gender are mainstreamed throughout the intervention by following existing capacities and procedures to ensure equal access to aid for vulnerable households. The assessment will be undertaken by the established CIL team and local partner prior to distributions. During the assessments, any threatening and cautious cases will be referred to the CIL accountability and helpline department. CIL is focused on integrating PSEA messages and information on access to services and shares them with the targeted households. Messages will be tailored to the needs of specific groups will be developed with a focus on their specific needs and priorities. CIL will conduct a GBV risk analysis for the intervention before distributing the WASH Kits, with an aim to assist CIL and RMF to mitigate possible GBV risks.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

CIL is committed to the universally recognized Core Humanitarian Standards, which include a commitment to safe and responsive mechanisms to handle complaints. Furthermore, an effective Feedback Accountability Mechanism (FAM) is central to CIL’s duty of care to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). To safeguard such accountability, the project will ensure consultation at the community level at the design, testing, and implementation phases. This will be done through CIL’s community-based design methods and proven techniques. Discussions with community members will take place to ensure data is relevant and needed, applying a do-no-harm approach throughout the intervention. CIL will facilitate consultations at the community level with People in Need, local authorities, and key community members, including volunteers to better understand the needs of targeted communities.
