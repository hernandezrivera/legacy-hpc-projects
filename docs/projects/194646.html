Project Summary

In the proposed action Save the Children seeks to implement integrated WASH interventions to reduce the burden of WASH related diseases as aggravating factors for malnutrition locations presenting an extreme WASH vulnerability and high prevalence of malnutrition. Services provided will break the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhea), thus improving food intake of malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition. The proposed action will also seek to address WASH needs in during emergencies such as response to floods and disease outbreaks. The proposed interventions will target Akobo, Tonj South, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North , Budi counties and Abyei which have a WASH severity classification of 3 and 4, communities high GAM and in which Save the Children has operational presence.
The proposed WASH activities include; construction/rehabilitation of water points in communities , water quality monitoring, hygiene promotion, construction of latrines in communities, WASH in Nutrition/health care facilities distribution of WASH kits , soap distribution and mitigation of Gender Based Violence, GBV through training of WASH staff on basic skills to provide survivors with information on where they can obtain support and GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) are incorporated into hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.
Due to the conflict and flood situation in Abyei , the proposed action will also target to improve WASH services for 20,000 people ( 10,082, female , 9,918 male ) in Abyei . Main activities will be rehabilitation of existing non operational water points , construction of emergency latrines , WASH in health and nutrition facilities , WASH NFIs distribution coupled with hygiene messaging.
The proposed interventions align well with the South Sudan
WASH cluster objectives 1; contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition, 2.1. Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties and 2.2. WASH specific activities for GBV (or mitigating WASH GBV)
Save the children targets a total of 84,438 beneficiaries (32,960 Male, 31,478 Females) across the five counties. The estimated cost to deliver the proposed WASH interventions is USD 2,500,000

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, WASH needs have been exacerbated by displacements, insecurity, economic downturn and historically low coverage of water infrastructure. Increasing insecurity, both ethnic and politically motivated conflict, coupled with localized violence and disputes over land, has been an underlying driver of WASH conditions in almost all states, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services while also restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure.According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022, It is estimated that in 2022 over 6.1 million people will be in need of WASH services. The WASH needs are severe in Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Based on the WASH severity classification the counties classified with the greatest needs are Tonj East, Fangak, Panyikang, Canal-Pigi and Twic East. only 39 per cent of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34 per cent in rural areas. In certain states, such as Unity, Warrap, Jonglei and Upper Nile, 33 per cent of households travel for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities. Difficulty in securing enough water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49 per cent of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. Overall, 80 per cent of households did not access a latrine. This aggregated figure however hides some extreme geographical variations. In certain states, such as Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei, the percentage of households accessing a latrine is consistently below 10 per cent, with open defecation the norm. On the opposite side of the spectrum, Western Equatoria has the highest proportion of households (60 per cent) accessing a latrine. Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Overall, only 16 per cent of the households reported owning soap and this figure was as low as 4 per cent and 11 per cent in Jonglei and Upper Nile States respectively.

2 - Project activities

The proposed WASH activities include; construction/rehabilitation of water points in communities , water quality monitoring, hygiene promotion, construction of latrines in communities, WASH in Nutrition/health care facilities distribution of WASH kits , soap distribution and mitigation of Gender Based Violence, GBV through training of WASH staff on basic skills to provide survivors with information on where they can obtain support and GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) are incorporated into hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility. The proposed action will adopt mitigation measures that address barriers to access to WASH services for people with disabilities and the elderly. To better understand the needs of the people living with disabilities and address their barriers to accessing WASH services, the WASH cluster Disability Inclusion checklist will be updated and the findings used to inform the program design. Priority communities and villages will be selected in consultation with WASH cluster subnational coordinator, WASH cluster partners and with support of the Nutrition partner. Communities and villages with nutrition facilities with no/limited access to WASH services, highest GAM prevalence and diarrheal disease incidence based on data available at nutrition facilities and sourcing drinking water from an unsafe source with a high risk of contamination with human excreta will be selected.Within the selected communities and villages, the most vulnerable households will be targeted. The most vulnerable households within the community are the households with a pregnant and lactating woman, female-headed household, households with a disabled and/or elderly, etc.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Several opportunities will be availed during the course of the project; at the inception, meetings will be organized with different stakeholders with representation of women, men, girls, boys and the elderly as well as youth to share the details of the project and its objectives and activities to be done, with details of the target and mode of implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project staff will train the community leaders , community volunteers on the Save the Children feedback and complaint mechanism . Which will include information desk , face to face meetings , suggestion boxes and toll free numbers which beneficiaries can use to provide information. Save the Children at the Country Office has developed online dashboard where analysis of all the feedback and complaints received are reflected and the closure status of the feedback loop can be visualized for each sector , each project and the locations . Before implementation and during implementation , the project staff will adequately provide information to the beneficiaries on the feedback and complaint mechanism put in place . Save the Children will proactively through engagement of community structures seek out those who are the most marginalized including girls, adolescent wives and mothers, children out of school, children living without appropriate care, and children with disability, to ensure they can access our program. Community and beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be established through an Accountability to Affected Persons Committee (AAP committee) that will be established, trained on ways they can monitor and report complaints with scheduled engagements with SC community mobilizers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will ensure all staff, volunteers and consultants upon recruitment, are oriented on child safeguarding, safe programming and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), information shared and acknowledge understanding the policies, sign and adhere to them. Clear reporting and response systems will also be put in place at the field level from community to entire field operations which is part of SC standards of operating. SC has PSEA and CSG focal persons at the field offices and they will oversee the monitoring and reporting of incidents, conduct refresher information-sharing during the course of the project. They will also establish reporting systems that is accessible, easy to use by all staff and stakeholders to maintain its functionality.Once the staff and operations have PSEA incorporated in the work system, information sharing will be enhanced and available to all beneficiaries at all times. At the community level, PSEA messaging will be shared at all meetings and gatherings from the time of inception of the projects and during implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries will be collected through different means including verbally and written. Community mobilisers will ensure that all beneficiaries are fully aware of their right to provide feedback and how they can do so. Information will be shared with them on beneficiary selection and targeting at the inception and during the project implementation as well as during monitoring visits and at the end of the project cycle.Beneficiaries will be deliberately asked to participate in meetings to gather their feedback and also encouraged to provide feedback directly to staff or through the selected AAP focal persons who would have been introduced to them at the start of the project or at the meetings.Feedback gathered will be compiled and detailed in monitoring reports and applied when planning continuing activities if it is established before the project ends but also used to inform project re-design in next phases by SC or other stakeholders targeting the same.SC will share all feedback through the coordination platforms for WASH at the county and state level. All feedback will be analyses through our MEAL staff and applied to our programs not only for WASH, but also other sectors as well.Feedback will also be adapted to strengthen relationships/rapport with beneficiaries, encourage community engagement and participation for ownership and sustainability of interventions.
