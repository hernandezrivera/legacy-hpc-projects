Project Summary

As the crisis continues and schools are destroyed or inaccessible to students, COVID 19 has been an added threat to them as the virus tends to spread quickly when in large crowds and so the schools that can be found are not equipped to take in students. The objective of this project is designed to match the second strategic objective and to create a safety program for continuing education reaching total beneficiaries 7525 ( 7282 children and 243 teachers and educational personnel ), and boosting community engagement and responsibilities for children in NW Syria. A focus will be on the IDPs ın camps high priority sub-districts of Dana, Harim, and Maaret Tamsrin in Idleb governorate, through formal and non-formal education programs, and inclusive Support for 9 schools.
This project is a continuation of existing education by Maram Foundation, as well as scaling up education activities in schools. the project will contribute to Help to create a safer and more secure environment for children. The project will focus on children aged 5-7 years in and out of school by providing set of services that targets the gaps and needs of the most vulnerable groups while ensuring the centrality of protection.
MF is keening to achieve the following:
Increase access 7282 Children (3815 girls & 3467 boys,110 CwD, 5 – 17 years old) affected by conflict in NW Syria to education services, by providing formal and non-formal education programs, divided as following: 5932 children will attend formal education classes and 1350 children will attend non-formal education for one cycle in the same school in line with the distribution of school bags in 9 schools in Dana, Harim and Mareet Masrin sub-district, also operational cost and other needed expenses will be provided, MF will conduct 9 BTL campaigns, focusing on encouraging both students and their parents to get back to schools.
Increase access 7282 Children (3815 girls & 3467 boys,110 CwD, 5 – 17 years old) to a clean, sterile and safe environment in 9 schools in NW Syria, by conducting light-scale rehabilitation 50 classes due to usage during passing last academic year(e.g: one window in every class, door, desk, etc), ensuring accessibility for CWDs, providing heating materials—Fuel-based heater and fuel— and conducting precautionary measures in schools such as measuring temperature regularly, social distancing and distribution of masks in those targeted schools.
Improve the quality of educational services within a safe and protective learning environment, by conducting a training program for 216 teachers. on PSS,EIE,INEE, educational subjects, child-centered and protective pedagogy, and PSEA. Trained teachers will reflect on their in-school attitude and will enhance relations and communications with students. Also, incentives will be provided for teachers and educational personnel.
The inclusion of CWD will take place throughout most of the project activities, for example, the rehabilitation will take into consideration CWD's needs, training for staff will be conducted on how to deal with those.
MF will ensure the participation of all relevant stakeholders at all stages of the operations management cycle. MF will be responsible for the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation (M&E), and closure phases. MF will manage the project, provide the contractual services, ensure the required coordination among clusters, and ensure high-quality results and efficient progress through the provision of capable programmatic specialized staff. in this project, the cost per beneficiary will 98.80 $.
There is no secured fund yet.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase.71% of school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme, or catastrophic education conditions mainly concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb and Rural Damascus governorates, an increase to last year’s 86%. Due to the compounding impact of the 2020 situation, many students are either behind in their learning, finding it difficult to return to school or at risk of dropping out.MF also conducted a technical needs assessment in Apr 20201 for 9 existing schools, that highlighted:The importance of providing incentives for 243 teachers and educational personnel to ensure quality educationlight rehabilitation is needed in (e.g., whiteboards, desks, lighting) due to usage during passing last academic year.Schools supplies and operating costs for 9 schools is needed for items such as stationary, cleaning supplies, fire extinguishers, and first aid kits.School kits and stationery for 7282 children segregated by age and gender is needed for all children in formal and non-formal education settings.capacity building program for teachers focuses on PSEA, EIE, INEE,educational subjects, Child Safeguarding policies. This training will be essential for new teachers since the teacher rotation is very high and as refresher training for old staff.

5 - Implementation of activities

MF will directly implement the activities since Maram has full access due to their previous project activities based on good relations with the local communities and IDP representative communities. The Project is focusing on provide non-formal and formal education programs. As the non-formal education will work to refer children to formal education after completion of cycle. The project will launch upon signing a memorandum of understanding with the Education Directorate as should be re-signed yearly. The face-to-face modality will be adopted in this project, given that the precautionary COVID responses which are encouraging social distancing measures, Maram field team will limit the hourly classes to be as short as 30 minutes with increasing the frequency of each class to contain small group in each classroom. The light rehabilitation is justified by that the frequent use of schools cause some minor damage. MF will rehabilitate students' desks, doors, windows, ..etc.MF has already designed monitoring and evaluation tools that ensure accountability of the affected populations, also it has provided beneficiaries with these tools. Monitoring and evaluation will be in line with all project phases, starting from initiation until the close-out phase. The monitoring tools that will be used: surveys, on-site observation reports, meetings with community key informants, interviews, and pictures. Besides, M&E staff has established many channels for complaints mechanism, and they were oversighting these channels that allow beneficiaries at all ages to send their feedback and complains anonymously. cost per person will be 98.80 $ through this project.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Learning facilities could be attacked. *Mitigation: To reduce the risk of a military-related attack learning facilities can be deconflicted using the deconfliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA, in addition to ensuring all schools have an evacuation plan in place to be prepared to any unforeseen hostilities. Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. *Mitigation: Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct. The code of conduct should cover the conduct of teachers and other education personnel that enables learning spaces to being welcoming, inclusive safe, violence-free and promote well-being and a protective environment Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by adults such as teachers, school personnel, organization staff, other adults or older students. *Mitigation: Child Safeguarding polices and related systems should be part of any education programing and should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children.*Mitigation:Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)policies should be in place. MF is a member of PSEA network so when it comes to sensitive complaints they are usually referred to PSEA focal point persons, which are the HR manager and M&E manager, therefore they deal with such cases in high confidentiality. Besides MF conducted trainings on PSEA as for all its personnel.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Local authorities remain supportive of educational activities and do not deny the need of education;-Target schools are not threatened or occupied by armed groups, always community people are the safe place to go when facing problems with armed groups.- the risk of having difficulties in purchasing the needed materials from the local markets, therefore MF will make sure to get the needed materials ready for the required activities in advance, so we could avoid such situations, not to mention our own past experiences in the field in the same areas, helped us to find new ways and methods to minimize the risks;- MF Organization has conducted many projects in these areas and we have very good community acceptance. All these conditions will play an important role to pave the way for children (girls, boys) and youth to have access to education;- Maram coordinates with the education ministry in unsupported school selection. - as the situation is stabilized and no displacement, the parents will not prevent children from school enrollment.- Schools are open, and no Covid-19 virus outbreakRisk1:Interference by local authorities Likelihood: Possible.
