Project Summary

In 2023 The proposed intervention aims at ensuring food security level of Most Food Insecure people for the conflict-affected Syrian populations in NWS with the focus on the IDPs as being among the most vulnerable groups. Within the targeted population the hosted people will benefited through this intervention, as well as the consequences of the Covid-19 pandemic that largely restricted their ability to find the minimum requirements of life and sustain livelihood. Furthermore, the enormous inflation to the local currency among other conflict-related circumstances also added to the sufferings of the targeted populations.
Food Basket Distribution:
The proposed intervention aims to target around 1000 HH / 5000 IDPs with food baskets that will be designed according to the FSL cluster guidelines. In Ma'aretmisrin (Kelly) area, that is sufficient to reach well nutritional requirements for a family of 5 individuals for a month. This allocation will be for 8 months as a direct distribution in NW Syria camps, with ensure coordinating with FSL cluster and local councils with fully reports. Distribution period will be started around March 2023 and durated until the end of 2023 as planned 8 rounds. The procurement process will be through a tender to ensure a competitive process by suppliers.
1000 Men / 1000 Women / 1500 Boys / 1500 Girls / 5000 Total
Free Bread Distribution:
The proposed intervention aims to target 1000 HH / 5000 IDPs daily with free bread distribution that will be designed according to the FSL cluster guidelines. In Ma'aretmisrin (Kelly) area, that is sufficient to reach well nutritional requirements for a family of 5 individuals for a month, that will be distributed around 180,000 bread bundles. This allocation will be for 6 months as a direct distribution in NW Syria camps, with ensure coordinating with FSL cluster and local councils with fully reports. The distribution period will be started around March 2023 and durated until September 2023 as planned 6 rounds for the same beneficiaries. The procurement process will be through opened tender inside Syria to to ensure the competitive between the suppliers.
1000 Men / 1000 Women / 1500 Boys / 1500 Girls / 5000 Total
Livelihoods Projects (Supporting Agricultural Value Chains as Olive, Wheat):
Olive Value Chain:
HAT will implement a saplings distribution in 5 sub-districts in Idlib governorate, this allocation funded by HA UK to support the food security through olive cultivation in NW Syria in targets:
1- Increase the olive production in NW Syria.
2- Contribute to increase the green lands in NW Syria.
52,500 olive saplings will be distributed in the first half of February in (Idlib, Harem, Ma'arretmisrin, Bennish, Salqine) for 1050 farmers whom owned at least 2.5 donums of lands, each HH will be benefited by 50 saplings to plant by himself. HAT conducted a need assessment and registration process in the mentioned areas. This intervention will secure a good livelihood income to the beneficiaries after 3-4 years by selling the olive crops when it start fruiting and contribute to enhance the food security for the families. HAT staff following the agriculture cluster coordination. Through this intervention there are several categories will benefit from this project as (widows, orphans, disabilities, poor) either hosted or IDPs.
1050 Men / 1050 Women / 1550 Boys / 1600 Girls / 5250 Total
Wheat Value Chain:
This intervention aims to support the farmers by fertilizing stage (Urea), in coordination with the cluster and local council, 104 farmers will be benefited through this project in Ma'arretmisrin sub-district in Idlib governorate by provide a kit 150kg of Urea fertilizer in February. The project will contribute to alleviate the huge expenses of the wheat cultivation for the farmers whom already planted one hectare of wheat. This contribute to enhance the food security for 520 individuals in NW Syria.
104 Men / 104 Women / 156 Boys / 156 Girls / 520 Total

3 - Needs

The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels.The impact of Syria’s crisis continues to hinder people’s ability to meet their immediate food needs and livelihoods, in a context marked by very complex drivers and contributing factors. This includes economic worsening and weakened local currency, soaring food and non-food prices, as well as drought like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity, and limited energy supply. In addition, food and agriculture commodities prices increase, inflation, lack of purchasing power due to limited livelihood and lack of income sources, population movements that shows no sign of abating. The WFP Syria (FSA, FSLA) estimates that seven in ten Syrian families is food insecure with the highest concentration in Idleb (74 per cent). Idleb Governorate continues to be impacted by the instable security situation and the widespread economic crisis, which hit local communities as well.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoid causing harm: Concern for the safety and dignity of individuals and avoiding causing further harm is central to mainstreaming protection. The safety of beneficiaries should be the primary consideration in humanitarian action. 2. Ensuring meaningful access to assistance and services: Recognized as a crucial component of all humanitarian interventions, will must seek to ensure that people have a meaningful opportunity to attain their rights by accessing available services and assistance. Barriers to access can exist indifferent forms, including logistical and social/cultural factors, insecurity and a lack of information on available assistance and services. 3. Accountability: Setting-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. 4. Participation and empowerment: The humanitarian community should strive to put affected population at the center of decision-making processes that affect their lives so that services are choice-driven and client-focused. Special attention should also be paid to ensuring the participation of, and consultation with, the most at-risk and/or marginalized members of society whose voices may not otherwise be heard. 5- Some trainings are given to the field team, and the beneficiaries are instructed to access for note their complaints in the event of any abuse by the field team.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1- Make sure that all staff sign the Code of Conduct declaration. 2- Train all staff in PSEA and be seen to take specific steps to prevent it. For example, discuss PSEA action plans in office meetings, meet with government officials to discuss prevention, discuss the issue with partners. 3- Provide specialized services in support of sexual exploitation by provide instructions to the beneficiaries and setting up phone numbers in the event of a complaint. 4- Engage in community outreach by provide people about the risks of sexual exploitation through many media resources. 5- Providing support to beneficiaries who have been subjected to harassment or sexual exploitation by educating them for the future, raising their morale, and ways to reach those responsible for their defense and protection. 6-Work to activate methods of control over field employees with the help of those responsible for the beneficiaries to ensure that the beneficiaries, whether women or children, are not alone and exploited. 7- Ensuring the rights of the beneficiaries always by adhering to the humanitarian standards of the United Nations and not tolerating any violations of field staff. 8- Programs identify risks of harm to children and women of beneficiaries and address them appropriately in project design the project proposals are accompanied by an assessment of the risks and harm inflicted on children and women in order to avoid these risks.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Some of distribution places near to frontlines and there might be aerial or artillery bombardment.Mitigation: Through the security department to confirm that the distribution place is safe coordinating with the local councils, arrange training by security department how to deal with these situations.Risk2: Fear for some abuses for against the women and children.Mitigation: Constantly, HR department arrange PSEA training for the field staff, also M&E field staff arrange awareness sessions for the beneficiaries with informing how to note a compliant during the complaint box or the numbers of accountability which noted on the banners to not be any exceed from the field staff side. Risk3: Prices increasing in the market. Mitigation: we get some precautions for this situation by propose 10% extra on the current price.Risk4: The supplier changes quantity or quality of the items.Mitigation: The field staff ask the supplier for samples and go for a visit to his warehouse before the distribution about 3 days to check the items, if there is any change in the distribution day the distribution leader will stop and delay the distribution to another day until commit in the BOQs of items.Risk5: Covid-19 is surrounding and increasing in the gathering. Mitigation: arrange training for the field staff and awareness session for the beneficiaries about the social distancing and using the covid equipment with checking their selves in the health center if there corona symptom.
