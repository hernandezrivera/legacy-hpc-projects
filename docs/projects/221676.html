Project Summary

Through this project, Action Against Hunger is aiming to target a total of 180,800 (86,784 males and 94,016 females) vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host community. Our areas of intervention will primarily be Fangak, Duk and Pibor Counties of Jonglei State, Gogrial West and Gogrial East counties of Warrap state and Aweil East County of Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Our response activities will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, and protection mainstreaming. All the under 5 children, pregnant and breastfeeding women will be screen for malnutrition at the health facilities and enrolled to our nutrition programming. All our health facilities will have minimum package of WASH services. We will consider protection aspect, and our medical team will be train on SGBV counselling and management. We will take into consideration the protection area by providing the first aid care to the SGBV victims and MHPSS as well as identifying and strengthening the referral pathway for clinical management of rape and mental health cases. Our response will focus on returnees, IDPs and host communities. The response plan will be prioritized based on epidemiological prole of disease outbreaks, such as cholera hot spot locations, measles outbreaks and malaria seasonality. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and disability tracking tools will be used to determine the feasibility of the response. Our operational plans will always involve the community members, women, girls, youths, PLWs (people living with disability), County Health department (CHD) in planning, MOH and Humanitarian partners in our operational areas. Meetings with communities and CHDs are some of the indicators used to monitor the accountability for affected populations. We will facilitate meetings with all stakeholders like partners, beneficiaries, women groups and local communities for their involvement in planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects in all stages of the program cycle. To improve access to essential life-saving health care services, and to address inaccessibility and long distance to access health services, the organization will use outreach clinics through community health volunteers BHI/ICCM to provide health services at the community as well as static health facilities (PHCCs and PHCCs) to provide Basic Package of health and nutrition services as per South Sudan guideline to achieve the following objectives:

1. Needs

Currently, South Sudan is experiencing a steep escalation in humanitarian needs, with 9 million individuals including refugees are in need as per 2024 HNRP. The situation is attributed to the factors such as flooding, food insecurity, inflation, subnational conflict and influx of returnees and refugees due to Sudan crisis. There are a number of complex challenges that are faced in the proposed areas of intervention (Pibor/Kizionora, Fangak, Du, Gogrial East, Gogrial West and Aweil East) including displacement of populations, losses of livelihoods, property, assets all of which contribute to people engaging in negative coping strategies especially in Pibor, Fangak and Duk Counties. The main health problems in the proposed counties are malnutrition, malaria, acute respiratory infections, diarrheal and measles among children particularly under-five. The key health services provided in the mentioned counties are outpatient and inpatient consultations, maternal and child health care, immunization, integrated community case management, nutrition and minimum water, sanitation and hygiene however the service providers are straggling due to lack of funding. Gogrial West County is one of the largest counties in South Sudan, with 37 health facilities of which HSTP supports only 14 health facilities and Action Against Hunger supports 5 health facilities. It is one of the major influx points of returnees due to the conflict in Sudan from which they then disperse to other locations within South Sudan. Both Gogrial East and West have suffered from inter-communal violence driven by border and land disputes as well as competition for natural resources and cattle raiding. According to the South Sudan Health Cluster Dashboard, Gogrial West and East are among the counties with the highest number of people in need of health services. Gogrial East has 19 health facilities (of which HSTP supports only 13), and Action Against Hunger supports 3 PHCU and provides mobile health outreaches at Mobiortiit and Malual Kuel in Gogrial East. Aweil County has a total of 22 health facilities but only 8 are functional including 5 PHCCs, 2 PHCUs and Aweil County Hospital. The county suffers from high levels of inaccessibility, particularly during the rainy season with large areas prone to flooding. This has led to reduced availability of food stuffs in the market, increased prices and heightened levels of food insecurity. Generally, primary healthcare access in Warrap, Jonglei and Northern Bahr el-Ghazal states is poor leading to huge disease burden and deaths particularly among infants. Limited financing, weakened healthcare infrastructure and governance, lack of medical supplies and equipment and some cultural beliefs are the key constraints undermining healthcare delivery and outcomes in the proposed locations. The targeted locations are among the counties with the highest number of people in need of health services (Health Cluster).

2. Project activities

The project will focus on implementing the following activities to address critical health challenges and strengthen service delivery:1. Boma Health Initiative (BHI)/Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM)To improve access and coverage of health services, the BHI/ICCM approach will be utilized to diagnose and treat common childhood illnesses, such as mild malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea, at the community level.• Boma Health Teams (BHTs) and Community-Based Distributors (CBDs) will be trained and equipped with:o Rapid Diagnostic Tests (mRDTs) for malariao Respiratory timers, thermometerso Oral antimalarial drugs, amoxicillin, zinc tablets, and Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS).• BHTs/CBDs will serve as the first referral point, referring the complicated cases to the nearby health facilities for advanced care.2. Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI)To enhance immunization coverage and respond to outbreaks:• Mobile vaccination teams will deliver services to communities, while static vaccination sites at health facilities will ensure wider coverage.• The organization will collaborate with UNICEF to provide essential equipment, such as Arktek refrigerators for hard-to-reach areas.3. Outpatient Department (OPD) ServicesOPD services will provide consultation and treatment for common illnesses, including:• Simple malaria, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), acute respiratory infections (ARI), urinary tract infections (UTIs), skin and eye infections, and minor wounds/injuries.• Active case finding for TB/HIV defaulters, visceral leishmaniasis (Kala-Azar), and referrals to specialized TB/HIV or Kala-Azar treatment centers.4. Antenatal Care (ANC) and Maternity ServicesThe project will provide comprehensive ANC and maternity services, including:• Monitoring blood pressure and hemoglobin levels during each ANC visit.• Providing pregnant women with ferrous sulfate and folic acid, albendazole for deworming, malaria prophylaxis, and rapid diagnostic testing for malaria.• Screening for syphilis, HIV, and other STIs.• Supporting normal deliveries at health facilities and referring complicated cases to Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC) facilities.5. Disease Outbreak ManagementThe project will strengthen outbreak response for diseases such as cholera, measles, Anthrax, hepatitis E Virus and Mpox by:• Enhancing Early Warning, Alert, and Response Systems (EWARS) and Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR).• Conducting training and coordinating with key stakeholders, including the Health Cluster, WHO, Ministry of Health (MoH), and County Health Departments (CHD).6. Support for Survivors of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV)The project will focus on:• Identifying SGBV survivors and providing psychological support and first aid treatment.• Delivering Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services and referring survivors to protection partners for further assistance.• Leveraging existing projects to enhance activities under the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP).7. Screening for Acute MalnutritionTo integrate health and nutrition services, the project will:• Train health workers and outreach teams to identify acute malnutrition in under five children and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) measurements.• Link malnourished individuals to nutrition programs for appropriate care.8. Support for People Living with Disabilities (PLWDs) and Mental Health Services through:• Identifying PLWDs and individuals with mental health conditions within the community.• Providing basic healthcare at health facilities and referring cases to specialized centers as needed.9. Humanitarian Response and Reporting• The organization will leverage its presence in three states of Jonglei, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states to contribute to the strategic plan of health cluster, WHO and MOH in the year 2025.
