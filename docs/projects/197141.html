Project Summary

The proposed project will help mitigate service degradation due to physical damage to water and sanitation infrastructure in Syria and enhance its climate change resilience. The challenge for the Syrian WASH Sector will be to identify strategic entry points to resume WASH service delivery. If this is not dealt with on time, there will be a "vicious cycle" whereby insufficient maintenance services, damage and destruction, and non-delivery of services compound on each other, leading to unrecoverable WASH infrastructure and services. The WASH sector is significantly affected by climate change and environmental degradation, as by the consequences of the current prolonged drought. Each stream of UNICEF’s humanitarian WASH response in Syria presents opportunities to act on climate, energy, and environmental issues to deliver more sustainable results, including addressing the water scarcity crisis. The “vicious cycle” of cumulative impact on all three components (people, hardware, and consumables) during the current protracted armed conflict in Syria may lead to a situation that is too technically difficult or simply too expensive to reverse. UNICEF will therefore repair and rehabilitate critical parts of water supply systems and infrastructure while being guided by the principle of “Minimum reliable services for All – rather than All services for Some”. Several criteria will be used for the project identification and selection, including cost per participant, level of project complexity, neutrality, accessibility, severity scale, number of returnees, quality and quantity of water, and sanitation services currently available.
Most rural and urban areas in Syria are witnessing electricity shortages, which have affected the operation of water systems. Electricity generation has severely declined in 2021 and 2022 and is at its worst levels since the beginning of the crisis. Most wells, water pumping stations, and main water tanks, especially in rural areas depend on diesel generators, which makes them more vulnerable to outages, price gouging, and the inability to Water. Boards to secure the required amounts of fuel to operate wells, water pumping stations, and main water tanks. UNICEF is committed to the longer-term sustainable restoration of water services not only by conducting the rehabilitation of damaged infrastructure but also by using solar power as an alternative solution for the electricity and fuel shortage, where it is possible in affected areas, to ensure the provision of clean water to people in need.
UNICEF will continue providing life-saving WASH interventions, as well as distributing supplies of non-food items related to WASH to the most vulnerable population in Syria. Preparation actions and contingency planning will also be maintained at a high level, together with stocks of necessary materials for responding rapidly to changing needs if access becomes more difficult or mass displacements occur in the northwest and/or northeast Syria. Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA), which is an alternative form of supplying hygiene kits and/or services related to WASH, will be promoted in the northwest.
The cholera outbreak that started in August 2022 also added to the vulnerability of the Syrian population, especially in the northern governorates. As part of the Cholera response, UNICEF will ensure appropriate lifesaving WASH prevention and response measures to control the spread of AWD/cholera, with special attention to the at-risk populations and improving IPC interventions at institutions. Also, UNICEF will support awareness raising as an integral part of all projects with a focus on risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) for Cholera prevention. The proposed project aims to reach at least 15,400,000 beneficiaries in 204 subdistricts (72% are high-severity areas) in 2023 which represents 70% of the total population for 2023.

3 - Needs

Up to 52% of the population is relying on alternative and unsafe water supply modalities to meet or complement their water needs. At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency. Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management, and hygiene supplies will remain a challenge for more than two million IDPs who rely on continued humanitarian assistance, with 95% of IDP sites having received WASH humanitarian assistance. Also, 43% of IDPs could not access one or multiple hygiene items and 32% faced barriers to effective handwashing. Disruptions and poor quality of services in IDP sites have also direct negative physical and mental well-being and protection consequences, notably on women and girls. The effects of climate change, cumulative drought trends, the collapse of the energy sector, groundwater overexploitation, lack of international treaties on water use, and the cholera outbreak that was declared by MOH Syria in Sept 2022. The outbreak is mainly attributed to the deteriorating socio-economic conditions, WASH system decay and the water crisis in northern Syria, which triggered a significant increase in waterborne diseases across the country. The outbreak is spreading fast and as of the 5th of November, 35,569 suspected and 1,491 confirmed cholera cases including 92 associated deaths.

4 - Implementation of activities

UNICEF will implement the proposed interventions through partners in close collaboration with WASH sector stakeholders and governmental counterparts. For public infrastructure, UNICEF will coordinate with the Ministry of Water Resources, Ministry of Local Administration and Environment, WASH sector, and local authorities. UNICEF uses these implementation and partnership modalities: (1) Direct contracting and procurement by UNICEF which has full control of the whole project and procurement cycle. UNICEF will mobilize third-party monitors who will support the utilities in the technical design preparation according to national standards. For WASH interventions, long-term agreements are in place with private-sector contractors (2) Programme Partnership Agreements or Small-Scale Funding Agreements. In these two modalities, UNICEF advances funds to the partner in tranches and monitors the implementation. UNICEF in Syria has continuously strengthened its field presence by establishing 6 field offices to ensure program monitoring. UNICEF Syria field offices at the governorate level will be directly responsible for implementing the proposed interventions with daily field supervision; the Head of Offices in Damascus and Gaziantep will be responsible for quality assurance, technical support to field offices, standard-setting, and coordination with relevant stakeholders. UNICEF has four monitoring modalities: partner monitoring, third-party monitoring, and feedback mechanisms.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Delays in obtaining government approvals for implementation (likelihood: medium) mitigation in place: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time. -Water distribution is not safe or accessible, especially for women, girls, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities (likelihood: low) mitigation in place: Consult separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design, and implementation phases to ensure that the distance of water collection points remains at a minimum distance. Ensure the accessibility of collective and low GBV risks WASH facilities to people with disabilities and promote gender-sensitive hygiene messages/actions. Train partners and contractors on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Also, UNICEF includes the standard mitigation measures in all design for the water tank, filling points and sanitation blocks by engaging women with the process of selecting the location, pavement of the roads, increase lighting and visibility in locations where females will approach. UNICEF, through its partners, will look to integrate suggestions from the local community that improve the implementation and encourage ownership, to ensure that the presence of the organization is not conducting harm within the community.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNICEF is co-chairing the PSEA in-country network with UNHCR since April 2018. In cooperation (and cost-sharing) with UNHCR and the UN RC Office, the recruitment of an International PSEA coordinator who will lead the coordination and support of UN common work on PSEA is ongoing. The PSEA coordinator will be based in the UN RC’s Office. Further, all UNICEF Syria staff and personnel have received the face-to-face training and completed the mandatory online training on PSEA. Concerning implementing partners, UNICEF is undertaking PSEA risk assessment for all implementing partners and assists in developing related Action Plans based on the findings from 2022 risk assessments Among the main points for action plans is the request from partners to roll out the PSEA training to all their staff. UNICEF further provided additional training activities on PSEA for most of the partners, requested them to complete the online training, and is providing them with financial and technical support. UNICEF is supporting partners to enhance the complaints mechanisms and raise awareness to the community on PSEA and provided implementing partners with complaint boxes and related communicationmaterials. Moreover, UNICEF has whistleblowing and reporting mechanisms and is supporting implementing partners in the due diligence process for reporting SEA cases and investigating any alleged SEA cases.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks -Water quality and quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards - Unequal distribution of services strengthen the position of armed groups or actors - Water distribution is not safe or accessible, especially for women, girls, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities -Risk of theft of chemicals such as Sodium Hypochlorite and WASH materials such as generators and construction materials by armed groups and used in conflict activities -Health risk associated with construction and exposure to raw sewage and gases -Lack of access affects implementation and monitoring -Delays in obtaining government approvals for implementation Assumptions: - Inflation rate is stable- Equipment and materials are available in a timely manner - National and local authorities are supportive -ensure the minimum distance of water collection points - Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when delivering assistance and service or in case access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups Mitigating measures: Raise capacities of staff and volunteers from implementing partners and third-party monitors, as well as targeting communities with awareness about SEA risks, and that humanitarian aid and services are rights. Apply mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff members and third-party monitors. Design specific complaint mechanisms to follow up on complaints, ensuring data protection and confidentiality
