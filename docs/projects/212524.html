Project Summary

The protracted Syrian crisis has disproportionately impacted women’s health both directly and indirectly. Direct attacks on health facilities has diminish utilization of healthcare. Indirect consequences include socioeconomic and sociopolitical effects of conflict, range from changing roles of women in the community, early or forced marriages, inadequate food or shelter and insecurity. Quality of services is further affected by the forced displacement of trained healthcare workers and interruptions in supply chains. According to the WoS-HeRAMS 2022 Q2 analysis, 41% of public hospitals are either not functioning or partially functioning, while 43% of primary health care (PHC) facilities are either partially functioning or not functioning at all . There is a notable decrease in hospitals and health facilities (HFs) providing services of basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care (BEmONC), comprehensive emergency obstetric and neonatal care (CEmONC) . According to MSNA 2022, 94% of households in Al-Hasakeh, report high costs for consultations, lab and diagnostic procedures as a key barrier to health access. This implies significant long-term effects on the health of pregnant women who may need to forgo health services due to non-affordability.
Reliable data regarding ANC access is lacking in NES, however, it is observed that many pregnant women do not access ANC due to inadequate service coverage, financial barriers, low health literacy, and poor health-seeking behaviors. Very young and old maternal age, high birth rates, and poor nutrition are other risk factors contributing to poor maternal and birth outcomes. Low birth weight, disabilities, and child malnutrition due to poor maternal health contribute to the vicious life cycle of ill health.
In 2024, the ‘Safe Motherhood’ maternal health program will scale up its service coverage by adding a second MMU to serve more beneficiaries in communities with limited access to maternal health services. During these mobile unit visits, pregnant mothers will receive WHO standard basic package of ANC services which include physical assessment of the mothers, foetal ultrasound examinations, maternal health and nutrition screening, medication, and counselling. Women are expected to access at least 5 ANC visits during their pregnancy. Women will receive general maternal health education from a maternal health office while waiting to receive services. Malnourished PLWs identified using MUAC screening will be referred to the SP TSFP program for treatment. Women experiencing obstetric/gynaecological emergencies will be referred to advance care through an established referral mechanism. A clean delivery kit (CDK) will be provided for all pregnant women in their third trimester. In relation to strategic objective 3.3, the maternal health program will be increasing access to maternal health services for all villages the MMU reaches.
Parallel to the MMU services, the safe motherhood program will implement mother support groups to educate and empower women. Called “Anti Ghalia” with the literal meaning of “You are precious”, this training will be used for the mother support groups. Samaritan’s Purse staff will lead these mother support groups for expectant mothers teaching a module every two weeks.
