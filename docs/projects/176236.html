Project Summary

Save the Children approaches nutrition programming through both a treatment and preventative approach, using community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) to identify and treat acute malnutrition among under-five children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). SC also works through community based behavior change interventions to promote, protect and support recommended maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) practices through one-on-one counselling and group education. To create a supporting environment for healthy practices, nutrition services will continue in 111 OTP/TSFPs and 8 stabilization center, and additional 15 OTP/TSFP sites will be established in the existing operational counties. Identification of cases of acute malnutrition amongst under-five children and PLW, and referral of cases of acute malnutrition to the respective nutritio facilities will carry on in the catchment areas of all nutrition facilities. Community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) are trained to screen and refer acute malnutrition cases to health facilities, to increase timely identification and coverage of the program. Linked to facility-based services, mother-to-mother support groups (MTMSGs) deliver behavior change communication (BCC) activities on optimal health, nutrition and care practices in the community. SBCC activities will also continue through individual counseling of target mothers and sensitization of influential groups (elders, religious leaders, grandparents) through community meetings.
The program will reach a total of 18, 471 (girls 9604, boys 8867) U-5 SAM, 38,648 (boys 18557, girls 20,091) U-5 MAM, 24,779 MAM PLW, through management of acute malnutrition and MIYCN interventions integrated with ongoing health, WASH, FSL and child protection interventions. The response will be focusing to achieve the result of Children and PLW screened end detected and referred to appropriate nutrition centers. This will be done through active case ﬁnding using CNV/HHP, provision of acute managements of nutrition service through OTP and TSFP static sites supplemented by outreach and mobile team to the area where service is not accessible. Community mobilization and sensitization activities are the backbone of the CMAM approach for early identification of cases of acute malnutrition, defaulter tracing and follow up. All children identified with SAM and MAM will be referred to the nearest OTP/TSFP sites.
This project will complement the nutrition cluster response objectives through implementation of emergency nutrition program mainly using four main intervention strategies contribute to decreasing suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations in South Sudan to;
1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2021. But more specifically in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and Warrap states
2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations of the nutrition cluster and Save the Children
3. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information through improved data collection of save the children nutrition sites and by conducting SMART nutrition surveys.
4. To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in save the children operation areas. The project will support provision of hygiene kits for SAM children and ECD kits, malaria testing will be conducted for every malnourished child, and Save the children will conduct GBV audit across each OTP/TSFP sites.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in South Sudan deteriorated in the lean season of 2020, with 16.2% GAM, the country has crossed the emergency threshold of 15%. The situation has deteriorated more in 2021 and an estimated 1.9 million people are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition including 313,391 children (163,913 boys and 149,476 girls) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1,078,867 children (562,697 boys and 516,170 girls) with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women. About three quarters (74%) of the needy people are under-five children while 26% are pregnant and lactating women, and 15% of the needy people are anticipated to have some disabilities. In general, 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021. This is the highest caseload for acute malnutrition since the start of the crisis in December 2013. Malnutrition in 2020-2021 is projected to be particularly challenged not only by the COVID-19 pandemic, but also by the 2019-2020 locust infestation. The locust invasion in early 2020 in Eastern Equatoria damaged an estimated 2000 hectares to recently planted crops (especially maize, sorghum and vegetables). With increased food insecurity, malnutrition for children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women is expected to increase exponentially, impacting the overall caseload and risking the lives of vulnerable children and mothers. According to the IPC AMN projection analysis of December 2020, 53 (68%) counties of the country are classified as IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) Phase 3 and above for the first quarter of 2021. The report also indicated the major factors contributing to acute malnutrition that include high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum Dietary Diversity: 15%). Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in most counties also contribute to acute malnutrition. Furthermore, poor access to health and nutrition services due to heightened inter-communal conflict and flooding mainly in the Greater Upper Nile are also contributing to acute malnutrition. COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates, as well as changes in SAM and MAM referral protocols for children have exacerbated lack of access to services. Out of the 29 counties in IPC AMN phase 4 (critical stage) 5 counties (Akobo, Niyrol, Bor South, Kapoeta South and Budi) and 4 counties among the IPC AMN phase 3 (Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East, Torit, Lopa Lafon) are Save the children operation areas for nutrition program. The second projection for the period of April – July 2021 also shown that seasonal deterioration of acute malnutrition situation is expected during the lean season due to increased morbidity, high food insecurity and poor infant and young child feeding practices. A total of 57 (72%) counties are projected to be in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) and above, with 19 classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) and 38 classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). Of which, 8 counties (Akobo, Niyrol, Bor South, Kapoeta South, Budi, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East, Torit, Lopa Lafon) and 2 counties among the IPC AMN phase 3 (Torit and Magwi) are Save the children operation areas for nutrition program. This implies all save the children operation areas are located in IPC 3 and above, and five counties will deteriorate as compared to the previous projection. This calls for additional effort for prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition in Jonglei (Bor South, Akobo west and Niyrol), EES (Torit, Magwi, Lopa Lafon, Budi, Kapoeta North, East and South) and Warrap (Abyei). In 2020, a total of 15,367 SAM children (8462 girls and 6905 boys), 32,449 MAM children (17,718 girls and 14,731 boys) of children 6-59 months, and 15,770 pregnant and lactating women were treated in the existing 111 static OTP/TSFP sites and 8 stabilization center.

2 - Project activities

Save the Children (SCI) intends to implement lifesaving nutrition interventions in contribution to the reduction of morbidity and overall mortality of children under-five and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) due to acute malnutrition in in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and Warrap states in South Sudan. SCI will provide timely and quality management of acute malnutrition and Mother, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) services embedded in CMAM program. This commitment will contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality of children under five and PLWs due to acute malnutrition in the stated locations through provision of timely and quality management of acute malnutrition and MIYCN services embedded in CMAM program. Save the Children intends to focus on treatment of severe acute malnutrition, MIYCN and system strengthening in the selected area. The treatment of moderate acute malnutrition will be done in nutrition facilities along with SAM management and MIYCN activities. By linking this project to the existing nutrition, health, FSL and child protection programs, it will enable a combined outcome that will be brought about through the implementation of related projects. To ensure sustainability and realize functional nutrition services among the communities, continuous monitoring mechanism and capacity mapping will be carried out. Religious and community leaders and other community figures will be considered and consulted efficiently as key players for information sharing and identification of community workers and CNVs. In summary activities will include, • Establishment and strengthening of stabilization centers, OTP/TSFP sites in remote areas • Provision of WASH Kits for families of SAM children • Establishment of kitchen gardens at OTP Sites • Conduct cooking demonstrations among MtMSG, • provision of ECD kits to OTP/BSFP sites • Conduct KAP and coverage survey • Provision of refresher training for CNVs and lead mothers of MtMSG on MUAC screening • Establish strong referral system and community mobilization, conduct regular and monthly nutrition screening for early identification of cases of acute malnutrition and provide referral services to the appropriate nutrition program • Support outreach sites and to MAM beneficiaries • Conduct community sensitization about screening, defaulter tracing and follow up of malnourished children and MIYCN key messages. • Children aged 6-59 months with SAM to receive adequate, quality and timely nutritional treatment and care including MIYCN counseling, • Provide refresher training for CNW and SCI staff on CMAM and MIYCN approaches focusing on SAM and MAM management, • Support Admissions and treatment of MAM children and pregnant and lactating women in accordance the national CMAM guideline • Conduct monthly outreach to ensure service accessibility and nutrition service provision • Deploy mobile/RRM team to inaccessible area and hard to reach people moving from place to place and provide comprehensive acute malnutrition services • Continue supporting existing and additional MtMSGs to promote optimal MIYCN practices among CMAM program beneficiaries Provide MIYCN messages to mothers of children (0-23 months) and pregnant mothers and counseling of mothers with feeding problems. • Conduct Nutrition Surveillance across the nutrition services points (OTP/TSFP) including regular NIS data, FSNMS, SMART and KAP surveys • Support rehabilitation/strengthening of OTP/TSFP sites affected by flood or other problems during the rainy season • conduct malaria test of children and treat/refer cases to the appropriate health facilities . Conduct GBV and safety audit of the nutrition sites.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Save the children has standard mechanism for training of staff on child safeguarding, gender and PSEA. There are also standard protocols save the children staff, partners and contractors working with save the children to sign and to be adhered during the course of project implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Save the children has hotlines to collect complaints from beneficiaries in areas where telephone networks are available. There is also a mechanism to submit their complaints verbally to the project staff and they can raise their issues during the regular visit of the MEAL team.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will conduct regular community sensitization workshops to give a feedback to the community leaders for the complaints collected in different mechanisms (suggestion boxes, hotline and reporting in person) and to raise the community awareness in complaint mechanisms. The target community will be informed on the complaint and feedback mechanisms using the nutrition education sessions in the nutrition sites, sensitization through community nutrition volunteers and mother to mother support groups. The projects has also a banner describing the nutrition services the community can access in each nutrition site, the ration sizes and the options for complaint mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Save the children has a mechanism to discuss the feedback mechanisms in the weekly and monthly program meeting. The MEAL officer presents the key findings and discuss on it to give the response to the target community and make changes on the project implementation. For instance, if the community complains about distance of the feeding center the project will consider for outreach services in their community to reach the needy people.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Households and their family members, mainly women and children, who are vulnerable to conflict and gender based violence will benefit from the project, especially from the awareness-raising and capacity-building building activities for the local communities and institutions on community based conflict mitigation and promotion of peace building. In addition to awareness and psycho-social treatments, households and their families who are affected and vulnerable to the ongoing conflict that could aggravate their malnutrition status will be supported. SCI will focus on the below mitigation measures to combat the high prevalence of GBV in South Sudan: I. Conduct safety and GBV audit of the nutrition center at least once during the project period and take correction measures based on the findings. II. Advocating for the prevention of different forms of violence based on gender roles (i.e. child, early and forced early marriage); III. Specific consideration to ensure safe spaces for trainings, travel to outreach sites (safety audits); IV. Adapt selection criteria to include specific protection related vulnerabilities - identification of at-risk and vulnerable cases, with referrals to protection service provider for support (including SCI’s Child Protection Program in the same area); V. Using opportunities (review and coordination meetings), enforce Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Safeguarding policies by adhering to four commitments (awareness, prevention, reporting and response); VI. Linking the proposed SSHF project with other programs under implementation in the area that strengthen community based structures which focus on training of community volunteers in broader protection issues such as GBV prevention and response. VII. Save the children has set the working hours of the nutrition centers and aware the community to use it accordingly to avoid delays in the nutrition facility, establish new nutrition sites depending on the number of beneficiaries to minimize the risk of violence because of high number of beneficiaries. VIII. Enhance community awareness about the existence of feed back mechanisms using the nutrition education session, community sensitization through CNVs and mother to mother support groups. IX. Let the community aware of the protection assistant like GBV using the nutrition education session, mother to mother support group discussion and community sensitization, and establish referral mechanism between the nutrition facilities and the nearest service provider on GBV.
