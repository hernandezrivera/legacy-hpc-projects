Project Summary

Rise to Inspire Africa Initiative (RIA) has been responding to the conflict in Northeast Nigeria for the past 2 years across different sectors Education, Child Protection and WaSH and is proposing to provide integrated lifesavings and preventive response assistance to women and girls in IDP Camps and Host Communities in Jere, MMC and Konduga LGAs with the hope to target 5,000 beneficiaries.
The project will be working in identified safe spaces within the selected Camps and host communities, and where there are none, would establish some in collaboration with the Camp Coordination and Camp Management committee (CCCM). Via this safe space, women and girl of reproductive age will have access to personalized life-saving assistance such as psychosocial support, group therapy, group, and individual counseling, psychological first aid and case management services in a safe and conducive environment to address SGBV related issues and cases of concerns. The project will also engage with communal and camp structures with a view to reinforce and mainstream into existing structures or establish where absent specific coordination, protection, and referral mechanisms to identify and address incidents of GBV within and around the camp. The project will provide the much-needed opportunities for women and adolescent girls to receive peer support in a friendly environment and facilitate access to other services.
The project will also procure and distribute critical dignity kits to women and girls. Recruited and trained volunteers who are inhabitant of the community will be used in providing gender-sensitive hygiene promotion targeting women and girls. There will be a robust implementation of community education and sensitization component led by very skilled social and outreach workers aimed at increasing knowledge around GBV issues, generating concern and support for GBV survivors and change behavior. Community education and sensitization will target 3 broad categories of the population with distinct messages.
• GBV Culprits will be targeted with messaging that seek to provide information on consequence of gender based and other forms of violence, while
• The public will be provided with information to generate concern and support for GBV survivors and issues.
• GBV survivors will be targeted with messaging to provide information and knowledge on the availability of service, knowledge on the right to report and seek redress and to provide information and knowledge on where to access needed services
Our organization proposes to fully participate in the 16 days of activism led by the GBV sub-sector by implementing camp and non-camp-based activities. Through the women and girls’ safe space, women and girls especially those of childbearing age will be targeted with sexual and reproductive health services. This would be achieved through the recruitment of locals who would work as community volunteers to deliver SRH messaging and services. SRH tools and manuals will be adapted from UNFPA and the GBV Sub Sectors.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria progressed in its twelfth year, and despite a sustained number of humanitarian actors responding to the crisis, humanitarian needs in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states remained dire and multi-faceted. According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2022, this lingering conflict has left an estimated over 8.0 million individuals (60% of whom are children and 19% are women) in dire need of humanitarian assistance. The protracted nature of the conflict continues to exacerbate the negative effects of gender inequalities across north-east Nigeria, with women/girls and children being disproportionately affected by the conflict and incidents of Gender Based Violence (GBV). According to the Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) 2022 mid-year report, women and girls were the victims of more than 99% of reported GBV incidents between January and June 2022. Seventy-nine per cent of the victims are adults, whilst 21% are children; 2% of the victims are women and girls with disabilities in north-east Nigeria. It is worthy to note that women and girls are frequently targeted while performing basic tasks such as travelling to water points, collecting firewood to cook food, and going to the communal latrines. They are often exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) as well as forced and early marriage. Women and girls abducted by AOGs, many of whom have been raped and forcibly married during their captivity, often face stigmatization and rejection from their communities upon their return. Additionally, due to the poor economic capacities of these individuals, most of them cannot afford to buy basic dignity needs, such as sanitary pads, laundry, and bar soaps, under wears, etc. In most IDP camps, the priority is to secure food for the family. Securing sanitary pad for a monthly flow is the last thing people worry about. Sanitary pads are not cheap and most women and girls living in IDP camps cannot afford it, hence result in using piece of clothing to collect menstrual blood, this is practically unhygienic specifically for women and girls who are struggling to cloth themselves. This underlying challenge has left a lot of women and girls susceptible to abuse. Poor menstrual hygiene can pose physical health risks and has been linked to reproductive and urinary tract infections and is a concern in Nigeria. While in most home, it is a taboo to discuss issues around menstruation, naturally, education on menstruation should begin from the family unit as girls and boys attain puberty. In most homes, however, sexual reproduction is a ‘no-go’ area, as far as discussions are concerned. The girl, especially, is forced to rely on her mother to educate her. Her mother passes on her limited education on the subject to the daughter, this misinformation is considered a barrier to improving the menstrual hygiene of adolescent girls within IDP camps in Northeast Nigeria.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

RIA Initiative will continue to mainstream protection throughout the implementation of this project by aligning every approach with the protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety, and dignity of both the beneficiaries and employers and volunteers. RIA Initiative, just like in our ongoing project will continue to prioritize safety, and dignity and avoid causing any form of harm to both benefi ciaries and workers. RIA Initiative will also strengthen already established mechanisms through which affected populations in selected LGAs can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. RIA Initiative will continue to ensure that the target population accesses the service in proportion to their needs and without barriers and discrimination. RIA Initiative will continue to pay attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. In the Life-skills based psychosocial support services component of this project, RIA Initiative will integrate GBV Risk Mitigation activities to minimize or mitigate GBV risk to beneficiaries. RIA Initiative will continue to work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. RIA Initiative will also ensure that all activities are carried out in compliance with the Nigerian Center for Disease Control (NCDC) and Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) guidelines against the spread of, and community transmission of COVID-19. In addition, RIA Initiative will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). All staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project will be given orientation on PSEA. They will be familiarized with RIA Initiative's policy on PSEA and they must express their unwavering commitment by signing the RIA Initiative's PSEA code of conduct. In addition, RIA Initiative will also lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately, they occur. RIA Initiative will also ensure that the identity of the reporters is kept very confidential. When convenient, RIA Initiative will encourage reports to be made on anonymous grounds.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Collection of vital feedback from the affected population, which constitutes the vulnerable group to be served in this project, RIA Initiative, will employ amongst other strategies; “Use an anonymous feedback platform where beneficiaries and employees can anonymously ask questions,". This creates a comfort level among employees and targets beneficiaries to be able to communicate freely. Similarly, we will Foster Trust: as It is essential that target beneficiaries or community members believe that their feedback will reach the expected destination and create an impact in the end. RIA will ensure accountability to affected populations through various activities. Firstly, an accountability and quality improvement committee (AQIC) would be set up which shall comprise community leaders, religious leaders, representatives of women, youth and other social groups and a representative of all ethnic groups present in the community. The AQIC plan community actions implement and act as a feedback mechanism between RIA and the community. This committee would be holding a monthly meeting to discuss several issues affecting the community. During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to Education would also be aired and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners.The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback mechanism that can be received using various means, such as FGD, interviews and feedback boxes. Information on relevant services including referral pathways if available will be shared in coordination with the Protection and other service providers' team.
