Project Summary

It’s estimated that over 6.3 million people, 47 per cent of the population, are experiencing high level of acute food insecurity including 2.1 million children likely to suffer acute malnutrition. They are in need of humanitarian aid and protection services in 2025 based on resent IPC for September 2024.
The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan is as result of accumulation of interlocking factors: flooding and climate change, economic shocks, sub-national inter-communal violence, limited public infrastructure and impassable roads limiting access for response. In addition the neighboring Sudan crisis is as well threatening to destabilize the already fragile situation in the country further with reported substantial rise in cholera cases and limited access to clean water and sanitation due to floods.
As of November 2024, flooding has affected over 1.4 million people in 44 counties. Approximately 379000 individuals have been displaced across 22 counties. Jonglei, Upper Nile, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal States comprising more than 51% of the affected population this is based on OCHA South Sudan flood snapshot. REACH as well FSL Cluster repository and sectoral analysis conducted by analysts from WFP, FAO, FEWSNET and FSNMS survey.
The overall impact of floods on cereals as of the end of September 2024 was quite significant. An estimate 74949 hectors of cropland was exposed to the floods during cropping season of June to October which translates to about 72453 tons of crop losses. This have greatly affected food production in greater Bahr el Ghazal and greater Upper States.
Other factors that influence the food insecurity situation include the seasonality of livelihoods, grain stocks, prices, cattle raids, and movement of livestock, nutrition and status of people. These multiple shocks have severely impacted people’s livelihoods and access to food from markets, livestock and farming in the targeted counties.
HRP project 2025 will align with FSL Cluster 2024-26 strategy by enhancing localization, this will be the key strategy for achieving sustainable food security and livelihoods goals as well as enhancing the use of complaint and feedback mechanisms to ensure accountability and communities perceptions on project being incorporated during implementation.
In the post – harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people which is 45% will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above. From the total 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4 and 31,000 returnees will likely experience Phase 5 Catastrophe.
In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people 57% will be in phase 3 or worse. This will include 2.53 million people in phase 4 and 63000 people in phase 5.
Food insecurity and livelihood negatively affects people’s health and nutritional wellbeing, and their ability to carry out manual livelihoods. Livelihood is extremely essential for protection and restoration of rural livelihoods to the affected population. This have been disrupted with mentioned drivers causing food insecurity. The Project will compliment Nutrition, health and WASH to integrate food security sensitive intervention based on HRP 2024 operational guidelines.
The project will target different beneficiary groups: IDPs, vulnerable host communities, returnees and shall involve men, women, boys and girls of youth age appropriate, elderly people including persons with disabilities and 50% of women in the project activities will be consulted.
The project will target 36000 targeted beneficiaries that constitute of women 9720 Men 8280 girls 9360 and boys 8640 of youth age by strengthening, promote communities capacities households food security livelihoods and access to nutrition whereby prevalence of GAM is above emergency threshold.

1. Needs

Humanitarian needs are mainly driven by the impact of exacerbated impact of climate change and conflict. The situation has been compounded by severe flooding including the focused 2025 HRP project counties exposing people to high vulnerable and risk level caused by assets depletion and impoverishment experience with their livelihood disrupted.Due to floods in the focused counties, the livelihood activities were interrupted and crops being submerged leading to severe food insecurity. Food insecurity magnitude and severity is anticipated to increase and worsen respectively as a result of wide spread of flooding and drought compounded by the impact of covariate climatic change, conflict and a protracted macro-economic shocks. Flooding have rendered 15 major supply routes impassable greatly limiting access to the impacted counties.The impact of climate change, communal violence, flooding, economic shocks, insecurity and displacement has severely disrupted the food security and livelihood system and impacted food production. The women and girls and children being vulnerable and most affected and exposed to increasing protection risks and are subjected to early forced marriage as well as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.The households are in IPC Phase 4 and will need immediate emergency food assistance complementary with emergency livelihood and livestock support across farming, agricultural and pastoral communities, for the medium and longer term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity. September 2024 IPC report and needs assessment, food security and livelihoods were identified as the most needed forms of assistance. There are extreme levels of food insecurity with exhaustion of emergency level coping strategies. Food and nutrition needs faces the most vulnerable groups. Women, children boys and girls under age of five years, people with disability, older persons, Pregnant and Lactating women and women-headed households are identified as the most vulnerable groups in these Counties.Food insecurity worsened and communities facing catastrophic needs. More children are acutely malnourished. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of GBV and psycho social distress. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices. These counties mostly face natural catastrophic situation seasonal and climatic shocks particularly floods and drought spells as well as insecurity increasingly due to large scale displacement resulting in limited food stocks, crop diseases and pests affecting crops and recurring lean seasons that further undermining people’s resilience by depleting household assets.The environmental impact of flooding, economic shocks and conflict and insufficient infrastructure prevented the communities from practicing their dominant livelihood activities that left the people highly vulnerable to the long-term effects of climate change. Agriculture constitutes the backbone of the economy, with many reliant on farming, livestock, and fishing for subsistence and livelihoods.Due to inadequate infrastructure, these have complicated further the transportation of goods, personnel and humanitarian operations that have resulted further deteriorated the socio-economic situation in the country and communities hosting the returnees. Acute malnutrition has worsened with extreme critical level of severity Acute Malnutrition GAM rate of >15%. Increases in Acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, widespread insecurity, displacement, high morbidity, extremely poor diets.The project will address the needs by scaling up the response to meet the severity of needs of the targeted vulnerable groups of beneficiaries. The affected households in these counties have several and different coping strategies against hunger and starvation including the use of their crop products during the lean season.

2. Project activities

The HRP 2025 project response will enhance sustainable agricultural resilience and disaster preparedness by distributing climate resilient seeds, training communities on climate smart practices, rehabilitating flood protective structures aligning with cluster objectives and strategy. Enhance on lifesaving activities as well as building resilience to meet the immediate food security and livelihood needs in the focused counties. • Protect livelihoods through complementary emergency cropping, livelihood and livestock support. The project will support emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds and tools fish, vegetable and crop kits distribution for access to food for consumption and income generation.• Protect livelihoods through dry season fish and vegetable kit distribution.• Implement food and nutrition assistance to targeted vulnerable populations .by training Community farmers and Nutrition Volunteers on kitchen backyard gardens after establishment of Kitchen backyard garden demonstration sites at all nutrition facilities.• Implement emergency livestock interventions vaccination and treatment of livestock diseases.• Conduct livestock disease surveillance during the project response.• Take measures to combat: Post harvest losses, pest surveillance and control especially FAW and Desert Locust.• Implement activities to help sustain community coping mechanisms and community build resilience.• Distribute fishing and rapid response livelihood kits as part of an integrated response e.g. integrated rapid response mechanism with WFP and FAO.• Provide emergency training and build capacity to work with community beneficiaries on post-harvest techniques involving women, men, and youth girls and boys in terms of improving the value chain for food products through better processing, storage preservation of seeds and marketing of agricultural products.• Train selected beneficiaries and build capacity for vulnerable households on marketing skills to support building resilience and empower vulnerable beneficiaries comprising of women and men, youth boys and girls carry out local trade which will enable them generate income to support their livelihoods. The above activities focusing on cluster objective two.• Provide conditional assistance in the form of cash or vouchers or food• Undertake general food distribution or unconditional cash or voucher transfers with blanket supplementary feeding for children under age of five years. This is in terms of cluster objective one: Provide food assistance.• The project will build resilience of households and communities through seed production, crop and vegetable production, school feeding, school gardens, income generation activities and post-harvest conservation, cash transfers for productive assets and social protection at community level, in particular for women groups.• The project response will also use Cash- or food-for-work activities to cover the survival deficit while helping protect productive assets especially conservation farming, plantation clearing, field preparation building resilience.• Project will enhance cash transfer agricultural inputs for both agriculture and livestock inputs. The provision will be geared toward restoring livelihoods and assets lost during the crisis and agricultural inputs and cash transfers for livelihood recovery distribution taking into consideration market situation and functioning, post-harvest management and local production systems.• Support community based seed multiplication.• Diversification and restoration of livelihoods, small grants for petty trade, village saving and loan schemes and micro finance.• Food conservation and reduction of post-harvest losses through provision of community or household managed silos, cereal conditioning bags, grain storage, cash transfers and technical support.• Rehabilitation of productive assets such as fishing boats and livestock restocking
