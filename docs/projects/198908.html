Project Summary

This project will undertake food assistance and provision of access to fuel efficient technologies, income generating activities and agricultural resilience support to affected persons in BAY states. SHO is proposing to achieve these through the food distribution (Wet feeding, in kind), delivery of cash-based emergency food assistance, support of income generating activities through cash grant and business skills training and development for livelihood, and improve agriculture production through seeds and other inputs, and transformation of skills for food and livelihoods to households. This project will also include a Wet Feeding component as part of the food assistance which is going to provide emergency response and act as first responder to new arrivals and returnees in the selected LGAs with high level of new arrivals.
Additionally, Agricultural Resilience Support - livelihood inputs/assets or community-based productive asset creation/enhancement activities through the most appropriate modalities (ex: in kind, voucher, cash or multi-modality and create new innovative approaches to food production and resilience which will contribute significantly to the healthy, physical and mental well-being of IDPs. Salient Humanitarian Organization aims to support addressing the underlying causes of continued and seasonal food insecurity through Agricultural resilient building and agro income-generating activities. Similarly, aims to move towards an increasingly integrated Food Security and agriculture Livelihoods response tailoring in innovative means of food production at will be locally accepted, building on existing projects of our wet feeding program, experience and capacity within SHO in Food security sector, supporting assistance increasingly to areas where this is targeted. This is to provide resilience-building activities for affected populations. Knowing the complex nature of Northeastern Nigeria, SHO and the food security sector will coordinate for participatory planning sessions in the communities at the start of the program implementation. This is to get to the community involvement in the planning of the project activities and getting the affected community share ideas and best approaches in implementing.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview, as a result of the ongoing crisis and high food prices, almost 1 in every 4 (22%) people living in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States will be food insecure and in urgent need of food assistance in the 2022 lean season – as projected by the October 2021 CH analysis. Just under half a million (473, 397) are projected to be at Phase 4 levels or above in the 2022 lean season – while alarming, this represents a 39% reduction from 772,175 people in the 2021 lean season. The most food-insecure LGAs (Phase 4), Mobbar and Gubio, are both in Borno State. There is also a population of about 13,551 vulnerable individuals facing catastrophic levels of food insecurity (Phase 5), again in Borno State (in Bama, Gubio and Magumeri LGAs). In addition, 14 LGAs in Borno, 10 LGAs in Yobe, and 4 LGAs in Adamawa are projected to be in Phase 3. This means that, in total, there will be fewer LGAs in Phase 3 and 4 during the 2022 lean season than in the previous year (38 in 2021) – although this still leaves 30 LGAs facing crisis levels or above of food insecurity. However, it is important to note that three LGAs (Abadam, Guzamala and Marte) that were previously categorized as Phase 4 for the peak of the 2021 lean season, were not part of the 2022 projections as samples did not meet the minimum threshold required for a representative analysis. There have been some notable changes in levels of food security in certain areas. In Adamawa, Madagali LGA went from CH Phase 4 in 2021 to Phase 3 in 2022 projections; however, Jada LGA deteriorated from Phase 2 to 3. In Yobe State, Karasuwa and Jakusko have moved from CH Phase 3 to 2, and Machina from CH Phase 3 to 1. Despite advocacy and resource mobilization efforts to scale-up emergency food assistance and agricultural livelihoods support to avert a potential catastrophic deterioration in some areas during the lean season, there was still an estimated 1.6 million people left unreached across the BAY states as of September 2021. Over 12 years of conflict, high inflation rates have contributed to soaring food commodity prices. This has been compounded by the economic downturn following the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. Environmental factors (flooding, erratic rainfall) and widespread poverty are also driving food insecurity in the BAY states. As of September 2021, the cost of the minimum survival food basket has increased by more than 30% in both Borno and Yobe states compared to September 2020, and by nearly 70% compared to September 2019. This has had a severe impact on food access for market-reliant households. High inflation, restrictions on trade, and higher transport costs for vehicles traveling on insecure roads have led to massive price hikes. The reliance on food assistance to meet daily needs is high among vulnerable households because of limited access to livelihood opportunities or income generating activities – largely due to the limited availability of land (as a result of widespread insecurity) for the production of both livestock and crops. Limited access to agricultural inputs such as fertilizers (which are heavily restricted), further reduces productivity and production. However, the October 2021 crop change analysis indicates increases in access to land for production in Borno State, including in some hard-to-reach and inaccessible areas such as Abadam, Marte, Monguno, Guzamala, Kukawa, Ngala and Kala Balge. This provides an opportunity for increasing resilience activities in those locations. Limited access to water – especially for dry season farming, fisheries and livestock production – further reduces livelihood opportunities and productivity for farming households. According to HRP, The October 2021 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) projects 3.5 million in need (IPC phases 3 or worse) during the peak of the 2022 lean season, of whom an estimated 13,551 people are anticipated to be in catastrophe like conditions (Phase 5), and 459,846 in Phase 4 (‘emergency)

2 - Activities

Despite largely favorable crop development and improvements in livestock conditions, localized flooding and dry spells coupled with persistent high levels of conflict in the north-eastern. Furthermore, this is leading to an increase in humanitarian assistance needs in the ongoing rainy season, as the harvest of early maturing maize, groundnuts, potatoes, and millet is not leading to large scale improvements in food insecurity. Conflict-affected areas in the Northeast are expected to face the most severe outcomes with Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes anticipated throughout the entire projection period. In these areas, displaced households outside of IDP settlements and urban centers have no food stocks with limited incomes and are likely to have difficulty in meeting their food and non-food needs until the harvest in October. With the harvest, households that engaged in crop production are expected to consume own foods; however, access to food is still expected to be generally limited and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected persist in much of Borno State. Areas, where populations are affected by significant losses of livelihood activities and remain inaccessible to humanitarian actors, are likely facing similar or worse outcomes as adjacent, accessible areas. A risk of famine (IPC Phase 5) persists throughout this period in a worst-case scenario in which there is a significant shift in conflict that would reduce household access to food and income.SHO shall conduct assessment BAY state prior to implementation of the program to screen vulnerable Persons of Concern households and deliver in-kind food assistance and provision of access to Fuel Efficient Technologies, income generating activities and Agricultural resilience support. Activity 1: Prior to implementation, SHO shall conduct a need assessment to screen vulnerabilities within IDPs’ households, returnees and host communities of the selected LGAs in BAY state. This is going to be conducted with the host community, IDPs, women and youth forming the committee and will be meant to achieve needs assessment, project design, proper delivering of assistance based on the needs assessed and review of projects. Activity 2: Provision of adequate/improved Wet feeding for New Arrivals for Five days. - Incorporate Protection Mainstreaming. Activity 3: provision of fuel-efficient technologies (stoves) to vulnerable households in targeted LGAs in order to reduce reported cases of Gender Based Violence during collection of firewood in the nearest bushes. Activity 4: Provision of Agro-based income generating activities in crop cultivation, livestock and aquaculture,Activity 5: Provision of accessible, effective and confidential complaint mechanisms that are well understood and respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can immediately be put in place. Staff the mechanism with both men and women and ensure it is accessible for children. - SHO will set up a rudimentary Help Desk and rely on PMC. Draft a report of complaints and feedback collected. - In other locations Help-desks will be made available and PMC consulted before and after a registration or distribution. - SHO will sensitise the PMC members, the community leaders and affected populations about the use and purpose of the Help Desk. - SHO will locate Helpdesks in safe locations at the sites under shades. SHO may also have to walk around collecting complaints and feedback or conduct a community outreach initiative with the PMC. Activity 6: Documentation of differences in dietary needs, preferences and restrictions and Monitoring whether women and men (boys/girls) receive fair and comparable benefits from interventions in food assistance, agriculture, livestock, fisheries, and/or livelihoods. Other Activities: Capacity building training, Provision of Cash Vouchers, Procurement of food for distribution and Monitoring and Evaluation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities; 2. Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services and 3. Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities. The project team will continue to assess the situation and respond to immediate needs among vulnerable groups, especially PLW, in the areas of intervention. SALIENT has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, SALIENT protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: A. Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: SALIENT will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. SALIENT’s FSS team will ensure that community meetings and sensitization campaigns are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. B. Ensure meaningful and equal access: the FSS Team will strengthen participatory camp governance structures, facilitating direct inclusion of beneficiary groups into decision-making processes, facilitating greater equitable and meaningful access to assistance programs. SALIENT will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy C. Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: Beneficiaries will be members of camp committees, ensuring strong participation in camp governance, channeling ideas and influencing the wider humanitarian coordination and response planning, and play a vital role in the implementation of site maintenance activities, providing foundation for community-led camp management and interventions. Daily laborers, hired to support the construction of the drainage network, will be recruited from the IDP community.
