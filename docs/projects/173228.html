Project Summary

The project aims to provide health care assistance to local population and to empower local health care resilience with a special attention to vulnerable groups in Libya.
Through ad hoc training of trainers and materials, medicines and medical equipment distribution, the project will act basing on an holistic approach. The project will guarantee a strong exit strategy thanks to the training of local health care trainers, which will guarantee the multiplier effects on a capillar level.
The project aims to give an immediate and emergency response, but at the same time complex and structured to the very first emergency situation to respond to the Libyan health shortages in response to the Covid-19 pandemic and the health emergency in the selected areas, with a particular pay attention to the age group 0-19 years.
The answer concerns the health conditions of the population through a targeted and coordinated activity of supplying equipment, medical materials and safety devices and properly trained and updated Libyan medical staff. Alongside the Covid-19 emergency, the project aims to respond, at the same time, to the health emergency with particular attention to vulnerable individuals in Libyan society.
Emergenza Sorrisi acts through a methodology that combines the practical cash response of medical assistance and an appropriate and programmed operational technical assistance capable of giving immediate responses to the identified emergency, at the same time increasing a system of access to health care that goes beyond the duration of the project and can, once structured, last beyond the project.
With a holistic approach that combines training in medical updating and the supply of goods, tools and materials, the project aims to guarantee a strong exit strategy at the conclusion of the activities.
Thanks to the surgical missions carried out in favor of Libyan children in February 2020 and July 2020 and the resonance that these actions have had on the Libyan population, the hospitals and municipalities have strengthened the confidence in Emergenza Sorrisi and its activity in the country.
For this reason, during the last projects, the activities aimed at the Libyan population implemented by Emergenza Sorrisi have had a strong impact on the Libyan population and on local hospitals: a surgical mission that unites the staff of 3 nations (Italian, Libyan and Tunisian) with a view to training and medical assistance in defense of children, it is a concrete, practical action that affects the lives of individual Libyan citizens, who live in a condition of constant emergency in the health sector. A methodology that combines medical knowledge, surgical precision, logistical organization and operational capacity of great level and impact on the territory.
This allows the staff of Emergenza Sorrisi to enjoy full trust on the part of the health structures involved and, in turn, on the part of the always attentive municipalities.

1 - Needs

The health sector in Libya is characterized by a profound inability to manage and deliver health services to the population, inability mainly due to fragmented governance and a chronic shortage of human resources, a weakened health network and unsuitable health services, especially in the south of the country. As reported by the United Nations reports, Libya has a sufficient number of health facilities (28 vs 2.0 per 10,000 people as per standards, the availability of general service based on infrastructure, workforce and use of services) is 79% and 17 % of hospitals (17 out of 97) and 20% of Primary Health Care centers (273 out of 1,355) are closed, while the number of human resources with a number three times higher than the standard (76 against 23 for 10,000 people), but with a deep gap in terms of efficiency.Health services remain the main need in Libya with a multitude of people lacking access to basic and specialized health services including the treatment of infectious and chronic diseases and obstetric complications due to shortages of medical materials and equipment, lack of staff and operational capacity. Continued migration and internally displaced people in recent years have worsened the availability of medicines, vaccines and equipment. Limited access to medical services with particular attention to those supporting psychological trauma is increasing with a percentage equal to 70% of people suffering from this lack, while 43% suffer from the lack of access to care in the case of injuries and physical illnesses. Meanwhile, the life expectancy of the Libyan population has decreased due to recent conflicts. Medical facilities are unfortunately increasingly subject to military and armed attacks, worsening the infrastructure situation and medical equipment: in 2019 56 attacks against medical personnel and facilities were recorded. Only $ 14% of PHC facilities provide antenatal care, 71% of the local population live with chronic diseases and have no access to life-saving medicines.The civilian population continues to be victims of indiscriminate use of weapons and at risk of death or injury. 17.5% of hospitals, 20% of first aid facilities (Primary Health Center) and 8% of other healthcare facilities are not operational because they are damaged by conflicts, lack of maintenance, impossibility of access. The availability of health services is particularly limited. Mental and family health services are completely absent in the south of the country and 1% are available.With a population of 6.5 million inhabitants, 1.6 million is the number of people affected, 1.1 are in need of humanitarian assistance. According to OCHA data, 1.62 million people have been affected by internal conflicts across the territory of Libya while 217,000 people are internally displaced.According to the classification of the WHO, Libya remains classified as a "clusters of cases" for Covid-19: 85.529 confirmed cases and 1.219 deaths. Even if the laboratories are increasing, we register a lack in the South and a serious lack of PPE devoted to medical staff working in isolation centers.In the Fezzan region, according to the analyzes carried out on the territory by the partners, there is a shortage of laboratories and equipment to perform swabs, a shortage of swabs and PPE. The local hospitals expressed specific requests at the time of the elaboration of the action, with a sharing in the definition of the needs and the necessary interventions driven by a confidence and enthusiasm rare to find on the Libyan territory, thanks to the echo of the activities carried out by Emergenza I smiled in favor of the Libyan population.
