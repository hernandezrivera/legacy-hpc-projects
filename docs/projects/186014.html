Project Summary

This project is aimed at providing GBV support to IDPs, vulnerable women and girls and other vulnerable population in the conflict affected communities in Kupe-Muanenguba Division. The project will be focusing on ending various forms of violence perpetuated on women and girls such as rape, sexual violence, physical violence, discriminatory practices, forceful and early marriages and other harmful practices and tradition that violate the rights and dignity of women and girls in society. The project will be targeting women of all ages especially from 18-59 years, girls below the ages of 18, albinos, women and girls with disabilities, elderly women from 59+, youths (male and female) and men.
The project will also be targeting women (married) undergoing domestic violence in the three sub-subdivision of Kupe –Muanenguba division (Bangem, Nguti and Tombel). Domestic violence is a major violence affecting women and girls in this division of intervention with some cases recorded recently at the Bangem district hospital where so women reported to the hospital seriously by their husbands with weapons. Also, some girls are being violated by youths (boys; bike riders) with beatings, refusing at time to provide for the girl and the child if the girl is refusing to abide to certain conditions while in a relation. This is caused mainly by the behaviour/perception of the people of this division who strongly believe the woman should be subjected to the man before she can do anything. Therefore, this project will using behavioural change of target through intensive counselling of couple, families on how to live a happy life through the respect for one another’s rights in the home, workshops, serminars, trainings on ending GBV, GBV talks, drama displays and continuous sensitisation and awareness raising through community radios, social media etc.
The reason for this intervention is to give women and girls the exposure, promote their rights and empower them to feel valued in society and see themselves equal to any other human being. Also, information regarding survivors will be treated with confidentiality by competent and trained GBV task force. All staff and volunteers that will be involved in the implementation will be trained with in-house capacity building GBV case management and other complexities including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse during all the stages of the project.
This project will carry out advocacy, campaigns, targeting traditional rulers through the Kupe-Muanenguba Chiefs Conference, religious leaders of different denominations, family heads, etc to lend support. It will also carry out case management, counseling, psychological and psychosocial support to survivors, referrals and community education of target groups on GBV to build community resilience towards ending GBV. Continuous sensitizations of target population in all the three sub-divisions will be carried by trained community mobilizers or community focal point persons who have a good mastery of the culture, language and other social aspects of these target communities and villages. This sensitization will entail communicative, educative, learning and awareness raising messages with the objective of creating self guilty and self consciousness in the minds of perpetuators of these acts of violence.
Also, this project uses the knowledge transfer based approach (education;-the first line of prevention) in that community mobilisers will be trained on the complexities behind GBV globally and specificities within the entire kupe-Muanenguba division emphasing on the causes of GBV, principles and standard operating principles of reducing the ill within communities. This will also include administering of psychological first aid to survivors, and those at risk of GBV to service providers. In addition, the gender task force (of implementing organization) together with trained community mobilisers will be carrying out groundbreaking community awareness.
