Project Summary

Silk Road aims to support the affected people in Aleppo and Idleb in the districts of Idleb, Harim, Afrin,and Azaz, through sustainable adequate and stable food availability, accessibility to all food items and required, utilized and affordable via all people in a fair and decent way saves people dignity via facilitating accessibility for those foods and enhance its continues availability in the market. Some 12000 households (about 72,000 individuals), consisting mainly of poor IDPs women and households headed by the women, will benefit from the project. The value of the monthly distrbuted voucher US$ 50 , this kit tailored to ensure achieving the recommendation of the FSL cluster to achieve 1500-2000 Kcal/person/Day. The implementation plan is based on the distribution of monthly vouchers to beneficiary families so that each family will benefit from eight consecutive rounds of vouchers program, in other words, 12000 families will benefit for seven months. achieving eight rounds of distribution, as two rounds of distribution will be conducted during the last month. Where the last round of distribution will be conducted by the end of the last month to be consumed during the next month.
enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuate discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation.
All project staff and stakeholders will share an ethical responsibility for mainstreaming protection across the humanitarian response, including general and sector staff, programming staff, advocacy staff, design, monitoring and evaluation staff, and their managers.

3 - Needs

According to 2023 HNO overview 15.3 M out of 22.1 M of Syrian population are in need for the humanitarin aid, in other words, almost seven out of ten people in Syria need assistance. All the population groups identified in the 2023 HNO are worse off in 2023 than in 2022. Silk Road had conducted Needs Assessment in 33 communities many people are facing shortages in food items in the assessed locations due to lack of money to buy food and the high price of food when available. The available food in these locations come either from markets or lending food to IDPs from markets or host communities. In case of food access difficulties, IDPs can cope this challenge by reducing the numbers of meals eaten in a day, borrowing food or limiting the portion size at meal time. The most needed food items according to the interviewed HHs are pulses and beans, cereals and bread, oils and fat, sugar, meat, and other items. Interviewed people declared that the most affected (most vulnerable) groups in the region classified as follows as the following (Women, Girls, Elderly, Boys, Men and PWD). prices, after the increase in the exchange rate of dollar, all foodstuff prices increased significantly. according to market assessment conducted by SRO: Food Items Prices/USD Rice 0.8 Chickpeas 1.7 Lentils 0.9 Beans 2.1 Vegetable Oil1.9 Olive Oil 2.2 Sugar 0.6 Pasta, Macaroni0.4. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Discrimination against beneficiaries or /and sexual exploitation and abuse /Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls Mitigation measures: Staff training and use non-discrimination policies/code of conduct and involve Local authoritative bodies. Establish a strong feedback mechanism Train staff on the code of conduct All staff contracts to include PSEA Establish a confidential complaint mechanism/ Address any SEA complaints 2- Inappropriate or poorly planned distribution of cash and/or vouchers might expose beneficiaries to the risk of exploitation and/or anti-social use of the provided resources non-humanitarian purposes. Mitigation measures: Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or cancelled Ensure that the distribution is completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk Communicate intensively with national and local authorities, stakeholders and community leaders Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as the disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs 3- Stakeholder conflict over proposed criteria (Its probability is low and its impact is high) Or/And Violation of agreements Mitigation measures: Will overcome by 1.1- Involve local leaders.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The risks of the violation of the conduct of Silk Road or/and Misconduct or violence in the workplace. (Likelihood medium - Impact high). PSEA related issues including rape, assault or sexual exploitation- This applies to employees, beneficiaries, workers, suppliers, partners and any party that may be related to Silk Road work. Mitigating Measure: - Silk Road has zero-tolerance for some special cases such as any acts related to terrorist or radical groups, PSEA related issues including rape, assault or sexual exploitation, blackmail, possessing a weapon, threatening or assaulting another person or corruption. - Empower children, women and other vulnerable groups- discuss with them their rights, what is acceptable and unacceptable and what they can do if there is a problem. - Bullying, harassment (including sexual, gender and racial harassment) and discrimination are not tolerated, along with any unwelcome comment or behaviour that is offensive, demeaning, humiliating, derogatory, or any other inappropriate behaviour that fails to respect the dignity of an individual. - Code of conduct of Silk Road automatically forms part of all contracts of employment and contractual conditions of service including any procurement). - Breaches of the standards of the Code of Conduct will be subject to investigation and possible disciplinary action in line with Silk Road’s Disciplinary Procedure stated in the Human resources and Management Responsibility and Accountability Policy


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1 Increased armed conflict due to increased tensions between different groups. This could delay, pause, or suspend programming. Mitigation measures: If this reaches Silk Road’s project towns, they will pause activities and wait until the situation on the ground becomes safer. 2- Conflicts of interest Mitigation measures: The selection committee and not one person Re-check the results before choosing Clear and consistent criteria A separate team for final selection 3- Corruption in procurement by employees, sellers or change in required specifications/commodities Mitigation measures: Daily monitoring Secret shoppers Spot-check by a different team Continuous follow-up of procurement and finance. 4- Voucher overly spent on non- food items, thus negatively impact food security outcomes Or/And Voucher not being equally shared/distributed within the HH. Mitigation measures: Conditional orrestricted CBR Provide vouchers or e-card not cash Engage with relevant authorities as appropriate. Vary the payment days Food security assessments to ascertain/inform needs Ensure close monitoring by female aid workers 5- Inflation, especially the cost of food items may result in higher than planned prices. If prices spike too high, then Silk Road may not have the budget to complete the project / Market system disruption. - Silk Road will monitor inflation and prices and include this risk in its contracts with service providers. If inflation rises, they will work with the donor to re-align budget
