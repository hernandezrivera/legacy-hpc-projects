Project Summary

Unity State is facing food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and malnutrition is critically high among children under five years, pregnant and lactating women. This project proposes the provision of integrated life-saving curative and preventive nutrition services to 41,640 internally displaced people and returnees (25,320 children under five, 15,312 pregnant and lactating women, 779 elderly and 229 people with disabilities) in Mayendit, Leer and Panyijiar County. The project aims to reduce suffering, excessive morbidities and mortalities related to malnutrition through increased equitable access and utilization of quality preventive and curative nutrition-specific services for boys and girls under give five years; pregnant and lactating women, elderly and people with disabilities. The project will provide quality screening and treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and counseling of mothers/parents or caretakers of boys and girls receiving SAM and MAM treatment.
Specific objectives are 1) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition-specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2021 2) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2021 3) To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2021 and 4) To increase equitable access to nutrition-sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2021.
Nutrition activities will include mass screening and treatment of boys and girls under five, PLW and people with disabilities identified with SAM and MAM. In addition, SPEDP will provide life-saving maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) services including protection, support and promotion of exclusive and continued breastfeeding through support to mothers and provision of counseling services on appropriate maternal and young child feeding practices.
Furthermore, SPEDP will scaling up and increase demand for nutrition services. It will collaborate and coordinate with food security and livelihood (FSL), health, water, hygiene and sanitation (WASH) and protection to increase access to quality and effective nutrition services by establishing nutrition sites (in static and mobile health facilities). Demonstration and kitchen gardens will be established in health facilities, schools, and other suitable locations in the target counties. In addition, the organization will secure and provide vitamins supplements, emergency food rations and nutrition supplies for the boys and girls under five, adolescent girls and boys, men, women, people with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups using the blanket supplementary feeding program (BSFP) model. Furthermore, the project will adopt and employ mother-to-mother support groups to disseminate information, education and communication (IEC) messages on appropriate feeding practices for the PLW, infants and young children. Other effective and appropriate interventions such as targeted supplementary feeding (TSF) and stabilization centers will be used for the treatment of boys, girls, and PLW with SAM with or without complications to reduce deaths related to malnutrition. SPEDP will align the project with the national strategy for an integrated basic package of primary health and nutrition services and Nutrition Cluster approach to effectively deliver nutrition-specific interventions to manage acute malnutrition among the target population. The project is planned to be implemented at a cost of USD 423,649.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition remains one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality among children under five years, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in South Sudan. In Unity State, food security is precarious and malnutrition is critically high. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) among children under five is 33% compared to 26% in 2020. The main drivers are rising food insecurity, poor child feeding practices, limited access to basic health care services; inadequate safe water, poor hygiene and sanitation. Other factors include high prevalence of communicable diseases, poor access to health and nutrition services driven by inter-communal conflict, flooding and flood-related displacement and Covid-19 interruptions among others. In addition, inter-communal violence and reduced access to sources of food have eroded people’s productivity and purchasing power. About 15,000 internally displaced people and 17,000 returnees are living in spontaneous settlements. The extensive flooding has also reduced pasture for the livestock, promoted livestock diseases outbreak, death of animals, hence reduced source of food for the affected population. They rely on food assistance and donation from the host communities. Consequently, severe malnutrition is rampant among the vulnerable communities. Again, Mayendit, Leer and Panyijiar County are facing catastrophic (IPC Phase 4). The burden of severe acute malnutrition is 33% compared to 26% in 2020. The burden of SAM, MAM and GAM among children 6-59 months are at (19%), (36.7%), and (28.9%) respectively which is above the WHO emergency threshold (15%). In addition, maternal, infant and young nutrition (MIYCN) is sub-optimal. Only 7% of children 6-23 months meet their minimum acceptable diet needs. Yet, they suffer from malaria, diarrheal diseases and respiratory tract infections which exacerbate malnutrition. Poor access to health and nutrition services is also complicating malnutrition among the affected population. Only 12% of IDPs and 13% returnees have access to health and nutrition services. Thirty five percent has access to an improved source of water at 30 minutes’ walk away and the rest are using unsafe surface water. Consequently, the ability of these vulnerable population to perform their manual livelihood activities have been eroded.More importantly, women and girls continue from sexual violence, rape and sexual slavery continue and protection services are sub-optimal in Mayendit, Leer and Panyijiar County. Men, women, children and people with disabilities are facing high levels of stress-related to loss of friend and family members, impacting negatively on the mental health and emotional development of children . In addition, poor living conditions, characterized by high congestions is affecting vulnerable people in IDPs and returnees settlements. Congestions in IDPs settlements, women and girls have no access to sanitary facilities and no privacy. Only 17% have access to sanitation facilities and 4% use public latrines. Most people defecate in open and this practice lowers the dignity of women, children and people living with disabilities. It also predisposes them to high risk of violent discrimination and gender-based violence. It’s on this background SPEDP is proposing the provision of life-saving nutrition services in Mayendit, Leer, and Panyijiar County.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP staffs are aware, trained and adheres to the UN Secretary-General's Bulletin on Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13) This project staffs will strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising sessions around PSEA with women, girls, boys and men in communities of concern as well as with other stakeholders and design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities. Beneficiaries will be made aware of their rights and entitlements through community meetings, focus group meetings and information sheets distribution to beneficiaries. In particular, SPEDP will identify and support feedback channels that communities prefer while ensuring that feedback mechanisms are sensitive to age, gender and diversity (AGD) and accessible to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities and others at high risk. Besides, SPEDP will also coordinate complaint collection and responses with other partners within these proposed project locations and establish clear tasks and duties (who will do what, where, and how) since it is critical to put in place an effective interagency complaint mechanism that permits and encourages individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially, and safely.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Communities will be encouraged to identify different means and methods/channels of communicating information on the project. Where relevant and appropriate, some platforms such as whatapp and SMS will be used. People with low literacy will be consulted to suggest the appropriate means of communicating feedback. Women and girls will be consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. They will be guided to outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by the project officer. Information obtained will be communicated to the project management to adopt it and incorporated into the implementation strategies and work plans accordingly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee’s to implement, with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback information will be received, adopted by the Project Management Team and incorporated as part of adaptive programming to strengthen implementation strategies and work plan. Information on the action(s) taken regards the beneficiaries' feedback, will be communicated back to communities by the PMC through scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing feedback mechanism loop/communication. The project team will manage all complaints and recommendations from the communities and provide feedback immediately. The project team will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints.

Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in the targeted project area - In order to mitigate local disputes around the land ownership following clearance operations, SPEDP will liaise with local authorities, traditional and religious leaders even before the teams start operating. A handover certiﬁcate will be issued to local leaders after the land being cleared and a community meeting will be called to ensure transparency and accountability. Additionally, SPEDP will closely monitor the socio-economic impact of its work by utilizing its own methodologies and dedicated M&E mechanism to ensure that targeted local communities remain at their locations and use the cleared land for agricultural or hunting purposes in an eﬃcient way. This will help to build their resilience and pave the way for self-suﬃciency at community and household levels. It will also reduce the level of vulnerability towards humanitarian aid. 8.10. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in the project target area - Risk will be mitigated by prioritizing accreditation of EORE Teams in a timely manner (refresher training and ongoing capacity development). EORE Teams are equipped with mobile phones of key focal points at UNMAS and NMSS should immediate assistance be required. Further, EORE Teams carry identifying materials for themselves and communities to be able to mark any UXOs discovered.
