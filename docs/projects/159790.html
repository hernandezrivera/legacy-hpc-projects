Project Summary

It is estimated that 60% of the 4.9 million school aged children in Somalia are out of school mostly in the South. Currently about 360,000 children are in need of education services. The goal of this project is to increase the reach to quality education for all children in 2020. WV will target 6,024 (3,132girls, 2,892 boys) across 40 schools to access EiE services in Gedo, Bay, Bakool, Nuugal, and Togdheer regions of Somalia/Somaliland. In order to build a resilient education system, the project will work closely with the respective Ministries of Education to preposition education supplies at District level in the regions mentioned to enhance emergency preparedness for the EiE departments. This will be obtained from WVI's already existing Gift in Kind (GiK) available at our operational bases in Garowe, Baidoa, Doolow and Hargeisa. Leveraging on existing livelihoods projects to promote enrollment and retention of children to school among the most vulnerable households benefiting from cash based interventions (food vouchers). Riding on the on-going It Takes Every Somali to End Violence against Children campaign, education advocacy will be integrated to promote awareness on the crucial life saving nature of education during emergency situations. The project includes a cash component which will be implemented as follows; after capacity assessment and training of CECs, the schools will be supported with $500 as seed funding which they will match in kind or cash to fund their school improvement plans.
This project has been designed to additional provide and child centered learning environment which comprises of temporary learning spaces, segregated latrines for boys, girls and teachers and adequate school supplies including age appropriate reading materials that support reading with comprehension competencies. School supplies will also be provided by the project. 120 teachers (46 female, 74male) will be recruited and supported with emergency incentives during the project period. In order to meet the dietary needs of school going children, the project intends to provide a school meals for 9 months of the project period. During the school hours the children and teachers will also have access to safe drinking water as well as water for hand washing. To support learning and psycho social support, the children will also have access to recreational materials for extra curricula activities. Further, the project also takes into consideration the need for structured support for the mental health needs of the children, parents and teachers and will build the capacity of 120 teachers on identification of children in need on protection and the referral pathways. The schools/teachers will be linked to the existing child protection committees and social workers where they exist to promote timely individual case management. The teachers will also be trained on psycho social first aid (PFA) and supported to provide services to fellow teachers and parents too. Cases that require specialized services will be referred to the existing health facilities. To add on to that, the teachers will also be equipped with teachers in crisis context competencies and other learner centered approaches to enable them provide quality services. Through WVI's positive parenting and discipline models, teachers and parents will be sensitized to abandon corporal punishment and other negative forms of punishments that retard children's development and promote school drop out. To continue making the schools a child friendly, the project will factor in child friendly accountability systems in addition to school clubs that will give children space to talk about issues that affect their well being in school and at home.
Community Education Committees of each of the schools will be equipped with management skills and facilitated to develop school improvement plans to enable them prioritize and act on the immediate school needs.

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Boys and girls, children with disabilities, and marginalized communities are impacted differently by the crisis and displacement, having distinct humanitarian needs and facing specific education concerns. Therefore, integration of age, gender and diversity principles in service delivery and assistance will be promoted. Protection risks include widespread GBV, child recruitment, physical attacks, forced early marriages, forced evictions, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/ordiscriminatory practices. The project will strengthen inclusion by instituting an accountability mechanism for effective protection of affected populations i.e robust complaints and feedback mechanism for instance a toll free telephone line 3d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Using ‘Do no harm principles’, WV will seek to avoid physical or psycho-social risks that would be detrimental to project beneficiaries, and will serve all people irrespective of age, sex or clan. Given vulnerabilities associated with issues related to clan dynamics, WV has done a thorough analysis of the context and has conducted the Do-No-Harm Assessments for target districts. Competing over access to basic commodities and services are often one of the major causes of community conflict. To avoid provoking fraction among communities, the project will closely engage community throughout the project cycle, ensuring they are involved in site and community education committee selection, fully informed of project details, and making sure accountability system is in place to allow them voice their concerns and complaints. 3c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: The project will ensure that education facilities are accessible and appropriate for all in the communities. This will take into specific education and protection needs are taken into account. This will include but not limited to making gender-segregated latrines and hand-washing facilities in the education facilities. The latrines will be equipped with locks. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to girls to address their menstruation need. While gender disparity in Somalia often exclude women from accessing information and making decision, the project will ensure education is accessed both by a girl child and boy child so that they can both have equality opportunity to quality education

Please provide further explanation

The project will work with Government Ministries with the main ministry being Ministry ofEducation, Education cluster, teachers and community leaders. Building capacity and strengthening the community education committees will ensure sustainability of project gains. The Community Education Committees will own the process of managing school assets created in the project together with Ministry of Education. The education materials that will be provided by the schools will impact the running of schools beyond the project period. Training of teaches and refresher trainings for teachers will ensure quality of education beyond the project lifespan. The project also plans to construct or rehabilitate school assets like classrooms, safe water points that will run beyond the project lifespan with the management of assets by the Community Education Committees and Ministry of Education
