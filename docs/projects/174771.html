Project Summary

The boko haram crisis in the Lake Chad Basin and the socio-political instability in CAR over the past decades have drained a massive influx of refugees to Cameroon. Nigerian refugees have settled in the far north region while Central African refugees are distributed in the regions of East, Adamaoua and the North. Both of these crises tend to persist over the years. Along with the settlement of refugees in the above-mentioned regions, there has been a strengthening over the years of the population of refugees settled in urban areas of Yaoundé and Douala. From 25,000 in 2017 to 33,000 in 2020. This increase is the result of the movement of refugees from rural to urban areas in search of well-being. Unfortunately they will be confronted with the harsh reality of the urban environment, in particular the difficulties of accessing basic services: housing, health, education, nutrition, employment, protection. In urban areas, they will no longer have the chance to have access to all the multifaceted assistance they received in rural areas from UNHCR and its partners. Socially, they do not have access to decent employment due on the one hand to their predominantly rural socio-economic profile and their low level of education. They can only do small jobs that are very poorly paid and small informal businesses along the roads. They do not have access to shelters and most live in abandoned construction sites in crowded conditions, sometimes without access to drinking water and latrines. On the school level, the children are not well educated because of lack of means of the parents. Teenage girls engage in sex survival. In terms of health The socioeconomic vulnerability of this population reduces their chance of having access to quality care in a Cameroonian health system characterized by the practice of cost recovery, that is to say that it is necessary to pay for have access to health care. Under these conditions, these populations have the only outcome that self-medication, resulting in an inflation of the rate of community morbidity and death for lack of appropriate care. Despite all these difficulties facing urban refugees, the aid budget allocated by UNHCR has been on the decline over the years due to an unfavorable global economic context. To continue to provide support to refugees in this context of dwindling resources, UNHCR calls for local, national and international solidarity. Responding to this call, the FAIRMED Foundation, which already supports the UNHCR in providing health assistance to refugees in general and urban refugees in particular, has undertaken to develop with the CELO foundation, a support project for urban refugees in Cameroon based on the monetary transfer. This project will have an experimental phase, a pilot phase and an extension phase. It aims to improve the living conditions of socio-economically vulnerable household’s Main objective: Facilitate access to basic needs in vulnerable families of urban refugees Specific objectives for the targeted families, this will be: 1. Facilitate access to basic necessities 2. Facilitate access to basic health care 3. Facilitate access to IGAs Methodology - Selection of beneficiary families will be done in close collaboration with UNHCR's community-based protection services to avoid duplication - Access to basic products will be done with the collaboration of prequalified stores where payment technology will be implemented. - Access to basic health care will be provided with the collaboration of prequalified partner hospitals or technology will be implemented for payment for care. - Access to IGAs will be based on the results of a socio-economic survey and the development of a validated bisness plan for each beneficiary.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Project activities will include:- Finalization of the project and its validation with the CELO foundation: under this project, beneficiary families will not receive money in cash. Terminals will be placed in hospitals and partner stores to allow payment. Vouchers may also be used to access services.- The CELO foundation will provide the necessary training for a good use of money transfer technology. A plan for these trainings will be developed and implemented.- Establishment of a collaboration with the UNHCR within the framework of this project: the choice of target families should be made with the collaboration of community-based protection to prevent the same families from receiving assistance from several partners while others do not. The framework for collaboration should allow UNHCR to be informed of the progress of the project and the results achieved.- Identification of partner hospitals and shops and establishment of a collaborative framework: if there are refugees who own shops and who meet the requirements such as having a bank account, they will be privileged. A collaboration agreement will be established with the partners. An SOP will be developed and validated with the partners.- Staff recruitment and training on CELO money transfer technologies.- Training of hospitals and partner shops on CELO money transfer technology.- Identification of beneficiary families in collaboration with the UNHCR community-based protection service and the partner in charge of the social component of assistance to urban refugees.- The beneficiary families will be briefed on the assistance procedures within the framework of the CELO project, more specifically on the use of vouchers and payment terminals in hospitals and shops.- A communication activity will be carried out within the refugee population in general for a good understanding of the criteria for access to assistance within the framework of the CELO project- A monitoring and supervision plan will be drawn up and implemented to ensure proper project execution at all levels, detect gaps and take preventive and corrective measures.- Establishment of the experimental phase followed by its evaluation. This experimental phase will concern 50 to 100 families. It will take place only in the city of Yaounde and may extend over a period of three months- Implementation of the pilot phase followed by its evaluation. This pilot phase could extend to Douala and include more beneficiary families. At the level of Douala, collaboration will be established with the UNHCR office in Douala and the social partner for joint identification of beneficiary families.- Extension of the project to Douala and Yaoundé: if the experimental and pilot phases are successful, the project could target a large number of families.- A socio-economic survey will be conducted to identify people who have the skills to implement IGAs. Refugees who already carry out IGAs in the informal sector will be able to benefit from project support to leave the informal sector and carry out their activities within a regulatory framework.- Joint evaluations of the project will be made with the UNHCR and the reports will be shared with the CELO foundation.
