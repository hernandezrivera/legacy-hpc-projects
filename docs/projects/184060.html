Project Summary

In order to address acute malnutrition, both treatment and prevention are essential. In the treatment of malnutrition, capacity must be built into local communities to be able to manage and implement a SC, OTP and Targeted Supplemental Feeding Program (TSFP). The 12-month HEW training outlined in the Health section will allow staff within HK to run CMAM and IYCF interventions without having to be dependent on outside help from staff who are not based in HK. This is necessary for long-term sustainable solutions in which acute malnutrition treatment is included in routine PHC services, rather than being managed through short-term emergency programs that rely on outside staff, with constant turnover.
Additionally, to reduce levels of acute malnutrition, SBC promotion needs to be an integral component of interventions. Using proven SBC approaches to promote optimal maternal diets, IYCF, safe motherhood, and hygiene behaviors can prevent many children from deteriorating to the state where they need care in a TSFP, OTP or SC. The Care Group methodology is an SBC platform that allows for optimal meeting frequency, significant coverage of the target population, participatory learning, and involvement of key influencers.
The purpose of reducing acute malnutrition in U5 children and PLWs is multi-sectoral in nature. Integration with the Health, Agriculture, ERMS, and WASH Sectors is crucial to achieving Nutrition outcomes. Children’s health affects their vulnerability to malnutrition. Optimal IYCF practices, particularly around complementary feeding, can only be practiced if people have an adequate supply of diverse food in their gardens, in their livestock assets, and/or in the markets, with enough money to purchase it. If food is in short supply or too expensive because of inflation, purchasing power assistance is critical in preventing the deterioration of the population’s nutritional status. WASH infrastructure and optimal hygiene practices are crucial in preventing diarrheal disease and the malnutrition that often results. The Care Groups platform is designed to incorporate behavior change curriculum that spans Health, Nutrition, Hygiene, Agriculture, ERMS, and Protection topics.

1 - Needs

Disease outbreaks, malnutrition, and climatic shocks continue to affect the lives and livelihoods of many Sudanese. In an assessment conducted within Hamesh Koreib and TelKook by Samaritan’s Purse, findings revealed that emergency/critical needs among target populations within rural Kassala are present in the Health, Nutrition, and Livelihoods sectors, among others. The assessment’s key findings show a severe lack of medical and health services, posing a great risk to rural communities. Delivery of and access to health services by the most vulnerable were negatively affected by the economic and political crisis in Sudan, as well as flooding during the rainy season. Needs continue to increase as health services have been stretched to their breaking point due to the lack of qualified personnel, a shortage of essential medicines and supplies, and inadequate infrastructure due to government austerity measures. Increasing cost and loss of raw materials for in country pharma production of medicines and medical supplies has restricted access to essential life-saving health care services. Top common morbidities reported are malaria, upper respiratory tract infections and diarrhea. Key informant interviews (KII) and focus group discussions (FGD) conducted in the assessment revealed that families are buying cheaper staple foods and decreasing intake of vegetables, fruit, and protein-rich foods because of rising costs. A majority of children who participated in the FGDs had only eaten biscuits and milk the previous day. Additionally, there is an influence from grandmothers discouraging exclusive breastfeeding and encouraging practice such as giving babies sugar water from 0-5 months and feeding meat to children only when they were older. Children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with complications are not receiving the life-saving nutrition or stabilization services they require.According to an SP Assessment in October 2021, many of the children 6-23 months old in the target areas were reported to have eaten only biscuits and milk in the previous 24 hours. One of the most significant barriers to dietary diversity was the cost of the more diverse foods such as vegetables, fruits, animal source foods or pulses. Children were also perceived to not be ready for animal source foods at a young age. Nutrition interventions will be vitally important for the survival of many rural Sudanese children in HK and TK in 2021 Within HK in particular, the nutrition needs for children are dire, as those who have SAM with complications are not receiving the life-saving services they need. A rapid mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) assessment of 299 children 6-59 months old in TK, conducted by SP, revealed a proxy global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of 23%, a proxy moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) rate of 14%, and a proxy SAM rate of 9%. During the SP assessment, it was discovered that women throughout the target population who were not exclusively breastfeeding had complained of decreased milk due to insufficient food intake. Compounding the severe lack of adequate food available throughout HK and TK to improve nutrition is the fact that many cultural beliefs lead to poor feeding practices. Some examples of these beliefs include the idea that babies will become dehydrated if they drink only breast milk and that sugar water should be incorporated into a baby’s diet.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In order to address acute malnutrition, both treatment and prevention are essential. In the treatment of malnutrition, capacity must be built into local communities to be able to manage and implement a SC, and other services. The 12-month HEW training outlined in the Health section will allow staff within HK to run CMAM and IYCF interventions without having to be dependent on outside help from staff who are not based in HK. They will also be trained in GBV identification and referral systems. These interventions are necessary for long-term sustainable solutions in which acute malnutrition treatment is included in routine PHC services, rather than being managed through short-term emergency programs that rely on outside staff, with constant turnover.Additionally, to reduce levels of acute malnutrition, SBC promotion needs to be an integral component of interventions. Using proven SBC approaches to promote optimal maternal diets, IYCF, safe motherhood, and hygiene behaviors can prevent many children from deteriorating to the state where they need care in a TSFP, OTP or SC. The Care Group methodology is an SBC platform that allows for optimal meeting frequency, significant coverage of the target population, participatory learning, and involvement of key influencers.Topics in the Care Group curriculum will include improved maternal diets, IYCF, and key hygiene behaviors. The Care Groups will also incorporate curriculum modules on VSLAs, home gardening, and livestock rearing, addressing some of the issues with access to diverse diets. Care Group meetings will provide continued support for Leaders Mothers so they can effectively cascade messaging to their Neighbor Women. Within the Neighbor Groups, women will be encouraged to share their personal experiences on a variety of nutrition topics and discuss the recommended practices for exclusive breastfeeding, early initiation of breastfeeding, sick child feeding, and nutrition during pregnancy, among others, with guided discussions facilitated by the Leader Mother.The purpose of reducing acute malnutrition in U5 children and PLWs is multi-sectoral in nature. Integration with the Health, Agriculture, ERMS, and WASH Sectors is crucial to achieving Nutrition outcomes. Children’s health affects their vulnerability to malnutrition. Optimal IYCF practices, particularly around complementary feeding, can only be practiced if people have an adequate supply of diverse food in their gardens, in their livestock assets, and/or in the markets, with enough money to purchase it. If food is in short supply or too expensive because of inflation, purchasing power assistance is critical in preventing the deterioration of the population’s nutritional status. WASH infrastructure and optimal hygiene practices are crucial in preventing diarrheal disease and the malnutrition that often results. The Care Groups platform is designed to incorporate behavior change curriculum that spans Health, Nutrition, Hygiene, Agriculture, ERMS, and Protection topics. Stabilization Center: Due to the high rates of SAM in HK, coupled with the inaccessibility of any kind of inpatient care for children with SAM complications, Samaritan’s Purse will incentivize staff provided by the MoH to run a Stabilization Center for children 0-59 months in HK. Referrals will come from local Community Nutrition Volunteers and Care Group Leader Mothers trained in MUAC and bilateral pitting edema screening through USAID-BHA funding. Children screened in the community or referred by their caregivers or from the existing OTP or TSFP who have medical complications will be treated according to the Sudan CMAM national protocols. The Stabilization Center facility will be staffed 24 hours a day. Local ambulance services will be supported through SP’s Health program and may be utilized for transfer of SAM patients with complications to Kassala as necessary.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection means undertaking activities to safeguard people from violence, coercion, deprivation, and discrimination and aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual. For activities with a Nutrition component, protection implies implementing interventions in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance.Protection mainstreaming is essential in the first three phases of the Humanitarian Program Cycle: Assessment, Activity Design, and Implementation. The principles of protection mainstreaming should also be incorporated during the fourth phase of the cycle, Monitoring and Evaluation. All activities will involve the four key elements of protection mainstreaming, which include: 1) avoiding causing harm and prioritizing safety and dignity, 2) ensuring meaningful access, 3) practicing accountability, and 4) promoting participation and empowerment.Samaritan’s Purse conducted an in-depth assessment in HK and TK to understand the health and nutrition situation in the target localities. During this assessment, great care was taken to ensure information was collected covering various demographics of the target group including boys and girls 0-59 months old, boys and girls 0-23 months old, and PLWs. FGDs were held to discuss nutrition needs and concerns within target communities. Separate FGD spaces were provided for specific, vulnerable populations based on gender and age.The Protection information collected during SP’s assessment have informed the design and implementation of this intervention to ensure:Safe and equal access to inclusive and nondiscriminatory provision of services. Through discussions via FGDs or KIIs, the project will identify the most vulnerable groups to ensure their participation in the project activities. People with specific needs or vulnerabilities will be prioritized and supported. The Care Group methodology conducted through the Nutrition interventions will assist in identifying the most vulnerable members of the target communities, ensuring that SP will be able to include and cater to their specific needs. The Activity is designed to inform all participants and their communities about the availability and location of services through community messaging, signage, and more. Nutrition interventions are designed to be respectful and inclusive of cultural and religious practices. Through FGDs or KIIs, SP will ensure that these interventions and activities are respectful and inclusive of cultural and religious practices.To reduce the risk for GBV, men will also be sensitized to the activities that the women are participating in, in order for them to be supportive in how the women are being taught to care for the children.The methodologies of service provision and space arrangements are designed to preserve safety and dignity, with regard to gender and people with disabilities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Any complaints of a sensitive nature, such as the reporting of SEA concerns, will be managed by the in-country Safeguarding Officer. The team will aim to respond in a timely, efficient and confidential manner. Once an account of potential SEA is reported to SP, the appropriate channels of action, will be strictly adhered to and followed. Samaritan’s Purse will investigate all accounts of potential SEA with the utmost diligence and priority as a means of safeguarding activity participants and staff. Disciplinary measures will be invoked as called for. Incorporated into SP’s culture is the zero tolerance policy towards SEA. A point person at the national level works alongside the PMs/team to ensure that Safeguarding is provided, including training on Safeguarding (PSEA), an understanding of the SP Code of Conduct, and the reporting mechanisms in place to report a concern of SEA. In all SP field offices/bases and intervention sites, awareness materials will be displayed to convey PSEA messages. All materials will be adapted to the local language and context. Project staff will inform and sensitize the local community/target population on awareness of PSEA and the requirement that SP staff adhere to a code of conduct, as well as ensure that the target population have knowledge of how to report an SEA concern or situation in a confidential manner. Protection principles are embedded into all aspects of programming.
