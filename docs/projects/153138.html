Project Summary

The proposed twelve (12) months project will be implemented in Upper Nile state targeting 15,000 HHs (5,000 Beneficiaries per each County) of Longechuk, Ulang and Nasir which is in crisis due to insecurity, yearly flooding, drought and where the farming practices among the predominantly peasantry community are very poor culminating in to problems of food insecurity, poverty and environmental degradation.
UNKEA intends to support the affected communities of Three Counties (IDPs, returnees, Hosts, Disabled, HIV/AIDS Patients, gender based violence (GBV) survivals, women headed households) with agriculture inputs (seeds and fishing kits and implementing technical guidance training to vulnerable communities including Beneficiary selection, registration and Verification.
UNKEA through the donor support will distribute livelihood inputs to 15,000 beneficiaries including boys and girls of 15 to 18 years who will be provided with agriculture inputs (seeds & Tools and fishing kits) support in the project area.
UNKEA will carry out the following activities; mobilization and sensitization of community through dialogue in the project area to create awareness of what project activities to be implemented, time frame and importance of the project to the community, identification and registration of beneficiaries, securing the agricultural inputs from the pipe line (FAO) and delivery to the distribution points, distribution of agriculture inputs to ensure access to agriculture inputs by the most vulnerable groups as a start-up package for increased crop production, Technical guidance/training on use of recession irrigation in vegetable production during dry season, technical guidance on agronomic practices including sustainable agricultural land use practices, environmental awareness, HIV/AIDS advocacy and mainstreaming including gender based violence (GBV), activities.
The community where this project will be implemented will be identified through consulting the local authorities and other stake holders on ground on the most vulnerable groups (IDPS, Women headed Household, child headed household, disabled, gender based violence (GBV) survivals and chronically sick) who will be prioritized in the selection.

Needs

Nasir, Longechuk and Ulang Counties are struggling to regain in the aftermath of armed conflicts, floods, ethnic confrontations, livestock disease and plant disease outbreaks. In July 2017, Longechuk County that’s currently classified under IPC 4 (January-March 2019 Projections) witnessed armed confrontations that left more than half of its 72,304 populations either internally displaced or forced as refugees in the neighboring Ethiopia. In the most recent assessment coordinated by UNOCHA (April 2018), about 34,500 people are either returnees or IDPs – majority of whom have adopted negative coping mechanisms including trading sex for food or engaging in theft of cattle to meet food needs. Ulang County has a population of about 98,000. Unfortunately 70% of its population has been under IPC 3&4 throughout 2018. Ulang County has been awash with influx of IDPs from Akobo (Jonglei State) and Nasir County (IDP Rapid Needs Asssesment February 2018). Fresh fights between government and oppostion forces in Feb. 2018 in neighboring Nasir left more than 15,000 people displaced, a good proportion took shelter in Ulang. In July, UNKEA FSL team identified attack of the Fall Army Worm in three payams in Ulang and six in Longechuk, a situation that will frustrate efforts towards bridging the food insecurity gap. The major cereal produced locally in Upper Nile is maize followed by sorghum. Unfortunately, these are the most affected plants by the Fall Army Worm, a situation that has left the population uncertain of future survival. Both counties experience seasonal floods married with massive population displacements especially in August which’s the peak of the rain season. In normal situations, the main source of food is maize. However, due to food scarcity, every household visited in April relied on wild fruits, leaves, roots and seeds of specific tree species (like Balanites aegyptiaca). During rainy season, the population move away from these alternative food sources in order to escape floods. Majority of the households visited by UNKEA and other agencies can only afford one meal a day on most days, and often skipping a meal on many extreme days. In a baseline assessment conducted in Ulang and Longechuk Counties by UNKEA in February 2018, about 73% of 140 respondents reported to have skipped meals due to food shortage; & 98% (137 out of 140 respondents) sold household assets to purchase food. In all assessments conducted, women and children constitute over 75% of the most affected population. This’ confirmed in successive assessments internally done by UNKEA in baseline and two post distribution monitoring reports (Feb-May 2018) where households having children below five years constitute 84%. In Ulang, out of new IDP arrivals totaling 9751 in only three affected payams of Barmach, Rirnyang, and Nyangore, women and children constitute 7,314. Children constitute 40%. As part of solutions to the challenge faced by the affected population, there is urgent need to increase access to food for the most-in need population and ensure tools and seeds (especially fishing kits, crop, & vegetable kits) are provided. Evidence from past UNKEA projects proves that cash transfer programming results in over 80% improvement in access to food (PDM Results, May 2018). Combination of fishing and vegetable kits provision and accompanying training support translates in to increased number of meals, amount of food consumed, and varieties of food at household disposal by 71% (PDM Results, March 2018). The project will provide tools and seed inputs for vegetable and crop cultivation to vulnerable households. Highly vulnerable households will be provided with cash assistance to meet their immediate food needs while securing crop seeds for the main season planting that begins in May/June every year. UNKEA extension staff shall continue to provide extension support to demonstrate best practises for farmers and on how to manage pests and diseases.

Activities

UNKEA proposes an integrated approach that provides immediate relief through cash support and livelihoods enhancement. The cash support shall comprise of both unrestricted/unconditional cash transfer and cash for work towards community-centred activities. Livelihood initiatives shall provide avenue for community resilience in the event of future shocks. Different categories of affected youth, women, and men shall be identified in groupings and technically guided farming and fishing best practices including post harvest handling, storage and management. The activities shall include; UNKEA will implement the following priority lifesaving activities and outputs during this emergency response intervention in Nasir, Ulang and Longechuk Counties, Upper Nile State.  Beneficiary identification and selection - considering most in-need households that qualify for support. Priority shall be accorded to households that have registered nutrition cases in the 27 nutrition sites in Nasir, Ulang & Longechuk. Specific attention shall also be accorded to women and child headed households. Out Put 1: Livelihoods of 12,000 vulnerable individuals, farmers and fisher folks are protected and their food security enhanced. Community Awareness campaigns and beneficiary identification/registration for livelihoods inputs (Cash transfers, crop seeds, vegetable seeds, Tools and fishing kits). Distribution of crop seeds, tools to identified beneficiaries Distribution of vegetable seeds to identified beneficiaries Output 2: Reduced vulnerability, improved capacities and built resilience of vulnerable communities Training sessions at community level Awareness creation on availability of farm tools and inputs provided by the project Needs assessment for community priority activities for cash and food for work – including options for fruitful engagement of women and other marginalized categories of vulnerable persons. Roll-out and supervision of cash/food for work activities – stakeholder consultations, oversight local committees Provision of unrestricted/unconditional cash to most vulnerable of the identified beneficiary households- Setting up demonstration gardens for vegetable cultivation groups as part of resilience capacity building in promoting best practices Disseminate information and provide training on FAW to avoid future disasters that may adversely affect crop yields Building capacity of authorities to facilitate sustainable access and utilization of local resources like land and water Considering that there is evidence of recurrent shocks in each of the project locations, it is prudent that local capacities are enhanced to enable communities cope up against future similar situations. This proposed initiative will provide the self-driven impetus for local productivity amongst the population, skills sets that may be valuable irrespective of where they may be displaced.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

UNKEA has developed a reporting mechanisms in place where we put a suggestion box in a public place in our area of implementations and beneficiaries write their complain and drop it in the box. One focal person is nominated to open the box and share the result with the committees. Those who can't write are able to present their feedback verbally to the focal point persons and will write it down to present to the HQs for major decision. At each project inception, the project convenes all stakeholders to provide details of project deliverables and communities appoint members of the project steering committees also known as cash/input distribution committees. Additional information is provided through banners displayed in public places to show forth what the project is mandated to achieve, participation of beneficiaries, and targets for the project.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project targeting among other parameters include elderly and women. As part of project commitment to the population, this crucial information are shared in advance with all beneficiaries. In addition, provisions are made to further support specific tailored messages that reduce abuse across age and gender. The Protection department develops key messages that food security and livelihood extenstion workers use to communicate to project beneficiaries. Each of the committees have member representations on genders and age-groups for easily communication and understanding. This because women tend to fear to represent their complains to men because some of their complain are sensitive to be address to men and we have females in the committees to listen specifically to female issues. This mechanism worked very well doing our implementation period in 2017-2018 projects and we continue using the same approach in the up coming project. We do also share the final finding with the beneficiaries for their satisfaction to ensure their complain has been put into actions and implementation.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project shall be guided by UNKEA's internal policies in regard to sexual exploitation and abouse. UNKEA has adopted the core humanitarian standard, also abides by the international humanitarian regulations. We commit to SPHERE standard, HAP, ICRC code of conducts. Internal provisions have been formally instituted to protect staff, beneficiaries, member of the beneficiary communities, and other humanitarian workers form abuse at all times. All project staff, contractors, and persons engaged directly in the project implementation shall sign up to the code of conduct that restrict behaviors to avoid harm. UNKEA has reporting protocal that is communicated to stakeholders at all interfaces to encourage communication of related instances of sexual exploitation and abuse. At field level, project officer is a recipient for cases where instances do not directly target staff but entities the project engages to contribute to project objectives. Cases involving have provisions where Payam oversight committees communicate directly with national office through senior management staff that travel to field on quarterly basis to interact with the committees.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultation was done with both community leaders and with community host members, internally displaced persons, church leaders and women groups and youth group to ensure that every member in the community is involved from the beginning of the project to ensure ownership after the end of project period. The project consulted four different categories of stakeholders which included:Local authorities - 30 (10 women and 20 men)Members of the host communities - 70 (20 women, 30 men, 15 boys, and 5 girls)Members of the IDP communities - 100 (50 women, 35 men, 5 boys, and 10 girls)Members of the Returnee communities - 100 (40 women, 25 men, 8 boys, and 7 girls)

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The project has two layers of complain mechanisms:First layer is the project steering committee that directly handles the complaints desk in the regular undertaking of project activities. They are comprised of beneficiary representatives constitution of reprentatives of IDPs, Returnees, host communities, women leaders, religious leaders, and local authority. The second tier is the Payam level oversight committee that does not handle any activities but observe and recieve complains on all actors including on project staff, steering committee members, and all actors in project execution. The oversight committee reports directly to Juba based senior manager who enagages with the field on quarterly basis.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through the project design, communities are engaged both at Project Steering Committee levels and at Payam oversight committee. In addition, monitoring and evaluation tools have included provisions to directly solicit inputs of the county agriculture department, commissioners, local authorities, who are not comprised in any of the two structures. In addition, beneficiary selection, local procurement, local staff recruitment are done with direct input and supervision of local authorities. Authenticity of documentation for each of the above categories of activities are only considered complete with endorsement of local authorities - as a mandatory internal requirement.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project does acknowledge risks that exists and part of the risk challenges is to be solved by this proposal. As hunger bites, women are tasked to trek long distances to look for wild fruits and edible wild options that endander their lives. This project will provide opportunities for life saving remedies in form of cash transfer and fishing kits - as crop cultivation and other longer term solutions unfold. In both counties, possible tensions of access to limited resources between IDPs and host communities have been factored in the project engagements with key stakeholders whom UNKEA is already currently engaging through other projects in the county. Past experience shows that when prior engaged, local authorities are willing to protect IDP populations to access resources. The evidence of similarities in ethnic backgrounds between IDPs and host communities have also played a vital factor in easing tensions that could otherwise emerge from competition for limited resources. In addressing the different needs of groups such as elderly, disabled, HIV/AIDS, women and child headed families, the project will ensure that project locations for training and input distributions are made closest to beneficiaries while allowing the most at-risk vulnerable person favourable time in queus. The vulnerable groups will be the core beneficiaries of this project because of their high risk of securing their own food.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project staffs will work in collaboration with local authority, community leaders, church leaders, women representative and youth representative in order to ensure law and order is respected and the distribution site is peaceful. Project staffs will make sure the crowds are controlled by lining up the beneficiaries while giving a priority to elderly people and child headed family as first to be served. Through the Cash/input distribution committees, there is a complaints handling and management desk whose role extend beyond the day of cash/input distribution to include issues that arise from beneficiary households as a result of items or cash recieved. The committee that also includes women representatives and local chiefs are empowered to address concerns to avoid incidences of domestic violence in benefiting households. Further, there is a deliberate effort to sensitise the public and the beneficiaries on protection related concerns. This is done through the inception meetings, during beneficiary mobilisation and sensitisation, during beneficiary registration, and on the distribution day.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In addition to above, other specific measures are in place to reduce indicidences of violence, coercion or exploitation. Cash beneficiaries are given cash in envelopes to avoid being subjected harrassment of any form. The project shall encourage very weak vulnerable persons to be accompanied by physically stronger members of their communities. The presence of different representatives of IDPs, women, and religious leaders is deliberate to boost confidence and encourage persons exposed or likely exposed to threats to communicate to the committee and to be supported on time.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring and evaluation tools - specifically the post distribution monitoring includes questions on the time it takes for beneficiaries to access distribution venues, access to markets, and time taken in distribution venues to be served. These are monitored from time to time to ensure that beneficiary access is unhindered. On the different concerns that beneficiaries may have, the input distribution committees are involved in tracking reported incidences. Reporting tools therefore include number and nature of cases that include among others, access constraints by beneficiaries. In additiona, local structures have direct interface with UNKEA's M&E to evaluate project performance on all parameters.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

UNKEA approach to beneficiary information is to limit information about the profile of beneficiaries for internal consumption. The project shall ensure that responses generated and compiled from the beneficiaries are made confidential. Beneficiary names are not published and neither are their contact informations. For cash based interventions, the project provides tokens that bear no name, cash value, and distribution due date. This is to limit exposure of beneficiaries to potential threats he/she may face from elements in the communities.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project considers the dignity of beneficiaries in the following areas;1) Waiting time beneficiaries spend before recieving the inputs earmarked to them for distribution2) Right of information about what to expect, when to expect, and in what quantities3) Distances that beneficiaries walk or how much they spend from their households to the distribution venues4) Time management by facilitators during training sessions called by the project These and other parameters are tracked through the monitoring and evaluation tools developed and applied by the M&E department of UNKEA.
