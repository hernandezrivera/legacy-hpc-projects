Project Summary

This project is a continuation of activities already underway since December 2020.
Concern works in collaboration with Al Makan, a Lebanese Civil Society Organisation based in Beirut, to jointly manage a transitional shelter for homeless and destitute migrants in Beirut. It was initially opened to address the increase in migrant homelessness in the wake of the Beirut Blast.
Homelessness continues to occur due to the economic crisis, which makes it increasingly difficult for sponsors to afford domestic workers, as well as there being an increase in abusive practices, leading to an increase in both evictions and escapes. Given the complicated legal status of many residents, the location of the shelter is kept confidential.
Homeless and destitute migrants are provided with safe shelter while they are waiting to either be repatriated or re-engage with another sponsor and re-enter into the workplace in Lebanon. The shelter has a maximum capacity of 30 people at any one time, in order to avoid overcrowding due to COVID-19 or overwhelming PSS and protection staff. As the shelter is not a ‘closed shelter’ (ie the residents are free to come and go) the shelter is not appropriate for complicated cases such as for people suffering from moderate or severe mental health issues – these cases need specialized care which this shelter does not provide.
Based on experience, residents in the shelter stay between one and three months before they can move on –depending on how complicated each individual case is, and how much assistance they get from their embassies or consulates.
While in the shelter, residents receive multi-purpose cash assistance which covers their food and communications costs. Water, electricity and wifi is also provided. All residents receive Psycho-Social Support (PSS) and demand-driven vocational training opportunities. All protection or health concerns that are identified are referred to relevant migrant-focused partners (e.g. Amel, Kafa, LAW, Caritas, This is Lebanon etc). Repatriations are case managed by referring them to embassies and consulates for issuing travel permits and clearing each individual with General Security, and referrals to relevant organisations (e.g. OUR and Amel) for covering flight and PCR costs.
The shelter residents are identified through individual or organizational referrals, handled by Al Makan. Hotline calls that are not eligible for shelter are referred either through assisted referrals or self-referrals, and Al Makan maintains an up to date service map for this purpose.
The shelter is staffed by a Protection and PSS Officer and two volunteers who share the daily management of the shelters, conduct PSS sessions, organize trainings, and assist with the hotline for receiving referrals. Al Makan takes on the daily management (operation and maintenance) of the apartments and receives/assesses referrals. Concern takes on the daily management of the residents from a protection and PSS perspective, provides MPCA and case manages protection concerns that may arise.
Al Makan has been fundraising with an aim to take over the long term running of the shelter, but their fundraising is proving difficult and slow to mature. An additional 12 months of funding are required to buy Al Makan time to raise the funds required to be able to take the shelter without ongoing support from Concern, creating a sustainable shelter solution for homelessness among the migrant communities.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The project targets homeless migrants; priority for shelter is given to single women/mothers, with no nationality restriction. Shelter residents have access to keys, but doors are locked at night. Men/families in the shelter have different apartments in another part of Beirut to where the women-only apartments are. Locations of all shelter apartments are confidential. MPCA alleviates the need for residents to seek additional (risky) income. Female shelter staff visit daily. Severe cases of GBV are referred to specialized shelters (Amel, Kafa, Abaad). Severe or moderate mental health issues are referred to MSF. For shelter residents, their protection concerns are identified through personal interviews with each resident. Legal protection issues are referred to Amel or LAW. Health issues are referred to MSF, IMC or public health centres. All referrals are followed up to ensure service delivery. Other issues are addressed by shelter staff – especially through PSS sessions.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Continual feedback mechanisms are in place through continual contact with shelter staff. Individual and group PSS sessions are designed to encourage feedback so that any issue can be addressed immediately. Formal feedback is given through exit interviews when ready to repatriate. Complaints about the shelter staff can be made through Concern’s complaints hotline number, which is made available to shelter residents.All staff are sign the Code of Conduct and associated policies which govern Concern staff behavior. This is extended to partner staff, visitors and any other person related to the project.Complaints are addressed through Concern’s complaints response mechanism.
