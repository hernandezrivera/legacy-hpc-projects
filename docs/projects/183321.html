Project Summary

Since 2019, Lebanon is going through a period of deep economic crisis characterized by social-political instability and a sharp drop in household purchasing power due to a collapse of the Lebanese pound equal to 90%. The economic crisis was compounded by the strong impact of the explosions at the port of Beirut on August 4, 2020, which caused heavy human losses and damage to infrastructure in the areas of Beirut and Mount Lebanon. A major consequence of this situation is school dropout further exacerbated by the impact of COVID-19, which has deepened inequities among students and contributed to leaving the most vulnerable kids behind. This phenomenon, by removing children from schools, deprives them of a safe space and exposes them, among other things, to serious protection risks and negative coping mechanisms such as early marriages and child labor. This project aims to facilitate access to education for the most vulnerable population and to stem the phenomenon of school dropout. The action will take place in Bekaa and Mount Lebanon at community level and in four schools (2 par each governorate) choose following vulnerability criteria and in coordination and collaboration with MEHE. The intervention guarantees an inclusive approach by targeting boys, girls and their caregivers from the Lebanese community and migrant workers and by promoting contacts and sharing moments between the two communities through the promotion of recreational activities involving children and caregivers. During the recreational activities will be treated sensitive issues such as cyberbullying, conflict resolution, communication strategies and online safety. The importance of education; as well as that of child protection, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse and positive parenting, will be promoted through awareness campaigns conducted online and "Back to school" campaigns within the targeted schools. The purpose of these activities is to involve care givers in the education of students and support the re-entry of Out of Education Children (OOEC) into the official school system. In order to support educational expenses, educational kits will be distributed containing stationery, backpacks and materials used in schools, as well as cash for education for the most vulnerable families. These activities will take place in parallel with case management and referral to basic services activities aimed at identifying the most vulnerable OOEC in order to facilitate their reintegration. This activity will involve the participation of Community Outreach Volunteers to establish sustainable community referral mechanisms. Remedial classes, catch-up programs, and retention activities will also be provided to help reintegrate OOEC and support children who are behind in the program. The program aims to engage a total of 3092 caregivers and 2,000 children through the use of online tools that will enable the outreach to a large number of beneficiaries and the continuation of activities in case of further closures due to COVID-19. The involvement of communities from the early stages of the project will allow an implementation based on real needs and in line with the cultural peculiarities of communities. This approach also aims to ensure the sustainability of the project after its completion.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

SO1- Activity 1.1: Implementation of 2 "Back to school" campaigns focused on the importance of formal education, the risks of dropping out of school, and the inclusion of children with disabilities: INTERSOS’ Social Workers will carry out 4 Back to School (2 par each Governorate) campaigns in the targeted municipalities. The aim of this activity is to encourage students and parents/caregivers to continue the schooling and to promote the importance of both primary and secondary education for vulnerable students, children with disabilities and those already out of the school system. 2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

INTERSOS’ programmes are designed to follow the principles of participation, do no harm, confidentiality, safety, non-discrimination, and quality and accountability. The services provided do not to discriminate against any persons based on their gender, age, or nationality. Activities will take place in culturally acceptable places. Psychosocial Support activities will include curriculum aimed at addressing bullying through different media, contributing to the development of an accepting and inclusive learning environment. Gender balance will be taken in to account in the selection process of staff. Protection, Age, Gender and Diversity will be taken into consideration throughout the proposed intervention. INTERSOS has a well-developed child protection/safeguarding and PSEA policies in place, in respect of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). INTERSOS will work with and through existing community networks, ensuring representation of marginalized and vulnerable groups.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

INTERSOS’ rooted presence in the targeted areas has bolstered the understanding and knowledge of the context as well as of the needs of populations. INTERSOS will ensure that feedback mechanisms are put in place to tailor and respond to beneficiaries’ needs and suggestions, and community-based focal points will be in place to facilitate the communication of feedback to relevant entities, in a secure and confidential manner. A component of the program entails working with community members; thus, beneficiaries will be directly involved in leading selected activities. Beneficiaries will be informed of the project's objectives through trained staff, information sessions, leaflets and explanation of INTERSOS feedback/complaints mechanisms. INTERSOS staff will ensure that complaint mechanisms are always available and that beneficiaries are aware of them. Feedback boxes will be set up so beneficiaries can anonymously provide their feedback and a hotline will be shared with communities.
