Project Summary

Syria Relief aims through this project to support the IDPs living in camps in harsh conditions, in damaged tents, and self-settled camps without proper infrastructure, negatively affecting the children, the elderly, and the people with disabilities in addition to the men and women.
The intervention ensures the transfer of those people to dignified shelters in planned camps with adequate access to primary humanitarian living conditions associated with the well-planned infrastructure and other facilities such as health centers- schools - community centers.
SR is planning to establish two planned camps hosting 1000 families (5000 individuals) with all the associated infrastructure and facilities; the dignified shelters will be designed based on the SNFI cluster guideline's recommendation and with the beneficiaries' consultation.
Moreover, SR will also keep an eye on the improvement of the existing self-settled camp's infrastructure by improving the sewage network, water network, and the graveling of the main and sub-roads to ensure that these campsites will not face any flood incidents, which is causing a massive loss of the assets and real danger on their lives.
SR is planning to support 3000 IDPs living in camps with the improvement of their camp's infrastructure, including establishing a sewage network, a drainage network, and the graveling of the camp's roads.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, " Primarily in northwest and northeast Syria, 2.05i a million IDPs reside in last resort sites, a marginal increase from 2.02 million in the previous year. Such locations lack crucial infrastructure and rely on emergency shelter solutions and continued humanitarian support. The majority of displacement is currently in excess of 3 years, and 37 percent of households have reportedly been displaced more than three times since 2011" Based on the need assessment conducted by SR, more than 80 % of the IDPs live in the self-settled camps. These camps are not supported with the proper infrastructure, which means the deterioration of the living conditions of those vulnerable people. The self-settled camps are established in rural areas. They cannot redesign the shelter's distribution, noting that more than 79 % of the people are still living in tents for more than three years. Those tents are not protecting them from harsh weather conditions and are subject to flood and fire incidents causing the loss of the people's assets. The PWD, elderly, and children are the most vulnerable in those camps, facing a lack of access to WASH facilities. Meanwhile, these conditions are causing a real risk in their daily lives.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SR is considering the protection from SEA as first priority during the implementation of its activities • All staff will be trained, acknowledge, and sign a code of conduct for sexual exploitation and abuse, moreover, SR has zero-tolerance against SEA. • PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the location of the project, PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp). • All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies to ensure full adherence to the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with firmly and expeditiously. • Ensuring that services and supplies are affordable, free, accessible to all of beneficiaries without any discrimination or exploitation. • Establishing internal SEA reporting and investigation mechanism, with ensuring that all protection cases, including GBV and PSEA will be referred to the respective focal point in the respective locations and sensitive data will be handled and kept confidentially with the PSEA field FP.
