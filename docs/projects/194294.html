Project Summary

To provide access to inclusive, quality, and protective basic education to vulnerable and marginalized children, Out of School Children and youths aged (3-18) in Cueibet, Rumbek North and Rumbek East counties of Lakes State. The programme will implement interventions that lead to increased access to education for children. The programme will ensure that access to basic education meets the quality standards as set in the INEE minimum standards of Education in Emergencies. Activities will promote enrolment, retention and transition of children to the next school year while minimizing the possibility of dropouts. The learning infrastructure and the school environment will be disability friendly and gender sensitive to learners. School environments will provide intentional MHPSS activities and have clear referral pathways including to CP to ensure school environments are protective. Teachers will receive CPD and volunteer teachers incentivized. The project will target 4,090 total beneficiaries desegregated into 3,272 children and youths (1,962 Boys & 1,310 Girls), 482 teachers (289 M, & 193 W), 336 PTAs/SMCs (184 M, & 152 W); with 15% comprising of persons with disability Lack of training for teachers on inclusive education for CwDs, No assistive or mobility devices for CwDs, host communities and IDPs. The project will aim at improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for 3,272 children and youths (1,962 Boys & 1,310 Girls) by: enrolling 3,272 children and youths (1,962 Boys & 1,310 Girls) children in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools and in learning centers. 6 Temporary Learning Spaces will be established, rehabilitation of 6 Temporary Learning Spaces, establishment of 8 gender segregated latrines, water points. Additionally, the project will be rehabilitating 8 gender segregated latrines and water points; and distributing or provide sanitation and hygiene materials to 26 schools (hand washing soap, sanitizers, and masks) and PPE for students and teachers.
The project will improve the quality of education, gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children by providing 1,636 (916 B & 720 G) emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials; providing teaching and learning materials for 26 schools in ECD, primary and secondary school education. The project will equally be conducting training 420 (231 M & 189 F) teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children; update 420 (231 M & 189 F) teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition. Providing life-skills and basic vocational training to 120 (64 G & 56 G) youth and adolescents provide of dignity kits to 1,310 girls in schools; provide customized support to 262 young mothers in schools and to conduct 1-disability assessment. All learning centres will have their referral pathways strengthened or established, with teachers trained on how to identify and refer cases through a child helpdesk.
In order to strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors the project will: train 336 (184 M & 152 W) PTAs/SMCs and education authorities on school management, governance, PSS and DRR. The project will support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets); conducting 5 back to learning campaigns. HAGI will implement directly and with local partners, supporting technical, operational and institutional capacity strengthening of county education system.

1 - Needs

SSEC ranked and classified Cueibet, Rumbek North and Rumbek East, Lakes State as high risk areas that require urgent interventions (SSEC Prioritization, 2022). Children in South Sudan said hunger was forcing more young people to drop out of school, putting girls at risk of early marriage and sexual exploitation, while also forcing some boys into crime (Rumbek North /Cueibet hunger Report. In 2022, 3.6 million people, 97 per cent of whom are children are estimated to need education services. Following COVID19, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. The current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million after schools re-open after 14 months closure due to Covid-19. Additionally, gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7 per cent between 2020 (2,727,209) and 2021 (2,523,204). (South Sudan HNO, 2022). Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality. In the targeted project locations, safety and security incidents in and around education facilities, as well as destruction or occupation was mentioned as barriers to access education facilities (FSNMS+ Publication, 2021). School functionality has been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 including in Rumbek East, Cueibet and Yirol East. (Education Needs Assessment (ENA), 2022). HAGI Cash transfer programming will be inform of both unrestricted and unconditional grants for support to girls and young mothers to remain in schools and conditional for schools operations. However, the use will be limited only to Protection and essentials education concerns only within the sectors. This project therefore seeks first to know how education functionalities can be part of project design to make an expansion of the use of cash in other sectors that can be supported by improved market analysis, an increased focus on income and impact measurement, absence of evidence of harming the market, functional and healthy market, increased availability of products in the market, increased of products in the market, resilience of households, Gender, protection and inclusion of vulnerable people, shifts in income and gender perception, decreased disease rate, quality occupancy of housing and increased labour capacity in construction.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

HAGI Cash assistance will strive to provide the most appropriate, effective and efficient responses to the needs of people affected by a crisis. It will be provided in many ways depending on needs and context. The choice of appropriate transfers (in-kind, cash or vouchers), or their combination, must be based on a thorough assessment and context-specific analysis, including cost effectiveness and efficiency, secondary market impacts, the flexibility of the transfer, local availability of goods and services, prioritization and targeting, gender and protection considerations, and risks of insecurity and corruption. Cash will be specifically provided to girls and young mothers to remain in schools and to schools as a capitation grants for schools operations. HAGI in the past has been delivering Cash for Seeds- Based assistance to schools gardeners and small farmers group through (FAO project) and also does so in protection programming. Therefore, Cash-based intervention has the potential to respond quickly and efficiently to a variety of urgent needs offering dignity, flexibility of choice to beneficiaries and supporting local markets growth for long term development. assistance covers both cash and vouchers that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief and which contribute to meeting their basic needs or to assisting them in protecting or reestablishing their livelihoods. Cash-based assistance, particularly when delivered in the form of unconditional cash is a people-centered approach.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will establish accessible, gender and age-friendly accountability and feedback. The mechanisms will systematically include participation of affected population (individuals with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) in project planning and review and will facilitate the provision of feedback from affected people on the services. In the targeted schools, accessible, gender and age appropriate feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established to enable children to share their issues and views. This will be a continuous mechanism that will continue to be active beyond the project period. Accountability assessment will be conducted to determine the preferred accountability mechanisms, which include complaint help desks, suggestion boxes, meetings as well as reporting issues directly to staff. Informed by this assessment, the project will establish the mechanism at schools and in CFSs. These diverse mechanisms should offer options for persons with disabilities, limited literacy, and for speakers of minority languages to report on both sensitive and non sensitive issues, and also allow literate and non literate as well the most vulnerable to access the mechanisms. Children will also be sensitized on the availability and location of feedback and complaint mechanisms to enable them understand what to report and where. #Feedback from vulnerable community members who are not literate will be collect through the existing Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs).

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HAGI has a Programme Accountability Framework (PAF) and AAP with four pillars that holds the organization accountable to the affected population they seek to assist. Through the PAF framework, HAGI will ensure timely information sharing with the affected population; consult the affected population on any issue before making decision; promoting participation of the affected population at every stage of the project cycle and establishing relevant feedback and complaint mechanisms. HAGI will ensure that all the staff involved in the implementation of this project are trained on humanitarian accountability standards (PAF, Sphere Standards and Core humanitarian standards) so that they will deliberately involve, inform the beneficiaries and seek their opinions at all stages of the project management. The project beneficiaries will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process and the quality of services they are getting from the organization. It is also planned that the project team will provide guidance to the beneficiaries on the nature of complaints to launch and the available feedback/complaint mechanisms put in place to collect and redress grievances. HAGI will put in place child friendly feedback/complaint mechanisms, suggestion boxes, help desks, dedicated phone lines and email address for this purpose. Printed posters containing these addresses shall be distributed by the organization and placed in all Payams and meeting venues for ease of access by the beneficiaries and the general public. This project will also use conflicts sensitive planning to apply the principles of Do No Harm. Additionally, the project will ensure active and consistent communication and feedback from key stakeholders, such as local officials and government staff, at both county and state levels. Coordination will entail meeting with and communicating project activities to key stakeholders to ensure that activities are appropriate and well supported. Additional meetings and discussions will take place as needed to ensure any follow-up on issues, challenges, or areas that need improvement. HAGI has had a longstanding presence in Aweil Centre, Rumbek Centre, Yirol East.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by HAGI officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project – Parents, Teachers, authorities, health club members and volunteers.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through the accountability mechanisms established, the M&E team will get feedback from teachers, pupils and parent and use this feedback to adjust the project to benefit children more. #The feedback collected from beneficiaries will be collated, analyzed and lessons learnt will be used to adapt the project. # to ensure adequate records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, HAGI team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability.
