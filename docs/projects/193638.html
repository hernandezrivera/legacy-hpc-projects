Project Summary

The United Nations, in its 75th session of the General Assembly on the 23rd of July 2021, issues the first ever Resolution on Vision as a contribution for Resolution 70/1 on the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development. “Vision for Everyone: accelerating action to achieve Sustainable Development Goals” is a historic global milestone acknowledging the massive effect of blindness as one, among others, main obstacle for development and a major cause of deepening poverty. According to the Resolution, “at least 2 billion people are living with vision impairment or blindness and 1.1 billion…., with half the global population expected to be living with vision impairment by 2050”, which reflects the urgency to take actions by the global actors and governments to fill in the massive gaps for equal and inclusive eye care provision, especially for low-medium income countries.
Given that Palestine falls under the global context being a medium-low-income country, St. John, being the single largest, and the only inclusive and charitable provider of eye care to the Palestinian People, is leading on developing eye care provision mainly to prevent avoidable blindness and visual impairment in the Palestine amid the controversial political and socio-economic situation.
St. John Eye Hospital has been focusing its efforts to improve accessibility and availability of essential eye care services to all Palestinians equally, with a major focus on the most vulnerable and disadvantaged population group, in prevention of avoidable blindness in a protracted conflict. Our strategy since the second Palestinian uprising is to reach out for people who cannot reach us in the main Hospital in Jerusalem, hence the expansions of our reach through static hospitals and clinics in addition to the mobile outreach clinics covering the entire West Bank and Gaza.
The mobile outreach programme has been running since 1984 and is considered a key aspect of St. John’s humanitarian ophthalmic mission in prevention of avoidable blindness in Palestine. The programme aims at providing access to primary eye care services to poor Palestinians living in marginalized, isolated localities in the West Bank and the Gaza strip where the provision of quality eye care services is unavailable. Due to the high need for our mobile outreach services, the proposed project aims at expanding the mobile outreach programme through adding one more mobile clinic in the West bank and turning the Gaza-based mobile outreach clinic into a full time, 5-day, operating clinic.
The programme expansion is required in the West Bank in order to widen our reach to fundamentally reach out to people living in Area C, refugee camps and officially recognized vulnerable population groups (people with disabilities, women, children and elderly). We have found that one outreach clinic to cover for all population categories is not enough and therefore we have decided to add one more mobile clinic so that each one focuses on certain categories and complement each other.
In Gaza, the current mobile outreach clinic was established in 2016 and currently operates 3-day a week and it serves boarder areas, certain refugee camps and vulnerable population groups to a certain extent. However, we have witnessed a greater need than anticipated especially for the most vulnerable population groups who can't access our hospital services in Gaza. Accordingly, and due to the long waiting lists of related CBOs, we have decided to turn the Mobile clinic we operate from 3-day to 5-day a week clinic in order to service larger number of patients.
In addition, there is a very long waiting list for essential eye surgeries in Gaza and the West Bank Hospital due to the backlog caused by Covid-19 and the latest escalations in Palestine. SJEHG took on a challenge by developing a special plan that enables the team to perform 1,000 additional surgeries in Gaza and the West Bank, to overcome the waiting list in prevention of avoidable blindness

1 - Needs

It is not possible to separate health service and eye health programming in Palestine from the political and socio-economic complexity of the region. The 2020 United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (UNOCHA) Humanitarian Needs Overview describes the situation in Palestine as a “protracted protection crisis characterized by Israel’s occupation, insufficient respect for international humanitarian and human rights laws, internal Palestinian political divisions and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israeli Security Forces and Palestinian armed groups”. There are an estimated 2.4 million people – around 40 per cent of the population – who are at risk of conflict, forced displacement, denial of access to livelihoods, food insecurity, and inadequate access to essential services including health. The humanitarian needs outlined above are magnified in the Gaza Strip, a sanctioned territory, where the governmental health system is on the verge of collapse after 14 years of blockade, intra-Palestinian political divide, chronic energy shortages and inconsistent payment of medical personnel. The Gazan economy is in “free fall” according to the World Bank, and deep poverty and unemployment are increasing food insecurity which has been exacerbated by the effects of COVID-19 on the sanctioned territory.Whilst the situation in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, is less acute, settlement expansion, access restrictions, demolitions and evictions continue to undermine hopes for a two-state solution. An increasingly constrained operational context with restrictions on INGO and unprecedented shortfalls in donor support for the Palestinian Authority, United Nations Relief Works Agency (UNRWA) and other humanitarian actors in general is making it difficult to respond to need.Data from the 2019 National Rapid Assessment of Avoidable Blindness (RAAB) survey, conducted by St. John Eye Hospital Group on a national scale, showed that the prevalence of blindness was significantly higher among women compared to men in both Gaza (5.0% cf. 2.9%; p<0.022) and West Bank (2.5% cf. 0.9%; p<0.022). Evidence from the literature indicates that women and people living with a disability are less likely to access health services – including eye health care. For the first time, the RAAB survey also showed that one in four Palestinians aged 50 years and older have a disability. The study also found that Cataract is the leading cause of blindness in Palestine (38.0%), SVI (45.0%) and MVI (39.9%).In the last 2 years, Palestine has gone through major catastrophic events that added to the socio-economic burden. COVID-19 and security/war escalations in Gaza and the West Bank has led to an increasing need for essential eye care services that most people in Palestine cannot afford to receive. Cataract being the leading cause of blindness, and as the waiting lists for cataract surgery has unprecedently grown, St. John is taking an action with the generous support of its international partners and donors. Throughout this project, SJEH will expand its outreach work in the West Bank and Gaza to reach the most vulnerable groups living in marginalized areas all over Palestine. In addition, SJEH will maximize the work of its team to perform 1,000 additional surgeries in the West bank and Gaza for patients that need to undergo urgent essential surgical intervention to save the gift of sight.

3 - Indicators

The new mobile outreach in the West Bank and the Gaza outreach expansion teams are expected to screen 6,000-7,000 new patients each, out of which 10-15% of the screened patients are expected to require further examination and treatment at one of SJEHG’s hospital and clinics across the West Bank and Gaza. The free-of-charge mobile outreach clinics will mainly serve underprivileged people mainly focusing on women and men over 40, diabetic patients, children (girls and boys) and people with disabilities. This project will target the most vulnerable population of the Gaza strip and the West Bank. The project will focus on Area “C”, East Jerusalem, and H2 Hebron area in the West Bank and refugee camps and boarder areas in the Gaza strip with the highest vulnerability status as recognized by the UNOCHA. In addition, a sub-focus will be given to population who need ophthalmologic care, but they cannot access it such as women, elderly people, children and people with disabilities. 1000 essential eye surgical intervention will be performed to patients in the West Bank and Gaza.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

First established in Jerusalem in 1882, St. John Eye Hospital Group (SJEHG) is the only charitable and single largest provider of ophthalmic services in the Palestinian territories (oPt). It has been providing quality services and preserving the “gift of sight” of the local community and the wider region for the past 140 years The local staff at the SJEH are aware of the social and cultural norms and the proper protocols. Based on the do-no-harm analysis the project will take into consideration the following points: • Every patient will be received in the hospital based on SJEHG mission and vision • Priority will be given to refugees, people with disabilities and women who have no alternative access to health. Information about services is therefore distributed among communities and organizations equally, to make sure that awareness about available services is adequately distributed and known • Sensitization of SJEH staff towards inclusive services through training them on inclusive eye care and low vision to make these services inclusive for people with disabilities. • Our services will be provided in a very safe environment abiding to the Health Authorities and international standards the Hospital Group has taken all the international standards for protection and cross-infection measures in its operations, not only in Mobile outreach, but also in all our clinical settings and facilities in Jerusalem, the West Bank and Gaza.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to support vulnerable groups, a permanent system of cooperation and referral between the providers of eye care and rehabilitation services and other community-based organizations will be established, which is currently lacking in the health sector. The access to eye health services and to necessary rehabilitation services can thus be ensured for patients in the long term. The established two-way referral system (from and to SJEH) will be the first step towards establishing closer cooperation between the programs, aiming to maintain a lasting and sustainable cooperation after the end of the project. Through our 140 years of service, St. John focuses on enhancing gender equality as we consider women to have a major role in the society and have an integral part in building our future generation. In addition, within the larger project scope the Hospital is currently implementing a national Gender and Disability assessment, a first ever study that research obstacles and barriers that prevent or limit proper accessibility to eye care services for women, girls and people with disability. The results of the study will enable St. John to identify barriers and improve accessibility and involvement of women and people with disability in the service delivery process and cycle
