Project Summary

In its ninth year, the crisis in Northeast (NE) Nigeria shows no sign of abating. The economic system of the NE region has been severely disrupted, with serious impact on agricultural and income generating activities resulting in reduced purchasing power at the household (HH) level and in the ability to meet their families’ basic food needs. Despite continued scale up in humanitarian assistance, significant gaps remain, as thousands of people affected by the conflict have unstable livelihoods sources. In 2018, 2.9 million individuals are expected to be in dire need of 3.3 million will necessitate agriculture security support.
To continue to support conflict-affected families and meet agriculture and livelihoods needs, CRS proposes a second phase of its “Multi-sectoral Support for Conflict-affected Families in Borno and Yobe States” project, initially launched in September 2016, and funded by the United States Agency for International Development’s (USAID) Office of Foreign Disaster Assistance (OFDA). Through a multi-sectoral approach, CRS will continue to provide vulnerable HHs with basic agriculture and livelihood needs while also addressing adverse coping strategies by strengthening their agricultural-related livelihood options. In total, up to 16,500 individuals (3,000 HHs) will be targeted for assistance through the provision of an integrated emergency package including agriculture support.
The project will continue to cover those Local Government Areas (LGAs) most affected by the conflict, namely Magumeri and Gubio LGAs (Borno State) and Gujba, Gulani LGAs (Yobe State). This is a scale up project where CRS is riding of the success and good practices from previous project which include but not limited to:
* Capacity building of farmers skills and knowledge contributes to improved livelihoods
*Setting up of irrigation infrastructures accelerate changes in livelihoods
*Linking farmers to markets is the best self replicating activity to improve livelihoods
This project will be implemented from July 1, 20119 to December 30, 2019 with a budget of US$100,000. The project will target 2100 Females and 900 males in the target areas.

Needs

The project should address Agriculture and Livelihoods needs in the target areas. The assessment done by CRS shows that IDPs and Host communities do not have access to quality seeds, tools and extension support as a result the insecurity in the target areas. With the insurgency going into 9 years, communities have reduced capacity to sustain own seed banks.
