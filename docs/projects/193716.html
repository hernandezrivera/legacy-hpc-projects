Project Summary

The project includes full standard recovery and overhauling of shelters and the provision of capacity building to lessen exposure of targeted beneficiaries to man-made/natural disasters and weather patterns, to manage and adapt internal spaces empowering affected people to live a dignified life. Providing adequate shelter moderates the vulnerabilities and risks associated with the coercive environment in area C and East Jerusalem, particularly for women, children, elderly and PWDs. Shelter rehabilitation and upgrading activities, combined with the implementation of community participation methodology, will allow for the empowerment of communities by boosting coping capacities, awareness and resilience. The action is fully in line with 2023 HRP Strategic Objective 2, in line with the minimum shelter cluster standards for adequate housing for all age, gender and diversity groups, including PWDs. This integrated approach guarantees communities are adequately secured and equipped with tangible assets to prevent forcible transfer. Beneficiaries of this action are 1256 people (252 adult men, 256 adult women, 352 boys, 356 girls, 40 elders, including 38 PWDs) among 211 vulnerable households in 33 communities with particular socio-economic and protection vulnerabilities, which are connected to their physical and politically-related situation: families with PWDs who need shelter/WASH facilities adaptation, families who suffer from IF and settler’s violence; households who lack access to basic services, safe shelter, and are under movement restrictions among many other hindrances. The action will target 33 communities, 17 communities are allocated for WWGVC, and 16 communities are under PHC target. Communities are distributed among seven governorates; Jenin (Khirbet Fraseen), Ramallah (Rantis Bedouins), Tubas (Ein al Hilwa - Wadi al Faw, Al Farisiya Ihmayyer), Jericho (Marj Naajeh Herders, Al Jiftlik-ash-Shuneh), Bethlehem (Barriyat Za'tara, um Al Hawa), Hebron (Khallet Al Mafateeh, Al Abaidiya, Um Qussa, Al Ganoub, At Taybe, Khirbet Bism, Abo El-Henna, Umm al Kheir) and Jerusalem (inside and outside the wall; An Nabi Samwil, Beit Hanina, Shofat, Sheikh Jarrah, Wadi Al-Joz, Al-Swaneh, Al-Tur, the Old city, Ras Al-Amoud, Silwan, Al-thouri, Jbal Al-Mokaber, Sourbaher, Al-Tantour, Beit Safafa, Um-Tuba, Issawieh). Shelter rehabilitation will be obtained through different interventions:
In Area C: rehabilitation of metal and concrete shelters with composite roof, internal renovation works, provision of PVC covers, insulation, windows and Covid-19 measures adaptation;
In Jerusalem: replace sandwich panel’s roof, ventilation, insulation, flooring, windows, internal walls, toilets, sinks and kitchens, access facilitation for PwDs if needed. Interventions in EJ do not need municipality permits since no new constructions will be executed, solely rehabilitation and in some cases rearrangements of existing housing units.
This project contributes to moderate protection risks linked to undignified living standards for vulnerable families, improving the quality of the proper housing stock, reducing land designation for housing and infrastructure by reestablishing the existing housing stock and improving the socioeconomic and health conditions for the beneficiaries.
Capacity building will be provided for the communities and provision of material support will be backed-up with activities and sessions to contribute to self-recovery: to reduce risk of SGBV and other protection-related risks; to increase privacy and PWD minimum living standards; to improve access to WASH facilities and hygiene. All interventions will be implemented in coordination with the PA. The action is part of a multi-year action to cover the humanitarian needs of the communities with a NEXUS prospective, the action responds to the communities’ needs, taking into account their resources and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of safety and dignity.

1 - Needs

Since 2015, WW-GVC has been gathering quantitative and qualitative data in the targeted communities as part of the Community-based Protection Approach (CPA), within the framework of the ECHO West Bank Protection Consortium, to compile protection-related data on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities, and coping mechanisms. The most recent round of assessment, CPA update 2021/2022, emphasized the ongoing protection risks, movement restrictions, and limited access to basic services (including shelter). 172 communities have been targeted in this quantitative assessment. The main findings showed that 1,673 shelters are considered sub-standard shelters such as caves, tents, and sheds, exposing the population to harsh weather conditions, needing construction or provision of 94 new shelter solutions in 12 communities to provide access to adequate housing. In fact, 942 shelters in 54 communities are in need of urgent rehabilitation to meet basic shelter standards, necessitating the provision of 148 residential tents and tent covers in 7 communities. Community members who participated in focus groups with both men and women as part of the CPA steps stated that certain groups, including young couples, households headed by women, pregnant women, children, elderly, and People with Disabilities (PWDs), are particularly vulnerable and exposed to shelter-related risks. Women represent a vulnerable group, given the patriarchal culture that stresses women’s role in the domestic sphere, they specifically suffer from improper and unsafe living conditions and they are more vulnerable to poverty and diseases; the same applies for children, elders and PWDs. PHC conducts regular needs assessments in the West Bank and EJ. The most recent assessment was conducted in June 2022 for the West Bank as a joint work with the Shelter Cluster vulnerability assessment team. Alongside EJ assessment is based on PHC’s waiting list and received applications for the Housing Rehabilitation Program. The assessments depend on: I) technical assessment in sight of minimum shelter standards and II) socio-economic assessment in sight of the needs of women, men, boys, and girls, and the needs of different age groups. Through the exit of the previous HRP phase, the project team conducted an assessment in June 2022 for 145 shelters in Jerusalem and found that 68 vulnerable families – around 50% of them are FHH – are in emergency shelter rehabilitation in the coming years. Findings of the assessment in EJ mainly wereI) Inadequate shelter conditions increase the likelihood of displacement from Jerusalem to outside the city, in particular for FHH’s families.II) Some families lack the safety, health, and comfort requirements due to overcrowded shelters and poor living conditions that affect family members’ privacy and dignity and children’s welfare resulting in the intergenerational transmission of social inequality.III) Families invest all spaces in the shelter especially in the old city due to the limitation and cost of building permits, restrictions, and land confiscation. Moreover, WW-GVC has conducted vulnerability assessments to assess shelters conditions in the period March – June, 2022. This assessment has been conducted in 300 communities located in area C, with ten (10) technical indicators which have been evaluated by the technical and social team to assess the shelters according to the minimum shelter cluster standards. The main finding from this assessment shows that 3,335 shelters need urgent rehabilitation in 300 communities distributed in 11 governorates. All of the various inputs of men, women, boys, and girls gathered through the aforementioned assessments were taken into consideration during the project design and connected to the rehabilitation of the shelters. The identified activities will be evaluated further through technical evaluations and community consultations in accordance with the relevant humanitarian standards, the parameters of the shelter cluster, and any new COVID-19 prevention measures recommended by the shelter cluster and WHO, as well as taking into account social acceptance to ensure gender-and age-appropriate solutions. In addition, social teams will examine the situation in the shelters with all family members, particularly women and girls, to identify the key issues relating to health, hygiene, and women’s and girls’ privacy. They will then rely this information to the technical staff, who will use it to determine the best approaches for working with families and people with disabilities. These factors will be considered in the final product design, drawings, and BoQ to ensure that they are adjusted to the end-users’ needs. Based on the needs identified during the consultation with field workers, women will be involved in designing the rehabilitation of the shelters. The project will place particular attention on vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities, families headed by women or by elders).

2 - Activities

The action will involve 211 households in 33 communities that are at high risk of exposure to or affected by natural hazards (such as the COVID pandemic, extreme weather, flooding), as well as man-made hazards (such as exposure to settler violence). The action will include upgrading households’ shelters, adaption of shelters and WASH facilities for at least 38 PWDs, and protective assistance for shelters exposed to natural and man-made hazards. Ensuring that men, women, boys, and girls, as well as PWDs are involved in analysis of needs, design, and carrying out of project activities, guarantee the representation of all AGD groups and effective programming. Female social team staff will conduct individual interviews and FGDs with women to analyze their needs. Next, technical assessment will be conducted prior to the intervention to design appropriate technical interventions that match the needs highlighted, specifying the materials. When donors' regulations allow, rehabilitation/upgrading will be completed by the beneficiaries themselves, who will be in charge of the implementation of the activities where individual MOU will be signed with affected people. Rehabilitation of the shelters will ensure adequate ventilation, reduction of water leaks, enhanced hygiene conditions, improved privacy and safety, as well as greater resilience to climate change while in full compliance of the shelter cluster’s standards. The shelters will be given suitable flooring, plastering and painting, enabling proper and respectable access for everyone, including PWD. When necessary, the renovation work will also include WASH infrastructure in the shelters to improve health and water conditions, sanitation, and hygiene management, as well as electrical infrastructure, which includes electrical cables inside the walls, and lights to ensure the availability of minimal luminescent. The project will provide technical support and supervision on the shelter upgrading through weekly field visits, detailed BoQs and drawings in Arabic and English languages, conducted by WW-GVC and PHC. The project team will monitor the work on a weekly basis, to ensure full technical support and to ensure progress is in line with each agreement and specifications. The project will also ensure constant coordination with the Shelter Cluster and relevant stakeholders, Governor's offices (based on signed coordination MOU), village councils, OCHA and legal aid providers. The specific approach selected for each community will depend on several factors (primarily the land ownership status) and will include: • Rehabilitation of concrete shelters with composite roof, which are comprised of a steel frame covered with insulated metal sheets and include a wooden wall framework treated with fireproof material; • Rehabilitation of concrete / stone shelters with asphalted roof and internal renovation works; • Provision of PVC plastic covers (supporting the structure of metal bars that the community members already own); • Internal caves rehabilitation. All the above-mentioned activities correspond to the standard HRP activity: “rehabilitation of damaged and/or substandard shelters (fully or partially) to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards, including adaptation of shelters to meet the needs of PWDs and improving the living space for vulnerable groups, in addition to shelter related support to people at higher risk of Covid-19” Full rehabilitation cost per shelter will vary according to its location and type of rehabilitation; The cost varies between shelters based on the conducted assessments in average amount of $7,500.0 for EJ, and $7,000 up to ceiling of $8,500 in area C. Female staff working for WWGVC and PHC will ensure the participation of women in communities by conducting capacity building with community members on self-implemented shelter renovation and techniques to improve shelter conditions, including protection from natural hazards and extreme weather events, and to ensure privacy and safety. WW-GVC and PHC will target communities who suffer from inadequate and overcrowded shelters with activities and sessions that contribute to self-recovery, to mitigate risk of infection of COVID-19 among family members, to reduce the risk of GBV and other protection-related risk, and to increase the awareness among the communities about privacy inside shelters for all children, women and men, PWD minimum living standards, with the capacity building trainings that will be done by WWGVC and PHC social teams based on RCCE materials. Additionally, WW-GVC and PHC will provide awareness sessions to HHs on the safe complaint-response mechanism (CRM) built into the project, and how to safely access the different channels and tools such as the hotline, satisfactory surveys and in-person field visits, and what is expected by WW-GVC and PHC staff in accordance to international standards. This will include awareness sessions with supporting materials on PSEA and related safe complaint channels available, in accordance with the oPt PSEA SOPs on Inter Agency Referral and HCT oPt PSEA Network. First aid trainings and kits were provided to the targeted communities under previous projects with the support of Palestinian Civil defense, new first aid kits will be provided to all HHs under risk of settler violence, crawling insects or any other risks during the coming projects as well. COVID-19 protection measures (wearing face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WWGVC and PHC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

3 - Indicators

The aim of the proposed intervention is to address the “humanitarian and protection needs of the communities” through provision of the required assistance to targeted 211 households in restoring their shelters, and improving their living and health conditions with the aim of upholding their feeling of safety and dignity. The proposed interventions will be assessed through the following output indicators: I) 100% of assisted HH living in upgraded unit in accordance with minimum standardsII) 100% of households protected and have enhanced their coping capacities As part of an efficient monitoring system, specific assessments are designed to track the progress while 2considering the impact and inclusion of all AGD groups such as women, children, elders and PWDs. The expected results will be measured through the following indicators under the Strategic Objective 2: Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities: I. # of individuals protected and have improved access to adequate shelter I.II. # of accessible shelters adapted to persons with disabilities. II.III. # of HHs have repaired or upgraded to the shelter minimum standard. The following technical criteria will be employed in order to evaluate indicators’ achievement: • Technical assessment after completion of the intervention. (This will be done through the conduction of an overall evaluation, including quantitative data collection with Pre & Post activity surveys) • Qualitative analysis through FGDs with women/girls and men/boys focusing on Displacement / Seasonal displacements / Family Separation / Safety / Dignity and Integrity • Individual interviews planned with PWDs and their care givers • The Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) through concrete community engagement in decision making related to intervention’s design and direct implementation, which increases the ownership of the proposed activity by the community The action responds to the needs expressed by the communities and aims to look beyond the mere material needs by focusing also on the broader issues of personal safety, dignity and protection. The new shelters will ensure: an improvement of the privacy for all family members, especially women and girls; good ventilation, assuring enough lighting and subsequent improved health conditions and better resistance to COVID-19 and other diseases for all family members especially children and elders during cold and hot seasons; increased security and safety for all family members, especially for women and girls who will be able to live their daily lives in a safe place. The proposed shelter interventions will significantly address the protection risk of displacement for the most vulnerable Palestinians, especially women and children. Reduced seasonal displacement will provide protection for vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities and women-headed households). Improved shelter standards will enhance vulnerable families’ financial, health and social situation and will allow for pregnant women to spend more time at home before and after their delivery, decreasing separation from their families and community. The separation of spaces will boost the sense of privacy and dignity for girls, boys, women and men.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Following a multi-year experience in the technical assessment for preparing technical BOQ and drawings, there are always main assumptions related to the total cost of shelter rehabilitation. However, for each technical BOQ for shelter rehabilitation there is a division into two parts which are the cost for construction material and labor cost. Usually, the construction materials forms about 60%, while the skilled labor costs form 40% of the total cost for each shelter.Rehabilitation works in PHC’s targeted communities will be by providing 100% conditional cash for beneficiaries.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

For both WW-GVC and PHC, core work is implemented through a participatory approach. Well-designed and well-managed mechanisms for handling beneficiary’s complaints, feedbacks and allegations are of utmost importance for an inclusive, reliable and properly informed decision process. WW-GVC through its Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and PHC through Accountability Framework to Beneficiaries commit to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneficiaries. The aim is to establish a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions and inquiries in a safe and inclusive way, and receive a response in a timely manner. All complaints are reported to the Complaints focal point, a person not involved in field activities implementation to guarantee confidentiality, anonymity and DO NO HARM principle. Upon registration of the data on a secure database, the focal point analyses complaints and classiﬁes each of them according to the internal sensitivity classiﬁcation system for internal and external follow-up. Additionally, through the Safeguarding focal point and relative procedures, the system in place to collect feedback and complaints also incorporate elements for a safe disclosure and proper Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). All inputs will be collected and analysed in order to incorporate them at operations and programmatic level and better inform decision making. In case previous consent was provided, complainants will be contacted by WW-GVC’s or PHC’s focal point to acknowledge the feedback, complaint or allegation, consulting on the possible next steps, and transparently informing on the timeline and how the inputs received have been taken into account by the organizations.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

WeWorld-GVC and PHC work with communities in a way which endeavors to minimize exposure to additional hazards and mitigate as many risks as possible. Needs, risks and capacities of community members are balanced through the Community Protection Approach (CPA) where the community is involved and meaningfully included in a thorough assessment to understand the vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the programming reflects preferences, needs and resources expressed by the members of the HHs in a participative way. The eﬃciency of this participatory approach lies in encouraging the involvement of all local actors and the community engagement throughout all phases of the suggested intervention, placing extra focus on preventing unintended negative impacts. Through this inclusive and comparative risk analysis, organizations increase the accountability creating the conditions to quickly respond to any unintentional negative impacts created by the intervention. The proposal elaborates on a remedial action to hazards and threats that caused a deterioration in shelter and protection needs of the beneficiaries. Response is carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risk, at the same time upholding their dignity throughout the process. Activities are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PWDs. Additionally, interventions in the shelters will be designed to comply with protection principles and AGD groups inclusion, such as meaningful access and accountability towards beneficiaries, taking into account the diverse needs, roles and dynamics based on gender, age and disability status, in each community. Furthermore, household members will also work in their shelter rehabilitation, contributing to the improvement of the sense of dignity and ownership. The field team participated in the protection cluster GBV training to improve their capacities and knowledge of safe disclosure and referral of sensitive cases. Moreover, the field team also participated in a training with War Child to safely detect and refer children violence. Complying with the “Do No Harm” principle, communication between the PA’s legal counterparts and NRC ensures that legal awareness sessions are coordinated to strengthen the target communities’ knowledge of their rights, risks, and procedures to be followed. Additionally, WWGVC is mainstreaming the AGD and PWD toolkits in the community-based protection methodology.COVID-19 protection measures (face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distancing) will be followed by WWGVC and PHC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Each project begins with the holding of public meetings in every targeted community, announcement about the public meeting is distributed in all public places as schools, shops and mosques, this is to assure that all community members are aware about the project with any bias to specific groups. Impartiality principle is carried throughout the action on the basis of needs, exclusively, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress and making no distinction on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class or political opinion. Impartiality principle will be recognized throughout the actions' entire phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring The CPA was developed according to IHL, Humanitarian, Do No Harm and the following fundamental principles: 1. the safety of affected population (both of groups and individuals); 2. restore the dignity of affected population putting a stop to emerging or established patterns of violations 3. place the empowerment of communities at risk at the core of any action to enable self-protection and realize their rights. The use of focus groups, random interviews and transect walks, ensures the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, a particular household. Throughout assessment and planning; the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed shelter and protection intervention responds to the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Giving everyone in the community a voice promotes community empowerment, protects the dignity of each beneficiary, and preserves individual dignity.
