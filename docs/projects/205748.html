Project Summary

South Sudan Education sector faces significant challenges that originated in years of conflict, displacement, lack of infrastructure, teachers and investments. More than 2 million children need assistance. PAH is an experienced organization with broad portfolio of successfully implemented projects in various sectors. PAH will use its expertise in education, protection, WASH and other sectors, team of specialists and well-established cooperation with various stakeholders to further provide assistance to vulnerable communities across the country.
Recognizing the vast needs in Education across South Sudan, PAH will target mainly areas of highest needs, basing on the regularly updated cluster priority lists. Secondly PAH will focus on communities affected by conflicts and floods displacements or returnee areas, with high percentage of displaced members that over-stretch the community resources, which may lead to nonfunctional schools, drop outs and negative coping strategies (inc. child labour and marriage).
PAH plans to ensure access to WASH facilities. As mentioned in the donors report (UN OCHA SSHF SSD-19/HSS10/SA2/E/INGO/13446) PAH provides high standard facility, namely latrines and hand washing models.
Thus, basing on the needs, PAH may implement broad variety of activities:
• Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces, construction of temporary learning spaces (TLS) and provision of educational equipment and other materials
• Establishment of child/youth-friendly spaces: organizing recreational activities, establishing sports activities for children in school; training teachers on coaching sports activities.
• Provision of psycho-social support and encouraging learning for the pre-school age children through provision of ECD materials.
• Establishment of youth-friendly spaces, and recreational activities for adolescents and youth.
• Establishment or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, including latrines (gender-segregated), water points and soap provision for hand-washing, and provision of dry chlorine.
• Health and hygiene promotion for children, youth and care-givers, hygiene messaging on cholera and other water born and water transmitted diseases symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention by school-based cholera response teams (PTAs/SMCs, teachers and adolescents/youth); menstrual hygiene education and menstrual hygiene kits provision
• Teacher professional development including training on Education in Emergencies-EiE, training of PTA and SMCs on school management and other cross-cutting issues
• Creating a school environment clubs to maintain the school environment through the planting of trees and vegetables. This will empower students to promote good environmental behavior in the schools and the community.
• MHM education and menstrual hygiene kits provision
• Psycho-social support and peaceful coexistence promotion activities in schools, learning spaces and friendly spaces.
PAH has designed and construct flood resilient WASH facilities, including institutional level latrines. These will be implemented in the flood prone locations where feasible.
As PAH implements multi – sector responses we provide access to multiple services for the beneficiaries. In case of the needs children and youth will be covered with Protection ( mainly AoR Child Protection) activities. Provision of WASH infrastructure will be implemented in the close cooperation with PAH WASH unit. For the sectors of Health and Nutrition PAH will closely cooperate with other partners.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.

1. Needs

According to 2024 Intercluster Severity Scales for Humanitarian Needs Overview 26 out of 78 counties was classified as extreme. 5 couties were clasified as serevity 4 level in terms of Education needs (Lafon, Pochalla, Cueibet, Aweil East and Pariang). Another 68 are level 3. Since April 2023 there has been an ongoing migration of the refugees and returnees from Sudan, stretching the already underfunded response in the country. At the same time the Education sector is the most underfunded in South Sudan (Financial Tracking Service).Many children never came back to school after COVID-19 period. Distance to schools, security incidents, population displacement, killings of teachers and scarce financial resources were limiting access for children to schools. Climate change, food insecurity and localized conflict are further exacerbating this barriers. Girls face additional challenges, including harmful social norms, lack of MHM kits and sanitation facilities, among others. It is estimated that 2.8 million children is out of school, with insecurity indicated as a major reason. Menstruating girls were said to have high drop-out or low attendance rates in school as they missed lessons while menstruating and found it hard to catch up. Many of the drop-out girls may never go back to school due to teenage pregnancy or early marriage. Boys on the other hand, often drop out of schools due to the risk of revenge killings and engage in child labour, including recruitment by armed groups and community-based militias. Additionally, lack of- or inadequate infrastructure, costs of accessing educations as well as lack of trained personnel are hampering the access the education. The other main challenge is lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout further deepening gender inequalities as girls are more severely affected. Hunger is a key barrier to education, resulting in poor academic performance, inconsistent attendance and eventual dropout. Most learners do not get a meal before school and over 60 % of schools assessed in the Education Needs Assessment do not provide food for learners. Lack of and delayed teacher salaries and incentives result in teacher apathy, high turnover and poor-quality teaching. Teachers’ salaries in South Sudan have not been adjusted for inflation. Children with disabilities are among the most affected as over half of schools report their latrines are inaccessible for children with disabilities. Teachers are not trained to foster or deliver inclusive education and school communities are not sensitized on the need to create enabling environments for children with disabilities. The number of child protection cases raised significantly during the schools closure related to COVID-19, with the high rise in learner pregnancy. Schools play a key role in identification of child protection cases, however, referral mechanisms in over 30% of schools are not functional. There is a strong need for integration of the two sectoral interventions.

2. Project activities

● Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces. Repair and/or construction of school fences, roofs, doors and/or windows locks. ● Construction and/or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLS) ● Establishment or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, including latrines (gender-segregated) using the Sani Tweak approach of consult, modify and consult approach, water points and soap provision for hand-washing, and provision of dry chlorine. ● Distribution of hygiene materials e.g. soap, sanitisers, masks for students and teachers. ● Health and hygiene promotion for children, youth and care-givers ● Chlorination, hygiene messaging on Covid-19, cholera symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention by school-based cholera response teams (PTAs/SMCs, teachers and adolescents/youth). ● Creating school hygiene clubs to support messaging, provide awareness on cleaning and safe use of latrines. ● Provision of furniture, equipment, textbooks, learning and teaching materials ● Provision of school materials support ● Organizing recreational activities to emergency-affected children, establishing sports activities for children in school; training teachers on coaching sports activities. ● Establishment of child/youth-friendly spaces, and recreational activities for adolescents and youth. ● Providing ECD materials for schools, ● Teacher professional development including training on Education in Emergencies-EiE (child-centred approaches to pedagogy, classroom management, lesson planning and cross-cutting issues), child well-being (physical protection and PSS) and life skills. This will also include in-classroom coaching. ● Training of PTA and SMCs on school management and other cross-cutting issues including protection issues, psycho-social support, the inclusion of children with disabilities. ● Menstrual hygiene education and menstrual hygiene kits provision, aiming to increase girls’ attendance. Educational sessions on menstrual hygiene target both boys and girls to raise awareness and limit the stigma. ● Creating a school environment clubs to maintain the school environment through the planting of trees and vegetables. This will empower students to promote good environmental behavior in the schools and the community. ● As a part of PAH's safety audits team will conduct analysis for Children with Disabilities to guide the activities and ensure inclusiveness for both infrastructure classrooms and sanitation facilities ● PAH recognizes the cross-cutting nature of Education and integrates its activities with other sectors and partners, including protection (including Child Protection and Children with Disability, FSL, nutrition and WASH. ● PAH will target Returnees, IDPs, Host Community and Refugees. The proportion will be dictated by the location of the project and priorities of Education Cluster. From the targeted population PAH will aim to reach at least 30% of Refugees and Returnees.
