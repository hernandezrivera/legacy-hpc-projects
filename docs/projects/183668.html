Project Summary

Strengthening the WASH and health services and preparedness of Health Care Facilities (HCFs) and improving under-served communities' access to adequate water and sanitation services, are considered extremely urgent needs in Gaza Strip, supporting resilience building and facing the protracted threats and chronic vulnerability. This project is aligned with the HRP Strategic Objectives 1 and 2, complementing WASH cluster objectives 1 and 2, and Health Cluster objective 2, as well as contributing to the achievement of SDG 6. The project also responds to UNICEF strategy 2016-2030, for which “every HCF should have safely managed reliable water, sanitation, hygiene facilities, and practices that meet staff and patient needs by 2030”. The project is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) and MOH (2021-25), implementing the National WASH standards for health facilities (UNICEF/WWGVC 2021). Improved WASH services in HCFs are considered as necessary inputs to be prioritized for achieving all global and national health goals, especially those linked to Universal Health Coverage (SDG 3), and the specific target 3.9 “to reduce the burden of disease from unsafe water, sanitation, and lack of hygiene” and 3.1 and 3.2 “call for reducing maternal mortality and under-five and neonatal mortality”. Effective sanitation, hygiene, and infection prevention and control (IPC) measures are one of the five objectives of the WHO Global Action Plan on antimicrobial resistance. WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC), in partnership with Qatar Red Crescent Society (QRCS), aims to enhance the resilience, protection, and dignity of the most vulnerable people in GS. The general objective of the project is to increase the efficiency, predictability, and availability, and accessibility of WASH and health services in health institutions and communities, contributing to mitigating the impact of COVID-19 and other infectious diseases. The needs and interventions were coordinated with and endorsed by the Ministry of Health (MoH), WASH and Health Cluster, Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), and the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA). The first specific objective of the proposed intervention is to enhance the access to WASH and Health quality services for Al Nasser medical compound in Gaza City, implementing sustainable improvement in the WASH infrastructure (construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in HCFs - handwashing, latrines, sewer system) and in the quality of health services (hygiene material, medical and protective equipment supply, monitoring tools) for three HCFs: 1) Al Nasser pediatric hospital (NPH), 2) Rantissi specialized hospital (RSH) 3) Ophthalmic hospital (OH), benefitting an average 280,000 people served by the three hospitals annually (data: health cluster HeRAMS Dashboard), specifically 53,000 girls, 55,000 boys, 85,000 women, and 87,000 men including 12320 elders, including 2800 PWD, as well as contributing to improving the public health conditions of more than 652,597 living in the target area. Therefore, the second specific objective of the proposed intervention is to ensure access to adequate water and sanitation services at HouseHold (HHs) level in Middle Area Governorate, particularly targeting 322 unconnected households (HHs) of two unserved communities, Al’Ansar (Al Musaddarc locality) and Block 8 (Al Buriej locality), through the installation/ rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks, and HH connections, and the installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connections, connecting 1,535 vulnerable individuals (392 boys, 374 girls, 359 men, 362 women, 21 elders men, and 27 elders women, including 15 PWD).

1 - Needs

Over 13 years of Israel’s blockade, recurring flare-ups in violence and escalation in hostilities, and the persisting COVID-19 pandemic, weakened the already compromised and economically fragile healthcare and WASH services system, triggering the key drivers for a humanitarian WASH and health intervention in the Gaza Strip. According to WASH in Health assessment led by the WASH cluster in August 2021, at least 17 HCFs in Gaza suffer from inadequate or unimproved WASH facilities (primarily toilets, drinking, and handwashing stations) to meet the standards set up by the MoH. The consequences are overcrowding in the existing facilities, undermining hygiene practices, all of which raise particular concerns in light of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. In September 2021 WW-GVC and QRCS conducted a series of key informant interviews (KIIs) with personnel at MoH identifying the WASH needs and Al Nasser medical compound, as one the most WASH vulnerable HCFs out of the 17 HCFs acknowledged through the Cluster assessment. Al Nasser medical compound consists of three hospitals (Al Nasser pediatric hospital (NPH), Rantissi specialized hospital (RSH), and Ophthalmic hospital (OH). NPH and RSH hospitals are the main pediatric clinics providing general and specialized services for Gaza Governorate as well as a referral hospital from North Gaza and the middle area, serving an average of 108,000 patients (53,000 girls, 55,000 boys) annually, including Neonatal Intensive care unit (NICU), Pediatric ICU, hemodialysis and the only pediatric oncology center in the GS. OH, is the only specialized hospital serving an average of 132,000 cases annually (64,920 women and 67,080 men mainly chronic patients with non-communicable diseases). Adequate WASH services in the three hospitals are fundamental to reduce the preventable causes of morbidity and mortality by minimizing the risks of healthcare-associated infection, especially the COVID-19, as well as improve staff satisfaction, patient care-seeking behavior, especially for immunocompromised patients. Concerning the access to the WASH services at HHs/community level, in GS persists major difficulties in upgrading and maintaining water and sanitation facilities, due to limited financial capacity and the constraints on importing materials given the Israeli restrictions. (WASH HNO 2022). Additionally, 290 WASH infrastructures in the GS have been affected during the May 2021 escalation, restricting access to water and sanitation services for more than 1.2 million Palestinians. Since then, WASH service providers are still struggling to fully restore and maintain the optimal functionality of WASH facilities (WASH Cluster, Gaza WASH sector damage assessment 2021). Regarding access to sanitation services, nearly 290,000 HHs in the oPt are not connected to any sewer system, and in GS almost 1,356 HHs are discharging their sewage water in adjacent streams and open areas, creating health and environmental hazards to downstream communities (MSNA 2021). In close coordination with CMWU and PWA and based on the WASH cluster vulnerability mapping updated in August 2021, WW-GVC identified two under-served communities in Deir al Balah governorate, Al’Ansar (Al Musaddarc locality) and Block 8 (Al Buriej locality), that have no access to sustainable piped water and sanitation services, being disconnected to water and sewage networks. The lack of services is currently affecting 322 HHs and 1,535 vulnerable individuals (392 boys, 374 girls, 359 men, 362 women, 21 elders male, and 27 elders females including 15 PWD), with a negative impact especially on children and women, triggering personal hygiene and health-related risk. Overall, the priority of Al Nasser medical compound that needs to be addressed, taking into consideration the special needs of staff, patients, and companions, mainly women, children, and PWDs are the following: (1) unsafe drinking and domestic water, due to poor quality (high salinity), which is eroding the old water infrastructure and damaging the desalination plants, besides the unavailability of regular water quality monitoring system, and lack of water connection for the central firefighting system in RSH, which jeopardizes the safety of staff and patients. This was evident in July 2019, when the fire suddenly broke into the adjacent incinerator unit; (2) lack of separated and sufficient toilets for staff and patients, women and men, as well as unavailability of adapted toilets for PWDs and children in any of the 3 hospitals; (3) old wastewater networks in NPH, and OH are causing frequent flooding and clogging in the system, in addition, the lack of proper stormwater drainage systems in the yard of the NPH; (4) poor hygiene and environmental cleaning infrastructure in the OH and NPH especially in inpatient departments, daily care departments, outpatient departments with a lack of designated patient waiting for areas; (5) lack of safe and protected designated areas for medical waste collection and storage, in any of the three hospitals, causing high public health concern especially for hospitals’ neighborhoods. Furthermore, the priority of the identified two communities Al’ Ansar (Al Musaddarc locality) and Block 8 (Al Buriej locality) regards the absence of functionals and adequate sewage and water networks. Consequently, communities’ members suffer from (1) lack of sufficient quantities of water for drinking and domestic purposes, without reaching the minimum Humanitarian Benchmarks of 60 l/c/d; (2) augmented risks related to poor personal hygiene and scarce environmental cleaning (3) exposure to unprotected, unsafe and, unhealthy environment prone to waterborne diseases; (4) higher risk of displacement; (5) delays, constraints, and difficulties of movement especially for children due to the flooding of wastewater (5) increased burdens and workload for women in charge of fetching water, struggling on performing the socially imposed role and domestic tasks.

2 - Activities

Proper coordination was performed with PWA and MOH, Local Authorities, WASH and Health Clusters, and major stakeholders active in the WASH and Health sector on the target communities to avoid overlapping. At the start of the project, WW-GVC and QRCS will carry out a detailed needs assessment, to be conducted in cooperation with MoH to prioritize and confirm needs and specifications. The proposed intervention will be delivered through different response modalities, encompassing primarily service delivery modality (69%) concerning activities 1.1, 2.1, 2.2, and 3.1. Moreover, the in-kind modality (31%) will be adopted for activity 1.2. WW-GVC’s will perform the WASH in health intervention targeting Al Nasser medical compound (three HCFs) through: Activity 1: Access to WASH and Health services in institutions Activity 1.1: The construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in HCFs (handwashing, latrines, sewer system). A1.1.1 - upgrade water systems services in NPH, OH, and RSH, including rehabilitation of three water wells (installation super duplex vertical pumps); rehabilitation of the main water networks with internal separation; and installation of one new desalination plant with a capacity of 60 m3/day in NPH, taking into consideration international and national standards for brine water management. The intervention will include upgrading of the existing hot water systems and reinstallation of water connections for the central firefighting system in RSH, in addition to firewater pump and rehabilitation of the groundwater tank, and the rehabilitation of the stormwater drainage system inside yards through readjustment of slopes and installation of galleys. A.1.1.2 rehabilitation of sanitation services in the three HCFs, including maintenance of 20 already existing toilets. All the works will take into consideration the gender needs and separation, equipping of toilets to meet the needs, women, staff men and women, patients, and companions as well as the special needs of PWDs and children, decrease the risk of GBV cases and ensuring safety and dignity to all. A1.1.3 - Rehabilitation/construction of 30 new hand washing basins and/or alcohol-based hand rub wall-mounted dispensers. QRCS will be responsible for implementing interventions to address Health gaps Al Nasser medical compound in medical items supply, personal hygiene and protection, environmental cleaning, and medical waste management, IPC control. Activity 1.2 - The provision of environmental and personal cleaning kits in HCFs, medical items supply, and IPC control. A1.2.1 - Construction of three safe and protected medical waste collection and storage areas, one for each hospital. A1.2.2 - The provision of 703 environmental and personal cleaning kits in HCFs including personal protective equipment (PPE), and supplying 600 cover mattresses and 600 pillows. A1.2.3 - provision of patient beds, companion´s resting chairs, essential supplies, and equipment such as safety box, detergents, and disinfectants to maintain the IPC standard.A1.2.4 - contribute to establish/develop a regular-based ME/compliance system through assigning and training focal persons in each hospital concerning IPC measures and break the transition of COVID-19 based on national standards guidelines and WASH COVID-19 Response Guidance. Moreover, awareness-raising inside HCFs will be performed including hosting an event for the 5th of May (the world hand hygiene day) targeting health and non-health staff. In addition, the advocacy, intervention will be including the development of the necessary Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials and a video to document all phases of the project from the design to the final stage.Moreover, WW-GVC will be responsible for securing access to sustainable water and sanitation services for 322 unconnected HHs in Deir al Balah governorate, 165 HHs in Block 8, and 157 HHs in Al’Ansar communities both are located respectively in Al Musaddarc and Al Buriej localities. Activity 2: Improving access to WASH service at HHs and community level. A2.1 - The installation/ rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks, and HH connections implemented through supply and install water UPVC 110mm, 0.85 Km length to extend the main collector to the existing network in Block 8 community. Likewise, for Al ‘Ansar but 2.5 Km length. A2.2 - The installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connections, will be implemented through supply and install sewage UPVC 355mm, 0.85 Km length to extend the main collector to the existing network in Block 8 community. Likewise, for Al ‘Ansar community but UPVC 110mm, 2.5 Km length. Activity 3: Developing awareness products on access restrictions to WASH (Advocacy). A3.1 - Development of 5 awareness products on access restrictions to WASH, specifically, WW-GVC and QRCS, will elaborate 1 sectorial analysis report, 2 videos, and 1 infographic, with a focus on WASH in health and IPC control measures. The advocacy action will also illustrate the Israeli restrictions on equipment and materials designated as ‘dual-use items’, and the poor governance of the WASH sector due to the limited technical and financial capacities of the WASH service providers.

The proposed intervention will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) framework. WW-GVC and QRCS will develop M&E plan at the beginning of the project to track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. Taking into consideration gender-sensitive quantitative data analysis, act as the core part of the project’s M&E system to track changes and progress of technical implementation, and beneficiaries’ (women, men, girls, boys, elderly, with and without disabilities) acceptance, knowledge and practices. Data collected will be gender-sensitive and disaggregated by sex and age for both qualitative and quantitative using different tools such as questionnaires, KII, stories of change, and focus group discussions (FGDs). 9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

All feedback and complaints coming from beneficiaries will be given high consideration. The WW-GVC and QRCS will jointly scrutiny the content of the complaints and/or feedback and if necessary, will further investigate the matter to assess whether an adjustment to the programming or if disciplinary actions are required. Accordingly, the Partners commit to addressing the complaints and feedback within a reasonable timeframe.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,c - Complaint box,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit,f - Other

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

WW-GVC has a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries and health workers and their communities/institutions, throughout the duration, and up to 6 months after the end of the project. This is part of complement WW-GVC’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability, and commitment to guarantee transparency, fairness, and equity towards all our beneficiaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions, and inquiries in a secured way, and receives a response promptly. All complaints/feedback are reported to the WW-GVC’s Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff, project managers, and/or focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to its sensitivity. If the complaint requires follow-up, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call from WW-GVC’s Monitoring and Coordination manager. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the monitoring and coordination manager analyses complaints and categorize each of them according to the internal classification system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by the WW-GVC CRM team.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Accessibility to all population groups is maintained through the whole project life-cycle. Every member of the field staff is obliged to inform and explain all direct and indirect beneficiaries of the presence of a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) and the referral pathway for their complaints, as well as provide the contact information/hotline for the CRM focal points depending on the type of the complaint (female focal point for GBV and PSEA; a male focal point for other types of complaints). Beneficiaries are also informed that the complaints are to be kept confidential specifically for sensitive issues, and they will be dealt with accordingly. The complaints can be verbal by the dedicated project hotline, by email, or by writing, and all complaints are documented in the CRM template by the focal point, dealing with each complaint based on its level of sensitivity. All complaints are tracked and documented from the first day till closing. and the beneficiary should be informed once it is closed officially.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

WeWorld-GVC and QRCS provide assistance adopting standards and methodologies that endeavor to minimize exposure to additional hazards and mitigate as many risks as possible. Needs and risks are balanced through the involvement of the communities and institutions, in a thorough assessment to understand the vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the programming reflects preferences and needs expressed by the members of the communities in a participative way. The eﬃciency is in encouraging all local actors and community involvement on the overall steps of the suggested intervention and prevent unintended negative impacts. Through this, inclusive and comparative risk analysis organizations increase the accountability allowing themselves to quickly respond to any unintentional negative impacts created by the intervention. The proposal elaborates a remedial action to hazards that compromise an already exhausted and economically fragile healthcare and WASH services system. This has an impact on the preventable causes of morbidity and mortality as well as the wellbeing of the affected population. WW-GVC and QRCS aim to reduce the vulnerability of the beneficiaries caused by the limited access to healthcare and WASH facilities, creating increased personal hygiene health risks, especially for women and children. The response is carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risks. Activities are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the communities, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women, and men of all ages including PWD. Additionally, interventions in the WASH facilities in HCFs will be designed adopting a gender-sensitive approach, to comply with protection principles, such as meaningful access and accountability towards beneficiaries, taking into account the diverse needs, roles, and dynamics of women and men, age, and disability status. Specific attention will be also given to the lessons learned coming from previous similar projects of both partners. The action takes also into account the indications provided by WW-GVC “Gender and WASH toolkit for Palestine”, which provides applicable and feasible suggestions on how to mainstream gender in WASH projects in the Palestinian context. Moreover, WeWorld-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint mechanism system, allowing beneficiaries and affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will then -if needed- influence management decisions and concrete implementation. Safety and dignity have been taken into consideration in the design of tailored activities to address the different needs and risks that girls, women, boys, and men are currently facing. COVID-19 protection measures (wearing face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WW-GVC and QRCS teams during all project activities to prevent the spread of infectious diseases.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposal is following the sustainable humanitarian approach, aiming to provide immediate relief, ensuring equity, sustainability, and impartial access to improved WASH and Health services for the most vulnerable communities and institutions of Gaza. The project aims to increase the efficiency, predictability, and availability of the WASH and health services and has been designed considering the direct and indirect impact on women, girls, boys, men with or without disabilities. Impartiality principle is carried throughout the action on the basis of needs, exclusively, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress and making no distinction. The impartiality principle will be recognized throughout the actions' entire phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring. This is illustrated by the fact that assessments have been conducted through the same tools and methods in every household and facility targeted, collecting the data directly from the duty-bearers as a way of accountability and transparency. During the design phase, several HHs and HCFs have been assessed by WeWorld-GVC and QRCS staff in coordination with the Ministry of Health, the Palestinian Water Authority CMWU and the OCHA WASH and Health Clusters, to identify the different needs, potential activities, and beneficiaries. Multi-sectoral needs are identified and ranked on the basis of the contextual WASH and Health risks of communities and individuals. The use of focus groups and informed interviews ensure the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession, and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, a specific situation, a particular household. Throughout the assessment and planning phases, the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed WASH and health intervention addresses the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each member of the community, and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, preserves the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowerment. The dissemination of information about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedbacks and complaints about the implemented project. Continuous coordination will be kept with these institutions and related stakeholders to update the collected data and to respond to the most urgent WASH and Health needs before designing and delivery of any response.
