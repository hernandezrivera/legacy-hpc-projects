Project Summary

According to the latest FSNAU/FEWSNET projections, approximately 4 million people remain in food security stress with 2.5 million people estimated to be in IPC 3 (crisis) and 78,000 in IPC 4 (emergency) levels. Somalia is an extremely fragile country and people's ability to cope with any additional shock is restrained. Malnutrition in Somalia has deteriorated with around 1.4 million children suffering from GAM, of which 1 008,500 have SAM. Somalia continues to have one of the worst infant and young child feeding and micro-nutrient indicators in the world. In addition to the 2.6 million protracted IDPs have been displaced due to the drought and crisis. IDPs represent three quarters of the people in acute livelihood and food crisis in the country. Moreover, forced evictions of IDPs and urban poor continue to be a major problem in Somalia's urban areas. According to the Health Cluster, since the start of 2019, a total of 68,000 cholera cases and 4,200 deaths (Case Fatality Rate 0.5 per cent) were reported in Somalia. According to health cluster 6,800 suspected cases of Measles were also reported, with 67 percent of these being children under five years of age. The health cluster Bulletin by WHO for September 2019, also indicate that there were outbreaks of Measles cases which remained at epidemic levels with a total of 9,200 cases seen between January to September 2019. 65% of these cases are in the target regions increasing morbidity and mortality as well as malnutrition overwhelming the already precarious health care system. There are multiple contributory causes to the unacceptably high levels of neonatal, infant and child mortality, the most significant of which are, neonatal issues, acute respiratory illnesses, diarrhea, vaccine preventable diseases and malaria. The high maternal mortality ratio relates to the fertility rate for women, which is one of the highest in the world. The average fertility rate is 6.6 children per woman, with unmet
The project aims to provide life-saving emergency primary health care to 15,000 drought affected IDPs and host communities in Galgadud and Mudug region through static and mobile clinics as well as provision of maternal and neonatal and child healthcare, immunization to the target communities. The primary health care center will provide ante-natal and post-natal care, skilled delivery birth attendants and services, micro nutrient supplementation, nutrition screening of children U5 years, immunization, treatment of common diseases, and health education. Capacity building of local health staffs will be strengthened in order to provide quality health care to the community. The Maternal Child Health (MCH) will be operational for all days due to the facility delivery needs and emergency referral. Project will focus on provision of both preventive and curative health services at primary level integrated with nutrition activities that are undertaken by DEH organization. Building the capacity of health workers through training on Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmONC) will be done to equip health workers with the required skills to deliver quality health care services to the affected population. Referral of malnourished children with complicated cases will be done to the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) centres managed by DEH. It shall also strive to coordinate with Health, WASH, and Nutrition clusters to coordinate response activities for maximum impact to beneficiaries. DEH through this project will provide life-saving medical consultations and drugs to the health facility, establish referral mechanisms between DEH facilities and secondary healthcare providers in Mudug and Galgadud region, improve the coverage of measles vaccinations and Vitamin A; and support safe motherhood and reproductive health while also ensuring readiness to prevent and respond to outbreaks such as malaria, measles or Acute Watery Diarrhea AWD/Cholera and promote health update through health education

Mitigation Measures

The project areas have high number of mixed pastoral and IDPs as well as scattered rural areas in Ceelbur and xaradhere as well as coastal DEH with different livelihood status, due the diverse in the nature of the target beneficiaries the rural communities displaced might not welcome positively to the IDPs and conflict between the previous establish IDPs managers and newly displaced might conflict hence creating community tension, DEH will ensure to provide better coordination and information sharing when such eventuality hits the target community, also on the employment issues there might be discrimination or disparities between the women and men and to address this DEH will ensure to competitively sources the local community with expertise for employment considering women in equal measures this will then reduce the risk of gender disparities and minimized community expectation.Location, distance and access routes to the OTP sites and services might be a challenge of preventing factors limiting the access of the OTP sites DEH will enable its beneficiaries have accessible to all of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women DEH will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, OTP mobile/outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities as well as the IDPs and host communities, limited information and communication might hinder the understanding between DEH teams in the field and community leaders, however, to mitigate any such eventuality DEH will create feedback mechanism and reasonable compliance officer within its office as well as hotline mobile services to ensure all beneficiary complains are addressed timely.Project staff will be trained on the basic human right issues and approaches to enable them understand the principle of equity and ensure those fleeing communities were well attended, the project will consult the communities where treatment centers will be place in avoidance of near military settlement as well as to avoid the risk associate with landmines and explosive targets. DEH will ensure all the structure within the mobile clinic sites are user friendly to disable and marginalized and minority children boys, girls and pregnant and lactating women.

Please provide further explanation

Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services, where priority will be given to the delivery of equitable access to basic emergency health care services, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of health centers attacks, to increase community uptake of provided services, community level demand creation will be strengthened. DEH will strengthen the community capacity through training of active community health workers who will in turn provide the required services for referral and treatment as well as information sharing within the community hence creating ownership and sustainability of the program even after the end of the donor funds.
