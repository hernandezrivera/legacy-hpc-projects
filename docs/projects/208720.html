Project Summary

This intervention aims to increase access to safe energy for cooking to vulnerable host communities in Geidam, Bade, Mubi North, and Michika LGAs of Yobe and Adamawa States. The project is targeting 1,882 HHs of which 1,129 for Yobe State and 753 in Adamawa State.
The intervention will create awareness of climate change, its causes, its effects, solutions, afforestation, and briquette as safe energy for cooking in targeted vulnerable communities.
Beneficiaries shall be trained on climate change, its effects, its mitigations, afforestation, briquettes, its benefits to climate change, and its health benefits, and these beneficiaries will help create awareness and lead campaigns together with the HARAF team in their communities on alternative safe energy for cooking. At the end of these sessions, beneficiaries will be given 100kg briquettes, a briquette stove, a firestarter, and a manual briquette extruder. This will increase briquette production in small quantities for home use, reduce the use of firewood which is one major cause of climate change, and reduce the hazards/abuses women and girls face when they go to forests or far farmlands to fetch firewood.
Youth groups shall be formed, trained, and given carbonizers to be able to outsource agricultural waste materials and carbonize them for the beneficiaries to use for briquette production at home. This will increase access to the raw materials for briquette production. This will also be an avenue for youth engagements, reduce risk among women and girls, create a livelihood for the youths within these communities, and reduce crimes/violence amongst youths. These youth groups shall be linked to farmers groups within the community, who shall be providing the youths with access to agricultural waste at no or low cost. Carbonizers shall be provided to these youth groups for carbonizing agricultural waste products and sale at a very low price to HHs. The carbonized raw materials shall be used by HHs for briquette production at HHs levels. The youths shall be encouraged to carry out carbonization at farmlands for easy access.
Financial literacy training will be conducted for the youth groups formed. This is an additional value activity. The training will be centered on; Basic financial literacy, Introduction to entrepreneurship, basic business records/bookkeeping, introduction to marketing, and Business risk management.
Awareness and training shall be conducted amongst farmers groups on best farming methods, postharvest management, and climate change. These farmers shall be provided with seeds and fertilizer support with the plan to ensure they can support the youths with free access to agricultural waste (maize stock, rice husk, sugar cane pills, etc) after harvest. Already existing farmers' groups shall be identified and empowered to ensure there is a linkage between the farmers, the youth groups, and the women/girls producing briquettes.
Community-Led Referral Pathway will be established to build strong partnerships with the community, local NGOs, healthcare providers, law enforcement, and educational institutions that will form the foundation for a coordinated and unified response to GBV cases. And Community-Led GBV
Awareness Campaigns will be organized on the effect of climate change on women and girls especially, and on giving women and girls access to safe energy for cooking. Our approach involves assembling a diverse and experienced campaign team, including community members, communication experts, GBV specialists, etc. This team will work collaboratively to ensure a holistic campaign strategy.
Tree planting exercises and campaigns shall be carried out in the community where a hundred trees will be planted per LGA. These tree-planting exercises shall be carried out in schools, government structures, health facilities, and HHs with water points.
HARAF proposes 12 months for this intervention which will directly benefit 600 IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable communities

1 - Needs

Climate change is a complex global issue with far-reaching implications for all aspects of life, including its effects, especially on women and girls. The challenge surrounding climate change revolves around the increasing concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, primarily due to human activities such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation, industrial processes, and agriculture. This leads to rising global temperatures, changes in weather patterns, sea-level rise, extreme weather events, loss of biodiversity, various environmental disruptions, and reduced agriculture food production. In recent years, research has highlighted how climate change affects women and girls differently. Women are more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change due to existing societal roles, limited access to resources, and economic dependence. They are more likely to face GBV and abuse especially when they have to go into forests and riversides to get firewood. The use of firewood and charcoal in cooking contributes to health issues that affect women and girls. Increased heart-related diseases, and lung and eye challenges are alarming among women because of the frequent use of firewood and charcoal in cooking. Clean cooking has been on the development agenda for decades. But only recently has the topic become a major priority on the global development agenda as part of Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 7: affordable and clean energy.In developing countries, most of the energy households use is for cooking. This makes cooking the most energy-intensive household activity. Worldwide, 4 billion people still lack access to clean, efficient, convenient, safe, reliable, and affordable cooking energy. People use polluting open fires and inefficient stoves. This is either due to habit, lack of awareness, limited access to safer energy for cooking, and cost. These inefficient cooking methods are harmful to health and delay economic and social development in developing countries. Examples of polluting and inefficient cooking methods include 3-stone woodfires or simple clay and metal stoves, which use wood or charcoal. There is still a huge lack of access to clean cooking in sub-Saharan Africa. Access only slightly increased from 15% in 2015 to 17% in 2018. Since 2015, only 25 million people have gained access to clean cooking in the region.In recent times, the global concern about climate change and its devastating effects has led governments and communities to take drastic measures to protect the environment. To combat deforestation and preserve the fragile ecosystem, the Government of Adamawa State bans the use of firewood, charcoal, and the felling of trees, this has caused significant discomfort to buyers and sellers, especially those involved in the charcoal trade. They are left with no alternatives to their means of livelihood. The impact of this decision is largely felt by women who happen to be the major retailers of firewood and charcoal in Adamawa State. Most of them are housewives who find it convenient to do business in the comfort of their homes. Their reason is simple – charcoal and firewood happen to be the most lucrative and convenient business for them.Adamawa and Yobe States, like many regions across Nigeria, face challenges associated with traditional cooking methods that heavily rely on firewood and charcoal. The use of these conventional fuels poses serious health risks due to indoor air pollution and contributes to deforestation and climate change. Our proposed solution promotes the adoption of smokeless briquettes made from agricultural waste as a cleaner and sustainable alternative for cooking. This intervention aligns with our commitment to mitigating climate change, reducing deforestation, and improving the health of especially women and girls who are the major users of traditional cooking methods in vulnerable communities of Adamawa and Yobe States.

2 - Activities

Key activities will include Staff recruitment, Community mapping, Community entry, Stakeholders engagements, forming of Community Project Monitoring Committee (PMCs), Community Target Committee (CTC), Beneficiary targeting, Beneficiary Selection and Beneficiary Validation; Forming of woman, girls, and youth groups, identifying of farmers groups, awareness/workshop on effect, causes mitigation on climate change, new innovations, advantages and production of briquette, awareness campaigns to educate communities about the benefits and usage of briquettes, best methods of use, handling, and maintenance of the briquette machines, workshops on accessing raw materials of briquettes, Establish local briquette production units, Distribution of briquette machines, agricultural inputs, market linkages, training on financial literacy, post distribution monitoring, reports, review of IEC materials, setup of Compliant and Feedback mechanism, review meetings, contract vendors and procurement procedures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To promote accountability to the affected population, the M&E and Compliant and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) team will set up and maintain a complaint and feedback mechanism using multiple channels like the Complain desk, Hotlines, and CFM box. A CFM assessment shall be conducted to inform the setup of the CFM, the aim is to find out the best and most comfortable CFM channels and timing for the beneficiaries and the community at large. This will provide project beneficiaries with channels through which they may provide feedback or lodge complaints related to the intervention. The CFM channels shall be suitable for all genders, people living with disabilities, women, girls, boys, the elderly, and the uneducated. In addition, HARAF shall disseminate information to raise awareness of beneficiary entitlements to express dissatisfaction and to provide feedback, success stories, and complaints. Feedback received will be analyzed and forwarded to relevant officers for action. The CFM teams shall follow up to ensure the resolution of all complaints issued until it is closed.
