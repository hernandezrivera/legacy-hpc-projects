Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges – intensified by the global economic downturn, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict – all adversely affect the food security of South Sudanese families.
As of 16 October, 317,993 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 88.5 per cent of all arrivals. (Situation Report OCHA No. 16 as of 20 October 2023)
The main purpose of this proposed intervention is to establish basic hygiene standards and provide clean water to the affected populations impacted by emergencies-women, men, girls and boys, especially IDPs, IDPs returning to their place of origin or chosen place of return, returnees and vulnerable persons in areas affected by conflict, natural disasters; alongside nutrition emergency responses or in areas with outbreaks of waterborne diseases.
The intervention will be achieved by integrating WASH with nutrition and community level through distribution of WASH NFI kits to SAM/caretakers children paired with hygiene promotion, rehabilitation/construction of water sources at nutrition and at community level, rehabilitation/construction of permanent and semi-permanent latrines and conduct safety audit at site level to identify GBV concerns for latrines and water points.
This intervention WASH proposed will target 60,000 beneficiaries in Fangak, Canal/Pigi.Nasir, Renk, Nyirol, Nasir and Ayod is anticipated to cost USD 1,980,000 that include returnees, IDPS and host communities about 72211 beneficiaries and secured 20% mainly Fangak County County
As of 3rd September according to IOM report more than 253,023 persons were registered crossing the border into South Sudan since the fighting in Sudan erupted on 15 April. The Sudan conflict prompted an escalation in the cost of commodities, including food, particularly in the counties neighboring Sudan consequently diminishing the purchasing power of the population. In August, according to UNHCR, more than 22,000 South Sudanese refugees² returned spontaneously to South Sudan through eight official entry points from Gambella, Ethiopia, citing the lack of access to basic services, livelihood opportunities and insecurity in the Gambella region as reasons for return.

1. Needs

There is continue influx of returnees and refugees crossing Joda entry point and many end up in Transit Centre in Renk, report indicates there is a total about 17,500 returnees and 5000 refugees. Inadequate access to safe water combined with poor sanitation and personal hygiene practices have detrimentally impacted upon the health and quality of life of millions of peoples especially in Payikang.Nasir.Famgak,Nyirol,Canal/Pigi, Ayod and Renk where majority of the communities practice open defecation which predispose them to health risks.According to IRNA reports conducted in several locations that include Renk Town Localized Flooding IRNA Report, 20th September 2023 where (2,830 individuals) that have been impacted and displaced that may be in need of wash services. In Nyirol IRNA report was conducted of fact findings on 5th -12th July to assess the situation of returnees and their humanitarian needs and it was determined that Returnees have raised issues of hygiene, and sanitation due to limited number of pit latrines in the community resulting into open defecation, lack of WAS NFI and open defecation were observed Generally most of the counties mentioned in this proposal are facing similar challenge that revealed that there is inadequate water for drinking, Lack of access for safe water collection and storage facilities., high turbidity drinking water, poor personal hygiene of the and rampant open defecation practices were observed .While in separate report in Nasir conducted on 22nd August 2023 indicated that 85% of the host community, IDPs and returnees are practicing open defecation and have poo excretal disposal and lack adequate sanitation facilities and there is inadequate WASH NFI for fetching water as result of increased number of returnees from Ethiopia and Sudan.There is limited access to safe drinking water, water collection and storage facilities, poor personal hygiene for mother of under five children and high open defecation practices, inadequate WASH/NFI (buckets, soaps, Pur/Aqua tabs and filter clothes, lack of MHM and dignity kits for women and girls of reproductive age, inadequate bathing shelters among returnees and refugees. High malnutrition rates are also linked to poor WASH conditions, as exemplified in the diarrhea-malnutrition cycle prevalent in vulnerable populationsWomen are often vulnerable to harassment or violence when they have to travel long distances to fetch water, use shared toilets, or practice open defecation. Women and girls often wait until nightfall to defecate, which increases the risk of assault, majority of the children suffering from malnutrition can be attributed to lack clean water supply and poor hygienic
