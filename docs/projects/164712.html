Project Summary

Nine years of crisis have made all population groups vulnerable, however, there is wide recognition that women and girls are particularly more at risk. Sexual gender-based violence (SGBV) against women and girls increases during conﬂict, including domestic violence, sexual violence and exploitation, and child marriage (Protection-HNO 2020). Ongoing conﬂict has particularly prevented girls from attending school: the number of female students in secondary schools dropped by 46% from 2011 to 2017 (compared to 36% for boys) (UNICEF, 2018).
As a result of the prolonged crisis, schools and hospitals have been wrecked with weakened protection, security, and justice systems. Thus, women and girls lack access to safe spaces and services where they can learn about their sexual and reproductive health rights (SRHR), discuss concerns and issues around SGBV, early marriage, harassment and other topics without the fear of social and peer stigma, and receive psychosocial support as needed.
The speciﬁc objectives of the project are to (1) improve the mental health of 400 women and girls by establishing 7 girl clubs in target areas as a safe space for women and girls to learn about healthy lifestyles and improve their life skills and discuss issues related to SRHR, SGBV, and mental health; (2) educating local community members, leaders and parents about the gender-speciﬁc risk factors of women and girls and the impact on mental health, which will include the establishment of 5 community management groups and 4 boys clubs to learn about and discuss these issues; and (3) through trained facilitators, identify women and girls requiring psychosocial support and refer to available mental health services in Salamieh District.

4 - Needs

Salamieh District has a population of 223,935 people, in which 20.2% of the population are youth (46.3% female youth). According to a study conducted by AKF in 2019, 80% of the population in Salamieh live below the national poverty line (2 USD per capita per day), 94% of households (HH) are assessed as food insecure, and vulnerable IDPs and residents also report high reliance on assistance to meet basic needs. The unemployment rate is particularly high among females (85% compared to 54% of males). The imbalance between household income and expenses is a key driver of vulnerability that contributes to negative coping mechanisms adopted by households. About 36% of children (age:0-18) in Salamieh are out of school due to the high cost of education (SYP 13,900/month/person), many of whom are engaged in child labor (6.6% of all children in Salamieh). In 2019, AKF consulted with girls and female youth, parents, NGOs and community leaders to identify the social, cultural, economic, and structural barriers to women and girl’s empowerment. The evaluation determined the following: lack of decision-making ability; a high risk of SGVB; personal safety in and around school; the lack of safe spaces and networks where they can share their concerns without the fear of social and peer stigma; the absence of trusted resources about sexual and reproductive health; the need of community awareness, including parents, regarding the transitions adolescents experience during puberty and how to address them. Adolescent girls also reported feeling particularly vulnerable to early marriage, as they face family pressure to marry due to family ﬁnancial burdens. It is evident that the challenges and barriers facing adolescent girls and female youth are numerous and have an impact on individual mental health, emotional distress, as well as impact life stage transitions, aspirations, independence, and self-conﬁdence.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will achieve the three speciﬁc objectives in the following ways: (1) improve the physical and mental health of nearly 400 women and girls: AKF will establish seven girl clubs in sub-districts of Salamieh as a safe space for women and girls to learn about healthy lifestyles and improve their life skills and discuss issues related to SRHR, SGBV, and mental health. In addition, 4 boys clubs will also be established in target areas to sensitize 60 boys and reduce stigma about the above subjects. This will include training a team of 22 female facilitators to mobilize women and girls, facilitate sessions, monitor progress and identify and refer women and girls to health facilities as required. (2) AKF will establish of 5 Community Management Groups (CMGs) in sub-districts consisting of 100 (75F) members of NGOs, Community Based Organizations (CBOs), MH experts, and community leaders to present the perspectives of the community, support with awareness-raising campaigns and provide feedback on the project. AKF will educate 355 (275F) individuals including 100 (75F) CMGs members, and 255 (200F) parents about the gender-speciﬁc risk factors of women and girls and the impact on mental health. AKF will also develop promotional materials in collaboration with these groups to reach a total of 7,000 women, men, girls, and boys. (3) identify and refer women and girls requiring psychosocial support, by the 22 trained facilitators, to available mental health services. Process and outcome monitoring activities under the project will include baseline and end-line assessments of the target group, ongoing feedback with project beneﬁciaries and FGDs with project stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A. Female adolescent and youth might face discrimination/abuse from their peers and/ or wider community such as bullying or negative judgements. likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will mitigate this risk by equipping sessions facilitators with proper training on how to deal with such cases, and establish clear sessions rules to create an atmosphere of respect, empathy, solid and cooperative relationships among girls. AKF will mobilize community organizations to expand awareness of project objectives and significance of such initiatives. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of abuse/exploitations and take actions against it.B. Facilitators might be criticized, accused, or even show of bias in their relations with girls; favoring some against others. likelihood: low, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will create a code of conduct which will be explained and signed by facilitators, along with closely monitoring their adherence to them and interfere when needed.C. Leakage of beneficiaries’ information outside the sessions to wider community. likelihood: medium, impact: high. Mitigation measures: AKF will ensure facilitators to sign confidentiality agreement, and hold private sessions for special cases.D. Safety and Security concerns to find community places that are safe and easily accessible all female adolescent and youth. Likelihood: low, Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will ensure safe and secure environment along with the availability of WASH facilities where sessions are delivered.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The project might face slight resistance from the side of local authority and community leaders considering the sensitivity of SGBV and SRHR within local culture. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will establish high level of coordination with local authorities and community leaders to explain the initiative objectives and secure their support before launching the project.Risk 2: Lack of local SGBV and SRHS experts for quality delivery quality of the project. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will coordinate with other INGOs and national actors to utilize their resources and experience in these fields. In coordination with local and national partners, AKF will develop a health-care referral pathway and explore the possibility to also provide legal one. Risk 3: Lack of community interest and participation in SGBV, gender, and SRHR subjects might affect female adolescent and youth participation and the activities reach. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will conduct a number of community mobilization through LCBOs and LNGOs, and ensure the involvement of community leaders and decision makers as supporters of the project.Risk 4: Lack of Community Management Groups’ long-term commitment to expand the project objectives. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will conduct regular meetings, and adopt a participatory and partnership approach to ensure community ownerships of the project. In addition, AKF will provide incentives such as acknowledgment ceremonies, and appreciation letters for the most cooperative members who play an effective role with high commitment to the project. Risk 5: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project budget.
