Project Summary

In 2021, conflict was the main driver of displacements in Ethiopia. The number of displaced people had reached 4.2M by December 2021. The most impactful conflict in Ethiopia in 2021 has been the Tigray conflict in the northern part of Ethiopia, with its associated spillover effects. In 2021 the cluster estimated 3M people are in need of NFI-ESNFI support and out of which the cluster targeted 2.4M people but only 31 % were reached so far while there is over all 46 % gap need urgent. As of December 2021,NFI-ESNFI Gap was 34% while emergency shelter gap was 71%, with The higher gap ,66% in Mekele followed by south western zone with 62%. January 27.2022 published HPC for Ethiopia HNO estimated that 357 news IDP in the camp and 1.425M news IDPs in the host communities are living sever to Catastrophic living condition. Furthermore, people live in undignified and unsafe conditions in many collective sites, exposing them to protection and health risks. IDPs in the host community have also reported that up to three families are renting a small room. Recently 34,200 new IDPs are displaced from western zone and now are living in Northwestern zone since November 20th 2021 which is in a dire need for NFI and Emergency shelter support. One of the factors contributing to the deterioration of living standards of IDPs is the congestion of shelters in most IDPs sites. Eastern, Central and north western zone of Tigray region is the most affected zone where the displaced communities in camp and Host communities are left without basic humanitarian aid related with NFI and SNFI.
Majority of the IDPs are living in collective sites like schools and are without basic shelter and housing. Most vulnerable people are living in collective sites that are sub-standard for shelter. The project locations are selected based on their severity level and the most congested IDP sites with higher gap in the region in terms of ESNFI-NFI. These are: Mekele town, shire Indesilassie and shiraro tawn . The project targeted a total of 55881 beneficiaries in Tigray (Shire Indesilassie town 16617, in Mekele, 19337 Shiraro towns 19927). Out of these 41911 are IDPs, 8381 are Host communities 5589are returnees. The project will target 15% Host community and 15% of its resources for disable people. The modality of intervention is bot in kind and cash provision for shelter. 30 % NFI-SNFI and SRk support will be through direct Cash provision and 40% will be in kind. This project will continue to reducing morbidity and mortality, and maintain human dignity, among vulnerable conflict-displaced and returnee communities through the provision of shelter kits, ESNFI.SRK non-food items and unconditional cash transfers. Within this HRP the proposed response is in line with the cluster objective. ESNFI Cluster objective CO2.1: and CO3.1. Major activities under this project will be: Cash for house rent assistance to conflict affected IDP HHS, Provision of ESNFI, provision of NFI, provision of Shelter repair kit, HLP, Cash for shelter, provision of in kind shelter.
All targeted households will be provided with information on the project, MCMDO, their entitlements, and modalities for collection of NFIs, as well as ways to make contact with MCMDO and provide feedback through the CRM or by other means. Male and female representatives from the targeted community will be selected to take part on a community feedback committee during the distributions. During beneficiary targeting, the shelter cluster beneficiary selection and targeting guideline will be used while the most vulnerable community group including child-headed household, the separated child in need of shelter, Children at risk, female-headed household, elders, and people with underlying chronic illness, a large family will get the priority. The projects will strictly follow and Adhere COVID-19 specific beneficiary’s targeting, registration, and distribution operation procedure to mitigate the outbreak

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Response are designed considering the real needs at the community level. MCMDO conducts quick assessments to identify the scope and types of humanitarian responses needs. The assessment is a joint assessment which involves the local government, sector offices, the community representatives, affected communities and other key stakeholders. MCMDO assessment teams use standardized templates to capture the existing response needs. Key informant interviews, Focus Group discussions, document review and site visits are some of the basic tools used to capture the key information to design the response. MCMDO will also conducts a discussion before the design of the response with the sector offices to discuss and agree on the strategies, activities and targeting. Using these inputs, MCMDO compiles the information and produces assessment reports which are used as a sole information to design the responses for resource mobilization. As resources are committed by donors, MCMDO submit the response to the regional or zonal stakeholders for appraisal. The stakeholders review and sign the project agreement as the project is designed jointly and reflects the primary needs of the affected communities. The response is aligned and harmonized with the existing government and other actors plans to avoid duplication of efforts and realize complementarity to meet the wider need of the communities. MCMDO advocates the stakeholders and the community to contribute resources including resources in cash, in kind, technical support or other forms of contributions. This helps the response to be sustainable and the beneficiaries and the stakeholders to ensure the ownership of the response outcomes. During the implementation of the projects, the stakeholders, community representatives and other actors engaged during the design stage will be part and parcel of the implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of the response. MCMDO invites the government counter parts to periodically review the performance and achievements of the response, provide feedbacks and capitalize the best practices. The beneficiaries and community representatives lead the implementation of the activities at community level. They are engaged in Beneficiaries selection & verification, delivery of response/support, modalities, efficiency, effectiveness and relevance of the response. Moreover, MCMDO will design and implement compliant and feedback mechanisms at grassroots levels which helps to ensure the accountability to the affected Populations (AAP). Such systems will be reviewed periodically and assess their effectiveness as well as relevance to the targeted communities. Thus, the targeted beneficiaries and the local stakeholders will make meaningful participations and lead the response to contribute for the realization of the planned objectives and achieve wider impacts on the lives of the beneficiaries.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Accountability to affected people (AAP) is one of MCMDO,s quality standards reflected in programs design and implementation through active engagement and participation of the community throughout the project cycle. MCMDO will engaged the targeted beneficiaries and local actors in all stages of the project. The project will establish robust feedback mechanisms to get feedback, complaints and other information from the community beginningMCMDO will establish a complaints and feedback response mechanism (CRM) for community feedback on the ES-NFI- distributions. All targeted households will be provided with information on the project, their entitlements, and modalities for collection of NFIs, as well as ways to make contact with MCMDO and provide feedback through the CRM or by other means. Male and female representatives from the targeted community will be selected to take part on a community feedback committee during the distributions. In addition, information regarding MCMDOs will be provided to all stakeholders for all project activities (including but not limited to Woreda and kebele government, community members, contractors, health workers and community representatives involved in the training), during meetings, and information will be displayed in MCMDO office.. The target groups in need of special support or protection include the elderly, women, and people with disabilities, as they are marginalized or generally more disadvantaged within society and may therefore develop less resistance to crises such as displacement and food insecurity. Women are largely excluded from decision-making processes due to cultural norms and have very few economic. They are often also the main caregivers of children and other family members needing care (sick, people with disabilities, elderly). In addition, elderly people and people with disabilities are particularly affected by various shocks and conflicts as they are also rarely involved in decision-making processes and their special needs remain invisible. For these reasons, Vulnerability criteria will be established and agreed on with the local authorities and target communities (such as pregnant and lactating women, female-headed and youth-headed households, households with elderly or disabled members, or large family size). Finally, the inclusion of these especially vulnerable target groups (women, people with disabilities, infants/children and elderly people) in the project will be first ensured by a transparent beneficiary selection procedure to ensure that they are involved in the decisions on project support from the very beginning.
