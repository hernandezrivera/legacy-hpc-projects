Project Summary

CDHI will implement 12-month MAM management for children 6-59 months and PLWs using locally available blended food – Tom Brown model. And promote malnutrition prevention through IYCF promotion and increased demand for consumption of nutrient dense locally available foods as well as increased access to specialised skilled IYCF counselling amongst nutritionally vulnerable groups particularly PLWs and caregivers of children 6-23 months. MAM PLWs and children 6-59 months identified using MUAC screening (MUAC 11.5 cm) in case of children will be enrol in a cohort and will receive intervention that treat MAM using local solution (Tom Brown). MAM management will include the use of Tom-brown model and with a linkage to other health and nutrition services such as MNP supplementation, routine medications as per the revised national guideline of community management of Acute Malnutrition, in collaboration with UNICEF and SPHCDA.
PLWs and caregivers of children 6 -23 months, adolescent girls women and men will receive MIYCF promotion activities through community peer group platforms using mother or father peer support group. IYCF promotion activities through the established support groups will be facility by trained CNMs and lead mothers based in the communities. Cooking demonstration will be an integrated in IYCF sessions. PLWs and caregivers of children 6-23 months will receive individual skilled IYCF counselling at the community or facility by trained CNM or CHWs respectively. Capacity of community structures will be built on malnutrition programmes to ensure that members of community take ownership and are able to respond to their nutrition needs using tested local models.
The nutrition activities will be linked with nutrition sensitive sectors; WASH, Health, child protection and GVB sub-sector. CDHI will distribute soap to IYCF peer support group members to encourage practice of hand washing. Similarly, MAM PLWs will be identified through existing ANC services at health facilities. Those identified at the community level will be encouraged to access ANC services. Caregivers of children 6-23 months in the communities will be encouraged to access immunization services at the facilities. CDHI will leverage on its existing UNFPA/PPFN project on GBV prevention to ensure that GBV prevention and mitigation measures are well mainstreamed in the communication package that will be delivered to the members of the community including father peer group through the group platform. gender and accountability to affected population is well mainstreamed in the programme designed.
A quasi-experimental longitudinal prospective cohort study will be nested within ongoing community-based MAM management local solution. The study will have two comparison groups which receive different intervention packages. All arms will have comparable health promotion and behaviour change communication (SBCC) activities. In one study arm, beneficiaries will receive tom brown meals about 200g/p/d for eight weeks without linkage to health facility for other complementary health and nutrition services in addition to health promotion and SBCC services. In another study arm, beneficiaries will receive tom brown meals about 200g/p/d for eight weeks with linkage to health facility for other complementary health and nutrition services (MNP and routine drugs) in addition to health promotion and SBCC services. Beneficiaries will receive interventions for a maximum of 3 months and will be followed until another 3 months post-intervention. The study will assess the effectiveness of use of tom brown in the management of MAM, implication, and cost effectiveness for linkage with health system in humanitarian setting. Finding from the operational research will be used as an advocacy tool and by programme managers to inform future response in the spirit of scaling up localization.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in NE Nigeria, now over a decade continued to subject people to widespread displacement, impoverishment, and threats of violence in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan, 8.7 million people require assistance across the BAY States, a 10% increase from 2020. Borno state is most affected hosting more than 80% of the over 2 million internally displaced people (IDP) in the NE. Violent attacks by NSAGs and military counter-operations resulting in widespread displacement has disrupted farming and other livelihoods and limited the functionality of markets and other basic services, with women and children most affected. The conflict has deteriorated the nutritional status among children and women, and devastated the already fragile public infrastructure, including health, education, water and sanitation and other public services capacities to protect children and women in fragile humanitarian settings.Food and nutrition insecurity is a pervasive feature in this crisis, and internally displaced people (IDPs) are most affected. They have lost their livelihoods and homes and consequently their coping mechanisms are compromised. Subjected to forced migration in search of safety, they depend almost completely on food assistance to meet their food needs, until they can return to their homes and rebuild their livelihoods. In host communities, food insecurity is worsening due to economic after-effects of the COVID-19 lockdown and climate-related shocks. the COVID-19 pandemic has compounded and complicated the humanitarian needs and response in the northeast. Nigeria’s economy has suffered a 34.1% loss in GDP due to COVID amounting to about USD 16 billion, with two third of the losses coming from service delivery sectors during the lock down period, resulting to 14%-point temporary increase in the poverty headcount rate, implying that 27 million additional people fell below the poverty line during lockdown in Nigeria.Findings of the March 2021 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis projected that about 5.1 million people in the NE will be food-insecure (Phase 3 -5) in the lean season between June and August 2021 – a 19% and 34% increase on the 2020 (after COVID-19 June CH Update) and 2019 figures respectively.According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 10, conducted in September 2021, the level of acute malnutrition increased in all the three states compared to 2019 and 2020. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates as high as 14.0% were recorded in Borno, 6.7% in Adamawa and 16.0% in Yobe. Prevalence of acute malnutrition amongst pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) using MUAC is also on the increase in all the three BAY states with about 11.1%, 14.8% and 20.6% in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states respectively. UNICEF FIT Sentinel alert report, September 2021, shows a deteriorating trend of the nutrition status of children less than 59 months. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rates have increased by 1.5% and 0.5% from the previous round. The report also reported a higher morbidity such as diarrhoea has been on the increase. The causes of Acute malnutrition are cross-cutting and multi-sectoral and include poor food security, poor health and poor IYCF, caring practices and other underlying factors related to WASH, education and protection including GBV. Movement restrictions and insecurity continue to hamper the ability of IDPs, returnees and the host communities to access basic services, livelihoods, and land for farming and grazing.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CDHI will focus on training all its staff, committees and stakeholders involved in programme delivery activities on the four key pillars of protection mainstreaming which includes: -- Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of your intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks- Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services - in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.– Accountability: set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints– Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. The training will seek to increase knowledge and consciousness of staff on ensuring these principles are part and parcel of design and implementation of activities. A key outcome of these trainings is the identification of protection risks within the frame of planned malnutrition prevention and treatment activities and how to mitigate these risks. CDHI intends to have a protection mainstreaming budget line within this project to address these risks. CDHI dedicated gender and protection officer who is 100% on this project will ensure that identified risks will be put into consideration in program implementation and mitigation measures established and strengthened. There will be a protection mainstreaming budget line which will remain flexible to address any form of protection related issue identified. CDHI staff have been specifically trained to identify and support extremely vulnerable persons and ensure they are supported appropriately throughout the project. CDHI will integrated in its sensitization messages mechanism to report issues related to sexual exploitation and Abuse. CDHI have and internal system of PSEA. Those systems and mechanism will be made available to the communities. Community members will also have the complaint box, and a dedicated community level help desk which could be utilized in raising concerns of SEA or suggestions on how to prevent SEA. CDHI ensures that its programming is informed by needs; CDHI will continually conducts assessments using qualitative approaches such as focus group discussions, key informant interviews and in-depth community discussions to remain informed on the need’s communities have and these needs feed into the program design and response. In addition to this, CDHI is guided by the principle of neutrality in responding to needs and ensuring communities understand that CDHI staff are neutral and trustworthy bound by strict principles of confidentiality and integrity. CDHI employs a consultative approach to supporting communities. Feedback about the project design, implementation and monitoring continually engages communities to give their views, opinions and recommendations. These then inform all programmatic actions thereon. CDHI protection staff (funded by this project) sole’s responsibility will be to ensure the highest level of protection and gender is well mainstreamed in the project implementation.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

Acute malnutrition is a major public health problem in several locations in the BAY states of Nigeria. Around 1.15 million children aged 6-59 months were expected to suffer from acute malnutrition during 2021 according to the IPC Acute malnutrition, with about half of them (543,643) expected to be moderately acute malnourished and over 123,000 pregnant or lactating women also expected to suffer from acute malnutrition.The ongoing conflict continues to have a direct impact on affected population’s nutrition status especially the most vulnerable and those in inaccessible locations. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months is on the increase across the BAY states when compared year-on-year, with Yobe and Borno state rates above the emergency and critical threshold at 16.5. % and 13 % respectively as of September 2021 according to round 10 nutrition and food security surveillance preliminary findings. In Ngala and Jere there was a significant increase in prevalence from 9.6% in 2020 to 12.9% in 2021 and from 9.9% in 2020 to 13.7% in 2021 respectively. September 2021, UNICEF FIT sentinel alert report reveals that the situation is significantly worsening across most of the affected LGAs. Major contributing factors of acute malnutrition in all the analyzed LGAs according to the March 2021 IPC AMN, include very poor food consumption patterns (both quantity and quality) and high morbidity rates. Assessments conducted to-date have also identified factors such as severe food insecurity, early marriage, poor maternal nutrition, poverty, poor sanitation and hygiene practices, low exclusive breastfeeding rates and inadequate complementary feeding as major contributors.Based on mapping of nutrition sector partners activities through the nutrition sector 2021 5Ws, all the accessible LGAs were covered by nutrition actors providing services for the management of severe acute malnutrition. 9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As an integral part of CDHI’s operations in the NE humanitarian context, CDHI will endeavors to deliver the planned malnutrition prevention and community-based management of moderate acute malnutrition project activities in a way that meets priorities of all women, men, girls, and boys without compromising their protection. This will includes involving the communities in decisions related to the program delivery to ensure that activities are implemented in a safe and accessible locations. This will includes engaging the communities to decide where and when to prepare Tom Brown food recipe and distribution of the recipe for MAM management as well as distributions sites of locally available nutritious food for supplementary feeding through the voucher. Locally available nutritious food or Tom brown recipe distribution will be community directed and will ensure convenient scheduling of distributions plans that will enable beneficiaries to receive their entitlements in a safe and dignified manner as well as minimize waiting time. CDHI will also ensure provision of Mats, chairs and other basic facilities as well train stakeholders like vendors on humanitarian principles and AAP. beneficiaries with any form of disability will be identified and prioritized in the programme implementation activities. Women will be encouraged to hold at least 40% leadership positions or participation in community level structures or committees that supports programme activities. CDHI will use the Washington group set of short questions on disability during beneficiaries registration and enrolment to identify targeted beneficiaries that have any of the forms of disability related to difficulty in seeing, hearing, walking, remembering/concentrating, self-care and communicating; the Washington set of short questions includes;- Do you have difficulty seeing, even if wearing glasses?- Do you have difficulty hearing, even if using a hearing aid?- Do you have difficulty walking or climbing steps
