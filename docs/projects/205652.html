Project Summary

Existing WASH infrastructure in Leer and Mayendit north counties is unable to provide adequate WASH services (water, safe disposal of faeces and hygiene promotion) due to the population increase, risking increased outbreaks of water borne diseases including acute watery diarrhea, cholera, and malnutrition. Inadequate WASH is a major cause of diseases in Leer and Mayendit counties and improving sanitation through hygiene promotion is known to have a significant beneficial impact on health in households. In Leer and Mayendit counties there are IDPs and host communities without good sanitation because it was not there from the very beginning, or it was destroyed by the many conflicts and flooding. There is need for maintenance of hygienic conditions, through improved availability of latrines, safe water source, waste disposal, bathing shelters, to raise awareness on water and sanitation related health/hygiene issues, especially to raise awareness of water quality in order to successfully distribute Homebase care kits, ( buckets, soap and water purifiers -water flocks sachets/Aquatabs) MHM kits to the deserving girls and women, and to reduce the risk of cholera outbreak, and other sanitation related diseases. The project aims to reparate 20 non-functioning boreholes, Construction of 5 protected shallow wells and reparation of 4 mini-water yard in several Payams of Leer and Mayendit north Counties so that the IDPs, the returnees and un-displaced population can access water at an average of 750 people (women, men, boys and girls) per borehole which is still below the UNHCR and SPHERE standards quality requirement but is far better than the current average usage of 1,250 per borehole and use of alternate unsafe sources according FSNMS 2023 and NAWG data, IRNA report Leer & Mayendit, where only 34% of the community are accessing safe water. Some of the displaced population has been forced to live within hosting population and IDP camps with very limited WASH infrastructure resulting to increased open defecation, overuse of functional boreholes and mini-water yards, institutional and shared family latrines etc. The project envisages to conduct hygiene promotion to influence behavior change from harmful practices that encourage spread of diseases, by involving women, men, boys and girls in the community through community hygiene promotion approaches, water and sanitation management committees, through involving all the stakeholders in the populations that includes people living with disabilities and the elderly. A total of 11,111 people (women, men, boys and girls) will benefit through provision of access to safe adequate water by reparation of boreholes and mini water yards, construction of protected shallow wells and improve sanitation through construction of gender sensitive latrines and hygiene promotion awareness creation. UNIDOR has been able to demonstrate that hygiene and sanitation awareness coupled with the installation of improved water facilities can trigger an improvement in attitudes towards hygiene and sanitation, good health, among the communities in Pariang, Mayendit, Koch, Leer Counties in partnership with SSHF, UNICEF, IOM. UNIDOR programing does conduct GBV safety audit before the construction and rehabilitation of water points, institutional latrines, creating awareness about hygiene and sanitation in advance of the implementation of distribution of WASH NFIs and construction of WASH infrastructure. This approach often guarantees a more enthusiastic participation of women, girls and boys, which leads to a higher level of acceptance and ownership by a larger group of community members and supports greater sustainability of the program by reinforcing the link between water, sanitation and hygiene. This project aims at; to enable the community access safe adequate drinking water, promote construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities while prioritizing the safety of women, girls.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to be affected by conflict and flood disasters in some areas and the threat of conflict in others. In Leer and Mayendit north counties Unity State, this situation coupled with high levels of poverty, high returns of refugees due to the Sudan conflict, destroyed infrastructure, food insecurity and flooding, has caused mass displacement of people (women, men, boys, girls) who are residing in IDP camps and within the receiving host communities. These vulnerable IDPs, returnees, host populations continue to suffer more burden due to inadequate and disproportional WASH infrastructure considering the SPHERE, WHO and UNHCR guidelines. This environmental, humanitarian, political crisis has negatively affected the already poor to access basic water, sanitation and hygiene services. According to HNO 2023, communities were hit hard by the intensified conflict in Sudan and another consecutive year of major flooding. An estimated 5,500,000 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2024 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. According to (FSNMS 2023), despite the fact that major waterways were replenished by the rainy season, only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34% in rural areas. In certain states, such as Unity, where 33% of households travelling for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities and the elderly. Difficulty in securing enough water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49% of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. Surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods. Limited access to water is also compounded by 14% households reporting having at least one container to safely store water (FSNMS 2023, IRNA and NAWG data). Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points, with 78% of households, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water. Of those, 12% reported of women and girls reportedly avoiding water points due to GBV concerns. Overall, 80% households did not access a latrine. This aggregated figure however hides some extreme geographical variations. In certain states, such as Unity, the percentage of households accessing a latrine is consistently below 10%, with open defecation being the norm (FSNMS 2023, NAWG data and IRNA reports). Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in Leer and Mayendit counties. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people (women, men, boys, girls), as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition. According to IPC snapshot, between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 thousand children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.17 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. An estimated 72% of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. In Leer and Mayendit Counties Floods and Conflict has caused families to retreat to remote areas with very limited WASH infrastructure and increased pressure on existing WASH services in host communities and disrupted livelihoods, further perpetuating economic challenge

2. Project activities

Reparation of boreholes/mini water yards, construction of protected shallow wells, construction and rehabilitation of gender sensitive latrines, Installation of handwashing facilities at key public areas (marketplaces, entry places to IDPs, settlements, densely populated areas, schools, nutrition facilities etc.). Training of handpump mechanics. Training of Community hygiene Promoters (CHPs) Training of health worker/volunteers/Nutrition worker on WASH minimum package. Provision of Home Base Care kits, Hygiene standard kit items, MHM items distributed to women and girls as planned in the project. Hygiene promotion messaging and campaigns. Equipping Nutrition/institutional facilities with WASH Minimum package for functional Water supply, sanitation system – waste management and disinfection of surfaces with HTH (Stabilization centers, OTP, TSFP).Equipping institutions i.e., Schools, and Nutrition facilities with WASH Minimum package for functional Water supply, sanitation system and hygiene promotion, waste management and disinfection of surfaces with HTH (Stabilization centers, OTP, TSFP), market places, densely populated areas, garbage sites etc. Access to sanitation facilities for IDPs, densely populated areas- markets. Access to safe, adequate water (IDPs, Returnee’s areas, hosting communities)
