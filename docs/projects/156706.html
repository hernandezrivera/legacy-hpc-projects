Project Summary

The aims of project To improve access to quality education for learners, educators, and school staff through creating safe learning environment, access to quality education and building the capacity of the educational staff, rehabilitation of classrooms ensure safe and protective learning spaces (including provision of WASH, school furniture and other amenities),
Train teachers and education personnel in child centered, inclusive, gender sensitive, protective pedagogy and psychosocial support.
The project is related to HRP 2019
through enable both males and females, girls and boys to obtain training and education services.
Specific cultural information and social norms will be considered throughout project implementation.
Through the rehabilitation, RF will take into consideration the children with disabilities, by providing ramps and proper toilets. Also, make separate toilets for boys and girls.
The project will target 12 schools, 8250 students (4125 male, 4125 female) and 450 teachers , education personal (225 male, 225 female).

Needs

The Syrian crisis started its eighth year with no political solution in the horizon. Children and adolescents suffer a lot from frequent displacement and being homeless. They are at risk of facing abuse and exploitation and suffer from trauma and psychological complications. Early marriage among girls has increased due to economic deterioration and lack of safe access to education; while adolescent boys are at risk of recruitment by armed groups. The recent change of areas of influence and front lines caused many displacements to northern rural of Aleppo. These areas require many services; access to education is a major oneSchools status different from district to another. Many reasons led to non-functionality of schools in northern of Syria. Beside the security and bombardment, there are fund unavailability, teaching staﬀ unavailability, usage as shelter, distance from the locality, …etc. Regardless the reason, the schools’ non-functionality is affecting significantly on drop-out rates, leaving the functional ones overcrowded and decrease the quality of the services provided.The migration of qualified teacher is getting bigger and bigger due to the hard living conditions and high risk situation, while remaining teachers suffer from psychological stress. Many teachers have worked voluntarily for long times, while some of them received little incentives from different organizations supporting education. To fill in the gap, many of the current teachers are fresh graduates, or university students. All these reasons combined led to reduction of the quality of education.According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019, with the crisis entering its eighth year, 5.82 million children andyouth from pre school to secondary school age (in and out of school).and an additional 270,000 education personnel, are in need of education assistance inside Syria. An estimated 1.75million children or almost one third of school age children (aged 5 17 years) from the school are out of school. A further 1.35 million are at risk of dropping out.The formal education system has lost a total of 150,000 education personnel, including teachers, negatively affecting the quality of education for all children.The conflict has seen one in three schools damaged, destroyed, or otherwise made inaccessible due to occupation by displaced families or military/armed groups.As a consequence, many children have missed out on years of educationIn the context of a humanitarian crisis situations have different impacts on girls and boys access to services and resources is different for each they face different risks and, accordingly, may experience different vulnerabilities, relationships, roles and responsibilities. Therefore there is a need to include gender sensitive approach for the implemented projects as equal access to the offered services through safe access of WASH facilities which meet the needs of men, women, boys and girls; to assess the needs in the community to ensure appropriate ways to govern girls and boys individual risk decrease.

Implementation of activities

The implementation of the project shall be carried out through the following activities: - doing deep needs assessment in targeted areas in which we will determine the schools most in need of minor rehabilitation, then we come to procurement stage in which we will receive quotations and purchase the supplies and consumables of the project (rehabilitation supplies, stoves and pipelines, fuel/firewood, stationery, school bags ......etc)- the rehabilitation of damaged classrooms during two months (October and November 2019)- fuel / firewood suppliers will be contracted as well in order to provide the fuel/ firewood to the schools on a regular basis for Nov and Dec 2019. - access to quality non formal education- Professional development opportunity for teachers and school staff. The implementation of project will be conduced through RF offices in Azzaz and Afrin with collaboration of the local councils, the Education Directorate and coordination with the working organizations in the area. Child protection and wellbeing, inclusion of people with disabilities and gender sensitivity are cross cutting components that guide the work of RF through the programs. The projects aim equal amount of female and male beneficiaries in the activities and are sensitive towards the gender equal balance of the teachers, support and management staff, to include female and male. Professional development for teachers will also support inclusive classrooms and protection components for girls and boys. RF’s ongoing projects in Syria include teacher professional development, which includes components of child and teacher wellbeing. Teachers are supported to recognize student needs and how to address them and when to refer on to experts. Teachers also learn methods of inclusive classrooms.

Protection Risk Analysis

In order to follow the do no harm principle the designing and implementing of the activities of this project promote collaboration and solidarity and avoid any predicted tension. Further provision of education and psychosocial services to the community will support beneficiaries to deal with the impact of conflict trauma and stress. It will build resilience within the communities and with the training to be included, whether around contingency/security planning or emergency drills, should improve the community engagement around self reliance. The input of further functional educational facilities will make perceivable progress in the strife against the recruitment of young people to armed groups and early child marriage. It has long been a development indicator in the humanitarian community that young female students kept in school have a lower chance of being married at an early age. The longer educational opportunities can be provided, especially in a country such as Syria with such a strong and balanced educational background, the less likely both recruitment to armed groups and child marriage/ labor take place. Further to ensure that community relations are not harmed but the most vulnerable and needy are targeted and protection principles as child protection and child safeguarding are upheld in the projects.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security concerns in the targeted locations may affect the movement of staff or implementing the project activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures:RF being a local organization having on ground activities has good level of acceptability by the local communities. However, in case any unexpected violence or security situation arises in the area, RF will adhere to security policies for field level operations. Also, at the time of induction of staff, RF will give priority to candidates with required skills who are locals of the area and understand local level sensitivities. In addition, at the time of induction, the project staff will be given an orientation on security protocols to follow during project implementation.Protection risk:The service delivery may not reach persons with specific needs and their families.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures:RF will ensure that specific needs and age related needs and constraints will be taken into account in the assessment of and support to individuals and families, ensuring equal and impartial access to the services provided. The wider community will also be engaged through participatory analysis, awareness raising and information sessions, which will aim at changing perceptions on persons with specific needs with a specific focus on promoting more protective environments for the target group. These activities are therefore fully focused on improving self protection capacities of persons with specific needs and their caregivers, as well as on promoting an improved inclusion of this collective in community based protection mechanisms.Protection risk:GBV, sexual abuse or facilities are not disability sensitive in the rehabilitated schools facilities.Likelihood: Low. Impact: HighMitigating measures:RF will ensure that once the project is implemented, each school facility will at least consist of:• Adequate amount of easy to maintain lockable latrines (special units for boys, girl and teachers);• Adequate amount of hand washing facilities near the latrines and inside the school• A well constructed and well maintained school building that protects pupils during classroom time;• Ramps and handrails for the disabled.
