Project Summary

This proposal is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country 2022 HRP strategy under objective 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL), while duty-bearers are increasingly held to account and objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. Furthermore, this project is aligned to the WASH cluster HRP Objective 1: In accordance with international humanitarian law (IHL) and international human rights law (IHRL), Palestinians living under occupation access safe and adequate WASH services with dignity and objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinian.
The overall objective is to contribute to strengthening the capacity of essential WASH services for the vulnerable population threatened by the ongoing Covid-19 crisis in the West Bank, while the specific objective of the project is to provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for Deir al 'Asal al Fauqa vulnerable village located in area C of Dura community in the West Bank to mitigate the insufficient access to WASH services that impacted women, children, girls and boys. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and Oxfam's recent assessments.

1 - Needs

In the West Bank, the restrictive planning system enforced by the Israeli authorities in Area C and East Jerusalem prevents development, and even routine repairs and maintenance of water structures in OPT, leaving the West Bank with inadequate WASH infrastructure (wells, cisterns, pipelines, etc.). More than 620,000 Palestinians in the West Bank reside in areas that are not connected to piped water services or are poorly supplied; consequently, they consume less than 50 litres of water per capita per day which is below the 100 litres recommended by WHO. This has undermined the hygiene practices of those families and the living conditions of families depending on herding and agricultural livelihoods, primarily in Area C. More than 100 communities in Area C still rely on rainwater harvesting cisterns and trucked water to satisfy their water needs, high water prices that can reach more than 30 ILS/m3, and water quality issues. Regarding access to sanitation services, nearly 250,000 HHs in the West Bank are not connected to any sewer system, and almost 10,000 HHs are discharging their sewage in adjacent streams and open areas, creating health and environmental hazards to downstream communities. The COVID -19 outbreak and its associated implications led to the erosion of WASH coping mechanisms by vulnerable households, alongside the adoption of negative practices, such as reducing water consumption and consuming water from unsafe sources. Based on the needs highlighted above, Oxfam's WASH strategy 2021-2025, WASH cluster Vulnerability Map updated in August 2021 and coordination efforts with PWA, the WASH cluster, AAH as Bethlehem and Hebron Governorates areas focal point and Oxfam's local partner ESDC, it was agreed to prioritise Deir al 'Asal al Fauqa vulnerable village under this intervention. Accordingly, a rapid assessment was conducted by Oxfam and its local partner ESDC during the 3rd week of September 2021 to identify and validate the specific needs of the village, where two FGDs and 2 KII were carried out in the targeted community with the participation of 18 persons (6 women and 12 men). The assessment revealed that more than 30% are classified below the poverty line, 25% are not connected with the water network, and the water price is 18 ILS/m3. The average water consumption is less than 30 L/c/d. People depend on cesspits due to the lack of a sewage network as the installation of sewerage networks is faced with great difficulties and restrictions by the occupation and the land topography. Moreover, participants confirmed that most of the current absorption pits are opened, detartrated and constructed randomly without considering any health or safety concerns. The assessment also found that the majority of the population have poor hygiene and safety practices which increases the risk of COVID-19 infection. Moreover, the assessment indicated that women are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sanitation services, reporting serious concerns about their children's hygiene, health and safety. WASH needs were prioritised to improve adequate water and sanitation services, including proper sewage disposal and water network through sustainable, environmental and low-cost solutions addressing the targeted community's current need.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to the needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries in the targeted area, including children, the elderly and people with disabilities. The construction of transmission pipelines and extension of water networks and HH connections planned to be implemented will improve access of targeted beneficiaries to sufficient quantity of water needed for cooking, personal and domestic hygiene and will alleviate the burden on women & children. In contrast, the construction of the cesspits will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children and mitigate the transmission of the COVID-19 outbreak. The cesspits will be implemented through a low-cost solution for solving current problems happening due to use of traditional cesspit, which is the improved cesspit system, as this solution treats wastewater and flushes it into the deep soil safely. The complementary hygiene promotion & public awareness campaigns will enhance gender & age responsiveness by enhancing women's knowledge & awareness. While developing the policy brief and animation video inspired by the World Bank's Area C and the Future of the Palestinian Economy report showcasing how the WASH sector would have been advanced sans the Israeli restrictions on the area. This, will contribute to promoting access to safe and adequate WASH services with dignity in Area C. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle. This is particularly important in the area C context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e. discrimination and exploitation, and child protection), will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, refreshed if the context changes. Oxfam will be responsible for implementing the action in partnership with ESDC and collaborating with the relevant stakeholders, including municipal villages councils, for ensuring access to systems, procedures, and standards to achieve effective, high-quality delivery. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam's humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the 'do no harm approach' ensuring that project activities or any resulting consequences do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available, and by conducting ongoing monitoring and collecting beneficiaries' feedback to be aware of any potential harm and work on prevention measures. The action responds to Oxfam's and PWA's needs analysis where women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Community mobilisation sessions on gender, water, and sanitation will be implemented to encourage the participation of men and women community leaders and mentors. These sessions will also serve as an awareness-raising tool to sensitise households and the community to accept women participation. This will contribute to mitigating any risk/ harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys, are addressed in line with Oxfam's gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action's response is grounded on the fact that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, including people with disabilities, youth, children and elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Key humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle, particularly important in Area C context, where social and political instability is evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at the community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured by conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided and safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence and coping with stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women's self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam's humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieve equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the intervention's vulnerability and need. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents an equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration, meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, using virtual platforms for information sharing, and actual work in the field will also work to address any obstacles related to the spread of COVID 19 that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people's concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles of access, whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention and would inform the response to ensure equality and impartiality.
