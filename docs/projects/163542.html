Project Summary

PUI proposes an action aiming at alleviating the suffering of the people of concern in the region. Built on key principles of resilience, the proposed activities improved thanks to the lessons learned from Phase I, are designed to:
• Respond to the most urgent needs of the affected population by supporting food production and income generation.
• Increase resilience by restoring and protecting agricultural livelihoods, while ensuring a sustainable management of the natural resources they rely on.

Besoins / Needs

The fight against Boko Haram has exacerbated the already delicate economic situation for the four million inhabitants of this region, which was considered one of the poorest areas of the country before the outbreak of the crisis. The Far-North region is not only suffering from the Lake Chad Basin crisis but also from structural problems that have hindered its economic and social development for years such as the lack of investments and the harsh impact of climate change, fuelling the deterioration of the ongoing crisis. In the Far-North, the proportion of households having inadequate diets is four times higher nowadays compared to 2007 results (from 10% in 2007 to 42% in 2019) with around 2 million people suffering from food insecurity in the Far North Region (OCHA, Term 4, 2019). 64% of the people in need are from the Far North while they represent only 41% of the total Cameroonian population. Since 2013, food insecurity levels have doubled, along with the number of displaced people. Although the situation is worsening in the whole country, the specificity of the location of the Far North Region with a mixture of climate change difficulties (droughts, change in seasons) as well as men-made violence makes the population extremely vulnerable to shocks. The crisis has led to changes in food sources from self-care modes (own production, purchase at the market, etc.) to dependency towards assistance due to the loss of means of production generated.The assessment of the strategies adopted by the households to access food during time of food shortage was based on the reduced coping strategies index (rCSI) method. This score measures the risk of developing harmful coping strategies such as prostitution, theft, begging, skipping meals, consumption of seeds, sale of cattle. It is based on a shortlist of 5 food-related coping strategies applied during the 7 days prior to the survey, and the same severity weights. It is seen as relevant to indicate the level of food insecurity of a household. Calculated at baseline and PDM stages of the ongoing Phase I of the project (October 2019), baseline survey results gave an initial picture of the situation of the beneficiaries, before intervention. In the present case, it reveals that among the beneficiaries of cash transfers, displaced persons represent the category with the most households reporting having adopted more than one negative adjustment strategy (23%). This justifies the RRM based approach (Component 2 – yet to be monitored).As of the beneficiaries of agricultural activities, the majority (68%) of beneficiaries are in “light” situation. This paves the way for the designed resilience activities. Still, this population is at risk and poverty alleviation solutions such as Cash will be proposed to support mainly the 32% of the beneficiaries (hosts 12% and IDPs 20%) that are either “crisis” or under “stress” situation. In order to better understand beneficiaries’ situation, a cross-referenced analysis between the FCS and the RCSI was made. It reveals that households with the poorest FCS develop the most negative coping strategies when they cannot meet their food needs (40%), which will further increase their level of food insecurity. Already after the first disbursement, the first PDM (November 2019), which aims to monitored the immediate impact of the project indicates that, overall, a large proportion of beneficiaries have significantly migrated from the crisis situation to the “light” situation. In fact, 57% of the households surveyed (Components 1 and 3) had a "light" rCSI at the Baseline compared to 81% at the PDM. This shows an improvement for 36% of the households targeted. This highlight the immediate tremendous effect of Cash on the reduction of negative coping strategies within a large proportion of the targeted population.
