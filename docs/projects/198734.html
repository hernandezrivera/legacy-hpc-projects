Project Summary

The North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon are affected by a complex humanitarian crisis that has resulted in a system-wide breakdown of infrastructure and services, especially in the health sector. This crisis has resulted in the displacement of over 1.1 million people, especially women and children1. Data from the MSNA conducted in August 2022, revealed that 202 (18%) out of 1115 health centers were no longer functional. Most of the functional health facilities are not functional to full capacity. Attacks on healthcare are a common feature of this crisis and remain a major threat to the availability of healthcare in the North-West and the South-West regions. There are limited specialized services to take care of victims of physical and mental trauma in these regions as the number of cases has been on the rise because of the crisis. This situation has been worsened by the numerous outbreaks (Cholera, Covid-19, Monkey Pox) that have increased the burden on a health system that has already been fragilized by the humanitarian crisis. At least 10 health districts (Ekondo Titi, Mbonge, Bakassi, Limbe, Tiko, Buea, Muyuka, Kumba North, Kumba South, and Mundemba) reported cases of cholera this year. Mbonge, Tombel and Kumba South health districts reported cases of Monkeypox this year.
In some of the most affected areas, health services are not available while less affected areas have health services of poor quality. This project aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of internally displaced populations (IDP) and affected host communities in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon affected by the humanitarian crisis by ensuring timely and equitable access to healthcare services. The project will target 373,069 IDPs persons (51% of this target are women, 16% are children under 5, and 11% are people over 60 years old). The areas of intervention will be Fako, Meme, Lebialem, Ndian, Manyu and Kupe - Muanenguba divisions in the South-West region and Mezam, Momo, Bui, Ngo-ketunjia, Mechum, Donga Mantung and Boyo in the North-West region. The project will emphasize on timely access to equitable healthcare through mobile clinics in areas with no functional health facilities and the use of vouchers in areas where health facilities are operational.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The situation in the North-West region and South-West regions has remained volatile and unpredictable for the past 6 years since the onset of the crisis. The combined effect of COVID-19, the cholera outbreak, the measles outbreak, and the crisis has had a huge impact on the fragile health system. Access to essential health services remains a challenge to most of the affected communities due to the complete or partial shutdown of health facilities, the distance to the closest functional health facility, or the inability to pay the cost of health care. The Multisectoral Needs Assessment carried out by IOM in August 2022 showed that 18% of health facilities are closed in both regions and over 368,742 people were still displaced from their homes. The persistence of insecurity is causing additional displacements in these regions. There is an urgent need to improve epidemiological surveillance in these regions as the risk of outbreaks of diseases like Cholera, Measles, yellow fever and Monkey Pox remain high due to the poor living conditions of affected populations and poor vaccination coverage for routine vaccines. It is important to strengthen the capacity of the health districts to carry out response activities during outbreaks and community-based surveillance for early warning of public health events. Persons living with disabilities, pregnant women, and teenagers are the most vulnerable and hence they need assistance to access quality health services. Given that most of the affected populations are living under poor conditions, they are exposed to many pathogens that increase the likelihood of illnesses. The brutal nature of the crisis has resulted in an increase in the incidence of trauma cases as a result of violence. Also, a good number of people are traumatized mentally and need mental health assistance. The project will have a mental health component to take care of the mental health needs of the affected populations.
