Project Summary

The School meals programme was introduced as part of WFP’s Syria crisis response in 2014 to meet basic food security needs of girls and boys particularly in areas hosting large number of affected populations with low food security and education indicators.
The programme includes:
- In kind food distribution including daily distribution of fresh meals or fortified snacks to children in primary schools ( grades 1-6) during school days
- electronic vouchers to students who were out of school and have returned to education and are enrolled in curriculum B ( 8-14 years old). The provision of the electronic voucher is conditional to students achieving 80% monthly attendance.
School meals activities covers all children registered in the targeted schools including children with disability attending those schools. The Activities are aligned with the timeframe of the school year, thus, they start in September and ends by May of the next year. Distribution of snacks may continue in the summer to support last chance camps.
The school meals programme aims to restore and stabilize access to education in crisis- affected areas as well as to enhance health and nutrition status of children in targeted areas. By encouraging regular attendance and enrollment, the programme also contributes to providing protection to children from reverting to negative coping mechanism such as child labor and child marriage. Therefore, the programme meets the first obhective of the education sector “Increased safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children”
The programme targets school-aged children enrolled in basic education and is implemented jointly with the Ministry of Education and other partners of the education sector in Syria including UN agencies and National NGOs. with the increase of needs in terms of food security and education severity, the project will target 1150000 students this year with the same 3 modalities ( in kind : fortified snacks and fresh meals , and Cash transfer).
WFP will continue to reach the targeted 1,150,000 children per month but less frequently due to the current circumstances that lead to closure of schools and lead to less requirement in terms of funding.
The programme does not offer universal coverage to all to all basic education students. In case of lower funding, priority will be given to location where severity scale of food security and education is more than

4 - Needs

According to the 2020 Education sector needs and severity scale analysis, 6,8 million children are in need of education assistance and more than one in three schools were damaged, destroyed, or otherwise made inaccessible. As a result, the education system inside Syria became overburdened and overstretched to the extent that schools and learning environments became unsafe, overcrowded and under-resourced. The key impediments to children accessing education included safety and security along routes to and from schools, poor facilities at schools and quality of education. This had negatively impacted enrolment and retention rates. On the other hand, insufficient food at home negatively affected children’s attentiveness and cognitive abilities. Evidence suggests that, due to severe poverty and a lack of awareness, parents and caregivers no longer consider their child’s education as a priority. Many children remain out of school to support their households . According to 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview, up to 2.1 million children and youth are out of school and 1.3 million at risk of dropping out. Nine years into the crisis, 6.5 million people in Syria were food insecure, while a further 2.5million people are at risk of becoming acutely food insecure. In total, this constitutes over half of the Syrian population who are in need of urgent life-saving and life sustaining food, agriculture and livelihoods assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

a) In-kind distribution- fortified snacks, the plan for the in kind is to target 1,000,000 children with the in-kind programme: 1- Discussion with MoE and Field offices to select schools based on low food security and education indicators (resources: food security assessment, Education needs assessments, MoE and local community). 2- To minimise creating pull factor to those schools, all primary schools in the community are targeted. 3- WFP send monthly dispatches of fortified snacks to targeted schools 4- daily distribution of fortified snacks to primary school children in targeted schools 5- Monitor the implementation of the activity by conduct regular monitoring visits by WFP. monitoring staff and using school meals monitoring checklist Each pack of date bar provides 344 KCAL ( 21% of Child's daily need) and contains :iron, zinc, copper and Vit (A,B1,B2, B3, B6, B9, B12,C, D, E)Fortified snacks are locally produced according to the quality standards of WFP. Quality of the product is continuously monitored by the Food Security and Quality section in WFP country office and undergoes internal and external quality and safety testing. b) In-kind distribution- fresh meals, the plan for the fresh meals is 50,000 children: 1- Selection of priority areas mostly in areas of return or high vulnerability to support stabilising return to education and providing nutritious food to children. 2-Support cooperating partners to establish healthy kitchens 3- Daily preparation of fresh meals by cooperating partners and distribution of the meals to the targeted schools 4-Monitoring of the hygiene and safety measures in the kitchens and of the distribution of meals to schools using school meals monitoring checklist c) Electronic Vouchers (cash transfer): the planned number of children is 100,000 children, which represents almost 80,000 households as some families have more than one child enrolled in curriculum B:
