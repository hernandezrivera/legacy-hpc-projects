Project Summary

This project will contribute to the strategic objectives of the 2020 HRP and all three of the FSL cluster objectives through the support of CARE donors and FSL partners to support improved livelihoods during emergency, early recovery, and resilience building of 121,730 (20,288HH) vulnerable women, men, boys and girls by improving food access, consumption and dietary diversity. This is expected to increase agricultural production, protect and build productive assets and restore livelihoods. CARE will use both in-kind, cash and voucher modalities to deliver the much needed assistance to the vulnerable households affected by conflict and natural disasters.
CARE will promote positive coping mechanisms among the most vulnerable beneficiaries including returnees, IDPs and host communities. Targeted vulnerable beneficiaries (IPC phases 3, 4 and 5) will identified, mobilized, assessed and verified based on a widely consulted agreed upon selection criteria for support through appropriate cash / food vouchers. For cash and voucher assistance, there will be a rapid market assessments conducted to determine the feasibility of the cash and voucher assistance (CVA).
In all the project sites CARE will organize community mobilization and consultation activities where households will be sensitized on expected project outputs and beneficiary entitlements. There will also be context specific messaging on proper utilization of livelihood inputs (for farmers and fisher folks), proper nutrition, hygiene and protection (for lactating / pregnant women and for children).
CARE will also provide season appropriate emergency livelihood inputs (crop, vegetable and fishing kits) after providing appropriate training and guidance.
CARE will also promote, through consultation and collaboration during community based participatory planning process with the targeted communities, construction of community assets using the food/cash for work approach where appropriate. These activities will aim at improving livelihoods at household and community levels, as well as building longer term community resilience. Special attention will be given to female headed households in providing support for agricultural inputs, protecting and building productive assets and restoration of livelihoods.

1 - Needs

As at August 2019, about 6.35 million people (54% of total population) have been estimated to be severely food insecure across South Sudan (South Sudan IPC, Sept 2019). This has been attributed to persistent conflict, disruption of livelihoods and agricultural activities, poor economic conditions and challenges faced in the delivery of humanitarian assistance during the peak of the lean season (June – August), when food shortages are typically at their worst levels. During the last quarter of 2019 (Sept to Dec), the situation in the country is projected to slightly improve by 15% due to the farmers’ own harvest of cereals (maize, sorghum & millet) and oil crops (groundnuts & sesame), however, this will not be sustained as the projection in the first quarter of 2020 (Jan – April) is expected to deteriorate with more populations moving back to IPC phases 3, 4 & 5 (47% of the population). According to the August IPC report, the proportion of the population facing acute food insecurity in the target states are Unity State (62%), Western Bahr El Ghazal (68%), Jonglei (65%), Eastern Equatoria (53%) and Western Equatoria (31%). Compared to the same lean season last year, the food security situation has slightly improved. This is attributed to the slight improvement in the security situation, which allows free movement of people, goods, and livestock. Although the rains this year started late in most of the areas, the harvest of maize and sorghum is expected in Sept and October, which will further improve the food security situation between Sept and Dec 2019 (IPC phase 3). However, the situation between Jan and April is expected to deteriorate with more populations slipping back into IPC phases 3 and 4. The ongoing dialogues regarding the implementation of the peace agreement (R-ARCSS) and related relative stability and reduced fighting contribute to a conducive environment for households’ own food production and the adoption of positive livelihood coping strategies. This, therefore, calls for increased support to households planning to cultivate through timely provision of quality emergency livelihood inputs and extension services, which are currently limited.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project will target both IDPs, returnees and host communities. The selection criteria will be developed, reviewed and endorsed by a targeting committee, which comprises of representatives from the communities. Beneficiaries selection will be based on household vulnerability levels. CARE, and local stakeholders will develop, discuss and approve standardized criteria for beneficiaries’ selection.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will be implemented by CARE in collaboration with the National, State and County authorities. CARE will work in coordination with the community leaders, community structures, line State Ministries of (Agriculture, Public Work, and Education), Food Security and Livelihoods sub-clusters to collectively identify and target beneficiaries and project sites according to the acceptable criteria. Community participation is considered critical in achieving project objectives, creating ownership and sustainability, and reaching a wide range of beneficiaries. CARE will provide all the required technical assistance to government and community partner organizations through capacity building training and technical support.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Although the safety and stability of the protection environment varies from one location to another, the expected implementation of the R-ARCSS is expected to bring calm and stability in most of the areas in South Sudan. This will create a conducive environment for medium and longer-term programming, providing opportunities for linking emergencies will resilience programming.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

CARE will ensure that project exit will be conducted in a responsible and transparent manner. Project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be informed in advance of the planned project closure so as to prepare them for the transition. Furthermore CARE will use the outputs from the feedback mechanisms to get the views of men, women boys and girls regarding the project closure and will act in a timely manner to address any concerns raised by the community and stakeholders. For resilience programs that involve assets creation, the community assets created are usually identified by the communities and therefore belong to the communities themselves. In the event of project completion, CARE plans to handover all assets created and or maintained to the community leaders and through the line ministries concerned. CARE will develop joint phasing out or sustainability strategy with identified partners and stakeholders. The project will consider some of following priority conditions to ensure sustainability:• Linkage to line ministries for follow-up monitoring of future situation. • Collection and sharing of appropriate data to determine if established closure criteria have been fulfilled. • Capacity building of personnel from local government structures through appropriate training to assure close monitoring of the activities. Great lengths will be taken to build the capacity of those involved in agriculture extension service delivery and vocational skills trainers.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?
