Project Summary

Support to the public health system with a special focus on Mental Health, by means of supporting the PHCs camps and host communities (targeting both IDPs and Returnees).
The project will focus on the provision of Mental Health services for IDPs and Returnees in Iraq, with different activities according to the specific needs of the locations and the populations targeted.
Capacity Building (mhGAP, PFA and other standard protocols) for DOH staff working inside and outside of IDPs camps; direct provision of MHPSS services integrated with basic Reproductive Health services for women and adolescent girls. MH service provision covering all layers of the IASC MHPSS pyramid (including psychiatric consultations) in PHCs in IDPs camps and outside of camps, with a strong capacity-building component for public health system (DOH medical and non-medical staff).
Reproductive Health services include: general Gynecologic visits, ANC, PNC, Ultrasound (provided by Ultrasound specialist), family planning, provision of medicine and brith control, awareness on reproductive health topics (ex. uterus cancer, nutrition for new borns, tetanus vaccine etc), referral for pregnancies at risk and emergencies.

Needs

The mental health sector is currently struggling to keep up with the needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities. Needs and gaps of the MHPSS sector in the targeted locations include:- lack of well-trained clinical psychologists and non-specialized mental health (MH) care providers, - gaps at capacity building level for health practitioners, - weak and fragmented public structure for MH services provision for outpatients, - ineffective referral system & pathways for MH cases, - increase of MHPSS cases in UPP managed centres in the past 9 months, - high rates of suicides.Overall, the Iraqi public health system requires strengthening of its MH sector at all levels, from the capacity building of psychologists and psychiatrists, to the re-structuring of the service provision: a way to ease the burden on the public hospitals is that of carrying out low-intensity MHPSS interventions (layers 2 and 3 of the IASC MHPSS pyramid) at PHC level, while only psychiatric support is provided at hospital level. Working on the strengthening of the PHCs in the governorates that have been mostly affected by the high number of IDPs, and in those where returns have started is even more essential and needed. An integrated approach which covers also Reproductive Health is particularly efficient, due to the high need of gynaecological consultations needed.

Activities

First Component: MHPSS services provision both inside and outside IDP camps. In particular, the Mental Health component, considered here as the specialized and non-specialized support (IASC layers 3 and 4, provided from psychiatrists and clinical psychologists) is supported in Primary Health Centre (PHCs) inside the camps to PHCs in the urban areas. In those PHCs, clinical care is delivered by psychiatrists while clinical psychologists deliver clinical counseling and clinical support trough evidence based practice for patients ascribed in the internationally recognized diagnostic systems or in need of MH for stress or trauma related reasons. The Psycho Social Support components (community workshops and events, resilience groups, self-help plus) will be delivered in the centres, acting as low intensity interventions to support vulnerable families and categories, but also as reliable identification system and referral pathway for those cases in need of further support of higher levels of mental health care (from primary to secondary).Reproductive Health services: general Gynecologic visits, ANC, PNC, Ultrasound (provided by Ultrasound specialist), family planning, provision of medicine and brith control, awareness on reproductive health topics (ex. uterus cancer, nutrition for new borns, tetanus vaccine etc), referral for pregnancies at risk and emergencies. Second Component: Capacity Building. Namely: Health staff of the Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCs) as well as educators of the Schools are trained by UPP staff in early recognition, identification, and appropriate referral to UPP facilities (upon approval of the Kurdish Authorities); Strengthening the collaboration across institutions providing basic needs to the PoCs (health and education) as well as data management in terms of confidentiality will be integral parts of the aforementioned trainings; Training on mhGAP (version 2.0, as required from DoH and MoH); Training on clinical counseling, evidenced based practices and case management for the treatment of the most common MH psychopathological diseases; Training for crisis intervention and suicidal cases management. SelfHelp+ has been piloted by UPP during all 2018 upon agreement with WHO and for 2019 the continuation of the pilot could be considered with WHO and other relevant stakeholders.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Ineffective external referral system to Psychiatric Hospitals 2. Shortage of Psychiatrists to be contracted by UPP, related to the limited availability of such professional cadre3. Deterioration of security conditions in the areas where the project operates

Mitigation Measures

1. Ineffective external referral system to Psychiatric Hospitals: Contract a Mental Health Focal Point, approved by the DoH, in order to establish a legal operational framework for external referrals; Constantly inform the DoH on admissions to / rejections from the Psychiatric Hospital, in order to provide the DoH with the evidence from the field.2. Shortage of Psychiatrists to be contracted by UPP, related to the limited availability of such professional cadre: Extend the collaboration of the Psychiatrists throughout 2018, after obtaining the written approval for collaboration from MoH and DoH; in case of need, involve the DoH in promoting UPP vacancies.3. Deterioration of security conditions in the areas where the project operates: arrange provision of larger quantities of psychotropic medications (directly with the Psychiatrists or with the remote supervision of the psychiatrists, in case of extreme circumstances) in order to overcome protracted interruption of psychiatric services; identify secure locations where provision of psychotropic medications might occur safely (i.e. Erbil Psychiatric Hospital); constantly monitor the security situation through UPP security focal point as well as BCF, and re-activate the services as soon as the security threats mitigate. Security and Safety risks: All UPP staff accessing the project locations in Ninewa Governorate is trained in VEPT (Violent Environment Awerness Training) and in mine/IED awareness. All UPP security manager and security Focal Points assess daily the security situation in the project location and decide whether activities can be implemented regularly or if any contingency plan must be activated. Personal Safety risks in project locations: All project locations are equipped with first aid kits and a First Aid focal point specifically trained in advanced first aid and first aid management is appointed in each UPP field office. Road Safety risks: all UPP drivers are regularly trained in Drivers Safety and Security and their performance and respect of the rules is monitored by both the fleet manager and the security focal points

How is your project accountable to affected population

This project aims to continue addressing the needs for MHPSS service of IDPs, returnees and host communities, while strengthening the capacity of the public health system and the structure of referrals from the PHCs in IDPs camps to the DOH health facilities (PHCs and Hospitals). Moreover, this progressive strengthening of the local public health system in MHPSS field contributes also to fight stigma regarding mental health that is still an obstacle. Feedbacks will be used to improve the quality of the activities and put into place corrective measures, if needed. Focused group discussions with women and girls will be conducted to involve more beneficiaries into the implementation of the project and the adaptation of activities in the light of lesson learned. Learnings will be documented and part of the monitoring and evaluation plan of the intervention. UPP staff will be trained on PSEA, Anti-fraud and Anti-corruption policies, and UPP Standards of Conduct and will be closely monitored during the implementation of the project.Other mechanisms used include: IDP Call Center, RPAs, monitoring missions, CwC and RNAs.
