Project Summary

The Darfur region of Sudan faces acute humanitarian needs gap, due to protracted conflict, inter-tribal tensions, and more recently with the influx of South Sudanese refugees in the eastern localities. The Darfur conflict has continued to displace populations, which has caused many households to be food insecurity category (phase3) the humanitarian situation exacerbated by harsh climatic conditions, with limited rainfall that negatively affects household food production. The population of North Darfur is constrained by a harsh environment, low access to services, protracted conflict, sporadic inter-clan violence, and an influx of refugees from South Sudan.
Korma Administrative Unit (AU) is located West of El Fasher 79 km. The total population is 90034 individuals(18,007HH). It inhabited by several tribes: Fur, Arabs, ,Zaghawa, Tunjur, Berty, and others minorities. The main activities of the people are Agriculture, Trade and Animal breeding. Korma Administrative Unit (AU) is the most affected areas by Darfur crisis in 2004 and continuous insecurity situation for along time. There is a huge gap in many service special in WASH, Health, Nutrition ,Education, protection, emergency food (GFD) and livelihoods throughout 204- 2019. There is a big gap in Education that needs to be addressed in the area. ZDPO has decided intervention through the following general and specific objectives:
Project Objectives
To address Live-saving/protection concerns, Students have access to live-saving education creating peaceful and productive livelihoods opportunities and improving security in the targeted community.
Specific Objectives:
- Set safe learning spaces for returnee’s students in Korma AU.
- Construction of class rooms, a fence and latrines in Kaila Basic Co-school in the targeted community
- Construction of offices and stores to improve learning environment for the students.
- Support peaceful coexistence in the community by bringing diverse communities including nomads to share & manage a facility.
- Contribute to addressing live-saving concerns and encourage voluntary return of IDPs to their origins.
- Create sustainable livelihoods options among the community of target.
- Improve learning environment for students in the area.
Strengthen the capacity of the Parent-teacher association (PTA) in groundwork of the school needs assessment (SNA) and the school action plan (SAP). The project is targeting 7,141 students dis aggregated to 2,923 male and 4,218 female.
ZDPO will use the following methodology implementation:
- Need assessment using community approaches such as technical inspection, Focus Group Discussions, personal interviews.
- Project implementation according to the standard specification of State Ministry of Education.
- Monitoring to be done throughout the implementation
- Evaluation to be done interim, and by the end of the project
- Closure of project and hand over.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The activities in this project are include: construction of class rooms D5x9meters, offices D4,5x5meters, stores D4x5meters, kitchens D3x3m, fences 60x100m and nurseries 3x3meters. capacity building for 100 teachers in teaching methodologies and EIE including psychological support, health and hygiene, mine risk education, disaster risk reduction and child protection and PTAs on school management, child protection, psychological support and disaster risk reduction and At-Risk youth in manufacturing of solid soil bricks (SSB) and construction skills. The provision of school supplies including benches & seats, teaching aid, water tanks (Tiger), hand washing tab, water pots, jugs, soap, pupil kits and recreational kits. The project will provide livelihood opportunities for pupils with special needs e.g. restocking of goats,sheep , chicken, provision of different seeds to product seedlings for vegetables, decoration, shadow and desertification reduction. The project will also pay education fees for the poor and pupils of special needs.

ZDPO would follow the Code of Conduct for the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement. ZDPO will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of intervention which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psycho social risks. ZDPO will arrange for people’s access to assistance and services - in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. ZDPO will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. ZDPO will support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education and other needs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ZULFA’s core accountability is to affected population especially women and children who are systematically being engaged and benefiting from CERF-led humanitarian aid in Korma AU. They are part of all stages of the humanitarian response and management, distribution, ensuring they have a voice and a hand in the decisions that affect their lives. For humanitarian actors: CERF and ZULFA built a respected and transparent partnership with a great willingness to work with affected communities and especially children with special needs. In practice, this entails including beneficiaries in needs assessments, programme design, delivery, monitoring and evaluation; establishing open channels of communication for feedback and information sharing; and facilitating participatory processes for decision making and mutual learning. Doing so is not only fundamental to applying humanitarian minimum standards and principles, but also a practical means to improve the quality and effectiveness of humanitarian aid and ultimately the sustainability of aid assistance. Do No Harm: Implicit within the responsibility of humanitarian actors to be accountable to affected populations is the concept of “Do No Harm”; that is, ensuring that aid programmes do not in any way further harm or negatively affect the children and women. Doing so is a fundamental element of providing humanitarian aid with respect and in meaningful consideration of affected children and women. ZULFA commitments to accountability to affected children and women by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into response strategies, supplies distribution, monitoring and evaluations, and in reporting. ZULFA will provide accessible and timely information to affected children and women on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected children and women over information provision.ZULFA and CERF Actively seek the views of affected children and women to improve response practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to, learn from) complaints.ZULFA and CERF enable affected children and women to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence in humanitarian response.
