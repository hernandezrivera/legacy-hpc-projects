Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, Secours Islamique France (SIF) will directly contribute to the Shelter Sector’s objectives of Syria by supporting the sustainable rehabilitation of housing and related public infrastructures in Rural Damascus, Dara’a, Hama, Homs, Al- Hassakeh, Dier- Ez-zor and Aleppo Governorates. SIF is in coordination with local authorities to gain access to Al-Hassakeh. The sub districts in these governorates are selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Syria and SIF’s field assessments. After a comprehensive needs assessment, consultations with the local community, related authorities and taking into account the sector priorities, SIF’s proposed interventions are:
(1) Rehabilitation and repair of damaged houses and (2) Provision and Installation of doors and windows (Shelter Package). A total of 4,000 beneficiaries will be reached through the proposed intervention. SIF meal team will conduct an assessment to establish which type of assistance will be provided to the beneficiaries, according to their needs and vulnerability.
SIF will continue to adopt protection mainstreaming approach in the design and implementation of this project. As part of the proposed action, SIF will consider threats of physical attacks and landmines in the selection of the locations, ensure clarity on HLP ownership of private houses in order to avoid eviction as well as involve local authorities in site planning and selection to avoid issues and ensure consent and participation from municipalities, governorates and the MoLAE. Specific needs will be addressed through including door locks and lighting, especially in WASH facilities, to better protect women and girls, particularly single women and female-headed households. SIF will also ensure that infrastructures are accessible to persons with disabilities and older persons, and safe for small children. Housing will be secured for unaccompanied children and, whenever possible, SIF will proceed to referrals to ensure the monitoring of the situation of boys and girls by specialized agencies. SIF will take into account local capacities and encourage the contractors in recruiting workers from local communities. SIF ensures participatory approach in all project cycle phases including the planning, project design, implementation and the monitoring phase. The MEAL team will put in place a complaints and feedback mechanism.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Need Overview for Syria 5.88 million Syrian people are inneed of Shelter assistance across the country. Out of the targeted governorates Deir-Ez-Zor with 73%, Al-Hasakeh with 69 % and Aleppo with 68 % are amongst the highest proportion of PiN out of the total population. The other 3 target Governorates have seen the highest relative increase in PiN compared with 2020 with Hama +21 % Homs +17 %, and Rural Damascus +9 %. Lack of adequate and/or affordable shelter, limited long term solutions added with the new challenges for shelter that are emerging out of the Covid 19 pandemic and the ongoing armed conflict are exacerbating issues. IDPs and returnees are affected the most. SIF conducted a needs assessment in December 2020 in Rural Damascus, Dara’a, Homs, Hama and Aleppo governorates in coordination with SARC, local authorities and municipalities. The assessment reported that in many of the targeted localities private houses were severely damaged due to hostilities. Unavailability of housing had further limited returnees’ movement and early recovery in the targeted communities. Families living in buildings or houses which were severely damaged by the conflict, reported to have higher protection risks especially for vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities (PwDs). Women and girls in many of these localities faced risks of sexual exploitation and abuse as appropriate security standards could not be ensured inside the buildings (no or broken doors and windows, lack of lockable doors, etc.) while PwDs and elderly lacked suitable access to housing facilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

SIF will rely on a contractor to achieve the rehabilitation of the damaged houses. Contractor selection will be based on SIF’s Standards of Procedures (SoPs) in order to ensure transparency and accountability of the selection process. SIF will coordinate with municipalities, Ministry of Local Administration and Environment and the Syrian Arab Red Crescent to conduct assessments, select beneficiaries, obtain the necessary approvals, and issue oﬃcial acceptance certificates for hand over. A period of guarantee will also be provided to each owner and public facility in case some repairs would be needed after rehabilitation work is carried out. The project will be complementary to WASH interventions in the targeted areas which will aim at addressing needs (through need assessment) and the WASH sector’s objectives and priorities in the localities, in order to foster communities’ early recovery and resilience. The activities will be monitored on a daily basis by SIF Shelter technical team through field visits, data collection and reporting to SIF’s HQ and donors to report progress, so as to highlight areas of concerns and ensure good completion of the works. Through monthly project and budget follow-up, SIF will ensure to abide by standards of project implementation and resources are being effectively used. Participatory approach, through regular structured focus group discussions with individuals of different ages, gender and backgrounds, particularly those with specific needs on shelter issues (e.g. persons with disabilities and elderly), will ensure that any protection concerns highlighted are discussed and mitigated. The MEAL team will put in place a complaints and feedback mechanism.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Discrimination or perception of discrimination may occur if assistance is provided to a specific group/ individuals while others are excluded.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) To ensure uniform and transparent beneficiary selections procedures which are agreed among the sector members and government authorities and are clearly communicated to the community. (2) Ensure that comprehensive service mapping has taken place that risk mitigation and communication with communities are proactive and informed. (3) Ensure standardize assistance package and guideline that are followed by the sector. Risk 2: Risk of gender based violence (GBV) and sexual abuse while accessing the infrastructures. Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: (1) conduct safety audit to ensure safe access of beneficiary to Shelter infrastructures sites. (2) ensure close coordination and consultation with local communities on the accessibility of infrastructures in order to receive their inputs and understand their perspectives in respect to safety of beneficiaries. (3) Consolation with women, girls, boys and men should be held. (4) sensitizing others sectors in gender sensitive measures in Shelter Assistance (5) ensuring locks on each door to provide privacy to girls and women and reduce risk of sexual abuse. Risk 3: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner, and/or exposing beneﬁciaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated. Likelihood: medium Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) SIF will not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless an agreement is secured with the owner, and based on a proof of registered HLP ownership and veriﬁed ownership claims.
