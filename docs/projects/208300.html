Project Summary

The Anglophone Crisis continues to have devastating effects on the people of the North West and South Regions of Cameroon. Girls and boys are the primary victims of the current crisis. This translates into serious violations of their fundamental rights and constant insecurity, causing both visible and invisible trauma in the short and long-term, separation from their families and limited access to basic social services such as education, for the past four years. Being direct or indirect witnesses of violence linked to the conflict, their wellbeing, both physical and psychological, is fundamentally at stake. Through this project, UNICEF and its partners will work to protect children affected by armed conflict from violence, abuse, and exploitation through a series of interventions. These will include mental health and psychosocial support targeting children and caregivers; case management for unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children; birth registration for conflict-affected children; sensitization campaigns, with specific attention to parents to pass on messages on child protection in emergencies, trauma early warning signs for children affected by armed conflict, effects of family separation, GBV in emergencies and other types of violence children are at risk of at the community level so that parents and community members can play an active role in child protection in emergencies.
