Project Summary

The proposed project aims to strengthen the effectiveness of CUAMM's integrated
humanitarian health intervention in Cabo Delgado by
- Enhancing the comprehensive support to targeted health facilities located in priority
districts;
- Improving the delivery of quality essential health services to the most vulnerable populations and those most affected by the humanitarian crisis;
- Strengthen linkages between different levels of care: from community to secondary level;
- Improve coordination mechanisms between the different actors involved in the response through active participation in coordination fora (clusters, TwGs, etc.), reducing duplication of interventions and meeting the urgent and life-threatening needs of the most vulnerable categories.
CUAMM will adopt a human rights-based and people-centred approach, implementing tailor-made and flexible activities to respond to the most acute needs of the population.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The proposed project builds on the good results obtained thanks to the actions carried out in 2024, aiming to reinforce them and to strengthen the collaboration with local authorities in order to maximise their involvement and ownership of the action. The activities will be implemented in an unstable security and humanitarian context. The security situation in Cabo Delgado province remains volatile despite some security improvements. Over 1 million people are currently displaced due to ongoing insurgent violence, including recent attacks in districts like Chiure, Macomia, and Mecufi. As of mid-2024, an estimated 580,000 people remain internally displaced, largely due to intensified attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) earlier this year. Over 601,000 individuals have returned to their places of origin, often under government pressure, but they face dire conditions including lack of shelter, healthcare, and basic services. The violence has triggered multiple waves of displacement, with children and women representing the majority of those displaced. Civilians continue to endure widespread rights violations, including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), forced recruitment, abductions, and killings. The humanitarian crisis is exacerbated by chronic funding shortages, with only a fraction of the needed aid reaching the affected populations. Limited resources have constrained responses to provide adequate shelter, food, clean water, and psychosocial support inIn this framework of action, CUAMM adopts a conflict sensitive approach, considering its structural and social basis in designing the project's activities. The intervention proposes an integrated and comprehensive approach towards health aiming at addressing the most urgent needs of the most vulnerable categories by: - reinforcing the existing and functional health units located in the target districts overwhelmed by the IDPs influx; - supporting the delivery of life saving health services in IDPs sites; - wherever no health units are functional and operative, organize integrated health, nutrition, protection mobile teams and mobile brigades to reach people living in hard to reach areas;- improving the referral pathways, in particular for PLWs, U5 children and SGBV survivors. With the present proposal, CUAMM will reach the most vulnerable populations : around 13.800 individuals, disaggregated as follows: SADD - around 3.400 children (<18 years old); - around 9.500 adults; - around 800 elderly; - around 2.000 PwDs; - around 7.100 male and 6.600 female. Considering the status of the beneficiaries, the present intervention targets: - around 4.000 IDPs- 4.200 host communities; - 5.300 returnees. Due to the complexity of the humanitarian crisis in Cabo Delgado, CUAMM considers it fundamental to direct its activities towards all people in need, regardless of their status, paying particular attention to their level of vulnerability.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project is aligned with the objectives of the health cluster and will operate at multiple levels. - At the health facility level, Cuamm will ensure the provision of essential lifesaving services to the most vulnerable groups, including the deployment of additional health workers (in particular, in overburdened health centres situated in proximity to IDP sites), medicines, medical supplies, and other necessary resources. The direct provision of services will be complemented by capacity-building activities, with the objective of strengthening the resilience of the system.- In IDP sites: CUAMM will provide support for the reinforcement of integrated mobile brigades and, where necessary, the establishment of temporary clinics with the objective of extending the coverage and increasing the quality and quantity of health services, in particular antenatal and postnatal care, U5 vaccinations, and U5 malnutrition screening. Specific attention will be devoted to cases of gender-based violence (GBV), with the objective of ensuring prompt identification and careful management. This will entail consideration of not only physical but also psychological aspects. Patients will be provided with appropriate clinical care and subsequently referred to the nearest health centre, hospital or specialised centres for psychosocial and legal support and follow-up, as required. The activities will be carried out in close coordination with CCCM, health and protection clusters, with the objective of implementing multi-sectoral assistance to support vulnerable IDPs in prioritised collective sites.- Staff will be trained and sensitised, and advocacy will be undertaken with the aim of ensuring the continued availability of all essential medical supplies. The quality of care provided will be strengthened through technical assistance and supportive supervision, and monitored through improved data collection and analysis, with the objective of informing decision-making processes to improve the current situation. The activities will be conducted in close collaboration with the Health cluster, with the objective of avoiding the implementation of redundant interventions and focusing efforts on the areas of greatest need. Furthermore, the initiative will engage with local authorities with the aim of enhancing their involvement and commitment to addressing the needs of the most vulnerable populations.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CUAMM designs its projects in close collaboration with local authorities to ensure buy-in and active participation of local stakeholders. Project activities are carried out through participatory approaches, ensuring that community members and beneficiaries are actively involved in the development and implementation of each project components, ensuring that their needs are effectively met. Prior to theimplementation of all activities and regularly throughout the project, information meetings will be at community level and with government services to ensure support and accountability. CUAMM, in coordination with the Cluster, will promote the establishment of effective project community feedback and grievance redress mechanisms that enable beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback on CUAMM activities in a safe, non-threatening and accessible manner. Community feedback and complaints could be collected at service delivery points through focus group discussions with beneficiaries and community members. In addition, CUAMM will continue to promote access to 'Linha Verde' as an effective inter-agency complaints, feedback and information hotline, including for sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA).

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project's activities encourage meaningful access to assistance thanks to the implementation of tailored integrated interventions strenghtening both the demand side and the supply one. On the demand side, CUAMM will identify the difficulties, challenges and barriers encountered by the different groups of population in accessing assistance. Togheter with the beneficiaries and the local authorities,common, accepted and feasible meaures will be identified and put in place. In addition, community awareness activities will be carried out in collaboration with local civil society organisations and activists to develop better attitudes towards gender and disability among community members and leaders, and to ensure that women and girls are fully aware of the services available at the nearest functional and operational centres. On the supply side, CUAMM, in collaboration with the local authorities and the cluster, will contribute to facilitate the referral and follow up system for critical and complicated patients from communities to specialised services. In collaboration with local CSOs and activists, CUAMM will produce accessible IEC materials and participate to inclusive awareness campaigns promotingthe active participation of disadvantaged groups. i.e. women, girls, people with disabilities and elderly persons. Finally, considering the inequalities affecting women and elderly persons in participating to the activities, CUAMM will implement specific measures to facilitate their active involvement and engagement in the intervention.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

CUAMM analyses the barriers, constraints and difficulties encountered by various demographic groups, impeding their equal access to humanitarian services and assistance. Specific attention will be given to identifying the barriers faced by individuals with disabilities (PwDs). A protection map will be developed by gathering community feedback and conducting an assessment of other partners/institutions.This will aid in identifying vulnerable groups and situations that may pose a threat to intervention goals. The map will assist in maintaining a focused approach, preventing the squandering of resources, and prioritising key areas and issues. In carrying out the initiatives, CUAMM will embrace and uphold a human rights-based and people-centred strategy to cater to the particular requirements of individuals with disabilities, encompassing women, men, girls, boys, and the elderly.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed programme aims to minimise its impact on the environment through sensitising healthcare workers and community health workers on the safe disposal of consumables, particularly personal protective equipment. Moreover, the project will prioritise raising awareness for environmental and personal hygiene, sanitation, and prevention/control of disease outbreaks for both host and displaced communities at the community level. During the project implementation, CUAMM will promote the integration of activities that involve vehicle movements, aiming to reduce the number of cars and motorbikes travelling to the same location and thereby decreasing fuelconsumption. Additionally, we will ensure the careful monitoring of fuel storage and management to prevent any potential leakage. Waste products are hazardous to the surrounding environment. It is imperative to engage in discussions with the local health authorities on how to safely handle the products resulting from the drugs store dejunking. Regular exercises should be carried out to avoid wastage being left in the field, potentially contaminating water sources or cattle feeding spaces.
