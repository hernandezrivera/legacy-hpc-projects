1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Maratane settlement, established in 2001 in Nampula province, is the only official reception centre for refugees and asylum seekers in the country. The settlement hosts approximately 8,000 refugees and asylum seekers, who are predominately from the Democratic Republic of Congo (67 percent) and Burundi (28 percent). Within the backdrop of high poverty levels in Nampula Province, about 82 percent of refugee and host community households were found to be vulnerable in 2021), measured by food consumption, livelihood coping strategies and economic vulnerability at household level.The overarching refugee response plan aims to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance where needed and support access to durable solutions. In alignment with this, WFP’s assistance to refugees and asylum seekers in Maratane has two aspects. 1) Provision of food assistance through General Food Distributions (GFD) and 2) Livelihoods support in support of more durable solutions. All the refugees and asylum seekers will be targeted for GDF, this is 7,935 individuals, with the most vulnerable households receiving a higher food ration. 500HHs will further be targeted for agricultural livelihoods interventions in support of the Call to Action initiative of WFP and UNHCR, to transition the refugees from receiving humanitarian assistance to local integration and self-reliance

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Objective 1: In Maratane, WFP prioritizes support for the 14 percent of refugee households identified as most vulnerable. This group includes elderly individuals, people with chronic illnesses, persons with disabilities, female-headed households, and children, as well as newly arrived refugees during their first six months. These households receive an enhanced food basket comprising 5.28 kg of cereal, 0.66 kg of pulses, and 0.225 kg of vegetable oil. For the remaining 86 percent of households with less acute vulnerability, a reduced food basket of 2.55 kg of cereal, 0.3 kg of pulses, and 0.225 kg of vegetable oil is provided. Objective 2: Livelihoods assistance will be implemented in close collaboration with the Instituto Nacional de Apoio aos Refugiados (INAR) and UNHCR. All refugees and asylum seekers registered with INAR and holding valid identification cards will be eligible to apply for farming land, which is expected to be allocated in the first quarter of 2025. To support these households in cultivating their plots, WFP will provide agricultural inputs, including seeds and tools, helping to reduce dependency on food assistance over time.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WFP is committed to ensuring the centrality of protection throughout the humanitarian assistance. WFP takes calculated measures to ensure a conflict sensitive approach so that actions do not negatively affect humanitarian access in the immediate nor long term, considering the rapidly changing dynamics in the evolving conflict. WFP works with humanitarian actors through local and national level coordination groups such as the protection cluster and community engagement/ AAP working group to share and apply feedback from the affected population. Findings from surveys and other community-based assessments are analyzed and reflected in operational planning, in parallel with reports from the CFM as relevant and possible in the dynamic context. WFP is committed to ensuring zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, ensured through codes of conduct, online and face-to-face training, and several reporting mechanisms. As the lead agency for Linha Verde 1458, WFP works with the PSEA network to ensure awareness of the service and to determine constructive approaches to handle SEA cases in a timely manner internally and with the Government as relevant. WFP is committed to action on SEA reports within 24 hours, prioritizing the safety and well-being of the survivor, working in collaboration with WFP’s ethics office and OIGI. WFP and UNHCR also work together with local leadership and administrator camp management to avoid and/or resolve any issues with the refugee community

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

WFP involves beneficiaries in all stages of the project lifecycle. In the design phase of this project, beneficiaries have been consulted on the choice of modality, considering security, distance from markets and food preferences. WFP works with the established refugee community committee that have been chosen via a community election process. The beneficiaries engage in consultations via the refugee committee platform and via focus group discussions ensuring continuous feedback on the assistance provided. Minority and vulnerable groups are targeted for focus group discussions to ensure that they have a voice. Well-established community feedback mechanisms exist, consisting of help desks staffed by CP staff and complaints and feedback boxes at distribution sites. Linha Verde 1458, the tollfree inter-agency hotline, managed by WFP is accessible to the affected population, providing clarifications on beneficiary rights, capturing needs and associated gaps in the assistance as well as linking the affected population to vital services in the cases of SEA, SGBV and child protection. The field office benefits from a designated protection and AAP team that ensures that communications are up to date and that issues raised by the affected population cases followed up on accordingly in order to close the feedback loop in a timely manner. Finally, WFP will continue operating an inter- agency hotline, 'Linha Verde 1458', on the behalf of the HCT.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

WFP identifies people with disabilities through the refugee committee and the Social Action Institution within the camp. Recognizing the challenges faced by people living with disabilities (PWD) to access assistance, WFP is taking targeted action to address this through several methods. For instance, by ensuring inclusion and participation of PWD in the refugee community committee, including PWD in the group receiving the higher ration amount, and ensuring the food distribution point is accessible to all. When required assistance is delivered to the homes of people with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All program activities will be screened for environmental and social risks during design, at a stage when the implementation details (i.e. location, targeted beneficiaries, inputs, transfer modalities, outputs, and timeline) are being defined, but before the design is finalized. From 2020, The WFP Environmental and Social Safeguards are the set of principles, standards, and tools to avoid or manage the risk of causing environmental or social harm to people, communities, or the natural environment. The WFP Environmental and Social Standards will be used to serve as a benchmark for the risk screening and risk management. All the asset creation activities planned as part of the FFA will be analyzed using WFP’s Environmental and Social Risks assessments tools. Risk screening not only helps identify environmental and social risks: each activity will be classified by the level of the risk: low, medium, or high. Any program activity that entails some risks (medium or high) will come with risk management measures that aim to avoid or reduce the risks or mitigate or offset the expected adverse impacts.
