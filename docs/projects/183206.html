Project Summary

The Danish Refugee Council (DRC) is appealing for support to meet the food and basic assistance needs of 2,340 highly vulnerable Lebanese and migrant individuals (630 households) in the Bekaa and North Lebanon Governorates. As per recommendations and coordination with the Food Security Sector Working Group (FSSWG) Basic Assistance Working Group (BAWG), DRC will provide the food assistance and the basic assistance simultaneously to each household for a period of 11 months, according to the sector guidelines with a transfer value of LBP400,000 per household for the basic assistance, and LBP300,000 per individual for the food assistance.
The food and basic assistance will support vulnerable Lebanese households and vulnerable migrant households already facing acute socioeconomic and protection vulnerabilities amidst Lebanon’s economic collapse, political and health crisis. Further deterioration of the living conditions of these households are anticipated as the economic collapse deepened, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) in Lebanon showing an inflation of 414.97%, while the Inter Agency estimates as of June 2021 that the Food Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) is LBP 1,341,290 and the Non Food Items SMEB is estimated at LBP 1,108,852.The population across Lebanon is also suffering the drastic lack of basic infrastructure and commodities such as electricity continuous cuts, water cuts, lack of basic medicines and increased prices of medical services, and dire increase of food prices to about 400%.
The combined assistance to vulnerable Lebanese and migrants households (HHs) will allow them to procure basic food commodities and non-food commodities, increasing their food intake and survival. The HHs will be targeted upon their socioeconomic vulnerabilities, highlighting mainly the absence of income, specific vulnerabilities of households’ members and negative coping mechanisms. The assessment is conducted by a DRC through a phone or door-to-door multidimensional assessment, independently and automatically scored. The households are being referred by DRC partners through the Referral Information Management System (RIMS), through its protection team or livelihoods team, having an outreach to the targeted population. For migrants, DRC will closely coordinate with the IOM to get referrals and conduct its assessment. The proposed response will be coordinated with the Inter Agency, FSSWG, BAWG, Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA), local authorities, and other humanitarian actors to harmonize the approach, avoid duplication.
The assistance will be distributed in the form of a cash assistance through a DRC financial service provider (FSP), either LibanPost at the rate of 15,000 LBP (as per the rate secured as of September and possible to evolve) if the assistance is distributed in LBP or through BoB Finance in USD, if the Food Security Sector and Basic Assistance Working Group decide as such. An e-card of this FSP will be distributed to each HH at the beginning of the project, they could then redeem at the FSP office the cash for both types of assistance on a monthly basis. The assistance will be closely monitored by DRC through spot checks as well as the monitoring and evaluation team, who will conduct quarterly Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) with assisted households to document any irregularities that might have occurred and to measure the impact of the cash assistance. Also, during the distribution of e-cards, households will receive a business card and information from the DRC team explaining about the availability of the MEAL hotline, in case they wish to provide any feedback/complaint on the assistance or need any information or support.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

A household based socio-economic assessment, encompassing protection risks, will be conducted by the DRC field team, upon referrals from other organizations and DRC's own outreach. During the assessment beneficiaries are made aware of the Feedbacks and Complaints mechanism of DRC. The scoring is automated, as per DRC scoring criteria, and the eligibility is shared back with the field team to respect segregation of duties. The selected households receive a briefing about the processes of cash distribution, the objectives, and are distributed with the FSP e-card.Each month the households receive a SMS informing them that their e-card has been credited and that they can redeem their assistance to the closest FSP point to them. The DRC team conducts spot checks and visits to the FSP offices during distribution to ensure the process is smooth. A regular follow-up with households is also ensure by phone to collect feedback and a post-distribution monitoring (PDM) is conducted every three months of the assistance to ensure the correct access and usage of the assistance.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Across all of DRC’s programming the mission integrates protection, gender, and social inclusion through tailored assessment tools and outreach. The assessment will be conducted to select beneficiaries of food and basic assistance specifically weighed in the protection risks of the HHs, prioritizing female headed and marginalized HHs. DRC has developed guidelines and methodologies to respond to protection risks and has trained implementing staff accordingly. DRC staff are who are accessing HHs alongside the assessment will be able to provide safe identification and referral to other services (protection, health, etc). DRC ensures that all staff involved in its programmes are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for PSEA, and the internal reporting mechanisms for SEA cases, through the mandatory trainings and the signature of the code of conduct. DRC will work on enhancing its awareness raising activities with communities to disseminate information on SEA key message

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

DRC ensures AAP by prioritizing the voices of PoCs to be valued and have an opportunity to participate in and influence relevant decisions affecting how DRC works with them. DRC involves PoCs in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation phases of the proposed activities. DRC will ensure opportunities to offer a response where PoCs raise feedback and complaints (F&C). Throughout implementation, beneficiary F&C will be gathered through different M&E tools, and DRC will ensure that F&C are incorporated to provide PoCs with a voice in program design and implementation. F&C are currently collected through feedback boxes (FB), a hotline and an email address. Information on these F&C channels to PoCs are disseminated by DRC staff through business cards. An information poster is installed next to each FB explaining its purpose. DRC staff are trained to explain the importance of these channels. F&C are then compiled, logged, categorized and then referred to the relevant staff.
