Project Summary

This project aligns with the Food Security Cluster (FSL) Response Plan for South Sudan (2024–2026) and aims to contribute to its three core objectives: a) Provide food assistance to prevent famine, improve food consumption, enhance dietary diversity, and strengthen coping mechanisms for vulnerable populations in IPC Phases 4 and 5; b) Enhance emergency food production through complementary support for vegetable and crop livelihoods, fishing, and livestock activities and; c) Reduce dependency on external food and agricultural inputs by strengthening households' capacity to absorb shocks.
In 2025, the project will deliver food and livelihood assistance to populations facing severe food insecurity across 8 counties in 4 South Sudanese states, targeting a total of 291,158 individuals classified under IPC Phase 3 or worse. The beneficiaries are distributed as follows: Central Equatoria: 1,701 individuals; Jonglei: 82,260 individuals; Northern Bahr el Ghazal: 163,950 individuals and Warrap: 43,247 individuals. Of these, approximately 88,960 people will receive livelihood inputs to bolster emergency food production. Additionally, fortified rice (MannaPack), a specialized supplementary food imported into South Sudan by ForAfrika, will be provided to 6,268 refugees and returnees in Aweil West, Juba, and Gogrial West counties.
The project is structured around two complementary interventions designed to address immediate food insecurity while fostering longer-term resilience: a) Direct food assistance: This intervention focuses on providing immediate relief during critical periods, such as the lean season, to stabilize food consumption and prevent famine; b) Restoration of local food production: where emergency livelihood inputs and resilience-building activities help restore agricultural productivity, safeguard remaining assets, and support the recovery of local food systems.
By delivering immediate food assistance, the project will stabilize household consumption and safeguard vulnerable populations. Building on this foundation, targeted livelihood support—including the provision of emergency agricultural inputs, skills training, and community-led asset creation—will facilitate the recovery of local food production and the development of durable, productive assets.
Specific interventions include:
a) General Food Distribution (GFD) and Asset Creation and Livelihoods (ACL)
These interventions aim to ensure access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food for the most vulnerable populations facing severe food insecurity.
• GFD: Designed to address immediate hunger, GFD will incorporate value-added activities (GFD+) to encourage a shift from dependency on food aid to a mind-set of self-reliance. Beneficiaries will be engaged in initiatives that enhance their capacity for self-sustenance.
• ACL: This component focuses on engaging beneficiaries in complementary asset creation activities, such as restoring croplands and road infrastructure. These activities aim to improve farmers' productivity, enhance resilience to shocks, and contribute to long-term food security;
• Emergency Livelihood Assistance; supports local food production through the provision of critical inputs and skill-building activities: a) Inputs for Production: Distribution of crop and vegetable seeds, agricultural tools, and fishing kits to vulnerable households and b) Nutrition Linkages: Kitchen gardens will be promoted as a simple, effective way to enhance household nutrition, particularly benefiting families with malnourished children. C) Skill Enhancement: Training and demonstration of best practices through demo plots and kitchen gardens to improve agricultural productivity.
The project will set up feedback mechanisms through which targeted community members can voice their needs, preferences and demand inclusive processes. It will also ensure conflict sensitive approach so as to safeguard the safety and security of target community and staff.

1. Needs

The food insecurity and malnutrition crisis in South Sudan is worsening due to a combination of factors, including an ongoing economic crisis (currency depreciation, high food prices), climatic shocks—primarily widespread flooding and dry spell—conflict, and insecurity. The situation has been further compounded by the influx of returnees and refugees escaping the conflict in Sudan, placing additional strain on an already fragile system. According to the latest IPC Report released (Nov 2024), between December 2024 and March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people are anticipated to experience crisis or worse level of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) and this is projected to increase to 7.7 Million people between April-July 2025. Of this total, 1.713 million people in 17 Counties will be facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 31,000 South Sudanese returnee are expected to face catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) between December 2024 to March 2025. The population in Emergency (IPC Phases 4) and catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) is projected to spike during April-July period reaching 2.53 million people and 63,000 people respectively. Moreover, the outlook for malnutrition is expected to be worse, with a total of 2.1 million children aged 6- 59 months and 1.1 Million pregnant and lactating mothers projected to suffer acute malnutrition between July 2024-June 2025. Of the most food insecure States between April-July 2025, expected to have high levels of acute food insecurity are NBeG (65%), Jonglie (62.4%), and Warrap (59.2%). Acute malnutrition burden will be high in this states where out of the 43 counties projected to suffere from acute malnutrtion between April-June 2025, 10 counties are in Jonglei, 5 in NbeG, and 4 in Warrap. The situation is exacerbated by several factors, including the seasonal depletion of food stocks for most households, leading to greater dependence on markets and subsequent increases in food prices. Market functionality is further hindered by poor road infrastructure, which deteriorates during the rainy season. Additionally, the ongoing economic crisis and currency depreciation continue to drive up food prices, weaken the purchasing power of poor households, and reduce income-earning opportunities. Furthermore, the conflict in Sudan is expected to have lasting impacts, including a continued influx of returnees and refugees, disruptions to market supply chains, and added pressure on already vulnerable host communities, particularly in the northern regions including NBeG and Warrap. With 95% of South Sudan's population reliant on farming and herding, climate shocks significantly threaten their ability to secure food and livelihoods. Climate change-driven heavy rains and floods now affect approximately one million people in South Sudan annually, worsening the already critical food insecurity situation. As of 29 November 2024, OCHA reported that flooding had affected 1.4 million people across 44 counties where more than half of those affected are in Jonglei and NBeG. Notably, floods affected regions that had not previously experienced such events, causing widespread loss of assets, crops, and livestock, alongside significant displacement.The project will focus on 8 counties facing acute food insecurity. These include Juba County in Central Equatoria State, particularly targeting returnees; Pibor and Bor South in Jonglei State; Aweil East, Aweil Center, Aweil South, and Aweil West (with a focus on returnees) in NBeG; and Gogrial West in Warrap State. In these counties, recurrent shocks, especially flooding, have left many farming households unable to participate in the previous agricultural season, depriving them of access to food and livelihood opportunities necessary to meet their immediate needs.
