Project Summary

Responding to the need for specialized child case management (including MHPSS) with this culturally sensitive approach, IOM seeks to enhance protection assistance for migrant and Lebanese children in need of protection, including victims of child labour, child marriage, and human trafficking. The project will aim to boost the capacity of local service providers, well positioned to provide the protection services needed. These include case management support, emergency cash assistance, psychosocial support, legal assistance for unaccompanied and/or undocumented children in line with their best interests and community-based child protection activities. Onward referral to multisector assistance such as food, education or health support will be mainstreamed across the project.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Address immediate basic needs, including food needs. Provide integrated child protection case management and specialized services for girls and boys and support safe identification and referral for vulnerable migrant children, including unaccompanied migrant children, undocumented children, victims of violence, exploitation, and abuse, etc. Provide case management via implementing partners to vulnerable children both migrant and Lebanese, including best interest assessment for migrant children in need of protection, alternative care services, family tracing/assessment and reunification, legal aid. Child Protection support also includes emergency cash assistance to address immediate and urgent needs of the children and caregivers. PSS counselling is offered as well within the case management support in addition to focused and specialized PSS when needed. Liaison with health, education, and voluntary humanitarian return, as well as cross border case management and cooperation is done as needed. Improve protection of beneficiaries through strengthened community and family support and engage caregivers, boys and girls in community-based protection activities.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IOM aims to strengthen the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). In the current situation in Lebanon, it is critical to increase efforts to ensure that vulnerable populations are well informed about their rights and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse, especially at the hands of those who are there to provide them with essential support. Essential to ensure frontliners are trained on critical values and principles of conduct during their service, violation of which can put the organization at reputational risks IOM’s Risk SEA risk mitigation plan is the following: - Awareness raising and capacity building of IOM Lebanon’s staff (All staff/volunteers/non-staff had been trained on a full day PSEAH training) - Key messages and awareness raising sessions on PSEA towards the affected population. - Visibility materials on PSEA to be set across all IOM offices. - Active participation in PSEA national network in Lebanon - Securing a referral mechanism for SEA survivors

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IOM’s Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) framework provides overview of IOM’s commitments to and actions on AAP, as well as definitions and explanations about the rationale and internal and external policies shaping the Organization’s approach. IOM Lebanon is in the process of refurbishing its complaint/feedback & response mechanism (CFRM) in order to create an effective, and efficient one. The mechanism is dedicated to receiving submissions related to complaints/feedback/suggestions from external stakeholders (including the affected population) and ensure appropriate and timely feedback to the complaints. The creation of the CFRM serves the overall objective of enhancing and reinforcing accountability towards the affected populations at IOM Lebanon. IOM Lebanon’s current CFRM channels are face to face, comment box, helpdesk (walk-ins), consultations with beneficiaries/migrant-community leaders, dedicated email address and the “We Are All In Platform”.
