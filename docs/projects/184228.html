Project Summary

According to JMCNA, there is an increase of 43 per cent from 2021 of the population in need of WASH services. The population in need of WASH services in 2022 includes approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities. About 6.1 million people are in the Severe category” who lack access to safe water, proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to Acute Water Diarrheal (AWD) and cholera while around 379,757 people (2 percent) are in “Extreme condition as they access water directly from rivers and surface bonds, practice open defecation and lack access to soap and other essential hygiene materials and are susceptible to communicable diseases.
The project seeks to deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute water needs among vulnerable communities as well as providing support restoration of access to safe and equitable WASH services. Thus,
it will primarily support cluster objectives 1 and 2 targeting 25,600 beneficiaries. Of these 20,103 will be non-displaced population and 5,497 displaced population in Balad, Baidoa and Qardho districts. Of these 5,545 will be from Balad, 18,408 from Baidoa and 1,647 from Qardho.
Proposed activities include;
Activities under cluster objective 1;
Target population: 25, 600.
Emergency water supply: Emergency water trucking, Household Water Storage, and Water Treatment, well disinfection and chlorination, rehabilitation of existing water infrastructure.
Emergency sanitation: Construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, vector control activities.
Hygiene promotion: Distribution of hygiene kits, and hygiene promotions. Cash Intervention will be integrated into this activity where feasible.
Proposed activities under cluster objective 2;
Target population:10, 500
Water supply: Construction and rehabilitation of existing water sources, desalination plant, construction/rehabilitation of Berkads, and WASH committees establishment.
Sanitation; Construction/rehabilitation of communal latrines/latrines in schools and health facilities.
To support the water safety index and accountability, a strong protection lens will be applied contributing to cluster objective 3; latrines with internal lights, solid walls and locks, gender-separated facilities, consultation with beneficiary HHs in design, and locations of water and sanitation. Further, the project will embed disability sensitive interventions within the project activities; design, construct and adapt water and sanitation facilities accessible to people with disabilities, ensure distribution points are close and can be accessed by people with disabilities, allocating facilities that will be designed adopting universal design principles when designing and/or building WASH facilities. Provide all communications on WASH while considering the needs of persons with hearing, visual, intellectual, and psychological disabilities. Engage community WASH workers to make the community aware of the rights of persons with disabilities. Under this objective QRC will have measures to ensure 65% of complaints/feedback is adequately responded.
Data collected and used will be disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming at all phases- design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. The programming will undertake a continuous assessment of power dynamics to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access and discrimination. WASH staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) to ensure inclusivity in the consultation process of all program phases. Further, all programs will have a feedback mechanism incorporated.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The ability of the markets to supply goods and services at an appropriate price and quality. The project locations under consideration have an adequate number of vendors who can adequately handle cash transfers or voucher redemption or who can be supported to undertake market-based approach activities. Access of beneficiaries to service and good providers: The beneficiaries will be required to travel short distances to redeem the transfers. Predicted effectiveness: The different transfer modalities have been considered to meet the appeal desired outcomes in the project areas under consideration. Additionally, they offer timeliness in their delivery. Different kinds of transfers will result in different results since people will rarely buy exactly the same items that are chosen for them (e.g. they may prefer to buy cheaper hygiene kits components or different kinds of food). Beneficiaries' actual choices are hard to predict since different households will make different decisions, based on their circumstances and priorities. The method chosen will be based on an assessment of how sensible any assumptions (e.g. about likely expenditures or consumption outcomes) and how well-reasoned the link is between the choice of transfer and desired outcomes at the decision-making stage. Timeliness is an important aspect of effectiveness, particularly in emergencies. The time required to set up and deliver an intervention is considered in decision-making when urgency is a priority. Impact: Potential impact beyond immediate project objective: Potential desired outcomes could include supporting the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs (i.e. not those covered by the objective), providing recipients with more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets, and promoting financial inclusion. Efficiency and cost: Cash and voucher transfers are considerably lower and require less effort to deliver in comparison to in-kind. Protection and gender: The transfer and delivery mechanisms aids are accessible to all genders and those who face constraints related to gender, age, disability, and other factors influencing access. The effect of transfers on the safety, dignity, and integrity of recipients is a consideration.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project considers that the feasibility of cash and vouchers rests on the expected future ability to appropriate price and quality and markets to respond to increased demand if cash or vouchers were provided as the modality. Thus, access of beneficiaries to markets and not simply on the state of the market before any intervention is critical. As part of planning, consideration of possible measures that could be taken to address any market constraints (e.g. addressing constraints facing traders, such as capital, credit, storage, and transport) are factored in. The project notes, seasonal, local, regional, and global trends can all influence prices. Possible price rises caused by external factors do not rule out the use of cash and vouchers if they can be managed through the program design. If in-kind assistance/voucher is purchased locally or regionally, the ability of traders to ensure timely supply at an appropriate price and quality may be critical. The potential impacts on markets of different types of transfers are also considered. Capacity: Some market players have more experience managing different types of transfers. There is a responsibility to provide appropriate assistance, and so choices should not be justified based only on pre-existing skills and experience: there is an obligation to ensure that necessary market skills and experience are recruited during designing. However, it is unreasonable to expect the capacity to deliver ‘new’ transfer types to be built up immediately. Thus, evaluation considers whether consideration is given to the vendor's ability to build its capacity to the scale of the project being undertaken – a massive, urgent emergency response may not be the time to depend on newly acquired skills. Delivery Mechanisms: The project's reliable delivery mechanism will be determined by the vendor supply chain, financial sector, and Communications Technology (ICT) capacity or in-kind systems. Qatar Red Crescent will consider the different delivery mechanisms that could be used with different transfers during design. The choice of transfer will consider the reliability of delivery mechanisms that exist or can be established.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. - Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire, or violence. -Ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and services are safe. QRCS will Identify central locations and bring services closer to vulnerable populations in IDPs and host communities. For example, QRCS will use water trucks to provide services to populations living further away from static WASH facilities to reduce their risk of exposure to protection issues. -Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the WASH facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. -Ensure services are offered within time to ensure beneficiaries travel back safely. 2. Risk of women, girls, and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. -Design and clearly mark separate gender facilities for males and females and account for persons with disabilities and children. -In coordination with protection actors, train health staff to be able to identify and appropriately respond to victims of grave human rights violations, including rape and physical abuse, and set up referral networks and protocols to protect the privacy of victims. -Ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers in direct contact with patients, WASH staff, and beneficiaries have received training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). -In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of WASH workers to monitor, report, and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. -Establish reporting mechanisms to report incidents including security ones concerning WASH interventions to advocate against attacks. -Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them. -Set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions. -Promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at the WASH infrastructure, phone lines. 4. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. -Avoid stakeholders tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to WASH services as well in projects mainly targeting IDPs. -Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community, including the host community, and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. -Adopt clear beneficiary selection criteria communicated to all stakeholders and target communities. -Identify the power dynamics in the area and integrate them into the project. -Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to their nutrition needs to mitigate external interference. -Ensuring all the stakeholders are well represented to mitigate to buffer any possible interference.
