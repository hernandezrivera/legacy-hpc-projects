Project Summary

This project will support flood & food-insecure affected 74,571 beneficiaries (Men 11,186, Women 18,643, Boys 20,134 and Girls 24,608) in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile and Warrap States. to improve essential healthcare services. HFO will undertake the responsibility to implement PHC services activities to achieve the health cluster and Ministry of Health sectoral objectives.
The response will focus on providing basic essential health services in line with national and international humanitarian standards and is based on the epidemiological evidence of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality and trauma-related emergency issues. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness and mitigation activities will be supported with special attention given to vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities.
HFO in collaboration with the Cluster at the national, state Ministry of Health, County health departments and other implementing partners will support different intervention modalities suited to the needs of affected people. readiness activities addressing the Hepatitis E Virus in Rubkona and Fangak. HFO will link static facilities with mobile outreach Teams and community-based healthcare systems (Boma health initiatives) to serve communities who do not have access to health care facilities (>5KM) as part of humanitarian development.
HFO plans to strengthen the capacity of health staff at the field level to support and coordinate with CHD, Boma health workers. HFO will be collaborating with developmental partners UNICEF and WHO by providing regular health services through static health facilities in crisis-affected 10 states of South Sudan by identifying communities that are still not reachable.
HFO will provide services through 2 MTs in each county as well as strengthening Mobile outreach Teams, PHCU and PHCCs in targeted Counties with improved access to essential health services will contribute to the overall reduction of the high maternal mortality rates and under-five mortality rates in women and children and mitigate the transmission of vaccine-preventable and other infectious diseases. HFO will coordinate with CHD, other partners in the planning and organizing of emergency health services. Our national, state and county-level coordination mechanism will be utilized to coordinate, collaborate, monitor and review the response activities. Management of SGBV as part of CMR and Referral pathways for mental health and SGBV survivors for psychosocial support. MNCH emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened.
Referral pathways will link the community and facility-based health care system to provide continuity of care such as BHIs linkages and engagement. HFO will encourage and support IPC and WASH in health facilities including Hepatitis E Measles appropriate preventive measures that are expected to continue improving health services outcomes of affected populations. Health project will ensure to coordinate with partners at all levels to comprehensively integrate and collaborate with Nutrition, WASH and in some extend with FSL. HFO targeted populations is informed by the inter-sectoral prioritization and information analyzed, the health sectoral severity analysis and a review of disease infection risk by county. We will monitor and evaluate all these activities through IDSR, 5Ws and DHIS2 to CHD, Cluster, WHO and MoH. HFO gets IEHK from WHO, MoH, CHD and PPE from IOM and WFP to support the response.

1. Needs

HNO 2023-South Sudan’s health system is unable to cope with, respond to and provide essential life-saving health services in humanitarian emergencies, as it relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance. Limited functionality of health facilities, capacity and poor coverage, including recent closures of health facilities due to reduced funding, has made it difficult to meet increasing health needs worsened by floods, food insecurity and conflict. Women, children, the elderly and PWDs are particularly vulnerable to limited access to health care. Children remain vulnerable to preventable diseases. Almost 69 per cent of all counties are classified as at high risk of measles outbreaks, with 20 counties identified as cholera hotspots and immunisation coverage (49 per cent Penta 3 coverage) remaining extremely low. As of October 2022, 20 counties in eight states have reported measles outbreaks.118 A cholera outbreak was declared in Rubkona county, Unity State in May 2022. Malaria continues to be the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 63 per cent of all morbidities and 69 per cent of all mortalities in 2022.119 Hepatitis E cases have remained above the epidemic thresholds in 2022, with over 3,473 cases and 26 deaths. Some 59 Vaccine Derived Poliovirus 2 (cVDPV2) cases have been reported since the outbreak in 2020, of which 42 per cent are female. Children services to some communities in many parts of the county (DHIS reports 2021). 58% of the Bomas in the county still do not have Boma health teams (BHWs). Only 46% of the population can access health facilities at <5 KM radius, 3 hours while 80% reported barriers to accessing health services exist. Fangak is among hot spot counties for Cholera that is already threatening due to damage to WASH facilities. The WUENIC coverage estimates for the third dose of the Penta-3, Maternal Mortality Rate remain a threat in the county because of lack of a Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Neonatal Care (CEmONC) facility in the County. lack of health facility functionality and low-skilled health workers the facilities are operated by local staff with limited training and no referral pathway for emergency cases. Diel population over 50,000 has been depending on a PHCU for two years (IRNA August 2021). Greater Upper Nile has the same needs of flood and community violence where all villages and payams are flooded people have moved to small high ground in 2021, an estimated 27 died in Fangak due to malaria in Phom (IRNA Sept 2022) and in Leer 2022 conflict left 28,238 people were killed and 145,000 were displaced by flood in Canal, Ayod and Twic East, Panyikang counties (IRNA 2022 and REACH analysis). The first 28 days of life remain the most vulnerable and challenging, with 12,000 children dying before 28 days of life (UNIGME, 2022). These absolute figures translate into under-five, infant, and neonatal mortality rates of 99, 64, and 40 deaths per 1000 live births in 2021. Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, at 1,223 per 100,000 live births (MMEIG, 2022), 39% of women accessed Four+ Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy and skilled birth attendance is low at 12.3% and 14.7% (South Sudan SARA 2018 report). Access to BEmONC is very low and contributes to pregnancy-related and newborn morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations in Fangak, Leer, Canal and Ayod respectively. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 yrs. Access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) remains inadequate Research study shows 41% of PTSD in Unity and Jonglei states. Gender inequality and gender-based violence are widespread Sociocultural norms and armed group presence.

2. Project activities

Healthcare Foundation Organization will carry out the following activities in our program where we implement this health project plan in Jan-Dec 2024 1. Continue supporting7 mobile sites and minor rehabilitation 12 health facilities and renovate that are destroyed by my flooding. 2. Deployment of qualified staff to support CHD staff in various health facilities and community by paying attention to gender equality by employing men, women including people living with disability as part of health team in provision to health services to the affected population. 3. Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response. 4. Carrying out Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities. 5. improving IPC in health facilities through regular water testing in health facilities, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities. 6. providing Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and MTs as part of humanitarian Nexus that will be linking static, Mobile and outreach modality as one of the new innovations to see the access difficulties being improved to be able to reach the population mainly with Malaria, ARI and Diarrhea; 74,571 beneficiaries (Men 11,186, Women 18,643, Boys 20,134 and Girls 24,608) in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Warrap and Central Equatoria states Counties.7. Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (3 PHCCs, and 7 PHCUs) and 7 mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. 8. Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population 9. Conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance and adapt the same changes to influence the project. 10. Promote humanitarian-peace-development bringing humanitarian, community actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources to realize peace for health. Healthcare Foundation will ensure the optimal use of common resources and will mobilize fundings (>2 million USD) from Interantional donor communities to support these planned project activities so that HFO will continue contributing to reduction to morbidity and mortality in the Community of Jonglei, Warrap,Central Equotoria, Upper Nile and Unity states' targeted countries.
