Project Summary

This project will support conflict affected children including children with disabilities and their caregivers have access to quality child protection case management in line with global standard.
The project will strengthen the ecological framework that will directly support in addressing the risk factors and strengthening protective factors at individual, family, community, and the society level so that, children will have positive emotions, feel safe, protected and lead a life free from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation and violence.
The project will also provide multipurpose cash assistance to caregivers of Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAGs) to enable the caregivers provide basic needs of their children (especially those escaping captivities of armed opposition group) in terms of education, health, nutrition and to support the economic activities of the caregivers.
In line with CP AOR localization strategy paper, CPPLI will support localization and system strengthening for national partners that will support the implementation of this project including other actors implementing on child protection project on case management best practices, PSEA and safeguarding. Relevant government stakeholders from the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MWASD) and Ministry of Justice will be targeted through advocacy and sensitization on child protection law implementation. CPPLI will also establish and strengthen community-based protection committees across the project location that will support the prevention and response activities within the communities.

1 - Needs

The flooding, conflict, and insecurity in Northeast Nigeria have led to an increase in child protection concerns and a decline in community-based safety networks and child protection systems. Children face multiple protection risks, including violence, child labour, early marriage, abuse, neglect, exploitation, sexual violence, psychosocial distress, and recruitment by non-state armed groups.In line with guidance note for HRP 2025 of the CP AOR 1,969,549 million children and their caregivers are in need of child protection services across the BAY states. Child protection needs are expected to rise in 2025 due to ongoing conflict and insecurity, displacement flooding and other socio economic factors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CPPLI will leverage on the existing toll free lines established to receive complaints of PSEA incidents across the project locations. CPPLI will conduct community mapping to identify the priority needs after consultation with the relevant sectors to select the community with the pressing concerns. CPPLI will conduct baseline survey with various age groups and across all genders to understand the needs of women, girls, boys and men also People with disabilities to design a more suitable response tailored to their needs. CPPLI will ensure participation across all age groups and also will conduct an FGD sessions with the various age groups to ensure participation of activities and decision making. All CPPLI response and activities are non discriminatory across all the age groups including the marginalized individuals and PwDs, and will ensure no conflict of interest within the communities norms and values across all the project locations

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CPPLI will leverage on the existing toll-free lines registered and established during the NHF-4 project and sensitize the communities on the existing channels for receiving feedback and complaints. including reporting channel for PSEA incidents, in order to prepare the communities of intervention about the existing channels. CPPLI will procure placards, banners and IEC materials containing the toll-free lines to reach wider communities during the sensitization activities.CPPLI will also engage feedback interns per location to collect, analyze and respond to feedback at the community level about the project activities. the feedback interns will report directly to the M&E officer in case of urgent feedbacks such as breach in safeguarding policies, PSEA incident and security concerns, which will be responded within the 24hrs. The interns will also support sensitization activities and will regularly hold an FGD session with the beneficiaries enrolled in the project activities to collect and respond to feedback and complaints.
