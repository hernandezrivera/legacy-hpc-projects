Project Summary

The latest Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) report highlights the urgent need for WASH assistance for approximately 13.6 million people in Northwest Syria. Nearly 2 million fewer people rely on water networks as their primary source, affecting 47% of the population. With 70% of sewage untreated, sanitation infrastructure is crucial. Damaged water infrastructure contributes to the spread of diseases, particularly among children. Urgent restoration efforts are required in Suran, Azaz, and Maaret Tamsrin.
SARD focuses on implementing more sustainable solutions at the community level through this project. This includes rehabilitating/upgrading 10 water networks to assist up to 3,000 families (approximately 15,000 individuals), as well as rehabilitating/upgrading 15 sanitation and sewage systems, targeting 4,500 families (approximately 22,500 individuals). Additionally, 20 water and sanitation facilities, including 15 schools and 5 health facilities, are planned for rehabilitation, benefiting up to 2,500 individuals directly and indirectly. The rehabilitation of public infrastructure facilities, such as water and sewer networks, schools, and healthcare facilities, is expected to involve 50% of the local population and 50% of IDPs.
As part of the emergency response, essential WASH Non-Food Items (NFIs) and emergency latrines are included in this project. Hygiene kits, recommended for use during emergencies, pandemic and epidemic preparedness and response, and other crisis contexts, will be provided to 4,000 families (approximately 20,000 individuals). Additionally, 200 latrines, benefiting up to 5,000 individuals dispersed in open fields and informal settlements, are anticipated to be provided.
All SARD WASH activities prioritize protection and include a well-structured complaints mechanism.
Under its First Line Emergency Response (Result 1), SARD targets the most vulnerable IDP families with rapid emergency WASH assistance, including hygiene kits for each family for three months and emergency latrines for every five families. Under Result 2, SARD focuses on implementing more sustainable solutions at the community level, such as rehabilitating and extending existing water and sewage networks, and rehabilitating water and sanitation facilities in schools and health facilities. Prior to site works, SARD's technical team conducts site visits and studies to prepare Bills of Quantities (BoQs). Site works are then implemented through contractors under close supervision by SARD site inspectors. All SARD WASH activities are implemented with a protection lens and include a well-structured complaints mechanism
