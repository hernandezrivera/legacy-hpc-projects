Project Summary

This project aims at improving access to lifesaving healthcare interventions in Dez by mainstreaming routine maternal and neonatal care into the current health response interventions in Syria. Health services continue to be disrupted by the ongoing conflict in Syria with attacks on healthcare facilities that have rendered 46% of the hospitals and primary health facilities partially functional or not functional, while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained and predictable access to populations in need, ( HeRAMS 2018 dataset ). Essential health services remain a critical need in Dez, with 37% of the affected population requiring routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services. This project aims at meeting the current healthcare needs by revamping neonatal care infrastructure at Kesra and Al busayira health facilities; prepositioning of newborn care supplies, and staff capacity building at the facilities. Activities will also focus on mainstreaming infection prevention and control (IPC) in neonatal care units to prevent infectious diseases morbidity and mortality among newborns at the health facilities.

Needs

The project aims at meeting the current neonatal healthcare needs of communities in Dez governorate. Health services continue to be disrupted by the ongoing conflict in Syria with attacks on healthcare facilities that have rendered 46% of the hospitals and primary health facilities partially functional or not functional, while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained and predictable access to populations in need, ( HeRAMS 2018 dataset ). Essential health services remain a fundamental need in Dez, 37% of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services. Health facilities in Dez communities lack basic supplies and equipment to deliver vital maternal, and child health services. The level of acute malnutrition amongst children under the age of is found to be within “acceptable levels” with a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of three per cent (UNICEF, 2017a). However, three governorates reportedly have GAM rates above 10 per cent (FAO Representation in Syria, 2016:14). Prevalence rate of stunting (due to chronic malnutrition) in under 5s is 12.7 per cent (UNICEF, 2017a; Kern, 2017. The most glaring gap is in newborn care – with no facility that has basic neonatal supplies. Premature deliveries are a death sentence for the newborn because there are no equipment, supplies, and personnel in Dez health facilities. The project target facilities – Kasrah and Al-Busayirah hospital are not an exception. They all lack basic neonatal care supplies and most of the skilled neonatal care personnel. This project has been designed to meet this urgent need of restoring quality neonatal care services in the facilities.Kasrah PHC: The facility serves as a primary health facility – delivering a wide range of health services with the support of partners. The facility lacks necessary neonatal care equipment, supplies, and skilled personnel to provide quality neonatal care. Al-Busayrah Hospital: The facility serves as a tertiary referral facility, but is partially operational due to lack of skilled personnel, and essential medical supplies. The facility has a dedicated neonatal unit space, but it is not functional.

Implementation of activities

RI will carry out rigorous project consultation meetings with all key stakeholders in Kesra and Al Busayira to outline project objectives and targeting. A dedicated team led by an expat project manager based in Kesrah will be hired to implement the project. The hired project staff will work alongside current facility staff to carry out project activities. The planned activities will include revamping neonatal care infrastructure at Kesra and Al Busayira health facilities; provision of maternal and neonatal care supplies and pharmaceuticals, staff capacity building at the facilities. Activities will also focus on mainstreaming infection prevention and control (IPC) in neonatal care units to prevent infectious diseases morbidity and mortality among new-borns at the health facilities. The supported health facilities will be primary sources of project beneficiaries’ data that will be collected on a daily basis using the conventional health management and information systems. The project will be monitored throughout the implementation using RI Monitoring and Evaluation guidelines. Patients data will be disaggregated by gender and age. RI's monitoring and evaluation team will regularly monitor the quality of services. A complaint mechanism will be established to get patients feedback and continuously improve the quality of services delivery.

Protection Risk Analysis

In the specific context of Syria, RIensures that patients regardless of their age and gender have equal access to health services, while theservices provided to them are appropriate to their needs. To this end, RI has put in place some measuresprovision of private spaces for women and adolescent girls in consultation rooms; educating staff on healthrights, and continually monitoring the gender ratios among patients. RI collects data from the healthfacilities according to standardized sex and age disaggregation methods.RI also attempts to maintain a cadre of qualified female staff at each supported health facility to ensurefemale patients feel comfortable accessing services

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to implementation areas: RI's Hub lead will coordinate and ensure different access groups on the ground, as well as monitoring and analysis of on-going changes to the context. Continued humanitarian space within which to operate: RI will closely monitor the target areas for any violations of humanitarian principles and will coordinate with NGO forums and other NGOs as well as other authorities to ensure a clear message regarding the importance of this neutral operational space.Cooperation with local authorities: Relying on good relationships and experience from RI's ongoing programs, RI assumes that the coordination will remain stable with the local authorities for the implementation of the program. Qualified and experienced staff will remain available: This project depends on staff being available in the implementation areas and their willingness to continue to work in Syria. A vast majority of qualified individuals have fled Syria over the past five years due to the on-going conflict. It is, therefore, challenging to hire qualified staff. However, RI will rely on existing core staff in the country.
