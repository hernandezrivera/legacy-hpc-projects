Project Summary

In Upper Nile and Jonglei States, 1,575,656 people face severe shelter needs due to 2024 seasonal floods causing displacement and damage to shelters according to the cluster population in need. The crisis is at the sometime exacerbated by the influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan, increasing pressure on limited infrastructure from the host communities in Malakal, Renk, Bor South, Pibor, Twice East, Panyikang and Baliet. Additionally, reduced funding in the SNFI sector has constrained shelter maintenance and construction efforts, leaving affected populations vulnerable to protection risks such as gender-based violence (GBV), overcrowding and exploitations. To address these challenges, HDC proposes a project targeting 38000 individuals with essential non-food items (NFIs) and transitional shelter construction for 1,970 individuals. Protection principles will be mainstreamed through needs prioritization for vulnerable groups like women, children, people with disabilities and elderly persons. Housing, land property (HLP) due diligence and design standards that ensure safety, privacy and dignity of vulnerable persons. HDC proposed project is based on Tier 1 focusing on life-saving interventions, particularly the distribution of emergency SNFIs, Tier 2 to address the reintegration of returnees and Tier 3 to involve the maintenance of IDP sites, explicitly targeting shelters in critical condition for repairs and refurbishment. The response will combine direct In-kind items distribution (70%) using a pipeline supplier from the cluster and cash/voucher assistance (30%) in areas with functional markets like Malakal, Renk, Pibor and Bor South.

1. Needs

In Upper Nile and Jonglei states reveal a critical SNFI gap, driven by 2.1 million affected individuals facing displacement from floods, communal violence, and an influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan due to conflict. Limited infrastructure in areas like Renk and Malakal plus others location in Jonglei leaving the affected population vulnerable. Vulnerabilities include protection risks, overcrowded shelters, and heightened GBV threats, underscoring the urgency for expanded support, improved infrastructure, and targeted interventions to address the escalating humanitarian crisis. There is total of 1,575,656 persons have been identified to be in Severe shelter needs according to the S/NFI cluster data. This need is due to seasonal floods in 2024 which have resulted in widespread displacement and damaged of shelters, losing of personal belonging like blankets, sleeping materials and mosquito net, reducing safe, habitable spaces in which Affected populations are exposed to protection risks such as unsafe living environments, lack of privacy, and increased vulnerability for women, children, and elderly persons. and in addition, due to the current Sudan crisis in which an influx of majority of refugees returning to South Sudan seeking safety and stay in their area of origin has increased the pressure on the already limited infrastructures for the population in Upper Nile and Jonglei State. In addition, reduction of funding in the SNFI sector in 2024 has limited maintenance, repair and new shelter construction efforts, which Increasing risks of GBV, and exploitation. Women and children are particularly at risk in overcrowded shelters without adequate lighting, or safe layouts. HDC has developed a project proposal to address urgent shelter and non-food item needs for 38000 individuals and 1,970 individuals for transitional shelter construction. This will be done with specific measures to address the protection of vulnerable groups through equitable distribution, site planning and safety audits. In Upper Nile state 22108 will be targeted, (3126 in Baliet, 8455 in Renk, 3655 in Malakal, 4078 in Manyo, and 2794 in Panyikang) and Jonglei State 15892 individuals to be targeted (4290 in Twic East, 5697 in Bor South, 5905 in Pibor). These individuals will receive essential non-food items to help the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host communities. This support will allow them to replace worn-out items due to prolonged displacement. The response strategy will allocate 70% of support from the core pipeline for shelter and non-food items to support the beneficiaries in Baliet, Manyo, Panyikang, and Twic East and 30% through cash and voucher assistance, enabling families to meet their specific needs in Bor South, Renk, Pibor and Malakal, because they have functional markets. Additionally, HDC plans to implement transitional shelters for 328 households, benefiting approximately 1,970 individuals in Malakal and Renk. Ownership of the land will be verified with local authorities to ensure access to adequate housing for returnees and host communities. This initiative aims to address critical shelter needs arising from ongoing conflict and natural disasters, improving the quality of life for those affected and supporting their recovery and resilience amid challenges.

2. Project activities

• Need Analysis: HDC will Conduct comprehensive need analysis in targeted areas to identify the specific shelter and non-food item (NFI) requirements of vulnerable populations, including IDPs, returnees, and host communities. This will involve data collection through surveys, focus group discussions, and collaboration with local authorities to ensure needs are accurately captured and prioritized. • Provision of NFIs Distribution:HDC will support 38,000 individuals across Twic East, Bor South, Pibor, Canal/Pigi, Baliet, Renk, Malakal, Manyo, and Panyikang. Essential non-food items (NFIs) like mosquito nets, blankets, kitchen sets, sleeping mats, solar lamps, and Fangas will be distributed to 70% of beneficiaries, while 30% will receive conditional cash assistance. Distribution will align with cluster standards and rely on needs identified through SNFI analysis. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable, including female-headed households, elderly, unaccompanied children, and individuals without community support. The M&E team will assess and report data for cluster approval.• Provision of Emergency Shelter Kits:Emergency shelter kits, including plastic sheets, rubber ropes, bamboo, and wooden poles, will be provided to 38,000 individuals to support the renovation or construction of shelters. This initiative aims to address gaps caused by the use of mosquito nets as ropes and mitigate deforestation. Efforts will prioritize safe site planning, prevent overcrowding, and incorporate privacy measures for women and girls. Community engagement will ensure safety concerns are addressed. A needs assessment, beneficiary registration, and coordination with cluster partners will prevent duplication of resources.• Provision of Semi-Transitional Shelters:HDC will construct 378 semi-transitional shelters for 1,970 vulnerable individuals in Malakal, Renk, and Bor South. Target groups include women in need, elderly, female-headed households, children at risk, and people with disabilities. Privacy partitions and solar lamps will enhance security. Materials such as wooden poles, bamboo, iron sheets, and tools will be provided. Mudding will be conducted by the shelter team under HDC supervision, with hired carpenters assisting.• Market Assessment: HDC will Conduct market assessments in Malakal, Renk, Pibor, and Bor South, where cash and voucher assistance will be implemented. The assessment will evaluate local market capacity, supplier availability, and price trends.• Provision of Conditional Cash and Voucher Assistance:Conditional cash and vouchers will support 980 households (8,191 individuals) in Bor South, Malakal, Pibor, and Renk for shelter and NFIs. HDC will collaborate with a service provider for procurement, allowing beneficiaries to redeem vouchers. Market and household assessments will determine cash amounts, ensuring alignment with local conditions. The protection team will train providers on ethical principles before seeking SNFI cluster approval• Community Engagement: HDC team will Facilitate ongoing community engagement sessions to involve local stakeholders in project planning and implementation. This activity will aim to address community concerns, gather feedback, and ensure inclusive participation in the design and delivery of shelter and NFI interventions, emphasizing safety and privacy considerations for women, children, and vulnerable groups• Post-Distribution and Construction Monitoring:HDC will monitor activities within three months’ post-distribution and construction. Enumerators trained by the M&E team will collect data through interviews with beneficiaries and analyze it using cluster-designed questionnaires on Kobo Toolbox. Additional insights will be gathered from focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and observations to ensure project effectiveness. Through these interventions, HDC will address critical needs while prioritizing safety, sustainability, and collaboration with stakeholders
