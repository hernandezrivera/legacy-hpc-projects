Project Summary

With this project CARE will respond to health and nutrition needs of vulnerable women, men and children integrated with GBV services in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The activities include :
1- Health Services : Vulnerable individuals in Aleppo and Idleb have access to health (SRH, Paediatric, Primary Health Care) services through BEMONC and CEMONC level heath services, primary health care, transportation between IDP sites and project facilities community health awareness raising sessions, ambulance networks, community based SRH services via AMAL initiative by implementing Young Mothers' Clubs for adolescent mothers, Community Engagement sessions with adults, Health Service Provider Sessions with health staff sensitasion in working with women and young girls.

2- Nutrition Services: The project aims to continue and increase the accessibility to essential life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services for all boys, girls, pregnant and lactating women, and the caregivers (other women, and men) with attention to people with special needs among IDPs and host communities in Aleppo and Idleb governorates . The project activities will be implemented by supporting 2 BEMONC and CEMONC centres.
The nutrition services will comprise of providing infant and young child feeding interventions, Micro-nutrient deficiency prevention, control, and improve equitable access to high-quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW.
The health and nutrition services are integrated and will be provided in:
1-Al Amal Hospital (CEMONC) (HF- B0204097) in in Azaz,Azaz, Aleppo with Violet
2- Al Amal PHC (HF-B0204110) with Violet in Azaz,Azaz, Aleppo
3-Aghtarin PHC (serving as BEMONC) (HF Code- B0204077) Aghtarin, Azaz, Aleppo with Shafak
4- Olive Grove PHC(BEMONC) (HF code-B0204056) in Azaz, Azaz, Aleppo
5- Milis PHC (BEMONC)- (HF code- G0703125) (January- March 2023 for 3 months) in Milis, Armanaz,Idleb with Syria Relief Development - SRD.
Mobile network of community health and community outreach workers linked to the HFs will screen children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition signs and will be conducting outreach activities in the communities around the health facilities to deliver key messages to community members with special focus on pregnant and lactating women, along with disseminating IYCF-E messages among mothers of children under five as well as SRH and communicable diseases (cholera, COVID-19) awareness raising. Hygiene promotion and hygiene kit distribution against cholera will be conducted in line with Cholera TF and RCCE pillars guidance.
All activities and nutrition intervention will be provided according to nutrition cluster recommendations and CMAM, IYCF- E protocols, including the adapted interventions in the context of COVID-19 such as simplified CMAM protocol where applicable.
All the prevention measures from COVID-19 and cholera infections will be applied during providing nutrition and health services, all health staff will encourage beneficiaries to get COVID-19 vaccination as the main prevention measure, and disseminate the communication key messages about COVID-19 and cholera preventions that are recommended by the RCCE.
The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health and nutrition staff on IYCF counseling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility staff and CHWs will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help to raise the awareness of the related communities.
GBV risk mitigation and prevention will be considered by meaningfully engaging communities in the beginning and during implementation of activities, by integrated SRH GBV activities in hospitals, WGSSs and capacitating health staff on GBV, safe disclosures and referrals.

3 - Needs

According to the draft HNO 2023 more than 15.3 million in Syria are in need of life-saving health and nutrition assistance in 2023. Based on NORTH-WESTSYRIA Situation Report 82 per cent funding gap risks the lives of 2.5 million people in NWS. HNAP recorded 19,545 new internal displacements in NWS in September – the highest record so far in 2022 - largely driven by the deteriorating economy. In the north-west, 4.6 million people are bracing for winter including flooding, snowstorms, and unpredictable weather events. Half of this population depends on winter assistance to meet their most basic needs, the majority of whom are women and children living in camps with limited or no access to health care , heating, electricity, water supply or adequate sewage systems.In addition to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, a cholera outbreak was declared by health authorities in September 2022. The outbreak is mainly attributed to the deteriorating socio-economic conditions and the water crisis in the northern part of Syria, which triggered a significant increase in waterborn diseases. Children aged below 5 years, adolescent girls and women of reproductive age who are the most affected by malnutrition are also faced with cholera (UNICEF, Nov 2022). July2022 NWS SMART survey indicates a deteriorating nutrition situation with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels of 5.0 %, The Severe Acute Malnutrition(SAM) prevalence SAM rate by MUAC doubled from 0.8% in 2021 to 1.3%.

4 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities address health and nutrition needs on sexual and reproductive health (SRH), general consultations, pediatric consultations, primary health care, awareness raising on health and nutrition, hygiene promotion, nutrition screenings by Community Health Workers, ambulance networks facilitating transport of high risk cases. The nutrition activities will systematically prevent, identify, treat, and refer, where required, acutely malnourished children between 6-59 months by providing integrated nutrition interventions of CMAM and IYCF. Referral and follow-up mechanism will be established for providing specialized care when needed. Community support components address SRH service access and participation of adolescent mothers, health staff and community members in understanding SRH needs of women and girls and equip the wider community with skills to facilitate women and girls’ access to SRH services. The activities are monitored via regular reporting, data analysis, monitoring, and evaluation by implementing partners as well as by CARE's health and protection technical consultants and independent monitoring consultants and programme team in Gaziantep Turkey . CARE and partners will design in the beginning of the project dedicated Indicator Tracking Tables (ITT), M&E work plans, and disaggregated database. The database tools, using Access and Excel Microsoft software and feed into CARE Application.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Children including adolescent girls and boys constitute a significant share of the IDP and other vulnerable populations. Many adolescents are out-of-school and lack a daily structure and perspective for the future, resulting in high psychosocial vulnerabilities and needs. Adolescents, in particular those not in education, are also at risk of being affected by different child protection concerns such as child, early, and forced marriage, child labour. The project is designed to meet the needs of the community especially women, boys, and girls considering the context and norms in the community. The location of facilities are accessible and easy to reach for all people, strengthened by community health workers enabling outreach to most vulnerable. In order to mitigate protection related risks, the health and nutrition staff will be regularly trained in delivering PFA, identifying CP risks and to be able to facilitate safe GBV disclosures and referral to specialised agencies.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

CARE recognizes we have a responsibility to protect people we work with, and who work for us, and we will continuously strive to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse and child abuse from happening. We take seriously all reports of sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. Our actions are informed by a survivor-centered approach which means that the needs and wishes of survivors guide our response, that survivors are treated with dignity and respect, and the rights of survivors to privacy and support are prioritized. CARE has a zero-tolerance approach toward sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. We will carefully examine allegations and investigate, and take appropriate disciplinary action where this is needed, taking into consideration the rights and interests of the survivor, consistent with CARE’s survivor-centered approach. We make very clear that sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse in any form, perpetrated by our staff, partners or other related personnel, towards anyone, will not be tolerated. This policy applies to all CARE Employees and Related-Personnel. CARE mitigates risks by capacitating own teams as well as non- specialised actors on GBV risk mitigation, including roles and responsibilities as humanitarian staff via dedicated trainings and safety audits in IDP camps to identify risks.The integrated SRH - GBV programming aims to capacitate health workers to response to PSEA and GBV risks.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

CARE maintains strong security protocols and closely monitors conflict developments. In spite of close monitoring and strong systems and protocols, it is impossible to fully mitigate this risk. CARE works on developing strong systems, identifies growing risks trending in the context, and continues to work with UN and donors to strengthen the community-wide response to this risk. CARE also continues to directly monitor project activities at several layers at the area and country office levels. The current UN Security Council Resolution (UNSCR) 2585 expires on 10th Jan 2023. Non-renewal would have a significant impact on activities and other essential services run by UN agencies. CARE has developed a draft non-renewal contingency plan which provides details on CARE’s planning efforts, scenario forecasting, and options to adapt and scale-up programming to address related gaps and needs. Displacement may continue during the program. Protection activities utilise outreach modality in all projects to have flexibility to respond to population movements. The spread of COVID-19 and cholera outbreak does not undermine CARE's ability to conduct field activities. CARE has developed contingency plans to continue to implement during a significant outbreak in NWS while also mitigating risks to field staff.
