Project Summary

Within Salamieh District of Hama Governorate, local markets and economies continue to function despite significant disruptions in supply chains, limited financing, and irregular access to productive assets. Furthermore, the misalignment between labour supply and limited demand has resulted in segments of the population experiencing chronic unemployment. Employment opportunities within vocational trades have emerged as an area of opportunity; however, the preparedness of the labour market in terms of skill set has resulted in limited ability to meaningfully engage with these emerging employment sectors. Furthermore, the enabling environment for business creation has been disrupted since the onset of the crisis. Business development services and facilitation in accessing financial resources represent a two prong challenge which has stifled local enterprise development.
Despite the multiple layers of challenges placing pressure on local markets, a number of businesses have emerged filling gaps in social services (e.g. water trucking, communal power distribution) and other market needs (e.g. food services, clothing, etc…). These micro businesses are able to capitalized on growing market demand and become important actors within the local labour market.
E4R seeks to build of the current local economic conditions within Salamieh District in order to improve employability of affected people and vulnerable groups and contribute to the creation of local enterprises. The overall objective of this project is to strengthen coping mechanisms of affected people and vulnerable groups, especially women and People with Disabilities (PwD), through vocational training and business development services. During the life of the project 300 affected and vulnerable people will be reached, of which 45% and 4% will be female and PwD, respectively. The project target is reflective of the number of unique project beneficiaries and has been determined based on maximum capacity of vocational training courses as well as the number of new enterprises which can be supported during the life of the project.
Specific Objectives:
• Increase access to market relevant skills/vocational training for 175 affected people and vulnerable groups, especially for women and PwD.
• Improve business development and management knowledge for 300 emerging and existing entrepreneurs
• Support socio-economic establishment and recovery of 80 micro- and small-scale enterprises through grants, loans, and asset replacement with a particular focus on vulnerable groups.

Needs

Salamieh District is located in Hama governorate of the Central Region in Syria with a population that increased from 240,000 (49% female) prior to the crisis, to 400,000 in 2013, while currently are accounted to 300,000 with an estimate 33% IDPs (Household Survey 2017, AKF)..Besides, over the years of 2012 and 2013 the population of Salamieh City rose from 70,000 to 280,000, and is currently accounted to around 200,000. The District rural population currently represents 37% of the district’s total population in comparison to 50% prior to the crisis. 60% of rural population are farming households representing an approximate 22% of district total population, and accounting to 17,000 households (85,000 individuals). Out of the 17,000 farming households, who are locally active within the agriculture sector, 10-15% are female headed. During 2018, AKF-Syr conducted primary assessments on immediate needs of households covering health, food, livelihood, household economy, education, vulnerability, and market prices. In December 2018 AKF-Syr compared all possible primary and secondary data to look for change over time in needs. To date, 33,256 households are registered in AKF-Syr cumulative vulnerability assessment database, up 6% compared to 2017 records. Out of which 28,222 households (85%) are vulnerable accounting for 123,951 individuals (55,760 F 45%). 55,931 households (HHs) live in Salamieh District out of which (28,222/50%) HHs were classified as vulnerable, up 5% from 2017. Of total vulnerable HHs, (6,770/24%) are IDPs HHs, (21,131/75%) are resident HHs, and (321/1%) are returnee HHs. Whereas (6,158/22%) of vulnerable HHs are female headed, (5,109/18%) HHs with at least one PwD, (13,585/48%) of HHs with at least one person with chronic illness. (6,151/22%) are HHs who have at least one elderly individual over 65 years old, and (6,260/22%) of HHs have at least one child under 5 years.94% of the district population are food insecure (HFIAS), whereby 24% of the district population have either poor or borderline food consumption (FCS). Economic vulnerable households have worse. food consumption scores with 36% considered to have poor or borderline food consumption. Assessments indicate that average monthly income is USD 112/month, inclusive of humanitarian assistance. Yet, monthly household expenditure is avg. USD 177/month for a family of four, of which, food (67 USD/month), and health (28 USD/month) are considered major expense categories. Shortfalls between income and expenditure is largely met by social safety nets and humanitarian assistance (71% HHs) and debts/loans (78% HHs). In Nov 2018, the average SMEB for a HH of five members is 195 USD/month (11% decrease from Jan 2018). Unemployment contributes to declining in HH economic status especially among vulnerable households (82%F: 54%M). 39% of vulnerable households do not have workers, out of which 62% are female headed, and 38% are male headed households. Negative coping strategies include: reducing spending on NFIs to meet food needs (90% of vulnerable households), taking-on debts/loans (71%), skipping payments of debts/rent to support other needs expenditures (45%), selling productive assets (32%). Extreme coping strategies such as child labour for under 16 years (11% of vulnerable households), and child drop-out of school (8% of vulnerable households) are also observed and largely within rural areas.

Implementation of activities

Improving livelihood recovery through market relevant skills training: Through the AKF-Syr -established Continuing Education Center (CEC), market relevant vocational skills will be offered to affected and vulnerable young women and men. Currently, CEC offers a variety of market-relevant courses that were identified based on an earlier labour market assessment including Pump and Generator Repair, Industrial Automation, Blacksmith, Motocycle Repair, Plumbing, Refrigerator and Washing Machine Repair, Wood Carpentry, Household Electricity, Electrical Devices Repair, Plastering, Computer Maintenance, Mobile Repair, Hairdressing, Makeup, Elderly Care, Tailoring, Handicrafts, Trees Pruning and Grafting and Food Processing. These vocations have been identified as current or emerging areas of labour opportunity. As part of the CEC course offering, all trainees are required to partake in a Life Skills courses which includes modules on communication, leadership, personal financial management, conflict resolution. These soft skills are seen as critical to preparing individuals for the labour market. In total, 175 individuals, of which 40% are female, will benefit from market relevant vocational and life skills training and also be equipped with kits and tools related to their respective vocation (e.g. tool kit provided to graduates of the household electrical course). In addition, a rapid labour market assessment will be conducted at the start of the project to validate the current course offering and identify additional vocations of opportunity. This assessment will evaluate the state of the labour supply and demand in order to shed additional light on labour market gaps and misalignments. Promoting the creation and strengthening of local enterprises: In order to promote local entrepreneurship, a 2-week course will be developed on entrepreneurship and business management. This course will equip CEC trainees as well as other aspiring entrepreneurs with the knowledge of how to generate a business idea, start a business, conduct a market analysis, create and accurately price product offerings, maintain basic accounting records. For individuals who have recently established a micro enterprise but are seeking to strengthen and/or expand their business, modules focused on growing a business will be developed to address this particular segment of enterprise owners. In total, 300 individuals will benefit from entrepreneurship and business management training. The target group for this activity is comprised of 175 CEC trainees in addition to 125 vulnerable individuals who have either expressed interest in starting a micro enterprise or are seeking to grow their current business. In order to support the creation and strengthening of local enterprises, the project will launch a micro seed start-up fund designed at increasing access to financial resources for individuals wishing to start or strengthen an enterprise. Upon completion of the entrepreneurship and business management training, individuals will be invited to submit a business plan for funding consideration. In total, 80 business plans will be funded with a blend of grant and loans. Through the project, AKF-Syr will provide a grant through enterprise related assets by bulk purchasing common equipment and/or disburse grant financing directly to the entrepreneur. In partnership with the First MicroFinance Institution, entrepreneurs will be supported to access a microfinance loan in order to increase working capital for the new and emerging enterprise. These 80 new and emerging enterprises will be supported with business facilitation services such as mentorship by identified skilled workers and business owners, legal advice, and other support services required for successful business start-up and operation.

Protection Risk Analysis

A: Gender constructs may limit ability of women to participate in some vocations. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Female trainees which are selected will be asked preference of specific vocation in order to ensure their comfort. C: CBOs members might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries, Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries Mitigation: AKF-Syr regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in rural areas within Salamieh District is deemed a high risk, AKF-Syr will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk2: Misalignment between labour supply and demand widening: Mitigation: Conducting a rapid labour market assessment in order to validate the current and future selection of vocations will mitigate any potential labour market gaps widening. Risk 3: Low desire to start-up micro enterprises: Mitigation: Given the context, the decision to start an enterprise takes into account a number of factors. It is possible that individuals who have successfully completed the entrepreneurship and business management training may wish to not start an enterprise.
