Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face increasing need for humanitarian assistance with food security and livelihoods assistance carrying the big part of the needed assistance. This is a one year livelihoods intervention program aimed at reducing acute food insecurity and malnutrition in the targeted areas through improved crop, vegetable and fish production. The project target is 433,784 people in the four counties. The main challenges being faced by the affected communities include climatic changes, low cereal production due to farming small farms, destruction by climatic shocks, low engagement of youth in farming, shortage of farming inputs and pests, disease and weeds infestation. The proposed activities that will tackle this include climate change adaptation training to the community members, provision of improved seeds and tools to cultivate more land and increase availability of farming inputs, sensitizing communities and encouraging engagement of youth in farming and fishing and distribution of farming and fishing inputs that include vegetable and crop seeds, tools and fishing gear. The beneficiaries will also be trained on how to best use these inputs and improve the value of the final product for increased proper utilization and marketing.
Therefore, to contribute to addressing the above problems, AHC proposes and seeks funding of USD 1,518,237 to respond to support 72,297 most vulnerable households (approximately 433,784 individuals) in Nasir, Maiwut, Longochuk and Ulang Counties of Upper Nile State. The project will focus on the distribution of emergency livelihood kits that include crop kits, vegetable kits, fishing kits and farming tools to 72,297 Households (433,784 individuals) as well as training farmers and fisher-folks on improved agronomic fishing practices to 3,500 households (21,000 individuals) and setting up of 16 kitchen gardens as a majority of households have access to land for cultivation and fishing grounds for fish harvesting.

1 - Needs

South Sudan food insecurity levels is high and is expected to get worse due to insecurity, the effects of COVID-19, persistent poor macroeconomic conditions and the impact of flooding on livelihoods. It is projected that between December 2020 and March 2021, 5.82M people (48.3% of the population) will be face severe acute food insecurity. This figure is expected to rise to 7.24M people (60% of the population) between April to July 2021 (IPC Dec 2020). In the Greater Upper Nile region, the food security situation is worse compared to last year due to the effects of excessive flooding, insecurity, low crop production, the economic crisis, morbidity and pests and diseases for crops and livestock. The flooding this year caused too much destruction of crops and pastures for livestock, disrupting farming activities, displacing livestock and causing a rise in incidences of water-borne diseases. All the counties were classified as crisis or emergency acute food insecurity classification with a further deterioration in the first and second projection. It is projected that between December 2020 and March 2021, 3.02 million people (66.5% of the population in Greater Upper Nile region) will face crisis or worse food insecurity. At the peak of the lean season in April to July 2021, it is projected that people in crisis or worse food insecurity situation in the Greater Upper Nile region will rise to 3.47 million people (76.4% of the population in Greater Upper Nile region). The deteriorations in these projection periods is driven by low crop production, seasonality, the economic crisis, and the impacts of COVID-19 that have been persistent up to this year (IPC Dec 2020).The county populations of Nasir is 286,628, Maiwut 128,913, Longochuk 72,633 and Ulang 137,691 (NBS, 2020). Due to deteriorating food insecurity situations in the counties, all the three counties except Longochuk were classified as IPC phase 4 acute food insecurity for current, first and second projection periods. Longochuk was classified as IPC phase 3 in the current and first projection and IPC phase 4 in the second projection (IPC, Dec 2020). The people in need of humanitarian assistance in the counties are 243,600 for Nasir, 103,100 Maiwut, 43,600 Longochuk and 130,100 for Ulang. Country-wide, 54% of the people in need are children, 24% are women, and 22% are men. Generally, 67% of the need in the country is driven by food insecurity. The number of people in need of FSL humanitarian assistance is 7.7M with 50% being female, 54% being children and 15% being people with disability (HNO, 2021).The percentage of households that cultivated in these counties in 2020 was between 60 – 99%. However, several challenges were faced during this farming period with the major challenges as shortage of rain during the start of the planting season, floods that destroyed crops, shortage of farming inputs such as seeds and tools and unusually high level of pests, diseases and weed infestations. Livestock ownership is also high (71 – 76%) but faced high reduction due to looting and livestock disease (FSNMS 26). Floods also destroyed pastures and increased outbreak of livestock diseases. It is estimated that 55.2% of livestock were affected and 5.0% died due to floods in the country. In Nasir county where this information was available, 92.9% of livestock were affected and 6.9% died due to floods (FAO floods assessment, 2020).Cereal production in 2020 was also low. It was even lower than 2019 due to the worsened effects of floods (IPC, Dec 2020). In 2019, the net cereal production in Nasir from 10,664 Ha of land was 6,825 tonnes with a deficit of 20,191 tonnes; Maiwut from 1,987Ha of land was 1,033 tonnes with a deficit of 2,737 tonnes; Longochuk from 3,535Ha was 1,701 tonnes with a deficit of 5,064 tonnes; Ulang from 4,165 Ha was 1,999 tonnes with a deficit of 6,973 tonnes (CFSAM, May 2020).

2 - Project activities

African Humanitarian Corps will do a two season response. The first response will be in the main season and the second response will be in the dry season. The main season will comprise of distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits with a compliment of light training on the basic best practices. In the second response the dry season vegetable and fishing kits will be distributed with light training. The specific activities will be as follows:Activity 2.1.1 Mobilization and sensitization of the stakeholders. This will be on the project activities, target beneficiaries, locations and numbers and the objective of the project. This will involve meetings with the local authorities, elders, religious leaders and representatives of special groups and agencies on ground and government counterparts.Activity 2.1.2 Formation of Accountability to Affected Population Committee. This will comprise of representatives from special groups, local authorities, government line departments and community representatives. They will be responsible for supporting the monitoring the activities of the intervention and act as a link between the beneficiaries and the organization. This will also improve on the feedback mechanism and support in dealing with any disputes arising during the project implementation.Activity 2.1.3 Beneficiaries identification and registration. This will be done consultatively with the local authorities and AAP committee taking into consideration the donor guidelines or recommended practices.Activity 2.1.4 Prepositioning of livelihood inputs. The organization will provide support on the logistics and transportation of livelihood inputs from storage areas to distribution sites where beneficiaries are located.Activity 2.1.5 Distribution of livelihood inputs. The prepositioned livelihood inputs will be distributed to the beneficiaries with observation of the accountability to affected populations.Activity 2.1.1 Monitoring and assessments. Monitoring to identify the satisfaction of the beneficiaries and how they will have used the livelihood inputs will be carried out throughout the project period. Assessments wil aslo be carried out to identify the challenges faced by the beneficiaries and the expected output of the livelihood inputs provided to them.Activity 2.2.1 Setting up of kitchen gardens. This will be done in the nutrition centres to help the communities know how to produce and utilize various nutritious vegetables important in fighting malnutrition.Activity 3.1.1 Capacity building and training on best agronomic and fisheries practices and value addition. Improved methods of crop and vegetable production, disease and pests, weeds management will be among some the areas to be covered during the training. Also value addition of products such as fish and vegetables will be included together with their marketing.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AHC will provide beneficiaries with information about protection against sexual exploitation and Abuse(PSEA) during community engagement forums. Through these forums we will strive to raise awareness on issues relating to PSEA by engaging women, girls, boys and men as well as the local leadership. AHC staff will actively be involved in making sure that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements regarding the project being implemented in their region. For instance beneficiaries entitled to the livelihood assistance will be informed that they are not expected to provide any forms of sexual favors and that demand for favors in exchange of livelihood assistance is strictly prohibited. We will actively put mechanisms that will enable beneficiaries to know how and where to report PSEA incidences assuring them safety and confidentiality. We will go the extra mile in ensuring that information regarding PSEA issues are disseminated in community meetings, forums and trainings.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We will ensure that feedback mechanism put in place are sensitive to age gender and diversity and accessible to the most vulnerable members in the community. We will further consult with the vulnerable population representatives to gain a greater understanding of their views, needs and concerns.The beneficiaries will have the opportunity to be involved in project design, planning and decision-making concerning activities as well as opportunities to lead or support the implementation of the activities. The participation is expected to be meaningful and enable beneficiaries to help shape, be involved in, and – whenever possible – lead on some of the activities. We will make a priority during implementation to make the beneficiaries understand when and how they will participate and how decisions will be made. This will be discussed and agreed with beneficiaries. Persons with disabilities often experience heightened protection risks because of their disability, and being a woman or a child with disability further compounds these vulnerabilities. Girls and women with disabilities endure violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation twice as often, over a longer period of time, and suffer more serious injuries as a result of violence compared to women without disabilities. Women and girls with intellectual disabilities are at a particularly higher risk of violence, including sexual violence. As such, these special groups will also be identified, considered and encouraged to provide their feedback in a manner that they are comfortable in.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

AHC will work closely with community leaders representing minorities in the community such as the disabled persons, women, youth and the elderly. Through collaborative dialogues with these representatives we will be able to ensure the voices are heard and we will further explore ways in incorporating feedback and complain mechanism in project implementation activities. During program inception meetings, we actively involve the community representatives in discussing the program design and delivery. This ensures that AHC put in mechanisms that takes account the specific needs of the vulnerable populations .Through dialogues we are able to address concerns relating to constraints to accessing livelihood inputs and inclusion . AHC actively conducts sensitization and awareness meetings across the payams informing the beneficiaries the importance of providing feedback and educating them on the various ways of offereing feedback in the field setup. For instance, feedback can be presented directly to the local authorities, community representatives or directly to the AHC staff.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AHC will use beneficiaries’ feedback to improve quality of activities implemented in the area of operations. From this feedback we will be able to make necessary adjustments to accommodate the beneficiaries’ needs especially when the information provided promotes accountability to the community. Beneficiary feedback will be used throughout the program. AHC actively collects beneficiary feedback through specific data collections tools .We have further trained our staff to actively listen and solicit feedback when engaging with the beneficiaries throughout the project cycle. At all times we strive to offer immediate response by communicating to the relevant authorizes concerned. We actively negotiate with our donors and with the local authority to act on beneficiary feedback. We have also put in mechanisms that promote clear referrals pathways for feedback

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The major drivers of food insecurity that drives the populations to worse IPC phases include low cereal production, effects of climatic shocks such as floods and droughts and shortage of farming and fishing inputs. The intervention is geared towards addressing some of these issues through training of households on improved agronomic and fisheries practices, climate change adaptation and provision of farming and fishing inputs that include seeds, tools and fishing gear. In the end, the amount of food produced will increase and the communities will be better adapted to changing climatic conditions. Therefore, more people will become food secure and reduce the populations in IPC phase 3 and above.Youth also are an important resource to the community, and are normally abused in conflicts and intercommunal clashes that include cattle rustling. They will be actively engaged in issues of farming and fisheries that will ultimately benefit their families and the community at large.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Although cross-sectoral services are vast, AHC will provide services related to food security and livelihoods. We will also coordinate with other agencies responding in other humanitarian sectors and share information on where there are need gaps. We will also strive to share beneficiaries where possible in order for the affected communites to receive a holistic humanitarian assistance. We will also coordinate with the relevant government institutions in order to provide timely, coordinated and effective assistance to the affected communities.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This will be used as one of the vulnerability criteria for selection of beneficiaries. Households with malnourished children and pregnant and lactating mothers will be given priority for registration to receive livelihood inputs and training especially on vegetable and fish production and nutritious utilization.
