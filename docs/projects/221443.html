Project Summary

The situation in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon remains a call for concern, characterized by violence leading to civilian being killed, kidnapping of teachers and civilians for ransom, arbitrary arrest illegal detention, forceful disappearance, displacement, burning closing of schools and destruction of properties. Education is used as political instrument by the parties in the conflict. Since 2017, the crisis has had a substantial social and humanitarian impact on children in the NW/SW regions of the country. The conflict has also had consequences on the education leading to disruption in schooling, increased vulnerability of children, teachers and education actors and heightened risks associated with safety and security in educational environment. An estimated 1,033,000 school-aged children have been affected by the crisis. They were denied the opportunity to learn and develop themselves, failing in achieving Sustainable Development Goal (SDG), specifically, the goal 4: ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all.
According to the JENA report of 2024, in the beginning of the 2024-2025 academic year, 41% of schools are not operational in North West and South West regions due to the disruption of education and threats to the safety of learners and teachers and other education stakeholders. Attack on education has been seen in several forms ranging from conflict parties occupying education facilities, kidnapping and or killing of teachers, students and parents, rape of students and learners to and from school. A very peculiar form of attacks within the context is ghost town and lockdown enforced by the NSAGs
A need assessment conducted by the Islamic Private Education Secretariat show that protection of education facilities and educators remains a priority need. This is also highlighted in the JENA and MSNA reports which show that continuous attacks on children, teachers, their parents and learning facilities has greatly impacted local communities negatively. Since 2017, the Non-state Armed Groups (NSAG) have demanded the closure of schools and violently imposed strikes or ‘ghost towns’ on certain days and/or occasions. They have also threatened and/or burned down some establishments that have remained open. This is evident in the JENA reports of march 2024 which reveals a total of 3,918 violence incidents on students, and 1,124 on teachers were reported, 157 attacks on schools and 2,303 school interruptions were recorded. This situation has created an urgent need for intervention that prioritize the safety and wellbeing of students and educators. Thus, this project seeks to address the education security needs of learners, students, teachers and other education actors in the Fako, Meme and Manyu Divisions through capacity building workshop and training of teachers, facilitators and Parent Teachers’ Associations (PTA) on school safety, child safe guarding and disaster risk reduction to mitigate impact of attacks on education. By empowering Parent Teachers’ Associations and educators, communities can be better protected there by meeting SDG 11: Sustainable cities and communities; Safe and inclusive cities can contribute to a sense of security and wellbeing. By securing funding for this project we will empower 500 teachers, facilitators and PTA members with strategies to curb attack on education through capacity building trainings and communities will be impacted with strategies to response to potential risks and mitigate the impact on education.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

According to the need assessment conducted by Islamic Private Education Secretariat for the South West Region in July 2024 revealed that safe access to education facilities by learners and teachers are amongst the most priority needs of the people in Manyu, Meme and some rural settlements around Fako. This is evident in the JENA reports of march 2024 which reveals a total of 3,918 violence incidents on students, and 1,124 on teachers were reported, 157 attacks on schools and 2,303 school interruptions were recorded. The JENA , MSNA reports also reveals that 41% of schools in the North West and South West are closed due to thread from Separatist Fighters, an estimated 955,700 children still don’t have safe access to schools, 44% of secondary teachers still can not resume work in the south west region due to security threads and an estimated 30% of primary school teachers are still out of their duty stations all pertaining to attack on education. It is for this reason that the Islamic private education secretariat deems it necessary to train 500 teachers, facilitators and PTA members in Fako, Meme and Manyu on school safety, child safe guarding and disaster risk reduction in order to 1. To train and build capacity of teachers, facilitators and PTA members in Fako, Meme and Manyu on safety protocols, child safe guarding awareness and disaster risk reduction. Many teachers and facilitators lack formal training on school safety protocols, emergency response plan and best practices for maintain a safe learning environment. Also there is a significant need for training on recognizing and address in child abuse, neglect and exploitation, as well as implementing safe guarding policies in school. More so educators and PTAs require knowledge on disaster risk reduction strategies to prepare for potential disasters and mitigate their impact on education.2. Community engagement and support: strengthening the involvement of PTAs in promoting school safety and child safe guarding in their communities will improve their capacity to mitigate potential risks in the community. 3. Promoting psychosocial wellbeing. The training will no only help to motivate teachers to resume work but will also build their resilience by providing them with techniques on stress management.
