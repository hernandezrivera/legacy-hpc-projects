Project Summary

Tdh will continue to ensure access to quality health services at two PHCs in camp 26 and 27, with a focus on infants, children, adolescents, and their mothers. Across both PHCs, the PRM funded program will ensure 237,000 OPD consultations this year. Given the PHC is one of the first entry points with an established service delivery infrastructure in the community, Tdh will continue to support capacity building of health care service providers in both PHCs, enabling them to adequately identify and refer vulnerable children and caregivers to child protection services. All health services will continue to be delivered with utmost adherence to the COVID-19 Standard Operating Procedures, if required. An Electronic Medical Record ( EMR) system will continue to be used, with the aim of enhancing data capture, validation, analysis, timely reporting, and informed decision making. Tdh will continue providing the full Essential Package of Health Services at the two PHCs, including services for Mental Health, Basic Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (BEmONC), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), Non-Communicable Diseases and nutrition. MHPSS support will be done by Psychologist. This will ensure a continum of quality care by improving patient diagnosis and treatment, reducing redundancy in diagnostic testing, and supporting more efficient and effective decision-making and care. Both PHCs have a dedicated pharmacy with an automated logistics management system and an adequate level of diagnostic facilities to address primary health care services with a provision of immunization services. Tdh PHCs will continue to support COVID-19 testing and vaccination as well as maintain triage and infection prevention protocol. Integrated community outreach activities will continue by a multi-skilled community outreach team reaching an estimated 15,000 beneficiaries. Sessions will focus on disseminating information on health, child protection and hygiene awareness in the camps, and host communities. To reduce home delivery, Traditional Birthing Attendants (TBAs) will continue to promote facility-based births. The project will further increase the capacity of maternity services to ensure deliveries under a high-quality standards framework, and that antenatal and postnatal care processes and results are in place. This includes strengthening Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services such as 24/7 BEmoNC, ANC, PNC, and family planning within both PHCs. Tdh will follow key protocols such as Helping Babies Survive, Helping Mothers Survive, Helping Babies Breathe, and will provide training to physicians on new-born treatment protocols.
Through the strengthening of counselling activities to mothers in the Breastfeeding corner equipped with an IYCF Counsellor, and through outreach activities, Tdh will continue increasing number of children under 6 months who are exclusively breastfeed. Due to availability of a SAM/MAM) centre adjacent to the PHC, Tdh will focus on identifying the children suffering from malnutrition, referring them, and following up after they received nutritional services.
Through nutrition screening and outreach team support, Tdh will be able to ensure 100% referral, engage Traditional Birth Attendant to improve referral for facility delivery, support drop out for EPI vaccination and promote family planning services. Through multi skill community volunteer support with PNGO work for integration of health, WASH and protection activities. Tdh will roll out EMR system and improve capacity building of other actors. Tdh will increase coordination with local actors for transitioning two PHCs through a detailed handover plan, selecting a local partner with capacity development.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Ensuring adherence to quality and respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities in the camps continues to be a challenge, in part due to the large caseload. The JRP 2020 identified critical gaps including essential health services; comprehensive emergency obstetric care; new-born care; family planning and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape as well as first line support of Gender Based Violence (GBV) survivors. As per 2022 JRP, Health care remains one of the most commonly reported need in the host communities. By end of September 2019, the proportion of women accessing facility-based deliveries had improved to 47% as compared with 37% at the start of the year, but this remains below target. Risks for communicable disease persist due to living conditions in the camps. Immunization coverage among children under 1 year old improved from 67% at the start of 2019 to 80% by end of September 2022, but substantive improvements are needed to achieve 95%. Capacities for non-communicable disease management, laboratory diagnostics; mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS), limited access of referral pathways from community health facilities to higher levels of care, and gender inequalities in access to health outreach, remains a major impediment to adequate treatment as observed by Tdh outreach team and indicated in J-MSNA 2021. Additionally, the proportion of children aged 6-59 months enrolled in nutrition programming remains low with only 15% of host communities and 60% of refugee respondents enrolled. TDH got approval from CIC to work in block C in Camp 27 and Block B,C,D,E and F in Camp 26.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will ensure access to quality Essential Health Package Services including supply of drugs and consumables to the transgender / LGBTQ community within the camp. Through psychologist Tdh will provide MHPSS services both in Rohingya and host community. Considering limited accessibility and stigma, they face a lot of harassment and humiliating situations when they want to take psychosocial support. So, it can be said that this assistance will be diversification activities among the targeted beneficiaries, and it will help them to reduce social inequalities and access to services and counselling.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project is an integrated project where the main components are CP, Health and WASH. In Tdh work, the targeted beneficiaries will receive services from all the mentioned components from the project, as well as the outreach team will engage both community (Rohingya and host) people to implement all the components and increase the meaningful participation of the beneficiaries/stakeholders which will help them in decision making during project activity and facility design, planning, and implementation, for example; The same beneficiaries will get WASH, CP and health services, WASH project will construct gender sensitive, age and disability friendly structures, hygiene promotion (including NFI), messaging (including handwashing, sanitation, MHM, waste management, water safety plan, AWD, Dengue and related to COVID-19) and will receive their feedback and complain from CFRM system and address them, and will follow do no harm and CHS, all service directly contribute to protection and to save lives. TDH will cover the population in Camp 27 block C and in Camp 26 in block B,C,D,E and F. TDH will not restrict people who are coming from other blocks of camp 26 and camp 27.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Essential Health Package services offered by the PHC will continue to include different age, gender and disable persons in seeking health care services with access to drugs and vaccination. Project ensured access to COVID screening and vaccination services as well as in collaboration with Upazila Health Complex. Project will continue to serve BEmONC services 24/7 and ensuring EPI vaccination as well. In addition, well structured referral services is in place for nutrition, disability and other emergency health care services. Also, any child protection issue detected will be immediately reported to the protection team which will ensure child protection.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Under the AAP IASC and Tdh framework, Tdh will implement activities following three main phases. (I) Taking account: the project team will involve beneficiaries at the beginning of the project in order to update the baseline data and to share all relevant information related to the project and to involve them in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: Tdh will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions. (3) Being held to account: Tdh will organize formal and informal meetings at key moments of the project (start, annually and end line evaluation) to give the communities the opportunity to assess the project and to give positive and negative feedback related to the project performances. Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism that is already established at the camp level will continue getting direct information from the beneficiaries and addressing them through proper channel. (4). TDH organized lessen learned exercise with project staff and implementing partners, reviewed the endline survey to see the performance trend and identified gaps and areas of improvements

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

During the implementation of the project, gender sensitive two focal person deployed at both PHC which will help in reducing PESA and in case of any problem related to PESA, necessary action will be taken as per the organizational PESA policy, and will be contacted with the concerned sector and area focal agency. Training to all staff of both PHC will be undertaken by safeguarding focal point and monthly reporting.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will contribute to disaster risk reduction during floods/flash floods, and AWD outbreaks. Project will ensure stock availability of essential supply of drugs and consumables, as well as will manage the ANC follow up, 24/7 BEmONC services and EPI vaccinations. The project will organize mobile medical team in ensuring emergency assistance for health care needs for mitigating sufferings of the Rohingya and host communities. Project will ensure adolescent friendly services and availability of Family Planning commodities as per need of the population.
