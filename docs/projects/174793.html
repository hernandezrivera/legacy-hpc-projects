Project Summary

IOM plans to provide tailored shelter/non-food item (NFI) and cash assistance through rental subsidies to the most vulnerable Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees identified through vulnerability assessments in Far-North region throughout 2021, to increase the overall capacity of the cluster/sector to address urgent needs and alleviate suffering. Vulnerability characteristics will be based on various household criteria, including but not limited to household size, number in household under age of 5, number of elderly members of household, and current state and condition of existing shelter. Interventions will be in line with the Shelter/NFI cluster guidelines, be done through different intervention modalities applicable to Cameroon (directing in-kind distributions, cash interventions, vouchers, among others), as well as be aimed at the targeted populations (IDPs in sites, collective centres, host communities and returnees) depending on the local context and vulnerability criteria.
IOM will use the displacement data collected through its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and notably the Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) in Far-North region to target and provide rapid emergency shelter/NFI support to the most vulnerable, newly displaced populations and returnees. The sectoral assistance will focus on addressing the needs of individuals who are forced into repeated short-term displacements and are seeking refuge in makeshift shelters or agricultural structures in the surrounding bushes of their villages. Repeated displacements, even if short-term, are forcing the affected populations to sell their assets to survive and resort to negative coping mechanisms, which in turn increases their vulnerability.
Meanwhile, and in response to the regular ETTs updates reported by the DTM on the most recent displacement situations, IOM seeks to preposition NFI and Emergency Shelter kits in its warehouse in Maroua in the Far-North to ensure rapid and effective response to crises and shocks. The NFI and shelter kits will be distributed to most vulnerable beneficiaries with the support of trusted and experienced local partners to increase operational access to hard-to-reach affected areas, based on experience developed in complementarity to on-going Shelter/NFI interventions in the Far-North. The NFI kits composition will be based on the sector standards for the region. In addition, IOM will provide a 3-month rental subsidy for affected populations (including vulnerable returnees and host community) living in urban areas. The specific amount disbursed to beneficiaries will be defined in coordination with the shelter/NFI sector partners. IOM will simultaneously make efforts to ensure the sustainability of this action through the complementarity of other existing IOM programming to vulnerable displaced populations, including Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS).
Throughout all actions under the proposed project, activities will follow cross-cutting themes such as a rights-based approach, gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populationsi, through integrating topics into trainings and coordination with implementing partners, as well as mainstreaming approaches with IOM staff both in the field and at key offices. Adequate monitoring practices will also be mainstreamed throughout all phases of the action including during distributions and post distributions to ensure that IOM quickly learns and adapts to new information when available.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Ongoing hostilities and violence have heavily impacted the Far-North region during the past five years, where lower magnitude displacements are still triggered due to small scale sporadic attacks by Boko Haram, natural disasters and other violent extremist organizations (VEOs). Since the beginning of 2020, the region has witnessed a resurgence in violence as ongoing hostilities have uprooted more than 321,886 IDPs, 48,769 out-of-camp refugees and 123,489 returnees. Women and girls have been particularly affected by this crisis as they are exposed to different forms of violence and abuse, including sexual violence and abduction by VEOs. Insecurity in this region is exacerbating the already limited access of displaced populations to essential social services such as education and health as looting and destruction of goods and public infrastructure are becoming more recurrent, further harming community cohesion and leading to higher inter-community tensions. IOM reports on displacements in the Lake Chad Basin (LCB) have highlighted the growing importance of return movements of former IDPs since 2017, with the total returnee population representing approximately 25% of the population affected by displacements in Far-North region, however this tendency looks to have decreased in 2020 with increased community tensions. In both crisis-affected regions of the country, increasing violence and hostilities as well as the lack of progress towards a political solution in the NWSW regions are predicted to cause further displacements and increase humanitarian needs. In addition, access to the affected populations will remain challenging due to increasing insecurity, COVID-19 restrictions and health measures, sporadic attacks on aid workers and poor road infrastructure.The intended results through Shelter/NFI interventions IN Far-North will be to ensure that throughout 2021 conflict-affected populations in these regions benefit from an immediate improvement of living standards, well-being, and dignity, with greater access to basic, safe and dignified emergency sheltering and Non-Food Items.
