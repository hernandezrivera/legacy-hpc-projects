1 - Needs

The needs and gabs basd on Simple, Spatial, Survey Method (S3M II) for Sudan - 2018. results and According to the HRP released in July 2020. Firstly the HRP released in July 2020, in Sudan 9,8m of people – 24 per cent of the total population - are in need of humanitarian assistance. Of them, 7,6m people require essential water, sanitation and hygiene services. Across the country, only 74 percent of the population has access to drinking water, 39 percent to limited sanitation services, and 23 percent to basic hygiene services. The most affected population groups are IDPs, returnees, non- displaced host communities and refugees. In particular, 1.7m IDPs, 0,9m refugees and 291,000 returnees are in dire need of WASH support, almost 30 percent of the total people in need. Amongst the population groups, in Sudan people with specific needs including unaccompanied and separated children, survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), women-headed households, elderly persons, persons with disabilities and people with specific health concerns represent a significant portion. Overall, 60% of the People in Need in the WASH sector are boys and girls below 17 years. Access to WASH in school and primary health centres is limited. Approximately 30% of schools do not have any functional latrine and fewer than 50% of schoolchildren have access to soap and water for handwashing. This is further compounded by the lack of sanitation facilities for physically disabled children and gender segregated. This negatively impacts children’s attendance and enrolment, especially for girls. In this critical context, the COVID19 pandemic has significantly increased WASH needs at school and consequently the humanitarian assistance required to ensure that children can study in safe learning environments (handwashing stations, latrines and long-term sustainable solutions for the provision of safe water). As part of the peace process initiated by the Transitional Government, the peace talks ongoing in Juba should contribute the internally displaced people to achieve durable solutions; in particular, it is expected that many thousand of IDP families in South Kordofan and Darfur will leave their temporary settlements and return to places of origin, or to ‘normalise’ their current informal settlements. Both processes will require substantial humanitarian assistance through integrated programming, including WASH long- term solutions. 2 - Activities/Outputs 1. Sanitation situation is also a problem with 55% of population in Kassala defecating openly, Gadarif 61% . Some localities had over 90% open defecation including Agig, Basunda and Rural Aroma. Hygienic practices including handwashing is very low in the 3 states. Overall, Kassala state had 7.7% coverage of hand washing facilities while Red Sea and Gadarif reported 7.2% and 3% respectively.
