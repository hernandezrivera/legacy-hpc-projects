Project Summary

Centre for Emergency and Development Support (CEDS) is proposing to implement a one year emergency water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) project in selected locations in Aweil East County with an aim of mitigating WASH related morbidity and mortality and also minimize WASH related gender based violence (GBV) cases. This project targeting a total of 15,000 men, women boys and girls will involve rehabilitation of 10 boreholes, construction on 10 new latrine and rehabilitation of 5 existing latrines all of which will be carried out in or around the nutrition centers. In addition to this, CEDS will secure WASH non food items from the UNICEF WASH core pipeline supplies and distribute to the targeted beneficiaries more specifically the caretakers of children admitted and or discharged from the nutrition facilities. Through the hygiene promoters, CEDS will conduct hygiene promotion both at the nutrition centers and also at the community level. To mitigate the risk of women and girls experiencing gender based violence while seeking WASH services, CEDS will train the community leaders and the Water use committees on gender based violence, intentionally include women in the WUC and also set up feedback mechanisms through which GBV cases can be reported and acted on. Furthermore, CEDS through the GBV officer will conduct community awareness sessions on WASH related GBV with an aim of minimizing the GBV cases. At the end of this project, it is expected that the targeted community members will have access to safe drinking water within locations that are safe to both females and males. Again it is expected that there will be improved access to appropriate sanitation facilities that are as well safe to both males and females. It is also expected that by the end of the project, the knowledge of the targeted community members on proper hygiene and sanitation will have improved and they will be practicing the same. These outputs combined will ultimately result into the achievement of the desired objective of this project.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is among some of the countries in the world with lowest development and health indicators. The country has one of the world’s lowest rates of access to safe water and sanitation facilities, with 90 per cent of the population living without access to basic sanitation facilities and 61percent of the population practicing open defecation, as per the 2018 humanitarian needs overview. WASH-related diseases, such as cholera, are widespread and often linked to limited infrastructure and access to health care, population displacement, food insecurity and poor hygiene practices. Gender based violence is also endemic in South Sudan, where decades of conflict have increased the vulnerability of women and girls. Unfortunately, many cases of abuse occur when women and girls are undertaking their normal survival activities, including when visiting latrines or going for open defecation and when they go to collect water. Further to this, women and girls are typically responsible for collecting water for their families in South Sudan. The risk of GBV is further increased when they are forced to walk long distances to access functioning boreholes, and also while collection water in bushy and unsafe /crowded boreholes. In Northern Bahr El Ghazal, the REACH initiative assessment (June 2019) notes that water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) continues to be a challenge across all the three states of the region, Aweil East county included. Only a few (23%) households reporting being able to access their preferred water source year-round while only 38% of the population using latrines. More than half (59%) of the households do not use latrine because of lack of the same. Though handwashing is practiced but 73% of the households do not use soap or any form of detergent to wash their hands. The poor WASH practices in the region is presumptively associated to highly prevalence of mortality as a result of water borne diseases especially diarrhea (21%) and typhoid (12%). A WASH knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP) survey carried out by CEDS on WASH indicators in Aweil (Aug 2019) noted than almost half (47.1%) of the households still rely on streams, ponds and rivers as their sources of drinking water and 58.2% of the households do not treat drinking water putting households members to high risk of water borne diseases as evident from 52.1% of the households who reported to have experience cases of diarrhea in the households. Again, 48.2% of the households do not have access to latrines with almost all (97.5%) of those who do not have latrine reporting to practice open defecation. Water borne diseases are known to be a compounding factor to malnutrition in children therefore poor WASH practices in Aweil East can also directly be associated to high level of malnutrition in the county with the current global acute malnutrition rate currently standing at 16.2%. The WASH cluster severity mapping of Aweil East further reported that while a about 84% of the population in the county have experienced WASH related disease, only 17% of the population have access to improved water sources while 63% having access to WASH non food items and leaving a total of 113,804 in need of WASH intervention in the county. Furthermore, A GBV project implemented by CEDS in Aweil East has pointed out that since most of the current water point and sanitation facilities are distant from settlement, opportunity is created that encourages gender-based violence as females and girls travel to the water points. With the limited access to WASH facilities and knowledge of the community and households on good WASH practices, it is evidently essential to undertake emergency WASH interventions with focus on minimizing WASH related gender-based violence in Aweil East. Specifically, it is important to enhance the quality and management of existing boreholes and drilling new ones to increase proximity of the communities to water sources can greatly enhance the safety of women and girls.

2 - Activities

To meet the WASH needs of the communities in Aweil East, CEDS will carry out the following activities;1. CEDS will hold consultative forums with different groups including males and female of different ages, people living with disability, key community leaders and other stakeholders, relevant state government ministries on appropriate locations for the proposed activities as well as the modalities in which the project can me implemented with "Do no harm" to the community members. 2. CEDS will carry either major or minor rehabilitation (depending on the status of the borehole) of 10 existing boreholes located in or around OTP or SC or in the villages surrounding SC's or OTPs in selected location on the needs assessment and consultations with relevant state government ministries. 3. Establishment of water user committee (WUC) in locations where the new boreholes will be drilled and reestablish or strengthen the capacity of the existing (WUC) for the existing boreholes. deliberate efforts will be made to ensure that there is at least 40% representation of Women in the WUC and also representation of persons living with disability in the committees as well. 4. Through the project officers, CEDS will conduct training for the WUC on management of water-points, collecting and handling of complaints and feedback as well as basic training on management of GBV cases, referrals and trauma healing support.5. CEDS will secure NFI including soap, water purification chemicals, hygiene kits from the WASH core pipeline supplies and distribute to 1500 beneficiaries. This NFI will target mostly mothers with children admitted to either OTP's or SC's.6. Establish feedback mechanisms including identification of a feedback point person among the water user committee members and also feedback volunteers within the community. The feedback mechanisms will provide an avenue for not only giving feedback about the project but also a channel for reporting protection related issues and concerns including GBV.7. Though hygiene promoters, carry out hygiene promoting both at OTP and SC's as well as at community a level. This hygiene promotion activities will be facilitate using information, education and communication materials in appropriate formats and language for communities. 8. Facilitated by the GBV officer, conduct open and closed forums with WUC, community key stakeholders, women groups and other community members on WASH related GBV. In addition, CEDS will carry out public awareness sessions on action against GBV especially related to WASH.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project will be implemented in collaboration with the local leadership and also with the support from the state government ministries especially ministry of water resources, irrigation and rural development. In identification of the locations for water points rehabilitation, CEDS will consult the ministry to provide a details on the existing boreholes within the county and the payams. CEDS will work with the already existing pump mechanics repair the boreholes and will set up the water user committee, a community system for managing the water resources. At the community level, this project will be implemented in collaboration with the payam leaders with an intention of ensuring that the payam leaders will take responsibility of supporting the water user committees.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is in line with WASH cluster strategic objectives and the 2006 South Sudan national water supply and sanitation policies.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. This project will be implemented in Aweil East which has been one of the most peaceful regions in south Sudan over the years. Though in August this year Aweil East experienced some security challenges due to rebel group that had been forced from Sudan into the region but the group was contained and there are no longer insecurity threats and the region is expected to remain secure as it has always been to allow the implementation of this proposed project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure sustainability of the this proposed project, the following activities will carried out;1. CEDS will collaborate with the state ministry of water resources and irrigation and rural development in the implementation of this project. This will also involve capacity strengthening of the ministy officials on WASH activities. It is expected that the ministry will continue to support the communities in the maintenance of the water and sanitation facilities as well as in hygiene promotion when this project comes to an end.2. The community based pump mechanics will be used to repair the boreholes. In this process, they will gain more skills in maintenance of boreholes and thus are able to support the community in maintenance of the boreholes when the project transitions.3. CEDS will strengthen the skills of the water user committee who will take over the management of the water resources when the project comes to an end. To prepare them for handover, CEDS will capacity build them on basic management skills that will be necessary for them after the project transitions. 4. All the hygiene promoters for this project will be recruited from within the targeted villages. CEDS will make efforts to capacity build these hygiene promoter to continue promoting good hygiene practices even after the transition of the project. Again, the knowledge on good hygiene practices passed to the community members during the hygiene promotion will be used by the households even after the transition of the project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

1. In line with the core humanitarian standards (CHS), CEDS will carry out meaningful consultation with the community member and all other community groups through community meetings as well as community leaders. CEDS will pay attention to consultation with the youths through focused groups discussions as well as other assessments in which the youths will be included. 2. As part of the project, CEDS will carry out specific activities including trainings to the water user committees and other community leaders to mitigate the risk of sexual exploitation. Again, CEDS has in place a policy on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse, CEDS at the start of this project will conduct a refresher training for all the staff and volunteers on this policy to enhance tier awareness on the same. This policy will as well be shared with other organizational collaborators. Again CEDS will put in place mechanisms where cases of sexual abuse including sexual exploitation will be reported at community level. At the office, CEDS has in place whistle-blowing mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation cases. CEDS will intentionally ensure that this is publicized. The policies, trainings as well as the channels for reporting the cases is expected to mitigate the risk of exposure to sexual violence and abuse. 3. To mitigate the risk of beneficiaries not having access to or using the complaints and responses mechanisms, CEDS will at the start of the project reassess the existing complaints and response mechanisms to realign them to the needs of the beneficiaries. As well CEDS will conduct consultations with community members to identify their most preferred feedback channels. CEDS will put in place multichannel feedback mechanisms again as a measure to minimize the risk of the beneficiaries not using the channels. CEDS will on-top of these carry out comprehensive community information provision of available feedback channels and how their feedback will be handled.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CEDS will ensure that the views of all beneficiaries are heard throughout this project and that the views are incorporated into the implementation of this project. CEDS has an accountability officer who will be tasked with this role. In order to ensure that different groups are given opportunity to give their feedback, the accountability officer will carryout focused group discussions and group meetings with different target groups e.g with a group of women, the disabled, girls, boys separately to collect their views which will then be incorporated into the project. The discussions in this groups will include which boreholes they want rehabilitated, how best can protection issues be addressed at the borehole and latrines being constructed, what NFIs are more beneficial to them, among others. Feedback to some of the queries raised during these meetings will also be shared with the specific groups in similar meetings. The officer will also have community meetings with all people in the targeted villages in order to get feedback and share responses to the feedback where necessary. In cases where the information is confidential, the officer will hold one on one discussions with individuals to get the feedback. A phone number will also be shared through which feedback can be shared. The accountability officer will also identify in each targeted village representatives of each beneficiary groups including women, the disabled who will form a community feedback committee who will be tasked with receiving feedback from the targeted beneficiaries which will then be discussed in a meeting with the same committee but chaired by the accountability officer. With these mechanisms, CEDS believes that all groups of beneficiaries will have a forum to share their feedback that will in effect be incorporated into the project.
