Project Summary

Although the formation of transitional government of national unity in February 2020 resulted in improved security however the subsequent shocks including COVID-19, flooding, inter-communal violence and political conflict in part of country continue to exacerbate the already fragile humanitarian context in South Sudan. According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO)-2020, 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. The HNO estimate that living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. The COVID-19 and current spike in positive cases in South Sudan has further increased the needs for WASH services considering a large population either living in congested PoC sites or in spontaneous camps.
Concern is the WASH cluster sub-national coordinator in Unity State, responsible for coordinating WASH interventions, ensuring good coverage and prioritization of WASH services to populations in need, identifying gaps in WASH responses and planning assessments and responses. The Bentiu PoC sites continue to host about 97,321 people. Concern is the WASH service provider in Bentiu PoC Sector 5 (hosting highest population) and implement WASH interventions in Rubkona, Guit and Leer counties.
Under this project, Concern will provide WASH services in Sector 5, Bentiu PoC and in Rubkona urban areas, Guit, Leer, Mayom and Koch counties with principal objective of reduction of morbidity and mortality. In Bentiu PoC, Concern will continue water provision through borehole, undertake waste management and repair and maintenance of latrines. Outside PoC, in Rubkona, Concern will continue the provision of safe water through operation of water treatment plant in Bentiu and the surface water treatment system in Rubkona. Concern will gradually handover the operations of Bentiu and Rubkona water system to state authorities while technically supporting the utility manager before exit. In Bentiu PoC, the WASH strategy envisions a complete takeover of the day-to-day management of the WASH services by the community. A life cycle cost analysis is currently in process to ensure that the community owns and runs the water services through a utility manager. Concern will conduct a WASH assessment in Guit, Leer, Mayom and Koch counties to expand WASH services provision in vulnerable locations as the return of south Sudanese refugees and potential transitioning of PoC will further increase WASH needs in return areas. In NBeG, the WASH response will be linked with Concern’s Health and Nutrition programme. Safe water access, sex-segregated latrines and medical waste disposal facilities will be ensured at health facilities. An adapted CLTS approach will be implemented in communities. Hygiene promotion informed by a recent WASH’Em survey will be part of all interventions conducted by hygiene promoters and by the CNVs, lead mothers and Male Change Agents from Concern’s Nutrition programme. Similarly Concern currently implements health and nutrition interventions in Kajo-Keji county in Central Equatoria, therefore the WASH services will be integrated in on-going nutrition and health program mainly repair and rehabilitation of water system, hand washing stations and latrines in health centres.
The WASH interventions are especially important during this COVID-19 outbreak, particularly ensuring that the Water quantity is sufficient for personal hygiene and for household use.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO)-2020, 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. The HNO estimated that 5.6 million (an increase from 5.2m last year) people would not have access to adequate WASH services in 2020. The people’s wellbeing and living standard eroded through high food insecurity, malnutrition, frail health system and limited access to safe water. According to WASH cluster severity mapping, across South Sudan, only 35% of the Households (HHs) reported having access to a borehole/tap stand, in under 30 minutes, without protection concerns. As per the HNO, about 77 per cent of households have no access to latrines. According to the WASH cluster severity mapping; only 39% households have sufficient access to safe water and only 11% have access to sanitation in Unity State. Due the unstable political situation and conflict, a large portion of IDPs continue to take shelter in Bentiu protection of civilian (PoC) sites. The PoC, Sector 5 has the highest population in the PoC and Concern has constructed a borehole and maintained the provision of water in sector 5 with current average water provision of 15l per person per day (l/p/d). Concern also improved latrine coverage in Sector 5, reducing the ratio of persons per latrine from 1:48 to 1:38 although this still remains below Sphere Standards of 1:20. There is a persistent need to continue supporting water and sanitation services in Bentiu PoC with overcrowding and congested conditions resulting in increased risks of waterborne diseases and malnutrition. Improvements in security around Bentiu and Rubkona towns has resulted in increased returns and traffic in / out of the POC, placing considerable strain on resources and humanitarian partners’ ability to adequately meet the WASH needs of both the PoC and town populations at large. Concern continues provision of water in Rubkona and Bentiu through Water Treatment Plant and Surface Water treatment (SWAT) producing an average of 1,180m3 of water from the two treatment plant. There is persistent need to continue operation of water system in Bentiu and Rubkona as well as capacity building of State authorities to ensure a proper handover and gradual exit. In Guit, access to improved water sources remains critically low, as per WASH cluster severity mapping, only 14.8% of households having access to a borehole/tap stand, in under 30 minutes, without protection concerns. In WASH cluster severity map, Mayom is reported among counties with lowest access to appropriate water quantity and sanitation. Similarly both Leer and Koch counties have limited access to safe water and sanitation facilities. In NBeG, according to the WASH cluster severity mapping; only 32% households have sufficient access to safe water and only 9% have access to sanitation. Access to water and sanitation is very limited in targeted areas of Concern program (Aweil West and North) therefore continuation of WASH assistance is critical to sustain the gain made during past few years. In Kajo-Keji- CE, Concern currently provides health and nutrition services, the water and sanitation at health facility level needs repair and maintenance. Due to the return of south Sudanese refugees, the demand for WASH assistance has increased further. As per the WASH cluster mapping, the overall access to water quantity in Kajo-Keji is between 21%-40% only with limited access to sanitation, WASH NFI and Handwashing stations. Considering high malnutrition rate, food insecurity, frail health system and current spike of COVID-19 infection, the WASH service provision is crucial for the wellbeing of affected population.

2 - Project activities

CO 1.1Concern will support access to safe water and promote safe hygiene in communities with high malnutrition and food insecurity. Based on assessed needs, Concern will provide water and sanitation in health centres to restore access to water and sanitation from our nutrition program. The communities residing in catchment areas of nutrition sites will also benefit from WASH services. The SAM children form nutrition sites will receive hygiene kits (cluster stock) and hygiene sessions. Through established network of the Mother to Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs), Male Change agents and CNVS, the care takers will be sensitised on the hygiene including awareness on COVID-19. CO-1.2 Concern will target counties reporting high diseases prevalence due to water related issues. Concern will continue to provide WASH services in Bentiu, Rubkona Town and in Bentiu PoC to reduce prevalence of WASH- related diseases and risk of outbreak. Based on assessed needs and coordination with WASH cluster Concern will also expand WASH activities in counties reporting high prevalence of water related disease. In Kajo-Keji, Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern’s WASH intervention integrated with health service provision where Concern will rehabilitate WASH infrastructure in health facilities and will target beneficiaries with hygiene promotion messages including awareness on COVID-19. Concern will promote latrines construction and use through CLTS approach in settled communities and return areas. CO-2.1Concern will provide WASH services in Bentiu PoC-5 and in Bentiu and Rubkona towns, Concern will support the State Ministry of lands housing and public utilities (SMoLHPU) with operation and maintenance of Surface Water Treatment (SWAT) and Bentiu Water Treatment Plant targeting IDPs, returnees and host communities. Based on assessed needs and in consultation with WASH cluster, Concern will expand its WASH program in other counties of Unity state which are most affected by regular flooding, high prevalence of malnutrition and food insecurity. CO-2.2 As per WASH cluster, 16 per cent of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns, representing 1.9M people (mainly women and children) facing a threat when trying to reach a toilet or a water point. Prior to the construction of water points and latrines Concern will conduct safety audit and use the WASH Cluster Protection Mainstreaming tip to ensure WASH specific GBV mitigations. The safety audit will analyse different needs, roles and dynamics to improve WASH interventions sensitivity to GBV risks. The adolescent girls will be provided with menstrual hygiene kits sourced from cluster stock. CO-3.1Concern is currently supporting WASH in Rubkona and Bentiu towns through water treatment plant and emergency surface water treatment units. A life cycle cost analysis is in process to ensure that the community owns and runs the water services through a utility manager. A willingness to pay, tariff setting and by laws formulation has been completed. Concern will gradually handover the operations while technically supporting the utility manager before exit. In Bentiu PoC, the WASH strategy envisions a takeover of the management of the WASH services by the community. The program will gradually reduce number of working days for the volunteers and focus on community management of WASH facilities. The counties most affected by flood and prioritised by WASH cluster will be targeted. Concern has trained and set up WASH committees in Guit to support in the maintenance of the water yards that provides long term access to safe water. In flood prone areas, Concern has redesigned the apron and drainage structures which has been approved and adopted by the SMoLHPU. Concern plan to identify and train hand pump technicians who will be linked to spare parts suppliers for resilience.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern informs them about right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In PoC sites Concern refers complaint to the camp manager and protection concerns to the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through lead mothers and CNVs hired from local community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Prior to initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern will conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, target criteria and their entitlements. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the distribution points.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engages with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The broader consultations with the community during the assessment will inform about the project design, priority activities and modality of assistance. The project/villages committees including most vulnerable such as women, elderly, disable will be formed at inception stage to engage in project delivery and monitoring. The project/villages committees from target beneficiaries share feedback to adapt the assistance accordingly. The regular monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with CCCM and Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Village Health Committees will conduct hazard mapping of the main health risks for women, men, girls and boys as well as PSNs such as elderly and disabled, and identify solutions to improve hygiene practices that are relevant to their respective communities. Latrines and bathing shelters constructed will be gender segregated along with being child-friendly and accessible to disabled and the elderly, and be lockable to minimize protection risks. Water Management Committees will also aim to have equal representation to ensure women’s, men’s, boys’ and girls’ water needs are met and to identify mitigation measures to ensure the program does not have any negative impacts on conflict. Water Management Committees will be supported to analyze the roles of women and men in accessing water and decision-making, and trainings will target both male and female community members. The location of water points will be identified in consultation with communities to ensure locations are safe and secure. Concern will ensure that water sources located in visible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from settlement. Traditionally, it is primarily women and girls who are responsible for water collection, so the addition of water points will reduce the need to travel long distances and minimize protection risks. Concern have a participatory approach for designing water activities and will consult community members to identify conflict risks associated with use of water resources such as through conflict mapping. Focus will be made on involvement of female casual workers in WASH programming to ensure safeguarding measures are in place and disseminated at community level. Efforts will be put to ensure WASH programs are not reinforcing exploitative gender stereotypes and inequalities but rather that all partners thrive to implement sensitive and transformative WASH interventions. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The access to adequate quality and quantity of water is essential for food production, health wellbeing and overall water born disease prevention. The income spend on water and health often taken from food budget thus the project will improve access to free water, sanitation and hygiene. Access to water will reduce community time spend on collection of water, thus the community particularly women can participate in income generating activities increasing households’ abilities to purchase nutritious foods. Concern also promote kitchen gardening at nutrition sites and home gardening, thus the access to water is crucial for food security outcomes contributing to decreased portion of population facing IPC 2, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Concern implement integrated program including provision of nutrition, health, WASH and FSL services. The beneficiaries will receive integrated assistance as far as possible. Concern will also coordinate with other partners to ensure that the gaps are filled and the targeting include most vulnerable.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The underlying causes of undernutrition are often directly and indirectly related to inadequate access to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). The project will improve access to water, improve sanitation and hygiene, reduction of waterborne diseases thus will contribute to decreased prevalence of GAM. The program is integrated with Concern on-going nutrition services in all target locations.
