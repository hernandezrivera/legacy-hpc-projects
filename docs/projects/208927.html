Project Summary

The goal of the project is to improve and maintain the health and security status of vulnerable IDPs and host communities by providing basic services in health, nutrition, water & sanitation and protection.
The Project expected to impact positively on the lives of communities in the targeted areas, through indicated activities, capacity building, and raise awareness on the health, WASH, nutrition, protection, and GBV.
This project is in line with the strategic objectives 2 and 3 as outlined below:
Strategic Objective 2: Directly respond to the conflict-driven protection needs of crisis-affected people through principled and conflict-sensitive assistance and advocacy as well as prevent and mitigate emerging protection risks, with due regard to international norms and standards.
This objective aims to address protection needs of 8.6 million people affected and those who are at protection risks through a principled and conflict-sensitive approach. This is to ensure protection risks are prevented and reduced and a conducive environment for the respect, protection and fulfillment of rights is created.
Strategic Objective 3: Provide safe, equitable, dignified and unhindered access to critical basic services and livelihoods opportunities for the most vulnerable groups to prevent further erosion of their coping abilities.
This objective aims to safeguard the wellbeing of 1.9 million vulnerable people and prevent further decline in their coping mechanisms. Provision of access to livelihood opportunities will enable affected people to recover and rebuild their lives.
This project will provide good opportunities in deferent sectors.
WASH: This project will increase access to sanitary and water services including awareness training and promotion of safe sanitary/hygiene practice to decrease incidence and spread of illness such as dairrhoea. The intervention will improve the waste disposal system, provide better water storage facilities, shower and washing equipment, hygiene education and materials. The poor qual ity of water is a major concern for the health of children in particular. According to the 2024 HNRP, the GOS as well as communities are not able to sustain and establish WASH services due to the limited financial capacity. This has caused outbreak of diseases such as cholera affected 4,114 persons with death of 131 people and viral hemorrhagic fever with more than 5,000 cases with 25 deaths. WASH services such as rehabilitation & establishment of new facilities is very crucial to minimize the occurrence of diseases. In addition, there is need for water trucking, establishment of new facilities including sanitation, and provision of hygiene and WASH supplies in the states with influx of IDPs. 60% of the IDPs are children prone to risk and should have access to appropriate WASH services that minimize the risk of physical and sexual violence.
Protection: Community-based protection approach is CDF centralized approach in accordance with SOPs and guidelines. This project will utilize Age, Gender and Diversity approach (AGD) and follow community-based protection approach; this will allow generating positive and sustainable outcomes. The analysis and data collection during the project will ensure the segregation of data by age and gender, which will allow addressing vulnerabilities and risks of boys and girls. The planned awareness raising interventions will ensure that sensitization will tackle gender subordination of women and girls and address gender-based violence. Community mobilization and sensitization meetings will be organized to enhance community participation for greater ownership and sustainability. Such participation will include the selection of beneficiaries, Identification, M&E, and supervision of project implementation, as well as any other risks, might arise during the implementation. Through the implementation period of the project, effective case management and referral systems will be reinforced in collaboration

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will provide integrated and specialized general protection, child protection, and GBV life-saving services. The project activities will contribute to the safety and dignity of beneficiaries at different levels. The project will train and mobilize CBPNs and social workers as front liners to monitor and report on the protection situation as well as contribute to mitigating community risks. The project has carried out a safety audit to asses protection risks especially that related to GBV and child protection and provided appropriate responses and prevention measures including improving access to protection services such as EVI assistance, case management, FTR, and Clinical Management of Rape to survivors and PSS. The project will also support the provision of dignity kits and personal hygiene kits (PKs) to vulnerable women and girls. The project adopted a community-based approach through consultation and sensitization of different groups to ensure their equal and active participation and meet their distinct needs. This ensures that all community groups including IDPs, returnees, refugees, host communities, and ethnic groups have equal access to project services. The project team will provide awareness raising and structured sessions that promote gender equality and advocate for the inclusion of PWD, minorities, and the empowerment of women and girls. The project has carefully followed the Do No Harm approach upon development to mitigate any harm that could be introduced through this intervention. During project implementation, CDF will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence, and abuse of their rights. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances to project sites. CDF has standards of safeguarding and PSEA which are implemented during the design and implementation of the project. The project management will focus on building the capacity of the staff and strengthening their understanding of GBV and PSEA will assign focal points for PSEA reporting. Segregated data will be collected and analyzed to address gender disparities.
