Project Summary

BRAC has been working on child protection since 2017 in the Rohingya camp with small geographic coverage and it has been expanded after influx happened in August 2017 to protect the child. During the influx, children exposed to child labour, child trafficking, child marriage, child separation as well as violence towards children in the camps. The refugees have adopted negative copying mechanism such as early marriage of their girls to increase food supply as well as to the host community in order to obtain legal status. Children especially adolescent girls are facing harassment e.g. abuse, exploitation, sexual violence, psychosocially destress and mental torture in the Rohingya camp. Community people have no sufficient knowledge on the protection and Rohingya community are used to send their children in distribution point, other harmful area. It is observed that the need of the protection for specialized structured psychosocial support through community- based child protection facilities, promote adolescents’ resilience and empowerment programming for both Rohingya community and Host Community are essential. BRAC child protection sector, keep in mind these risks, expanded its intervention and has started working almost all the camps to serve the children and adolescent. The Child Protection hub will serve as one stop center where a range of CP services will be available including case management, psychosocial support and adolescent engagement activities. The project will therefore provide life skills occupational skills to the adolescent boys and girls. The adolescents who are exposed to violence, will be assisted in collaboration with the GBV Sub-sector. In addition, awareness-raising on child marriage, child labor, SGBV, human trafficking etc. is required amongst all communities for creating a protective environment for the children and adolescents. As well, multi-sectoral referral arrangements and case management services need to be strengthened.
The purpose of the project is to make sure that children who are at risk and has a protection concern are receiving adequate services through the intervention. BRAC Child Protection sector will serve 11960 individual person that include children, adolescent, community people. The targeted number are the beneficiary from Rohingya Refugee camp and Host community. The project will serve the children from 25 Rohingya camp and 3 areas of host community.

Needs

There has been rise in the number of children exposed to child labour, child trafficking, child marriage, child separation as well as violence towards children in the camps. The refugees have adopted negative copying mechanism such as early marriage of their girls to increase food supply as well as to the host community in order to obtain legal status. Lack of livelihood opportunities as well as formal education has increased risks faced by adolescent, climate disasters has left children and adolescent increasingly exposed to child trafficking, abuse and exploitation. This is particularly true for the most vulnerable and marginalized among them, including adolescent girls, who often live in female- or child-headed households and face high-levels of child marriage and early childbearing as well as inability to access services as most of them are confined in the vicinity of their homes. These children have experienced widespread trauma, and many continue to be subject to violence, including sexual and gender-based violence within the camps . The project will therefore provide life skills, vocational skills and occupational skills to the adolescent boys and girls. The adolescents who are exposed to violence, will be assisted in collaboration with the GBV Sub-sector. In addition, awareness-raising on child marriage, child labor, SGBV, human trafficking etc. is required amongst all communities for creating a protective environment for the children and adolescents. As well, multi-sectoral referral arrangements and case management services need to be strengthened. Girls are often the first to experience barriers in accessing services, and the last to be consulted about their needs. Advocacy and capacity building of the child protection staff including other actors need to also be addressed. There is also the need to increase coverage of specialized structured psychosocial support through community- based child protection facilities, promote adolescents’ resilience and empowerment programming for both Rohingya community and Host Community. The Child Protection hub will serve as one stop center where a range of CP services will be available including case management, psychosocial support and adolescent engagement activities. There is also the need to strengthen our case management for vulnerable and at-risk children by ensuring proper couching, supervision and mentorship of staff to ensure effective and timely response. Adolescents, through adolescent groups/ clubs, will be supported to build their resilience through development of Life Skills, engagement in sports and cultural activities, as well as development of livelihood skills, and to participate in and support the community in areas such as environmental management or green gardening. Community-based child protection committees will be strengthened to prevent, mitigate and respond to Child Protection risks.

Activities or outputs

1. BRAC will introduce the Humanitarian Play Lab curriculum in the Child Friendly Spaces (static and mobile structures) with a harmonized and holistic services for children in the host community and the refugee camp with special regards to age, gender and diversity. BRAC will collaborate with nutrition and education sector in referring children to participate in the CFS. BRAC will also work with Religious leaders in including religion in the CFS activities. 2. BRAC will establish Child Protection Hubs in both the refugee camp and in the host community to provide integrated services for adolescents.3. BRAC will promote the adolescent girls and boys engagement in both the host and refugee community through collaboration with the education sector in referring children for education. BRAC will also provide them with life skills, leadership skills and occupational skills training. BRAC will collaborate with partners on the ground in organizing for education and leadership debates.4. BRAC will provide specialized and structured psychosocial support for boys and girls affected by memories of violence and exposure to violence as well as trauma as a result of missing family members. 5. BRAC will provide case management services to children at risk including unaccompanied and separated children, children with specific needs and will collaborate with the GBV sector partners in responding to children exposed to SGBV. 6. BRAC will mainstream Child Protection in other sectors through capacity building on PSEA and CPSS guideline on child labour to ensure children safety while accessing the services. BRAC will also work with this sectors in monitoring risks facing children and referring children in need of child protection services.7. BRAC will enhance protection environment for children in the community through Community Based Child Protection Committees who will monitor CP risks and develop community response those risks. 8. BRAC will support staff working on case management and psychosocial support through provision of case conferencing, strengthen coaching and supervision by having specialized psychologists in the field for mentorship, capacity build staff on case management and also promote activities on staff self-care. 9. BRAC will support the Child Protection Sub-sector in coordination at the camp level as well as in Cox's Bazar by assigning staff as focal points for child protection and to deputize the CPSS Coordinator.10. BRAC will strengthen the Lost Child and Caregiver meet up point to act as a centre for lost children during a disaster and strengthen the CP structures to reduce the risk (DRR).

Key project/performance indicators

1) 6200 children, including adolescents, identified in need who are benefiting from the humanitarian play lab curriculum and structured Psycho-social Support (PSS) services through Static and mobile Child Friendly Space.2) 2200 adolescents and youth receiving services including life skills, and VT, Self-reliance activities3) 700 identified Children at risk who received individual case management services to meet his/her unique needs4) 2700 people reached by community led prevention messaging on key child protection risks and social norms through Community Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC), Positive Parenting sessions and awareness raising activities/ workshop5) 160 staff trained on key child protection approaches delivered to staff of government, local counterparts and humanitarian actors.
