Project Summary

According to FSNAU Technical Release of September 2019, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are projected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2019. An estimated one million children under the age of five are also likely face acute malnutrition through mid-2020.
Though Hagaa rains intensified in August along coastal parts of southern Somalia, the overall amount and distribution was below average. Reports from agro pastoral livelihood zones indicate heavy pest infestation on late planted crops (stalk borer and armyworms). The most affected crop is late-planted/replanted maize as farmers tried to take advantage of Karan rains which were completely destroyed. In the southern and central regions, poor rangeland conditions persisted due to below average Gu and Hagaa rains. (August 2019 Monthly Rainfall and NDVI (Issued September 23, 2019)
According to FSNAU, current high levels of food insecurity is expected to deteriorate. This is driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu season (April-June), which began late and finished early. Many households are still recovering from the severe 2016/2017 drought or have been affected by conflict, and 2.6 million people remain displaced. The Gu cereal harvest failed in most regions, leaving most poor agropastoral and riverine households unable to meet their minimum food needs given significantly below-average food stocks and income from seasonal agricultural employment. In pastoral areas, the impact of the drought on livestock led to a lack of access to milk, and many poor households have accumulated large debts while struggling to feed their families and rescue their remaining livestock. Although the 2019 Deyr (OctoberDecember) rains are forecast to be average to above average, the positive impact on food security will not be realized until late 2019.
Aid Vision proposed project is aimed at providing access to lifesaving and sustaining food security and livelihood interventions to 24,000 crisis affected population in Banadir and Middle Shabelle Regions, Aid Vision has presence in the two regions for close to two decades and established networks of community structures and local authorities. The project will provide Cash Based interventions, rehabilitation of farming infrastructure through canal rehabilitation, provision farm equipment and tools, supply of certified seeds, fuel subsidies, training of farmers on contemporary farming techniques, provision of fertilizers and provision of agricultural extension services. It is envisioned that the interventions will go a long way in improving food security in the target regions and enhance community resilience to shocks in future.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1: Provide immediate food security and livelihoods assistance through Food vouchers (commodity voucher) valued at 60 USD to 6000 women, girls, boys and men including people with disabilities and minorities in crisis or stress for 3 months per selected households of IDPs and host community, with consideration to women headed households, HHs with malnourished children (boys and girls),elderly persons with no economically active persons taking care of them . This method of intervention is feasible in the target area as : a) food commodities readily available in the market b) credible vendors to provide the food items are locally available c) the intervention will not distort the market as no food items are brought from elsewhere d) local businesses will be supported as the target beneficiaries' purchasing power will be increased – redeem vouchers from selected vendors within beneficiaries’ locality e) beneficiaries will be spared of the indignity of queuing for long hours at distribution points as they will get their supplies anytime of their choice f) it reduces protection risks to women and girls g) it is easy to ensure exclusivity and targeting with gender and age lenses h) distribution points/vendors store are within the reach of the elderly, children, pregnant and lactating women and thus reducing protection risks of beneficiaries. Activity: 1.2: Rehabilitation/excavation of farm canals. Old and blocked canals will be rehabilitated and new ones excavated using CFW modality. This will increase the acreage farms under irrigation thereby reducing food insecurity. It will also have the multiplier effect of putting cash in the pockets of young men and women thus giving gainful engagement to youths would have otherwise been taken advantage of by armed groups or remained idle. Activity 2: Distribution of emergency agricultural inputs packages to targeted 200 farmers. The package will be comprised of: provision of tractor hours, assorted certified seeds and fertilizers to 200 farmers (40% women) in Balcad, district Middle Shabelle Regions. This will give impetus to recovery and build resilience benefiting around 9,000 women, girls, boys and men affected by crisis to protect their assets and generate income for their households. Provision of assorted fertilizers and pesticides will be done to improve soil nutrients and protect farmers from loss of yields to pests respectively.Activity 3: Training and provision of agricultural extension services to 200 farmers on good farming practices. This will increase farmers capacity and build their resilience to shocks. Further, this will have positive impact on both quality and quantity of yields as farmers will benefit from training on best farming practices from land preparation to post-harvest -storage and marketing. The farmers will also be trained on basic book-keeping. Farmers will also have opportunity to share knowledge and experiences. Provision of agricultural extension services to farmer will enable them receive guidance on best farming practices and enhance quality of their produce. Trained agronomist and agricultural extension officers will be available to targeted farmers throughout the project life ensure farmers get guidance and have their right skills/knowledge in real time.

Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups and socially excluded groups like women, children, people living with disabilities, elderly and people suffering from chronic diseases risk being excluded in consultation, decision-making and targeting as beneficiaries. This will be addressed by deliberately targeting them in the selection criteria of community representatives and target beneficiaries. All committees formed will be representative of all sectors of the community, with particular focus on the marginalized and the often socially excluded groups, decisions making process will involve women, girls and boys who are often excluded. The project will provide clear guidelines on who the beneficiaries are, and how they are expected to benefit/participate in the project.There is the risk of women and girls exposed sexual violence, sexual exploitation and abuse. To safeguard women and girls from being exploited/abused by organizational staff, project staff and vendors will be inducted on Aid Vision’s Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse policy and staff code of conduct that stipulate the consequences of engaging in SEA. The community will also be educated on their rights and how to report on incidences of sexual exploitation and abuse by organizational staff and contractors. Sexual violence may be meted on women and girls on the way to/from distribution sites. To forestall this, vendors selected will be from within the proximity of the communities served, thus girls and women will not have to travel for long distance to get their entitlements/ redeem their vouchers. The food voucher system used will be flexible to allow beneficiaries redeem them at any time of their convenience. This will go a long way in the protection of women and girls from sexual violence.There is also the potential risk of humanitarian assistance affecting peaceful coexistence and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. For instance, selection of Food Voucher and CFW beneficiaries may trigger hostilities between IDPs and Host communities or agrarian communities and pastoralists. Aid Vision will, from the outset, conduct sensitization and education of the project to make who should benefit from it clear and involve both IDPs and Host Communities in decision-making. The selection criteria developed will be inclusive and participatory. The project will also target the most vulnerable of IDPs and Host communities that meet the agreed upon selection criteria.

The major risk associated with the project is exclusion of women headed households, marginalized and minority groups during targeting. To mitigate this, the project will give special attention women headed households, marginalized and minority groups, people with disabilities, elderly and households with large family size. The project will prioritize needs and needs only and adopt community-based targeting methodology.The project will target both genders for training, community management structures and also final beneficiaries. Project will provide timelines for training and community engagements that are conducive for both genders to benefit and ensure protection concerns of women and girls are addressed.
