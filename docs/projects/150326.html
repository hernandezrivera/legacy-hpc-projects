Project Summary

The project aims at providing inclusive, comprehensive and holistic services through the existing facilities at CFTA (SRH, psychosocial and legal counselling & representation and referrals, dignity kits, and support to women survivors of breast cancer, also it will contribute to the increasing awareness of women and girls rights including women and girls with disabilities, reducing social stigmas, and campaigning for zero tolerance towards GBV. In addition, economic empowerment of survivals and women most vulnerable through initiatives that are based on their skills and abilities. Community-led, accessible, evidence-based campaigns on S&GBV prevention, particularly on issues surrounding the rights of women & girls are led by rights holders and based on international human rights frameworks. The
advocacy actions will utilize new creative tools. The project will be located in Albureig Camp, however it will service all the middle camps area through the centre itself and the outreach program. Women and men, girls and boys are the main target group of this project

Needs

The 2030 agenda of SDGs included a standalone goal (Number 5) on gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls. Underneath this is the target: eliminate all forms of violence against all women and girls including sexual and other types of exploitation.S&GBV remains significant problem in the oPt, undermining human rights and impeding social development. The baseline study of CFTA (November 2017) estimated that 71.2% of women and girls are exposed to psychological violence; 48%.8 denied access to resources andopportunities within the family and community; significantly in sexual and reproductive health, education and community participation. Additionally, 41.4% of women andgirls are exposed to physical violence, including beatings and burns. 39.5% of women and girls experienced forced marriage or/and marriage under age of 18. VAW/G is under reported and classified as an important issue, also responsive services are limited. The Gap analyses study of CFTA (2017) highly prioritized access to multi-sectorial services and advancement of referral pathway to women and girls. This was enforced by recent UN women (December 2017). The strategy is to make high qualityservices available and accessible, work on attitude change and empowering of women and girls specially survivals. Responding to the needs of women who are mostvulnerable calls for integrated and tailored sectoral responses that ensure “no one is left behind”. The key challenges is the politicization􀁂on of the humanitarian situationwhich directly affects the services. The lack of financial resources and continued deterioration of the humanitarian situation are other key challenges. The lack of qualityand sensitivity/confidentiality to providing GBV services is the highest risk encountered.Cultural acceptance of VAWG is high, and discriminatory laws re-victimize survivors; the strict social codes of segregation between men and women meant that women and girls are confined to the private sphere of their homes and are mainly reliant on male family members for their livelihoods as well as access to critical information about the security situation, work opportunities, humanitarian assistance and services. Added to that, an increase in incidence of domestic violence has been recorded in Gaza over the past years, which is largely attributed to the impact of the occupation, the internal Palestinian political divide, high poverty and unemployment and the overall restrictions on movement of people and goods as enforced by the blockade. According to most recent reports on GBV in Gaza, is still in the increase Disability is one main source of vulnerability that leads to gender based discrimination. Although women with disabilities experience many of the same forms of violence that all women experience, hence vulnerabilities stemming from gender inequalities and disability intersect; violence takes on different forms and has more serious consequences on their well-being, mobility and access to services. According to recent research conducted by the Women’s Affairs Centre in 2016, 2.4% of the total population from Gaza suffers from disabilities, 45% of them are females who face particular challenges and discrimination. According to the research 59% of the research respondents (total of 988 women with disabilities) have suffered of one form of violence, 31% have reported being subjected to neglect and house confinement, 23.8% were denied the right to get married and start a family. While confronted with many legal and psychosocial challenges, 91% of the respondents had never received legal services and 33.9% believed that the availability of psychosocial support was limited. These challenges come against a reality of limited availability of tailored services to women with disabilities.

Activities

Access to quality health services to 800 women & 200 girls suffering from GBV including persons with disabilities: this will include: gynecology check-up and treatment , ultrasound check ups, clinical early detection of breast cancer and STDs, internal referrals within the center, external referral, pregnancy follow up, diagnostics of SGBV, pharmaceuticals and family planning methods. Providing full comprehensive services to women survivors of breast cancer, this will target 750 women per year as well as a minimum of 4000 through the awareness raising and manual check-ups Providing pharmaceuticals, supplements, vitamins and family planning methods on bases at the clinic Providing laboratory examination on daily bases, this will target 1200 women 300 men and 50 girls Providing dignity kits following FGDs with the target groups to identify priorities of items to be included, this will target 400 women (the kit may include items for girls depending on the FGDs for assessment of the needs. Access to counselling targeting women, girls, men and boys including people with disabilities (1100 persons including 12 women with disabilities ): Providing psychosocial individual for 600 women and 120 girls , 200 couples and groups counselling services for victimized cases Providing legal counselling and court representations for victims. this will target 500 women Providing counselling sessions with 60 men and 10 boys aiming at supporting women and girls Conducting two different peer support groups for survivors which will include 80 women and 20 girls Conducting 6 different recreational trips and open recreational days for 240 women survivors of GBV Conducting emotional supervision for the counselling staff (7 women and 1 man) and leaders of the Protection UNIT Receiving, documenting and conducting case management services with clear referral pathway for women and girls in coordination with Legal Task force, GBV WG and MHPSS WG with the several actors and clusters for 200 cases Awareness and advocacy activities targeting at least 5000 women, 200 girls, 100 men and 100 boys including people with disabilities: Implementing outreach, home visits and workshops which will target a minimum of 15000 persons through social media, workshops, radios etc Conducting 4 different women and girls (35 women and 20 girls) led advocacy by using creative advocacy tools such as social media, photography, video including testimonies by survivors/ or position paper & fact-sheets Organizing debate sessions led by women leaders/survivals about women rights issues in Gaza which will be attended by 120 people Organizing 1 questioning session right barriers by the women leaders which will be attended by 50 persons Implementing survivors led economic initiatives based on successful previous projects by CFTA. Coaching and technical support targeting 60 women. (quality of products and packaging, accounts, marketing, promotion) and other ad-hoc training which will be designed for each initiative. Setting up the selection criteria to include: most vulnerable women, ability of women to lead such initiatives, social and economic status of women, number of family members, women being the main bread winning of the family, women who are survivors of GBV or the social stigma of the breast cancer (ex: women who got divorced because of cancer). Providing practical in-job training which will support women to run their small initiatives. following the training, women will be divided according to skills (some inatiatives will be run jointly by several women) the WHC will provide follow up on weekly bases and will support promoting the nominatives. All needed items for the initiatives will be bought by the center directly & no cash transfer will be made. e. Implementing adolescents led community and peer to peer awareness & advocacy initiatives which will be led by 10 girls and will reach out to 500 community members
