Project Summary

As part of SHAFAK Gender-based Violence Program (GBV) in 2019. SHAFAK plans to respond to the growing needs of conflict-affected and most vulnerable families (women, adolescent girls, men, adolescent boys, and people with disability) within northwest Syria by providing GBV services projects.
The projects' activities will respond to the Gender-Based Violent (GBV) survivors by providing free GBV services within women and girls safe space (WGSS) centers. The response services will target (women, adolescent girls, and women and girls with disabilities) GBV survivors.
The WGSS centers will provide GBV case management, as well as multi protection support activities, such as recreational activities, psychosocial support, life skills, and razing awareness sessions for the center beneficiaries.
Furthermore, the centers will allocate specific activities to target young mothers (age under 18). The activities will aim to raise the young mothers aware of GBV risks, RH, and CP issues, by using young mother club curriculum developed by UNFPA.
Moreover, the outreach’s teams will work on GBV prevention activities by conducting raising awareness sessions and GBV campaigns for men, and adolescent boys, in the community, to increase their role in preventing GBV in the community.
On another hand, and in case of emergency situation the outreach team will response by Provide integrated emergency protection services (provision of specialized child protection services and specialized GBV services, and as conducting risk education for at-risk groups), Disseminate key messages, including GBV, CP, Risk Education and provision of Non- Specialized Protection Services, Distribute dignity kits in coordination with UNFPA, Refer MWBG to non-specialized Services, Identify CP / GBV cases, refer to specialized CP Services, and provide direct support to high priority cases.
Under emergency the coordination between SHAFAK and protection actors will set in two levels; in the field by support coordination at field level, through regular meetings organized by field focal point, and in protection cluster level by implement protection monitoring assessment and Child Protection Situation Monitoring in cooperation with the PMTF and CPMTF.
Also, the outreach teams will use the awareness raising toolkits; which developed by GBV sub-cluster by targeting men and adolescent boys in GBV awareness raising sessions.
The GBV program teams will ensure in delivering the protection information to survivors in an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and options to access, using the GBV sub-cluster SOPs.

SHAFAK's GBV program will work on increasing the capacity of humanitarian actors, as well as the protection workers, in GBV and protection topics. The GBV capacity building component in the program will include GBV, PSS, and PFA training.

Needs

According to Protection Monitoring Task Force (PMTF) quarterly report April-Jun18 indicates that communities in north and northwest Syria continue to experience challenges in accessing basic needs and services, and face a variety of, and often inter-linked protection risks. Also, the GBV-focused analysis of FGDs, as well as the KI interviews conducted in 2017 for the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), identified this to be a common issue, mainly affecting women and girls who are trying to access aid (Voices from Syria, 2018). Moreover, the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA 2019) has indicated that girls and boys of all ages still face violence in multiple life situations: at home, at school, at work, and in the community. Adolescence, in particular, is a time when exposure to new forms of violence increases. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms of gender-based violence, including sexual violence. According to “Protection Sector DRAFT HNO 2019 chapter (2019) v.3 – 09-12.2018” women and girls are disproportionately affected by all forms of gender-based violence in Syria, in both the public and private spheres. Also, women who have experienced divorce and widowhood and women and girls with disabilities continue to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination, as well as forced marriages. Men are particularly affected by restrictions of freedom of movement and forms of violence directly associated with the conduct of hostilities. Qualitative sources indicate that during situations of hostilities and displacement, persons with a disability are at increased risk of separation from their families or careers, or the assistive products which support their independence. Under the assessments part in Protection Sector DRAFT HNO 2019 chapter (2019) v.3 – 09-12.2018; the disproportionate impact of Gender-Based Violence on women and girls the estimates have indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, in the market, at distribution sites, etc.) as well as sexual violence, often associated with kidnapping, is a concern raised by consulted women and girls and contributes to psychosocial stress and limits their movements. Additionally, various means of technology—e.g., texting and social media—are used to harass and facilitate early/forced marriages sexually. The restriction on freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately curtails their rights, with widows who have experienced divorce and widowhood and adolescent girls disproportionately impacted. The shame and stigma surrounding sexual violence mean that survivors often do not talk about violence when it happens. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family. Voices report 2019 Assessments reaffirmed that GBV continues to pervade the lives of women and girls, particularly adolescent girls in Syria. Most of the participants across FGDs indicated that they faced sexual violence and harassment, domestic violence and early/forced marriage. Through SHAFAK experience in working on GBV projects since 2016 in northwest Syria. Based on FGD who had conducted by SHAFAK MEAL team at the end of 2018, with women and girls in Ma'ra Misrin, Idlib city, and Big Orm rural Aleppo. Early marriage remains the main problem facing girls, as well as forced marriage, domestic violence, and marital rape. Despite the development of a multispectral referral system, GBV survivors continue to face challenges to access to relief and shelter services compared to obtain to RH service

Implementation of activities

1. Provide safe spaces for women and girls where they feel comfortable to come and to express themselves without fear of judgment or harm, as well as build their social networks, receive support from their peers and have fun. Also provides a place where women can access confidential services, discuss issues and concerns with other women and professional staff, and have access to other safe and non-stigmatizing GBV response services. 2. Provide specialized GBV services to survivors, including case management, psycho-social support, caring for children survivors, and referrals. 3. Provide for GBV empowerment and prevention activities including recreational activities, vocational training, life skills activities, individual and group counseling (non-specialized), Psychological First Aid (PFA) and awareness-raising activities. 4. Provide for GBV comprehensive services for adolescent girls including recreational activities, vocational training, life skills activities, individual and group counseling (non-specialized), Psychological First Aid (PFA) and awareness-raising activities. 5. Training of case managers on SOP and Case Management. 6. Training of WGSS staff on WGSS minimum standards using relevant curriculum.7. Provide supervision to GBV service providers, particularly case managers through case discussions and learning sessions. Under protection emergency response, SHAFAK is an emergency phocal point for Idleb; coordination will take place with actors in Syria and Turkey at the district level, as well as higher level coordination with Shafak, IHSAN, SEMA, HRO, Global communities, and the protection cluster. SHAFAK will coordinate the response in case of an emergency. SHAFAK will coordinate within our respective districts with non-protection actors to facilitate referrals, share information aboutprotection services, and engage in joint activities. Monitoring Activities: Shafak will monitor the GBV program progress through an integrated mechanism with appropriate mixed methods collecting both quantitative and qualitative data collection using proper and advanced data collection tools and mobile data collection tools. The monitoring utilizes a customized monitoring tool developed by SHAFAK to evaluate GBV program performance in critical areas of operation, including staff management, visits reports, activity management, record beneficiaries, service quality, and beneficiary’s satisfaction, among others. Monitoring results and recommendations will share with each team to provide feedback and indicate needed points of action. SHAFAK will utilize a GBV program-specific Performance Monitoring Plan (PMP) to track the performance and progress of the program indicators. For GBV case management activities, the GBV supervisor will monitor the quality of GBV case management services by uses a goal-setting and well-being form to evaluate and measure improvements and achievements against benchmarks outlined in individual case management plans. Furthermore, the GBV supervisor will observe the performance of case managers while providing case management service.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (1): Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by distributors and male beneficiaries while queuing and before/after distributionsRisk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure:- recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old)- make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA- set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children Risk (2):Tension between the host community and IDPs (who were evacuated)Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure:- clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the activity.- harmonize the assistance delivered as per Cluster instructions and guidelines.- coordinate with the local council in the targeted area as well as being active in field coordination Risk (3): Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities (PWD)Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure:- Register all adult women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients of Kits distributed- During post -distribution follow-up, understand how women, children, and other groups accessed the distributed goods and their feedback on the process and materials
