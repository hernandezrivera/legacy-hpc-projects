Project Summary

Shafak Organization proposes to mitigate/respond to the protection risks faced by the conflict-affected people in Jabal Samaan, Jarablus, Afrin, Al-Bab and Azaz districts in Aleppo governorate, as well as in Harem, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Ariha and Idleb districts in Idleb governorate through a community centers and protection mobile teams.
The purpose of this project is to empower local communities and conflict-affected individuals in North West Syria, and support their role in building society and in determining their future by provision of protection services and emergency protection services to 141700 IDPs and host community members, through four community centers, two protection tents and 25 mobile outreach teams. The provided protection services will comprise both mitigation and response services as well as specialized and non-specialized protection services.
The proposed project will offer gender and age appropriate general protection service including, structured PSS, recreational and MHPSS activities, case management involving life-saving assistance to vulnerable people in ensuring their access to existing services, referral to specialized and non-specialized services, awareness raising on protection related topics, life skills and vocational training to enhance the community resilience and livelihoods opportunities, legal assistance and legal awareness raising session of HLP and CSD through qualified and well-trained legal teams, protection monitoring, as well as supporting community committees. Shafak will maintain gender-balanced team in all its projects in order to facilitate the access of all gender groups to the services in line with the humanitarian principles
Moreover, training on HLP and CSD will be delivered to partners’ legal staff and community actors in Syria and Turkey by specialized legal staff in accordance with the protection cluster and HLP WTG guidance and SOPs.
These services will be offered to vulnerable IDPs and host community members with particular emphasis on quality services to the following groups:
• Women headed households
• Children headed households
• UASC, CAAFAG and Child labor
• PWDs
• Elderly women and men
• Family has children at risk of child labor
• UASC and orphans who are being cared for by another family.
• New IDPs who fled their home and have no financial means
In case of emergencies, the project activities will be shifted to respond to the emergency protection needs of new displaced people delivering the protection emergency package like: PFA, referrals and case fund support to respond to the urgent needs of IDPs
Shafak will consult with the community members at the beginning of the project to introduce the project activities and have them involved in the selection of the community centers locations, as well as Shafak will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on protection topics such as; CPMS, Safe identification and referral and communication skills to enable them identify and refer people in need to the case management team. Moreover, the community committees will play a significant role in raising protection issues that are faced by the community and mobilizing them to participate in project activities.
The activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social specificities of targeted populations. All static centers will be rehabilitated and well equipped to accompany PWDs.
COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken in line with WHO, Global Protection Cluster and the Protection Cluster’s Guidance, including strict enforcement of physical distancing, minimizing of participants per activity to maximum eight people and maintaining the social distancing between BNFs and staff, the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) by staff/beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

10 years of conflict has created a man-made catastrophe in Syria, where needs resulting from food insecurity, loss of livelihoods, housing, property and sub-standard living conditions, impact of COVID 19, economic crises and sanction, among others, result in specific protection risks and consequences and further reduced the capacities of the internally displaced and vulnerable host communities to mitigate protection risks. Within the people in need, many groups have heightened vulnerability towards protection risks such as people with disabilities, older people, women, adolescent girls and children, child laborers, child survivors of violence, juveniles, female-headed households, vulnerable returnees and women who have experienced divorce or widowhood. These are further compounded with a combination of one or more factors.Civil documentation, HLP issues and freedom of movement remain major and countrywide protection issues. Civil documentation was reported by respondents as occurring in all assessed communities. HLP and freedom of movement were reported as occurring in 59% of assessed communities. The occurrence of these issues derives directly from the effects of hostilities and forced displacement.Lack/loss of civil documentation continues to be described as a protection issue affecting all population groups. (Draft HNO 2021)As per Shafak need assessment in Dec 2020: 72% of KIIs reported the increase in the protection concerns in the area. Majority of KIIs reported the shortage of proper services for PWDs. 81% of KIIs reported that there is a need for PSS. 67% reported that older people are no longer able to access protection services. 50% reported an increase in violence against women and girls due to COVID-19.

5 - Implementation of activities

Project activities will be delivered through the static community centers, protection tents and outreach teams and will include:-Establish referral pathway in the area to support CM services. (service map to be updated on a monthly basis) by the outreach workers.-CM services for 1800 cases, cases will be identified by outreach teams, and referrals from the community committees. -Case fund support (IPA) for 2500 vulnerable cases. This service will complement the CM services and will be delivered by the Case workers. -Structured-PSS activities (within the center and outreach) for 10000 individuals. -Non-structured PSS and PFA for 30,800 through the centers and the mobile team by the PSS and outreach workers. -Referral for 5300 cases, these cases will be identified through the project activates and referred to the available services based on the service map. -Awareness session on Protection-related topics and PSEA for 68000 (13000M, 21000W, 17000B,17000G) through the centers and the mobile team.-Legal awareness for 20000 BNFs on HLP and CSD. -Legal counselling for 2500 (1400 M, 800 W, 100 B, 200 G)-Trainings for humanitarian workers on HLP, CSD, gender mainstreaming, protection mainstreaming and other protection topics for 800 staff and humanitarian workers (400 M, 400 W). -PM assessment in cooperation with the PMTF in 80 communities. -Supporting 25 Community CommitteesMonitoring plan:monitoring visits will be carried via male and female MEAL staff using session evaluation form, checklist, pre-post evaluation and satisfaction surveys.CM will be monitored and evaluated the trained CM team ensuring confidentiality principle through satisfaction survey tool and checking the CM files by the CM supervisors.

Risks: 1. Sexual exploitation and abuse by the project personnel. Risk2. Tension between IDPs themes selves, and with the host community as well. Risk 3: Transmission of COVID 19 to project staff and BNFs due to project activities:Mitigating Measure: 1- All staff, volunteers and associated personnel should sign the policy on PSEA and child safeguarding, receive induction on PSEA and safeguarding children and are aware of their obligation to report harm to children/SEA/misconduct. All staff and volunteers receive regular training/refreshers on PSEA and child safeguarding Policies. Shafak has developed a policy on PSEA and child safeguarding and communicated it to all staff in local languages. Make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA. Set-up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children 2- clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the activity. - harmonize the assistance delivered as per Cluster instructions and guidelines. - coordinate with the local council in the targeted area as well as being active in field coordination 3--Project team will follow all COVID 19 prevention measure to avoid the risk of transmission the disease between the staff and beneficiaries.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security related SOPs in place.1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to pre-empt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly.Risk 2: Loss and/or damages to program equipment and supplies and/or staff injured or killed due to airstrikes or other security-related incidents; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:2.1 Ensure adequate security/surveillance at Shafak warehouses, facilities, and offices.2.2 Security related SOPs in place.2.3 Minimizing value/quantity of items at any given site while ensuring balance with programmatic needs and contingency.Risk 3: Road cutting due to internal clashes and IED detonations; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:3.1 providing clearance for the field team before conducting any missions, identifying safe roads from/ to the both centers and informing staff to use it.3.2 Avoiding areas with a history of explosion/bombardment incidents.Risk 4: Aid diversion, corruption, fraud; Likelihood: low; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:4.1 Appropriate mitigation measures within the procurement process (including mandatory reviews from Compliance and other relevant departments).4.2 Anti-fraud, anti-corruption, code of conduct, humanitarian principles and other relevant trainings provided to all staff.
