Project Summary

Clinic-Based Nutrition Services: Building off the established rapport in Tel Hajar clinic, SP will address the pervasive rates of malnutrition and poor nutrition practices by using the clinic as a base for outpatient management of MAM and uncomplicated SAM. A nutrition specialist will work with the clinic's pediatricians, midwives, and Obstetrician-Gynecologists to identify and treat through the provision of RUTF PLW and children under five years old months with SAM and MAM. Nutrition screening, education, and referrals will continue after SP's partnership ends; nutrition services will continue to be provided at a high capacity to the community.
Community-Based Mobile Nutrition Services: Three full-time CHWs will work under the supervision of the existing CHW team lead in the rural communities in Hassakah, Serekaniye and Washokani IDP camps, estimated by the NES Nutrition WG to host a combined total of 56,000 children aged 6-59 months with SAM or MAM. CHWs will target communities within a one-hour radius of the clinic with systematic nutrition assessments to screen for and identify children ages 6-59 months and PLWs in need of RUTF. The Nutrition Specialist will accompany the CHW TL and CHW team to distribute RUTF, conduct physical examinations of beneficiaries, and provide nutrition education on an established schedule.
Confirmed target locations include Washokani and Serekaniye camps, the Hassakah National Hospital, Tel Hajar Clinic and the Shaddadi PHC. Other target locations to be selected by highest need pending SMART survey and KAP survey data currently being collected by the Nutrition WG.
Beneficiary Eligibility Criteria
 Children between the ages of 6 and 59 months at the commencement of this program whose: MUAC score is 11.5- 12.5cm will be admitted to the program as MAM.
 Children between the ages of 6 and 59 months at the commencement of this program whose: MUAC score is <11.5- 12.5cm will be admitted to the program as SAM.
 Children between the ages of 6 and 59 months of age at the commencement of this program who present with edema, severe infection (including but not limited to respiratory infections), severe acute watery diarrhea, bloody diarrhea, and/or fail the appetite test will not be eligible for inclusion in the program but will be referred to inpatient treatment at the Heckma Hospital stabilization center (SC).
 Children <6 months of age who present with MAM, SAM, or any of the complicating conditions listed above will be referred to inpatient treatment at the Heckma Hospital SC.
 PLW with a MUAC score of <21cm will be admitted to the program as MAM.
 Patients who have previously defaulted in treatment will be eligible for readmission to the program after case review.

4 - Needs

Almost 90,000 children under the age of five years in Syria are acutely malnourished (HNO, 2021). These children face life-threatening risks and require immediate treatment (Ministry of Health, WHO, UNICEF: SMART Survey, 2019). Almost one in three pregnant women are anaemic, while maternal malnutrition remains a major public health and nutrition concern in Syria, leading to poor intrauterine growth, high risks during pregnancy, and childbirth complications (ibid). It is estimated that more than 0.2 million mothers are malnourished (2021, HNO). According to a recent Syria national SMART survey, one cause of malnutrition among children is the improper IYCF, which is endemic in the region (ibid). Of particular concern, astonishingly low exclusive breastfeeding rates in children 0-5 months indicated an urgent need to support new mothers with early and exclusive breastfeeding support. Data from the two surveys combined demonstrate a clear and urgent need for emergency supplementary feeding interventions combined with continued IYCF education for mothers and health care workers alike. Addressing critical needs, SP will provide nutrition services. With the project's comprehensive approach to nutrition, incorporating IYCF education, MtMSG, and mobile medical team model, CHW led nutrition education will encourage not only improved health outcomes for the community.

5 - Implementation of activities

Pervasive malnutrition rates and poor nutrition practices will be addressed through outpatient management of MAM and uncomplicated SAM from Tel Hajar clinic. SP will identify and treat by providing ready-to-use therapeutic food for PLWs and children aged 6-59 months. Patient education on nutrition practices will also be emphasized, with attention to infant and young child feeding education, community mobilization, and nutrition. Additionally, two mobile units will work in the rural communities of Hassakah, Serekaniye and Washokani camps. SP will distribute RUTF, conduct physical examinations. In addition, patient education on nutrition practices, with special attention to IYCF, CMAM, community mobilization, and nutrition education via CHW and nutrition specialist-led campaigns and support groups. Attention will also be given to promoting education centered on breastfeeding, health, hygiene, waste management, and diet education. Nutrition education will incorporate family life and mental health principles, such as supporting and promoting mother-infant interaction and prevention of violence and abuse. M&E: Patient data workbooks are updated and maintained daily, noting patient progress, interventions performed, prescription, and consultation schedule. The tracker contains details such as patient demographics, diagnosis, treatment, unique/returning visits. 4Ws will be reported to the respective WG/Cluster as requested.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

a)Staff or patients assaulted en route to/from the mobile unit locations;b)Risk to beneficiaries related to the receipt of ready-to-use therapeutic food (nutrition program) and/or assistive devices and/or medication, the perceived economic value of which may make the beneficiaries or their families targets within the community. c) Risk to beneficiaries around inequitable access to programming for females and children, due to cultural context which gives preference to adult males as recipients of programming and/or programming commodities. d) Risk to female beneficiaries of forced pregnancy in order to have them qualify for nutrition programming benefiting pregnant and lactating women. All staff involved in the project are trained in managing and/or referring specific gender and protection concerns and have an operational awareness of protection risks/issues. A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon. SP will assess the contextualized assistance needs of other vulnerable groups, including PWDs and ensure their equitable participation and inclusion in the project. Across NES, the numbers of disabled people are quite high – HNAP's 2021 report puts the number at 37% of the population.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks identified:• Contracted doctors violating PSEA policy;• Beneficiaries exploited by SP staff members, partners, and/or vendors. Samaritan's Purse mitigates PSEA risks with the following measures:• Background checks on all personnel, safeguarding training for all staff, protection messaging displayed in the clinic, clear reporting system for violations. There is an HR and complaints processing department to support this risk mitigation.• A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon.• Reporting mechanisms for each project.• Hotline.• Email hotline.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time. This is mitigated through the CoH staff and SP staff being able to continue activities in the interim period. • COVID-19 cases amongst health worker staff, SP or partner. Continued training and enforcement of infection prevention and control protocols. • Pockets of higher risk area due to tribal tension and ISIS sleeper cell activity. Mitigated through security assessment of the area to identify processes and protocols and coordination with local community actors and members. • Consistent provision of medication and consumables. The supply chain has remained relatively stable as SP procures all medications and consumables locally within NES. Samaritan's Purse has been able to successfully mitigate medication and supply shortages resulting from supply chain instability by setting up contracted agreements for the supply of 6-9 months' worth of medication from several local vendors. This medication supply is delivered to the clinic monthly or bi-monthly, which allows vendors time to plan well in advance, taking advantage of times the roads are open, to have adequate stock in their warehouse to continue to supply the clinic.
