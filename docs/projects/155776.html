Project Summary

Natural and anthropogenic disasters take a variety of forms, but all of them can severely affect people’s lives and livelihoods through loss of assets. The very diverse geopolitical, terrain and climatic conditions of Pakistan make it susceptible to various forms of disasters. North-western part of the county is facing humanitarian crisis since last few decades. More specifically, the Newly Merged District of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (formerly known FATA) remained the most impoverished and marginalized part of the country and have experienced their worst period of anthropogenic disasters and instability in the last two decades. Almost half of FATA’s population (approx. 550,000 families) was displaced to other part of the country due to insurgency and counter-insurgency. In response to FATA Sustainable Return and Rehabilitation Strategy (SRRS), The government, with the help of humanitarian partners, have facilitated the displaced families to return to their home. The economic background of the returned household are quite weak. Displacement coupled with fragile economy, pervasive poverty and unemployment has rendered many families’ food insecure and susceptible to future shocks. The families that have returned have extremely limited capital to repair or rebuild homes, to re-establish livelihoods, or to restore critical community infrastructure. More than 97% population of tribal districts reside in the rural area which relies agriculture as the mainstay of their subsistence and livelihood. Local food production in tribal districts is far from adequate to meet the needs of the population. The returned families have extremely limited capacity to re-establish their livelihoods. Their productive agriculture lands were left barren for many years. Large numbers of livestock were either lost or sold at lower prices, their shelters were destroyed and a substantial amount in the form of wheat straw, maize stock and fodder seeds has been damaged.
Support is agriculture sector is time critical to ensure foods security and livestock-based livelihood of the affected families. If these families are not supported timely, severe food insecurity and nutrition challenges will emerge, especially for the more vulnerable and impoverished communities. The failure to respond immediately can also serve as a trigger for returning families to adopt negative coping strategies.
The proposed activities will target approximately 458113 Individuals to resume their agriculture-based subsistence and to revitalize their agriculture-based livelihood in district newly merged districts

Needs

he Newly Merged District of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (formerly known FATA) remained the most impoverished and marginalized part of the country and have experienced their worst period of anthropogenic disasters and instability in the last two decades. Due to series of military operation and armed clashes almost half of FATA’s population (approx. 550,000 families) was displaced to seek shelter in other parts of the country. The government, with the help of humanitarian partners, have facilitated 442,067 (94 percent) of the displaced families to return to their home. Displacement coupled with fragile economy, pervasive poverty and unemployment has rendered many families’ food insecure. The families that have returned have extremely limited capital to repair or rebuild homes, to re-establish livelihoods, or to restore critical community infrastructure.More than 97% of tribal districts population residing in the rural area of which 71% of the population are dependent on agriculture. Several assessments showed that affected population has lost significant parts of their livelihood assets during the displacement. Agricultural lands have remained barren for several years. Animal of better genetic potential were lost. Forest and rangeland areas, were also affected due to uncontrolled cutting of trees on both public and private lands. Households earning from the agriculture sector were reduced from 39% to 25%. Furthermore, according to “In‑depth food security and livelihood survey of FATA returnees” overall 24 % of the returned households are food insecure. Among them only 1 % is severely and 23 % are moderately food insecure. Furthermore 69 % of households in tribal distrits, compared to the national average of 44 %, have an average food consumption lower than 2,350 kcals per adult equivalent per day. Women in the affected areas live in difficult conditions, with little or no education opportunities, poor access to healthcare less ownership on resources, and the additional burden of routine household activities and child rearing. Due to strict social condition is some area women have little role in livelihood activities which requires focused and careful attention. At present, the biggest challenge is restoration of live and livelihoods ideally to a level where the affected communities become more resilient to future disasters. Support for agriculture and livestock is very important to help the returned households resume their agriculture production and enhance their food production Prompt resumption and restoration of food security and agriculture-based livelihoods presents a precondition for the sustainable return of IDPs. The FAO response intervention will address acute food and livelihoods insecurity of recently returned families. The proposed action will have a direct and positive impact on the food security and nutritional status of the recently returnees to tribal districts. Improving the current nutritional status of agriculture and livestock, will help support productivity and health, increase resistance to disease, and reduce malnutrition and stress and mitigating the distress sales of productive livestock assets. Furthermore, it would build resilience of food and livelihoods insecure population and communities by reducing their vulnerability to crisis and disaster risks. The program will integrate women specific needs through provision of livestock feed, poultry packages and vegetables seeds packages for targeted households to enable vegetable backyard gardening to support the household’s economy and a with access to more balanced household and nutritional diet especially for women, adolescent girls and children. Furthermore, support to restore the health and productivity of livestock which are strongly controlled by women will also directly contribute in addressing gender needs. Livestock is often the only asset to which women have real access and over which women have some degree of control.

Activities

In line with the " In‑depth food security and livelihood survey of FATA returnees 2017", FATA Agriculture Action Plan 2016, and Multi‑CLUSTER Assessment of IDPs and Returnees KP/FATA, FAO will provide time critical agriculture and livestock support to most recently returnees to tribal districts through the following specific activities that will contribute to the households’ food consumption, preservation of livestock assets and income generation. A) Revitalization of Essential Food Crop Production to Ensure Household Food Security: ‑ Provision of 292 MT of high Yielding maize seed and vegetable kits to 11,700 most vulnerable families including 1950 women beneficiaries for Kharif 2019 cropping season;‑ Provision of 650 MT of improved variety wheat seeds and vegetable kits to approximately 13,000 vulnerable families for Rabi 2019 cropping seasons; (15% would be women beneficiaries)‑ Basic Agriculture tools and seed storage facilities to 5,500 resource poor families already supported through Kharif/Rabi Agriculture packages;‑ Provision of production packages for high value seasonal and off‑seasonal crops on 65 (Acre) to support 520 families (15% would be female headed HHs);‑ Establishment of tunnels farming for off‑season vegetable production on 175 Acre of land owned by 640 families (women be given priority) along with provision of micro irrigation schemes;‑ Re‑construction/ Rehabilitation of 08 damaged fruit/plant nurseries in the area of return;‑ Establishment of damaged orchards on 1000 acres of land owned by approximately 1000 families (including 150 women headed HHs) through provision of high value fruit saplings; B) Rehabilitation of Irrigation and Water Management Systems. ‑ Rehabilitation of 43 community managed water courses/ water control/harvesting structures to support 1075 Families (including 15% women Headed HHs). C) Revitalization of livestock and poultry sector through; ‑ Provision of poultry kits (comprising of 10 birds, feed for 1‑1.5 months and other necessary utensil) to 6,500 women beneficiaries who don’t have livestock;‑ Approximately 3300 fully mature female sheep/goats be provided to 1650 women beneficiaries to restock their lost livestock assets (50% beneficiaries would be women and female headed families).; ‑ Kharif fodder support to approximately 3,800 livestock owning families (including 15 % women Headed HHs) through provision 57 MT multi‑cut fodder seed along with feeding kits (comprising of water and feeding troughs) and milking kits for hygienic collection and storage of milk and milk products.‑ Rabbi fodder support to approximately 3,500 livestock owning families (including 15 % women Headed HHs) through provision 53 MT multi‑cut fodder seed along with feeding kits (comprising of water and feeding troughs) and milking kits for hygienic collection and storage of milk and milk products.‑ 456 Chopping Machines for fodder preservation and storage to support to 7,300 families who have already received fodder support;- Reconstruction of 35 damages poultry farms to rebuild the poultry-based livelihood of the affected families. D) Strengthening Livestock Support Services and Community Capacity Building; ‑ strengthening 25 veterinary Hospitals/Dispensaries provision of equipment and medicine;‑ Veterinary supplies (vaccinations and deworming interventions) to livestock owned by approx 25,000 families (Returnees and stayees families).‑ animal insemination tool and supplies packages to 20 veterinary centers selected veterinary;‑ Artificial insemination (sexed based) to 22,500 cows through selected veterinary institution to produce high yielding heifer offspring.
