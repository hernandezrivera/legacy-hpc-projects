Project Summary

Fayyaa Integrated Development Organization (FIDO) proposed to implement interventions to contribute to the response to the humanitarian crisis in the Oromia region due to drought resulting from the failure of both "hagaya" and "ganna" rains. According to the Oromiya Regional State Response Plan for Drought and Conflict Affected Report for February 2022, the situation in this zone is particularly concerning, where about 432,701 people need critical humanitarian support based on the health-related assessment that the health facilities were not ready to accommodate the affected population. There is a shortage of medications to treat the cases, and the burden extends to primary health care provisions on routine healthcare activities. In addition to drought, the current case loads of SAM were aggravated by COVID-19 and the previous impact of desert locust infestation. Furthermore, the proxy GAM for screening under-five children was 7.3% and 71%, indicating a dramatic increase in malnutrition cases.Regarding caseload burden, Moyale (265), Dillo (218), Guchi (205) and Dire (165) are among the top SAM burden contributors in the Borena Zone. There were several GBV and disability-related crimes identified during the previous period and of the current emergency in the zone, which exacerbated the problem. In addition, there has been high staff turnover due to the weather conditions, lack of transportation, and inaccessibility of some major kebeles. These may affect community-level disease prevention activities like immunization, which may create a risk of vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks (especially measles). It is difficult for the community to receive urgent and life-saving essential health care or mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Women and children are at heightened risk of abuse and exploitation.
The health and nutrition emergency situation through the establishment of mobile health and nutrition teams (MHNT). The aim is to enhance the coverage of health and nutrition essential services for the most vulnerable communities in the Oromia region; pastoralists, IDPs, and those hard to reach and not covered by any services.
Towards this end, FIDO will implement a comprehensive project that focuses on increasing access to basic health care services for hard-to-reach areas through MHNT and surges of prioritized health facilities through provisions of basic medication and medical items, immunization services, MCH services, SRH services, and clinical offloading, provision of psychosocial support services and GBV case management to prioritized health facilities through MHNT and trained health workers, strengthening of surveillance for epidemic-prone diseases, etc. Moreover, FIDO is planning to address cross-cutting issues such as PSEA, AAP, and gender to make protection the center of the proposed intervention. This project also integrates COVID_19 activities into the project implementation to prevent infection and curb the possible spread of the virus as well as support the children affected by the pandemic. Some of the planned COVID-related activities include: developing information education communication (IEC) materials and messaging relevant to COVID and conducting awareness-raising activities that address COVID-related issues in line with the national guidance on social distancing and avoiding large group activities, etc.
The proposed project will take place in 2 woredas of the Borena zone; Moyale and Guchi for 6 months with a total budget of $282,692.38. The proposed project will prioritize the most vulnerable sections of the population and will reach a total of 53,973 beneficiaries through these health interventions. The project will contribute to the improvement of the quality of life of the drought-affected population in the target woredas of the Oromia region. FIDO’s interventions are in line with the cluster’s suggestions in terms of prioritized location, intervention types, and budget allocation.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Initially, the project is designed based on the identified gaps, and like any other project, FIDO will create an effective communication channel between the government stakeholders and the affected population prior to the implementation of any of the project activity in accordance with the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) as well as sphere standards. FIDO ensures the participation of community members throughout the life cycle of the project. It will establish a clear mechanism that allows the community members to provide feedback on the project implementation. FIDO recognizes that the essence of accountability is to respect the needs, concerns, capacities, and dispositions of those with whom we work and to answer for our actions and decisions. Hence, the organization is keen and committed to mainstreaming this accountability frame throughout the project cycle management following the Humanitarian Accountability Partnership Standard. Accordingly, relevant program information, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiaries’ entitlement from the project will be made available to communities in a timely, accessible, and accurate manner, communities will be informed and understand and agree with key decisions relating to our intervention. FIDO will devote itself to purposely empowering communities and building their capacity to participate in all components of the program cycle. Communities will be sensitized about their right to provide feedback and complaints and community feedback and complaint procedures that are accessible, safe, and effective will be provided for beneficiaries during the startup of the program and will continue to be communicated. Information sharing and awareness creation about accountability to the affected population will be held during health education activities by using different posters, banners, and printed IEC/BCC materials. These procedures will alert communities on their rights. All staff must become aware of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies as part of the FIDO mandate since they are obligated to uphold and implement them. Staff will be provided with an orientation on the PSEA policy and the FIDO code of conduct after recruitment. Staff will be made aware of what sexual exploitation and abuse mean to FIDO and that FIDO has a zero-tolerance policy for it, given the urgency and frequently chaotic nature of emergency operations. A complaint handling committee will be established and along with responsible government stakeholders and FIDO, project staff will involve handling any concerns or complaints that arise from the beneficiary and acting accordingly. The committee will be comprised of women, people with disabilities, elderlies to ensure maximum community participation. Aside from the committee, FIDO will also set up alternate ways for feedback and complaint mechanism to promptly respond to concerns of beneficiaries and adjust implementation. Mechanisms to receive this complaint will consider the preferred mechanism of the community but will mainly use Help desk, community feedback suggestion boxes, provision of local stakeholders of contacts of FIDO staffs and selected focal representative of the community. FIDO project staff and community volunteers will be trained on humanitarian accountability and its importance to mainstream them on the ground at every level of the project. FIDO will also ensure the collection and utilization of sex and age disaggregated data that adheres to recommended age groupings.
