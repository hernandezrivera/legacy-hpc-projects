Project Summary

The action will provide safe, inclusive and equitable quality basic education to conflict affected and vulnerable boys and girls. It will reach beneficiaries comprising of girls, boys, teachers, and Parents-Teachers Association (PTA) members and community leaders. The project will strengthen community participation and ownership of children's education ensuring protection, resilience and improved learning outcomes. Save the Children (SC) will prioritize emotional and psycho-social protection, physical protection (Safe Schools), teaching and learning (Literacy Boost in Emergencies - LBiE) parents and community participation (Improving Learning Environments Together -ILET) and school leadership and management. To achieve this, SC will work with education community structures including PTAs, case workers, local leaders and relevant local authorities. Priority will be on bringing back to school out of school boys and girls who have not been attending school due to a variable amount of reasons. SC will work with relevant authorities and schools to raise awareness on the Safe Schools Declaration. The purpose of the program is to enhance the ability of the most deprived children of vulnerable groups to gain access to quality basic education to lead to socially and economically productive lives, and to strengthen their resilience and that of their communities. Subsequently, this, the action will contribute to creating better living conditions, enhancing social cohesion, and reducing the risk of these populations being pushed into negative coping mechanisms, which include forced displacement. The project is using the model Save the Children's integrated approach that links education to other sectors: WASH, Health Protection and Food Security &Livelihood.

Needs

The assessments reveal that in 30 IDPs camps, 56% of school-age children lack access to education. 64% of IDPs schools are not connected to water sources and 45% of the school latrines, when in place, are not functional. Poor water and sanitation facilities in schools contribute to the spread of diseases, including Acute Watery Diahorrea (AWD), and have a negative impact on education, contributing to higher drop-out and lower completion rates for girls. Support is also needed to ensure the provision of access to inclusive and protective life-saving education for children affected by the crisis (IDPs, refugees, returnees or host communities). This includes safe learning spaces, psychosocial interventions, life-skills, and health/hygiene protection. Support is needed to ensure the provision of access to inclusive and protective life-saving education for children affected by the crisis (IDPs, refugees, returnees or host communities). More classes are needed in schools to accommodate the IDPs. There is also high prevalence of child labour, potential risks of recruitment of boys into armed groups (child protection), SGBV and other reproductive health issues. In addiction, there is a lack of teaching and learning materials, desks, chairs and uniforms in all schools and particularly where IDPs are enrolled. In Central Darfur, SC provided education to 1835 Chadian refugees (935 boys and 900 girls). Moreover, SC rehabilitated 30 classrooms (including seating facilities), constructed 5 teachers' offices and supported 31,977 learners (17,075 boys/14,902 girls) with teaching and learning materials and teachers (50 women and 25 men) have been trained in EiE related trainings including Child Safeguarding.

Activities

Output 1) Children from newly displaced, protracted emergency and host communities (at least 50% girls) have improved access to safe learning spaces through establishment and furnish of temporary/semi-permanent classrooms and community awareness to include out of school children. 1.1.Conduct needs assessments to identify the education need. 1.2.Establishment of semi-permanent learning spaces (number to be identified based on needs).1.3. Construction of classrooms and teachers’ offices using community based approach and provide seating sets for the newly constructed classrooms.1.4 Conduct community awareness sessions and mobilization on issues of girls’ education, SGBV, child protection, children with disabilities and other issues hindering children' access. 1.4 Conduct Child Protection Rapid Assessments 1.5 Support out of school children/adolescent with Accelerated Learning Program (50% girls) Output 2) School children have access to adequate water and sanitation facilities and the hygiene situation is improved through provision of water tanks for drinking, construction of latrines with hand washing stations, establish and train children hygiene clubs and conduct hygiene campaigns. 2.1 Provision of gender sensitive WASH facilities for pre and basic schools and ALP centers 2.2 Establishment of Students Representative Councils/children clubs and train their members on life skills based hygiene/health promotion, child rights and protection to promote their participation and peaceful coexistence. 2.3 Conduct hygiene campaigns in schools and surrounding communities 2.4 Provide life skills, hygiene and health education for girls and boys. Output 3) Children (at least 50% girls) have access to improved quality basic education through training of teachers on psychoscocial support 3.1 Training of teachers on psychosocial support and life skills. 3.2. Train teachers on identification of child protection issues in children and referral mechanism
