Project Summary

Health service delivery in South Sudan is wanting due to years of conflict and destruction of health infrastructure. This are key pillars in health service delivery and are critical in quality and effective delivery of services.
Establishment of strong health systems in a country plays a major role in reduction of mortality and morbidity rates in any country. Robust health service delivery plays a critical role in reduction of maternal and child mortality rates. South Sudan is struggling with poor health indicators with the country having on of the worst health indicators in the country.
Climate change effects like flooding have also had their toll on the heath infrastructure, destroyed medical supplies and displaced populations that includes health workers. This has greatly affected health service delivery leaving the communities with great need of integrated humanitarian assistance that includes health, WASH, Food assistance and Nutrition services.
Over 60% of the total South Sudan population are in serious food in security with some of the counties even facing starvation (IPC analysis 2023). This is interlinked with lack of health services that can lead to mass casualties as vulnerable and food insecure commodities struggle to get access to food and health services.
Rumbek Center and Cuibet Counties have health challenges due to lack of health facilities and nonfunctional health facilities (Health Facility Functionality Survey 2022). This has affected health service delivery and people are forced to long distances to get health services exposing them to even greater protection risks especially to the girls and women. Over 44% of the total population in the country have limited access to health services
South Sudan has huge challenge of communicable diseases with malaria being a big challenge and contributing to almost 56% of the morbidities reported in the country and measles and other vaccine preventable diseases like Hepatitis B and E being reported in the country.
Conflict in Sudan has led to influx of returnees with the country having over 260000 people coming in the country fleeing from Sudan crisis, This has strained the already scarce available health services in the country.
Women Aid Vision therefore would like to bridge this gaps by providing mobile health services in Cueibet and Rumbek Center Counties. WAV will ensure that the people who are not able to reach the health facilities are reached with live saving health services.
This will include Provision of outpatient consultations to the vulnerable and affected populations, ANC and PNC services that will include folic acid distribution to pregnant mothers, Vaccination to the vulnerable and affected children. Vitamin A supplementation, Malaria treatment, COVID 19 awareness, capacity building of health staffs, Weekly EWARS and IDSR report submissions.
WAV will also support mental health services, integrate GBV, WASH and Nutrition services in her programming to ensure that children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers have been screened for malnutrition and referred to nutrition centers for management. WAV will carry out case management of rape and referral of GBV victims also offering Psycho social support to the vulnerable populations.
All the activities will be done in collaboration with CCUAM and the County Health Department to ensure that the most vulnerable in the community are reached with live saving health interventions avoiding duplication of services.
WAV intents to reach 8000 vulnerable and affected populations in the two counties with live saving mobile health services of which 3900 will be male and 4100 female. WAV will ensure disability inclusion with 731 people with disabilities supported with health services and assistive therapy where possible.
WAV will offer primary health care services through 2 Mobile clinics in each of the counties and 1 PHCU in each county to offer integrated primary health care services.

1. Needs

South Sudan has weak health systems that can be attributed to the political instability that has destroyed health infrastructure, displaced health workers thus affecting health service delivery in the country. The situation was made even worse by the flooding that has affected more than 1 million people (Relief web flood situation Nov 2023). The flooding has also affected over 36 counties in the country with lakes state among the states that have been affected by the flooding. The flooding situation has severely destroyed health facilities and medical supplies, displaces populations including health workers and other essential health service providers. They have also cut off populations from accessing health services thus posing a big challenge to the vulnerable and affected populations.Cattle raiding and intercommunal fighting have also affected health service delivery as the health workers are displaced during the conflict. This has also led to increased maternal mortality and morbidity rates. (CSRF report lakes state 2022) .The country is receiving a lot of returnees and refugees from Sudan due to the conflict in the that started in the Sudan in April 2023. This is straining the very limited health resources. The country is also struggling from shocks of prolonged measles outbreak that has affected over 50 counties. Rumbek Centre County has also reported some cases. Despite efforts to curb the spread, the outbreak is still spreading in the country.(IDSR report September 2023)Food insecurity and high GAM rates are also a big challenge and according to the IPC analysis, Cuiebet and Rumbek Center counties have been classified in Emergency IPC phase 4 which therefore calls for integrated health service delivery (IPC Report December 2022).The country faces huge gaps in the immunization services. According to National Immunization coverage Survey 2017, the penta 3 vaccination coverage is estimated at 49.3% a very low coverage compared to the immunization target of 80%. Rumbek East was which boarders Rumbek Centre and Cueibet experienced an active measles outbreak in January 2023 and this can be attributed to low immunization coverage and lack of cold chain facilities (IDSR week 2 2023).With poor Maternal indicators (MMR 786/1000 live Births UNICEF 2023), and very low number of midwifes, there is a huge need to support health facility deliveries, According to health facility functionality bulletin Rumbek Center and Rumbek Center are having 27% and 14% skilled birth deliveries respectively. This is a very worrying situation as it increases the chances of maternal mortality rates.There is therefore need to offer mobile health services to the vulnerable and affected populations in Cueibet and Rumbek Center to ensure that the vulnerable and affected populations who are food insecure and have been displaced by floods and conflict access health services thus reducing the morbidity and mortality rates in the two counties.

2. Project activities

• Health education sessions will be offered during the mobile health clinics to ensure that the communities get to know the importance of health seeking behaviors and COVID 19 Prevention messages.• Outpatient consultations in the mobile health clinics • Vaccination services will be offered to children to ensure that there is a control in the vaccine preventable infections.• ANC services will be given to the pregnant mothers• Vitamin A supplementation to the children under five years• Folic Acid supplementation for the pregnant mothers• Skilled Birth attendant Deliveries to be conducted in the mobile health units and in the community and referral of complicated cases to the health facilities.• Mental health services will be offered in the health facilities • The facilities will offer case management of rape and SGBV services to victims including referral and linkages to protection partners for support.• Screening for malnutrition and referral of the SAM cases with medical complications to OTP centers.• Disease surveillance through EWARS and IDSR reporting and 5W monthly reports• Training of health workers on IDSR, CMR, IPC and other capacity building sessions• Coordination with other partners on ground and the County Health Department as well as the health cluster.• COVID 19 awareness on prevention measures in the community and in the health facilities• EPI services in the mobile units and at the static facilities
