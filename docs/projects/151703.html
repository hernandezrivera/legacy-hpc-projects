Project Summary

In line with the Food Security Sector Objective 1, the intervention will aim to support and provide emergency food assistance including condiments, fuel and energy related support to meet the needs of the most vulnerable conflict affected families.
From the most vulnerable, 20.000 selected beneficiaries will be provided 9 cash transfers distributed through money agents. Selection of beneficiaries is based on their Food consumption Scores, Coping Strategy Index and a score based on their social economic situation. The selected beneficiaries will receive an unrestricted cash transfer amount based on the 70% MEB as set by the Borno Food security cluster. ZOA will promote diet diversification through awareness and training among project beneficiaries to increase average dietary diversity. ZOA will work with community animators to provide this training
In line with the Food Security Sector Objective 2, the intervention will foster resilience of the communities by an improved agricultural production, strengthening of productive assets and supporting income generating activities
ZOA plans to use the diet diversification and nutrition training to stimulate beneficiaries to consume healthy food and increase their income by growing rapidly maturing vegetable crops. ZOA will provide training in small scale cultivation. Along with the training, the 5000 targeted beneficiaries will be provided with the tools, seeds and materials needed to set up their micro garden. Community mobilizers guide the participants is setting up and maintaining their gardens. Furthermore, female headed households find it especially difficult to earn an income. ZOA wants to build initiatives by selecting women with a proven entrepreneurial mindset and to train them further in business skills and administration and to provide a small cash grant to expand their business. This should lead to an increased income for these women, making them more resilient and less dependent on aid.
Concerning PSEA, Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. ZOA has a focal person in place for its office in Maiduguri and for offices in the field. ZOA staff members have received training on how to prevent and respond to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. ZOA is part of the PSEA network in Maiduguri.
As a humanitarian emergency organisation, ZOA is commited to Accountabilty to Affected Populations (AAP) commitment in the way we are taking and giving account to the people in need we work for. ZOA is doing that by mainstreaming protection in community based interventions taking in account the Age , Gender, Diversity (AGD) inter-sectorial guidelines. In ZOA projects, beneficiary participation, feedback and complaint mechanisms are integrated in all aspects of the program starting from the inception phase. Beneficiaries are represented in Community committees trough the whole project cycle which provides channels of communication with the beneficiaries and use them to disseminate information about procedures and processes that affect them, so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices. Examples of mainstreaming protection in our projects are safe spaces to distribute cash using the RedRose platform , the use of local vendors to distribute the cash. having a majority of female cardholders to collect the cash, market assessments and post distribution monitoring processes , compliant and feedback mechanism,
All ZOA staff are involved in mainstreaming protection within projects of ZOA, however 0.6% of the budget will be reserved for a dedicated protection/GBV/PSEA officer combined with support visits from a external consultant for further training on GBV/PSEA/protection.

Needs

The ongoing conflict in the northeast of Nigeria has resulted in cutting of traditional trading routes, population displacements and restricted road access during the rainy season and also due to vehicular and human movement restrictions. Furthermore, movements of displaced populations due to attacks as well as feeling of insecurity in their hometowns have continued to drive severe acute food insecurity and limit the efforts of humanitarian partners in providing assistance to displaced persons in the region including those in hard-to-reach or inaccessible areas. ZOA is well familiar in the Jere and Maiduguri LGA,s and the proposed activities will be a follow up on former interventions . For Kukawa and Monguno LGA,s, assessments on food security and available markets have been undertaken and needs identified. According the HRP of 2018, 3.7 million people remain in need of lifesaving food support. A substantial proportion of the population in the northeast continues to face crisis or emergency acute food insecurity, with an increased risk of high levels of acute malnutrition and excess mortality especially in less accessible areas where the risk of famine is still a considerable threat. Food is still considered the main priority for conflict affected groups across all geographical areas of Borno. The latest results of a FEWSNET market survey showed that market functioning in conflict affected areas of Borno State are gradually improving. However, staple food prices remain higher relative to the same time last year and well above average price levels. Shortages at the household level are mostly due to accessibility issues, and rarely from unavailability of food on local markets. Some of the most common food insecurity underlying factors are the lack of purchasing power combined with food price increases and reduced access to cash sources. The lack of purchasing powers stems from the lack of income generating possibilities and the unavailability of land, tools and means to grow or purchase their food and to take care of other household priorities including health, shelter and education. Without meeting other unmet household needs, beneficiaries tend to sell food ration to meet to those needs, thereby undermining food security sector objectives of meeting the minimum food needs of households..

Activities

Based on the needs as been outlined above, ZOA proposes to improve access to emergency food assistance for beneficiaries through the provision of cash grants, supporting income generating activities and the restoration of productive assets. The beneficiaries are crisis affected families, consisting of IDPs and returnees living in camp and non-camp settings and vulnerable host community membersZOA will target food insecure HH regardless of their status, although the targeted areas will show that the majority of the selected beneficiaries are IDPs. Targeted communities will make an initial list of vulnerable households within the community based on their perception of vulnerable households. The households on this list will interviewed by ZOA staff who will verify family size, actual residence and state of vulnerability Based on the outcomes of the FCS, CSI and Socio economic indicators a selection will be made of beneficiaries entering the programs .Concerning the livelihood asset and support component. Beneficiaries will be selected on their vulnerability but also have to motivate their willingness and show certain capabilities or experience to be able to enter the program successfullyThe proposed activities will be implemented in Jere, Maiduguri, Monguno and Kukawa LGA,s. ZOA has a well established presence in Jere and Maiduguri concerning Wash and Food security interventions for donors such as ECHO, UN OCHA, DRA, CFGB and FFP. For Dutch Relief Alliance we work closely together in a consortium and ZOA has secured funds for the implementation of food security projects in Monguno and Kukawa LGA in cooperation with other DRA partners.
