Project Summary

This project aims at ensuring that the people of Leer, Mayendit and Pariang in Unity state achieve sustainable recovery and resilience in food security and livelihood. HAD will target 10000 HH equivalent to 60,000 individuals. The project is in line with SO3 and CLO2&CLO4.

Needs

According to IPC September 2018 findings, Unity State has 19000 people in need who fall under phase 5. This is so detrimental to the health and nutrion status of the community. Over 71% are classified under phase 3, 4 and 5: crisis, emergency and catastrophe! This is so worrying for food security. The situation has been compounded by the fact that the protrcated conflict and its effects are still rife in the community. The cost of living is too high thus the cost of food is almost unaffordable by most of the population. The consumption gap has been created and the food basket has reduced. People have resorted to coping strategies like one meal a day with children , the elderly, the sick and pregnant and lactating women being given priority.The frequent escalating conflict especially in our areas of southern and central Unity state has negatively impacted and therefore disrupted markets and supply chains, compounded by a seriously general economic crisis affecting the whole country. According to the March 2018 CFSAM special report, Food insecurity reached new highs during the 2017 harvest period with 70 % being food insecure and 14 %severely food insecure with a bleak projection for the 2018 lean season; 2019 will be a difficult year in terms of food security. Widespread events of cattle raiding as well as altered marketing/migration routes occurred in most areas of major conflict/insecurity. The livestock vaccination and treatment campaign is targeting approximately 40000 livestock and shoats.Due to inadequate veterinary services, livestock diseases have threatened to erode livestock contribution to food security and coping strategies among agro-pastoralists with resurgence of RFV outbreak among other endemic diseases reported across the country in 2018. This has been further exacerbated by conflict disrupted migratory pattern which end up creating new disease outbreaks, inadequate pasture and water, lootings among others (FAO CFSAM Reports, 2016). Despite the recent peace deal, armed conflict has persisted among various states in South Sudan subsequently leading to multiple displacements. So far about 2 million people have been internally displaced (50% estimated to be children) and more than 4 million have fled as refugees to neighbouring countries. This has been compounded by unprecedented food insecurity levels in the country in 2018, projected to deteriorate further into 2019(IPC September 2018). According to HNO 2019, an influx of returnees is project come 2019. These people will be returning to virtually nothing in terms of livelihood. Yet they also do not want to continue depending on food aid. They would like to go back to normal life- thus the need for support for own production and livestock health services.HAD is currently on ground in our field offices competent and experienced staff who can really deliver and implement this project.

Activities

 Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits; Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits/ rapid response kits (fishing kits/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF; Support value chain addition e.g. through milk & fish preservation & marketingTraining & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes. clear communication is the pillar for HAD. This is practiced throughout the project cycle. we use our local staff to explain the goal, objective and all that is involved with the project to the beneficiaries. The community representatives are also involved like women and elderly reps. They then explain to their colleagues group specific information.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. We have packaged our information in such a way that when we communicate with the different groups, we make sense and they understand. We actually even use illustrations and mother tongue to ensure the different categories of gender and age groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Protection from sexual exploitaition and abuse is one of the most important aspects of crooss cutting and protection issues that HAD takes very seriously in all our interventions. We are keen to listen and be sensitive if such happens. We have trained our staff on PSEA nad they know what do and how when such happens. We do our level best to maistream procteion in our work. This is by telling the nbeneficiaries that they do not have to be sexually used to get the services of the project. We also speak up in our meetings to create awareness and sensitize the community about this problem. The fact that we do our distribution and trainings in public places and in the time frames set by FAO is a plus towards the mitigation of sexual exploitation and abuse. We also allow for people to openly discuss any issue that they have . such avenues are key . we have PSEA officers in all our project sites.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes, we have this mechanism in place. We normally hold biweekly meetings with different beneficiaries as the project from the beginning or the project till the end to assess whether we are doing as we agreed in the beginning or not. We also get back to them when they have concerns.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Monitoring and evaluation is an important aspect of the project. Together with the stakeholders including women men boys and girls, we gauge our progress and then the impact of the project measured against the set targets and indicators. All of us get to see whether there is need for corrective measures or not. No one is left out. We also encourage our female staff to participate in the process to allow the women and girls to be comfortable and openly express themselves.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Protection risk analysis was done to arrive at issues surrounding the different needs of the different population groups. For example, bearing in mind that women and girls can be attacked and sexually abused on the way to and from the distribution sites as well as the training, we schedule for all these to happen following standardized FAO guidelines. We also are aware of GBV issues if women stay out of of homes for too long, that is why we bring the services as close to the people as possible to allow them run their other gendered roles without conflict.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. The main access barrier has been and would be conflict. When there is conflict people fear for their lives because the bullet does not know a civilian or a force man or woman. The other challenge would be tariffs and blockades that hinder humanitarians from coming to the people with the much needed assistance. The other challenge or barrier would be natural disasters like drought and flooding. This environmental barrier affects beneficiaries access to services of this project. for example if the farms do not do well because of long dry spells or the crop is swept away by floods.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Each group has been considered from project inception be it men, women, boys or girls. Their different needs are considered by for example following the FAO gender rule of having more female headed HH benefit in our interventions. This is because each gender and age has their different challenges. We sometimes have to allow some men to receive inputs to avoid the women being beaten up and the inputs taken away from them.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, our activities are gender sensitive. When we do project sensitization and awareness, we make it clear what we are going to do. This helps the beneficiaries clearly choose what is best suited for them.
