Project Summary

The proposed project will focus on Sector Objective_06: ‘Promote peace and security by reinforcing interventions that promote the humanitarian and civilian character of asylum and social cohesion’ by engaging the communities in and enhancing social cohesion by fostering gender-responsive and inclusive community-led initiatives that address the root causes of disputes within and between the host communities and FDMN communities in Cox’s Bazar. The communities will be engaged in peace promotion campaigns through sports, cultural activities, popular theatre and dialogues. Communities will be capacitated on Leadership, Communication Skills and Peace Practice. The project will basically focus on peaceful coexistence of the FDMNs with their hosts thorough dialogues on peace between the community members within the same communities and with other communities, if they like. Alongside the project will build the capacity of selected 8 Peace Promotion Groups (PPG) including 180 youth members (aged 18-24) and 8 Community Forums with 320 members (Age 24+) of the targeted communities. This platform intends to organize ordinary community members in place of the current leaders like Majhees and local government representatives to ensure extended participation in leadership. The PPG members will be capacitated to facilitate peace promotion activities including dialogues, courtyard sessions, sports, and popular theatre. 8 Community forums are supposed to arrange need based events including organizing dialogues including inter-generational dialogues to promote peace within the communities. Forums will be established and functional to strengthen existing conflict prevention mechanisms by ensuring commitments from the community members to stop harmful practices like domestic violence, child marriage, polygamy, and to promote good practices like undertaking environment friendly and peace friendly activities, and good neighboring behaviors. 60% of members of the PPG groups and forums will be female as the project wants to promote women's empowerment and leadership in the targeted communities addressing the gender inequalities in the context. The Community Forum and PPG members will select/elect the leaders themselves. We consider these community peace forums and peace promotion groups to be a means for local resource development for peace promotion in the long run. The Forum Leaders and Peace Promotion Groups shall jointly conduct at least 150 courtyard sessions on, gender equality, peace and security, harmful social norms, peaceful coexistence etc. Stakeholders Engagement workshop will be organized with all relevant stakeholders including local government, authorities, community elites, service providers, humanitarian actors, UP representatives etc. to explain the project’s activities and mandate. It will work in close coordination and collaboration with all the relevant protection sector actors in the respective camps to avoid duplication. The information of the trained forum members and PPG members will be maintained and shared with the sectors and other relevant actors in the camps through different coordination meetings. The project will ensure rigorous coordination with the protection sector to ensure effective implementation and learning sharing and will follow the do no harm principles and the sector protocol for confidentiality

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"The protection situation for Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar remains critically alarming, characterized by a significant increase in serious protection incidents including violence, abductions, extortion, and physical assaults, directly impacting the safety and security of the refugee population The incidents have increased tensions and anxiety as well as fear among both Rohingya and Host communities of Cox’s Bazar. The protection situation reports highlight significant challenges affecting social cohesion, security, and the well-being of vulnerable groups, particularly women and children. The joint protection monitoring report identified 69 incidents of social conflict in the Quarter 2 report in 2024 which is a 77% increase in the incidents compared to the first quarter. These incidents primarily involved disputes over financial matters and resource access, indicating strained relations between refugees and host communities. A total of 1,840 cases related to housing disputes were reported, affecting both refugees and host community members. Organized groups are actively recruiting young men, exacerbating tensions within the community. This recruitment often involves coercion and manipulation. While males are predominantly targeted, young females also face risks of exploitation and forced involvement in support roles. The involvement of adolescents in armed conflict raises concerns about community safety and cohesion. Approximately 64.6% of women and girls reported feeling unsafe in the camps, highlighting a critical need for interventions to enhance their safety and security (Joint Protection Monitoring Report, 2024). Disputes over shared resources such as water and latrines have created social tensions, particularly affecting female-headed households. The allocation of land for refugee settlements has led to overlaps with private and forest land, creating disputes and insecurity of tenure. Female-headed households, which represent 43% of eviction cases, are disproportionately affected by these disputes. (Overview: Rohingya Housing, Land, and Property Issues in 2023, Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh."").The influx of new arrivals has placed additional strain on the already limited resources available to both refugees and host communities, exacerbating competition for food and shelter. There is a lack of effective referral mechanisms for GBV survivors. While the majority of female respondents would refer GBV cases to local leaders (CiCs or Majhis), there is insufficient training for these mediators to handle such sensitive issues adequately. Obstacles such as age, mobility issues, and favoritism in aid distribution hinder equitable access to assistance. This disproportionately affects vulnerable populations, including women, persons with disabilities, marginalized groups and the elderly, indicating a need for more inclusive mediation strategies. Fear of retaliation and lengthy legal processes deter refugees from seeking formal justice mechanisms. Women may face additional barriers due to societal norms and potential stigma associated with reporting violence or seeking justice. Local mediators, including Majhi’s and community leaders, often lack training in gender-sensitive mediation practices. This gap can lead to biased outcomes that do not adequately consider the needs and rights of women and marginalized genders. The report highlights the underrepresentation of women in leadership and mediation roles. This lack of representation can hinder the effective addressing of issues specifically affecting women, such as gender-based violence (GBV) .Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), December 2023, Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh.ISNA Report 2024, Protection Sector.J-MSNA 2023 Factsheet – Refugees and Host Community.Joint Protection Monitoring Reports (Q1, Q2, Q3).HLP Factsheet 2023.GBVIMS Annual Report 2023

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"The project shall prioritize the safety and dignity of the affected population by conducting a thorough analysis of the protection context within the community. This includes identifying vulnerabilities and risks faced by different groups, particularly those most at risk, such as women, children, and individuals with disabilities. By utilizing participatory assessments, the project will gather input from affected populations to understand their specific needs and contexts. The analysis shall guide the project intervention strategies to minimize harm and enhance the well-being of the targeted participants. The project shall tailor every activity prioritizing do-no-harm principle and accountability to the affected population and following sector-specific guidelines.The project shall ensure that information about available services is widely disseminated and accessible in easier and more appropriate languages and mediums. Community engagement will be integral to the approach, as the project will involve local stakeholders in decision-making processes to foster trust and ensure that the interventions are relevant and effective. A safe, accessible, and functional channel to address and respond to project specific PSEA, safeguarding issues, and corruption issues where 10% of randomly selected beneficiaries who have given consent will be surveyed to identify their overall satisfaction with the service provided. The project shall regularly monitor and evaluate activities, incorporating feedback to adapt and improve the services. Training on protection principles adhering to humanitarian standards and existing services in project intervention areas will be ensured for all staff. The project shall rigorously collaborate with relevant actors to enhance protection, avoid duplication in response, and address and respond to context-specific risks.

2.3 - Age & Disability

"The project shall tailor strategies to enable an accessible and inclusive program by adhering to the Humanitarian Inclusion Standards for Older People and Persons with Disabilities, as well as the IASC Guidelines on Inclusion. The project shall use the Washington Group Questions (WGQ) enhanced set for effective identification of persons with disabilities (12% ) and the project shall strictly follow the do no harm principle to enable an inclusive program in close coordination with protection and relevant sectors to minimize harm to the targeted participants. Additionally, the project will provide training for staff and volunteers on inclusive programming, ensuring that all information regarding the project is accessible. The project shall inspire peace promotion groups (PPGs) to tailor peace-building programs, i.e., courtyard sessions, sports, dialogues, and games in a way that they are inclusive and accessible for everyone including older persons and persons with disabilities. The project shall ensure necessary adjustments in the intervention modalities based on the specific needs of persons with disabilities to ensure an inclusive program. All the PSEA and safeguarding channels in the facilities shall consider accessibility including accessible complaints and feedback boxes to address any PSEA or safeguarding issues caused by project staff.The project shall collect and provide gender, Sex, Age, and Disability segregated data of all project participants and extremely vulnerable individuals as identified by each sector. The project shall ensure accessible information during awareness-raising sessions."

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"The project is designed based on the findings from protection monitoring reports, JMSNA 2023, ISNA 2024 and other relevant assessments to accurately reflect the needs and priorities of the targeted communities. The project shall ensure regular consultations with affected populations to explain the beneficiary selection criteria, ensuring transparency and inclusivity. By involving community members in evaluations and sharing results in accessible formats, the project aims to foster a sense of ownership and trust between the project and the communities. All the assistance will be tailored based on the priorities and needs of the targeted participants followed by relevant assessments engaging affected communities. The project shall enable effective Feedback-receiving channels to gather feedback from beneficiaries regarding the adequacy and quality of the tailored interventions. Regular monitoring will be conducted to assess community perceptions regarding the program, utilizing existing mechanism and relevant assessments. These insights will guide project adjustments and improvements, ensuring that the voices of the targeted participants. This approach will not only enhance the project's responsiveness but also promote community empowerment and community-led initiatives. Furthermore, the project will ensure that all groups are included in AAP planning and implementation prioritizing the specific needs of the targeted participants to ensure their representation in decision-making processes. By providing information and updates about service changes or assistance in clear and understandable formats, the project will maintain transparency and encourage ongoing dialogue with the communities. All IEC will be available in English, Bangla, and Burmese transcription in accessible formats. "

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"FIVDB is an active member of the PSEA network and a designated PSEA focal shall maintain regular coordination and work in collaboration with the network. The organization has an active PSEA policy and all staff must sign the PSEA code of conduct of FIVDB which is aligned with IASC core principles. All staff, contractors, and vendors shall receive mandatory training on the PSEA, gender, Safeguarding, GBV, and all relevant policies of FIVDB to maintain a safe and inclusive environment. This includes mandatory reporting protocols, ensuring that every allegation, regardless of its origin, is communicated to the UN Resident Coordinator. Regular SEAH risk assessments will be conducted by the project through community engagement.All Identified risks shall be addressed with the technical support of the PSEA network. The project shall regularly raise awareness among all project participants regarding PSEA and reporting procedures through accessible and understandable culturally appropriate IEC materials, and awareness sessions, followed by community consultation. All the facilities will be installed with visible and accessible complaint and feedback boxes followed by community consultation. The complaints and feedback will be responded to and followed up regularly ensuring community engagement. The project will conduct periodic assessments of the functionality of the complaint and feedback mechanism system engaging beneficiaries and community people. FIVDB will take necessary actions regarding SEA complaints that are in line with the SOP for the SEA complaint referral. FIVDB has a dedicated gender cell to deal with all SEA complaints and take necessary actions. Regular monitoring of PSEA practices and functionality of CFRM will be ensured based on a developed checklist including PSEA indicators. The project focal shall work to create and maintain an environment that prevents SEA. PSEA will be a standing agenda at all project coordination meetings. "

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The program is designed to equip communities with knowledge and skills for natural disasters and monsoon seasons, available DRR services by disseminating messages, and involving community members in response efforts. It also facilitates staff and outreach workers' effective participation in disaster response by providing information on damage and needs assessments, as well as social mobilization interventions, disaster risk reduction (DRR), and emergency preparedness. Moreover, the project adopts an integrated, cross-sectoral approach that recognizes the links between WASH (water, sanitation, and hygiene), site management, and DRR committee in natural resource management, all of which contribute to overall community resilience. FIVDB will ensure effective coordination among relevant government entities (DRRO, RRRC, CPP), ISCG’s operational partners, and sectors, along with the Energy & Environment Network (EEN). Alongside this, the project will coordinate with other humanitarian and development actors including camp DRR committees to ensure that climate adaptation and mitigation strategies are effectively integrated into all interventions. The project will refrain from any construction that could harm the environment and will discourage the use of plastic. All project staff will receive orientation on proper waste disposal and waste management practices. The project will conduct a disaster vulnerability assessment during facility selection and shall equip facilities with safety materials including fire extinguishers. FIDVB’s own DRR contingency plan aligned with ISCG’s guidelines for DRR will be adopted in the project. The project training modules will include Early warning system , DRR and environmental issues for project participants to improve their knowledge regarding DRR and other environmental issues

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project incorporates Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures that is aligned with ISCG’s Multi-Hazard Response Plan 2022, Standard Operating Procedures (e.g Cyclone and Fire SOP), National Anticipatory Actions protocols, and the National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025). The project shall emphasize on integrating disaster risk reduction (DRR) strategies, ensuring that all proposed activities are in harmony with local risk analyses and existing protocols. Additionally, the project outlines a commitment to effective coordination among agencies, thereby minimizing duplication and enhancing overall response efficiency. During project implementation, FIVDB shall adhere to Sector-specific contingency and business continuity plans, EPR plans and the EPR WG workplan to ensure effective coordination with Sectors, EPR WG members and ISCG’s EPR focal point to address potential issues. Furthermore, the project emphasizes the need for gender-inclusive strategies within its EPR initiatives. This includes the active participation of vulnerable groups in disaster management planning and execution, ensuring that specific needs are addressed. Training and capacity-building efforts will be implemented for staff and volunteers to effectively engage with affected communities, fostering an environment that promotes safety and resilience. By embedding these EPR measures, the project not only aims to reduce potential losses during emergencies but also seeks to empower communities through participatory approaches, thereby enhancing their capacity to respond to future crises effectively.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

"Yes, the project actively engages youth aged 18-24 through various initiatives aimed at promoting peace and social cohesion in the Teknaf region. Specifically, at least 180 youth leaders will be trained in leadership and communication skills. These youth will keep playing crucial roles in undertaking peace-building initiatives in the targeted communities to enhance social cohesion.Additionally, youth facilitators will conduct courtyard sessions to address issues like harmful social norms, ensuring that at least 60% of participants in these sessions are women. By focusing on youth participation, the project aims to empower young people, enhance their leadership skills, and enable them to contribute positively to their communities.Through these efforts, the project not only addresses immediate resilience-building needs but also invests in the long-term development of youth as leaders and agents of change within their communities. Overall 40% youth participants will be included in the project interventions. The project shall apply IASC Youth Guidelines to ensure that the project is youth-responsive. Overall, the project shall create participatory approaches that actively engage youth in program design, implementation, and monitoring, ensuring their voices are heard. The project shall emphasize on age and culture-appropriate youth engagement strategies to ensure youth empowerment and effective response."

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

"Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by intensive capacity development approaches and linkage with relevant service providers that empower FDMN communities. Following the capacity-building training, the project will link the targeted participants with relevant stakeholders including service providers available in camps. Additionally, the project shall take the initiative to refer the targeted participants to site management, livelihood and resilience actors, DRR actors, energy and environment etc. based on their vulnerability and needs. The project shall establish a community protection mechanism that shall improve social behavior and change. By engaging youth in peace promotion activities, the project promotes youth leadership and empowerment. The targeted participants including peace promoters and youth groups will act as changed agents and continue undertaking awareness raising and peace promotion activities within their communities."

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project activities in the host community align with development initiatives and priorities in Cox’s Bazar district. The project emphasizes enhancing social cohesion, and improving quality. Additionally, the capacity-building efforts for local youth and adults to enhance local governance and peace promotion capacities, aligning with the district's goals of fostering community resilience. The project's initiatives, such as organizing inter-community dialogues and training sessions, support the broader development agenda by addressing root causes of conflict and empowering marginalized groups by cooperating with each other, particularly women, thereby contributing to sustainable development in the region. The project shall link the targeted beneficiaries with microfinance institutes, FIVDB’s livelihood projects including HICRA project, and other relevant actors based on their demands and necessities.
