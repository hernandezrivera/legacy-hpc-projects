Project Summary

Borno State, situated in Northeast Nigeria, continues to grapple with the devastating impacts of prolonged conflict, displacement, and poverty. Children, as the most vulnerable segment of the population, face multifaceted challenges including exposure to violence, family separation, exploitation, and limited access to essential services. These challenges undermine their development, well-being, and future prospects, leaving lasting scars on individuals and the broader community.
In alignment with the Borno State 25-Year Strategic Plan under the Enrich Initiative, this project seeks to strengthen resilience and enhance child protection systems to address immediate vulnerabilities while building a foundation for sustainable recovery and development. The initiative underscores the critical importance of fostering safe, inclusive, and nurturing environments that enable children and their families to rebuild their lives with dignity and hope.
Key Challenges
Weak Child Protection Systems: The capacity of community-based child protection structures and formal systems has been severely eroded, leaving gaps in service delivery and case management.
Psychosocial Distress and Trauma: Many children suffer from the psychological impact of conflict and displacement, with limited access to psychosocial support.
Family Separation and Unaccompanied Minors: Displacement has resulted in numerous cases of family separation, exposing children to heightened risks of exploitation and abuse.
Limited Access to Education and Life Skills: Education and life-skills training, critical components of resilience building, remain out of reach for many children in the region.

1 - Needs

The Strengthening Resilience and Child Protection Services project targets critical needs identified in Borno State, particularly among children and their families affected by conflict and displacement. These needs stem from gaps in protection systems, psychosocial support, family stability, and access to essential services, as outlined below: 1. Strengthening Child Protection SystemsCurrent Situation: Child protection structures, both formal and informal, have been weakened by years of conflict, resulting in poor coordination, lack of resources, and limited coverage. This leaves many children vulnerable to abuse, exploitation, and neglect.Assessed Need: Capacity building and resource allocation for child protection systems to ensure that at-risk children receive timely and effective support.2. Addressing Psychosocial Distress and TraumaCurrent Situation: Children exposed to violence, displacement, and loss suffer from significant psychological distress. Many caregivers also experience stress, which limits their ability to provide emotional and physical support to their children.Assessed Need: Establishment of safe spaces and delivery of psychosocial support services tailored to children and their families, to help them recover and build emotional resilience.3. Family Tracing and ReunificationCurrent Situation: Many children have been separated from their families during displacement or conflict, leaving them vulnerable to exploitation, abuse, and child labor.Assessed Need: Robust family tracing, reunification, and alternative care services to provide safe and stable environments for separated and unaccompanied children.4. Addressing Gender-Based Violence RisksCurrent Situation: Girls face heightened risks of child marriage, sexual exploitation, and abuse due to the lack of protective mechanisms in camps and host communities.Assessed Need: Gender-sensitive child protection programs to mitigate risks, raise awareness, and provide services for survivors.5. Access to Education and Life SkillsCurrent Situation: Education disruptions caused by conflict have left many children and adolescents without access to learning opportunities, further perpetuating vulnerability and dependence.Assessed Need: Provision of formal and non-formal education opportunities and life-skills training to empower children with the knowledge and tools they need for resilience and self-reliance.6. Community Awareness and EngagementCurrent Situation: Limited understanding of child protection principles and stigma around mental health issues hinder effective response and community support for vulnerable children.Assessed Need: Community sensitization programs to raise awareness of child protection issues and promote collective responsibility for safeguarding children.7. Building Resilience and Long-Term StabilityCurrent Situation: Dependency on external aid remains high, with minimal investment in building local capacities to sustain child protection services over the long term.Assessed Need: Strengthening community and institutional capacity to foster sustainable, locally led child protection initiatives.

2 - Activities

project will implement a range of activities tailored to meet the assessed needs of vulnerable children and families in Borno State. These activities are grounded in evidence-based practices, community engagement, and alignment with the Borno State 25-Year Strategic Plan under the Enrich Initiative. Below is an outline of the chosen activities, their rationale, and the modalities through which they will be delivered. 1. Strengthening Child Protection SystemsActivities:Train community-based child protection committees and government agencies on case management, reporting mechanisms, and referral pathways.Develop and disseminate standard operating procedures (SOPs) for child protection services to enhance coordination and quality assurance.Support the establishment and functionality of child protection desks in local government areas (LGAs).Rationale: Enhanced systems will ensure a coordinated, responsive, and sustainable approach to child protection.Modalities: Capacity-building workshops, mentoring sessions, and provision of technical resources to local stakeholders.2. Provision of Psychosocial Support ServicesActivities:Establish and operate child-friendly spaces (CFS) in camps and host communities to provide safe, structured environments for children.Deliver age-appropriate psychosocial support sessions, including play therapy and group counseling, for children and caregivers.Train community-based psychosocial facilitators to ensure continuity and sustainability.Rationale: Psychosocial support helps children and caregivers recover from trauma, rebuild emotional resilience, and improve overall well-being.Modalities: Integration of culturally sensitive approaches, leveraging local languages and facilitators to ensure accessibility and effectiveness.3. Family Tracing, Reunification, and Alternative CareActivities:Deploy trained caseworkers to identify and document separated and unaccompanied children.Collaborate with partners and local authorities to trace and reunify families using established databases and networks.Establish alternative care arrangements, such as foster care, for children who cannot be reunited with their families.Rationale: Family reunification ensures stability and long-term protection for children, reducing their exposure to risks.Modalities: Use of digital tools for case management and close collaboration with local and international child protection partners.4. Education and Life-Skills DevelopmentActivities:Support formal and non-formal education programs for out-of-school children, including catch-up classes and vocational training.Distribute school kits and materials to children to facilitate their reintegration into learning environments.Deliver life-skills training on topics such as conflict resolution, leadership, and health education.Rationale: Education and life-skills development provide children with tools for resilience and self-reliance, breaking cycles of poverty and dependence.Modalities: Partnerships with schools, NGOs, and local education authorities to expand access and quality of education services.5. Gender-Sensitive Child Protection InterventionsActivities:Conduct safety audits of child protection services to address risks faced by girls and young women.Deliver targeted programs on menstrual health management and prevention of gender-based violence (GBV).Establish women and girls’ support groups to foster peer support and awareness of rights.Rationale: Tailored interventions address the unique vulnerabilities of girls and young women, enhancing their safety and empowerment.Modalities: Engagement with women-led organizations and inclusion of female facilitators in all activities.6. Community Awareness and EngagementActivities:Conduct community sensitization sessions on child protection principles, psycho social well-being, and the importance of education.Develop and disseminate information, education, and communication (IEC) materials.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming across all project activities, the Strengthening Resilience and Child Protection Services project will implement several key modalities to safeguard the rights, dignity, and well-being of all beneficiaries, especially the most vulnerable. These modalities are designed to integrate protection principles into the project cycle and ensure that child protection concerns are prioritized and addressed in every aspect of the project. 1. Inclusive and Non-Discriminatory ApproachModalities:The project will ensure equal access to services for all children, regardless of gender, disability, ethnicity, or socio-economic status.Specific measures will be taken to reach marginalized groups, including children with disabilities, adolescent girls, and children from minority or displaced communities.Community participation mechanisms will be inclusive, with dedicated outreach to ensure that the voices of the most vulnerable, particularly women and children, are heard and acted upon.2. Gender-Sensitive Child Protection ServicesModalities:Gender-specific risks, such as early marriage and sexual violence, will be addressed through tailored programs for girls and young women.The project will design safe spaces for girls and women to receive psychosocial support, education, and protection services.All child protection training and awareness-raising will incorporate gender-sensitive approaches, ensuring that the specific needs of both girls and boys are addressed.3. Safeguarding and Prevention of Abuse and ExploitationModalities:A strict code of conduct will be enforced for all project staff and partners to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).PSEA training will be mandatory for all personnel involved in project implementation, ensuring staff are equipped to recognize, report, and respond to abuse or exploitation.Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be set up, allowing beneficiaries to report any concerns related to mistreatment or abuse in a confidential, safe, and accessible way.4. Protection of Child Rights in the Design and Implementation of ActivitiesModalities:All activities, including education, psychosocial support, and family reunification, will be designed with the protection of children’s rights at the core, ensuring that services are age-appropriate and respect children's dignity.Regular protection assessments and safety audits will be conducted to identify and mitigate any risks faced by children in the program.Case management protocols will ensure that children in need of special protection, such as those separated from families, receive timely and adequate care.5. Community-Based Child Protection SystemsModalities:The project will strengthen community-based child protection structures, including child protection committees, and empower local leaders to promote a protective environment for children.Training will be provided to community members, caregivers, and local authorities on child protection, positive discipline, and the identification of early signs of abuse or neglect.Community-based monitoring systems will be established to ensure that the protection needs of children are continually assessed and addressed.6. Accountability and Monitoring of Protection OutcomesModalities:A robust Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEL) system will track the protection outcomes of the project, ensuring that child protection principles are being upheld and that services are effectively meeting the needs of the most vulnerable.Regular feedback from beneficiaries and community members will be gathered to ensure that protection mechanisms are functioning properly and that children’s voices are central to the process.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure that feedback is effectively collected from the most vulnerable community members, the Strengthening Resilience and Child Protection Services project will implement inclusive, accessible, and confidential feedback mechanisms. These mechanisms will be designed to prioritize the participation of those who are often marginalized, including children, women, elderly individuals, persons with disabilities, and displaced persons. The following strategies will be used to ensure that the feedback process is inclusive and responsive: 1. Multiple and Accessible Feedback ChannelsModalities:Community Help Desks and Feedback Boxes: Placing feedback boxes in community centers, camps, and local offices ensures that individuals can leave anonymous comments or concerns.Toll-Free Hotline and SMS: A toll-free helpline and SMS system will be available for beneficiaries to submit feedback without any cost or barriers.Face-to-Face Meetings: Community meetings and focus group discussions (FGDs) will be organized to allow community members, especially the most vulnerable, to provide feedback directly. These will be conducted in safe and comfortable environments, ensuring that marginalized individuals are encouraged to speak up.Mobile Outreach Teams: Mobile teams will visit remote or hard-to-reach areas to collect feedback from individuals who may have difficulty accessing centralized feedback points.2. Gender- and Disability-Sensitive ApproachesModalities:Gender-Sensitive and Safe Spaces: Separate spaces for women and girls will be created where they can provide feedback on services related to child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) without fear of stigma or retaliation.Assistance for Persons with Disabilities: Feedback mechanisms will be tailored to be accessible for people with disabilities, including the provision of sign language interpreters, accessible feedback forms, or in-person support for those with mobility challenges.
