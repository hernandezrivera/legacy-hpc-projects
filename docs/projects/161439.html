Project Summary

Under DRC’s CCCM programme, the following major interventions will be implemented: - Camp management of the Malakal Protection of Civilian (PoC) site, including ongoing management, service coordination, developing and sharing information management products monitoring and community engagement. – Mobile site management and site coordination for camps, collective centres and camp like settings in Unity State as part of the ‘Beyond Bentiu Response’, including service mapping, rapid assessments and monitoring, community engagement and community governance support. - Mobile site management, site coordination, service mapping, rapid assessments and monitoring, community engagement and community governance support in the twenty counties prioritized by the CCCM cluster due to the large numbers of displaced persons. These include Rubkona, Awerial, Wau, Luakpiny/Nasir, Tonj North, Jur River, Juba, Yei, Magwi, Pariang, Malakal, Fangak, Yirol East, Bor South, Terekeka, Nyirol, Melut, Duk, Fashoda, Mundri East. including. - National mobile emergency site management support in camps and camp like settings through emergency deployable CCCM teams. The project will support approximately 185,000 individuals at unit cost per beneficiary of approximately 10 USD.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Response Plan analysis, there are currently an estimated 1.28 million IDPs living in camp- like settings and spontaneous settlements. Out of this, the CCCM cluster plans to target 800,000 Persons in Need in 2020. Populations have been displaced across South Sudan as a result of conflict, economic decline and food insecurity. This population has not been adequately supported by CCCM mobile services and other cluster interventions in 2019. Out of the people in need, an estimated20% are in very critical need of CCCM services while the rest 80% still have high and severe needs. Throughout 2019, approximately 229, 090 IDPs remained in PoC and collective sites, many of them expected to remain in displacement throughout 2020 as conditions for voluntary returns are still not feasible due to inter-communal conflict that have intensified in some areas. Frontline CCCM activities will therefore be required throughout 2020. These include the provision of basic services and assistance, ongoing coordination, site care and maintenance and service monitoring. In all these locations, coordination of humanitarian assistance is essential to ensure that provision of services is efficient, accountable and needs-based. This will also ensure that displaced population do not resort to negative coping mechanisms, which may exacerbate their vulnerability. Provide all activities Malakal PoC: Currently nearly 32,608 people live in Malakal PoC, many of them seeking safety from armed conflict with limited options for access services, accommodation or livelihoods opportunities. Many of the current population come from nearby towns including Malakal Town where their land has now been occupied by people from other ethnic groups creating further barriers for return. As a result, it is highly likely that a significant number of those living in the PoC site will continue to live there throughout 2020, requiring coordination of basic services and assistance across the camp. According to an intention survey contacted by IOM in May 2019, only 44% of the population intend to leave the PoC when conditions for return are feasible. Upper Nile & Unity States camp like settings: Besides Malakal PoC, over 8,359 IDPs are living in the Aburoc displacement site where they are reliant on humanitarian assistance for basic services. There are also a significant number of displaced people and returnees living in collective centres and spontaneous sites across the West Bank of the Nile with approximately over 50,000 identified in settlements in Baliet, Fashoda, Malakal, and Panyikang Counties. Many of these people have been disenfranchised from the traditional humanitarian response as they have not been living in recognized formal camp settings and face disproportionate difficulties accessing needs-based service provision. In addition, coordination of service provision, monitoring of populations movement flows and coordination community engagement is largely lacking in Upper Nile outside of the Malakal PoC and Aburoc IDP camp. Mobile response: DRC’s deployment to areas of new or existing displacement have often noted a lack of capacity for coordination of humanitarian assistance at site level in camp like settings, a lack of coherent communications with communities and a lack of access to robust needs assessment information on displaced populations living in camp like settings. For example, DRC’s rapid multi-sectoral assessment to Pibor, New Fangak, Renk, Longochok and Twic (May-August 2019) identified populations displaced due to recent conflict. However, despite the displacement, there was limited presence of partners to coordinate humanitarian assistance in these spontaneous and camp like settings. DRC will scale up its mobile activities to underserved and affected populations and where need be establishing semi-static presence for longer-term sustainable interventions. DRC will also strengthen its strategic partnership with NNGOs.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.1 DRC will carry out sensitisation among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments in the consultations/project implementation. DRC will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions. 8.6 One aspect of the project, is increased agricultural production through the distribution of agricultural inputs. The conflict has seen a rise in the incidents of housing, land, and property (HLP) disputes with the land of displaced households often resettled in their absence. The situation is particularly challenging for female headed households, who have land rights under the constitution and national framework, but often experience discrimination in fulfilling these rights at the local level due restrictive social and cultural norms at the household level. Considering these challenges, this project will be closely implemented with DRCs Protection team who are currently implementing HLP projects. In addition, DRC will carry out community sensitisation and ensure land verification (in so far as possible) to ensure that households have the right to carry out agricultural activities on that land. 8.10 As was observed in Bentiu, Unity State, late last year, women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the locations for latrine construction and screening of locations with a GBV risk assessment.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC is a certified member of the Core humanitarian Standards (CHS) and as such fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. DRC has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanismand operates complaints desks to enable community members to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline Number (9686) that beneficiaries can call to register complaints, feedback or requests. Through its activities, DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the F-CRM will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project.
