Project Summary

South Sudan has remained in protection crisis in 2021 due to instability and lack of sufficient investment in essential service provision. Over 6,000 Gender Based Violence cases were recorded between January and September 2020. Majority 97% of the GBV cases reported are female, a fifth involved girls below the age of 18 years, and more than 50% of the cases reported intimate partners as perpetuators. Most of the time, the women lack access to essential services in GBV case management. Women and girls are living in fear. The HNO,IPC reports and protection cluster has identified Panyijiar, Mayendit, Leer,Longechuk and Maiwut among locations with prevalence of GBV and other protection concerns. Additionally these locations are all in IPC 3 and 4 and require urgent humanitarian intervention. Integrated with different protection packages, other clusters, CH will prioritise these location with GBV response. Coalition for Humanity would like to address the above gaps through provision of emergency GBV services, case management, PSS, community based referral mechanisms, establishment of women and girls friendly spaces, and awareness raising.
This project will target 20,000 Individuals for GBV across 5 counties. Segregated by gender
Men = 2,000
Women = 7500
Gils = 7500
Boys = 3,000
The key activities for the project will include prevention and response to GBV.
1. Awareness raising in GBV risks, mitigation including Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).
2. Training and Capacity building of frontline workers in GBV case management, PSEA, PFA,
3. Establishment of community-based referral path ways and feedback mechanisms on GBV cases
4. Establishment of Women and Girls Friendly spaces (WGFs)
5. Establishment of GBV service centers and PSS
6. Distribution of Dignity kits to vulnerable girls and women
The project will make considerations to involve community, people with disability, and people with special needs, the vulnerable minority and all gender. The project will provide referral systems and community-based feedback mechanisms, through door-to-door surveys, PSS groups, suggestion box, and telephone contacts.
As part of the exit strategy, the project will train community volunteers, case workers in PSS and PFA. The established women and girls friendly spaces will be handed over to the community for management. Community leaders and local authority will be trained in PSEA, GBV risk analysis and mitigation and linked to community based referral pathways in connection to other partners in the project area.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has remained in protection crisis in 2021 due to instability and lack of sufficient investment in essential service provision. Covid-19 pandemic, floods, and intercommunal conflicts arising from cattle raiding, housing land and property have worsened the humanitarian situation. According to HNO and IPC 2021, More than half of South Sudan population, 7.24 M people are in need of humanitarian assistance. At least 20% are in IPC4 and 1% in 1PC 5 bordering catastrophic, 1.6m people remained internally displaced. The IDPs, and returnees are putting pressure on the limited resources by host communities. Due to Covid-19 that hindered service provision across the country.Over 6,000 Gender Based Violence cases were recorded between January and September 2020. Majority 97% of the GBV cases reported are females, a fifth involved girls below the age of 18 years, and more than 50% of the cases reported intimate partners as perpetuators. South Sudan women have for a long time been victims of gender-based violence. Most women are sexually abused by family enemies, during cattle raising and during cases or revenge killings. Such cases are normally not reported due to the shame and embarrassment that comes with rape. Most of the time, the women lack access to essential services in GBV case management. Women and girls are living in fear, most WASH facilities are not segregated by gender, and do not provide safety and privacy for women. Unity state, central Equatoria, Warrap,Jonglei and Lakes remain hot spots for rape cases, gang rape during cattle raiding and other communal violence. The HNO,IPC reports and protection cluster has identified Panyijiar, Mayendit, Leer,Longechuk and Maiwut among locations with prevalence of GBV and other protection concerns. Additionally these locations are all in IPC 3 and 4 and require urgent humanitarian intervention. Integrated with different protection packages, other clusters, CH will prioritise these location with GBV response. Coalition for Humanity would like to address the above gaps through provision of Emergency GBV services, Case management, PSS, referral and awareness raising. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for humanity has a PSEA code of conduct signed by all project staff and enumerators, all humanitarian staff, community leaders are taken through 1 day training on PSEA and sensitize the community members are also sensitized on their right to access the humanitarian assistance without being exploited.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project has designed a house-to-house monitoring form to be used by community leaders and hygiene promoters, the form will be used to follow up on households that are not able to use suggestion box, they will provide means in which they can be contacted by phone, or will give verbal feedback to leaders and hygiene promoters to document. The Post distribution survey will be administered verbally and entries made by literate enumerators.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will hold inception meetings with community members and brief community about project objectives. The project will also hold community dialogue days and forums to give feedback to the community members. The project will establish a suggestion box and telephone contact for communication. The project will conduct end of project survey door to door to get feedback in beneficiary satisfaction

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from community will inform the GBV risks, most needy Payams, Bomas, most affected population, and this will inform prioritization at community level. Feedback on needs beyond this project will be referred to other partners in the county who have capacity to handle cases beyond this project. Further feedback will inform change in strategy, and future proposals

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Violence and GBV issues meted on women and girls have direct effects on overall IPC levels in different locations. The communal violence, lack of access of key services mostly by women have resulted in vulnerable member of households (women, children, elderly people and sick) suffer more and hence go to IPC levels. By mitigating those occurrences through concerted efforts of different partners(referral mechanisms) the project will help minimize or reduce population facing IPC 3,4 and 5.
