Project Summary

Food for the Hungry, in partnership with Medical Teams International, formed the Joint Rohingya Response Program (JRRP). In 2020, the JRRP will continue to carry out health services at two Primary Health Centers (PHCs): Shondar Palace (SP) PHC in Camp 12 and Football Field (FF) PHC in Camp 5 and three Health Posts (HP), Main Road (MR) HP (and Water Tower (WT) HP in Camp 5 and Power Pole (PP) HP (in Camp 6. JRRP will continue to engage with the health sector and with the other actors operating in these camps.
In addition to the health services provided at the PHCs and HPs, JRRP will continue to engage a network of 130 male and female Community Health Workers (CHWs) and Community Psychosocial Workers (CPWs) from the community. CHWs will be equipped and trained to cover 12,328 households in Camps 5, 6, 7 and 12 in Kutupalong Camp. CHWs will act as a link between the community and health facilities by providing preventative and health promotion services to men, women, boys and girls through social and behavior change communication.

Needs

Needs: Decades of human rights abuses against the Rohingya in Rakhine State has resulted in a massive influx of this population into Cox’s Bazaar, Bangladesh. As of June 2019, the ISCG’s JRP, inclusive of the host population, identified over 1.24 million people in need of integrated health services. Of JRRP’s targeted population, 52% are female, 55% are below 18 years (19.5% are children under 5 years of age (CU5)), and 3.5% are above 60 years (UNHCR Camp Population Data, Sept 2019). Of the 74,263 outpatient consultations conducted in 5 of the JRRP facilities from January - October, 2019, 60% were female and 28% were CU5, a clinic utilization rate of 2 consultations/person/year. Although access to health services has improved, in some camps, including 7 and 12, the distance to reach a facility is over one hour of walking for 8% of the population. Throughout the camps, medication and human resource capacity is often insufficient to meet the needs and the refugees are unaware of available services (NPM, 06/2019, Camp Profile). Sustainable quality health services remains a challenge partly due to high patient loads and staff turnover. Improvement in the supply chain systems for essential medicines and medical commodities is ongoing and requires additional attention to prevent stock outs, minimize irrational drug use, streamline expired medicine disposal and stop unregulated private prescriptions in camps (MSNA, 2019). The top morbidities in JRRP facilities were Acute Respiratory Infections (ARIs) 18%, Skin Diseases 14%, and Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) 8%. In 2019, the refugees experienced outbreaks of Measles, Dengue Fever, AWD, Chicken Pox and Diphtheria. Although Expanded Program on Immunizations (EPI) activities are in place, complete coverage was low in 2019 and there was a high dropout rate. Improved coordination is needed to ensure proper mapping of EPI activities to avoid missed opportunities. The risk of communicable diseases is exacerbated by poor housing structures, insufficient water points with long waits, congested shelters and extreme weather (NPM, 06/2019, Camp Profile). Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are prevalent and increasing amongst refugees. This trend is consistent with global trends, although the increase within the refugee community may reflect increased surveillance and reporting. There is a continued need for response efforts to focus on management of NCDs, MPHSS interventions, and nutrition screening combined with appropriate treatment, referral and follow-up. Critical gaps remain in the provision and referral of comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care, Antenatal Care (ANC), Postnatal Care (PNC), family planning provision, availability and timely access to 24/7 clinical management of rape/intimate partner violence services, and support for gender-based violence survivors. There are also unique challenges in reaching women as 20% of married women and 28% of unmarried women are unable to move in the camps unaccompanied (MSNA 2019). Although BEMONC services are available in most camps, the proportion of JRRP facility-based deliveries from July to September 2019 was 16.5% which is significantly below the target set by the health sector of >55% (Health Sector 2019 Strategic Plan). To increase facility-based deliveries, increased engagement of key decision makers including men, community leaders and traditional birth attendants is needed. Kutupalong is highly susceptible to natural hazards which is exacerbated by the temporary nature of construction and overcrowding. Despite efforts to reduce risk of natural hazards over 18,378 households were affected in 2019 (ISCG JRP 2020 Reference Note: Extreme Weather, Seasonal Variety and Disaster Risk in Cox’s Bazar). It is essential for training on emergency preparedness, mass casualty response, Psychological First Aid, preparing mobile medical teams and prepositioning stocks to minimize impact.

Activities or outputs

The JRRP will contribute through; A comprehensive primary healthcare package will provide services including outpatient consultations, child health services including Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) and Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI), essential medicine supply and management, NCDs, MHPSS, In Patient Services, and essential laboratory/diagnostic services. A clinic utilization rate of more than two will be targeted, with at least 60,000 women. Preventative nutrition practices such as Infant and Young Child Feeding and nutrition screening using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) and Weight for Height will target 22,000 CU5 and 90% (6,000) of PLW to prevent and detect malnutrition. Access to comprehensive sexual and reproductive health and HIV services will be ensured through targeting 28,000 women of reproductive age (WRA), and adolescents. Sexual health education, ANC, PNC, and BEMONC functions will be provided. Family planning will be provided and uptake promoted to reach at least 15% of WRA. All health staff will receive gender sensitive training to provide MHPSS to beneficiaries, including Sexual Gender Based Violence survivors. Waiting areas are separated for men and women, so that women feel secure. Indicators will be disaggregated by gender and monitored to ensure positive impact. Adolescent Friendly and Responsive services will be implemented to address their unique challenges. Disabled and elderly will be prioritized in the waiting area to shorten their wait and enhance their experience. CHWs will enhance community-based health access through education at the household and community levels. Topics will cover hygiene, disease prevention, and emergency preparedness and response. CHWs also help with referrals for those in need of EPI, ANC, PNC, deliveries and MHPSS. MHPSS includes recreational or creative activities, supporting community level meetings to discuss solutions, and emergency response. To increase facility-based deliveries, CHWs will focus on maternal and neonatal care follow up and mapping households with PLW and TBAs, community leaders and male family members will be mobilized and trained. By reducing home and community-based deliveries, maternal and neonatal mortality will reduce. CHWs will continue demographic data surveys so that JRRP can deliver applicable services. For CU5, CHWs will conduct MUAC to screen and detect malnutrition. Additionally, CHWs will capture immunization status to help prevent disease and help JRRP respond in case of an outbreak. PHC and HP services and staffing will aim to achieve the standards in the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services for primary healthcare facilities, and referrals will follow the referral pathway. Infection Prevention and Control will be prioritized for safe service delivery including proper waste management. The Waste Management Complexes will serve as a medical waste disposal and treatment unit for JRRP and other health actors. Additionally, JRRP will focus on emergency preparedness and response through supplementary mobile medical teams, communication planning, emergency evacuation drills and training for mass casualty events. The supplementary teams will operate at in Camp 5, HP 052, and PHC 110 while CHWs will serve as first responders in community-based response. Senior staff and international medical volunteers will support staff to ensure quality and compliance to standards. Accountability to beneficiaries through structured and semi-structured modalities will be collected to inform planning and improvements. Feedback will be collected through exit interviews, health committee meetings, community surveys, complaint boxes and focus group meetings. The JRRP will implement these activities in coordination and collaboration with all relevant stakeholders. Health Information Management will continue to align with the MoH/DHIS, Health Sector, and donors.
