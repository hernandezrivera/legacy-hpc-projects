Project Summary

Purpose and overall goal:
In line with health sector objectives, the project will seek to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality healthcare services in 6 counties of Upper Nile state (Longechuk, Maiwut, Renk, Maban, Ulang, and Nasir). This will be achieved through implementing the basic health package that includes maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to girls, boys, men, and women in emergency situations, with a specific lens on accessibility for persons living with disability (PWD) and the elderly. In partnership with the Ministry of Health and the County Health Departments, the project will support the delivery of the Basic health care package, and support in facilities in refugee camps, transit centers, and host communities. RI will directly support 14 health facilities in Renk, Maban, and Melut providing primary health care services. To reach the population in hard-to-reach locations in Nasser, Ulang, Maiwut, and Longechuk counties, RI will continue supporting seven integrated Mobile Medical Units (MMUs). RI will also support the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) in the 6 target counties.
Methodology and target:
RI’s project focuses on the provision of primary health care in areas of high need and where existing services are inadequate. Activities include the provision of curative consultations in primary health facilities; community-based treatment of common illnesses in children under five; routine vaccination; reproductive and sexual health including the provision of ANC, skilled delivery, PNC, and clinical management of SGBV; and mental health treatment. Medair conducts ongoing training and supportive supervision for capacity-building staff of RI, CHD, and other partners in order to improve the quality of service provision. RI primarily targets children under five, women, and people with disabilities and mental health conditions, due to their unique vulnerability, though not exclusively.
RI aims to increase the capacity of CHD, SMOH, and the local community to be prepared for future shocks.
All of RI's health activities are integrated with WASH, Nutrition, and Protection activities to enhance the effectiveness and address underlying causes of morbidity and mortality. The project will target 203,757 (107,991 Female, 95766 Male) among which, 69,277, 79465and 10,187 are Hosts, IDPs, and people with disabilities respectively.

1. Needs

People in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints, operational interference, public health challenges such as measles and cholera outbreaks, and climatic shocks resulting in extraordinary flooding and localized drought. These, in turn, have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods and hamper access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene, and health services. Protection concerns remain high, especially for women and girls. People affected by violence have limited access to basic needs such as food.The health situation in South Sudan is worsening, with the HNO 2024 reporting that 6.3 million South Sudanese urgently need health services, an increase from 6.1 million in 2023. Upper Nile is one of the states classified with severity level 4 of health needs. In 2024, the health cluster plans to reach 3.2 million people with essential lifesaving health servicesSouth Sudan health cluster has estimated that 456,260 people will be in need of lifesaving primary health care in the targeted Nasser, Ulang, Maban, Renk, Melut, Maiwut and LongchukLongechuk Counties; of which 59,660 are children under five years and 236,634 are returnees. The already critical humanitarian situation has been exacerbated compounded by the third consecutive year of atypically severe flooding. Floodwaters have destroyed critical infrastructure &and have limited humanitarian access to heavily affected populations, while simultaneously limiting the ability of people to migrate to access services. Barriers to movement, imposed by flooding and insecurity, have also limited the ability of vulnerable groups in affected areas, particularly women, &and children. All Counties targeted in this plan are among the worst affected population group Recurrent outbreaks of diseases including measles, cholera, and malaria further weaken and overwhelm the health system and cause additional morbidity and mortality. Nutritional emergencies exacerbate the vulnerability of the community to disease, particularly for children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Children under five are particularly vulnerable to morbidity and mortality from communicable diseases such as malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia, as well as outbreak diseases such as measles. Following the influx of returnees from the Sudan crisis, there were measles outbreaks in many Counties of Upper Nile (Renk, Melut, Maiwut, Longchuk, Ulang). In July 2023, there were malaria and measles outbreaks in Maiwut and Longechuck that seriously affected inaccessible people in areas like Dajo and Pashimbe in LongechukL Counties. Ongoing underinvestment in healthcare by the government combined with reducing funding from donors for health system strengthening has led to year-on-year reductions in both coverage and quality of healthcare services. This leaves facilities with insufficiently trained personnel and insufficient essential supplies. Insecurity and natural disasters such as flooding, as well as seasonal inaccessibility due to lack of roads, transport, and infrastructure further limit access to life-saving health services, especially in RI-targeted locations in Upper Nile (Renk, Meban, Melut, Maiwut, Longchuk, Nasser and Ulang). Inadequate access to basic primary health care leaves South Sudan’s population vulnerable to experiencing unnecessary morbidity and mortality. Pregnant women continue to experience excess mortality due to inadequate reproductive health services during pregnancy, delivery &and postnatal. Only 39% of women are accessing the recommended four Antenatal Care (ANC) visits during pregnancy, and skilled birth attendance is low at 14.7%, leading to poor outcomes: maternal mortality ratio (MMR) of 789 per 100,000 live births, &and new-born mortality rate (NMR) of 39 per 1,000 live births In Upper Nile state only 19% of pregnant women provided with antenatal care at 4th visit.

2. Project activities

The primary focus of RI’s health programming is supporting the provision of essential primary health care through support and direct implementation of health facilities; mobile clinics, and community-based interventions. All activities are integrated with nutrition and WASH to ensure optimal benefit to the beneficiaries, and in order to address the underlying causes and drivers of poor health. Within the primary health care services provided in the target locations, RI will prioritize the most vulnerable populations, particularly PLW, infants, and children under 5 years, in order to reduce morbidity and mortality in these populations which bear the greatest burden of disease and poor health. RI works within the existing structure of the MoH and according to the South Sudan BPHNS, supplementing with additional emergency activities as needed to prevent excess morbidity and mortality, and in order to meet Sphere standards of both geographical coverage and essential health service coverage. The focus on basic primary health care encompasses curative consultation of common illnesses, including IMCI/IECHC for children under 5 years; EPI; nutrition screening and referral; and reproductive health, including ANC, PNC, SBA, and family planning and CMR services, and MHPSS. RI will also provide additional essential primary health care services including BEmONC services; emergency care for severe illness including inpatient care, stabilization, and referrals; and mental health services. RI will support 39 health facilities in Renk, Meban, Melut, Maiwut, and Longchuk to provide the BPHNS through the HSS project to reach 143,565 (70,347M, 73,218F) beneficiaries. This will be done through training and supportive supervision of staff, provision of essential supplies and medicines, and operational support to CHD-led health facilities. Contingent on getting additional funding, RI will recruit and assign critical qualified staff missing to improve the quality of care in reproductive health and outpatient care.A significant number of people in Maiwut, Longchuk, Nasser, and Ulang had barriers to accessing health services from existing health facilities in the Counties due to distance and inaccessibility during rainy seasons. In order to improve Sphere standards of access to health care within 1 hour, RI will continue providing primary health care services through the current seven Medical Mobile Units (MMUs). This intervention aims at improving equitable access to and utilization of health services by 60,192 people (29,494M, 30,698 F). The package of primary health care through the MMU will include outpatient consultations including OPD for all ages, ANC/PNC, EPI services, nutrition screening, CMR services, and MHPSS as well as referrals to health facilities as required for additional care and Supporting BHI. Boma health workers (BHWs) selected from the community level will support the delivery of assistance under the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) and ICCM activities.RI will continue its current emergency responses to the Sudan crisis-affected people and will try to respond to emergencies that will occur in RI’s targeted locations throughout the course of the project period. RI will prepare separate emergency preparedness plans and always try to incorporate them into its existing and future projects.RI supports disease surveillance through all health activities, including health facilities, community-based health care, and MMU. RI will train all facility and community health staff on the recognition of outbreaks. sample collection and case management. All health facilities supported by RI share weekly reports using EWARS, WHO’s Early Warning, Alert and ResponseAll of RI’s health activities are implemented as multi-sectoral responses & WASH activities and infrastructure are embedded into all health activities. water for handwashing, cleaning & drinking; handwashing infrastructure; access to latrines including for people with restricted mobility
