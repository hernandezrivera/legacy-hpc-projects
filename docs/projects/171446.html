Project Summary

The numbers of people reached with health services dropped significantly from over 300,000 people in February, 2020 to less than 150,000 in August. Whilst the risk of poor health outcomes due to a wide range of hazards including malnutrition, water-borne diseases and vaccine-preventable illnesses could dramatically increase due to flooding and reduced hospital visits due to fear of contracting COVID. Humanitarian access remains a challenge across southern Somalia which impedes vulnerable populations’ access to lifesaving health and other humanitarian assistance.
3.2 million Somalis are in need of health services, with 65% of these in severe need and a further 10% in extreme need. This is in line with findings from RI’s needs assessment in which 75% of respondents in remote, hard to reach areas of Belet Hawa and 56% of respondents in Bule Burte reported that health facilities were too far away. According to Somalia demogratic health survey 2020, Under-five mortality of 117 deaths per 1,000 live births. Children under five as well as pregnant and lactating mothers are particularly vulnerable to diseases that could be prevented by immunization, yet immunisation rates remain low with only 11% of children aged 12-23 months having received all basic vaccinations. Sexual and reproductive health services are particularly limited. One in twenty women would be expected to die from pregnancy-related causes during their reproductive lifetime and only 31% of women aged 15-49 who had a live birth in the five years prior to the survey, received antenatal care from skilled health personnel. Further, only 32% of births were delivered with the assistance of a skilled health care provider. Women and children continue to be disproportionately affected with those from marginalized groups, such as IDPs even more at risk of GBV. A recent study conducted in Hirshabelle shows 45% of female respondents reported experiencing physical violence with 28% reporting domestic violence and 21% reporting incidence of rape. Data on mental health status across Somalia is scant, access to specialized service providers for MHPSS are limited and there is a lot of stigma attached to mental health issues. Yet based on the multiple crisis that people living in Somalia have had to grapple with including protracted conflicts, recurrent floods and drought coupled with COVID’s direct and indirect impacts, the mental and psychosocial wellbeing of Somali is affected. Particularly people living with disabilities and those from minority clans face social stigma, exclusion and rejection which negatively affects their mental wellbeing.
In line with health cluster strategic objectives, RI will address the health needs of displaced, people living with disability, elderly and rural PLWG and children under the age of 5 in need of immediate and sustained integrated health interventions (Primary health, Mental health and Psychosocial support, GBV response and Nutrition). RI currently supports 12 static health centres in the target regions that provide a comprehensive package of health and nutrition services, GBV and PSS. To address long and unsafe distances covered by girls, women , PLWDs RI will deploy mobile health and nutrition teams to serve underserved host and internally displaced communities outside the main urban hubs with lifesaving primary health services, including ante- and post-natal care, and integrated management of childhood diseases, GBV and PSS in the targeted areas, these units will be linked to static facilities. RI recognizes that people with disabilities do face additional barriers in accessing health services in safe and dignified manner, this project will therefore consult people living with disabilities to understand access and inclusion barriers they face and integrate mechanisms to remove these barriers and promote their safe access to health services including MHPSS services to enable them cope positively with life challenges.

Project Activities

RI will implement the following activities in alignment with the health cluster strategic objectives: - Provide primary health care services at 10 static and 2 mobile facilities, including consultations and referrals to vulnerable people in need (men, women, children, host communities, IDPs, PLWDs, returnees and refugees), total of 102,000 consultations will be done(16,320 boys, 17,340 girls, 20,400 male and 47,940 female) - Improve access and reproductive and child health services including ANC, PNC, skilled delivery services and child wellness visit, and EPI ; - Procure and provide essential drugs for the static health facilities and mobile clinics; - Train health facility staff to ensure competency and quality care [target: 100 staff trained]; - Conduct community sensitization and awareness on key health messages through door to door, radio outreach and facility based sessions [target: 200,000 sensitized]; - Support measles and polio campaigns with Somali MOH, WHO and health partners [target: 10,000 immunized] - Train health workers on EPI planning, forecasting and tools for immunization Improve early warning and surveillance systems [target: 24 health workers trained] - Train health workers on sentinel reporting and response [target: 24 health workers trained]; - Train health workers including CHWs on AWD identification and response as part of rapid response team Community Engagement [target: 60 trained] - Conduct health promotion and disease prevention awareness campaigns [target: 20 campaigns; 4 per region] - Train community health committees to support management of health facilities and serve as a conduit to better meet community health needs [target: 10 health committees one for each of 10 static facility trained]; - Train community health workers on integrated community case management of malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea [target: 40 CHWs trained]; - Link traditional birth attendants to qualified midwives at the health facility and train TBAs on danger signs of pregnancy Systems Strengthening [target: 20 TBAs linked ]; - Establish referral pathway from community to PHU, health facility, referral health center, to tertiary health center [target: 10 referral pathway for 10 static facilities and 5 pathway for each of 2 mobile unit based on villages served(10)]Train health workers on Psychological First Aid and PSS, Clinical Management of Rape and GBV[target:34 health care workers ];Equip health facilities with PEP medicine and supplies[target: 15 static and 5 mobile health facilities];Establish referral relationships with existing MHPSS specialized service providers[target: 6 regions];Community outreach awareness conducted on MHPSS [target: 2 campaigns done across all project locations]. All project personnel will be sensitized on barriers and needs of people living with disability to ensure they are sensitive and seek to address barriers faced, for example during community door to door campaigns, households with people living with disability will be prioritized and information disseminated in diverse ways that persons with disability can be able to access the information including use of sign language interpretations. CHCs will be sensitized and supported to ensure health facility infrastructure is friendly and accessible to people living with disabilities. RI will ensure implementation of its elaborate safeguarding policies and procedures throughout the life of the project and ensure we cause no harm to the communities.

Mitigation Measures

- Mitigation measures for risks associated with distance to access services; Under the GBV project, RI facilitate communities to conduct safety audit and identify mitigation plans. RI will intentionally facilitate communities to review conditions for ensuring safety of girls, boys, women and men accessing the static and mobile health facilities among other critical services. The project will identify ways of making access safe for all including for persons living with disability, elderly persons, boys and girls. Girls, boys, women, men, PLWDs will be consulted for identifying suitable locations where mobile clinic will be stationed. CHWs will raise awareness among girls, boys, women, men, PLWDs and elderly about safety planning such as moving in groups, avoiding walking while it is dark. - Mitigation measures for barriers faced by beneficiaries to access complaints and feedback mechanisms; RI will hold consultations with different members of community (boys, girls, women, men, IDP, minority clans, host community, PLWDs and elderly) to gather their preferred mode of providing feedback, identify issues of access, safety and community confidence in the feedback mechanism. These inputs will be factored in the design and operationalization of the feedback mechanisms. RI will ensure there are diverse age, gender, and diversity friendly ways of providing feedback. RI will ensure that, once the mechanisms is set-up, it is highly socialized among beneficiaries using diverse user friendly and appropriate communication modes. Communities will be made aware of the expected support and code of conduct for personnel involved in the project and encouraged to report any concerns and unacceptable behaviors. RI will uphold confidentiality, safety and ensure prompt response is provided to issues raised through the mechanism. - Mitigation measures under risk of unsafe/unethical data management; RI will train all personnel involved in the project including clinical, non-clinical staff including community Health Workers on data protection. Informed consents will be sought before any survivors information is shared with other service providers during referrals. Personnel will be required to sign up safeguarding protocols that have data protection protocol as a way of committing to protect the beneficiaries' data. RI has elaborate data management and protection policy which guides all our data management processes, this includes ensuring safety of hard copies case files by keeping files with personal data locked and only accessible to staff on 'need to know' basis. Soft copies are safely safeguarded with passwords that are renewed after staff leave or take up other responsibilities. Official information including survivors and patient information is only saved on few selected office laptops and sent/received through official emails only (with passwords). Case files are coded and coding information kept under lock and key in a separate cabinet. MoH staff will be mentored and supported to uphold high level of safeguarding patients’ data.
