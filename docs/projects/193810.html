Project Summary

The education system in Gaza Strip has been hit hard. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview showed that the education system in Gaza Strip faces several challenges and obstacles. Approximately 600,000 Palestinians (56 per cent females) across the oPt, including around 13,400 Children with Disabilities (CWDs), need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022. Nearly 74 per cent live in the Gaza Strip. The report stated that among the longstanding issues affecting access to education include insufficient and/or inadequate safe school infrastructure, the May escalation on Gaza, which led to the early termination of the school year. These challenges have been significantly compounded by the outbreak of COVID-19. All of these challenges put children at huge risk of losing access to education with increased possibilities of drop-out and be left behind for the marginalized groups of children mainly children with disabilities (CWDs) and children living under poor conditions. Thus, this project will contribute to the 2022 HRP Education Objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access”. The project will focus on supporting safe back to school by improving the student’s academic performance and remediate the learning loss caused by the political instability and implications of COVID-19 for vulnerable children students (Out of school children and Children with disabilities aged from 7 to 15 years, and children at high risk of dropping out aged from 12 to 15 years) from the North Gaza Governorate.
Under this project, PSCF seeks to support vulnerable children in lower and upper elementary grades from 2 to 9 to ensure better access to education. PSCF proposes to deliver a project which will directly improve the children’s learning outcomes and psychological wellbeing, through removing barriers to regularly receive education services that enable them catch up with their peers, dismantling barriers to their current education, rehabilitating of safe and protective learning school environment, and re-integration of OOSC in education system.
The project extends to 12 months and intends to be implemented in coordination with known, registered and reputable 2 community based organizations in full coordination with Ministry of Education.
Under this project, a total of 500 vulnerable children will be targeted who have been identified as suffering from low academic achievement, psychological and behavioural disorders, physical disabilities, hearing and visual impairments. The project will target particularly those who are failing to meet minimal standards in the subjects based on the school achievement records in the past year. Special tests will be prepared to determine which students are in need of support. The project will focus on the basic skills in the Arabic language, English language and Mathematics.
The project will build on well-designed catch-up/remedial programs implemented by PSCF and will benefit from materials developed and tested.
PSCF will use methodology already proven effective through the on-going and previous projects implemented by PSCF team. The target children will be instructed by a well-qualified teachers.
Moreover, considering the psychological impact of COVID-19 and the political instability on students, the project will provide psychosocial support through the implementation of PSS services including individual and collective counseling sessions to improve the overall psychosocial wellbeing and learning loss of students. As well, parents of these children will be oriented about self-care, first aid psychological support, and stress release. Furthermore, it will support students with disability through rehabilitating the school environment and upgrading resource rooms as well as providing children with assistive devices to ensure their equal access to inclusive quality learning opportunity.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Gaza Strip has hindered children from accessing and enjoying their full rights to education. According to OCHA HRP 2022, it showed that the main drivers of education-related vulnerabilities across the Gaza Strip include attacks on schools, COVID-19 restrictions, lack of MHPSS support, insufficient or inadequate safe school infrastructure, and poverty. The May escalation added additional layers of vulnerabilities to school aged children in Gaza Strip. The education cluster reported (2021) that at least 331 education facilities in Gaza Strip, including over 136 schools, were damaged during the May escalation, affecting 121,092 children (59,008 girls; 62,084 boys) and of these around, 18,089 children (9,476 girls) are at risk of dropping out of school in the Gaza Strip. This has worsened the existing shortage of classrooms, where 65% of schools are operating on double or triple shifts. The children are at huge risk of losing safe access to education, more trauma, increasing the possibilities of drop-out, especially CWDs and children living under poor conditions. Also, the COVID-19 outbreak and the measures imposed by the MoE have had major implications on the education of children. The closure of the schools has a harmful impact on students’ academic achievements and wellbeing. According to the MSNA report (2022), 7.1% of households with school-aged children not attending school due to different reasons. 35.6% reported that they cannot afford to pay school related expenses, 28% lack of interest (of children) in education, and 7.6% reported that schools cannot accommodate child with disability. According to the recent study prepared by Humanity & Inclusion (2021), it shed the light on the main barrier to education and reasons for drop-out faced by children with disability based on parents perspectives which include: 1) fear that the child with disability will be the target of bullying or jokes by other children (25,26% for girls and 23,44% for boys); 2) fear that their daughter with disability will be sexually abused (21,05%), 3) the lack of public transportation to and from school (15,79% for girls with disability and 18,23% regarding boys with disability); 4) the lack of special education schools (13,16% for girls and 17,19% for boys); 5) the lack of adaptation of mainstream education system (13,16% for girls and 13,02% for boys); 6) the need of the child’s financial support of the family through paid work (7,81% for boys and 2,11% for girls); and 7) the feeling that he/she doesn’t progress (4,74% for girls and 4,17% for boys).As well, PSCF has conducted deep consultations with the MoE focal points to develop this project. Most of figures met assured that the disruption of education due to COVID-19 has affected negatively the learning process for all school children. They reported that the blended learning approach that adopted as a method of teaching during the pandemic has its own challenges in that not all children are able to benefit from the learning opportunities. The MoE provided us with the results of the recent statistical report that finalized in 2022 about “Student Dropout in Gaza Strip 2020/2021”. The report showed significant and worrying increase in the number of children who dropped out of school and at high risk of dropping out. The report pointed out that 6759 (4115 boys & 2644 girls) children are out of school from grade 1 to 10 in the five Gaza Governorates where the highest percentages was reported in the northern governorate. The report also stated that almost 3001 children with disabilities are enrolled in the public schools, and the highest percentage is also in the north governorate.Within this project, PSCF builds upon the assessments conducted on the ongoing project funded by oPt Humanitarian fund that aims to provide PSS for school children. The project team realized that most of the children targeted have low academic achievement and they are at high risk of dropping out schools.

2 - Activities

The project will target approximately 500-vulnerable children who are out of school and at high risk of dropping out with and without disabilities aged from 7 to 15 years. The activities that will be implemented include the following: 1) Identification of vulnerable 500 Children out of school and at high risk of dropping out including children with disabilities distributed as (250 boys and 250 girls). The process of identifying the OOSC children will involve conducting field visits to the project target areas. It will also involve visiting government schools and obtaining a list of children who have dropped out and at high risk of dropping out. 2 Social workers will be hired to work for 60 days to conduct home visits to identify the OOSC and children with disabilities to finalize the list of OOSC who will receive catch up classes. They will use a questionnaire developed to the project purposes. 2) Provision of catch up and remedial classes for a total of 500 children including OOSC and children at high risk of drop out and children with disabilities aged from 7 to 15 years. The target children will benefit from the catch up sessions in 3 main subjects: Arabic, English, and Math. 4 qualified staff including (basic education teachers, English teachers, Arabic teachers and Math teachers) will be hired. To implement this activity, 2 hosting CBOs will be selected to carry out the remedial classes. These classes will be run in double shifts; each shift will receive 4 groups of students (15 children in each divided into Math, Arabic and English in addition to counselling groups), 2.5 hours per day, and, 3 days per week. Approximately 4320 sessions will be conducted for 500 children during the project duration. In addition, an education supervision will be conducted by external supervisors to follow up the team in the field. A total of 120 supervision hours will be conducted.3) Provision of individual and groups counselling sessions to 500 girls and boys paying specific attention to the children with disabilities.Approximately 4-8 sessions will be conducted for each case needs individual intervention.Approximately 80 group counseling sessions will be conducted in the 2 hosting organizations during the project duration.4) 20 children will be re-integrated in the governmental schools. 5) Upgrading 4 resource rooms and adapting the school’s premises to facilitate the access of children with disabilities to education. 6) Provision of education kits, uniforms, and assistive devices. 7) Building the capacity of 20 male and female teachers in 10 schools through conducting 20 training hours on inclusive programs for CWD’s. 8) Deliver 12 awareness sessions for 200 teachers and 40 school students on better preparedness and response capacities improvement, including first aid training.9) Provision of safe accessible transportation for vulnerable children including children with disabilities to enable their access to the project activities and public schools.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PSCF has a clear complaint system managed by permanent employee. At the first meeting with parents and caregivers, they will be informed about the complaint system where they have the right to submit any complain or suggestion through PSCF's website, email or locked complaint box. The complaints and suggestions will be evaluated to take the appropriate measures.There will be a regular meetings with parents and children caregivers, so that the project-team will be acknowledged with recent recommendations,preferences or any complaints.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :
9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

During the course of the project, measures will be put in place to ensure that targeted communities can provide feedback on all the proposed services in a secure and confidential manner. Locked complaints boxes will be placed at the hosting CBOs where the activities will be conducted, so beneficiaries can anonymously provide their comments and feedback. Beneficiaries' feedback will be taken seriously and will be used to steer the implemented activities. Due to any restrictions that arise during the implementation phase related to political or health issues, PSCF will put in place feedback and complaints mechanisms that beneficiaries could access remotely. The mechanism will involve sending SMS messages to beneficiaries that explain how they can contact with PSCF team either by phone or through a generic email. Also, PSCF will run randomly selected phone calls to collect feedback from beneficiaries. The feedback will be collected on a monthly basis by PSCF teams. Mechanisms of collecting feedback will involve data collection through (1) pre and post questionnaires will be used to evaluate the effectiveness of activities implemented, (2) periodically, intervening beneficiaries to ask about the perceived quality of the provided services, and (3)conducting focus group discussions to assess the quality of provided services from beneficiaries perspectives.
