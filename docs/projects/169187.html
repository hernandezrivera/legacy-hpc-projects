Project Summary

As part of its response to the Beirut Explosion, WVL will provide mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and recreational activities to children affected by the explosion to help them restore a sense of normalcy and create better coping mechanisms with the aftershocks of the explosion. WVL will also provide MHPSS kits and activity sheets to children and their caregivers. Kits include material that can be used by the children and their caregivers as well as MHPSS printed material. WVL will also provide CP/ MHPSS and psychological first aid, to caregivers and children.
The proposed intervention will be conducted through WVL's direct implementation or through local partners in the areas of Achrafieh, Medawar (Gemmayze, Mar Mkhayel, karantina), Rmeil (including Geitawi), Bourj Hammoud, Sin El Fil, Mazraa. WV will ensure that close coordination is maintained within the CP sector to avoid overlap. WVL's response will aim to reach 14,000 individuals as part of its response. WVL will work when possible through local partners, but also has the capacity to conduct direct implementation. For PFA particularly WV has 38 social workers available for support.

1 - Needs

Initial assessments conducted following the explosion show that affected communities would be suffering from acute stress and mental health issues and that mental health issues such as anxiety, sleeplessness, or night terrors are likely to be particularly experienced by children, possibly for up to 16 months after the explosion . Adults are also likely to be in need of psychological support due to shock, stress, and other mental disorders that is commonly triggered by acute stressful events. If not addressed, this can lead to a risk of longer term mental health conditions, including post-traumatic stress disorder, anxiety, depression and addictions. As per the rapid needs assessment conducted by WV in its areas of intervention in Beirut and Mount Lebanon, 59% of adult household members and an alarming 90.5% of children were reported to suffer from psychosocial distress . Children were already negatively impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and the country wide school closure measures taken since March 2020, while the deteriorating in household economies have had a detrimental impact on child wellbeing. Children’s coping mechanisms are at serious risk of being overwhelmed and causing long- term developmental and health implications. Worryingly, partners have reported an increase in trends (detected through their observations, programs, referrals and hotline reports) as compared to previous month(s), mainly at the level of violence against children which was 90% in May, gender-based violence increasing to 80%, child labour increasing to 70%, street and working children increasing to 68% and substance abuse increasing to 47%. The financial hardship exacerbated by the restriction of movement, confinement under heightened levels of stress and fear is likely to produce stressful environments that precipitate violence. Similar to worldwide reports, GBV and violence against children are the most reported trends since the start of COVID-19. Girls, often spending more time at home because of security concerns and more restricted movement than boys, were already at greater risk. Following previous WV’s recent field assessment released in June 2020, findings show that a portion of interviewed caregivers living in Beirut urban areas cannot protect their children. Findings revealed that caregivers prefer to use verbal and physical violence to children and believed it is a proper way of raising children. Young girls feel these impacts in a different, often more significant way than boys. In an assessment conducted prior to the current emergency, more than half of girls living in Beirut surveyed said girls their age (between 10-19 years) face physical violence, sexual violence, sexual harassment and emotional or verbal abuse where they live. Community-based child protection activities and caregivers’ programmes remain key for disseminating information about child rights and reinforcing knowledge for preventing and addressing key child protection violations.

2 - Activities

Provide PFA support to children and caregivers through remote or face to face modalitiesProvide PSS sessions to children showing signs of psychosocial distress and CP concernsProvide PSS sessions to caregivers of affected childrenBuild the capacity of staff, volunteers and community-based organizations on MHPSS, CPHA, Early Identification and Safe Referrals and other related topics based on needs and follow up through ongoing coaching by technical staff WV will provide mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and recreational activities to children affected by the explosion to help them restore a sense of normalcy and create better coping mechanisms with the aftershocks of the explosion. WVL will also provide MHPSS kits and activity sheets to children and their caregivers. Kits include material that can be used by the children and their caregivers such as pens and pencils, papers, jumping ropes, clay, coloring books, as well as MHPSS printed material. To help caregivers cope with the impact of the explosion, WV will provide MHPSS to caregivers through support group sessions to respond the psychosocial needs of those affected and answer their parenting needs during the emergency. WV will also provide awareness session on important psychosocial, child protection, and GBV topics during the emergency. World Vision Lebanon aims to provide topics covering both MHPSS current needs and Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPHA) issues which impede the healthy development of children and the recovery of caregiver’s psychosocial resilience. This will be done through CP/ MHPSS and psychological first aid, with caregivers and children. Both face to face (where possible and safe) and remote support is planned to be delivered to target project participants. World Vision will help build the capacity of the staff, volunteers and community-based organizations on MHPSS, CPHA which is the prevention of and response to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children that saves lives, alleviates suffering and maintains human dignity during and after disaster and strengthens preparedness for any future crises. Providing early identification and safe referrals as well as other related topics based on the needs and will follow up through ongoing coaching by technical staff. Safe spaces to deliver MHPSS sessions to children will be identified and full COVID-19 prevention measures protocol will be followed during these sessions. Highly vulnerable women, girls and boys identified through registration, field visits or remote MHPSS sessions, who are in need of specialized support will be linked to available services in alignment with human rights and do-no-harm approaches.
