Project Summary

The project objective is to respond to the Health and nutrition cluster objective to ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in West bank including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building
This project aims to improve the quality and accessibility of Hebron residents to essential and lifesaving health care services.
The project will include both responsive and remedial interventions to meet the needs of children, especially neonates, and pregnant and lactating mothers.
It will address critical gaps in the health sector response for women and children in West Bank, especially in Hebron (H2 and Area C) communities, including the need for the maternal and child health, and neonate services.
In addition, the proposed activities are aligned with the UN strategy for Hebron, and responds to the latest multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by OCHA, and the neonate services assessment recently completed by WHO. The project will address the gaps through the provision of critically needed equipment, medical consumables, and drugs. The project will also address the needs to ambulatory services, through conducting basic life support training to front line health professionals and community members, to enhance the skills and competencies to respond to immediate life-threatening incidents, this will include neonate resuscitation training, and infant and young child feeding in emergencies training.
The total number of women, children, and men that will benefit from the HRP funding will be 40,000 across the H2 area and two localities in Area C in Hebron district over a twelve-month period. It is estimated that at least 14,750 high-risk pregnant and lactating women and 10,000 children under age of five (5,000 boys and 5,000 girls) will benefit from urgently needed lifesaving health and nutrition interventions. 300 female front line health care providers, and community members, and 200 male front line providers and community members will receive lifesaving training interventions and preparedness training. Some 14,750 men will receive primary health care services as part of the minimal package services

Needs

A key issue for the fulfillment of the right to health in H2 in Hebron is the availability of health services with poor emergency services and primary and secondary health care for pregnant women and children. (Joint United Nations Strategy for Hebron, 2016). In the OCHA H2 multi-sectoral household assessment of essential services, health care seeking by the residents were affected by several reasons. the crossing of checkpoints to access services, (202 HHs, 72%) was the main reason for preventing H2 residents from meeting their needs. Another 52 percent explained that the cost of health services was the reason; while the cost of transportation was a third reason. 37 percent restricted mentioned that settlers’ harassment on the roads. (MULTI-CLUSTER H2 HOUSEHOLD SURVEY, 2018). These challenges, along with a lack of proper infrastructure, equipment, adequate staffing, affect the quality of health services in H2. According to the findings from WHO assessment conducted in 2017, on availability, accessibility and distribution of neonate services in Hebron, the results showed there are insufficient neonatal services in Hebron. Leading to a high percentage of referrals to the northern West Bank and East Jerusalem, increasing the risk of medical complications and disabilities in newborn babies. The MOH expressed the need to support the neonate services in H2 and provided a list of required medical equipment. So, Al-Mohtaseb hospital has the potential for improving access to secondary health care for residents of the H2 area, mainly in emergency services and neonate lifesaving interventions; the need for the installment of new equipment is required. UNICEF’s interventions at Al Mohtaseb will complement to the UNFPA support. The medical equipment and life-saving drugs will be coordinated with MOH and are focusing on pediatrics, neonates, as well as emergency ambulatory services. UNICEF will ensure coordination and information sharing with UNFPA to ensure complementarity of items and equipment procured (Please see Responses submitted on 23Oct). Further, an estimated 3,000 boys and girls under age of five (female: 1,500; male: 1,500) in Area C, H2 and Hebron, including at least 1,000 children suffering from anemia, are acutely vulnerable and are at high risk of life-threatening conditions and developmental delays and disabilities. This situation is similar among pregnant (38 percent) and lactating women (28 percent). MICS 5.According to the findings of the recent Health and Nutrition scoping mission report, completed by the Save the Children in June18, (see attached) the current situation in oPT affected nutritional status amongst the children under the age of five, pregnant and lactating women, and increased their vulnerabilities and the nutrition risks (Save the Children, Health and Nutrition scoping mission in Gaza; June 18. (Report shared)The H2 assessment also showed high food insecurity among surveyed households. Only 6% indicated that the family has enough food. The initial results of the HH survey could be considered as an anecdotal evidence that malnutrition, anemia, and food insecurity were becoming evident given the economic degradation and restricted access of the civilian population to goods, services, and employment. We could conclude that the dietary intake and nutritional status are of great concern because of their known adverse impact on the physical and mental development of the child as well as the loss of individual achievement, poor quality of life, and significantly adverse effects on social and economic development at the national level. Despite the fact, that all residents including men, women, children under the age of five and the disabled people are affected by the restriction of movement and barriers to health care seeking, women accessibility to essential reproductive and maternal health services form a critical challenge for communities in these areas.

Activities

With UNICEF support, the MoH, NGOs will address identified gaps and improve access to adequate lifesaving maternal and child health and nutrition interventions through both responsive and remedial activities in Area C, H2 and Hebron.UNICEF will continue partnering with the MoH, UNFPA, and NGOs and other Health and nutrition Cluster agencies (WHO, UNRWA, MAP UK) to address identified needs and improve access to adequate lifesaving maternal and neonatal health care services interventions. Furthermore, the project will ensure complementarity with implementation of the WHO’s and UNFPA’s HRP 2019 projects, and will jointly contribute to the implementation of the National Every Newborn Action Plan 2017-2022.In addition, UNICEF will ensure the implementation of the minimum package of essential health services for communities that require access to primary healthcare services, working jointly with the Ministry of Health. Furthermore, the project will ensure complementarity with implementation of the WHO’s and UNFPA’s HRPs 2019, and will jointly contribute to the implementation of the Every Newborn Action Plan 2017-2022. In order to reach the 2019 HRP objective to Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building” the following activities defined under the Health and nutrition cluster(HNC) will be implemented: HNC Act:.1:.Respond to the needs of the most vulnerable people by ensuring that they have access to essential secondary level health care in West Bank. (Responsive)1. Provision of medical equipment and essential supplies to primary health care clinics operating in Hebron H2 area, and Al Muhtaseb hospital. 2. Provision of critical medical equipment to neonate unit at Al Mohtaseb hospital3. Provision of critical emergency/ambulatory equipment to the emergency unit at Al Mohtaseb hospital 4. Basic life- support training to frontline health care providers and community members; women and men in H2.HNC Act:2: Provide access to health care for the most vulnerable children under the age of 5, neonates and pregnant and lactating women through life-sustaining interventions at the facility and community level and Minimal Initial Service Package (MISP) (Remedial) 1. Infant and young child feeding in emergencies training 2. Provision of early screening and detection of developmental delays and or disabilities among children through training of medical staff 3. Provision of community/parent education services, to enhance parents’ skills in dealing with children of developmental delays and disabilities.
