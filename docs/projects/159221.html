Project Summary

Action against hunger provide food security and livelihoods support to Pastoralist, agro-pastoralist, peri-urban/urban and IDP populations affected by persistent periods of armed conflict, political instability as well as environmental and economic shocks in Somalia. The project target 97,594 individuals (25,765 male, 32,792 female, 19,128 boys and 19,909 girls) living in Bakool (Xudur, Elbarde, Wajid, Yeed), Bay (Baydhaba), Lower Shebelle ( Afgooye), Banadir (Khada) and Nugaal (Eyl, Garowe) regions. The project plan to reach 22,586 individuals (both IDP and host in IPC 3,4 and beyond) with immediate lifesaving interventions including Unconditional, conditional cash assistance and fresh food voucher to meet their immediate acute food needs to mitigate shocks/stress. Further 75,008 individuals’ (IPC 2) resilience capacities (absorptive, adaptive and transformative) will be strengthened to meet their immediate recovery needs, leverage on existing assets to withstand the effects of a shock, lay the foundation for future economic growth activities (adaptive capacity). In order to foster more sustainable stress mitigation mechanisms, ACF activities will focus on creating greater resilience for the beneficiary population by promoting economic growth activities. These activities capitalize on the foundation laid under strategies designed to enhance absorptive and adaptive capacity and promote a pathway to income generating opportunities while ensuring that the systems that support economic growth are appropriately developed and protected for future sustainability. The ACF livelihood programming strategy incorporates a short, medium and long-term perspective, provides a series of actions, which will enable affected Somali households to protect, sustain, and re-establish livelihoods, while leveraging existing and establishing new productive assets for a longer-term resilience-building goal.
This overarching project objective will be achieved through the following five results areas, which act as pillars for programming:
(i) Providing a means of addressing immediate needs post shock, planning for future mitigation through asset rehabilitation, income generation, and planning.
(ii) Ensuring the protection of existing and new productive assets, leveraging and developing planning processes, laying the foundation for future activities to promote economic growth, strengthening, and enhancing the support systems for sustainability to mitigate the effect of future shocks.
(iii) Developing economic growth pathways by ensuring entry to inclusive market systems, strengthening economic recovery and supporting systems, and developing strategic partnerships and value chains to ensuring impact through scale
(iv) Working with government, its policies and institutions to ensure an enabling environment with regulatory frameworks in place for sustainability.
(v) Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) approach to ensure that activities are monitored to illustrate progress, the impact of interventions can be assessed with respect to the goal of enhancing resilience, and that any learning that comes because of activities creates an evidence base for advocacy for policy change and informs an evolutionary program strategy.

Project Activities

Under this project, Acf will prioritize implementation of activities, which provide mechanisms to discourage negative coping strategies, protect productive assets and offset exposure for immediate recovery from shock. These intervention take place under the auspices of emergency aid provision and leverage the income-generating opportunities to, simultaneously, generate income and repair damaged infrastructure in support of current and future livelihood activities. These includes:- Cash for Work (CfW) to undertake activities such as the rehabilitation of productive community infrastructures; interventions designed to provide households with income to meet basic needs and to lay the foundation for recovery. - Unconditional cash transfer the most vulnerable households who do not have the capacity to participate in works programs; the project will provide an Unconditional Cash Transfer (UCT) or FFV to meet immediate hhs food and other basic needs.- Emergency agricultural inputs such as seeds, tools, irrigation pumps and fuel subsidy to assist farmers to immediately offset losses resulting from the emergency and lay the foundation for immediate improvements in food security- Livestock health, such as vaccination and immediate disease treatment for livestock and the provision of an income for Community Animal Health Workers.Acf will further invest in Disaster Preparedness/Management and Planning activities for future resilience to shocks.- Establish and operationalize the community of Early Warning, Early Action systems.- The project will facilitate the establishment of mechanisms to leverage collective strategies, such as community contingency funds, and link these to the creation or strengthening of existing informal safety nets to mitigate exposure to shocks so that communities can meet their own needs. - Establish Savings Group Schemes leverage existing savings both at individual and group level (social or contingency funds- CfW and UCT recipients will be encouraged to remit a proportion of funds received to a scheme to start generating future savings and social protection. - Support development of village level CAAP, periodically review action plans and link this plans to district and regional plans.- The project will prioritize activities for support from CAAPs, which focus on ensuring the systems that support community development and economic growth strategies are both functioning and sustainable. These systems include the socio and ecological systems such as the natural ecosystem, including water, rangeland and agricultural crop land. The proposed project will support governance mechanisms to ensure the best management for sustainability practices through capacity building of local governance structures such as conflict resolution Committees, WRMC, CAHW network systems, EWEA, AND NRM committees. ACF will provide direct support in the form of incentives, training and in-kind support to key line ministries such as the Ministries of Agriculture, HADMA. The will provide technical back up for decision making, and support evaluations, best-practice and lesson learnt to inform future government plans and strategies for programming.. The project will promote Inclusive Market Systems through:- Enhancing the capacity of smallholder farmers, pastoralists, women and youth to strengthen production activities for markets- Value chain analysis and value addition support.- Support evolution of Savings Groups and producer groups to form Business Councils, which can be leveraged to access larger amounts of credit and other banking services, allowing links to financial instruments for participation in formal market economy. - To increase employment and entrepreneurship through TVET, BDS, life-skills training, IGAs, etc. and develop linkages between employers and potential employees. - ACF will bring together groups of producers, private sector actors and government to establish Private Public Partnership.

Mitigation Measures

Lens 3: Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries: 3 (a) Protection concerns stemming from displacement: ACF will apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries to ensure that vulnerable persons, such as older people who may easily be excluded do not miss out. ACF will ensure sensitive data collection and analysis that seeks to identify the specific protection challenges of the target population at project implementation stage. Targeting of project locations and beneficiaries will be guided strictly by need with specific attention to households that may be marginalized due to family and clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. ACF staff are already trained and sensitized on PSEA and have signed a code of conduct that commits them to strictly adhere to humanitarian principles when providing services. The key risks include Service delivery being appropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary on their way to service delivery points and attacks on humanitarian staff among. ACF applies conflict sensitivity approaches in its project implementation and this will be mainstreamed in the project. All volunteers engaged in this action will be sensitize on the implication of this engagement in line with power dynamics at the household and community level. Deliberate action will be taken to discuss also the implication of this action on individual beneficiaries at the community level and if they are concerns, proper mitigation measures will be instituted. ACF local teams are well trained and experienced in handling sensitive community issues, especially if related to power dynamics and protection of all involved in the successful completion of this project. Additionally, beneficiary information will be protected and treated as confidential with consent being sort if any information such as pictures are to be shared. ACF will also be strictly guided by humanitarian principles, key among these principals being neutrality when providing services. Strict security protocols will be employed to ensure staff are not put in harm’s way, for example by ensuring that staff are back from the field stations early enough. Mobile outreach services will also target the remote but accessible areas, regardless of the community dynamics.

Please provide further explanation

• The proposed intervention is aligned to the Food Security and livelihoods HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building activities in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. The regions where the interventions will be implemented are characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to deforestation, land degradation, increasing aridity, overgrazing and water scarcity. By increasing access to water through rehabilitation of essential water sources and construction of new ones, the project reduces abnormal migration of human and livestock to other regions where they inflict significant environmental damages through over grazing and exerting added pressure on scarce water sources. In addition, the project will support communities to improve natural resource management to reduce land degradation and conflict over natural resources. This proposed project is foreseen to impose minimal risks to the surrounding environment. With the planned rehabilitation of key water infrastructure and establishment of effective community based natural resource management mechanisms, the project reduces negative pressure on the environment and significantly contributes the health and wellbeing of the habitat. The project will jointly work with line ministry representatives support the establishment and capacity building of Natural Resource Management Committees to ensure resilient community systems are in place. The immediate/emergency components of the project will be linked to ACF’s resilience program in the country and handovers to the capacity built community resource committees will ensure that the resources established are maintained and contribute to building resilient communities. The services and commodities planned for transfer to beneficiaries under this project adhere to government policies and have been designed jointly with community user groups. Local authority representatives to ensure it fits within the existing legal framework at district level will witness the transfer of the services/commodities. More specifically, the project will: Support formation and strengthen transparent and accountable governance structures at community, district and national levels such as village development committees, Water management committees, Natural resources management committee and link to local/district authorities – to ensure eco-system health and promotion of equitable and sustainable natural resource management of rangeland and water. • The projects will be implemented in close collaboration with relevant line ministries for technical support and establish good pathways for exit strategy. • Promotion inclusive markets and economic opportunities development through VSLA, sustainable animal health services delivery and Technical skill development. • Lobby/advocacy for Inclusivity – Women/youth/PLWD in governance structures of programs and targeted as vulnerable beneficiary.
