Project Summary

The overall objective is to prevent/mitigate the impact of violence on communities, and to increase the protection of population affected by conflict and/or disasters. NP will conduct direct protection, violence prevention, enhancing community protection structures, and supporting humanitarian response through protection mainstreaming advice/training and assessments.
In light of the protection and GBV needs, NP will respond to direct physical violence through direct protection services (patrols, protective accompaniment or referral to services). These measures will deter violence and ensure safe access to services by survivors. Referral pathway strengthening further help monitoring/responding to protection concerns.
Risk prevention includes controlling rumor so communities can make informed decisions about how to respond protection crisis – thereby reducing tension and displacement. Workshop/advocacy targeting communities and key stakeholders will help transform attitude and secure community participation in risk prevention/responses.
Building communities capacity to self-protection is critical to amplifying project effectiveness, relevance and accountability. It also ensures communities are resilient to, and can recover from violence/harms without NP’s presence. Concretely, EWER training/assessment – focusing on existing coping strategies – will support communities’ capacities in identifying and responding to protection risks especially in high-risks locations. Community protection structures will also be trained and involved in project activities; this will lead to greater community participation in identifying/enacting effective, relevant and sustainable protection strategies. By drawing members from different community segments, NP encourages inter/intracommunal interaction and participation of marginalised groups; this in turn promote social cohesion and overall inclusivity in community decision-making processes.
Inter/intracommunal violence has to be diffused before escalation. At NP fieldsites, there had also been reports of returnees and host communities tension as the two compete for resources. In response, NP will facilitate dialogues and meetings between parties, community members/leaders, local security actors and authorities. These will lead to enhanced understanding and trusts between parties, resolve issues before they escalate, and minimise risks of harm in case of violence. In line with the aforementioned local capacity building approach, violence prevention, protection and response workshops will also be provided to communities, using training manuals on peacebuilding, Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) and conflict mitigation.
Finally, to facilitate centrality of protection in humanitarian operations, NP will provide training/technical advice to partner agencies when there are identified gaps. NP will also use its unique access to provide regular assessments on ongoing protection crises, and thereby provide critical information to humanitarian responses.
NP has static teams in Central/Western Equatoria, Unity, Upper Nile, Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Lakes. Mobile team deployment is coordinated with the Protection Cluster, and once response is triggered by PC’s clear criteria, mobile teams can be immediately deployed to where violent conflict happens or where there is no presence of protection actors to respond.
NP has unique expertise in facilitating community-driven protection. Teams are embedded in community and present 24/7 to address protection needs. NP relies on strong relationship-building, resulting in community trust and acceptance that helps NP negotiate unique access to areas. NP staff includes advisors in protection, women peace & security, social cohesion, safety and security and M&E: guiding NP to be effective and efficient whilst ensuring safety. Operational support systems are in place to ensure effective and timely actions for programming and reducing operational risks.

1 - Needs

Since 2018 there has been a ceasefire between signatories to the R-ARCSS, though the implementation of peace agreement is slow and violence between non-signatory armed groups has increased. Clashes also occurred between non-signatory and signatory armed groups, as well as between their splintered elements. According to NP’s reports (June – December 2020), systematic targeting of civilians is committed by armed forces/groups in Pibor and the Equatorias; the scale/execution of atrocities suggests they are linked to politically-fueled conflict. NP also received reports of cattle raiding and revenge attacks in Lakes, Warrap, Unity and parts of Jonglei and Upper Nile. These took place along ethnic line, or between the constituent clans or sections. Meanwhile, as transition from PoC sites to IDP camps continues in Bentiu and Juba, protection concerns arose due to gaps appearing in service provision and law enforcement. Furthermore, HNO 2021 and observation from NP field sites (e.g. Ganyliel, Bentiu , Pibor, Lankien, Raja) indicated that South Sudan is becoming more vulnerable to climate change - especially in terms of growing flood intensity that resulted in losses to lives, property, food/crops, and agricultural production capacity. Changes in grazing areas also forced pastoralists – already affected by conflicts – to migrate close to the farming communities; this in turn led to conflicts over scarce resources. Finally, the COVID pandemic exacerbated vulnerability of the population. It also caused additional economic burden on communities and led to higher prices of food. According to HNO 2021, there are 1.6 million IDPs in South Sudan and 2.2 million refugees in nearby countries. Nearly 1.1 million IDPs and returnees reported conflict-related incidents over a six-month period, and a third of women and girls living in communities avoid areas out of safety concerns. This happens when host and returnee/IDP communities are increasingly competing for land and resources, or disputing over ownership of land/house/properties that were occupied during the owners’ displacement. NP field sites in Pibor, Yambio, Ganyliel, Bentiu, Ulang and Lankien reported that civilians are indiscriminately targeted amidst intercommunal violence and armed conflicts, leading to losses of lives and property and abduction. GBV in South Sudan is a protection concern for women and children, mostly girls. Common forms of GBV reported from NP field sites (e.g. Ganyliel, Bentiu, Lankien, Pibor, Yei, Raja) include rape, harassment, sexual exploitation, domestic and intimate partner violence, child, early and forced marriages (CEFM), assault and beating, and denial of resources. These risks are heightened in a context of flood, food insecurity and conflict. GBV cases are still underreported and do not receive timely treatment, on account of social stigma, lack of trust with authorities, fear of retaliation, road insecurity, travel costs, lack of access to required services and poor communication networks. Access to justice is also limited due to inability to access police and courts, as well as communities rely on traditional justice mechanisms that are not in line with relevant standards. Existing risks are exacerbated by the ongoing COVID pandemic, recent floods, food insecurity and economic crisis, as financial strains lead to more household arguments and GBV, as well as destitute families marrying their underage daughters off to receive a dowry. Although partners – in face of high demand for GBV prevention/responses services – do their best to scale up services for response and keep referral pathways updated/strengthened, remote areas and those affected by active conflict are still underserved on GBV services for prevention/response mainly when it comes to immediate support to sexual violence survivors and safe access to services. Places like Kajo Keji, Pibor, Mundri East and West, Central Unity remain in the radar to enhance prevention and response on GBV.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NP staff have the responsibility of raising awareness of the community members on sexual exploitation and abuse, so that they know that no NP Staff or any other humanitarian staff can sexually or otherwise exploit project participants. All project participants and affected persons will be encouraged to use complaints mechanisms and a trustworthy focal point (from NP or any other community structure) in case an incident occurs. Additionally, NP is committed to protecting whistleblowers. Such information will be provided as part introductory sessions and will be incorporated as standard topic for each activity.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NP will ensure most vulnerable members of the community have adequate access to feedback/complaint mechanisms. In the case of formal feedback and complaint mechanisms, NP will make the effort of including most vulnerable community as part of the aforementioned community feedback/complaint committees. Various communication channels will also be established (NP reception desks, suggestion boxes, telephone hotline etc.) depending on the need and appropriateness of the context. Similarly, and in more informal settings, NP will ensure adequate representation in more informal settings, either by ensuring participation of most vulnerable people in community protection/security meetings, training sessions, focus group discussion during assessment. Recognizing the issue of underreporting due to social stigma, NP will ensure the use of these feedback/complaint mechanisms by sensitizing communities on protection concerns, women and children’s rights; informing them of the ways to access these feedback/complaints mechanism and procedures. In line with NP’s emphasis on community empowerment, the organization will also work with the community in developing and adopting action plans for addressing the protection concerns. When receiving community feedback and/or complaints, NP ensures the confidentiality and safety the persons reporting. This is essential for preventing retaliation and encouraging community members to continuously submit their inputs. As such, NP will protect the identity of the individual/groups who are involved in an complaint or feedback mechanism process unless there is express consent from the reporter to waive the confidentiality procedure and when it is in the critical interest for protection of human lives and dignity (e.g. when there is a risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, or when human lives are endangered).

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NP focuses on building relationships and trust with the community members it works with. This is achieved by listening to the needs of the community members and proposing possible prevention and response activities within the scope of the proposed projects. NP therefore ensures that community members are able to make informed decisions and choices. NP proposes more in-depth information sessions, including the possibility of submitting feedback and complaints throughout the project. Appropriate mechanisms will be established – in accordance to NP policy – to receive and respond to community feedback and/or complaints. These mechanisms can exist both formally (e.g. in the form of a community feedback/complaint and response mechanism) or informally (e.g. interaction with communities, community protection/security meetings). During project implementation, NP will inform participants of protection mainstreaming trainings on the availability of complaint/feedback mechanisms. Before and during project implementation, NP will also also inform stakeholders (community members and leaders, women and youth leaders, local authority and security, humanitarian actors) about the mechanisms.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

NP shall design, monitor and evaluate the goals and objectives of programs with the involvement and feedback of affected populations. This information will feed back into planning process and implementation for the project period, thus ensuring that suggested activities of the projects will be evidence-based and community-driven. Principles of Accountability to Affected Populations shall be integrated into relevant organizational processes and documentation, such as; assessments, country strategies, program and project proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, partnership agreements, and reporting in a manner that is participatory to the community members. For project design and implementation, beneficiaries’ feedback is constantly collected through engagement with project participants via different channels such as trainings, workshops, community protection/security meetings, patrols, key informant interviews and meetings. When NP engages in peacebuilding and social cohesion activities – such as the implementation of shuttle diplomacy – NP also consults local actors beforehand to solicit their views on how best conduct such activities. Feedback is also gathered as part of any monitoring and evaluation exercises, which in turn provide important information for the design of future projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Risk 8.1Medium Likelihood, High ImpactBefore the registration/distribution event, NP will work closely with the concerned humanitarian partner to identify the appropriateness of the distribution site including the distance between the location of targeted population and planned distribution site, and space of distribution site. In addition, NP will help identify potential protection risk during this distribution exercise and will respond accordingly, for example through protective presence and/or patrolling that would inter alia help facilitate safe access to distribution/registration sites. Technical assistance – for example in the form of training and partner agreements – will also be provided. Risk 8.4Medium Likelihood, High Impact In its project locations, NP established that survivors – especially those living in remote communities – find challenges in accessing protection/referral services due to insufficient coverage of the said services, social stigma, and/or not being able to afford transportation cost. Similarly, survivors have little to no awareness of the aforementioned services due to insufficient information on available services. To counter this, NP will engage with available service providers and communicate their functionalities to concerning communities. This will be done through direct awareness raising and empowering multiple entry points – community chiefs, local protection structures such as WPTs and YPTs – to equip them for further awareness raising, post-incident support and referral. Furthermore, NP will disseminate information in at least one other local language to reach the majority of the population. It will also engage influential figures, men and boys to secure their support in addressing the socio-economic barriers for survivors to access post-incident care services. Furthermore, NP will work with referral pathway and protection actors to review and upgrade the accessibility and quality relevant services. Finally, when there is a protection case where survivors either face direct threats or require support to access post-incident services, NP will facilitate access and deter threats through protective accompaniment and referral. Risk 8.5Medium Likelihood, High ImpactAs part of its community engagement effort, NP will make a conscious effort of including marginalized groups, PwD and Older persons, women, boys and girls into its consultations, decision-making processes and/or supported community protection structures. Whenever engaging with a community, NP will identify most at-risk persons such as PwD, Older persons, single mothers, widows etc., and by passing this information to humanitarian partners NP could ensure access to humanitarian assistance. Besides, NP will required provide support and/or protective accompaniment to certain humanitarian assistance, for example distributions. Specific and appropriate measures – such as the use of visual/hearing aid – will be used to ensure feedback is collected from at-risks persons. Once feedback is collected, NP will disaggregate data by age, sex, and PwD, thus ensuring age, gender and disability specific concerns are captured and can be used in project cycle. Risk 8.9Medium Likelihood, Medium Impact To mitigate the risk of land dispute and tenure insecurity, NP will facilitate dialogues between host and returnee communities. At project start, NP will engage host community to set a peaceful ground for returnees by conducting sessions on peaceful co-existence and social cohesion. In cases of actual disputes, meetings will be facilitated as soon as tension arise or violence erupts. Regular communication channels, engagement of local authorities and community protection/peace structures, such as WPT and YPT, as well as training on violence prevention and social cohesion will act as additional measures for preventing tension and mitigate conflict.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This project will contribute towards indicators “Number of adolescents, girls, young women, men and boys accessing integrated HIV and GBV services” and “Number of capacity strengthening opportunities and awareness raising designed and delivered to relevant target groups for GBV prevention and response. NP will contribute towards these indicators through direct protection services and strengthening of community protection structures. The former indicator will be achieved primarily through protective accompaniments and referrals – an activity that deters threats and empower/build confidence of individuals in receiving proper care and addressing the structural and physical barriers to accessing services. This activity will target those facing direct GBV threats or require post-incident support to access relevant services. NP’s approach is survivor-centred and will not be imposed on individuals. NP will provide information on all the available services to support affected persons in making informed decisions. NP will also establish or strengthen referral pathways to encourage reporting, access and utilization of support services for GBV survivors. This activity will begin with service gap mapping and stakeholder analysis, followed by the formalization of pathways through partner agreements. Thereafter regular coordination meetings will be conducted to review and seek ways to improve the pathways. Where agency-based services are unavailable, NP will work with and train suitable community-based protection structures, such that they can also identify cases, provide essential post-incident support and to refer cases to partners as required. As a protection agency, NP will try to ensure the pathways are capable of providing high quality survivor-centred care with efficient access, as well as the capacity to deliver services in line with the centrality of protection approach. NP will adopt a multiprong approach – targeting different key stakeholders within the community – as it contributes towards the second indicator. This will involve women’s protection and GBV prevention/response workshops for men, women and youth, where participants will be trained on gender equity and norms transformation, provision of basic GBV response and support, and basic assessment tools. Training will also increase demand for justice/post-incident medical services by sensitizing participants on their existence and ways to tackle socio-economic barriers to help-seeking behaviors and reporting. A cornerstone of the UCP methodology is the engagement and strengthening of local protection mechanisms. In the case of GBV prevention and response, NP will work closely with Women Protection Teams (WPTs) and Youth Protection Teams (YPTs) whose purpose is to encourage and support women and youth, in particular, to take on substantive participation and leadership in community protection and security issues. Specifically, NP will form teams and train them on skills relevant to responding and preventing GBV concerns and incidents. Regular meetings will be facilitated with the WPTs and YPTs to encourage members to reflect on their observation and activities, and to plan future interventions that relevant to the local context and needs. Once sufficiently capacitated, NP will conduct joint activities with the WPTs and YPTs – including patrols, awareness raising, training and advocacy – and in so doing developing the WPT’s and YPTs autonomy and sustainability.
