Project Summary

The protracted insurgency-fueled humanitarian situation in the Northeast region of Nigeria remains grim, unpredictable, and underserved. The ongoing conflict is now entering its thirteenth year, and the increase in violent attacks witnessed over the past year has resulted in further deterioration of the security situation, mass displacement of people and a deepening of humanitarian needs. 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimated a total of 8.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection . Out of the 2,171,652 persons displaced across the three North-Eastern BAY states, Borno state, with a population of 4.2 million people is considered the epicenter of the crisis with around 1.6 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) . The results of the IOM Round 41 of Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) assessments showed a total of 2,197,824 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) were identified in 452,219 households in BAY states, a 1% increase (26,172 individuals) compared to Round 40 when 2,171,652 IDPs were recorded in March 2022 . With the closure of most IDP camps in Maiduguri, Borno state capital, the decrease of IDPs in M.M.C. was reflected in increasing IDP numbers in the Mongonu, Gwoza, Jere, Ngala, Konduga and other LGAs, further straining limited resources. For instance, DTM 41 showed Mongonu hosts 152,485 IDPs, which is a 2% increase (2,301 individuals) compared to 114,595 IDPs in Round 40 DTM. Acute malnutrition is widespread across Borno sate, and the surrounding areas. Within Borno state, the LGAs in IPC Acute Malnutrition Classification Phase 4 (Critical), include Bama, Dikwa, Gubio, Guzamala, Gwoza, Killable, Kaga, Magumeri, Monguno, Ngala and Nganzai, while LGAs in IPC Phase 3 (Serious) include Maiduguri, Jere, Marte, Mafa, Konduga, Damboa, Mobbar and Bayo. The main contributing factors to acute malnutrition include poor food consumption (quantity and quality) and poor health seeking behaviors. Additionally, poor health services, feeding practices, access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services and facilities, and food insecurity play a role. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance (NFSS) round 12 SMART survey prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) was high in Borno (12.3%) while the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) was 1.9% in Borno. By domain, prevalence of GAM was highest in Northern Borno (16.5%), followed by East Borno (15.2%). Prevalence was higher among boys than girls in all domains. Prevalence of GAM is highest among children in the younger age cohorts 6-17 and 18-29 months. Through Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition in Emergencies strategy and Management of Acute Malnutrition, IRC will deliver an integrated package of community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) services at 65 Nutrition sites across BAY states, as well as 7 stabilization centers. The selected communities have been impacted by the recent IDPs resettlement and camps closure which saw large populations settle in these areas. This resulted in high malnutrition rates in the region, especially with new arrivals from liberalized areas who are accommodated in transit camps. Services provided in these locations will include outpatient therapeutic program services (OTP), targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) services, Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) Corners, provision of micro-nutrient powder (MNP), and sensitization, counselling, nutrition best practices messaging, and follow-up provided by mother-to-mother support groups (MtMSGs) and Men-to-men committees (MtMCs). Children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications will be referred for inpatient care at the nearest stabilization centers, with emergency ambulance services readily available. IRC will also strengthen services at government facilities by building capacity of staff supporting the program and supporting with essential supplies including nutrition commodities

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in Northeast Nigeria is now entering its twelfth year, and the increase in violent attacks witnessed over the past year in the crisis-affected BAY states have resulted in further deterioration of the security situation, mass displacement of people and a deepening of humanitarian needs. According According to the integrated phase classification (IPC AMN) for acute malnutrition published on 23rd November 2022 that analyzed malnutrition situation between May 2022 and April 2033, it is projected that nearly 6 million children aged 0-59 months have likely been suffering and will likely continue to suffer from acute malnutrition. The continued COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated the situation by placing additional strain on already weak services and existing needs, impacting movement, livelihoods, access to services, and protection and health Nutrition sector.Nigeria carries the second highest burden of stunted children in the world, with a national prevalence rate of 32% for children under five and even higher levels in Borno (33.1%) and Yobe (43.7%) states. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months is on the increase across the BAY states when compared year-on-year, with BAY state rates above the emergency threshold at 12.3 per cent, 7.2 per cent and 10.6 per cent respectively as at September 2022 (Borno and Yobe states are classified as Phase 3 – Serious and Adamawa at Phase 2 – Alert levels). This includes about 300,000 children currently suffering from SAM requiring urgent treatment and over half a million moderately malnourished children. Without adequate treatment, one in every five of these children could die. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID-19 on the socioeconomic status. This is compounded by the poor breastfeeding practices in the three states; only 41% of infants less than 6 months in MMC/Jere and Konduga LGAs in Borno state are exclusively breastfed. risks, particularly for the most vulnerable populations.Activity Justification Why purpose and proposed activity? Since 2009, AOGs have terrorized communities in Northeast Nigeria and brought essential services to a near standstill. People face grave protection threats, markets are targets of attacks, and people are unable to practice agriculture safely outside of LGA capitals resulting in limited ability to earn income, severe food insecurity and malnutrition. Health infrastructure, staff, and medical supplies are limited. Large scale displacements of people into neighboring states and urban are as such as Maiduguri places additional stress on host communities with limited services. The Military is also scaling up its intervention in Borno state, recapturing areas from AOGs and revealing massive needs. The camp closures following the launch by the Borno State Government of the IDP return process in August 2020 lead to premature and coerced returns which population movements and increased humanitarian assistance need in the areas IDPs settled. This policy is already having an effect – Gwoza LGA is currently witnessing large numbers of IDP returns with the Gwoza Garrison Town reaching 5,154. By the end of the year, Gwoza is expected to host far more returnees than Maiduguri. The COVID-19 pandemic has only increased protection risks, reduced access to services and livelihoods opportunities, wreaking havoc on the most vulnerable populations.In response to the complex humanitarian crisis, the IRC proposes to implement a multi-sector, integrated emergency response program to meet the immediate needs of IDPs and host communities in the three BAY states, primarily targeting Michika, Konduga, Monguno, Gwoza, Damaturu and Gujba LGAs. The proposed program is designed to correspond to Strategic Objective 1 of the 2023 Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP).

2 - Activities

The IRC will maintain and support 7 SC. These facilities will accept SAM cases with medical complications from the surrounding communities and other partners, and likely remain at maximum occupancy based on current trends. Between January and October 2022, 6,856 SAM cases were admitted at at these centers, compared to 5,456 during similar period in 2021, which indicates the need to provide this lifesaving activity. Bed occupancy in these facilities constantly remained above 50% with the occupancy rate going above 100% during peak periods when surge of malnutrition cases is experienced. In the SCs, the IRC will maintain doctors, nurses, and other auxiliary staff to ensure quality of care. In addition, MoH doctors and nurses seconded from the MoH will work alongside the IRC staff to improve their capacity in managing in-patient treatments for SAM children. IRC will procure pharmaceuticals and medical supplies and other supplies necessary to run the center. The program will continue engaging a consultant pediatrician to facilitate quick and specialized reviews for malnourished children capitalizing reverse referrals where the consultant comes to the IRC’s SC rather than the IRC transporting children to tertiary facilities. This will reduce the lead time and logistics to conduct reviews. These children are fragile and weak with possible worsening of their illness. SAM cases with medical complications are admitted to the SC using WHO’s SAM admission criteria. In the IRC’s SC, medical care and supervision will be provided 24-hours a day by trained nurses and medical doctor. The SC will ensure that admitted children are stabilized and then discharged, based on discharge criteria (recovering from complication, good appetite, reduced edema, and weight gain) and transferred to the OTP nearest the family residence to continue treatment. Help mothers are volunteer women who are trained to support the hourly feedings of admitted children. Under the proposed program, help mothers will also provide basic psychosocial support to caregivers. Like in the OTPs, mothers will be counseled on IYCF and will be encouraged to learn from each other. Harmful practices such as the use of bottles or teats and commercial milk will be discouraged, and mothers will be educated on the dangers of using them with their children. The IRC has also signed framework agreements with a service provider to provide food for caregivers at the SC. The tender process was competitive, and the selection took into account the safety and quality of food, including food hygiene. An IRC dietician will monitor the quality and quantity of the food provided at the SC. The caregivers will be served three meals per day, including fruits and vegetables. In addition, an IRC Reproductive Health staff will rove between facilitiesoffering family planning services and educating mothers on pre-natal care (PNC) issues and other needs. On discharge, if cured, a child will be given a ration of RUTF and mothers will be taught on how to use it in the home. The IRC will support 65 OTP sites across the BAY states where severe acute malnutrition without medical complications will be admitted for treatment. MIYCN-E services will be integrated into each site with communities targeted for best practices messaging. TSFP services for MAM cases will be incorporated where IRC supports such activity or referral pathways will be established to attach them to other partners for support.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection Mainstreaming in Nutrition Sector:The IRC Nutrition team works with the IRC Protection team to ensure that caregivers and children seeking nutrition services receive the case management services necessary to cope with the stressors caused by their situation. The IRC will station helpdesks at each OTP and SC, which are managed by staff and community-based protection structures trained on how to refer people of diverse ages, genders, ethnicities, religions, abilities, and socio-economic groups to appropriate protection services. The IRC Nutrition and Protection teams will also proactively identify the elderly and PWDs in need of services, including through homevisits. Child Protection services will be provided to children with SAM cases to resolve any underlying protection concerns that has resulted in acute malnutrition through case management services. Stabilization centers will have a safe space corner equipped with child friendly recreational materials for children to build their resilience and improve bonding with their caregivers. One on One parenting session will be conducted with caregivers of children to improve positive relationships with children, improve caregivers’ stress management skills, non-violent disciplines, and supportive guidance for children. The IRC WPE team will connect mothers and caretakers of malnourished children at the SCs to services provided at the WGSSs and CWCs, such as psychosocial support such as counselling. Mother to Mother Support Groups and Men to Men Committees will have a prioritized gender balance and will receive the IRC’s GBV Key Concept Training. The Protection and Rule of Law (PRoL) team will review and ensure that all Nutrition facilities meet minimum requirements for access of individuals in a safe and dignified manner. The PRoL team will facilitate community engagement on the locations of these facilities and through ProL-facilitated mainstreaming action plans developed by all sectors, appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Identification and special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services will be rendered through the IRC’s community structures including Protection Action Groups (PAGs), and Community Health Volunteers who will conduct door to door sensitizations and mobilizations. The IRC will prioritize the needs of women and girls to narrow the gender gap. Women will be encouraged to participate in mother to mother support groups (MtMSGs), which are groups of mothers who receive training on best mothering practices and use their knowledge to educate the community. They will also be encouraged to become CHVs. Their ability to visit homes more easily than men will be particularly useful for follow up of family planning clients, home deliveries, malnourished children and defaulter tracing. At the same time, the IRC will explore ways to also reach men without reinforcing gender norms. For instance, men’s participation in health awareness-raising sessions and men-to-men support groups will be encouraged. The IRC will integrate the four principles of protection mainstreaming in their workings in all of its health facilities which will be accessible to the beneficiaries. The IRC will ensure that all vulnerable groups safely access all facilities; where necessary, the IRC’s Health and PRoL teams will collaborate to ensure that basic structures like ramps are constructed and all pathways made comfortable for persons with limited mobility. Health facilities will have private consultation rooms for privacy and confidentiality purposes, and screens/curtains provided for privacy. The health team will continue to engage members from both host communities and IDPs as CHVs while adhering to the age, gender & diversity mainstreaming principles to ensure that issues are addressed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The IRC has established Accountability Feedback Mechanism (AFM) in all its operational areas. This AFM will be maintained to receive feedback and complaints and provide responses on actions taken or to be taken will be provided to community members. In addition, the IRC will also receive feedback from the established IDP camp compliant mechanism, and action plans to be taken and responses will be provided to community members. In addition, other approaches the IRC uses are through dialogue meetings with the community leaders. The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions specifically with women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums to collect beneficiary feedback on quality emergency response actions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, suggestion boxes and community engagement meetings/focus group discussions will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serve as reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews will be conducted to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allow the IRC to make informed adjustments in project implementation
