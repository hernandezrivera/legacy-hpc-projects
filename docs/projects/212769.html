Project Summary

The education sector in Syria faces severe challenges exacerbated by an earthquake in February 2023, leading to a 4% increase in the education sector's PIN. This crisis has left 2.4 million children out of school, with 1.6 million at risk of dropping out. The earthquake's aftermath poses additional risks, including increased child early marriage, child labor, and psychological distress among children. Women-headed households, already struggling, report an inability to meet basic needs.
The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) presents a comprehensive Education Intervention as part of our integrated response in Syria. In collaboration with NRC’s Information Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA)/Capacity building, Shelter, WASH, and Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS) programs, this initiative aims to address the urgent needs of conflict- and displacement-affected populations, providing pathways to durable solutions. The proposed interventions align with the HRP strategic objectives 1 and 3.
Expected outcomes of NRC’s interventions include Increased enrollment and retention of conflict-affected children and youths in formal and non-formal education settings, enhanced skills and knowledge for teachers, facilitators, and trainers, Improved learning environments through rehabilitation works and greater community involvement and awareness. These interventions will target conflict and displacement-affected children and youths, including persons with disabilities, Syrian IDPs, IDPs and refugee returnees, host community members, third country nationals, and Iraqi refugees. Teachers, facilitators, and trainers across formal and non-formal education settings. Equal access to male and female educators, promoting positive male role models will be put into consideration.
NRC’s Child and Youth education interventions aim to.
• Deliver pathways back to learning, integration into formal education, and skills expansion for employment.
• Implement Child Education Pathways (CEP) and Youth Education Pathways (YEP) programs with a focus on Emergency, Transition, and Recovery modalities.
• Target newly displaced and returnee children and youths in emergency response.
• Address long-term displacement challenges for out-of-school children and youths in transitional response.
• Target vulnerable populations in post-conflict and stabilized settings during recovery activities.
The proposed interventions are broadly categorized as follows;
a. Education Programs:
- Child Education Pathways (CEP): Transition, Remedial, Basic Literacy, and Numeracy.
- Youth Education Pathways (YEP): Transition, Employability, Basic Literacy, and Numeracy.
- Creating Welcoming Schools program with soft and hard components.
b. Teacher Training:
- Adapted from INEE Teachers in Crisis Context.
- NRC’s Better Learning Program for psychosocial support.
- Integration of UNICEF’s “Ready to go back: Teacher preparedness Training Package.”
c. Rehabilitation Works:
- Rehabilitation of WASH facilities, learning spaces, and provision of basic furniture.
- Adherence to protection standards, ensuring gender separation.
- Installation of solar systems to address energy challenges.
d. Parental Engagement:
- Civil documentation not required for assistance.
- Parental engagement through awareness campaigns.
- Establishment of Parents and Teachers Associations.
NRC’s Education Intervention in Syria addresses the immediate and long-term challenges in the education sector, providing holistic support to conflict-affected children and youths. Through integrated programs, teacher training, and infrastructure rehabilitation, this initiative aims to rebuild educational pathways and contribute to the overall resilience and recovery of the affected population.
Education programming is primarily implemented by NRC. In exceptional circumstances, select Education activities may involve partner implementation for anonymity reasons.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NRC’s education program is intricately aligned with the No Lost Generation (NLG) initiative, specifically targeting the needs of children and youth affected by conflict. Our curriculum is designed to provide not only quality education but also tailored psychosocial support and life skills training, essential for fostering resilience among this vulnerable demographic. Through community engagement activities, we empower children and youth to become agents of change within their communities, further bolstering resilience at the grassroots level. By prioritizing the unique needs of children and youth and aligning with NLG objectives, our project is dedicated to catalyzing positive, sustainable outcomes for this critical demographic in crisis-affected areas. NRC is currently working with approximately 10% of our target population of Children with Disabilities (CWD). Our assessment, utilizing methodologies such as the Washington Group Questions and case studies within operational limits, helped estimate this figure. Moving forward, we aim to enhance documentation to better demonstrate our efforts in supporting CWD, aligning with international standards and ensuring inclusivity. NRC has put in place robust Monitoring and evaluation framework suitable for the Syrian context, and continuously strengthens this to monitor activities and measure the impact of interventions more efficiently and accurately. This includes regular assessments and feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries and stakeholders. NRC implements its Project Cycle Management (PCM) approach throughout its projects to track and address any challenges on time, ensuring the ultimate outcome of the activities. NRC includes robust lessons learnt sessions in its programming to ensure that insights into effectiveness, efficiency, internal process and programmatic integration, impact, and sustainability are identified. NRC also has in place an elaborate Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Safeguarding policy with a clear complaints channel to ensure any instances of abuse are reported and handled conclusively.
