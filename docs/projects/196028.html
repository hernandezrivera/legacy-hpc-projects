Project Summary

BRAC aims to contribute significantly to the education of the children of Rohingya refugees and the adjacent host community in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazila of Cox's Bazar. Considering this goal, the ISCG education sector’s objectives, and analysing the needs, BRAC targets 31880 children aged 3 to 24 years under this programme from both the host community and Rohingya refugees. A total of 8800 girls and boys from the host community and 23080 girls and boys from the Rohingya camps will be included in this programme. Among them, at least 65% will be girls, and 3% will be children with disabilities.
In line with Education Sector Objective 1, BRAC will construct/renovate 360 existing learning centres (LCs) to access quality education for Rohingya children. In addition, to provide inclusive and quality learning opportunities, 385 gender-segregated WASH facilities, including Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) facilities, will be set up for the learners in these LCs. Besides, BRAC will open and operate 250 adolescent centres using BRAC Humanitarian Play Labs in the camps where 17500 adolescent girls will have access to basic literacy, numeracy, and life skill-based education. BRAC will conduct a survey to determine the camps to avoid duplication. Moreover, BRAC will provide learning opportunities for host community children through 250 pre-primary schools targeting 7500 pre-primary learners, 1000 members from 3 multi-purpose community-learning centres (MCLC), and 300 adolescents from 5 adolescent clubs. This will contribute to increasing the number of school-going children and developing life skills among adolescents. Host community children’s engagement will help to ease the tensions and contribute to social cohesion between the communities. Furthermore, BRAC aims to identify and provide support to 1179 learners with disabilities as per their needs in both host and camp communities. All Learning Facilities (LFs) will be made with disabled-friendly support structures.
To contribute to Sector’s objective 2, BRAC aims to develop the capacity of 500 adolescent leaders to provide support to 17500 adolescent girls from the Rohingya community. Moreover, 300 host adolescents will receive training in performing fine arts and ICT. Staff and volunteers’ capacity development will also be prioritised. Atotal 125 teachers will be capacitated through introductory, refresher, and subject-based training in Myanmar Curriculum, basic pedagogical training etc. For the host community teachers and staff, BRAC will ensure the basic training, refresher training, and subject-based training for 250 pre-primary teachers, 5 adolescent leaders, and 3 librarians of MCLC. In addition, training on PSS, PSEA, and inclusiveness will be carried out for the Host community teachers as well.
In line with Sector’s objective 3, all community stakeholders (parents, community leaders, SMC, and other education groups) will be actively engaged in capacity-building and mobilisation activities to support inclusive education initiatives and promote the return to school/LCs. BRAC will sensitise the community on some issues, such as gender, environment, social cohesion, increasing girls’ attendance, safe back to school, awareness raising on COVID-19, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), and roles and responsibilities of community members to minimise pre-existing socio-cultural norms hindering children’s education. BRAC will emphasise rigorous monitoring and the Sector’s contingency plan to prepare for disasters and emergencies for man-made and natural disasters. BRAC plans to reconstruct 225 LCs among the 330 LCs safe against disasters. BRAC will coordinate with all stakeholders, including the RRRC office, associated government agencies, and other implementing organisations, for the smooth implementation of programme activities. For localisation and smooth operation of the programme, BRAC will involve the local implementing partners, who will be supported and monitored by BRAC.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Needs in Rohingya communities: Education provision for Rohingya children in camps is entirely informal. According to the GAGE consortium report published in March 2022, 49% of Rohingya adolescents attended informal schooling. Gender and age critically influence educational attendance and retention; older Rohingya girls are most adversely affected; only 2% of older girls reported engaging in learning. They face multifaceted challenges, including age and gender-based violence, disrupted educational prospects, widespread psychosocial distress and worry, and limited health service uptake. Children with disabilities face more significant challenges in accessing and progressing through education. Covid-19 has exacerbated the existing inequalities in all sectors. In the camps, governmental guidelines led to the complete or partial closure of education services. Learners were supported with a home-based system. However, it was not as effective as planned due to the restricted movement of staff and volunteers in camps and limited internet connectivity. Further challenges have also arisen, such as the marriage of girls and/or pregnancy, lack of guidance from learning facilitators, and children entering an age group that has lost interest in education. According to JMSNA 2021, the main concerns of education were: Children not having accessed home-based learning (44%), Children not having been enrolled in learning facilities pre-COVID (40%), and Children not returning to learning (35%). Girls are significantly out of reach due to patriarchal attitudes against girl education and the lack of menstrual-friendly WASH-segregated facilities in LCs. Children with disabilities have additional barriers to accessibility (camp, road, and geographical terrain) and a lack of inclusive teaching-learning materials. They are less likely to attend learning facilities. The lack of qualified and trained teachers from the Rohingya community is also one of the significant challenges now. Besides, congested camps in Cox’s Bazar have further constrained possibilities of establishing adequate learning centres. The lifespan of a bamboo structure is about two years, and most of the BRAC learning centres were constructed in 2018. These structures need to be reinforced to ensure the safety of the attending learners. Needs of host communities: The refugee crisis is having an adverse impact on the host community schools of Ukhiya and Teknaf, where enrolment and retention were the lowest in the country. The quality of education has also deteriorated due to community teachers seeking higher-paid jobs in the camps with humanitarian agencies. Countrywide lockdown due to the pandemic directly impacted children’s retention or enrolment further. In pre-Covid time, 37% of households with school-aged (6-18) children reported at least one school-aged child not having been enrolled in formal schools. Though during the pandemic, learners were supported with home-based education, JMSNA 2021 finds that 50% of households reported at least one school-aged child as not having regularly accessed home-based education. The community barriers to home-based education were inadequate technological devices, teaching materials, poor mobile networks and being perceived as ineffective by parents. Besides this, the proportion of retention of children aged 15-18 was slightly lower after Covid-19. Parents and children are still not going back to school. Households with persons with disabilities were significantly less likely to retain children, especially girls, during the post-Covid situation. Reduction in income and fear of infection with COVID-19 also remain significant concerns. Moreover, almost one-fifth are using unimproved sanitation facilities, and there is a need for gender-segregated WASH facilities and raising awareness of the habit of using WASH facilities among families and the community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This proposed education programme is aligned with the rationalisation principles of the Education Sector. This programme will implement all kinds of educational packages for its stakeholders, including ECD, Myanmar Curriculum, Basic Literacy and Numeracy Education for adolescents, including Accelerated Learning Package (ALP). BRAC will consult with the education sector to avoid geographic overlaps and gaps. BRAC is now operating its different services in all 33 camps of Cox’s Bazar; however, BRAC is planning to consolidate its all services in 15 camps gradually in future. As BRAC plans to undertake multi-sectoral activities in the same camp, its programmes are aligned with rationalisation principles. Moreover, BRAC has been ensuring stability in the refugee operation for the last five years and improving relations with refugees, the Government and other partners in the camps. As a result, BRAC is now considering multi-year funding, especially for critical activities that may require sustained longer-term engagement in Education, particularly adolescent girls' education. In addition, BRAC is committed to serving in the education sector in the same camps for several years, which is mentioned in its 05 years humanitarian action plan that will support strengthening relations with government agencies, the refugees, and other implementing partners at the camp level. All activities are aligned with the JRP following current Sectoral/Working Group objectives and strategy. We also actively participate in the relevant Sector and/or Working Group and are active members of the Cox’s Bazar PSEA Network and appointed PSEA Focal Points and Child Safeguarding Focal Points. BRAC has a strong government Liaison team to obtain the relevant approvals from the government promptly.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

BRAC will ensure that all stakeholders are safe while providing education services. As a PSEA network member, BRAC has policies on complaint mechanisms prioritising protection and safeguarding with an emphasis on 'Do No Harm' to the programme participants. Therefore, BRAC has a comprehensive safeguarding policy to prevent sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation for all including children and people with disabilities. Protection and anti-harassment policies are strictly followed in BRAC. It demonstrates a zero-tolerance approach to any safeguarding concern. Beneficiaries can provide feedback and complaint against any protection issue through different channels that allow us to prevent/mitigate adverse situations. All programme staff and volunteers will be trained on protection policies including reporting mechanisms. Participants will be informed about the protection issues reporting system through a home visit by volunteers and notice boards placed in the centres.

3.2 - Gender

The programme is committed to ensuring that the special needs of all gender groups are identified and taken into consideration. Following BRAC's Gender policy, it will emphasise empowerment and inclusion of vulnerable groups, irrespective of gender. The programme will conduct a needs assessment with specific questions to understand the needs of different gender groups. Based on the findings, this programme will ensure providing vulnerable groups-specific education support. Based on current analysis, it's been found that adolescent girls and children with disabilities are more vulnerable, so the programme is focusing on 65% of girls and 3% of children with disability inclusion. Moreover, the selection of female teachers will be prioritised; all the adolescent leaders will be female. Finally, the programme will set different indicators to measure age and gender-segregated data. And, it will ensure all staff are well trained on gender mainstreaming and all facilities are gender friendly.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

BRAC has a strong safeguarding policy including child safeguarding. All staff of BRAC will receive safeguarding training, and the programme will follow BRAC safeguarding policy to ensure the safety and security of PoCs including children participants and other children in the community. Additionally, BRAC will strongly avoid employing any child labour in any form of BRAC’s work. Based on need, different tailored activities will be developed. BRAC's Education programme designed all activities for the well-being of children such as adequate recreational and educational activities and co-curricular activities.

3.4 - Diversity

BRAC always believes in diversity in the project or workplace. A wide variety of activities will be conducted with the support of a diversified group of people. All age, gender, ethnicity, race, and religion-diversified children will be eligible to enrol in the learning centres though the most vulnerable will be prioritised, including disabilities. Regarding staff selection, everyone has the opportunity to be a staff regardless of gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, or disabilities irrespectively. A broader spectrum of diversity will be promoted through activities and policy.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

BRAC acknowledges the importance and value of involving affected communities throughout the project cycle. Participation and inclusion of the affected population will be ensured from the design phase to the final evaluation phase. Community-level meetings will be arranged to share relevant and actionable information with communities. Some community groups will be formed, regardless of sex, age, disability status, and other diversity, who will identify their education-related needs and address their vulnerabilities. Community feedback systems will be in place to enable affected people to assess and comment on the performance of our services, including on sensitive matters such as sexual exploitation and abuse, fraud, corruption etc. Regular consultation meetings with the community people will be organised for this purpose. The monitoring team will also manage time-to-time evaluations to capture community feedback. The project team will respond to the feedback of the community.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Considering the disaster risk of the area, this programme will provide DRR training to volunteers, teachers, and community members thus they can act as early responders to any disaster. BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF), which sets out the scope and implementation modalities for identifying and avoiding or mitigating environmental and social risks. This framework focuses on safeguard requirements, while BRAC’s environmental policy includes the “do good” criteria and the “do no harm” requirements embedded in this framework. As per ESSF, BRAC will not directly or indirectly support any activity that will significantly convert or degrade critical natural habitats for environmental sustainability. BRAC will ensure that the operation does not have potentially adverse severe impacts on biodiversity and ecosystem services.
