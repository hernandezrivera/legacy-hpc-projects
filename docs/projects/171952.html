Project Summary

In response to the crisis in Eastern Ukraine, the intervention proposed by ACTED, jointly with IMPACT Initiatives and the Danish Red Cross is aimed at a) informing the humanitarian community for evidence-based planning of the humanitarian response, b) piloting a project to strengthen coordination and capacity-building mechanisms for improved response by and transition of responsibility to local actors in Volnovakha raion.
The armed conflict in the East remains protracted and local communities in frontline areas still bear the brunt of the shock of the active conflict.. To address heightened vulnerabilities in these communities, especially for women and the elderly, it is critical to mobilize and support initiatives from authorities, communities, volunteers and active citizens, contributing to better collective resilience and preparedness to mitigate the effects of shocks. ACTED, in partnership with the Danish Red Cross, plans to support coordination and strengthen local capacities of the Ukrainian Red Cross Society volunteer network in Volnovakha raion as a pilot, community-based approach to support the sustainability of the response in Eastern Ukraine.
Building upon their experience with communities and local authorities in frontline communities of Donbas, and in line with the area-based/localized approach which integrates the needs of the most vulnerable, particularly women and the elderly, partners plan to provide an additional layer of support in Volnovakha raion through material support for first responders, including the local branch of the State Emergency Service of Ukraine (SESU) for Disaster Risk Reduction/Management (DRR/DRM), and recently or to-be established hromada administrations within the boundaries of the Volnovakha raion.
It is also planned that communities (volunteers, CSOs) be provided with information products developed by ACTED and IMPACT to further support the local capacity for evidence-based planning.
Furthermore, REACH will inform the humanitarian programming cycle and coordination, and promote better integration of highly vulnerable groups incl. women and the elderly, by systematically-collecting perspectives of affected populations disaggregated by key demographic data including gender, age, and disability markers. Building off of work conducted since 2016 and with adaptations to COVID-related dynamics, REACH will support the inter cluster coordination group and humanitarian partners with inter-sectoral needs assessments analyzing household needs, with a focus on gender, ability, and age disaggregated data in government (GCA) and non-government controlled areas (NGCA).
Through this project, REACH will be able to contribute to a more nuanced understanding of how COVID-19 measures may have exacerbated existing inequities and/or have led to new vulnerabilities, and considering multiple compounding vulnerabilities such as age and gender, while monitoring humanitarian trends and refining a multi-sectoral understanding of humanitarian needs. In line with commitments from the Grand Bargain, REACH will continue supporting the Ukraine humanitarian community with joint and impartial needs assessments to develop the HNO and HRP, while also continuing to strengthen local level capacities through its Information Management Hub technical trainings.
Beyond the immediate impacts on population health and the health system, the secondary and medium-term effects of COVID-19 are only beginning. The planned REACH activities will therefore aim to provide timely and relevant analysis, which include gender and age specific considerations, as information needs evolve in 2021, and the pilot to be run by ACTED and the Danish Red Cross in Volnovakha is aimed at supporting populations with an additional safety net at the community level to increase resilience, empower communities and encourage local ownership of the response to shocks.

1 - Needs

2020 has been dynamic and unpredictable, and as such, the need for up-to-date, comparable information is essential to guide understanding of how households (HHs) already affected by years of conflict continue to cope throughout 2021. It is clear that globally, the impacts of COVID-19 are not uniform across all population groups, and evidence collected so far indicate that heightened vulnerability has been spread unevenly among communities, with the most vulnerable (including elderly and isolated people, people with disabilities) facing increased risks of unemployment, isolation, while lacking access to proper healthcare and information. Additionally, an ACTED study found that residents of rural areas, women, and elderly individuals are more fearful and concerned about COVID-19 than other groups (KAPA, 2020).The need for information and support to community resilience during this time is therefore more critical than ever, as it not only affects the ability to control the virus itself, but also the subsequent secondary and tertiary effects of this global pandemic. Access to reliable and timely information is critical to meet the multifaceted and evolving needs of women, men, girls, and boys affected by conflict in both GCA and NGCA. Since 2015, REACH Ukraine has supported clusters, UN agencies, and the HCT in designing and implementing multi sector needs assessments to inform UN, donor and NGO strategies. Only through the implementation of large-scale household surveys using similar indicators, including gender, age, and disability markers, can the humanitarian situation be monitored accurately and reliably for the benefit of aid programming. This approach will complement programmatic local level assessments funded outside of the HRP. The project will continue engaging with the inter cluster coordination group and NGO partners to ensure that the data collection tools are adjusted where necessary. These assessments will also provide significant field evidence with regards to accountability to affected populations, especially those with compounding vulnerabilities incl age, gender, and disability. Future humanitarian programming will benefit from specific data on these highly vulnerable groups paving way towards interventions which are better tailored to the specific needs of women and the elderly. Based on agreed indicators from the inter-agency standing committee (IASC) on AAP, REACH assessments will continue monitoring the implementation of accountability mechanism in the field as per good practice over the past four years. Leveraging their experience acquired working with communities and local authorities in Eastern Ukraine to strengthen multi-layer preparedness and awareness of DRR/DRM, ACTED and the Danish Red Cross, have designed a multi-sectoral, area-based pilot to encourage transition of responsibility of the response to local actors; which would be implemented in the territory of the Volnovakha raion. Volnovakha being in close proximity to the LoC has been directly and indirectly affected by the conflict, notably increased needs for local employment and provision of services after the loss of access to NGCA facilities and NGCA residents coming to seek services (notably administrative) in GCA through the EECP. While the local capacity for provision of services has been thinly stretched, 79% of respondents to IMPACT’s 2020 Hromada Capacity and Vulnerability Assessment consider that ensuring appropriate level of social, medical and educational services for the population, including those for vulnerable groups, such as women and the elderly, is among the top five priorities for the development of their community. Proximity to the Novotroiske EECP also increased needs, in particular for people recently attempting to cross the LoC during COVID-19, when mobility on both sides of the contact line was severely reduced.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: Humanitarian aid actors have improved access to reliable evidence to inform their programming, especially as it relates to highly vulnerable groups incl women and the elderly Activity 1.1: comprehensive multi-sector needs assessment in GCA and NGCA to inform strategic level aid programming.The 2021 HNO preparation revealed information gaps, e.g. regarding HH needs further from the contact line (20+ km), or localized information specific to sub-regions of both GCA and NGCA. Changes to Ukraine’s sub-national administrative boundaries may also impact humanitarian planning and underlying needs assessments. Therefore, REACH may adapt the sampling strategy of the 2021 HH survey in GCA. In NGCA, REACH will continue to gather and triangulate information through a multitude of sources, including phone surveys, key informant interviews, individual interviews for those crossing the checkpoints.Activity 1.2: 2 household economic security assessments in GCA and NGCA with a focus on HH-level coping, income and expenditure patterns, and debt.In GCA, the Household Economic Resilience Assessment (HERA) focuses on HHsin raions bordering the LoC and seeks to understand how the economic impacts of COVID-19 have further eroded ability to cover basic needs, taking care to disaggregate data into various gender, age, and disability groups to understand the specific impact on each. Thus, future programming can be based on information which reflects the specific needs of highly vulnerable groups such as women and the elderly. In NGCA, the Humanitarian Situation Monitoring focuses on how populations have been affected by EECP closures re:access to income streams and basic services.Activity 1.3: Building capacities of partners in GCA. REACH will continue workshops on: i) mobile data collection ii) data analysis and iii) geographic information systems, following 3 core principles (using open source/widely available solutions, small training groups, and applied learning methods). REACH will also provide standing capacities for ad-hoc partner requests.Staff will be trained and assessments will be designed i and reviewed to ensure the highest quality and respect to AAP commitments, including the integration of gender, age, and disability markers into data collection. Data will be disaggregated by gender, age and HH indicators to identify the specific vulnerabilities for women, elderly, HHs caring for persons with disabilities, and IDPs.Outcome 2: Local actors are better able to take ownership of preparedness and response mechanisms and resourcesActivity 2.1: Capacity-Building of Volnovakha raion’s local URCS branche(s)Based on the URCS Organizational Capacity Assessment of Volnovokha local branch, the focus for capacity building are: management, communication and community engagement/mobilization. As a local entity, the URCS branch has a vested interest in building community resilience, especially for the most vulnerable incl. women and the elderly, and activities will focus on strengthening its volunteer structures via trainings on volunteer management, community engagement and accountability, communication, and resource mobilization.Activity 2.2: Provision of Equipment to first responders in Volnovakha raionIn synergy with Act. 2.1, ACTED and Danish Red Cross plan to provide equipment to first responders (SESU, URCS, and communities themselves, who are often the first to respond to emergencies). Partners plan to handover equipment for a mobile transit point, PPE and emergency tools (lamps, helmets, water pumps, fire extinguishers, etc). Building capacities will encourage transitioning ownership from humanitarian to local actors, thereby ensuring response is locally-owned and more sustainable.This pilot will build on the work done by ACTED and the Danish Red Cross through the 3P Consortium whereby partners work with first responders to build their technical capacity to plan for and respond to emergencies,including in Volnovakha.
