Project Summary

The nutrition situation in South Sudan remains critical as food insecurity is expected to worsen. Currently the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels in South Sudan are at 23% which is above 15% threshold of an emergency (UNICEF 2018).
In Upper Nile GAM, SAM and MAM rates are even more higher reported at 21.8%, 5.8% and 16% respectively, The FSNMS ( Food Security and Malnutrition system Rates) shows increasing levels of malnutrition in the Upper Nile States and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) 2018 classifies region to be in the Emergency level, grading malnutrition at critical levels. Its estimated that 1 in every 4 persons are food insecure. (IPC May-July 2018).
Contributing factors include limited food availability due to reduced planting and harvesting, insecurity, economic crisis and limited humanitarian access. The high cost of living combined with high levels of inflation has continuously suffocated livelihoods in families and children are hit hardest by this catastrophic situation change.
The Objective of this project is to:
• Deliver quality life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk under-five children, pregnant and lactating women
• Increased access to maternal infant and young child nutrition programs preventing under-nutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk.
• Enhanced nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information for early warning timely coordinated response decision making.
The modalities to achieve the above objectives will be delivered through:
• In patient management of SAM with Complications, Out Patient therapeutic program, and Targeted supplementary feeding programme.
The project targets a total of 3,436 direct beneficiaries: 721 SAM, 21618 MAM (among Children aged 6 to 59 months) and 97 MAM (among Pregnant and Lactating Mothers)
The major activities will be:
• Treatment of SAM and MAM among children aged 6-59months and Pregnant and Lactating mothers,
• Routine nutritional screening at facility entry points and community.
• Establishment of one stabilization centre at Baliet PHCC, 12 OTP and TSFP service sites at all the 12 functional health facilities of Baliet and panyikang.
• Training of Service on IMAM, CMAM and MIYCN.
• Establishment of 12 Mother support groups and conduct Individual and group IYCF counselling
• Conducting community outreaches and engagement meetings.
• Conducting Smart survey to access nutritional status of the population.
This proposed nutrition project will be integrated in to the already existing health programmes provided being provided in this location with support from World Bank. The integration will better address the nutritional needs of children and P&LW.
HLSS will implement this project in collaboration with other stakeholders such as the county health department and other clusters to ensure a holistic approach in service provision.

Needs

The nutrition situation in South Sudan remains critical as food insecurity is expected to worsen. Currently the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels in South Sudan are at 23% which is above 15% threshold of an emergency (UNICEF 2018). In Upper Nile GAM, SAM and MAM rates are even more higher reported at 21.8%, 5.8% and 16% respectively, The FSNMS ( Food Security and Malnutrition system Rates) shows increasing levels of malnutrition in the Upper Nile States and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) 2018 classifies region to be in the Emergency level, grading malnutrition at critical levels. Its estimated that 1 in every 4 persons are food insecure. (IPC May-July 2018).HLSS is also operating in Baliet and Panyikang County which is located in Upper Nile State, in a place hit hardest by the recent economic crisis that affected the most vulnerable groups within the communities thriving under basic amenities. Since the aftermath of these crises, there have been a few organizations providing humanitarian assistance in these locations. Baliet County is located in Eastern Nile of South Sudan; bordered by Malakal County to the South, Melut County to the North and River Nile to the West. Panyikang County stretches from western half of the upper Nile state boarders of Unity and Jongolei states. Its also boarders Sudan from north, Tonga from south, the rivers of sobat and Fulus from the east and with routine rebel attacks. The irregular rains coupled with the poor roads and insecurity is a major factor hampering accessibility to some of the furthest areas like Galachol and Benythiang. Access to Baliet and Panyikang during heavy rains is only possible by Boat. Panyikang access is even more limited by political insecurity and can only be reached upon security clearance. There are only Twelve (12) functional health facilities (10 in Baliet and 2 in Panyikang), Currently HLSS operates 4 OTP sites in Baliet County and served this entire 2 counties. Given the distance of the 2 counties from Malakal and Polouch Hospitals, plus limited access especially to Panyikang County, It’s evident that the current nutritional services do not fully meet the hug nutritional needs of these populations in terms of coverage. In line with the Humanitarian Response Plan 2018, these project aims expand access to nutritional services by establishing 12 OTP/ TSFP sites and 1 stabilization center. This will be integrated in to all the 12 functional health facilities. This allows to better address the nutritional needs of children and P&LW (Pregnant and lactating Women): Besides, it’s important to stress that there are powerful synergies between nutrition and primary health care including the focus on community-based approaches through engaging community nutrition volunteers that will be adopted.

Activities

Health Link South Sudan intends to use funds under this project to support the implementation of nutrition activities. HLSS is currently implementing health services in Baliet and Panyikang counties with support from World Bank and Providing facility and community based nutrition services with Support from UNICEF. This proposed nutrition project is meant to be integrated in to the existing health and nutrition system to increase access and ensure provision continuum of services ranging from health to nutrition under one roof stop centre. The project will employ the following modalities to provide services to beneficiaries:• Inpatient management of SAM with medical complication at stabilization centres.• Out Patient therapeutic feeding Programme for management of SAM• Target supplementary feeding programme for management MAM.• Institutional feeding program that support caretakers of children admitted at SC.While HLSS will directly implement the activities on the ground in close collaboration with existing structures on ground which include WHO, UNICEF, the national and state Ministry of Health and other actors in the health in the counties who will play a key role in integration and establishment a referral system across clusters. The major activities that will be carried out include: • Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children at 12 functional health units.• Treatment of severe acute malnutrition with medical complication at Stabilisation Centre • Treatment of MAM among children 6-59months and among Pregnant and Lactating Mothers.• Nutritional screening at health facility entry points and Community level. • Training of health and nutrition care providers on IMAM, CMAM and MIYCN while focusing on case detection and referral for community health workers.• Stock monitoring by stock cards and consumption based requisition.• Conduct Quarterly sensitization to raise community awareness of services available.• Provision Vitamin A and deworming and enhancing provision during NIDS.• Conducting individual and group IYCF and MIYCN counselling.• Creation and training of mother support groups. • Screening / testing and treat malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months• Establish 12 kitchen gardens for demonstration, will be managed by mother support groups.• Establishment of child friendly spaces at the service delivery points.• Conduct nutrition SMART surveys • Conduct sensitization meetings to make raise community awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in relation to nutrition.• Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by availing suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Few external conditions may have great impact on this project implementation, which include:The security of the area: The country is always very volatile, and may change at any moment, and could prevent and/or delay the implementation and monitoring of activities. HLSS has ready warning systems to ensure the evacuation of international personnel and continuously realize capacity building of local staff to monitor actions from remote.Collaboration between health/nutrition actors: is essential to ensure a coordinated and timely response in case of disease outbreaks or any emergency.For this reason, regular coordination meetings are held for the timely sharing of action plans in the context of the action carried out by the different clusters. Unavailability of qualified local staff: is an essential condition to ensure the provision of quality nutrition services. Coordination with the nutrition clusters and with the partners present in the area for the recruitment of nutrition workers remain the preferential channels for the selection of the profiles most relevant to the positions sought; HLSS will advertise and source in wider coverage beyond the county if implementation. Price and local currency stability: access to food remains challenging due to the increase of prices related to depreciation of the South Sudanese pound, increased transport costs and disrupted trade flows linked to insecurity. HLSS will only do planning based on Bank rates and will report to donor in cases of inflation. Poor roads and network coverage Communication network coverage remain a challenge this locations except Juba county that is fairly accessible, HLSS will use Thuraya for communication to field locations. The roads remain critical and become worse during rainy seasons where regular cars cannot deliver supplies to health facilities. HLSS plans preposition supplies and will hire trucks/ unimog to transport supplies , which is so expensive but also the only option in heavy rains.
