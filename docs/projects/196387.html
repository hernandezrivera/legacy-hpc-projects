Project Summary

DCA will implement a basic education programme for 2,000 adolescent girls and young women (aged 15-24) from the Rohingya and host communities, who are illiterate or pre-literate and therefore would not otherwise be able to benefit from the Myanmar Curriculum Pilot (MCP). DCA will provide literacy and numeracy sessions in English and Bangla or Burmese, using DCA's Non-Formal Curriculum Frameworks for Rohingya and Host Community Youth and Adolescents, as well as the Self-Learning Kit Phases 1-3, a community-based, autonomous learning program that can be implemented as a standalone or a supplement to the Curriculum Framework. Lessons will be delivered in person in Women and Girls Safe Spaces, as well as through community-based approaches like the SLK. DCA will develop Level 2 of its Curriculum Framework for Rohingya Youth and Adolescents, which will build on Level 1 (endorsed by the CXB Education Sector in 2021) and support learners to develop the foundational literacy and numeracy skills they need to engage in further livelihoods and skills development initiatives. Through the provision of education programming aligned with protection and its work in shared spaces with DCA's GBV team, DCA will ensure female learners are able to access a full array of services (education, protection, GBV).
Further, the project will work to mitigate a significant gap in teacher training opportunities for female educators from the refugee and host communities. Throughout the last two years, DCA has developed, implemented and refined a remote Female Teacher Professional Development (FTPD) package for female Rohingya teachers and developed a 23-module training course adapted from its Remote TiCC Training Pack. The modules were adapted and contextualized to meet female Rohingya teachers' latent literacy and numeracy skills and were provided over the course of several months to include repetition, practicums, and scaffolded content. DCA will further refine this training course that can be implemented both in-presence and remotely using a combination of print-based materials, audio/visual tutorials, and interactive audio instruction via MP4 players. This blended approach will also allow DCA to expand its reach among teachers, accounting for the movement restrictions women face in and between the Rohingya camps. As part of the continuous TPD modules, DCA will focus on developing Myanmar language literacy and numeracy tools to support teachers and learners in the acquisition of Myanmar language skills. DCA has developed a strong working relationship with Burmese language experts at Translators Without Borders who have supported the development of DCA’s audio files, workbooks, and Self-Learning Kit. DCA will support the development of a training on language pedagogy and ways to support teaching Myanmar language, which will provide an opportunity to help teachers upgrade their skills and accelerate learning. DRR will be integrated into the FTPD modules as well as considered an outstanding agenda item during the Alternative Learning Committee meetings.
The project will continue to support community engagement and awareness raising on the importance of ensuring girls can return to learning opportunities through its robust network of Alternative Learning Committees, and equip adolescent girls and young women with the skills and knowledge to support themselves, their families and communities. The project will continue to provide training on PSS, DRR, PSEA, and inclusive education and will expand its ECD programming as a lack of childcare means that female learners must often bring their children to the learning spaces. Ultimately, the project will support teachers, caregivers, adolescents and youth, contributing durable solutions that aim to generate resilience across the entire community.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic delayed the planned sector-wide roll-out of the Myanmar Curriculum Pilot (MCP), which will prepare Rohingya students for their eventual return to Myanmar and scale up over the next two years to reach a planned 600,000 learners. In the pilot stage, enrolment inequalities between boys and girls are already visible, with 10,810 total students enrolled–however, only 1,793 (16.5%) of those students are girls (CXB Education Sector Working Group, September 2022). Education partners implementing the MCP regularly emphasize the need to reduce this discrepancy and improve girls’ enrolment at each stage of the planned scale-up or risk leaving girls behind permanently. Per DCA’s 2020 Education Needs Assessment Report, the main barriers to Rohingya adolescent girls’ engagement in educational programmes are cultural, with girls’ movement and ability to travel to centers to participate in educational activities often dramatically restricted after age 10 or 11. Adolescent girls and young women must request permission from male heads of household and community leader to attend education activities, often have caretaking responsibilities that conflict with session timing and face harassment and other safety and security threats when traveling to centers or learning spaces (DCA, 2020). These findings are supported by the most recent 2021 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), which found that 18% girls of age 15-18 years old were enrolled in learning facilities before learning facilities closed in March 2020 (pre-COVID), and only 15% regularly accessed home-based learning since the start of the 2021 school year until support for home-based learning stopped at the end of March 2021. The report also highlights that only 12% are reported to be sent back once learning facilities will re-open. The education coverage for female Rohingya youth during COVID-19 aged 19-24 and the intention of re-enrollment is even more dire. Among 19-24 year females, only 3% were enrolled, 2% received regular home based learning and 2% are reported to be sent back after learning facilities reopen. For girls, marriage and/or pregnancy was the second most commonly reported barrier towards home-based learning, while it was the most commonly reported reason for not sending them back. The lack of female Rohingya teachers in the classroom also contributes to the lack of female adolescent students overall. The 2021 J-MSNA indicated that recruiting and retaining qualified female teachers in the camps is one of the most significant barriers to educational attainment for female Rohingya refugees, and the 2021 REACH Assessment of the Education Sector Response to the Rohingya Crisis, over 80% of the Rohingya teaching corps are men. Due to cultural practices, community perceptions of education, and limited access to educational opportunities in Myanmar, Rohingya women often lack the required competencies to serve as classroom teachers. As the MC scale-up with kindergarten (KG), Grade 1 and Grade 2 began in July 2022, the current teaching corps of 6,585 teachers is roughly half female (49%); however, the vast majority of the corps is composed of host community women and Rohingya men (69%). Though the qualifications to teach the MC were adapted to allow for more Rohingya women, they make up only 14% of all MC teachers (CXB Education Sector Working Group, September 2022). Further, there are fewer women working as Master Trainers and female teachers attend trainings at lower rates than male teachers (CXB Education Sector Working Group, September 2022). Teacher quality is one of the highest predictors of educational achievement and therefore quality, sustained TPD that supports the iterative process in which all teachers engage (particularly new, female teachers) is imperative.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

DCA's education activities specifically focus on the needs of vulnerable women and girls from the Rohingya and host community. In the Rohingya camps, DCA’s activities serve as a complement to the broader sector initiatives such as the LCFA and the Myanmar Curriculum. DCA specifically works with women and girls aged 15-24 who are illiterate or pre-literate and would therefore be unable to engage in or benefit from the Myanmar Curriculum Pilot. Because of this focus, DCA ensures no duplication with other education actors supporting activities for adolescents and youth and DCA’s education program responds to a critical gap. Further, DCA provides linkages to other Education Sector partners offering the MC or Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) so learners can continue to progress and build on the functional literacy and numeracy skills they have built upon graduating from DCA’s program. For learners who are not eligible for the MC or ALP, DCA refers learners to livelihoods and skills development activities, building learners’ self-reliance and preparing them for their eventual return to Myanmar. DCA will continue to participate actively in all coordination mechanisms, including the new Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector, and places particular importance on building strong, positive relationships at the camp level. DCA has been active in the Education Sector since 2018 and will continue to advocate for the education and protection-related needs of Rohingya and host community women and girls until no longer necessary. As DCA’s programming is designed to provide longer-term support to learners as they work their way, with the support of Education staff, through multiple levels of literacy, numeracy and life skills content, DCA strives to maintain a continuous presence in all project locations in order to ensure that DCA can build strong relationships with communities, government authorities and other organizations.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

DCA recognizes the centrality of protection in humanitarian action and works to mainstream protection throughout all project activities to maximize the positive impacts of the Education program. From the needs assessment phase, DCA identifies protection risks and threats that affect adolescent girls and young women from both the host and Rohingya communities, particularly those that negatively impact education access and retention. DCA then builds its response upon those findings and designs its educational programming and content to mitigate the relevant protection risks. Further, Education staff receive ongoing capacity building trainings on protection-related topics, such as PSS, gender equality, inclusion, PSEA and GBV core concepts, and DCA will maintain its referral mechanism across all project locations. DCA also ensures the inclusion of protection-related indicators in all education projects and disaggregates all data by age, gender and disability, informing future programming.

3.2 - Gender

DCA's education programming is specifically designed to meet the needs of adolescent girls and young women from the Rohingya and host communities, mitigate specific barriers to education, and contribute to an enabling environment in which women and girls can be self-reliant and support themselves and their families. Gender responsiveness and transformation are the foundation of DCA's Bangladesh country program, and gender is mainstreamed throughout all education activities. DCA conducts needs assessments that focus on women and girls in the camps and host communities and then designs and implements education programming to respond to the needs, barriers and threats identified through assessment, regularly consulting with rights holders and adapting programming as necessary to ensure usefulness and relevance. DCA includes gender-related indicators as part of its education programming and regularly disaggregates data by gender, age and disability.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

DCA employs a Child Safeguarding Policy, as well as an institutional Code of Conduct and Policy to Prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse which applies to all ages. DCA considers the needs of all children and adolescents (those involved in education and those out of school) on issues related to education access and retention (including physical, informational, cultural and others). DCA regularly consults with rights holders and families before, during and after project implementation. DCA supports educators and provides teacher professional development focused on child protection and safeguarding, including modules on PFA, social and emotional learning, inclusive education, positive discipline and classroom management, participatory learning methods and child protection and GBV principles and referral mechanisms. DCA will work directly with caregivers and community members to raise awareness and improve strategies related to positive child caregiving, gender norms and stereotypes, etc.

3.4 - Diversity

DCA ensures non-discrimination through needs-based programming focusing on specific vulnerabilities in the target groups and will continue to ensure rights holders' participation through conducting regular community consultations and supporting community-led initiatives. DCA's Education activities are specifically designed to meet the needs of adolescent girls and women (aged 19-24) from both the Rohingya and host communities, and DCA commits to reaching 3% female adolescent learners with disabilities, whose needs are often underreported and overlooked. Additionally, all of DCA's educational content reflects the principles of conflict sensitivity and is designed to encourage respect for differences and diverse perspectives. DCA's FTPD package includes six modules on "soft skills," which include gender, safeguarding, community engagement, effective communication, conflict resolution and leadership, providing teachers with practical tools and strategies for mainstreaming diversity.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Participation and inclusion of affected communities, particularly women and girls, is the foundation of DCA's work. Throughout the project cycle, from design to inception to evaluation and lessons learned, rights holders will be involved in planning, implementation, and monitoring project activities. All rights holders and the wider community will be enabled to provide regular feedback on all activities, which will then be incorporated into ongoing and future programming. At the organizational level, DCA has a Code of Conduct and associated policies which take all reasonable steps to protect rights holders from harassment, exploitation, discrimination and abuse, including PSEA, perpetrated by anyone associated with the project or with DCA. DCA maintains an AAP policy and framework and is CHS certified. The DCA CRM, which includes complaint boxes at all project locations and a hotline, can be utilized by all DCA and partner staff, rights holders, and the wider community.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, DCA's Education activities include components of DRR, including training for staff on DRR and DRM, teacher professional development modules on DRR/DRM and community awareness sessions conducted through Alternative Learning Committees (ALCs) on DRR/DRM.
