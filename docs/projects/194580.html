Project Summary

Currently approximately 6.6 million people of South Sudan’s population which is 54% of the population are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, driven by the impact of the economic crisis, climate-related shocks, conflict and insecurity and low agriculture production as well as economic impacts of the conflict in Ukraine that saw the increase of fuel and food prices in South Sudan. These exposing the people to high vulnerable and risk level caused by assets depletion and impoverishment experience with their livelihood disrupted. While the overall, 2.2 million people are experiencing worse conditions in Emergency IPC Phase 4 acute food insecurity and an estimated 61,000 people in Catastrophe IPC Phase 5 acute food insecurity in some counties in different states.
In the post-harvest, for projection period of December 2022 to March 2023, an estimated 6.31 million people will likely face Crisis IPC Phase 3 and 4 acute food insecurity, with 33,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe IPC Phase 5 acute food insecurity.
During the upcoming lean season projection period of April to July 2023, an estimated 7.76million people 62.7% of the population will likely face Crisis IPC Phase 3 or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe IPC Phase 5 acute food insecurity in some counties in different states. This is according to IPC report released on October to November this year and FAO and WFP situation reports released on October - November 2022.
An estimated 1.4 million children under the age of five in South Sudan will likely suffer from acute malnutrition over the course of 2023, including 345,000 children who will be severely malnourished and in need of treatment this is as well according to IPC report.
Based on FAO, WFP, REACH and FEWSNET and FSNM round 28 situation reports, people in needs of food security assistance estimated 7.76 million which is 62.7 % of the population, Severe food insecurity is expected at the height of the lean season of 2023. Cluster targeting to reach 6.0 million people.
The project focused counties Tonj East, Tonj South and Aweil North Counties in Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal States are as well affected and face multi context crisis including covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks causing displacements, wide spread flooding affected most communities and disrupting the lives and livelihoods of people.
The response will address the acute food insecurity needs by immediate scaling up food security response and build resilience and recovery at households and community level as delivery platform. The will target IDPs, vulnerable host population, returnees’ male female as well as children and persons with disabilities will be prioritized with focus on cluster objectives.
The project will enhance food production and build resilience through complementary crop, vegetable and fishing kits to the most severely affected households and protect their existing and increase resilience.
The project will put emphasis on GBV prevention by awareness rising and improve on safety audits to mitigate the risks on women. The most affected are children, people with disabilities, older persons, and women-headed households, identified as the most vulnerable to increases in food insecurity.
The project will target 33000 targeted beneficiaries that constitute of women 7920 men 7590 girls 8580 and boys 8910 of youth age by strengthening, promote Communities capacities households food security livelihoods and access to nutrition whereby prevalence of GAM is above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent in focused project counties.

1 - Needs

Based on the current IPC acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition analysis period of October to November 2022. The food insecurity across the country remains elevated due to the impacts of the economic crisis, climatic related shocks flooding and dry spells, low agricultural production, conflict and insecurity. In the focused project counties Tonj East, Tonj South and Aweil North, the recurring flooding and dry spell in conflict, a protracted macro-economic crisis, higher food prices; and devaluation of local currency are all threatening the food security of households. The households have high level food consumption gaps. The project counties are with chronic vulnerabilities that have been worsened by frequent climatic related shocks severe flooding and dry spell and conflict.The project focused counties in terms of nutrition are IPC acute malnutrition phase 4 where the targeted population groups suffers from acute malnutrition based on analysis results from the SMART survey and Food Security nutrition monitoring system FSNMS survey according to the current IPC released report analysis. Food and nutrition needs faces the most vulnerable groups. Women, Children boys and girls under 5 years, people with disability, older persons, Pregnant and Lactating women and women-headed households are identified as the most vulnerable groups. The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases.An estimated 6.64 million people 54% of the population are facing Crisis IPC Phase 3 or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.22 million people are facing Emergency IPC Phase 4 acute food insecurity, and 61,000 are facing Catastrophe IPC Phase 5 acute food insecurity. The households in the project focused counties are in IPC Phase 3 and 4 with extreme levels of food insecurity with exhaustion of emergency level coping strategies. The project counties will need immediate emergency food assistance complemented with emergency livelihood and livestock support across farming, agricultural and pastoral communities, for the medium- and longer-term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity.The main drivers of acute food insecurity and malnutrition in project focused counties include and not limited to: Climatic shocks floods and dry spells that have affected both crop and livestock production and productivity, Conflict and insecurity that continues to disrupt livelihoods, cause displacement and affect smooth flow of commodities from main markets to end markets, The continued poor performance of the economy, coupled with a depreciating currency and limited employment and livelihood opportunities, The economic impacts of the conflict in Ukraine that saw the increase of fuel and food prices in South Sudan, Increasing vulnerability and poverty because of the depleted and depleting asset base caused by the prolonged crisis that has forced many to rely on asset especially livestock, productive assets etc. disposal to cover the income gaps created by the poor performance of the economy, limited livelihood options etc and finally the reduced humanitarian investment that has led to scaling down of response to the population in need.There is need for immediate scale up of humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent total collapse of livelihoods in focused counties are in emergency IPC 4 and catastrophe IPC 5 acute food insecurity to protect livelihoods. Immediate and continue provision of food aid to most affected displaced population. Provision of agricultural inputs mostly crop and vegetable seeds and tools for both main and dry season production and introduction of flood resistance crops varieties, provision of fishing kits to the affected households which are positioned to be potential fishers comes fishing season and finally strengthen disaster risk reduction strategies
