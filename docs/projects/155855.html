Project Summary

This planned programme provides streamlined life-saving, humanitarian multi-sectoral assistance and strengthening the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites, as well as disseminates operational information on movements of IDPs and returns on a timely basis. The programme is targeting Aleppo and Idleb camps which have little space for those arriving, 1,000 new tents have been erected nearby to accommodate the latest arrivals 1,000 HHs (5,000 IDPs) especially for female headed families and children. CCCM teams suggest that IDPs sheltering outside of camps need tents, NFIs, food, hygiene, and sanitation services. Winterization of tents in the existing IDP settlements before the next winter is a very high priority in the absence of alternative shelter solutions and scarcity of heating fuel. The programme also aims to preserve heat and decrease fuel consumption by 40 percent through insulating 6,000 tents targeting about 6,000 HHs (30,000 individuals).
Based on its experience, QRC will establish and manage an emergency reception center based on needs and in coordination with the CCCM cluster, this will provide multi-sectoral full services with the capacity of hosting 2,000 IDPs. Improving infrastructure in the targeted locations will be based on needs to target about 50,000 IDPs and easing the sufferings resulted from floods through rain drainage channels construction.
The programme will seek feedback from both male and female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access and equal opportunities. Recruiting and training equal numbers of men and women for registration team. The programme ensures that the service is equally accessible and responsive to women, girls, boys and men.

Needs

As per HNO 2019, during sudden displacements, IDPs have often no choice but to seek shelter in planned camps, informal sites, transit/reception centers and collective centers. Of the estimated 6.2 million internally displaced persons in Syria, an estimated 871,150, or 14 per cent of all IDPs, are living in IDP sites where they receive humanitarian assistance and services. Between September, 2017, and August, 2018, the CCCM cluster has tracked almost 1.1 million population movements across northern Syria – a significant increase from the 804,300 reported in the 2018 HNO. During this time period, an estimated 18% of IDPs resorted to, or transited through, IDP sites. Across Syria IDP site population increased by an estimated 16 per cent, with such increases witnessed almost uniformly across all parts of the country. According to CCCM cluster last update in January 2019, the CCCM Cluster tracked 119,183 displacements since September 2018 (2019 HNO) in north-west Syria. Of those, 80,799 displacements were to Idleb, 37,617 to Aleppo and 767 to Hama governorate. As of January 08, the CCCM Cluster has reported that 6,079 IDP families living in sites are affected from the flood, and 4,983 tents are totally damaged or partially affected. the CCCM cluster revealed through its latest reports 322 IDP sites in northwest Syria, children make up 54 per cent of the population. In IDP sites in Idleb and Aleppo, the Cluster has tracked 1,401 female-headed and 466 child-headed households.Detailed need assessment will be conducted before implementing the programme and in coordination with the CCCM cluster to ensure that no overlapping or gaps might occur. The RC construction will be in response on emergency situation in case of the security situation deterioration. This center will provide equal services to both male and female IDPs prioritizing those vulnerable groups, and protecting displaced persons (Women, men, girls and boys) from harsh weather conditions by delivering the emergency shelter in the temporary transit center.

Protection Risk Analysis

The program has considered the Do-No-Harm principle in that it will use local field staff that will evaluate and assess the security situation, so that mitigation and response measures can be taken where and when needed. QRC is committed towards mainstreaming protection principles into all of our projects and do so through the use of several mechanisms. These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff on protection and mainstreaming protection throughout all phases of implementation, protection cluster involvement and creating a work environment that encourages the consultation and active participation of women and children throughout programming. Collection of beneficiary information is kept internal and only shared at the request of beneficiaries. Our documentation and media activities, including photography and videography, incorporate strong consideration for protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity.
