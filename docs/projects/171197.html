Project Summary

Somalia faced a growing number of threats to its food security in 2020, starting with the ongoing Desert Locust crisis, followed by COVID-19 first reported in March, severe flooding between April and September and poor rains forecast for the Deyr 2020 season. Based on latest projections, 2.1 million Somalis are in IPC Phases 3 and above through December 2020. Humanitarian needs could increase further in 2021 due to the influence of the La Nina developing, which tends to trigger dry conditions in Somalia. If the forecast below-average Deyr rains (Oct-Dec 2020) is followed by below-average or delayed 2021 Gu rains (Apr-Jun), this could lead to a rapid and substantial worsening of the humanitarian situation.
Rural populations are at a high risk of further deteriorating food security, given the direct and indirect impacts of these threats on rural livelihoods, related assets, food production and income. FAO’s proposal responds both to the lifesaving and life-sustaining needs of rural populations in IPC Phases 3 and above, against a Population in Need (PiN) of 3.1 million people prioritized for Food Security Cluster (FSC) assistance under the 2021 HRP. FAO’s planned activities will directly support 103,850 households in IPC 3-4 under both FSC Strategic Objectives (SOs), while further delivering support to protect food security in Somalia at a wider scale through countrywide animal health campaigns, among other activities.
Under SO1, to improve access to food, FAO will deliver lifesaving cash assistance to 76,300 rural households, including IDPs, in the form of Cash+, UCT and CFW activities delivered through FAO’s e-Platform for Mobile Money and Livelihood Assistance. All cash-based programmes include trainings on good nutrition practices, covering infancy to old age.
Under SO2, to protect livelihoods, FAO will provide in-kind and technical support to anticipate, mitigate and respond to food security threats across Somalia’s rural livelihood zones, including the delivery of integrated cash and livelihood support to 53,500 households targeted for Cash+ interventions under SO1 (while supporting an additional 7,550 households with agricultural inputs only). FAO’s planned Cash+ activities combine cash transfers with tailored livelihood support packages to assist rural families out of hunger sooner. Alongside cash transfers, the assistance packages will protect and restore the livelihoods of households in IPC Phases 3-4 by providing: (i) farmers with quality inputs to produce a nutritious variety of food crop (cereal, pulse and vegetable seeds, storage bags, fertilizer and irrigation/tractor services, where appropriate; (ii) pastoralists with nutrient-rich feed blocks and deworming to improve livestock productivity especially milk supply; and (iii) coastal communities with a variety of basic fishing gear and processing equipment to improve catch and consumption.
Also under SO2, in anticipation of drought conditions in early 2021, FAO will also strategically position water bladder tanks and provide nutritive animal feed blocks to protect the small milking animals of up to 20,000 pastoral households impacted by scarce pasture from dry conditions and/or locust damage. Countrywide livestock vaccination and supportive treatment campaigns will further protect livestock assets belonging to at least 2.2 million men, women and children. Preparedness for animal health emergencies will be enhanced through strategic stocking of vaccines and related supplies (PPR), and training to local partners in Livestock Emergency Guidelines and Standards (LEGS), antimicrobial resistance, disease surveillance and reporting, among others.

Project Activities

1. CFW/UCTs: 22,800 HH. Households with members able to work will engage in 24 weeks of paid labour to rehabilitate community infrastructure (e.g. water catchment, canal) or undertake work to improve their productive environment (Prosopis clearing, contour bunding). Households unable to work (pregnant, elderly, ill, disabled people, etc.) will receive UCTs of the same amount and duration. 2. Cash+ Livelihood Packages: 53,500 HH. FAO will provide 3-6 monthly cash transfers and tailored livelihood packages for food insecure farmers, pastoralists and coastal ﬁshers, including: (i) Cash+ Agriculture for 42,300 HH (cereal, legume, veg seeds, tools, tractor/irrigation hours, fertilizer), (ii) Cash+ Livestock for 9,300 HH (rangeland cubes and deworming); and (iii) Cash+ Coastal Fisheries for 1,900 HH (fishing gear, safety supplies, fish processing equipment, coolers, handling & cooking kits). Households without land/livestock assets may receive UCTs alone. FAO will provide all beneﬁciaries training in climate-smart practices, complemented by sessions in nutrition (infancy to old age) under Nutrition Cluster efforts. 3. Drought preparedness and response: In anticipation of drought conditions, FAO will strategically position water bladder tanks (3,000 units) and provide nutritive animal feed blocks (2,400 MT) to protect the small milking animals of up to 20,000 pastoral households impacted by scarce pasture from dry conditions and/or locust damage. 4. Countrywide livestock supportive treatment (parasites, wounds, respiratory infections): Treatment of 14.7 million livestock (sheep, goats, camel and cattle) belonging to around 367,500 households in all regions of Somalia in response to combined impacts of desert locust and drought conditions. 5. Countrywide CCPP vaccination: FAO will vaccinate 14.5 million goats against CCPP, protecting the assets of around 362 500 households, and pilot a related vaccination cost-recovery scheme in two regions of Somaliland. 6. Preparedness for animal health and other emergencies: This includes the purchase and positioning of 3 million doses of PPR vaccines as strategic stock and 3,000 PPR rapid pen-side test kits for rapid detection of PPR. The training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) will enhance rapid response to animal health emergencies. Training will also be provided to other local partners on Livestock Emergency Guidelines and Standards (LEGS), antimicrobial resistance and disease surveillance/reporting, as well as Fishery Emergency Response Guidelines Training.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash payments enable households to make their own decisions on needs prioritization. Empowering households to make such decisions also avoids exploitation by vendors and supports best value for money as households purchase the specific items they require, in the quantities they need, when they need them. Cash can be delivered remotely through mobile money transfer, which is especially important for (i) pastoral/nomadic households that have to keep moving in search of grazing ground for their livestock, (ii) households in relatively insecure areas where in-kind or voucher delivery may be hampered by volatile security situations, and (iii) rural areas because cash stimulates production across the whole value chain when beneficiaries purchase locally from local vendors.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Markets across Somalia are responsive to increased demand. In designing this project, FAO considered the capacity of local markets to respond to increased demand. The geographic spread, timing and caseloads of the proposed cash interventions take into account pressures on local markets and their functionality. Further, FAO will continually monitor markets in Somalia through the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) for any significant issues related to market dynamics that would impact the efficacy of cash and other interventions.

Mitigation Measures

--- FAO mitigates the risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services by: (i) Carrying out beneficiary registration and service delivery at village level. (ii) Providing services to beneficiaries remotely through mobile phone, including cash transfers and e-vouchers for livelihood support, disseminated by SMS. (iii) Using a consensus-based approach to beneficiary targeting and selection through a tripartite, that includes a committee of elders (representative of the local community), NGOs (ensuring compliance to FAO SOPs), and district authorities (ensuring the safety of the intervention and representing the government). (iv) Contracting NGOs and traders, authorized by local authorities to work in selected districts. --- FAO mitigates the risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, through: (i) Developing activities that do not require travel outside of the targeted villages. (ii) FAO’s Compliance, Complaints and Feedback (CoCO) system that enables two-way communication with communities, including confidential reporting on SEA. (iii) Toll-free hotline number (disseminated by bulk SMS before activities start, monitored through systematic surveys by FAO's Call Centre). (iv) Mandatory training to partners on PSEA and AAP. --- FAO mitigates the risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities through: (i) The above-described consensus-based approach to beneficiary selection and targeting. (ii) Systematic Call Centre surveys, which embed AAP questions, query the fairness of beneficiary selection and ensure at least 30% of beneficiaries are women. (iii) FAO’s Hotline, through which communities are able to report back. (iv) Mandatory training to partners on how to target and select beneficiaries against FAO criteria, AAP, Gender and PSEA, among other topics. (v) Provision of UCTs, to ensure CFW and other activities do not exclude people living with disability or illness, elderly, among other groups. (vi) Mobile money, e-vouchers and applications (e.g. voice recognition, voice messages) to provide more convenient, inclusive services to people who are less mobile, illiterate, etc. (vii) Field and Third Party Monitoring (TPM). --- FAO mitigates the risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, through: (i) FAO’s CoCO system (explained above). (ii) Call Centre systematic surveys, for a sample of beneficiaries, to receive feedback and complaints. (iii) Provision of SIM cards and allowance for the purchase of a mobile device (under FAO cash-based interventions) ensuring access to complaint and feedback mechanisms. (iv) TPM and field monitors, who have direct interaction with beneficiaries and report feedback. (v) Communication and sensitization sessions with communities, bulk text and voice SMS and radio campaigns to communicate FAO’s complaint and feedback mechanisms, and to update beneficiaries on their entitlements and other key information. --- FAO mitigates the risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries through: (i) Building consensus on the selection of villages and beneficiaries, through a tripartite approach (as above). (ii) District targeting by FAO, based on IPC data. (iii) Training partners on beneficiary selection criteria, AAP, PSEA and other mandatory topics. (iv) Mediation mechanisms, in place with district authorities, community representatives and government (federal and state). (v) TPM and FAO field monitors, who review and mediate cases of external interferences.
