Project Summary

In Syria, there is a pressing demand for NFIs to address the essential needs related to sleep quality, living conditions and personal warmth. This need has been further intensified by the worsening economic conditions and the devaluation of the Syrian currency. Around 10 million individuals lack adequate winter clothing and safe heating equipment to ensure comfort and warmth in their living spaces. The situation is exacerbated by the deteriorating quality of shelters, including issues with electricity supply, lighting, and insulation, which expose households to severe weather conditions and increase the risk of fatalities, especially during the winter season. It is projected that by 2024, approximately 6.1 million people will require assistance with NFIs.
The project focuses on the distribution of NFIs including winterization kits to the most affected and vulnerable families. The initiative commences with the publication of an advertisement, followed by a registration process to identify and select the BNFs based on vulnerability criteria. Tenders will be published by EPDC multiple channels, and the supplier offering the most competitive pricing will be chosen. NFI kits will be stored in nearby warehouses. The distribution phase will commence by notifying BNFs of distribution locations and schedules via text messages. Awareness sessions will be organized as needed. The distribution process will be overseen by EPDC program, MEAL team, and volunteers. MEAL team will be responsible for educating BNFs on the importance of addressing concerns and complaints in cases of poor service delivery. Finally, ongoing monitoring and evaluation activities will be carried out to assess the efficacy of interventions and gather feedback from stakeholders. NFI project prioritizes the rights and safety of vulnerable individuals, including women, girls, young boys, and the elderly. Gender equity is promoted, and measures are in place to protect against PSEA. A code of conduct and Complaint Response Mechanism are implemented to address these issues. Data collection tools consider gender and age, and community participation efforts incorporate gender considerations. EPDC aims to hire a gender-balanced team and collects protection-related data disaggregated by sex and age. Child protection and safeguarding standards are upheld, and a safe environment for children is ensured. A comprehensive risk assessment is conducted, and mitigation measures are implemented for different age groups. Monitoring, evaluation, and beneficiary feedback are utilized to assess services. The project follows the principle of 'Do no harm' and promotes inclusive participation. Measures are implemented during distributions to prevent PSEA, including gender-specific queues and priority lines. External risks encompass factors such as conflict, insecurity, international sanctions, ongoing instability, potential devaluation of the Syrian currency, and unforeseen events like the resurgence of COVID-19 and earthquakes. Mitigation strategies for these risks entail conducting security assessments, maintaining close communication with local authorities, identifying alternative routes, advocating for streamlined fund transfers, and closely monitoring prices and market conditions. Risks associated with interventions include Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion), Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population, The risk towards the humanitarian nature of the intervention for the presence and activities of military and non-humanitarian actors. To mitigate these risks, ensure that staffs have read the Code of Conduct, develop a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly, inform people about their rights regardless the documents they have, training staff about the BNFs rights and increase communication with community leaders.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

EPDC intends to provide assistance to 30,000 of the most vulnerable BNFs, which include host communities, returnees, refugees, and IDPs. This support will involve the distribution of two types of NFI kits to 6,000 households in various locations such as Al Sulaymaniyah and Big Hmeimeh in Aleppo, Arbin in Rural Damascus, Khan Shaykun in Idlib, and Dar'a community and Masmiyyeh in Dar'a.The contents of the kits will be as follows: - In-kind kits comprising (three blankets, three pillows, and one solar light) in 138$ per kit for each HHs.-Winterization kits containing heaters and vouchers for biofuel (tamaz), which include (a heater with tray, two steel elbows, two pipes, and 250 grams of tamaz) in 162$ per kit distributed in three rounds over a three-month period for each HHs.EPDC will follow the same distributing procedures for the two types of NFI kits, starting with publishing the advertisement, registration process door to door, final selection of BNFs upon the vulnerability criteria, Quotations & Contracting with suppliers, distributing of kits and finally preparing the tool for the following up process that will be conduct by program and MEAL team
