Project Summary

Venezuela is facing its 8th year of humanitarian crisis, caused by a deepening economic contraction, a constant inflation linked to episodes of hyperinflation, the worldwide fall of the cost and production of oil as well as the consequences of foreign economic sanctions. The visible results are related to an estimated outflow of over 6M people, who are leaving behind an exacerbated situation of vulnerability, especially affecting women in child-bearing age, children and their elder caregivers. COVID-19 has further aggravated existing severe vulnerabilities. The lack of fuel coupled with the mobility restrictions due to the pandemic led to the closure of many businesses as well as the contraction of remittances. This overall context has decreased as well the capacity of the Government’s subsidies supporting social programs. In 2021, an estimated 65% of the population is living in a multi-dimensional poverty situation. Moreover, the security conditions are also a contributing factor, especially in some bordering States, where armed groups are controlling illicit economic activities, as well as in main urban areas, where OSV are also increasing the vulnerability of affected communities.
The project aims at continuing health and MHPSS activities in Lara State started in 2020, as well as starting a similar intervention in Delta Amacuro, based on the lessons learnt acquired throughout its experience in the country, but customized to the peculiarity of this context, specifically referring to Waraos indigenous communities identified as target.
PUI together with its partner AVESSOC, has conducted assessments in both areas, identifying priorities locations and health centers according to the needs in Lara and Delta Amacuro. Generally speaking, the humanitarian emergency resulting from the profound socio-political and economic crisis affecting the country continues to be reflected in the public health system aggravated by COVID 19, a shortage of medical and nursing staff, a reduction to the bare minimum of essential medical supplies and medicines, failures in public health care facilities, serious limitations in service coverage and inability to control public health problems. Mobility restrictions increased unemployment, worsening the risk of child labour. MH is severely impacted by the listed health, protection and socio-economic issues and is also a common concern. Private care is not financially accessible for the majority of the population, so their only option is to go to low-fee charity health facilities when illness is advanced and home remedies become ineffective. This situation has been proven true by the interventions already conducted in Lara State, as well as by the KIIs and FGDs conducted in both States. In addition, these finding are consistent with the 2021 ENCOVI study that found 54% (a reduction from the previous year’s 59%) of people accessed a healthcare facility or pharmacy when they have been unwell. 46% did not go to any kind of consultation and this was due to them deciding to self-medicate, or did not have the money to pay for the service, or did not consider it.
Considering the mandate and capacity of PUI and AVESSOC, the focus of the intervention will be on ensuring access, quality and coverage of primary health care services, with a focus on SRH and child health, as well as the provision of MHPSS services with a focus on prevention and awareness of GBV. Services will be provided through the support of both fixed (OPD Maria Auxiliadora - Barquisimeto, Duaca and Tucupita) and mobile clinics, ensuring the proper equipment and drugs supply, staff training and health promotion activities. In Delta Amacuro, an in-depth anthropological study will be conducted with a focus on protection, thus to tailor culturally sensitive interventions and programs aimed at strengthening and empowering the communities and preventing GBV. PUI will at all times respect the do-no-harm principle, adapting activities whenever needed.

Plan fields

1 - INTERSECTORIALIDAD

Tanto en Lara como en Delta Amacuro, PUI intervendrá a través de un enfoque integral, que incluye atención primaria de salud (sobre todo salud materna e infantil), salud mental y apoyo psicosocial y protección en términos de prevención de VbG. En Lara, se continuará con la intervención que se viene haciendo desde hace dos años, apoyando a dos centros de salud y organizando jornadas de salud para abarcar comunidades vulnerables que se van identificando a través de la red establecida por el socio local AVESSOC. De igual manera, esta intervención se va a empezar hacer en Delta Amacuro, apoyando a un centro de salud y organizando jornadas para brindar servicios de salud primaria y de salud mental, con un enfoque a comunidades indígenas. El enfoque integrado de PUI en este caso se adaptará a la situación especifica del contexto de Delta Amacuro, tratando entender de manera antropológica como las comunidades indígenas afrontan temas de salud, salud mental y VbG, para poderlos abarcar teniendo en cuenta de una sensibilidad cultural peculiar al contexto.

2 - ESTRATEGIA BIANUAL

El proyecto en Lara ya lleva 2 años, mientras en Delta Amacuro va a empezar en 2022. La continuidad y sostenibilidad de la intervención se va dando a través de la formación continua y supervisión técnica que PUI garantiza a su socio local AVESSOC y a los centros de salud que se apoyan en el proyecto. Todo personal medico, de psicología y de promoción de salud es capacitado y supervisado constantemente para asegurar la calidad de las actividades.
