Project Summary

Palestine refugees continue to struggle after ten years of con ict. As stated in 2021 Humanitarian needs overview (HNO), it is estimated that sixty percent have been displaced at least once and 40% remain in protracted displacement, with Yarmouk, Ein El-Tal and Dera’a Palestine refugee camps still largely in ruins. A number of Palestine refugees have been spontaneously returning to these camps and to other places of habitual residence, despite the challenging conditions. UNRWA estimates that 418,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2021. Many among them struggle to secure basic shelter and NFI need, and will be supported by UNRWA through this project, as part of the 2021 Humanitarian response plan
NFIs (including mats, mattresses and blankets) will be provided to approximately 27,000 Palestine refugees affected by shocks and emergencies, including newly displaced persons and those directly impacted by new or increased hostilities. Families and individuals spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas that were severely affected by the confliict and/or lacking basic services will also be eligible for NFIs based on assessed needs. Cash for winterization will be provided to 187,000 individuals (55,000 Palestine refugee households).
Small-scale shelter repair initiatives will be implemented in selected locations to support 1,7000 of the most vulnerable refugees whose shelters have been damaged during the con ict (about 500 shelters). The targeted locations would be Ein El Tal, Yarmouk and Dera’a, Palestine Refugee Camps depending on the return rates and our capacity to secure the necessary approvals are secured. This will contribute to protecting the most vulnerable while preserving their resilience, decreasing the burden created by rent on their ability to provide for their basic needs. A self-help approach will be applied. This modality directly involves the families whose shelters are being repaired, respecting their dignity and generating employment within the local economy, whilst supporting cost effective shelter repair. Each family will receive on average US$ 3,000 for their shelter repair.

4 - Needs

Palestine refugees continue to struggle after ten years of confict. It is estimated that 60% have been displaced at least once and 40% remain in protracted displacement, with Yarmouk, Ein El-Tal and Dera’a Palestine refugee camps still largely in ruins. A number of Palestine refugees have been spontaneously returning to these camps and to other places of habitual residence, despite the challenging conditions. Many among them struggle to secure basic shelter and NFI needs. The impact of COVID-19 on these vulnerable refugees is also very concerning. According to an assessment conducted by UNRWA in July 2020, 90% of Palestine refugee households surveyed reported that they struggled to purchase food and other basic items due to the increasing market prices, with many going into debt to meet their basic needs. The living conditions of the households including the lack of ventilation, lack of electricity, lack of internet, overcrowding in the shelter, etc. impacted the performance and level of learning of students at home during the school closures. The current economic crisis in Syria has significant protection implications for Palestine refugees residing in the country. Groups at a particular risk of poverty and exploitation include female heads of households, unaccompanied and separated children, older persons and persons with disabilities. Palestine refugees, especially vulnerable groups, might risk losing their shelters as they are running out of means to support themselves due to the current economic situation. Palestine refugee beneficiaries (especially older persons) asked for NFIs and hygiene kits, including WASH items.

5 - Implementation of activities

NFIs, including mats, mattresses and blankets, will be provided to about 27,000 Palestine refugees affected by shocks and emergencies, including displaced persons and those directly impacted by new or increased hostilities. Those spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas that were severely affected by the conflict and/or lacking basic services will also be eligible based on assessed needs. Out of the 27,000 individuals, about 21,900 of them live in areas experiencing acute emergencies (Ein El Tal camp, YBB, Mzeireb, Jillin, Sweida and Yarmouk camp) and 5,100 external returnees and refugees affected by shocks will be targeted.Cash for winterization will be provided to 55,000 households in need for support to prepare their shelter for winter (about 187,000 individuals).Small-scale shelter repair initiatives will be implemented in selected locations to support 500 of the most vulnerable refugee families whose shelters have been damaged during the conflict. This will contribute to preserving their resilience, decreasing the burden created by rent on their ability to provide for their basic needs. A self-help approach, previously used in Syria and other fields, will be applied. This modality directly involves the families whose shelters are being repaired, respecting their dignity and generating employment within the local economy, whilst supporting cost effective shelter repair. Each family will receive on average US$ 3,000 for their shelter repair. This cash assistance will be disbursed in instalments. To receive the subsequent instalment, targeted families will demonstrate that they have completed 75 per cent of the previous stage of the self-repair project in line with the agreed budget and rules.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1: Beneficiaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from NFI distribution points • Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium • Mitigating measures: In order to minimize problems in accessing distribution points, UNRWA allows flexibility within the families as to who collects the assistance and a delegation system allows individuals of families to nominate a trusted person to collect the assistance on their behalf. RISK 2: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment• Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium • Mitigation measures: - UNRWA staff prioritizes vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and PwDs in order to reduce their waiting time. - UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming ensuring principles such as safety and dignity and access are incorporated RISK 3: Exposure to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW), including when accessing UNWRA services. • Likelihood: low, Impact: high• Mitigation measures: - UNRWA prioritizes awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks - UNRWA sends text messages to beneficiaries to advise them on changes to services in order to minimize unnecessary movementsRISK 3: Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. • Likelihood: High, Impact: medium • Mitigation measures: - Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is agreed upon with the government (GAPAR) and is communicated widely within the communities - Engage with community leaders to raise awareness - Establish complaint mechanism at community level - Training to staff.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: SEA in the context of NFI distributions and/or shelter repair (identification of beneficiaries and/or implementation) • Likelihood: medium • Impact: high • Mitigation Measures: 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. 2. UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. 3. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. 4. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure conﬁdentiality and set up accessible and eﬃcient complaint mechanisms. 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RISK 1 UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitoring of implementation. RISK 2: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. RISK 3: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
