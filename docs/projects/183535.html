Project Summary

DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid (DCA/NCA) and its partners, MAAN Development Centre (MAAN) and Women Affairs Centre (WAC), propose an integrated multisectoral WASH-GBV project in the North Governorate of Gaza strip- Farata (Beit Hanoun) and North American School (Beit Lahia).
Project objective: The project aims at improving the access to safe and equitable WASH services and appropriate GBV response mechanisms in accordance with the dignity of the underserved and most vulnerable communities.
The project will focus hardship cases, female headed households, GBV survivors, and people with disabilities. The intervention is designed considering a comprehensive assessment that addresses people’s access to equitable, safe, and sufficient water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services and examined the related risks of Gender Based Violence (GBV). An study (conducted in 2020 by DCA/NCA partners, WAC and MAAN) revealed that Poor access to adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation services plays a significant role in promoting gender-based violence against women. Upon the study, 53.8 % of the women reported that the bathroom in their homes lack safety and security standards.
The objective of this action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the OCHA HRP 2022 as such:
• For WASH component: The intervention is in line with HRP STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL” In addition, it meets WASH Cluster Specific Objectives 2. "Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians ".
• For GBV component: The intervention is in line with the HRP Protection Cluster STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence.
The WASH partner (MAAN) will identify the most vulnerable families with deteriorated WASH conditions (based on socioeconomic and technical assessments) to improve access to WASH services. This will be followed by the GBV response (by WAC) to address one of the consequences of the deteriorated WASH situation manifested in high rates of GBV. Maan will prioritize the families that include PWD’s to address their specific needs (i.e., rehabilitate the bathroom to be suitable for the use of PWD’s, adding ramps, hygiene kits content to consider PWD’s needs, etc.). This is applied for GBV response as well, where PWD’s will be prioritized for GBV services. Project activities will consider the COVID-19 related risks, needs, restrictions, etc. (i.e. virtual platforms for GBV services, Hygiene promotion and hygiene material support to consider COVID-19 needs, etc).
The intervention will mainly address both WASH and GBV themes, aiming at:
WASH (implemented by MAAN): Improving equitable and adequate access to WASH services in accordance with dignity (i.e. distribution of water storage tanks, installation/rehabilitation of sewer lines, rehabilitation of sanitation facilities inside homes, hygiene promotion (HP) and hygine material support.
GBV (implemented by WAC): Preventing and responding to GBV activities will include referral and case management, psychosocial support (PSS), protection and GBV related awareness, legal counselling, cash for protection, multipurpose cash assistance and dignity kits.
Complementarity and harmony will be ensured between the two components, where proper coordination will take place for this purpose. DCA/NCA are active members in the UN clusters, DCA/NCA and its partners, MAAN and WAC, will be engaged effectively in all the processes and activities including joining meetings, coordinating the response and provision of information needed for the 4 W’s systems and COVID-19 response reporting.

1 - Needs

During 2020, MAAN conducted a study assessing the relation between GBV and the poor access to WASH services. The study concluded that poor access to WASH services plays a significant role in increasing GBV against women. Upon the study, 53.8 % of the women reported that the bathroom in their homes lack safety and security standards. This is expected to increase the family pressures that women often bear. Therefore, it is quite natural to find privacy and physical safety considerations not available in these bathrooms as 65% of women stated. 90.6% respondents’ women experienced psychological pressure and distress during rashes, while they were forced to close windows to avoid unpleasant odors and mosquitoes. 65.8% of women confirmed that they are subjected to violence in times of sewage rash. 79.5% of women are unable to purchase detergents. 68.4% of women reported that lack of domestic water exposes them to violence. Psychological violence came in the forefront of the types of violence against those women by 53.46%, followed by physical violence with 11%, and deprivation of resources by 3.15%, then sexual violence is 0.79%. On the other hand, 73.5% reported that they were exposed to health risks due to the bad quality of drinking water such as intestinal infections, diarrhea, kidney diseases and intentional diseases affecting children due to contamination of drinking water. This has increased the economic burden on families and for women, especially widows and poor women.In September 2021, DCA/NCA partners, MAAN and WAC, have conducted in depth assessment for the WASH and GBV situation in the proposed target area- in North Governorate of Gaza strip- Farata (Beit Hanoun) and North American School (Beit Lahia). The assessment revealed that Gaza north is among the most vulnerable areas in Gaza strip in terms of water borne diseases (according to records of MoH 2021). Additionally, it is among the most affected areas due to the latest military escalation in May 2021. 47.8 % of families in the targeted areas have insufficient water storage capacity, 91.3% of the families with poor toilet conditions and 93.7% with poor kitchen conditions. The assessment involved 79 women through interviews and focus group discussions. 40.5% of the participants were between (18-34) years, (30.4%) was for the age group (35-44), (25.3%) was for the age group (45-59), and (3.8%) only for the age group (60 years and above). This variation indicates a good statistical indicator in selecting the age group to provide accurate results on the exposure of women to violence in these areas. Women with disabilities represented 3.8% that varied between mobility and visual disabilities, and they are the largest group exposed to various forms of violence. The assessment of WAC revealed that majority of the consulted women & girls reported facing an increased stress or/and violence after the recent escalation and during the quarantine period. In addition, the percentage of women who are subjected to varying degrees of violence (rarely, sometimes, regularly) is (94.9%), half of women in the sample are subjected to violence on a constant basis. The results of violence cases showed that (62.7%) of women are subjected to psychological/verbal violence and despite many attempts to stop physical violence. The cases of violence in the sample show that (38.7%) of women who are subjected to violence are physically abused, social violence (21.3%) and sexual violence (4%). Women in the sample are exposed to GBV in high degree with relative weight of (76.8%) in the mentioned areas.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

• E vouchers: The developed and operational electronic voucher system will be for hygiene items support. Th list of hygiene items and top up amount for each hygiene voucher/family will be coordinated and agreed upon with the WASH cluster considering the standardized hygiene kit content. The e voucher system operates autonomous without regular power supply or internet. Vendors are provided a terminal which is an Android smart phone with a special E-voucher app pre-installed. Beneficiaries are registered with an electronic data collection app feeding data directly into the E-voucher registration database and are instantly issued with a SMART card which contains a chip where all the data is stored. Voucher-based assistance is a widely used modality in Gaza. Affected people prefer using voucher systems to meet their basic needs instead of direct in-kind distribution. The market is accessible by rights holders, the needed items are available at reasonable prices and the suppliers have the needed capacity. Rights holders will have the opportunity to select their preferences from a diversified lists of hygiene items based on their needs. and Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA): will also be adopted to address the multisectoral needs of GBV survivors, bolster women’s social protection and provide a pathway for independence for women. The MPCA transfer value will consider the survival minimum expenditure basket recommended by Gaza CWG, that is app. 45 USD per individual per month. Post distribution monitoring will be done to assess how beneficiaries use the cash and address their satisfaction, where corrective measures can be taken accordingly.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA will support partners to increase the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. This will be ensured through engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. Beneficiaries will have access to both DCA/NCA and its partners complaint mechanism. DCA/NCA and its partners will consider the beneficiaries feedback and recommendation through direct contact and consultation with them. The complaint mechanism will provide the chance for all beneficiaries especially women and people with disabilities to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities through web site, hotline, complaints boxes, directly to the field monitors, and directly to partners’ or DCA/NCA offices. The applied complaint mechanism considers specific standard operational procedures in case of gender sensitive complaints. The received complaints and feedback will be documented and analyzed by the M&E and program staff to feed into the decision-making process and inform possible corrections during implementation that ensures a better response to the needs of the vulnerable groups. Community engagement committees will be formed to build community confidence and networks and ensure efficient and continuous participation of the community in the implementation and monitoring processes and to inform decision making. The project will organize regular focus group discussions with the community members to collect feedback on the provided services. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The projects focus is to enhance protection of GBV survivors and other vulnerable groups by providing a multisectoral/integrated WASH – GBV response. The project design recognizes the rights and needs of beneficiaries in terms of safety, security, and dignity and proposes activities that foster the realization of these needs and rights. It adopts gender-sensitive and culturally responsive approach to implement the project activities which will highly contribute to addressing the social and cultural risks that might undermine the participation of the beneficiaries, especially women in the project activities. DCA/NCA and partners use culturally responsive approach which involves respect for the cultural backgrounds of all right holders, particularly women and people with disability and fully recognize and understand differences, norms, taboos, and stigmas surrounding social and cultural back draughts of the right holders. During the inception period, the project elaborates an environment and social impact assessment to ascertain the environmental and social impacts of the infrastructural subcomponents; and to manage, mitigate, and monitor any possible negative impacts. All safety and security measures will be considered when implementing the project activities to prevent incidences and reduce safety and security risks. DCA/NCA and partners will keep close monitoring for the security situation and the COVID-19 developments and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. In case of closure of any targeted locality because of COVID-19, WAC is ready to move with the work online. WAC will coordinate with the relevant authorities to deliver all items needed by women and girls including those with disabilities under quarantines. DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency (www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population. PSEA mainstreaming: DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA. • DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaint handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality (either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism: me.complaints@dca.dk and/or using the partners complaints mechanism channels - WAC helpline 1800333666 or complain@wac.org.ps - MAAN Website: www.maancomplaints.com Hotline: 1800900901) • DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA training to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities that will include awareness raising messages sent via sms messages among other tools . • DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism. • DCA/NCA’s PSEA local focal point is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. Accordingly , DCA/NCA will continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information especially on available referral pathways for sensitive complaints and will continuously seek support from the available network services when needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The response will be directly implemented by DCA/NCA and its local partners in Gaza. DCA/NCA will manage this response through its office and staff in Gaza and its Country Office in Jerusalem. DCA/NCA and partners will implement the activities following the instructions and guidelines of local authorities and UN clusters in relation to COVID 19 situation. the selected local partners and DCA/NCA staff in Gaza have experience working under conditions that lead to restricted humanitarian access and adjust timing and approach as required. DCA/NCA has developed an Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan for assuring access. Networking with CBOs and NGOs in different areas, as well as empowering women leaders within safe spaces, is an added value in the proposal because it guarantees support in restrictive time in the buffer zone. The DCA/NCA will conduct regular visits to the response areas, following up on the indicators and response plan, and assessing response implementation via discussions with the response team and rights holders and by visiting the response sites.DCA\NCA with its partners will ensure equal and impartial access to WASH and GBV services as follow:- DCA\NCA access approach is consistent with the humanitarian core principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence, and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women and girls; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favour any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women and girls solely based on need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic, or other objectives. - DCA\NCA with its partners monitor access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g., persons with disabilities, elderly).- DCA\NCA with its partners use their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls to GBV services towards implementing UN 2030 agenda of “LEAVING NO ONE BEHIND” and to “endeavour to reach the furthest behind first”.- DCA\NCA with its partners facilitate access to WASH and GBV services for persons with reduced mobility (e.g., persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) via making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. Partners facilitate access to GBV services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities via providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations. Furthermore, to manage security risks and access, DCA/NCA employs a security focal point who monitors the security situation daily, exchanges information with other international organisations and the UN and updates staff. Standard security procedures are in place, which all staff are aware of and follow. Furthermore, DCA/NCA has an extensive network of both humanitarian and development partners, as well as actively participating in the humanitarian cluster system, which facilitates information sharing regarding recent security incidents and forecasting of events. DCA/NCA is connected and follow UNDSS security updates, which is shared with its local partners, who also follow their local networks for security updates. Additionally, daily assessment for the security situation on the ground in coordination with relevant organisations, partner CBO’s and local committees in the area will take place to ensure the safety and security of staff. . DCA/NCA will involve its community groups/Community engagement committees in the project implementation and monitoring. This aims to build community confidence and networks and ensure proper access to affected people, efficient participation of the community in the implementation and monitoring processes and to inform decision making.
