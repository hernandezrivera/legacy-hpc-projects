Project Summary

Since the eruption of armed violence mid-April, food assistance was highlighted
as a priority need in particular in states that have received significant IDPs and other
affected populations that have settled in informal sites and in camps in the Rapid Needs Assessment conducted by ACTED staff in April and May 2023. This aligns with the findings of assessments by other humanitarian actors in Sudan. This includes urban displaced populations and IDP hosting households. The present project aims at scaling up Acted's pre-conflict, multisectoral operations to respond to the new emergency food assissance related needs of conflict-affected populations including IDPs, refugees, and local host communities in White Nile, Blue Nile, Al Jazirah,
Sennar, Gedaref and North Kordofan.

1 - Needs

Acted's RNA indicate that affected people reaching Gedaref and Al Jazirah are increasingly moving towards urban areas incl. collective sites and informal settlements, while in White Nile and Blue Nile they tend to move towards rural or existing larger camp settings (raising the issue of overcrowding and insufficient capacity for basic services in existing camps). The RNA also indicates that (at the time of surveying) the government had set up 3 collective centes in Gedaref and 5 in Al Jazirah (each hosting a few hundreds individual but lacking service provision). Only Sudanese nationals were permitted to stay in formal sites, with non-Sudanese facing secondary displacement in camps in Kassala, White Nile and Gedaref. Food assistance was highlighted as a priority need in particular in Blue Nile and White Nile states, for instance as the former has seen a large influx of returnees from South Sudan, overcrowding the five existing returnee camps in Ulu. Due to the ongoing conflict, prices of household commodities and food have fluctuated greatly due to the disruption in the local supply chain and the increased demand created by the arrival of displaced persons in IDP and refugee hosting areas. As many of the displaced have had their livelihoods disrupted by the conflict, the RNA found a critical need for emergency food assistance, particularly in the initial stages of displacement. It should be highlighted that the needs of the conflict-affected populations are multisectoral in nature - reason why the present food assistance project is to be implemented jointly with emergency interventions in the S-NFI, WASH and protection sectors. In addition, special consideration must be given to ensuring persons with special needs, as well as populations with vulnerabilities predating the current crisis, are covered.

2 - Activities/Outputs

During the project, ACTED will provide emergency food assistance to displaced households in settlements and urban areas. ACTED is currently planning to begin the initial phase of response using an in-kind modality due to the disruptions in the banking system before exploring cash modalities once and if the banking system recovers from the initial disruptions in service. The initial distributions will target 3,960 households across the intervention areas, providing them with one month's food ration. These distributions will be followed by a post distribution monitoring exercise conducted between 2 and 4 weeks after the distribution.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ACTED will seek to use local suppliers where feasible to mitigate the impact of relying on international suppliers for food assistance. However, given the current challenges facing the Sudanese banking system and local suppliers, it may be necessary to use international suppliers in order to meet the emergency lifesaving needs of the local communities. In the event that international suppliers are used, ACTED will prioritize regional suppliers to mitigate the environmental impact of shipping. When developing and implementing the project, ACTED will consider the following environmental mitigation measures:- Encouraging local procurement to limit the environmental impacts associated with the supply chain. Engaging suppliers or manufacturers with sustainable or environmental certifications would, for example, help to reduce the environmental footprint of operations.- Make a strategic selection of items for distribution, to reduce resource consumption and waste production.Finally, as part of on-site monitoring activities, the MEAL unit will also pay particular attention to the environmental impacts of ACTED's activities under this project. Indeed, in the framework of all its projects (including emergency responses), Acted measures its carbon footprint and defines measures to reduce it.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach.Preparation Phase:- Before the intervention, ACTED will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan.- The whole community will be sensitised on the project’s scope and activities and informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (e.g. single women, PLWs, children, the elderly, PwDs).Program Implementation Phase:- ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during community sensitization and trainings.- waiting time and safety during traveling will be addressed through adhoc protection measures.- First aid kits and protective wear are available on distribution sites.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, Acted will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly through its CRM. Acted has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment:- Acted prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by Acted already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies.Administration and employee cycle:- Signature of Code of Conduct for staff;- Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected- Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination.- Training program related to safeguarding issues.- Acted has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. ii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. Acted Complaints and response mechanisms.- Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures -Acted has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (Acted SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; Acted Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). Acted must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. Acted also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (Acted SEA policy, article 23). Acted has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with Acted's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
