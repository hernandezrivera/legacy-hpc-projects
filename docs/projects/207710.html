Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen water, sanitation and hygiene response to affected population in Far North region of Cameroon with a focus on IDP camp, communities hosting IDP and returnees. In water domain, rehabilitation of boreholes and construction of small water supply network powered with solar energy will be prioritized. On hygiene and sanitation issues, communities approached for total sanitation will be implemented at municipality level. Construction of community latrines and showers will be limited to IDP sites. Hygiene promotion with distribution of minimum WASH kits will be implemented in target communities. The area targeted cover four divisions of Far North region: Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Danay and Logone & Chari. With these interventions, this project targets 60,000 people for access to safe water, 50,000 people for access to adequate basic sanitation and 150,000 people reached with critical WASH supply (including hygiene items) and services. This project will also include a component relative to capacity building of WASH actor at regional level on key issues such as design, installation and maintenance of solar system for water supply, cholera response, flood response, protection and gender mainstreaming in WASH sector.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In Far North region, about 0.677 million of people need humanitarian assistance. This situation is a result of low investment in the water and sanitation sector. The WASH situation in Far Nord regions is exacerbated by the numerous humanitarian crises affecting the region: internal and cross-border population displacements (Nigeria to Cameroon), inter-community conflicts, floods, and cholera. Internally displaced persons living in the main IDP sites of Mayo Sava (Kolofata, Mémé, Double and Dougdje, Magdeme, Meme), Mayo Tsanaga (Zamai, Ouro Tada, Zamalva, Tourou, Moskota) and Logone &Chari (Afade, Abankouri, Blangoua Bache and Mafoufou) do not have 15l / person / day and are more than 20 people per latrine. Investments made since 2018 did not significantly improve this situation at standard level due to the continuous increase in people in the different IDP sites and insufficient investment in this sector.The access to safe drinking water is estimated at 40 per cent (RDWE, 2022) with differences among the six divisions, and 12,8% of population practice open defecation (EDS 2018). According to MSNA assessment (OCHA, Sept 2022), 34 per cent of population in Far North still collect drinking water directly from stream and rivers. Also, for 35 per cent of assessed villages, insufficient of improved water point and non-permanent functionality of existing water point remain the main challenges. For 5 per cent of assessed localities, access to improved water point remain a challenge for people with disabilities. In addition, in 31 per cent of assessed villages, the distance to water point is too long and 1 per cent of them report gender-based violence as one of the main difficulties faced. Concerning water quality, 66 per cent of assessed localities have reported an issue. For sanitation even if 81 per cent report using latrine to ease their self, 58 per cent of the same population practice open defecation simultaneously. This is due to insufficient of sanitation facilities (68 per cent), lack of privacy and poor security of sanitation facilities (34 per cent) and 4 per cent of them report gender-based violence. Therefore, there is an urgent need not only to increase access to sanitation facilities but also improve the quality of sanitation services offered to affected population in Far North region. To this situation is added the risk of spread in the region of the cholera epidemic, In 2022, 523 cases have been notified (with 18 deaths) in the departments of Mayo-Tsanaga (Mokolo), Mayo Sava (Mora, Kolofata ) and Logone and chari (Fotokol, Mada, Makari) and Diamare (Maroua 3).
