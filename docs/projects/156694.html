Project Summary

DRC will provide non-formal educational support to 34,000 individual children along with support to rehabilitate schools and capacity-build educational personnel, increasing the quality of educational provision in Syria, reducing school drop-outs and providing pathways for out-of-school children to re-enroll in formal education, and creating protective and accessible learning environments for children.

Needs

Protracted crisis in Syria has had profound impacts on the education of children, leaving over 5.8 million school-aged children in need of education assistance inside Syria, more than 1.8 million of whom are out of school entirely. Educational infrastructure, supplies, and the capacities of existing educational personnel are severely overstretched, with one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes, and classrooms in the most affected areas overcrowded with up to 50 children. Schools lacking doors, windows, electrical systems and safe, gender-sensitive WASH facilities are in urgent need of repair to protect children and encourage enrollment of out of school children, especially girls whose access to education may be blocked by lack of safe WASH facilities. Stressful learning environments compound behavioral challenges amongst children, including aggressive behavior which was among the top three challenges reported to DRC by school principals. Using the Joint Education Needs Assessment Toolkit, DRC field staff conducted a joint education and shelter assessment of more than 40 schools across 5 governorates (Rural Damascus, Dar’a, Homs, Hama, and Aleppo). In parallel, DRC engineers conducted technical assessments of the schools covering water, sanitation, and electrical connections maintenance and needs for rehabilitations. While results are being analyzed, preliminary findings indicate profound educational needs in heavily conflict-affected areas. For example, in East Ghouta 11 schools assessed had average classroom occupancies of 27 to 65, with schools running double shifts simultaneously coping with larger classroom sizes. DRC staff counted only an average of just one staff toilet and five student toilets per school. Across all areas assessed, DRC identified severe damage to school infrastructure, barriers to education, and child protection risks including early marriage and common incidence of child labor. Sources: HNO 2018 and HNO data 2019; DRC social data, 2018; DRC focus group discussions in coordination with the Ministry of Education (MoE), DRC key informant interviews with school principals, 2018; DRC school needs assessments, 2018.

Implementation of activities

DRC has field staff present in all areas of operation who, led by and in coordination with project management and technical staff, conduct needs assessments to assess and select schools in line with DRC’s targeting criteria; liaise with educational personnel and community figures to spread awareness of DRC activities int eh community and assist with registration of children; monitor activities on a weekly, if not daily basis, and jointly conduct distributions of supplies as well as supervising pre- and post- tests. Implementation of non-formal education is done directly by teachers registered with the MoE in public schools; DRC will also pilot non-formal education programs in a small number (one or two) satellite centres. For school rehabilitations, engineers will conduct technical assessments and supervise works delivered by private contractors. Wherever possible, local contractors will be preferred, multiplying benefits to local economies. Hand-over of the repaired infrastructure will be made to MoE after two months of works have been completed; a warranty period of two months will be put in place to ensure full completion of works in accordance with MoE guidelines and WASH sector minimum standards (for the WASH facilities in schools). DRC’s strategy is to integrate child protection programming into non-formal education. This means that this project is closely interlinked with DRCs Protection/Child Protection project, with the majority of beneficiaries of the self-learning program also receiving child protection interventions from DRC (structured and sustained PSS, sensitization sessions, community-based initiatives and prevention and response plans formulated and delivered by children).

Protection Risk Analysis

PR1: Host community rejects DRC’s presence in schools where remedial education is conducted (due to feeling stigmatized/mistreated; disagreement with activities implemented, lack of understanding of selection criteria for remedial classes, or other factors). Risk: low; Impact: high. Mitigation: regular discussion with parents, teachers, social counsellors; clearly communicated and transparent selection criteria for non-formal education programs; close coordination and engagement with local educational authorities. PR2: Children drop out of school to attend self learning program. Risk: medium; Impact high. Mitigation: ensure that the purpose and nature of the activity is clearly communicated, so that it is not understood as the equivalent of formal education; only target those children who have already dropped out of school. PR3: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measure: planning and on going communication with local education authorities, community leaders, schools, etc; comprehensive school rehabilitation including installation of handrails, rehabilitation of wash facilities, fixing of electrical systems, and other interventions to ensure the health and safety of children and teachers. PR4: Inter or intra community tension due to service delivery locations (schools) causing perceptions of discrimination or favoritism. Risk: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: selection of new service delivery points done according to transparent selection criteria, targeting the most vulnerable and selection criteria clearly communicated to the community. Additionally, ensuring adequate service mapping, needs assessments feeds into selection of new locations, and ensuring coordination with local authorities and local humanitarian actors, as well as humanitarian leadership on the Damascus level, has been conducted before entering into new locations. PR5: Population targeted by the intervention is put at risk due to location of schools; including lack of accessibility, lack of consideration to local/contextual factors. Risk: medium; Impact: high. Mitigation measures: location chosen according to transparent selection criteria, targeting most vulnerable, and ensuring adequate service mapping, needs assessment, and coordination with local authorities and local humanitarian actors, as well as humanitarian leadership, has been conducted before entering into new locations. Continuous situation monitoring by DRC Safety unit, standard SOPs in place and applied in case of security incidents, closure or reduction of opening hours in centres if necessary. PR6: Exploitation/abuse by staff or other service providers. Risk: medium; Impact: high. Mitigation measures: clear and regular communication with beneficiaries, including reinforcing the message that humanitarian services are free of charge; regular consultation with beneficiaries through group discussions; operation of complaint mechanism, including regular advertisement.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Should any of the planned areas of intervention become inaccessible due to safety concerns, DRC would consider alternate locations in line with its standard targeting criteria and procedures. 2. Should exchange rate fluctuations between donor currencies and the Syrian pound mean that DRC had less actual funds available in Syria; and/or, should this project not be fully funded, DRC may be unable to deliver all activities as planned. DRC would prioritize those communities in most urgent need of Protection assistance according to verified data from the field. 3. Should all necessary approvals not be received from all concerned stakeholders and authorities (including especially MoE) for new activities such as establishment of satellite centres, DRC would as a last resort return the related funds to donors.
