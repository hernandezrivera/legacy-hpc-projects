Project Summary

Due to massive damaged of the public WASH facilities and the infrastructure, over 4 million People are suffering the lack of pure water access that kept people out of access to sufficient water resources which are basic human need. Although WASH services and hygiene supplies are mostly available, the major barrier to access them is severely reduced purchasing power of families, especially for displaced households, self-organized returnees, and in particular for female-headed household within those groups. Simultaneously preparedness actions and contingency planning must be kept up to date and at sufficient level to enable timely response to emerging needs, especially in light of anticipated rapid changes in the level of access and mass displacements. GOPA is going to provide a water tanks for the most vulnerable families in Homs, Der Azzor, Dara, Aleppo and Rural Damascus. Distribution the water tanks will ensure that families are getting the needed amount of pure water without having the burden of purchasing or walking for long distance to reach the water sources.

Needs

Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted in mid-2018 to inform the 2019 HNO, including an Individual household level survey at sub-district level (~24,600 samples from 265 sub-districts across Syria) and a household survey focused on IDP camps and informal settlements in northwest and northeast Syria (~1,950 households from 156 camps and 100 collective centers). Based on WASH assessments there are an estimated 15.6 million people who require WASH assistance (people who are at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement), 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need (displaced people in camps, informal settlements and host communities). IDPs living in IDP sites are amongst the most vulnerable groups in Syria. the scale of displacements to IDP sites witnessed since the beginning of 2018, has contributed to a deterioration of already overused WASH facilities and increased the need to scale-up operations and enhance the focus around more sustainable solutions (connection to existing water networks and/or establishment of simplified water and sewer networks). The very high levels of displacement combined with increase in the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites has presented significant challenges to WASH sector partners, in view of the increased cost and complexity to deliver in IDP site settings than in host communities. Sanitation needs are also considerably higher among displaced people living in informal settlements and camps compared to the rest of the population, with over-crowding, cleanliness and protection concerns linked with substandard conditions of sanitation facilities.To identify response priorities, the WASH sector considers a number of key factors which generally indicate a high severity of needs. These factors include displacement, with the timely provision of life-saving WASH goods and services essential to address needs of newly displaced people who had to flee from their homes at short notice, leaving behind their belongings. According to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the scale, severity and complexity of needs across Syria entering the seventh year of the crisis, remain overwhelming. Some 12.8 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance, including 6.2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs). Of these, millions people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities and limited access to basic goods and services.

Implementation of activities

GOPA will conduct the necessary assessment and home visits to affected and vulnerable families to ensure that these criteria meet the selection criteria for the activity, which must be IDPs and returnees who do not have access to clean water. Female head of Households, large families, persons with disabilities and the elderly are priority Follow a visit from the technical team to the site to determine the relevant works, which is providing the water tanks and accessories to ensure having a secure Water supply network.The distribution process begins with communication with the beneficiaries to schedule a date and place for the delivery, where they should provide the identification documents. Moreover, the technical team will install the tanks and ensure the quality of work. Followed with Conducting pre and post evaluations to track the activities and its impact. Also regular focus groups with communities will ensure that the program is covering the needs.Taking the number of the beneficiaries into consideration, GOPA team will carry out with the distribution procedure in the targeted areas as follows:• Aleppo: 800 Tanks• Dara’a: 1200 Tanks• Deir Ezzor: 1000 Tanks• Rural Damascus: 400 Tanks• Homs: 250 TanksIn order to counter the negative consequences that the prolonged conflict has on the capacities of the government, private health facilities and especially on proper hygiene, GOPA-DERD will provide hygiene promotion sessions to affected and displaced people in schools, shelters, and public spaces. HP activities will target beneficiaries consist of children, women mainly pregnant women, mothers and housekeepers, health workers, teachers and other. HP will consist of many activities like group sessions, School club, mobile puppetry shows and mass media campaigns on social channels; Hygiene kits distribution, printed materials (booklets, posters and brochures) will be distributed on the activities.

Protection Risk Analysis

Assessment results in some of the IDP sites in northern Syria confirmed that protection issues including the lack of door locks and adequate lighting, lack of gender-segregated facilities, long distances to the facilities as well as the lack of privacy and the fear of harassment on the way to WASH facilities are considerable concerns for women and girls. Facilities adapted for children and for persons with disabilities are often lacking, therefore those groups are most likely to be affected by lack of safety and this gap needs to be addressed. In addition, IDP and returnee female-headed households are more vulnerable in terms of access/affordability of WASH items, including sanitary napkins and other hygiene items traditionally provided by WASH actors, and services.GOPA as a member of WASH & Protection sectors will ensure the “Do No Harm” principle by carrying out a Protection Risk Analysis as part of its strategy development. As an example, support to water supply systems present the risk of unequal distribution of services. To minimize such risks, GOPA will establish strong links with water boards and operators for joint planning and include communities at the early stages of project implementation.In some urban and crowded shelters, humanitarian and beneficiaries may face a GBV abuse and Sexual exploitation, GOPA will make sure that its staff is well trained on SGBV basics. Moreover, GOPA had developed a misconduct internal policy to be fit with the humanitarian works in all sectors and locations.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security situation to be in tension in some places of Syria might affect the implementation of the planned activities(Low risk). Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: GOPA will consult with sector about the shifting of planned activities to other provinces. Level of risk: Acceptable.Pending approvals from Ministry of Water Recourses according to location may delay the implementation of the program, GOPA will make sure to have additional assessments for beneficiaries and targeted locations to re-allocate the activity when face such risk in coordination with WASH sector.
