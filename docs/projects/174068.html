Project Summary

Building on Goalprime organization Nigeria’s expertise in designing and supporting the delivery of art based psychosocial support programs, this partnership will address the following needs:
I. Improving the quality of the current psychosocial support program being implemented by the Borno State MWASD through the design and implementation of a training mentorship program for community volunteers
II. Integration of psychosocial support services in primary schools, and designing and implementation of adolescent tailored activities mainly using the IDEALs and Adolescent kit for expression and innovation in emergencies toolkit.
III. Identifying out of school children and linking them with schools for enrolment
IV. Training of teachers, headteachers, SBMC's and CBCPC on Child protection and assigned CP focal roles including GBV/PSEA; how to identify, report, and seek help.
Provide mentorship program for community volunteers
Conducting psychosocial support, recreational and non-formal education activities requires a diverse skillset spanning across the fields of child development, social work, physical and health educational, and education (teaching). While community volunteers currently receive training to introduce them to the basics of facilitating psychosocial support to children through the Child-Friendly Space (CFS) model, community engagement, and child protection, the current training does not provide direct coaching and mentoring to volunteers to enable them to properly organize and conduct activities with children. Feedback from monitoring visits shows that volunteers often lack the skills to create, organize, and lead structured activities that facilitate learning and skill development in children. In brief, while children are playing at Child-Friendly Spaces, they are missing a prime opportunity to acquire valuable life skills that will reduce psychosocial distress, increase their resilience, and prepare them for success in formal education and beyond.
In order to build the capacity of community volunteers so that they can run creative, recreational, and non-formal education activities within a psychosocial support framework that impacts and improves the lives of children, Goalprime Nigeria will develop a hands-on mentorship program for community volunteers which will be rolled out in collaboration with the Borno State MWASD. The mentorship programme will be hinged on creative art and life skills interventions for providing psychosocial support to children and include skills and methodologies for creating and maintaining structure when working with children in CFS, facilitating learning and acquisition of skills, as well as upholding rules and redirecting negative behavior.
The mentorship program will be designed and delivered in three parts:
I. The first part will focus on introducing the community volunteers to the art and life skills-based psychosocial interventions and related resources that Goalprime Nigeria will develop for use by the community volunteers.
II. The second part will focus on deploying mentors to directly work with children in selected CFS as community volunteers observe the work of the mentors.
III. The third part will focus on having the community volunteers take on the work with children, as mentors observe, and based on their observations, hold feedback and mentorship sessions to address gaps identified.
Integration of psychosocial support services in schools and design and implementation of adolescent tailored activities
Currently, psychosocial support services for children in Borno are mainly being provided through CFS. Although this is reaching a substantial number of children, the integration of psychosocial support services within education provides a great opportunity to reach more children.
GPON will strive to use a combination of house-structures within schools to build the capacity of housemasters/mistresses and prefects to organize PSS.

1 - Needs

Emergencies such as we have in Northeast Nigeria has severe consequences on children and their families as well as affect their ability to build back better. According to Nigeria’s HRP Needs Overview on https://reliefweb.int/report/nigeria/nigeria-2019-2021-humanitarian-response-strategy-january2019-december-2021’ In 2019, 7.1 million people (2.3 million girls, 1.9 million boys, 1.6 million women, and 1.3 million men) are in need of humanitarian assistance in north-east Nigeria as a result of a crisis that is now in its tenth year. The projections of the Child Protection Subsector in the 2020 Nigeria HRP estimates that 2.7 million young children and adolescents (1.5 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the BAY States are in need of protection and cross-sectoral services. Threats and risks to the well-being of girls and boys include family separation, explosive remnants of war, gender-based violence including child marriage and conflict-related sexual violence, recruitment and use of children, abduction, and other grave child rights violations. At least 6,000 children (more than 50 percent of girls) are estimated to have been violated by the Non-State Armed Groups. The crisis evidently has caused severe psychological distress amongst over 2 million young children and adolescents; Borno State being the epicenter. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but has also negatively affected the ability of parents and communities to appropriately care for their children. At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children have suffered from severe psychosocial distress. The social relations disruption, the anxiety and fears that accompany the event before, during, and after, and uncertainty for the future have rendered young children, adolescents, and their families helpless thus preventing them from taking care of themselves and their environment. As a result, they are evidently losing hope for the future hence limiting their active participation in the realization of national priorities. If the situation is left unaddressed, and this proposed program is not implemented timely, the psychosocial effects of the conflict may lead to continuous suffering, indifference, depression, frustration, a negative coping mechanism, violent behavior, and other antisocial dispositions, thereby increasing social tension, prolonging the recovery process and weakening the resilience of children in the long-term. In response to this situation, GPON has been working with UNICEF and the Borno State Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MWASD) to deliver critical child protection in emergency services including case management services to unaccompanied and separated children and other children with protection concerns, support for socio-economic reintegration of children associated with fighting forces and armed groups, and basic psychosocial support services to children. The Ministry has a huge network of trained community volunteers through whom it works to deliver basic psychosocial support services, but program quality has remained a gap. And is missing the opportunity to reach more children through integration with other sectors, mainly education and nutrition. The partnership with GPON will focus on training of CV’s currently engaged to deliver MHPSS services for Children, as part of the partnership no new CV’s will be trained. The proposed action is to improve the quality of services as the capacity of CVs is low The PSS assessment recommends that there is a need for tailored training are conducted especially for CV's on remote programming and other skills. The current CV's capacity will be strengthened to improve the quality of services as the capacity of CVs is low. Goalprime organization of Nigeria is an organization dedicated to using interactive arts for psychosocial support, community interaction, enhancement, development, and capacity building GPON approach to the provision of PSS.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime’s accountability to the affected populations spans through the Program Cycle from the Rapid Specific Vulnerability Assessment through to design, through to implementation, and through to monitoring. The proposed project prioritizes constant creating of opportunities for review and feedback from beneficiaries, community leaders, partners present in location and donors. This integration of feedback and input from communities closely aligns with both the SPHERE Standards; Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Minimum Standard, and guidelines set forth by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). The pre-design assessment on the feasibility of this project in target locations collected information from local beneficiary populations, IDPs camp coordinators, host community Bulama and a review of what other partners are doing in target LGAs via the WASH sector ReportHub partners update from target LGAs. As a result, the proposed project uses direct contributions in its proposed structure, scope, and proposed activities to allow a more community informed implementation process. Throughout the implementation of this project, GOALPrime shall engage participatory Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) and establish a platform which will allow participation and feedback from the community on project goals. GOALPrime shall conduct regular feedback surveys among the beneficiaries to gauge levels of satisfaction and solicit suggestions for project implementation and improvement.
