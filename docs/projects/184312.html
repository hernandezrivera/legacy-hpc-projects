Project Summary

Severe food insecurity continues to worsen owing to current depleted livelihoods and diminishing coping mechanisms that has significantly impacted into the lives of the most vulnerable host communities and marginalized IDPs and returnees including the elderly and persons with living with disabilities in Galgadud, Hiraan, Bay, Middle Shabelle and Lower Shabelle Regions, Somalia. The situation is attributed to combined effects of poor and erratic Gu 2021 rainfall against the background of previous successive erratic and below average Gu and Deyr 2020 rains compounded by previous successive drought seasons as well as floods along riverine areas of Hiiraan, Middle Shabelle regions hence below-average Gu crop production. The situation is further exacerbated by impacts of desert locust that continue to pose a serious threat to both crop availability and crop production. The project will be implemented through the following activities: Provision of food vouchers for most vulnerable and displaced women and children headed households and those with chronic illnesses and the disabled, rehabilitation of water catchments, farming lands, irrigation canals and feeder roads on CFW scheme, Provision of hay/fodder to the most vulnerable and drought affected, pastoral families, distribution of farm inputs (seed package including seeds and fertilizers) targeted to farming households as well as provision of tractor hours to the targeted farming households.
According to FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Report of September 9, 2021, approximately 3.5 million people in Somalia face acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in late 2021 whereas reports of current weather forecasts indicate an increased likelihood of below-average rainfall during the 2021 Deyr (October-December) season across most parts of the country which will seriously affect food security and nutrition outcomes thus food insecurity is expected to deteriorate further to 2022. The project targets 47400 persons (19714 women,18202 men,4740 boys and 4740 girls) and the elderly as well as those with disabilities whose plight is exacerbated by eroded purchasing power and very weak and negative coping strategies.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. The project will ensure inclusion of minority groups such as minority clans, IDPs, women, elderly, children headed households and persons with disabilities in comprehensive consultation on projects needs identification and prioritization where their concerns will be put on consideration to form key decisions of the project. This will extend to all stages of project implementation including regular monitoring where they will where their concerns will be part of recommendations and suggestions made during the project implementation hence addressing their needs alongside those of other project beneficiaries.2. The project will strengthen protection of women and girls from sexual violence by ensuring their safety during implementation of the project activities. This will be realized by ensuring that distribution of assistance takes place during the day at appropriate times to limit them from walking under the cover of darkness where most Sexual violence do occur. Furthermore, project activities will be done at central locations to limit women and girls from covering long distances away from their homes and settlements and further limit exposing them to incidences of sexual violence. 3. The project will be implemented under strict adhering to PSEA policy by conducting comprehensive social mobilization and sensitization to ensure that women and girls are fully aware of the project activities and what to expect from the project in order to mitigate any form of sexual exploitation and abuse by the project staff and authority as well as security apparatus during the project. Moreover, the project will ensure that complaints and feedback structures are in place and easily accessible by the targeted beneficiaries and the community at large such as suggestion boxes and toll free hotlines. 4. The project will further ensure that any form of violence between the targeted beneficiaries and the community does not occur by adhering to the principle of Do No Harm during the implementation process.
