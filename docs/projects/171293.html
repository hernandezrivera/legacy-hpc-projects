Project Summary

To respond to increased food insecurity due to unemployment and loss of livelihood as a result of COVID-19 and related movement restrictions measures, UNRWA will distribute food baskets and inclusive hygiene kits to 18,000 vulnerable Palestine refugee households unable to meet their basic needs. UNRWA's distribution is following strict safety and security measures responding to the changing environment around COVID-19 emergency, while guaranteeing a fair, equal and consistent support to food-insecure Palestine refugees in the West Bank.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 emergency is as much a public health crisis as it has come to be an economic crisis affecting national economies with impact on individual households, especially the most vulnerable. Regulations implemented by the Palestinian and Israeli authorities to contain the COVID-19 outbreak have progressively limited movement into and within the West Bank, with areas being closed off from each other. Public health measures to control transmission of the virus through restriction of movement and extended periods of home quarantine of entire populations are having an impact on those in particular that were already economically vulnerable prior to the crisis. These often are populations that have relied on daily or informal labour or that have been income constrained altogether because of chronic illness, age, disability and gender restrictions to employment opportunities. According to the World Bank’s preliminary estimates on the negative impact of the COVID 19 pandemic economic decline, it is expected that the share of poor households in the West Bank will increase from 14 to 30 percent. Unemployment is expected to disproportionally affect women according to a recent study by the Ministry of Social Development. Vulnerable households often do not have the required resources to purchase basic requirements for survival for longer time periods as they rely on constant flows of casual labour income or various sources of assistance over time. The near cessation of economic activity is reflected on the rates of unemployment, poverty and food insecurity which is expected to rise over time. The risk of marginalization and exacerbation of vulnerability are expected to increase among the already most marginalized and poor refugee population. Furthermore, due to the deterioration in socioeconomic conditions of refugee families, the pandemic has posed additional challenges in terms of sanitation and personal hygiene. The reduced purchase power of households has undermined their ability to access hygiene items, which are critical in preventing the transmission of COVID-19. In particular, the needs of women and elderly must be taken into consideration in the selection of items for the hygiene kits, as they become one of the most vulnerable groups in times of conflicts and displacement. Menstruation does not stop in displacement situations and the need for minimum hygiene standards become paramount. Sometimes women look for alternatives in the absence of hygiene and sanitary items which would threaten their health. Further, the lack of sanitary napkins and other hygiene material can interfere with women’s ability to access needed resources or services and can keep girls home from school. Women and girls may become increasingly isolated due to their lack of mobility. The provision of menstrual hygiene supplies and provisions has also been identified as an essential part of the COVID-19 response in guidance provided by the Interagency Standing Committee (March 2020).

2 - Activities

UNRWA, as the primary humanitarian organization providing services to Palestine refugees, aims to mitigate the food insecurity among the most vulnerable as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak. This is addressed through the provision of in-kind food assistance reaching up to 18,000 vulnerable households. In addition, UNRWA will support vulnerable households in home quarantine with the provision of inclusive hygiene kits, designed and distributed according to hygiene promotion working group and WASH cluster guidance and requirements. The kits contain hygiene and sanitary items explicitly tailored to be inclusive of women, girls, infants, and the elderly’s needs such as sanitary napkins, hypoallergenic sanitary soap, infant pampers and incontinence pads. These items are intended to help maintain dignity, health, mobility, and security. This project provides a critical opportunity to support and sustain households facing acute economic shock. It aims to stop the most affected segment of the refugee population from using negative coping mechanisms to deal with reduced livelihood opportunities and the deterioration of their purchasing power. The project will serve the most vulnerable refugee households in the West Bank, both inside and outside camps including 1. refugee families under obligatory quarantine due to infection or direct contact, 2. poor families affected by the COVID-19 crisis that live remotely and do not have adequate access to banks and local markets, 3. vulnerable families with increasing needs due to the COVID-19 ( for example, female-headed households, families with PwDs, families with chronic diseases). Assistance will be delivered through the distribution of food baskets and hygiene kits to vulnerable households in quarantine and/or in a situation of full lockdown. Food baskets include locally produced items such as flour, rice, beans and milk. Beneficiaries will be selected from refugees identified previously by UNRWA’s Social Safety Net Programme as poor, and those recommended and referred by UNRWA’s Social Workers, Camp Services Officers, local committees, and hotlines. The project will, in general, prioritize the most vulnerable categories such as female-headed households, elderly households, families with members having chronic diseases, families with multi-disability members, families who have lost their source of income due to COVID-19, and families who have reached out to UNRWA psychosocial first aid hotline to request for assistance. Amidst the COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA increased the number of distribution points to avoid crowdedness at the distribution centers. In case of further restriction of movement due to the COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA is ready to adjust its food distribution modality ensuring the protection of public health and safety of the population in the West Bank. In 2021, UNRWA will continue to distribute using a safe modality with enhanced measures according to COVID-19 health recommendations, this will include controlled management of crowds, adjusted spaces to ensure physical distances and shading in open areas, strict use of PPEs, and enhanced hygiene and cleanliness measures. Furthermore, assistance will continue to be provided to refugees in isolation centres.

3 - Indicators

18,000 vulnerable refugee families receive food baskets and hygiene kits to improve quarantine capacity of refugee households and to minimize the spread of COVID-19. UNRWA will regularly monitor the quality of the food distribution, ensuring the continued feedback from the community through focus group sessions and regular communication with beneficiaries. Currently, UNRWA is working on a post-distribution monitoring system for the COVID-19 response that will track the impact of the intervention on the households, considering the vulnerabilities within the families. Monitoring activities will enable the gathering and reporting of detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneﬁciaries and to measure the project outcomes of different target groups. Accountability to beneﬁciaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism, including through UNRWA hotline.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

UNRWA Relief Social Services Programme (RSSP) regularly conducts focus groups to discuss key items such as the quality of the food assistance, the quantity of the food basket and the process of food distribution. UNRWA-RSSP has a complaint system in place to receive beneficiaries’ feedback. An appeal mechanism as well as a committee feedback system are also in place for the families who were confirmed “not-eligible” from the intervention based on the results of the criteria. Anyone can access to RSSP area offices across the West Bank. UNRWA RSSP replies to the families regarding their status based on their complaint or appeal. UNRWA’s Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) unit is also involved through regularly conducting interviews with beneficiaries across all distribution centres as well as other distribution points, beneficiaries’ homes, or via phone calls, to collect their feedback regarding the quality of the received commodities, the behavior of the staff in charge of distributing the food and any other issues related to their satisfaction.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. Distribution centres and social services are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do no harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. During the food distribution cycle, UNRWA staff based at the food distribution centers including social workers, ensure that the targeted beneficiaries receive their food assistance with dignity and respect. In addition, regular monitoring work is conducted to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on key items of the programme e.g. the quality of the food distribution process, the content of the food basket, the overall quality of the food distribution or the behavior of the staff involved in the food distribution. These feedback helps to continuously improve the intervention.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all Palestinian refugees living under the poverty line with food assistance, without any discrimination. Information to beneficiaries, eligibility criteria and other services are available to all refugees where application and claim can be made to Relief and Social Services Programme offices, often located in the same area of the distribution centres. Location of distribution centres are following safety measures, especially for women and girls, recognizing that catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. The distribution takes into consideration specific vulnerability concerns and adopted specific SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize assistance to vulnerable groups (pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow specific concerns and potential protection cases. Furthermore, distribution centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Staff in the distribution centres, whom is regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection and gender mainstreaming, are able to refer specific protection cases to social services for follow up. A crowd control system is in place and a specific calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance, especially amidst COVID-19 emergency. The unified eligibility criteria system enables vulnerable groups such as widow or divorced women as well as people with disabilities or anyone not able to go to the distribution center to be provided with specific assistance in order to receive their food basket. In order to ease the process for targeted persons with disabilities, there is a delegation system in place that enables those people to delegate one of their family members to receive the food assistance on their behalf. The delegation is set during the home visits of the social workers and following their reports’ recommendations. UNRWA will use home delivery modality for those who are under home quarantine and home isolation to make sure that they will continue to receive food assistance.
