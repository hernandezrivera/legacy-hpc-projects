Project Summary

The political, economic and humanitarian crisis in Syria continue to exert significant pressure on the Palestine refugees remaining in the country with high poverty levels. The results of a PDM survey conducted by UNRWA in October 2023 show that 92 % of households had to borrow food or rely on help from friends and 64% reduced the number and quantity of meals per day to cope with being unable to purchase enough food In addition, 40 % of assessed households showed to resort to emergency livelihoods coping strategies characterized by begging (4 %) and selling productive assets (such as sewing machines, wheelbarrows and bicycles) that are difficult to recover (36 %), indicative of refugee households’ diminishing ability to cope with the increasing poverty.
This PDM has also assessesd the household perceptions on ability to meet basic: About 45 % of assessed households reported that they are able to meet at least half of their basic needs, a slight reduction from 48 % at the same time last year. When disagregating against broader household vulnerability categories, 46 % of the most vulnerable households reported ability to meet at least half of their basic needs compared to 43 % in the other vulnerable category. About 7 % reported being unable to meet any of their basic needs, about the same percentage for both vulnerability categories. Lastly, 48 % of households reported being able to meet a few of their basic needs, 47 %i n the most vulnerable category and 51 % in the other vulnerable category. The main reason for inability to access basic services was lack of money to buy basic services, reported by 99 % of households while 1 % reported lack of basic items in the market. This finding generally reflect the worsening economic situation in the country marked by persistent increase in prices of basic goods and services.
UNRWA plans to provide up to 420,000 Palestine refugees with MPC to meet their basic needs. The Agency will provide this assistance based on a targeted approach introduced in 2019, prioritizing refugees assessed as most vulnerable with a higher cash transfer.
In 2024, it is expected that around 155,500 Palestine refugees will be assessed as most vulnerable based on the four vulnerability categories used by UNRWA (female headed households, families with persons with disabilities, families headed by an older person and unaccompanied minors/orphans). The remaining 264,500 vulnerable Palestine refugees will receive a lower cash transfer value as explained below.
The transfer values were based on discussions with the CWG and aligned with CWG recommendations. The cash transfer values were set to:
• SYP 2,194,209 / month for a family of 5 members belonging to the most vulnerable categories. This transfer value takes into consideration the reduction of the in-kind food assistance to be provided to the most vulnerable beneficiaries which is $ 7.133 /month / individual.
• SYP 1,716,000 /month for a family of 5 members belonging to the remaining caseload of vulnerable Palestine refugees who are not eligible to the in-kind food assistance. UNRWA distributes cash through BBSF Bank and Al Haram Company in addition to its own cash distribution centre in Damascus.
Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures:
Assumptions:
1 Humanitarian needs of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable
2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially
3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further.
Risks linked to CVA implementation e.g. further inflation, liquidity issues, economic deterioration, impact on the market, etc.
Mitigation: UNRWA is engaged very closely with the UN Country Team in its negotiations with the Central Bank of Syria CBS to address the spread between the informal and formal exchange rate and the liquidity risk is discussed with CBS.

Protection and SEA Risk Analysis:Protection Risk: Beneﬁciaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from distribution points or when withdrawing their cash assistance from the FSP outlets. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA contracts a big network of FSP outlets to ensure the proximity of cash distribution to the beneficiaries. 2. Distribution dates and times are staggered over a period to reduce exposure. Risk: Exclusion of beneficiaries due to lack of official IDs to collect cash from FSP outlets. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: UNRWA Mezzeh office has a cash distribution centre equipped to distribute cash assistance to beneficiaries without the correct IDs to collect cash from the FSP outlets. Risk: Exclusion (selection criteria misunderstood) and some beneficiaries who believe they are eligible are excluded. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: UNRWA has an operational Complaints and Feedback Mechanism and implements remedies.Risk: Unaccompanied children are excluded from cash assistance. Likelihood: Low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: Delegation system in place.Risk : Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion): UNRWA has a CFM to capture GBV risks and staffs have read/are aware of the Code of Conduct . PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN ClearCheck recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations.UNRWA plans to provide MPC to Palestine refugees covering 12 months over 3 rounds in 2024, with each round covering 4 months of MPC. Ideally, the rounds would start on 1 Jan, 1 May, and 1 Sept. In reality, UNRWA might adjust the start date and duration of each round depending on fund availability and taking into account cash flow considerations and donor requirements for the different grants.
