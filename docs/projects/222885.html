Project Summary

The project aims to empower young men and women (ages 18–24) from Rohingya camps and host communities, equipping them with essential skills, assets, and networks needed for transitioning into decent work and adulthood. Focusing on participants who have received formal or non-formal education, the project provides market-driven training to youth in camps and host communities, addressing their unique needs and challenges. The approach emphasizes sustainable employment, offering targeted interventions that foster agency, build resilience, and facilitate dignified livelihoods and self-reliance activities for youth in both refugee and host communities. Social cohesion activities are integrated to mitigate tension through mutual relationships and local structures.
Participation in skill training is aligned with individual interests and trade availability, identified through consultations and assessments. This personalized approach ensures that the training is relevant and effective. Diverse training methods, including outreach centers, a blended model, and referrals to government and private providers, will be utilized. Host community participants seeking formal employment will undergo assessments to determine skill levels and obtain necessary certifications.
A gender-sensitive rapid market assessment was conducted at the project’s onset, analyzing labour market conditions and identifying employment barriers in the host community. This assessment guides sustainable, environmentally friendly livelihood options and informs curriculum development. The curriculum will be developed or updated in coordination with the Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector partners and validated through sectoral partner workshops/meetings for ICT programs in the Rohingya community. Instead of a comprehensive labour market study, consultations and capacity assessments inform skill placements, training packages, and Income-Generating Activity (IGA) suitability for participants.
Training will take place in safe, accessible centers, with facilities designed to be gender- and disability-inclusive. The project, with a strong emphasis on inclusivity, will renovate existing centers and create new ones in partnership with community representatives. Participants will receive input support to build productive assets, including financial literacy training and access to loans for host community members. The AAL package will be introduced for camp participants, promoting their educational and skill-building growth.
To encourage youth, particularly young women, in livelihood pursuits, the project will engage family and community gatekeepers, addressing barriers to refugee self-reliance and employment. Capacity assessments and entry points like the AAL package will support vocational education and social integration. Host community efforts will strengthen resilience through diverse income sources, skill development aligned with market demands, financial safety nets, and market linkages.
The project will continue to be implemented in Camp- 07, 09, 10, 15, and 21, with specific interventions: accelerated adult learning across all five, non-formal technical training in Camp- 07, 09, 10, 15 and 21, and vocational training in each camp. In host communities, technical training, internships, and job linkage as livelihood support will be provided to develop self-sustaining income streams.
The Mobile Training Center (MTC) program has been proven successful. It empowers youth through basic ICT training with a 120-hour curriculum. Participants gain hands-on experience, practical skills, and a professional mindset, enhancing employability. The program balances technical and soft skills, equipping learners with a comprehensive foundation to succeed across various sectors. This intervention will be implemented in Camp 07, 09 and 15.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In the 21st century, Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) is essential for fostering economic growth and sustainable development. The Government of Bangladesh (GOB) has prioritized TVET to build a skilled workforce capable of meeting domestic and international market demands. In its 8th Five-Year Plan (2021–2025), TVET is reinforced as a strategy to achieve Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) by 2030 and to reach developed nation status by 2041.Rohingya refugees, facing limited income opportunities, skills gaps, and restrictive regulations, depend on humanitarian aid, with 95% of households identified as moderately to highly vulnerable. Skills acquisition among the host community is traditional, with limited access to TVET. Gender-based barriers also restrict girls’ and young women’s access to quality education, employment, and entrepreneurship.According to the J-MSNA 2023 report, only 7% of Rohingya households receive skills development or self-reliance support. About a quarter of refugees engage in activities like food-for-assets, cash-for-work, day labor, small businesses, tailoring, or NGO employment. Coping strategies for limited earnings and food insecurity in the host community include reduced food intake, homestead gardening, borrowing, and working outside camps. Approximately 95% of host households receive no food or cash support, and 96% lack agricultural and non-agricultural livelihood assistance. Access to natural resources like land and water is limited, with 62% of households overall reporting lack of access, with Rohingya families particularly affected.Female participation in TVET in Bangladesh remains low, with only 9%–13% enrolled in public institutions and 33% in private institutions affiliated with BTEB. The government’s Vision 2021 for Education, Training, and Skills Development aims for a gender-inclusive, demand-driven TVET sector, guided by the National Strategy for Gender Mainstreaming in TVET and aligned with labor market needs. The National Skills Development Policy (NSDP) 2011 sets ambitious targets to expand TVET, aiming for TVET students to comprise 20% of all secondary students, increase overall enrollment by 50%, and grow women’s enrollment by 60%.A rapid market assessment by Save the Children reveals greater economic engagement within the host community (18%) compared to the Rohingya community (15%), with common jobs across both groups including tailoring, technical work, vegetable gardening, and small businesses. However, literacy, numeracy, and technical skills are generally low, especially among the Rohingya, and skills often mismatch with job market demands, underscoring the gap between training and employability. Vocational training participation is also higher in the host community (15%) than among the Rohingya (9%).In the Rohingya community, demand is high for off-farm skills like handicrafts (81%), tailoring (74%), and electrical repair (69%). Among the host community, key off-farm skills include tailoring (92%), mechanical work (71%), and mobile servicing (60%).Both communities face significant barriers to employment. Most participants reported challenges in accessing vocational training (86%), a lack of job opportunities (88%), and limited relevant skills (68%). Rohingya respondents cited especially high barriers to vocational training (88%) and employment (91%), as well as skill gaps (71%). Financial constraints, lack of training, and limited family support further hinder income generation. Financial pressures and family responsibilities also lead many to discontinue education or training, emphasizing the need for targeted interventions to improve skills, create job opportunities, and support educational continuity for youth in both communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project is designed to prioritize Centrality of Protection by integrating protection measures across all outcomes. A sectoral integration approach will support this effort through cross-sector referrals and capacity building for staff and volunteers, equipping them to identify and respond to critical protection needs. Child Protection (CP) activities will align with Education, Livelihood, and Skills sectors through capacity building on CP and effective referral mechanisms. In line with CP Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action, Sphere Standards, and Core Humanitarian Standards, the project prioritizes safety, dignity, and minimizing unintended risks for all beneficiaries. Access will be fostered through a non-discriminatory approach addressing barriers related to disability, age, and gender. SC’s accountability, feedback, and response system will ensure meaningful engagement of affected communities throughout the project cycle. To empower vulnerable populations, the project will enhance the capacities of children, caregivers, and community members to advocate for their rights. A ‘Zero Tolerance’ policy on safeguarding and PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is enforced, with clear reporting channels. Additionally, all project staff and volunteers will be trained in SC’s core policies, including Safeguarding and PSEA, ensuring a safe and inclusive project environment.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project ensures meaningful access and inclusion for persons with disabilities (PWDs) through a comprehensive approach, addressing barriers and promoting equitable opportunities. A set of specific indicators will monitor PWD participation in skill development and livelihood activities, targeting at least 3% inclusion. Activities will be tailored to their needs, such as offering assistive devices, transport allowances, and facility modifications.Adequate resources and budget allocation will support inclusive activities and specific accommodations. This includes caregiver involvement as an alternative solution when necessary, ensuring continuity and accessibility for PWDs in training and livelihood programs.Community, staff, and partners will be sensitized on the rights of PWDs through awareness sessions, fostering an inclusive mindset and reducing stigma. Criteria for cash, input support, and livelihoods assets distribution will be adapted to ensure safe access for PWDs, while information about livelihood opportunities will be provided in accessible formats like Braille, large print, and audio.Training venues will undergo accessibility audits to ensure they meet the needs of PWDs. Safe and accessible working environments will be created to facilitate their participation, with accommodation such as transport allowances or modified entry requirements when needed.PWDs will be directly involved in consultations and decision-making processes, ensuring their voices shape the project design and implementation. Careful planning will guarantee that all interventions, including skills training and distribution of resources, are aligned with the unique requirements of PWDs, fostering dignity and inclusivity at every stage.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability forms the core of Save the Children's mission to Shift the Power. Being accountable to youth and communities means we are dedicated to using our influence responsibly. This approach ensures that youth's voices drive our actions, enabling youth and communities to genuinely participate in and significantly impact all decisions that affect their lives.The project will establish a comprehensive Feedback Response Mechanism (FRM) to ensure inclusive accountability for host and camp children. The FRM will employ various methods such as home visits, feedback boxes, hotlines, help desks, and participatory sessions, providing adolescents and youths with accessible ways to share their feedback. These methods, along with staff capacity-building, will help address and respond to protection needs.An information-sharing plan, integrated into the implementation strategy, will ensure that all materials are accessible to participants with low literacy levels. Feedback will be uploaded to an online FRM system, where the project manager will categorize, investigate, and resolve issues. An accountability focal point will verify each case using established SOPs, ensuring the program team can effectively close the feedback loop. Regular accountability reports and project review meetings will assess and integrate feedback, enhancing program impact.To promote accountability, a contextual information-sharing plan will communicate clear accountability messages to all staff, fostering a unified approach across Save the Children (SC) and partners. Quarterly accountability reports will be shared, and in line with SC's localization goals, training and support will strengthen AAP mechanisms for all implementing partners.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project is firmly committed to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within its activities for Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, emphasizing the safety of vulnerable youth, especially women. Integrated within the project’s PSEA measures include age-specific, accessible reporting tools, such as participatory sessions, home visits, feedback boxes, and help desks, allowing children and adolescents to safely report instances of sexual exploitation and abuse. A dedicated Feedback Response Mechanism (FRM) supports confidential reporting channels specifically for PSEA concerns. Trained staff will be equipped to handle PSEA cases sensitively and ensure that survivors receive immediate support, reinforcing the project's commitment to a protective environment. To build awareness and maintain transparency, the project will disseminate PSEA guidelines through an information-sharing plan, making essential guidelines accessible to all staff and volunteers. Quarterly accountability reports will review PSEA actions and outcomes, promoting a culture of accountability. By embedding PSEA measures within the project’s core activities, this initiative fosters a safe, responsive environment that actively prevents, addresses, and mitigates risks of sexual exploitation and abuse for all participants.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project integrates Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) to enhance resilience in the Rohingya camps and host communities of Cox’s Bazar, addressing risks from cyclones, floods, and landslides. DRR activities aim to boost preparedness, reduce risks, and build adaptive capacity. Key components include community-led risk assessments and Early Warning Systems (EWS) to enable swift disaster responses. Community volunteers and youth will receive training in disaster preparedness, response, and recovery, focusing on essential skills like first aid, emergency planning, and evacuation. The project prioritizes climate-resilient infrastructure in learning centers to withstand extreme weather. Environmental management practices, such as reforestation and erosion control, will be included in DRR training to reduce landslide risks and restore degraded land. Gender-sensitive planning will ensure active participation from women, children, and people with disabilities. Implementation will involve community-based DRM committees to oversee planning, resource allocation, and activities, ensuring inclusive decision-making. Training will follow a phased approach, starting with needs assessments and activity prioritization, in collaboration with local government and humanitarian agencies to align with existing DRM strategies. Progress will be monitored through indicators like training participation, EWS functionality, and infrastructure upgrades. Regular monitoring visits and community feedback sessions will track outcomes. A real-time monitoring system will collect data on disaster readiness, with quarterly reviews to refine strategies and ensure sustainability.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Bangladesh’s southeast coast faces critical challenges due to climate change, including water scarcity, which impacts resilient livelihoods by reducing productivity and affecting local communities. Frequent natural disasters—floods, landslides, and cyclones—worsen these issues, disrupting lives and livelihoods. To address this, the project integrates Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) with livelihood interventions in the Rohingya and host communities, equipping them with adaptable skills and resources to withstand recurring crises. The integration of EPR into livelihood programs includes: Risk Assessment and Mitigation in Livelihood Planning: EPR is embedded in livelihood strategies by assessing vulnerabilities and applying risk mitigation to support both Rohingya refugees and host communities. Emergency Resource Management: EPR-based training teaches participants effective resource management during stable times and crises, fostering resilient practices. Creating Local EPR Networks and Market Linkages: Local networks connect participants with emergency response groups, markets, and government relief programs, supporting swift response and recovery. Community Engagement and Accountability: EPR ensures community voices shape planning and execution, aligning interventions with community priorities and fostering ownership. Emergency-Ready Infrastructure in Livelihood Programs: Safe training centers with essentials like solar power and durable storage enable continuity of livelihood activities during emergencies. By weaving EPR into livelihood interventions, the project does more than support income generation; it builds self-reliance and equips Rohingya and host communities with resources, skills, and networks to recover from crises. This approach strengthens community cohesion and lays a foundation for sustainable, resilient livelihoods.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

This project actively includes youth aged 18-24 by providing them with market-driven skills training, specifically tailored to meet local labor demands. Through non-formal technical programs in ICT, tailoring, electrical work, and business skills, the project equips youth with technical expertise essential for employability. Moreover, young people have the opportunity to take on leadership roles in community-based initiatives, particularly in disaster preparedness and awareness, which strengthens their capacity to support community resilience. Soft skills training in areas such as financial literacy and communication further enhances their readiness for employment and entrepreneurial ventures. The project also incorporates gender-sensitive measures to create a welcoming environment for young women, aiming to engage at least 45% adolescent and youth girls in training programs. Accessibility is enhanced with disability-friendly training facilities, engaging 3% of youth with disabilities to promote an inclusive learning space. Through continuous feedback channels like participatory sessions, help desks, and a hotline, youth are encouraged to voice their needs and suggestions, making them integral to shaping the project’s implementation.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project promotes self-reliance and resilience among Rohingya refugees and host communities by offering essential services and skills training to vulnerable youth. It provides practical training in trades like tailoring, handicrafts, bamboo crafts, and Electrical Installation & Maintenance (EIM), equipping participants with employable skills for sustainable income. The project also offers tools, such as sewing machines, and support for micro-businesses, fostering independence and reducing aid dependency. By promoting social cohesion and peaceful coexistence, the project mitigates resource-related tensions. Additionally, mentorship, counseling, and market linkages help build social and economic resilience, enabling refugees to adapt to challenges. Ultimately, the project creates a foundation for safe, dignified livelihoods, reducing aid reliance and fostering long-term self-sufficiency.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project aligns with Cox’s Bazar’s development priorities by supporting poverty reduction, improved livelihoods, and enhanced community resilience. It strengthens access to education and Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) resources while focusing on livelihood improvements through skills development. This approach addresses poverty and helps delay early marriages by empowering youth with practical, market-driven skills for employment. Activities focus on ICT, agriculture, and entrepreneurship, aligning with district goals to build a skilled workforce for local and national job markets. By creating opportunities for both host and Rohingya communities, the project reduces economic vulnerability, promotes social cohesion, and supports the district’s objectives for inclusive growth, sustainable livelihoods, and strengthened resilience, as outlined in Cox’s Bazar’s December 2022 development framework.
