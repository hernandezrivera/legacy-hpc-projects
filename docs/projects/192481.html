Project Summary

This project aims to address the increasing nutrition insecurity conditions in the country, in particular those affecting the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant communities. The response of AAH under the Nutrition Sector Response is divided into interventions that cover both prevention and treatment of malnutrition. In order to ensure that services aiming to prevent and treat malnutrition complement each other, the response entails improving nutrition knowledge, in addition to implementing preventive and treatment nutrition services, alongside provision of nutrition supplies. As AAH always aims to achieve amplified impact of nutrition interventions, especially in the current context, AAH will also support the most vulnerable households, based on in-depth assessments, with multi-purpose cash assistance under its Food Security Response, especially households with Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and children U5 that may be more nutritionally vulnerable. AAH will also dedicate efforts to ensure that multi-sectoral needs expressed by targeted communities are actively referred and followed up, especially that impact on nutrition outcomes are amplified when WASH, food security, protection, GBV, health, especially mental health, are simultaneously addressed. Interventions under this program will target communities in Beirut, Bekaa, and South governorates, though it is important to note that not all interventions included here below will be implemented in all 3 governorates concomitantly, but depending on the needs, context, as well as AAH's capacity.
Overall, 3350 caregivers and 2400 children aged 0-59m will be targeted, inclusive of LHF activities. As AAH is newly targeting migrant communities and given that the proportion of PLW, infants and children is not high among this community and more importantly in AAH's areas of intervention, a maximum of 5% of the targets will dedicated to support migrant communities.
In order to address malnutrition prevention, prioritizing pregnant and lactating women and caregivers of young children with nutrition, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and hygiene promotion and counseling is a priority in modalities that meet the needs and concerns of the communities supported, whether individually or in groups, in health facilities or in the community, all while mobilizing the community. To complement these interventions, ensuring that caregivers are empowered to assess and follow-up on their U5 children's malnutrition status is as crucial. For this reason, training teams on the use of Family Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) and in return, training caregivers to use this approach will be prioritized. Caregivers will also be supported to seek timely medical attention in the nearest malnutrition treatment centers accordingly. Both PLWs and children U5 will also be supported with micronutrient supplements to improve and prevent micronutrient deficiencies. In parallel, AAH staff and stakeholders, including health facilities, will be supported to create and maintain enabling environments that protect, promote and support breastfeeding and optimal feeding practices. For this reason, they will be trained on IYCF principles and oriented on the implementation of Law 47/2008 that aim to ensure proper use of breastmilk substitutes, while facilities will also be supported to create safe, private spaces to support breastfeeding. AAH will also plan to conduct a training in collaboration with UNICEF to support hospitals to provide quality and timely in-patient treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition treatment, AAH is planning to complement this initiative by coaching, supporting and supervising three of the targeted hospitals to maintain these quality services and strengthen the referral pathway from malnutrition treatment centers to in-patient treatment as needed, as well as engage the community.
All interventions prioritized by AAH are in line with the ERP's Nutrition Sector's response.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The response of AAH under the Nutrition Sector Response is divided into interventions that cover both prevention & treatment of malnutrition. In order to ensure that services aiming to prevent & treat malnutrition complement each other, the response entails improving nutrition knowledge, & provision of services & supplies, including:- Provision of IYCF counseling, nutrition awareness, hygiene promotion through individual & mother-to-mother groups- Recruitment & training of IYCF specialists to ensure provision of skilled counseling and support, at least twice to PLWs & caregivers of U2- Training staff on the use of Family MUAC approach & empower caregivers to assess their U5's malnutrition status & actively seek medical attention- Targeting PLWs and children 6-59 m with micronutrient supplements- Targeting staff, health facilities & stakeholders with awareness on Law 47 & The Code, aiming to promote, protect and support breastfeeding, while ensuring proper use of breastmilk substitutes- Supporting hospitals with coaching & supervision to provide high quality in-patient management of Severe Acute Malnutrition. To ensure that there is no duplication of services, AAH will partner with other organizations.In order to achieve amplified impact, AAH will support the most vulnerable HHs with multi-purpose cash assistance under the Food Security Response and ensure referrals for multi-sectoral needs are made. Some interventions may be conducted as a UNICEF implementing partner.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Prior to any project, AAH conducts a Protection Risk Analysis taking account the specific vulnerabilities and capacities of beneficiaries and identify the threats they are exposed to and the risks they may face because of the intervention, taking into account mitigation measures. This will be a living document and will be updated to integrate new, unforeseen risks as they arise. AAH’s code of conduct, gender policy, child protection policy, and PSEA SoPs are systematically incorporated across all programs, with staff trained recurrently on all policies and beneficiaries informed of rights. The safety and dignity of right-holders in accessing the services will be monitored by MEAL and through the FCM hotline. Nutrition programming will take into account needs and vulnerabilities and adapt accordingly. Services will be provided at both health facility and community levels and will be adapted as per the requests/needs, especially the most vulnerable, including migrant communities.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

AAH will ensure the consultation & participation of beneficiaries and stakeholders and will conduct needs assessments, post intervention/distribution monitoring & community consultations. AAH has established a dedicated hotline throughout its interventions. AAH has extensive experience assessing and monitoring nutrition-related programs, including group sessions & individual counseling. All referrals are tracked through RIMS. Post intervention/distribution assessments will be carried out by MEAL to make programmatic adjustment and evaluate programs’ impact. AAH has established a Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) through which right-holders are explained how to reach AAH hotline and able to raise complaints/feedback on access to services, impact of receiving services, etc. AAH has a two track FCM to address non-sensitive feedback, addressed by MEAL/project teams and for sensitive complaints against AAH staff, its stakeholders, PSEA, a separate, confidential channel is established.
