Project Summary

A major humanitarian emergency has occurred following the December 15 2023 Sudan`s conflict spread to Al Jazeera state and the IOM estimated that 40,750 IDPs had found refuge in White Nile as of January 8. According to an NRC assessment report (Dec 2023-Jan 2024). Similar conditions are being encountered by the IDPs of Gedaref state which received 64,551 IDPs following the Madani capture.
The response will enable short term livelihood support through various interventions, addressing present needs and sector gaps. Overall the response will contribute to household coping strategies, especially under the current ongoing fighting in many parts of the country. The response will provide necessary resources to protect and start rebuilding livelihood assets. The interventions will include: Income generating activities, provision of tools and raw materials, distribution of improved staple crops seeds and vegetables seeds, support to community committees, establishment of women’s Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLAs), and establishment of goat banks as an effort for provision of livestock. Farmers will also be supported on the production of new crop varieties and experience sharing, as part of efforts for improved access to crop production farming inputs. Other interventions will include livelihood cash support, targeting IDPs settled in urban or semi urban areas in select programme implementation locations. The asset replacement interventions will follow labor market mapping to identify the availably of sufficient skilled labor that are equipped and have necessary assets and tools in the community to meet the growing demand for their services. This will facilitate identifying the jobs, professions, and services required for a growing market. It will also support graduates and non-graduates in efforts to follow job placement opportunities to play their vital role in potential job market. In addition, conflict resolution trainings for community engagement committees, farmer herders and community leaders will take place. Trainings and provision of tools for community engagement committees to also increase disaster preparedness and risk reduction. In efforts to support business management skills, different groups will be provided gender sensitive market oriented vocational trainings. Income generating activities will also provide tailored support to women groups on business management trainings and provision of startup kits. Protection mainstreaming activities include the support to women to women groups with attention to business management, including awareness raising sessions on protection issues. The implement of the food security and livelihoods interventions will involve the integration of gender equality by informing women, girls, men and boys of the available resources and how to influence the project. By applying the project`s gender marker, FPDO will ensure that the role of women/ girls is strengthened through the transfer of knowledge on resilience and adaptation. They will be involved both as actors and beneficiaries of all project interventions taking into account women and girls` needs and rights and thus represent the interests of these particularly vulnerable populations. FPDO will also ensure that the project feedback mechanisms are inclusive to women, girls, men and boys as part of the interventions and also ensure that the access to the livelihoods assistance by women, girls, men and boys is monitored by observing the indicators designed to measure change for women and girls or men and boys based on the assessed gaps and dynamics.
The project will be targeting a total of 11,280 individuals as per 4,512 women among whom 316 are PWDs and 135 are elderly; 6,768 men including 474 PWDs and 203 elderly people. Boys and girls will indirectly be benefiting from the project through their family members participating in the project activities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In all phases of the project management, FPDO will ensure that proposedinterventions promote the safety, dignity, and integrity of the people receivingassistance and services. Special attention will be given to the different needs and capacities of women, men, girls and boys with the aim of ensuring that they avoid or minimize unintended negative consequences or impact of assistance and services.This will also ensure meaningful access, accountability, participation andempowerment. Protection related considerations are mainstreamed across all sector response plans. Moreover, joint actions will be taken to support capacitystrengthening efforts of FPDO project staff and stakeholders to integrate protection principles throughout the implementation cycle. Women and girls will be consulted on their needs in order to ensure that sufficient privacy and protection is offered, and that distribution and service provision points are in safe and accessible areas. Decisions for selection of sites will be based on consultations with the targeted community representatives (girls, boys, women and men, including persons with disability and elderly) to mitigate GBV risks, and encouraged to undertake quarterly safety audits as part of peer review and joint monitoring. FPDO project staff and stakeholders will be supported to assess GBV risks and ensure integration of risk mitigation interventions.This will be done through capacity strengthening initiatives, in close coordination and collaboration with the protection sector partners, including GBV Area of Responsibility. This will enable FPDO to engage with community structures, including women and girls, to identify any emerging risks and concerns from communities and to develop actions points to address them.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

FPDO operates under the established principle of do no harm. This means that all project staff must always consider the unintended consequences of the interventions, and act to address those consequences. The do-no-harm approach will be applied throughout the project life, mainly to avoid creating or fueling tensions and conflicts or undermining any group / communities. In order to do this FPDO will make all efforts to understand the respective contexts, the different stakeholders and their relationships, communities concern and social/cultural norms and beliefs. FPDO will establishCommunity Feedback and Complaints Mechanisms (CFCM), prioritizing community engagement and enabling diverse channels for feedback and reporting. This would include Child Friendly Feedback and Response Mechanisms that were also developed as part of previous FPDO`s programming with other actors to capture the views and concerns of children, especially girls among other vulnerable and at risk community groups. Community members will be actively involved where appropriate by working through existing community structures whilst ensuring representation from children, women, youth, people with disabilities, elderly, and relevant CBOs in the programme cycle management. FPDO will ensure that the mechanism is relevant and accessible to all community members regardless of gender, age, mobility, socio-economic status, or any other differentiating factors. The mechanism will be basically designed with target groups at the initial stages of project roll-out and rolled out in implementation.Entry points of the accountability mechanism may include hotlines, suggestion boxes, community meetings, peer-to-peer feedback, WhatsApp groups, help desk, surveys, SMS, registration logbooks, home visits as well as visiting offices of partners directly.Existing community structures will be used to disseminate information concerning existing CFCM entry points to share information, feedback, complaint or reporting.FPDO will ensure communities are well informed of existing mechanisms, usingdiverse channels of communication. Through these actions, programme participants will be able to engage as active participants and will be able to influence the programme implementation, decisions and contribute to continuous improvement.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

FPDO will implement the project on participatory and community based approaches.Regular meetings and information sharing activities will be delivered to community to inform about project services. Project will highly consider best interest of women and children when handling, sharing data and information about identified cases. Through M&E team will evaluate and adapt interventions to mitigate SEA/GBV risks and ensure that SEA/GBV risks are not being created or exacerbated through the implementation of response activities. FPDO will organize meetings with community leaders and other relevant stakeholders to present the activities and explain the accountability and feedback measures. FPDO will present the different ways to communicate issues (special complaints during distribution, direct contact with FPDO staff, visit FPDO office, complaint boxes, etc.). When a complaint is received by FPDO, it is recorded by the M&E officer and sent to sector managers or Country Director (according to degree of severity) to review. Following internal review of the information FPDO ensures to close the feedback loop and provide (where possible) the community member or group with a clear response and outcome.
