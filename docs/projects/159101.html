Project Summary

According to 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the closure of UNRWA mobile clinics, alongside the limited capacity of the MoH has left more than 370,000 people in the West Bank, including 162,663 people living in approximately 165 communities in Area C, seam zone and Hebron/H2, without access to basic primary healthcare services. Currently, WHO is providing primary health care services by mobile clinics to an estimated 11,000 people in West Bank. WHO plans to work with the MoH and Health cluster partners to support the provision of essential primary health care services to an estimated 20,000 affected population living in the most vulnerable communities identified by the cluster, and who do not have access to fixed health centres. An estimated 2,000 of them will also benefit from community training on first aid and will receive first aid kits. WHO will also conduct a review/assessment of the mobile clinics' in collaboration with the MoH and partners. By implementing the outlined interventions, the WHO will strengthen the preparedness and capacity of the health system to withstand future shocks and will contribute to humanitarian-development-peace nexus.

1 - Needs

Since the Israeli-Palestinian Interim Agreement on the West Bank and the Gaza Strip (the Oslo II accord) and the Protocol Concerning the Redeployment in Hebron, the West Bank has been divided into Areas A, B and C and Hebron/H2 in Hebron, with Palestinian civil and security control in Area A and H1; Palestinian civil control and Israeli military control in Area B; and Israeli civil and military control in Area C and H2. After 1967, Israel incorporated east Jerusalem into the Israeli municipality of Jerusalem, according its residents a different status than to Palestinians in the remainder of the occupied Palestinian territory. Since the early 2000s, east Jerusalem has further been physically separated from the rest of the West Bank by the separation barrier, which also cuts into other parts of the West Bank creating an area called the Seam Zone. This legislative and physical division of the West Bank has created particularly vulnerable populations in Area C, the Seam Zone and H2 in Hebron. In 2020, the Health Cluster identified 377,803 people living in the West Bank are in need of humanitarian health interventions. Checkpoints, barriers, and settlement expansion have cut off communities from basic primary healthcare services, and even emergency care in certain cases. The expanding settlement infrastructure includes 40km of road inaccessible to Palestinians, and the extensive system of fixed and ‘flying’ military checkpoints hampers free movement and creates unpredictable delays, including for ambulances. The continuing movement restrictions, particularly for ambulances and paramedics, the frontline healthcare system and support to those in need of timely healthcare assistance, remain under threat. In Area C, H2 and the ’Seam Zone’, 165 out of a total of 176 communities now need humanitarian assistance, a total of 162,663 people. There have been efforts to establish more permanent facilities for some communities, but these efforts are hampered by restrictive planning policies towards Palestinians in Area C, where Israel has civil and military control. This affects the establishment of permanent fixtures for health clinics, which is severely restricted for Palestinians in this area. Even delivery of health services through mobile clinics is prone to road closures and adverse weather events. Often, the existing mobile clinics are prevented access to communities in Area C and Hebron. In 2018, six of these clinics were denied access to communities for a period of at least two weeks and Israeli authorities confiscated one mobile clinic vehicle.

2 - Activities

This project is fully in line with the Health and Nutrition Cluster objective 1: Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building. The project falls under the following cluster activity: Provision of L-2 PHC health care services using mobile clinics, including consultations, supply of drugs/equipment/disposables, capacity building, Integration of Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and prevention and referral of GBV; and rehabilitation services for PwD and community awareness raising on disabilities and other cross-cutting issues.With this project, WHO aims to ensure that mobile clinics have capacities and equipment/supplies, as well as operationalize gender-sensitive mobile PHC L-2 services, provide training of staff on life-saving skills and integration of cross-cutting issues (MHPSS, GBV, disabilities awareness), and advocate to facilitate access to the target communities working very closely with WHO programme on access to health.WHO will partner with national and/or international NGOs and the Ministry of Health to support the L-2 mobile clinic services for the most vulnerable population in Area C and H2. WHO will provide the support required for mobile clinics: supplies, per diem and/or transportation costs for mobile clinics NGO staff. The Health Cluster West Bank Mobile Clinics working group platform will be used to make a final decision on the possible partnership as well as the exact locations to avoid duplication with other partners’ mobile clinics. During the selection process, WHO will prioritise the local service providers (MoH and local NGOs). However, if these partners would not be available or would not have the capacity, an international NGO would be approached as the last resort. In such situations, international NGOs will also support the capacity building of new local partners through ”shadowing”. If needed, WHO will also cooperate with staff of international NGOs and UN agencies to build the capacity of local NGO staff on mainstreaming gender, GBV and disability in the work of mobile clinics. The key activities are the following: 1) WHO will ensure the availability of essential medicines and disposables to equip the mobile clinics in targeted Area C communities, Hebron/H2. If need be, support will be provided to the partner(s) to cover the per diem and transportation costs for the mobile clinics' members. This will be discussed and agreed with the selected partner(s); 2) WHO will support the capacity building of frontline health workers in the provision of integrated essential primary health care interventions as per MoH guidelines for L-2 PHC services. This will include the integration of MHPSS, disability awareness, and detection, referral and prevention of GBV cases; 3) WHO will support the provision of community awareness sessions targeting women, men, young boys and girls on first aid and emergency preparedness in addition to the topic on health-seeking behavior and; 4) WHO will support a systematic review/evaluation of the services provided by the mobile clinics' partners. This would help identify the gaps in quality, operations and scope as well as the possible impact of mobile clinics. WHO will use the results of the review to improve the quality and coverage of mobile clinics. The Health Cluster Mobile Working Group will be used to determine the ToR, including the scope and partners for this review/evaluation.

3 - Indicators

Selected indicators to measure the achievements/progress include: 1. number of people with access to primary healthcare services in the West Bank;2. number of health workers received training on lifesaving interventions;3. number of people from the West Bank community to benefit from the provision of first aid training.
