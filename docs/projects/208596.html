Project Summary

The project aimed to Support community members to provide access to cultural recreational, creative and life skills activities for children as an entry point to reach girls and boys affected by conflict-related CP concerns with case management processes/services (including family tracing and alternative care placements), referrals and non-specialized psychosocial support.
The project will target 6000 direct beneficiaries (2000 girls, 1800 boys, 1300 women and 1500 men) which include UASC received comprehensive case management services including alternative care and support, as well as MHPSS and life skill services.

1 - Needs

The Child Protection crisis in NE Nigeria is driven by regional conflict and displacement and resultant societal disruption. The 2023 HRP identifies approximately 2.1 million people need child protection services due to the absence of child protection systems and community-based safety nets, out which 1.3 million children need child protection services. Communities have insufficient access to practitioner- and community-based child protection infrastructure. Priority needs are case management services for victims of abuse, neglect and violence, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative community care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), socio-economic reintegration of former CAAG and other vulnerable children, addressing child marriage, trafficking, child labor, low birth registration rates, social norms affecting children’s rights and critical support to children with disabilities. Coordination mechanisms cannot effectively meet current needs, further hampering execution and timely follow-up of referrals made. These challenges have been made more acute by a considerable Child Protection needs have increased in 2023 due to the sustained military offensive leading to high influx of people from NSAG, inflation, high poverty rates and relocations of IDPs.

2 - Activities

• Comprehensive multi sectorial case management support to boys and girls at risk of (abuses, neglect, exploitation, child labor , trafficking,& child marriage etc) including children with disability.• Family training reunification / Restoring Family Link for UASC. • Place children affected by protection risks (family separation, violence, neglect, abuse, etc.) in alternative care, kinship care, informal foster care, monitoring of group living arrangement)• Multi sectorial referrals for children in needs (Health, MHPSS, livelihoods, Education etc)• One Off Unconditional Cash support to vulnerable caregivers of children placed in alternative care. • Recruitment and training of Case workers and community volunteers • Service mapping and quarterly referral pathway updates.• Set up of 4 Child Friendly Spaces across project locations.• Recruitment and training of CFSs facilitators and animators.• Structured recreational and creative psychosocial service• Tailored Life skill education to girls and boys at risk.• Tailored MHPSS support to children and caregivers in needs including referrals for specialized services (Tier 4)• Positive parenting skills sessions for caregivers of conflict affected children• Established or strengthened CBCPC in prevention and responding to child protection issues.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

LABDI consults widely with all community members during project design andprovides a platform for all beneficiaries to give feedback freely. The project will targetschool children and utilize child representative platforms to seek their views on alldecisions that concern them. LABDI also implements projects in consultation withcaregivers of children, who are part of the Key Implementing Partners (KIPs) and arefully involved in decision-making. To ensure that everyone's voice is heard, LABDIutilizes community volunteers and team leads selected from the beneficiaries andserve as the first point of call for beneficiaries to be able to give feedback. Inimplementing this project, LABDI will use the approach of focus group discussion givefeedback of the intervention and raise any concerns they may have. This focus groupdiscussion will be done across all project locations, including schools and ChildFriendly Spaces.
