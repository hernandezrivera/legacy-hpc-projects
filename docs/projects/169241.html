Project Summary

Provision of emergency cash assistance, protection and psycho-social support, risk mitigation and in-kind distributions to ensure impacted vulnerable households are able to meet their basic needs, access safe services and face reduced risks in their homes and communities.

1 - Needs

The destruction of shelters is expected to result in increased risks of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) for women and girls. With an estimation of over 300,000 being rendered homeless, thousands of women and girls are residing in partially damaged houses, temporary relocation sites or shared shelters with multiple unrelated families. Privacy and dignity may be undermined, while increased SGBV risks are expected to result from inadequate and unsecure living conditions. Many women and girls are living in homes without windows and doors, and due to the rapid nature of the crisis, GBV specific safety assessments have not been conducted in relocation sites. This coupled with pre-existing GBV, increased risk of intimate partner violence and early marriage due to COVID-19 the protection environment for women and girls has deteriorated. Additionally, the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse is heightened as women and girls are increasingly dependent on humanitarian assistance, and community structures have been uprooted. Women and girls with disabilities and female headed households are likely to be particularly marginalized, at increased risk of violence and abuse, and in need of humanitarian support during this crisis. Dedicated response services for survivors of GBV and risk mitigation activities are an essential, life-saving component of all humanitarian responses, but they have unfortunately not yet been scaled up within the Beirut humanitarian response. Children are at particular risk of family separation due to the destruction of homes, death of family members and hospitalization of their caregivers. Children affected by the blast have been exposed to collective trauma, displacement, loss of family members and will continue to see adults and children in their immediate environment suffer for months to come. In addition, particularly vulnerable families such as families of children with disabilities, vulnerable Lebanese, Syrian refugee children, and migrant children need emotional and psychosocial support as their families and communities come to terms with the disaster and regroup. An estimated 10% of those affected by the blast have a family member with a disability. Of those 34% are related to the blast. Persons with disabilities, including with pre-existing disabilities and new disabilities (resulting from the blast) will require access to urgent specialized care including rehabilitation facilities and replacement of mobility devices. Moreover, for both older persons and persons with disabilities, mobility and access to basic services may be increasingly difficult due to the presence of un-cleared debris and destruction of pre-existing infrastructure; particularly in heavily hit and densely populated neighborhoods. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) needs are expected to be high, particularly for vulnerable groups. According to an assessment conducted by World Vision International, 41% of parents interviewed reported that their children were manifesting psychological distress, including having nightmares (40%), unusual crying and screaming (26%). Throughout interventions there will be a need for streamlined psychosocial first aid (PFA) for communities who will be reeling from shock.

2 - Activities

Provision of emergency cash assistance (Target = 1,022)will be given to mitigate protection risks. In line with the response priorities set out in the Flash Appeal, the IRC has commenced the provision of ECA to address urgent blast-related needs under existing funding streams. At the outset, the IRC has responded to referrals of urgent needs from local frontline responders and specialized agencies providing services for persons with disabilities and older persons. The IRC’s ECA is aligned with existing guidance provided by the Protection Sector, and will be further adapted as additional guideane on the use of the ECA is developed by the sector/cluster. ECA is utilized to support individuals and households mitigate protection risks, and is provided to all cohorts who; 1) were impacted by the Beirut Port Explosion, 2) have additional vulnerability factors 3) have limited coping capacities. The provision of ECA will be closely linked to the emergency case management and specialized PSS provide by the IRC’s Child Protection and Women Protection and Empowerment (WPE) program, to ensure that child protection risks and risks specific to women and girls are addressed in a comprehensive manner. The IRC also has dedicated referral focal points who ensure that persons receiving ECA are also referred to other specialized services including medical support, rehabilitation services for persons with disabilities and livelihoods programming. All vulnerable cohorts impacted by the Beirut Port Explosion will be covered including Lebanese, Migrant Workers, Syrian refugees, refugees of other nationalities and stateless. The IRC will place a specific focus on persons with disabilities and older persons at risk. Case Management and Psycho-social Support (PSS) (GBVV: 225 CP; 453 PFA; 295 NFI)(Child protection: 120 CM; 320 community based PSS): Through mobile rapid response teams, the IRC provides specialized emergency case management, Psychological First Aid, and focused psycho-social support for women and children impacted by both the primary and secondary impacts of the Beirut Port Explosion. Those services will be offered within temporary safe spaces in which women and children will also be able to exchange and access information, get information on service provision and updates on the situation, link to and access support from other women and girls in the community, and exercise their own voice and agency. The locations of the safe spaces will be decided upon assessments and discussions with women and children and other key community stakeholders. The IRC will also provide technical support to first line responders of local actors to build their capacity on GBV prevention, mitigation and response. Risk Assessments and Safety Mapping (500 CP risk mitigation sessions): The IRC will lead and advocate for activities designed to reduce risks women and girls face in the emergency and post-emergency context, and to protect women and girls who have already experienced violence from experiencing further harm. This will include conducting weekly or bi-monthly safety audits, or other risk-identification activities to determine contexts in which women and girls are most vulnerable to violence and harm. Activities will include distribution of non food items such as dignity kits, flashlights (to support safety during power cuts) and hygiene materials to support dignity and maintain the health and hygiene of women and girls, especially given increasing transmission of COVID-19 and safety audits. 24/7Helpline: A dedicated helpline has been activated to ensure 24/7 support to GBV survivors through emergency case management. The IRC will further expand the reach of the helpline through dedicated staff providing crisis intervention support and case management remotely. The helpline will serve as a safe and confidential pathway for women and girls to access PSS services as well as access to information.
