Project Summary

IhsanRD will provide eight rounds of Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) per month, with a transfer value of $100 USD per vulnerable household (HH), benefiting 113 HHs (comprising 565 individuals) in targeted sub-districts. This assistance is based on the coverage of the survival minimum expenditure basket as determined by the Cash Working Group (CWG). Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) responses will be adjusted over time to reflect market recovery after disasters, thereby increasing purchasing power in the targeted market.
Priority is given to targeting females and the most vulnerable individuals, including men, boys, and girls. Factors such as disability and age group are also considered, alongside integration with Existing Recipient List (ERL) beneficiaries and referral cases of protection.
Under this project IhsanRD will consider the following risk matrix:
Assumptions:
1. Stable security situation during project implementation.
2. Financial Service and E-portal system providers remained operational.
3. Local councils welcomed and facilitated the initiatives without interference in selecting beneficiaries (excluding or including beneficiaries regardless of the criteria designed for the project).
4. Timely disbursement of needed funds to start project activities.
5. Distribution points are safe and easily accessible to all BNFs.
6. A contracted FSP is not banned by a donor and/or local authorities.
7. Success in the beneficiary selection process with abundant community engagement and documentation verification.
Risk & Mitigations:
1. Changing the security situation that may affect the implementation of project activities.
Likelihood: Low - Impact: High
Mitigation: Monitoring and analysing security situation, temporary suspension of fieldwork to protect the field team and BNFs from the expected risk. In case of high risks to security and according to the contingency plan, the decision to stop movements in the project area and suspend the activities will be taken by the field office manager, followed directly by reporting and coordination with the donor to make the best action.
2. Enforce the beneficiaries to pay taxes or fees or make other payments from their aid transfers in relation to CVA.
Likelihood: Low – Impact: High
Mitigation: Raise beneficiaries’ awareness that the assistance is free of charge and that they are not required to pay ‘fees’ or ‘taxes.’ Provide information on beneficiary feedback and response mechanism. Awareness should be raised with local actors on humanitarian principles and aid diversion prevention measures.
During project setup, the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) department collaborates closely to ensure quality implementation. A detailed work plan outlines activities, indicators, methodologies, and responsibilities, facilitating comprehensive monitoring and evaluation throughout the project lifecycle.
Baseline and end-line assessments are conducted, followed by verification of beneficiaries to ensure they meet selection criteria. On-site distribution and redemption monitoring, along with post-distribution interviews and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), ensure transparency and accountability. Regular supervision, spot checks, and satisfaction surveys by the MEAL team further enhance project monitoring and effectiveness.
