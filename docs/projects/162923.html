Project Summary

This project aims to deliver the basic life saving nutrition services to the vulnerable men, women, boys and girls in the states of Blue Nile (Tadamon, Damazin and rossieris localities), White Nile (Rabak and Kosti localities) and Kassala (Aroma, North Delta and Rural Kassala) targeting vulnerable residents, with a goal of creating a substantial decrease in the high GAM rates prevalent in these target localities. The project will deliver complete CMAM package and nutrition services interventions for the treatment and prevention of malnutrition among children U5 and PLWs through the provision of the needed supplies and nutrients, building the capacity of the medical staff in the target nutrition centers and inclusion of community participation in outreach activities to ensure project sustainability by conducting trainings on CMAM and IYCF. The project also intends to raise the awareness of the mothers and/or care givers in the target communities and create a positive change in their feeding practices to decrease the high rates of GAM among the vulnerable PLWs and children U5 through IYCF counselling.
additionally, this project intends to raise the communities' awareness through conducting integrated health and nutrition awareness sessions that address various nutrition and health topics that include sessions on prevention of communicable diseases, AWDS/Cholera and many other health related issues.

1 - Needs

Blue Nile state is considered one of the highest needs states according to the 2020 HRP/HNO. This is more emphasised by the recent outbreaks of Cholera in September, 2019 as announced by the Ministry of Health, where the first cases identified and confirmed of Cholera in Sudan were in Blue Nile. The target localities of Rosseiris , Damazine (where many of the identified Cholera cases were from) and Altadamon , have a population in need of nutrition interventions of (161,295, 13,655 and 68,686 respectively) in accordance to the 2020 HNO/HRP, and high GAM rate prevalence in all the three target localities. There are 1 OTP, 2 SFP centres in Damazine, 2 OTPs, 2 SFP centres in Rosseiris and 2 OTPs, 1 SFP centre in Altdamon, as provided by the national nutrition program data from the federal ministry of health. These numbers of nutritional centres are not enough to address the high needs for boys and girls U5, and PLWs who are in need of nutritional interventions. The importance of improving the nutritional health in these target localities is essential to reduce the risk of future AWDs outbreaks. Blue Nile state is also at high risk of armed conflict evolving, and this could cause disruption of the already compromised feeding practices, thus preventative measures must be in place to minimise the negative impact of any disturbances, through IYCF counseling and awareness sessions.White Nile is also one of the highly ranked states in Sudan for high Humanitarian needs, and ranked 4 in nutritional needs. In the target localities of Kosti and Rabak, the total population in need of nutritional interventions are (23,300 and 16,913 respectively) and the GAM rate for U5 is relatively high (11.7 and 10.13 respectively). These numbers indicate the importance of having further nutritional interventions as there are only 1 OTP and 1 SFP centres in each of the target localities (FMOH), which are not enough to reach and provide the needed interventions for these high numbers. the target localities have suffered from floods in 2017 which resulted in an outbreak of AWDs, and are still considered of high risk of AWDs outbreak, that solidifies the need for improving the nutritional health and consequently the general health of the vulnerable residents, and decrease the GAM rates. Kassala state is one of the states with the least humanitarian interventions and the highest humanitarian needs according to 2020 HNO/HRP (ranked 5). Kassala has been prone to many outbreaks in this year as announced by the ministry of health. In the three target localities of Aroma, North Delta and Rural Kassala the need for nutrition interventions is quite high as is evident in the number of population in need for nutrition interventions (16,719- 18,267 and 18,610 respectively). The feeding practices in Kassala also contribute very much to the elevated GAM rates U5 (18.36, 12.51 and 11.52 respectively). The number of OTPs in the target localities is (2, 3 and 3 respectively) and the SFP centres (2, 2 and 3 respectively). Despite these interventions it appears that the GAM rates are still high, and this may be due to the cultural context in these target areas, which requires more community awareness and community based interventions.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The nutrition interventions in the target localities (Rosseiris, Tadamon and Damazine in Blue Nile, Kosti and Rabak in White Nile and Aroma, North Delta and Rural Kassala in Kassala state) will include the rehabilitation/equipping of OTP/SFP centers, referral and treatment for SAM children (boys and girls) U5 with or without complications in SCs/OTPs respectively. The activities will also include the referral and treatment of MAM children U5 and PLWs in the respective SFP centers. The project will also conduct community outreach activities for active case finding and follow up of detected cases, in addition to conducting screening for boys and girls U5 and PLWs in the target communities, and provision of micronutrient supplementation for prevention of malnutrition occurrence. The project will also conduct IYCF programs for mothers and/or caregivers, conduct awareness sessions for the target communities on various nutrition topics. Additionally, the project will train community volunteers (men and women) on CMAM and IYCF and will build the capacity of the medical staff (men and women) in the target OTPs, SFP centers and SCs through conducting trainings and/or refresher trainings on CMAM and IYCF.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During this project’s design, the environmental effects and climate changes in the target states and locations, as provided in 2020 HRP/HNO, have been given pronounced considerations. The high risk of droughts in some of the target locations may cause additional disruption in the normal feeding practices of the residents of these areas, which may increase the GAM rates in the long run if no appropriate preventive and curative measures are put in place. Some of these areas also have a high risk of flooding and have suffered from it in the previous years of 2017, 2018. The floods also have a negative impact on the agriculture and livestock which may lead to disturbance in the positive nutritional behavior. The project aims to mitigate these negative effects on the nutritional health of the vulnerable communities through provision of micronutrient supplementation for prevention and treatment of malnourished cases. Additionally, floods may cause AWDs outbreaks that necessitate the presence of efficient and responsive health plan, as proposed in the activities. To ensure that the project will have no negative effects on the environment the design includes establishment of a medical waste management system, to guarantee safe disposal of the waste from the target HFs and nutritional centers. And for the rehabilitation, where fired bricks or local materials that use wood from trees and may cause an increase in deforestation rates, awareness sessions on importance of environment preservation and encouragement of tree planting and the impact of deforestation on the environment. Green fencing of the HFs and Nutrition centers will also be considered.To encourage the use of clean energy, the project considers using solar panels as sustainable sources of power.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During the project design, segregation data influenced the choice of interventions and how they are implemented. The project ensures that community-based interventions are in place, and encourages community involvement, while ensuring gender sensitivity in this involvement. The project is also designed to include topics of protection that women face in the target communities such as FGM and early marriage and child protection topics in the awareness sessions that address these topics for the community, especially in any social events explicit to women, and conduct specific trainings for the community volunteers and medical staff on these serious topics. A special consideration is also given to create a separated and safe environment for women, young girls and children in the targeted health facilities while receiving their medical and nutritional services. In addition, the project design is created to allow people with disabilities easy use of the health facilities and nutrition centers, through construction of PwD-friendly outlets, and ensure Pwd inclusion in services provided.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with community leaders with aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Before the beginning of the implementation, GAH will train all appointed staff on PSEA, and make sure all staff have signed the PSEA, code of conduct and child protection. The vendors associated with GAH will also sign a vendor code of conduct. GAH will also conduct awareness sessions for the community in PSEA. During the implementation of this project, interviews with beneficiaries on the quality of the services provided will be conducted, in addition to provision of complaint boxes in each of the targeted health facilities and nutrition centers, and informing the beneficiaries of the importance of reporting and how it will not have any consequences on them or on the services provided for them. The beneficiaries will also be informed on that these services are given to them without anything in return, and if any violations occur from our staff, they should be reported via a hotline number given in each HF and nutrition center. All the information and violations, should they occur, will be reported to the respective donors and agencies, after being rectified and immediate actions taken by GAH.
