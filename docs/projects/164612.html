Project Summary

The project aims at proving emergency child protection services, which is urgently required by the children in the Al Hole camp.
The children have psychological related problems, due to the loss of the beloved ones and witnessing some of the atrocities.
Some children have become stateless, due to poverty; the families have resorted to negative coping mechanisms.
To address the child protection needs, the project will ensure provision of psychosocial support through structured activities that are gender sensitive. Case management services will be provided by the trained social workers, and appropriate referrals made for those who need additional support.
Community structures will be identified and strengthened, to ensure they identify and respond to child protection risks and make referral to other actors in the camp for complicated cases.
Parenting training will be provided to improve care and protection of children especially among child headed household. Training will also be provided on child protection minimum standards to ensure appropriate package service is provided to the children, and there is no united harm.
Community will be engaged through the project process.
The project will target approximately 19,150 persons (11,489 females, 7,661 male)

4 - Needs

MSJM team is working in al-Hole since the 15/5/2019. The activities have mostly focused on provision of health services in Camp. MSJM has performed a full assessment of the humanitarian needs in general and the protection needs in particular in al-Hole specifically related to children. Our findings meet the HRP strategy. 73,782 individuals reside in Al Hol camp, 91% are women and children. Humanitarian partners are revising their planning figures.The camp hosts a colony of children (more than 22000), many of them are facing numerous child protection risks, and they are living in small tents, with no heating at all. They lack most of the basic necessities of life including adequate protection.Most of these children suffer from psychological problems due to prolonged hostilities that they have faced right from their areas of origins. Displacement has uprooted children from their homes, they have lost family property, family breadwinners, and the journeys to the camps have been long. All this has psychologically affected the children. Our medical mobile teams inside the camp have observed that 92% of the children do not have any place or space, where they can play.• Regular and sustained access to third country nationals is a challenge, especially for those providing health and protection services requiring not only regular engagement and care provision to the displaced but also a cultural adaptation to the mindset of those specific persons in need.More than 89% of population of Camp has no knowledge about the child rights and how parents have to deal with their children, how they must provide safe environment for them to play, and support their emotional development.The current situation has exacerbated family poverty of the IDPs, most families have resorted to negative copy mechanisms to adapt with the new situation. Early marriage and child labor are very common occurrences.It is important for partners there to contribute towards a protective environment.

5 - Implementation of activities

Child Friendly Spaces will be established and equipped after deep discussions with community key persons, qualified staff will be selected, and training for persons from the community conducted, structured activities will be implemented as well as Psychosocial support will be provided to children and awareness created on child rights. Also, Parents will be trained on parenting skills, this will target both male and female, a greater emphasis, will be on child headed Household. Community structures will be identified and trained to identify child protection risks. Awareness will be provided to community structures, which will in the end conduct awareness sessions in the camp.Individualized case management support, will be provided through individual session by trained personal and appropriate referrals made to other services providers. Ensuring the sustainability of the activities will be through training to all child protection actors in the camp on child protection minimum standards so that children are provided with appropriate care and services. Community Engagement:MSJM, will engage the community in the planning, where community members will be consulted on the key parameters of the project, on implementation phase, the community structures and community volunteers will be key to project implementation such as running the Child Friendly Spaces, creation of awareness by community structures and the community will be key on monitoring of child rights violations. M&E Process:MSJM will assign two members as monitoring supervisor in field to send reports about the achieved activities, those MSJM field coordinators will conduct daily monitoring of the project activities. The M&E team will also conduct weekly monitoring, while the senior management will also conduct monitoring on a monthly basis. Monitoring will inform how best to improve the programme implementation.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: the difference of culture and ethnic backgrounds may form obstacle in front of the project and executing activities.Mitigation measure: 1- awareness raise campaigns will be made to spread information about child protection issues.2- Close coordination with the camp administration will be conducted to facilitate the activities implementation. 3- Meetings will be made with parents to demonstrate the objectives of the project and convergence of views. Risk2: stopping the approvals granted to MSJM for this project for any reason.Mitigation measures: MSJM will train persons from camp to be qualified and to be professional of providing child protection service and thus we secure the sustainability of the program beyond the MSJM.Risk 3: Shift of control on the camp, it is possibly that the control of the camp goes to Syrian Governmental authorities.Mitigation measures: MSJM will keep a good relationships and close coordination with all parties concerned in Al Hole camp, so the shift of control will not influence on implementation of the project. Risk4: The security restrictions imposed by the camp administration on freedom of movement outside the camp will negatively influence on project activities related to referrals.Mitigation measures: close coordination with camp administration to find a mechanism through which we can send the referrals and dealing with the acute cases of children.
