Project Summary

According to OCHA Somalia Humanitarian report released in September 2020 indicted, a
total of 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase
3 or higher) outcomes between now and December without sustained humanitarian
assistance. An additional 3 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2),
bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 5.1 million. The FSNAU
post GU 2020 report released in September indicated a median GAM prevalence of 11.8%
and 1.8 % SAM. The FSNAU and partners estimated that nearly 722,622 children under the
age of five years will likely face acute malnutrition through December 2021, including 162,
007 who are likely to be severely malnourished. In addition, triple threat of Flooding,
Locust infestation and the on-going COVID-19 pandemic worsening the existing chronic
humanitarian crisis in Somalia. SOS anticipate that the nutrition situation is expected to
deteriorate if urgent humanitarian support is not provided. Underlying factors related to
high malnutrition among children and women include high morbidity, low immunization,
poor care practices, drought, and continued displacement due to conflict and flooding
contribute to the persistent high GAM rates. Therefore, the proposed project aims at
providing integrated Nutrition treatment and prevention services for displaced and nondisplaced
under five children and pregnant and Lactating Women for a period of 12
months. It will contribute to reduction of nutrition related morbidity and mortality rates to
below emergency threshold through scale up of therapeutic feeding and supplementary
programs. The project will support treatment of severe acute malnutrition cases through
admission to Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP), Supplementary Feeding Programs
(TSFP) and establishing referral linkages to stabilization centers supported by other
Implementing partners in target locations. For moderately and severely malnourished

under five children identified through community screening by community health and
nutrition workers, they will be referred to respective interventions. Besides Nutrition
treatment services, the project will offer Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) promotion
through group and individual counselling sessions, mother to mother support groups to
sensitize pregnant and lactating mothers on IYCF. The activities will be implemented
through 6 fixed facilities and, 3 mobile teams 1 stabilization center. A network of trained
community volunteers will help with provision of services at community level.
SOS CV has been implementing an integrated health and nutrition programme in Bandir,
Bakol and lower Shabelle region and due to the critical humanitarian situation, SOS
propose to continue implementing its nutrition program and expand to new regions that
are critically in need of such services to prevent further deterioration of nutrition status
and subsequent loss of life.
SOSCV will implement this intervention directly, while working closely with the District
Health Management and Village Health Committees (VHCs). Merlin also is planning to
increase the awareness of local community on health emergencies as well as hygiene and
sanitation. The local community will also be engaged in the program implementation to
enlist their participation on key health education issues with the community.
The proposed project target to reach 29,500 pregnant and lactating mothers and 21,000
under five children in preventing and treating acute malnutrition and reduce nutrition
related morbidity and mortality rates

Mitigation Measures

- Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiariessufficient time to travel home before nightfall. - Identify central locations - beneficiariesshould not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to accessdistribution points. - Avoid placing distribution points near military installations orthreats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should notbe present inside distribution points. - Consider the frequency of distributions – thegreater the time between distributions the larger the quantities distributed, which putsbeneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage. - Ensure the ration size is appropriate forthe beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financialburden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them. 2. Risk of women,girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse(SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation -Consider separate waitingareas for males and females. -Have a proportionate number of female health staff. Iffemale doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation betweenlocations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a femaledoctor will be available. -Employ health staff with skills and experiences working withchildren - Install lights outside and on the access route to the facility 5. Risk thatmarginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do notparticipate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or areexcluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures -Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and preventdiscrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups -Identify individuals who lackdocumentation to prevent exclusion from services. -promote active participation andconsultation with target beneficiates including women, girls and disable personsduring project planning, implementing and monitoring. - Provide information aboutservices in both verbal and written form -Make facilities and infrastructure disabilityfriendly.
