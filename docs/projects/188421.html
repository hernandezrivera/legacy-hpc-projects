Project Summary

Youth in Syria are essential agents of change. In Syria's affected communities, their presence and contribution in all local services are becoming more and more provident.
The aspirations of these youth, what they want to become and achieve in the future in terms of education, and employment and social life, will profoundly shape future Syria's recovery and development. Domestic violence is increasingly reported as Syrian parents struggle to cope with their circumstances. Bullying is said to be on the rise, reflecting tensions between host and refugee communities caused by the tremendous pressure on basic services. These daily stresses have severe and lasting impacts on Syrian youth's psychosocial wellbeing, and for some youth can worsen the effects of trauma. Hence, the proposed project aims to support the continuity and expansion of the Nasaem Community Centre for Youth in Idleb city to respond to the increasing needs of youth affected by the prolonged war. The proposed project is planned to provide services to Syrian youth affected by the conflict in Idleb, Syria, through a holistic intervention, consists of the following three corners:
The first is the provision of protection services for youth and their families. The second is implementing an awareness-raising programme focusing on the most urgent and pertinent issues facing youth in Idleb city. The activities of this intervention are as follow:
1. Conduct twenty focus-group discussions for youth groups (male and female) within 13-20 years old to understand their needs and aspirations better. Each group will be consisting of about 10 persons.
2. Lay counselling services to promote social-emotional learning. Social-emotional learning is an opportunity for collaboration between counsellors and youth.
3. Provision of non-specialised psychosocial support, group and individual sessions.
4. Recruit, train and deploy mobile/outreach teams: the outreach teams will conduct activities such as recreational activities for youth in high schools, discussion and support
groups of youth to deal with the challenges they encounter in their daily life (recognition of their education certificate, child marriage, child labour….etc.). Specific groups will be developed to address the needs of male and female youth.
5. PSS mobile teams' training: PSS mobile teams (4 persons: 2 males and 2 females) will consist of selected IDPs and host communities. The PSS mobile team members will be provided with intensive training to build their capacity to provide basic PSS services. This will encourage a sustainable approach, as once the population moves from the site, they will still be able to use to acquired knowledge and skills within their local communities.
6. The Prevention and Service Support Team (PSST) will be responsible for developing, coordinating, delivering, and facilitating addiction-related training events throughout Idleb.
The will be consist of 2 persons (mentors) who are qualified in this subject matter. The team's main objective and commitment are to provide a wide range of education and training initiatives that focus on improving awareness, competence and knowledge of those affected by drug use and addiction and other negative coping mechanisms and those working in the field.
7. Nasaem's Community centre for Youth will act as a focal point and safe space for beneficiaries to access services, for partners to refer cases or access MHPSS, and training venues for MHPSS capacity building. Besides, the centre will be open to being used by other actors implementing activities to improve psychosocial well-being.
8. Conduct GBV prevention and empowerment activities, as well as COVID-19 awareness-raising interventions (including on associated GBV risks and GBV key messages) for around 400 individuals (male and female) to cover the following topics: GBV prevention and empowerment activities, PSEA, prevention of early marriage.

4 - Needs

Based on the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, A decade of conflict has trapped civilians in a pervasive protection crisis where people’s rights to safety, security and well-being are regularly violated. The deteriorating economy and widespread poverty, lack and loss of livelihoods, destruction and loss of housing and property, protracted and multiple cycles of displacement, substandard living conditions (even for people in areas of relative stability), family separation and the breakdown of family or community support structures have depleted the coping abilities of individuals and communities and generated high levels of psychological distress. As a result, people resort to harmful coping mechanisms (including child labour, child recruitment, different forms of exploitation and child/forced marriage) while over-reliance on humanitarian aid persists. Within the affected population, protection issues disproportionately affect groups such as women, children, especially adolescent boys and girls, older persons, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable, marginalized or socially excluded people and groups. Older persons and people with disabilities are at increased risk of separation from their families, care providers and dependent on the assistive products which support their independence. These are significantly increased in displacement sites. In Idlib the complexity of protection needs of youth, creating longer lasting impact of unmet needs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project prioritizes services to groups considered particularly vulnerable (PWD, Youth and adolescents). Risk: A breach of ethics. - Mitigation measure: Nasaem will train thestaff on the code of conduct, child safeguarding, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies and ask them to sign them. - Risk: Staff may findsensitive protection cases. Mitigation measure: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the areas based on the updated service map. The project will priorities the most vulnerable persons (males and females including IDPs), considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with disabilities or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project. The project is designed to enable equal access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups and not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity andimpartiality. Awareness sessions will be conducted about strengthening self-protection mechanisms, rights' assisting, GBV services, increasing knowledge about woman and main roles and rights. In addition, males will attend sessions about GBV risks.Do No Harm approach to integrate conflict sensitivity wherever. The feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All staff and volunteers working in this project will be trained on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) aspects to understand, sign and adhere to a code of conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standardsand the rights of beneficiaries. Ensure measures in case of violation and fight impunity. NKO will ensure that mechanisms are in place for Protection from PSEA and that they are, regularly updated and are functioning effectively. NKO willcontinue to strengthen coordination with the PSEA network in addressing sexual exploitation and abuse. Respond to all complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can or need to be put in place following existing principles andprocedures recommended by the PSEA network. In partnership with PSEA network, identify dedicated focal points and raise awareness about PSEA. Provide clear information that beneficiaries do not have to provide services or favors in exchange for receiving services or accessing facilities. Ensure that vulnerable groups and individuals are able to access services equally and without any discrimination. It will be confirmed that the service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against girls, boys, women or men. Apply the principle of different needs equal opportunities.
