Project Summary

This project will support 500 farmer households (3000 individuals: 644 men, 677 women, 824 boys, 855 girls/ Each farmer will receive 125 Kg of wheat seeds which will be used to plant 5 dunums (25 kg per dunum) with agricultural inputs and technical support for wheat crop planting. This support will improve bread production (the staple food for Syrian people) which has been negatively affected by the lack of wheat and high prices of production supplies. The project will strengthen local production capacity and local markets, therefore, the self-reliance of the community to be stable against external conflicts.
This project will also support 400 vulnerable households including female-headed and child-headed households living in peri-urban and rural areas by distributing vaccinated chicken and chicken feed to 200 families (1200 individuals: 257 men, 270 women, 330 boys, 343 girls/ each family will receive 10 chickens, 2 cocks, and 180KG chicken fodder) and distribution of sheep and fodder to 200 families (1200 individuals: 257 men, 270 women, 330 boys, 343 girls/ each family will receive 2 female Awas sheep and 250 KG of fodder per household), in addition to supporting them with the required knowledge and basic advice to achieve the best possible production of eggs, milk, and meat.
This will strengthen and re-establish productive small backyard chicken and sheep flocks to support local food production and to improve household nutrition, especially for children and pregnant women, and to open up additional sources of income for rural and peri-urban women through small-scale marketing of eggs, milk, and meat
The project will also support 100 women-headed families (600 individuals: 129men, 135 women, 165boys, 171girls) by vocational training, Tools and equipment, and raw materials to prepare dairy products, in order to start their own projects and secure incomes to cover their families’ needs.
The project will also include Rehabilitation of key irrigation infrastructure for agricultural plains of Azmarin village, including the main intake pumping station (3 main pumps, control panels, and main station building), main water pipes (60cm diameter, 660-meter length) which shifting water to the main water collection basin (100 m3), open agriculture channel system (2500 meter) and water closed channels which distribute water from open channel to the nearest farms (10,000 m) along with their valve and gates.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO 2023- the syrian crisis has severely affected the agriculture and agri-food sectors and supporting its resilience has become an urgent national priority. The sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was driven by weather anomalies, including issues with water scarcity and availability, damaged infrastructure and productive assets, disrupted services and scarcity of energy resources, especially diesel and electricity. In addition, the high prices of agriculture production inputs, especially fertilizers, herbicides, and others, continues to limit agriculture input use by farmers and adherence to standard seed and dose rates. According to the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reforms, the total area planted with wheat in 2021/2022 was 1.2 million hectares (down 25 per cent compared to 2021). Furthermore, the harvested area is less than 60 per cent of the total cultivated area Livestock has not also been spared from the impact of the prevailing water shortage and scarcity, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed, and the generally unfavourable weather conditions. The unfavourable 2021 – 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development, increased demand for fodder, limited supply, and high prices, especially imported feed and this trend is likely to continue if the 2022/2023 rainy season is not favourable.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

**The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm to some women and may cause domestic violence. - Likelihood: M. - Impact: H. -Mitigating: -We will not distribute at a late time of the day. -We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute them in each village separately. -During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. **The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers. -Likelihood: w. - Impact: H.-Mitigating: 1- We will choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities. 2- 4- Ensure that the complaint mechanisms for reporting SEA/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email,..) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties.3-In case any exploitation happens, BINAA will take appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation where allegations of SEA/GBV are reported involving our personnel (suspending staff till the investigation is conducted, referring the victim to PSS, and even witness protection program if needed) and investigate allegations in a timely and professional manner.
