Project Summary

QC will continue the shelter interventions to provide safe living conditions to Rohingya Refugees / FDMNs, with careful consideration of Age, Gender, Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM), and Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVI) families. As part of sector objective 1: Ensure timely and equitable delivery of Shelter, NFI , Camp Coordination and Camp Management services to. Rohingya Refugees/FDMNs and host community (living in close proximity to the camps) to reduce exposure to physical and protection related risks including ensuring access to safe energy solutions to Refugee/FDMN households.QC plans to construct 800 MTS in Camp 21 (Block A). The decision to select ""Block A"" was based on the extremely poor condition of the shelters in that area. QC has coordinated with UNHCR (AOR focal point) to ensure there is no overlap with other ongoing efforts. Furthermore, QC will carry out post-distribution monitoring upon the successful completion of the shelter project. The design and location of these MTS in Camp 21 will be based on an assessment conducted by the SCCCM Sector and approved by the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC). If space is unavailable or an emergency arises in another camp, QC will collaborate closely with the SCCCM Sector, RRRC, CiC, and camp focal points to achieve the intended objectives.
The proposed shelter program will follow an inclusive and community-driven approach, emphasizing active involvement from the community members. Special consideration will be given to female-headed households, the elderly, households with special needs, and persons with disabilities. QC will meticulously document data related to these vulnerable groups, using the SADDD (Sex, Age, and Disability Disaggregated Data) framework. It is anticipated that 12% of persons with disabilities (PWD) will receive shelter assistance based on the criteria established by the Washington Group on Disability.
In addressing Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) concerns, QC will adhere to the principles outlined by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) pertaining to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, in addition to following the established guidelines of the Cox's Bazar PSEA network as required. QC is an active member of the PSEA network in Cox's Bazar and is committed to upholding high standards in this regard. As part of our commitment, all staff members will undergo two refresher training sessions throughout the project's duration to ensure that they are well-informed and vigilant in preventing PSEA.
Furthermore, QC will ensure effective coordination and open communication with various stakeholders, including the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG), SCCCM Sector, WASH teams, Protection agencies, Camp-in-Charge (CiC), Site Management teams, Deputy Commissioner (DC), Upazila Nirbahi Officer (UNO), and any other relevant stakeholders.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The total number of Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar is around 10,10,298 individuals. A high majority of the Rohingya refugee population is concentrated in 33 extremely congested camps within Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar district, Bangladesh. The refugees are dependent on the assistance provided by the humanitarian community and the government of Bangladesh. In the camps, shelters are exposed to cyclic monsoons and face risk of floods, landslides, fire and cyclones. Temporary materials such as bamboo and tarpaulin have a limited capacity to resist weather impacts, and thus require regular repairs and replacement. Use of adequate material (treated bamboo, good quality tarpaulin) along with the design, site plan, and proper technical details for the materials connections, can improve the lifespan of materials if properly followed (Shelter Performance Standard Assessment Report 2023). The findings of the 2023 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) indicate that access to food and shelter materials is the foremost priority among the needs in the Rohingya camps and the host community. Specifically, the assessment reveals that 41% of refugee households have not been able to enhance their shelter conditions. Among this group, the majority reported the lack of improvement was primarily due to a shortage of humanitarian assistance (60%) or the assistance received being insufficient (34%). 4W data (as of 31st July 2023) from the Shelter and NFI (SNFI) sector showed that around 21% of Rohingya households reached with shelter assistance. (TSA, SUM, R&M, MTS, HH level site improvement). This highlights the pressing demand for heightened support and additional resources to meet the shelter needs of the Rohingya refugees, totaling 10,10,298 individuals, residing in the Cox's Bazar camp areas.The challenging combination of adverse weather conditions and the effects of climate change has made the shelters susceptible to heavy monsoons, flash floods, landslides, fires, and cyclones. Following government guidelines, these shelters are constructed using bamboo and tarpaulins, which need to be replaced annually, causing significant environmental impact. In an effort to reduce the strain on forests and extend the lifespan of bamboo, many shelter partners are utilizing treated bamboo. Nonetheless, sourcing high-quality treated bamboo remains a challenging task. However, it is imperative to explore alternative solutions that are weather-resistant and fire-resistant. Shelter partners must collaborate with the RRRC, ISCG, SCCCM sector to advocate for these alternative solutions in the camps, ensuring the provision of more durable and sustainable shelter options that can withstand the adverse environmental conditions and reduce the ecological impact. To effectively address the need for gender consideration, it is essential to include gender-responsive strategies that meet the specific requirements of women, men, girls, and boys. Women and girls often face increased safety and privacy concerns in overcrowded camps, especially in shelters where inadequate space and a lack of structural privacy can elevate the risks of gender-based violence (GBV). Therefore, enhancing privacy measures within shelters is crucial, ensuring the availability of private spaces and secure, designated areas for women and adolescent girls. Furthermore, gender-sensitive approaches in shelter design should take into account ventilation, drainage, and fire protection, particularly for women and children who may spend more time in or around the shelter. QC has coordinated with UNHCR (AOR focal point) to ensure there is no overlap with other ongoing efforts. 2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

QC is committed to mainstreaming protection in all Shelter activities, adhering to guiding principles such as non-discrimination, participation, and inclusiveness. In the implementation of Shelter projects, QC will conduct assessments to ensure that gender-related considerations are integrated, aligning with the updated minimum shelter performance standards. During beneficiary identification and data collection using the SADDD framework, specific barriers for Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) will be assessed. Shelters will be made accessible through consultations with refugees and relevant stakeholders, with adjustments implemented wherever feasible. A special emphasis will be placed on supporting all Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) with porter assistance, including shelter construction. To enhance awareness and vigilance in preventing Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), all staff members, including supplier personnel, will undergo two refresher training sessions throughout the project's duration. This comprehensive approach aims to ensure that Shelter activities are inclusive, protective, and aligned with established standards.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The proposed shelter interventions aim to establish safe and secure living spaces suitable for various age groups, taking into consideration child safeguarding issues. Qatar Charity will assess the vulnerability of children within the family and community, paying special attention to child-headed households, separated children, or those at risk. Special consideration will be given to female-headed households, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. QC will meticulously document data related to these vulnerable groups using the SADDD framework. It is anticipated that 12% of persons with disabilities (PWD) will receive shelter assistance based on the criteria established by the Washington Group on Disability. To ensure the inclusion of 12% of Persons with Disabilities (PwDs) and older persons in the shelter project, the following strategies will be implemented: 1. needs assessment and data collection, 2. accessibility of shelter, 3. awareness and sensitization, 4. participation and empowerment, 5. inclusive communication, 6. monitoring and evaluation.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Qatar Charity is committed to upholding and promoting compliance with the commitments outlined in the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) framework. Throughout the project lifecycle, including planning, implementation, and post-project phases, community consultations will be actively conducted. This engagement will take place through various channels such as house visits, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), and Key Informant Interviews (KII).To further enhance open communication channels, complaint boxes and festoons will be strategically placed in targeted communities, sites, and public spaces, with information presented in the local language. In addition, Qatar Charity will establish complaints and feedback desks at all distribution points, providing Rohingya families with a dedicated platform to share feedback and voice any concerns about the assistance they receive. This comprehensive, multi-level feedback mechanism will extend beyond shelter activities, encompassing various aspects of assistance, thereby enriching the overall effectiveness of the program.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In addressing Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) concerns, QC will adhere to the principles outlined by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) about Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, in addition to following the established guidelines of the Cox's Bazar PSEA network as required. QC is an active member of the PSEA network in Cox's Bazar and is committed to upholding high standards in this regard. As part of our commitment, QC will organize community-wide awareness sessions to inform beneficiaries about PSEA including tailor sessions for different groups, utilize multiple communication channels, engage community leaders and feedback mechanisms. additionally, QC will provide tailored PSEA training for staff, volunteers, vendors, and relevant third parties. Each group will receive an initial training session followed by a refresher, and all participants will sign a Code of Conduct (CoC) for the duration of the project to ensure they are well-informed and committed to preventing PSEA.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project incorporates several Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) elements, considering the historical threat of climate change and disasters in the area. In response to the needs of the population, the shelter project will utilize treated bamboo and metal footing. This approach aims to prevent the poles from being affected by insects, thereby extending the lifespan of the poles, reducing reliance on natural forest resources, and minimizing solid waste at the camp level. Qatar Charity (QC) will collaborate with site management to implement household-level site improvements, including guide walls and drainage around the shelter, to enhance the safety of beneficiaries. Additionally, QC will organize awareness sessions on climate change, fire safety, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) measures, and the safe use of tie-down kits, bracing, and cooking area safety. These protective strategies will enhance the resilience of shelters against cyclones and other hazards, reinforcing the project's Disaster Risk Management (DRM) approach. This will ensure a more robust and effective response to potential disasters and adverse weather conditions.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project includes several measures for Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR). These measures are designed to ensure that the shelter support program can effectively adapt to and respond to unforeseen events and emergencies, such as heavy monsoons, flash floods, landslides, cyclones, and other natural disasters that are common in the Teknaf area. Business Continuity Plan (BCP): The project will develop a business continuity plan in collaboration with CiC, Site management, and other shelter partners to maintain essential shelter operations and distribute critical shelter materials during disruptions. This includes clearly defined roles and responsibilities for staff and partners to ensure minimal interruptions in shelter support services. QC will adhere to the SCCCM and ISCG risk analysis for the shelter location. Additionally, QC will coordinate with the CiC, site management agency, BRAC, and UNHCR to mitigate any potential risks. For emergency response and evacuation, QC will follow the SCCCM and ISCG guidelines and SOP, ensuring close coordination with the CiC, site management agency, BRAC, and UNHCR.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project actively engages youth aged 18 to 24 as part of its shelter support activities. Young people participate in various roles, including community awareness and shelter maintenance. Specifically: 1. Awareness and Education: Youth are trained to conduct awareness sessions within the camps, focusing on safe shelter practices, fire prevention, and emergency preparedness. 2. Shelter Construction and Site Improvement: Young individuals are involved in shelter construction and site improvement tasks. They receive basic training in repair techniques and support with upkeep tasks, such as reinforcing bamboo structures and securing tarpaulins. 3. Skill Development Opportunities: The project includes skill-building workshops that teach youth construction and maintenance skills, which can be beneficial for future employment opportunities. By involving youth, the project strengthens community resilience and fosters a sense of purpose and contribution among young refugees. This engagement helps them actively enhance the safety and quality of shelters in their community.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The Shelter Support project actively promotes self-reliance and resilience among Rohingya refugees through various initiatives that equip them with the skills and resources needed to improve their shelter conditions and adapt to difficult circumstances. Key aspects of the project include: 1. Skill Development in Shelter Maintenance: Refugees receive training in essential shelter maintenance techniques, such as tarpaulin repair and structural reinforcement. 2. Promotion of Environmentally Sustainable Practices: The project encourages the use of treated bamboo to minimize the need for frequent replacements and prevent forest resource depletion. Through these efforts, the project enhances the capacity of refugees to maintain and improve their shelters, fostering a more resilient and self-sufficient community that is better equipped to face ongoing challenges.
