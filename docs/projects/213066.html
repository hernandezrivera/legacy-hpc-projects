Project Summary

UNFPA will continue to reduce maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality, support IPC during the protracted phase of the humanitarian response in Syria, provide family planning (FP) commodities, provide medical assistance and care to GBV survivors, and provide the needed information, micronutrients and supplements for Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in the most vulnerable rural areas in all Syrian governorates. This will be achieved by (1) ensuring the availability of, access to, and utilization of integrated quality SRH services and information for PLW; (2) building the capacity of health workers and CHWs; (3) reactivating and activating services in areas where no health and nutrition services exist; (4) ensuring availability of essential supplements and micronutrients; (5) ensuring delivery of complementary comprehensive services to detect early malnutrition ; and (6) strengthening referral mechanisms and improving Health and nutrition coordination.
UNFPA will continue to follow the Nutrition Sector (NS) approach, with implementation from Damascus targeting all 14 Syrian governorates. Vulnerable population groups targeted include women and girls of reproductive age, IDPs, host communities, returnees, adolescents, and PwDs.
Key objectives for 2024-2025 include:
• Increasing access to lifesaving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health & nutrition services for the most vulnerable and in need persons.
• Increasing access to primary healthcare by providing essential primary health services to cover the nutrition needs of PLW and repairing facilities.
• Improving access to secondary and tertiary healthcare for malnourished pregnant and lactating women (PLW), and acute cases which cannot access treatment in their area, particularly rural areas.
• Supporting access in areas with service gaps through integrated mobile teams and the midwifery network in Rural areas in all governorates with focus on NWS and NES.
• Building the capacity of health workers and CHWs on nutrition principles, referral to treatment, and to address any technical gaps.
• Strengthening and consolidating supply chains, ensuring availability of safe and quality medicines for essential primary healthcare, micronutrients, ferrous sulfate and folic acid.
• Pre-positioning emergency supply stocks of supplies in line with contingency scenarios for reduced access and increased hostilities.
• Strengthening health and nutrition coordination, emphasizing enhancing protection, increasing access to health and nutriton services, and continuing coordination within and across the country, including thorough joint contingency and preparedness planning.
• Engaging in activities that support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, respond to IDP movements and shifts in control, and beneficiaries’ participation in programming.
• Adapting, updating, and adopting the national curriculum for non-physician clinicians, including nurses and midwives, with the MoH, and developing training packages for academic and practical training related to PLW nutrition status, which can be subject for potential accreditation.
• Continuous monitoring of implementation based on the five AAP principles and UNFPA’s global results-based monitoring framework. UNFPA staff or third party monitors and IPs will jointly conduct monthly supervision visits, subject to access and approvals. Standardized tools including the supported services and supervision checklist are used to assess service availability/quality. Clinic registration data will be collated monthly. Beneficiary feedback (complaints box, FGDs, exit interviews) will be collected.
