Project Summary

In line with our organizational Core Commitment to Children in Emergency, UNICEF will continue to provide the population of South Sudan especially children, pregnant and lactating women, with quality preventive, and curative services as well as vaccine preventable outbreak response activities. The Scale up to new emergencies - outreach and mobile clinics enabled UNICEF, through direct implementation as well as in partnership with Implementing Partners (IPs), to provide high impact lifesaving emergency response in hard-to-reach and conflict affected areas but this intervention stopped before COVID-19 pandemic. UNICEF will ensure availability of core pipeline vaccines with all accessories and cold chain supplies to respond to vaccine preventable disease outbreaks.
This project aims to increase access to quality equitable essential primary health care interventions through scale up to new emergencies (through - Outreaches and Mobile Clinic) and providing a core pipeline to increase access to quality equitable immunization services by vulnerable populations including refugees and returnees, Internally Displace Persons (IDPs) and host communities in conflict affected areas across the country.
The activities to be implemented are:
• Procurement and distribution of vaccines for emergency responses
• Procurement, distribution and maintenance of cold chain equipment
• Technical support for improved vaccine and cold chain management
• Conduct scale up to new emergencies through - Outreaches and Mobile Clinic
• Procurement and distribution of LLINs
• Procurement and distribution of Health Emergency Kits, Clean Delivery Kits and items for diarrhea (IEHK2017 basic unit kit and Supl.1-medicines kits, AWD Periphery Kit drugs), malaria and Pneumonia prevention and treatment.
The expected beneficiaries of core pipeline supplies are 700,000 vulnerable children aged 6 months-15 years of age (348,699 Females; 351,301 Male) living in camps, PoCs, formal IDP camps or host communities in conflict affected areas, and refugees and returnees. The children will benefit from emergency vaccination to sure up their immunity to vaccine preventable diseases. Vaccines against measles, and traditional childhood vaccine. UNICEF will also support repair and maintenance of the damaged cold chain equipment and devices as well as replacement of the non-repairable equipment to ensure the continuity of the vaccination services.
The curative consultations will be provided for an estimated 28,000 children (13,972 female; 14,028 male) during the emergency response scale up through Out. These will include children aged 6 months-15 years who will be reached with immunization services and curative consultations and pregnant women who are expected to benefit from ANC services, tetanus toxoid vaccination, curative consultations and the provision of long-lasting insecticide treated nets (LLITN).
Under the IDP response through Outreaches and Mobile Clinic; provision of Emergency Maternal and Newborn care will prioritize provision emergency health kits for obstetric emergencies during curative consultations. Also mainstreaming GBV referrals and strengthening the referral pathway for Psychosocial first aid.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face severe health crisis due to multiple factors. About 8.9 million people in the country need one or more forms of humanitarian assistance, especially in the counties most affected by floods, conflicts, IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons), returnees and disease outbreaks. The health system in South Sudan is unable to cope with the emergencies and provide life-saving services. It relies heavily on international aid and faces many challenges, such as lack of funding, staff, medicines, and equipment. The people suffer from hunger, violence, and poor health. Women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities are the most vulnerable groups. They have limited access to health care and face high risks of death and illness. (Health Cluster)A total of 53 per cent of the people in need are children, 24 per cent are women, and 23 per cent are men. The capacity to provide general health services (in terms of having basic amenities, equipment, IPC standard precautions, diagnostic capacity, essential medicines) continue to be limited. According to Ministry of Health and WHO health facility functionality system 2020, 7.5 per cent of the 1,763 reporting health facilities provide basic health services. Damage of cold chain equipment continue to be reported from the States, in 2020 and 2021 the State cold chain store was damaged, the equipment was spoilt because of the floods and cold chain equipment were also vandalized in some health facilities. This makes access to health services difficult for the large proportion of the vulnerable internally displayed person and host community members. Overall, health service operation in South Sudan is very expensive due to poor road networks, necessitating the use of costly charter flights to transport supplies to some locations. During the dry season, whilst more road transport routes are open, the likelihood of ambushes and looting is very high which makes transportation of supplies by road very difficult. Meanwhile, rains during the wet season render approximately 60 per cent of roads in the country unusable. Routine immunization coverage for Penta 3 is at 73 per cent in 2022 and much lower in former conflict affected states at about 49 per cent. Even though 71 per cent of the facilities provide immunization services, only 40 per cent offer the services daily and 28 per cent offer the services weekly (SARA 2018). The low immunization coverage predisposes children living in IDPs camps and refugees and returnees in transit settlements, to vaccine preventable diseases such as measles, pertussis, and diphtheria disease. From January to October 2023, a total of 6,387 suspected cases of Measles including 524 confirmed cases and 149 deaths (CFR of 2.3%) have been reported from all ten states across the country. 68.7 per cent of the cases are children below 1 year and 4 years of age and 7.6 per cent are children of 15 years of age and above. The situation alarming despite Measles Follow up campaign conducted in May 2023 during which 92% (2,386,290) of children 6-59 moths of age were vaccinated. The routine MCV1 coverage in 2022 was at 72%, hence the weak delivery of routine immunization services in some areas, and admin results seen in the 2023 SIAs coverage put South Sudan at risk of large measles outbreaks. South Sudan is in the African Meningitis belt; hence it is prone to seasonal outbreaks, in 2018 about 107 suspected meningitis cases and 28 deaths were reported. Cholera outbreak ended after one-year long transmission in 2017, four counties and three POCs are identified as hotspots for cholera transmission hence vaccination needs to be given to the population to prevent cholera outbreak. Against this backdrop, refugees and returnees from Sudan, people in hard-to-reach areas, conflict-affected areas with relatively periods of stability and access, a key strategy for delivery of lifesaving health services is the use of Outreaches and Mobile Clinic.
