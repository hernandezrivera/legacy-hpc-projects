Project Summary

The action aims at strengthening health care facilities (HCFs) preparedness and support resilience building in the face of protracted threats and chronic vulnerability especially in terms of WASH and Health. This project is aligned with the WASH allocation strategy, which aims to “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and suﬃcient WASH services with dignity for the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank”. WASH in HCFs is prioritized as a necessary input to achieving all global and national health goals especially those linked to Universal Health Coverage (Sustainable development goal SDG 3), plus a specific target (3.9: to reduce the burden of disease from unsafe water, sanitation and lack of hygiene). Other targets are 3.1 and 3.2: call for reducing maternal mortality and under-five and neonatal mortality. All of which are directly impacted by WASH conditions in health care settings. The action is in line with SDG 6 aims to ‘ensure available and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all’ and includes targets for universal access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene for all by 2030 (targets 6.1 and 6.2). The project responds to UNICEF strategy 2016-2030 that every health facility should have safely managed reliable water, sanitation, hygiene facilities and practices that meet staff and patient needs by 2030. Furthermore, this project will contribute to the Strategic Objectives 2 and 3 of the Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRP) 2019-2020, by enhancing the provision of quality basic WASH services and improving the WASH facilities in health institutions. This project will work for mitigating threats and improving preparedness for potential outbreaks. Effective sanitation, hygiene and infection prevention measures are one of the five objectives of the WHO Global Action Plan on antimicrobial resistance. Lastly, this project will enhance the resilience of vulnerable people increasing their access to safe, affordable and reliable WASH services inside the most vulnerable health institutions in Gaza. The proposed project envisages rehabilitation of the WASH facilities inside Al Nasser medical compound in Gaza city. The specific objective is to enforce sustainable improvements in the WASH infrastructure and services in Al Nasser Medical Compound. The project will target four HCFs in Al Nasser medical compound: 1) Al Nasser paediatric hospital (NPH), 2) Rantissi specialized hospital (RSH) 3) Ophthalmic hospital (OH) 4) Psychiatric hospital (PSH). The project will improve the access of quality WASH and Health services for an average 280,000 people served by the four hospitals annually as per the health cluster HeRAMS Dashboard including 53,000 girls, 55,000 boys, 85,000 women and 87,000 men as well as contribute to improving the public health conditions of more than 652,597 living in the catchment area. The priority needs and interventions of the project were built based on key informant interviews (KIIs) with personnel in MoH and the four hospitals conducted jointly by WW-GVC, Qatar Red Crescent (QRC), Juzoor for Health and social development (Juzoor) according to gender and protection approach. The needs include: 1) improved access to safe water through rehabilitation of the aged water infrastructure upgrading the water systems including wells, desalination plants, networks, storage and others 2) Rehabilitation of sanitation services for staff and patients as well as old wastewater networks in NPH, OH and PSH, and stormwater drainage system, 3) Rehabilitation and Upgrading of hygiene and environmental cleaning infrastructure including installation of medical waste collection area, 4) capacity-building activities targeting health and non-health providers on WASH, WASH in Emergency preparedness and response and infection prevention and control (IPC) with provision of essential supplies, equipment.

1 - Needs

The health system is struggling to cope with the ongoing influx of conflict-related trauma casualties each week, as part of the Great March of Return (GMR) while infrastructure, human resources, and the referral system remains only partially functional after 12 years of blockade while the recent acute emergency events have required prioritization of emergency interventions (Health HNO 2020). In 2019, PWA has witnessed a deterioration of the WASH infrastructures in institutions, mainly in public HCFs as well as in schools (WASH HNO 2020). WW-GVC in cooperation with UNICEF and ECHO fund conducted a WASH assessment in 2019 targeting 21 MoH run HCFs in the GS. The assessment highlighted critical deficiencies in WASH services and infrastructure, which endangers the quality of health services, especially for immunocompromised patients. The assessment enforced the needs of sustainable improvements in the WASH infrastructures inside HCFs with the need to establishing a WASH in health capacity building and monitoring system. Key informant interviews (KII) with key personnel in Al Nasser medical compound’s four hospitals were conducted in September 2019 by WW-GVC, QRC and Juzoor to agree on priority emerging WASH needs. NPH and RSH Hospitals are the main pediatric hospitals providing general and specialized services for Gaza Governorate as well as a referral hospital from North Gaza and Middle area serving an average of 108,000 cases (53,000 girls, 55,000 boys) annually including Neonatal Intensive care unit (NICU), Paediatric ICU, haemodialysis and the only paediatric oncology center in the GS noting that women are the only companions of children in the hospitals. OH is the only specialized hospital serving an average of 132,000 cases annually (64,920 women and 67,080 men mainly chronic patients with non-communicable diseases). Following the GMR, the hospital became overwhelmed with face and eye injuries which constituted around 10% of all injuries. PSH is the only hospital providing inpatient psychological services for an average of 5,000 cases annually (2450 women, 2550 men). Adequate WASH conditions in the four hospitals are fundamental to reduce the preventable causes of morbidity and mortality by minimizing the risk of health care-associated infection, improve staff satisfaction, patient care-seeking behavior, especially for immunocompromised patients. The priority needs to be addressed is taking in consideration the special needs of staff, patients and companions mainly women, children and PWDs as follows:(i) Unsafe drinking and domestic water due to poor water quality (high salinity) which is eroding the aged water infrastructure and damaging the desalination plants beside the non-availability of regular water quality monitoring system. Lack of water connection for the central firefighting system in RSH jeopardizes the safety of staff and patients and was evident in July 2019 when fire suddenly broke into the adjacent incinerator unit. (ii) Lack of segregated and enough toilets for staff and patients, women and men as well as non-availability of adapted toilets for PWDs and children in any of the 4 hospitals. 60 years old waste water networks in NPH, OH and PSH are causing frequent flooding and clogging in the system beside lack of proper storm water drainage system in the yards of the PSH and NPH. (iii) Poor hygiene and environmental cleaning infrastructure in the OH and NPH especially in inpatient departments, daily care department, outpatient departments with lack of designated patient waiting areas. (iv) Lack of safe and protected designated areas for medical waste collection and storage in any of the 4 hospitals which is of high public health concern especially for hospitals’ neighborhood. (v) Lack of WASH Emergency preparedness and response plan and regular capacity building programs for health and non-health providers on WASH and IPC.

2 - Activities

The project intends to improve access to WASH Services inside key health public health institutions in Gaza governorate through enforcing sustainable improvement in WASH infrastructure inside Al Nasser medical compound. The 4 hospitals in Gaza target an average 280000 people (53000 girls, 55000 boys, 85000 women and 87000 men) served and contributes to improve the public health conditions of more than 652,597 living in the catchment area. At the start of the project, a detailed needs assessment will be conducted in cooperation with QRC, Juzoor and MoH in order to prioritize and confirm needs and specifications. WW-GVC’s vast experience on implementing WASH and capacity building projects will target the four hospitals through: 1) Upgrading water systems services in NPH, OH and RSH including Rehabilitation of 3 water wells (installation super duplex vertical pumps), rehabilitation of the main water networks with internal separation and installation of one new desalination plant with a capacity of 60 m3 in NPH, a complementary to WHO fund to support the other 3 hospitals with 160m3 desalination plant, taking in consideration international and national standards for brine water management. The intervention will include reinstallation of water connections for the central firefighting system in RSH in addition to firewater pump and Rehabilitation of the ground water tank. 2) Rehabilitation of sanitation services in the 4 HCFs including maintenance of 20 already existing toilets and construction of 4 new toilets. The works will take in consideration the clear segregation and equipping of toilets to meet the needs of women, men from staff, patients and companions as well as the special needs of PWDs and children as recommended by the JMP. The intervention will include rehabilitation of the storm water drainage system inside yards through readjustment of slopes and installation of galleys. 3) Construction of 4 safe and protected medical waste collection and storage zone for the 4 hospitals with provision of personal protective equipment. QRC is a leading INGO in health and WASH services and will be responsible for implementing interventions to address gaps in hygiene and environmental cleaning conditions through rehabilitation or construction of 48 new hand washing basins and/or alcohol-based hand rub wall mounted dispensers. This will also include necessary rehabilitations inside the inpatient, daily care, pharmacy and pharmaceutical store and outpatient areas with provision of patient beds, companion´s resting chairs, ventilation, paintings, essential supplies and equipment to maintain the IPC standards including Capacity building of 20 health and non-health key staff (10 women and 10 men) on WASH in health Emergency preparedness and response. Juzoor is a leading local NGO as the first American Heart Association (AHA) licensed International Training Center in Palestine with its sub center in the GS (Al Hayat training center in the Islamic University). Under this project, Juzoor will be responsible for capacity building of health and non-health staff on IPC targeting the 4 hospitals’ staff through training and on job coaching for 511 staff (women and men). The activity will establish a regular based auditing system through assigning and training focal persons in each hospital. Advocacy for WASH in Health inside HCFs will include hosting an event for 5th of May (the world hand hygiene day) targeting health and non-health staff. The intervention will be including development of the necessary IEC materials and a video film to document all the phases of the project from the design till the final stage and advocate for the WASH in health. In the initial phase of the project, coordination will be established with the WASH in health working group which is led by PWA and facilitated by WW-GVC and WASH cluster with technical support from the MoH and WHO to ensure coordination, monitoring and following up the implementation of the activities

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

A wide range of project groups, local authorities, local partners, project staff, beneficiaries should have the chance to provide their feedback in relation to any project activity they are taking part in. Context analysis before deciding on a particular feedback mechanism was conducted taking in consideration the literacy and barriers to access different mechanisms especially for women and children. The project will be implemented inside MoH run Hospitals and will involve many stakeholders from the design to the implementation to the phase out and even supervision. Sensitization of target beneficiaries to the purpose and process of Feedback Mechanism (FM) is essential to build confidence and overcome fears about giving feedback and allow time to build trust in the mechanism. Based on this, the best communication channel is selected through complaint boxes. It is important to inform the targeted groups on how the FM works, in terms of availability, template(s) to capture the data, focal points and the handling of the information collected. transversal considerations regarding gender mainstreaming and protection are considered in all the phases of the mechanism. Beneficiary feedback supported real-time adaptation of projects to the needs of their target groups and contexts, and accountability of programmes and service providers. Sufficient scope in the programme design will be ensured in order to make changes and respond to requests to increase or reallocate resources; negotiate with the donor if necessary. Moreover, feedback is intended to be integrated with monitoring systems, given careful consideration to how feedback will be analysed and aggregated and the capacity of staff and systems to do that.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The do no harm principle is assured by the whole implementation of the project to prevent any negative impact of the action and guaranteeing the safety and dignity for the beneficiaries. WW-GVC, QRC and JUZOOR will ensure the addressing of the needs of the beneficiaries since the first phase of in depth assessment, considering special needs for women, girls, boys, elderlies and special patients. All the activities proposed will be designed according to JMP standards and will meet the needs of the patients of the hospitals, guaranteeing a positive effect on the quality of the service and an improvement on the condition of the beneficiaries themselves. The action will enhance the privacy and safety trough segregation of toilets between men and women and securing doors, having a great impact on reduction of risk of GBV cases. The installation of safe and protected medical waste collection and storage zone will ensure the proper storage of medication and will reduce the risk of infection inside the clinics. Furthermore, the action will consider different needs, priority and cases in the provision of medical equipment guaranteeing items that match with meaningful access and that respect the cultural values of the PwDs, children and caretakers, women and men. Besides, training and awareness session will be tailored based on the result of the initial in-depth assessment to address the main problems risen up by the same beneficiaries. Medical and non medical staff will be trained on WASH in health Emergency preparedness and response plan and IPC training and on job coaching to guarantee the sustainability of the action and empower the capacity of the medical complex to cope with emergency. Save of the environment will be considered with proper protective measure as the rehabilitation of the fire central firefighting system and the stormwater drainage system. Upgrading water systems services rehabilitation water network and installing desalination plant will ensure clean water to all the patients decreasing the risk of hygiene infections and the presence of back up source during emergencies.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

WW-GVC, QRC and Juzoor has conducted a thorough context analysis in order to prioritize the targeted interventions that will impact the lives of the most vulnerable population in the GS. The chronic emergency situation has shifted the humanitarian needs to prioritize interventions to respond to the emergency on the expense on other vulnerable groups: people with chronic diseases including children, women and PWDs other than trauma patients. Based on the WASH assessment conducted by WW-GVC in partnership with UNICEF in 2018, 80% of the HCFs doesn’t have adapted toilets for PWDS and 100% doesn’t have adapted toilets for children even in pediatric departments. While the clear segregation of toilets between women and men (patients and staff) with a signage was not ensured in more than 80% of the assessed toilets. Such specification is representing the WASH in health sanitation core indicator for the WHO-UNICEF joint monitoring program. The continued deterioration of WASH facilities in HCFs raises serious concerns about resources, the ability to respond and to deal with emergencies, which have serious consequences on the health status of patients, especially children, women, the elderly and PwDs. The rehabilitation of water network plus the installation of the desalination plant will ensure clean water with a consequent positive impact on beneficiaries’ health in terms of morbidity and mortality and on cost of services due to prolonged hospital stay. Thus, the proposed project was designed to ensure the improvement access of women, men, girls, boys and PWDs to WASH service inside the hospital. The new standards will meet the core indicators for WASH in Health as defined by the WHO- UNICEF joint monitoring program taking in consideration the necessary needed interventions to improve access of women, children and PWDs to WASH services. Also, the needs assessment on which the project was built took in consideration the needs of women staff and staff with disabilities (who represent 2% of the staff working in the hospitals) regarding the availability and number of sanitation services including to construction of new sanitation services. The action also takes in consideration not only the patients but also the companions who are in the majority women to ensure safety and dignity during the treatment journey and hospital stay through rehabilitation and upgrading of the hygiene and environmental standards inside the inpatient and outpatient areas. Active participation of women staff and patients will be ensured in all the capacity building activity and community empowerment trough a feedback mechanism and by several key informant interview to detect the different needs, role and dimensions of the targeted beneficiaries.
