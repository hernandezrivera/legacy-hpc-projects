Project Summary

Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 200 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. PUI seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the local capacities, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs.
Through this project, PUI seeks to improve the food security, nutrition status and sustainable livelihoods of the conflict affected population (IDPs and vulnerable host communities) in Borno State. PUI will support 63,901 individual beneficiaries with food assistance on a monthly basis for 12 months. The monthly disbursement will cover 70% of the food security needs, as per sector guidance, and will be divided into 50% food items to be retrieved from selected vendors and 50% to be received in cash. 1,500 households’ beneficiaries will be supported with a start capital and training to launch an Income Generating Activity; this activity will promote self-reliance and reduce dependency on humanitarian assistance. Each beneficiary will receive 250 USD, and then he will be supported and trained to create its IGA.

1 - Needs

PUI’s strategy in Nigeria follows an evidence-based, integrated approach with the proposed intervention aimed at enhancing the overall living conditions of affected persons - both vulnerable host communities and IDPs – through complimentary Health, Nutrition, MHPSS, Food Security & Livelihoods and Protection activities. In Bolori II, Maiduguri, PUI seeks to transition to more sustainable programming by promoting self-reliance– while continuing to meet the acute needs of the populations. Recent findings show that an estimated 7.9 million persons in North East Nigeria were in need of urgent humanitarian assistance in the beginning of 2020, out of which 3.7 million were food insecure. Measures to contain the spread of Covid-19 outbreak such as lockdown as well as intra and inter-states movement restrictions have caused the loss of livelihoods for households depending on daily wages, and thus increased the number of food insecure households. Recent projections estimate that the number of persons in need of humanitarian assistance may reach 10.6 million while the food insecure may rise to 7 million in North-East Nigeria. The “Cadre Harmonisé” of March 2020 estimates that 1,431,620 persons are in IPC phase 3 (Crisis) and 4 (Emergency). In Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC), a study recently conducted by IMPACT estimates that 80% of IDP households and 52% of host community households in Maiduguri live in extreme poverty with less than 377 Naira (1.04 USD) per day per person, as defined by the National Statistics Bureau (NSB, 2020). The IMPACT study also reveals that 48% of IDP households and 18% of host community households reported not having enough money to buy items from the food Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB). This highlights the fact that these households cannot afford the 224 Naira (0.6 USD) per day per person necessary to cover the cost of the average daily per capita caloric allowance, as estimated by the NSB in 2020.According to the food security and livelihood needs assessment conducted by PUI from 27th July to 5th August, 2020 in Bolori II, Covid-19 pandemic accentuated vulnerability for Bolori II populations by negatively impacting their social and economic situation and their sources of income. For instance, the pandemic negatively impacted teachers, casual labourers and traders’ sources of income due to the lockdown (school closure, impact on the informal sector, etc.) as well as movement restrictions. Furthermore, because of the lockdown associated with Covid-19, people with lower socioeconomic status including the IDPs and host communities have faced greater challenges in accessing services such as health, education, and infrastructure. From the assessment, access to food is the top challenge for 68% of surveyed households. For 42% of surveyed HHs, their incomes are insufficient to cover their basic needs. Findings of this assessment also revealed that only 13% of the households had received unconditional cash transfer from NGO (including PUI Cash Project), NEMA or other actors, during the previous six months. Access to food was identified as the main difficulty being experienced by the community (68%). In addition, lack of access to land by a majority (70%) of the surveyed population depicts limited capacity to practice farming which would provide important source of complementary livelihood.Given the context and persisting needs of the population, the project will address the lack of food as well as lack of start capital and skills which are major challenges hindering beneficiaries to launch small business.

2 - Activities

Livelihood restoration: PUI aims to assist 426 households (4000 individual beneficiaries) to restore their livelihoods through Income Generating Activities (IGA). These selected households will be supported to rebuild their livelihoods based on their prior skills, experience and motivation in their livelihood of choice. Beneficiaries will attend a business skills training course covering the following topics: marketing a product or service, estimating costs and prices, managing finances and bookkeeping. After the training, awardees will receive a cash grant not exceeding 250 USD per individual through e-vouchers modality aligned with the productive assets’ lists developed during the business planning stage. Out of the 426 households targeted for this activity, 300 households (2820 individual beneficiaries) will be upgraded former beneficiaries of food assistance. Youth who graduated from vocational training programme implemented by the National Directorate of Employment (NDE) will compose the second caseload of 126 HHs (1180 individual beneficiaries). In addition to the training and start capital, these 4,000 households will receive food assistance up to 5 months to protect the provided capital and allow the beneficiaries to establish their small business. Food security: the improvement of food security will be achieved through a 12-month program of cash and food e-voucher. Beneficiary targeting will be determined through consultations with local stakeholders including community committee representatives and leaders, partner NGOs and the food security sector as necessary. PUI will follow a mixed (qualitative and quantitative) targeting approach based on needs and vulnerability criteria (Coping Strategy Index, Food Consumption Score, HHS and HH Diversity Diet indicators) to identify beneficiaries. All beneficiaries validated will be issued with e-voucher cards and all beneficiaries who receive an e-card will attend sensitization session about the amount of assistance he/she is entitled to, how to redeem his/her entitlements, how to provide feedback about the programme in general and most particularly how to raise and channel complaints to the Complaints Response Mechanism. Food assistance will be provided on a monthly basis to 63,902 individual beneficiaries regrouped in three caseloads. In the regular caseload, 49,482 individual beneficiaries selected among IDP and Host Communities (HC) through food security criteria will receive 12 monthly disbursements. In the protection caseload, 11,600 individual beneficiaries selected through protection criteria will receive 12 monthly disbursements. Finally, PUI will select 2,820 individuals newly arrived IDP in Bolori II that will receive up to 10 months of food assistance.The monthly disbursement will cover 70% of the caloric needs, as per Food Security Sector guidance, and will be divided into 50% food items to be retrieved from selected vendors and 50% to be received in cash. The assistance package is in-line with the Food Security Sector (FSS) per capita recommendations. In defining the items included in the food basket, several factors where considered including beneficiary preference, availability, seasonal price fluctuations and the ease with which households can prepare the items in the food basket considering the available cooking fuel. Beneficiaries will redeem their food voucher and cash at partner vendors who have shops in the targeted communities in order to reduce working distance for beneficiaries while also allowing beneficiaries to buy their food at their own pace and convenient time. PUI will use Red Rose platform to allow cash transfer to the beneficiaries’ e-cards and to monitor the redemption of the cash and food voucher. Beneficiaries will be notified via SMS about the top up of their e-card

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming, and the do-no-harm approach, the intervention will:- Utilize e-vouchers as a mechanism to reduce security risks associated with direct cash- Include a protection caseload that addresses food insecurity resulting from protection risks or issues. This includes marginalized communities, individuals whose situation has suddenly deteriorated, and at-risk children and women. The assistance to this group aims to mitigate protection risks and to reduce impact of protection issues.- Integrate community participation within the design of the intervention at various project phases especially design of assistance package and modality, beneficiary targeting, and vendors selections. - Ensure that all staff, workers, and vendors participating in the implementation of the program are trained on protection mainstreaming, and are accountable to PUI’s core policies including PSEA, Child Protection, and the zero tolerance policy on fraud and corruption. This is ensured through mandatory core policy trainings.- Identify and address protection issues and complaints/feedback in a timely manner as captured by PUI’s Protection and Community Outreach team, community committees, and the MEAL unit through the complaints and Feedback Mechanism.- Set up an accessible and safe environment in which assistance is provided in order to protect beneficiaries from experiencing theft, sexual assault, robbery, intimidation or extortion Furthermore, through complementary grants, PUI will implement protection monitoring, ensuring the regular monitoring of the protection environment of targeted populations. On a quarterly basis, PUI will carry out quantitative and qualitative assessments within Bolori II community, including through surveys, focus group discussions and key Informant Interviews. Collected data will be analyzed to identify trends in the occurrence of rights violation incidents, the main types of protection concerns, and inform on service gaps. Protection monitoring findings will be shared with the Protection Sub Working Group and partners through quarterly reports. These reports will support PUI to develop local advocacy messaging targeting Civil Society Organizations, ministries, international NGOs primarily through the protection sector. Individual cases identified during protection monitoring activities will be timely assisted and referred to complementary services. Furthermore, community-level awareness sessions will be conducted on different identified topics in order to foster the emergence of a protective environment in Bolori II Ward.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PUI has a strong mechanism in place that relies on various channels. Feedbacks are mainly captured through: (1) Community Committees Meetings that are organised once a month with community leaders, elders, women and PUI program staff to discuss project activities, gaps and needs; (2) Community Volunteers Network which comprised all outreach workers who directly link PUI to targeted communities; and (3) PUI Complaint Mechanism which consist of helpdesks in the community and a toll free helpline managed by dedicated trained staff.
