Project Summary

In line with the WASH cluster objective #1 and country strategic objective #2, the action aims to ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services with dignity for the un-served, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank through ensuring basic WASH services for conflict-affected, un- or under-served and vulnerable populations and institutions in the Gaza Strip. The proposed intervention responds to the SDG #6. SDG6 aims to ‘ensure available and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all’ and includes targets for universal access to drinking water, sanitation and hygiene for all by 2030.
The project aims at reducing the public health and environmental pollution risks that may occur as a result of wastewater percolation from existing cesspits in the target locations in Rafah area. It will benefit nearly 12000 people of which 47% are women in four locations in southern part of Gaza namely in Al Salam Neighborhood South of Rafah, Msabbah Neighborhood North-East of Rafah, Al Fourkan Neighborhood in Rafah and Junian Neighborhood in East of Rafah area. Total number of beneficiaries in each location can be listed as follows: Al Fourkan 6000, Al Salam 1700, Msabbah 1600 and Junian 2700. Moreover, the project will indirectly benefiting all people in the target four locations through direct pollution prevention of their areas and also prevent groundwater pollution.
According to the information obtained from CMWU and PWA during the coordination meetings organized with them on 25 and 26 September 2018 respectively, these information were updated again on 29 September 2019 in coordination with PWA, CMWU and area focal point in Rafah (Islamic Relief – IRPAL) and they identified the four locations listed above which are in need of sewer system to connect more households in order to avoid flooding of wastewater to neighborhoods and to coastal aquifer. The need of each location can be summarized as follows: Al Fourkan needs 3000m UPVC sewer line and 100 concrete manholes and connecting 250 households. AL Salam needs 500 m UPVC sewer to connect 40 households while Msabbah and Junina needs 500 m each to connect 30 household in each one of them. Accordingly, the project will contribute by supplying and installing 4500 m of UPVC sewer lines, 150 concrete manholes and 350 house connection. Moreover, the project will also provide awareness and capacity building for local communities on best hygiene practices and will provide capacity building for municipal, CMWU and PWA staff (both male and female) on the operation and maintenance of wastewater sewer lines and wastewater operation and maintenance in general. The project will contribute also in closing down all the cesspits in the target locations to stop further pollution of aquifer and prevent public health risks, hence, reducing ensuring no harm principle.
The project design has considered gender mainstreaming and also mainstreaming of protection principles including meaningful access, safety and dignity. PHG will adhere to the protection principles throughout the implementation of the project and will develop protection monitoring, vulnerability mapping and impact assessment to ensure project compliance with these principles.
PHG will also implement the project and will be responsible for its overall management and reporting toward the donor. PHG will ensure the quality implementation and management of project in accordance with its ISO9001 - 2008 standards and will also ensure the timely implementation of the project in an efficient manner within the budget limit and will allocate relevant qualified human resources to implement it properly.

1 - Needs

PHG has carried out several meetings with relevant stakeholders including CMWU, PWA, area focal point in Rafah (Islamic Relief – IRPAL) and Municipalities to identify the needs and to prioritize the areas where intervention can be made. Both CMWU and PWA has recommended the four locations to focus on.Following to this recommendation PHG has then conducted a rapid needs assessment in these areas where it found that more than 80% of the household in the target locations are not connected to wastewater network and they use cesspits in most cases. In addition, PHG has made several interviews with Municipal Staff both male and female and also with households who are not connected to the wastewater system and they still have cesspits where women also have been interviewed for their opinion about the problem resulted from the overflow of wastewater from the cesspits and how it impacts their health and livelihood. Most people interviewed, especially women emphasized the needfor connection to wastewater network in order to avoid any pollution threats to their houses, property and also negative impact on their and their children health. Moreover, they indicated that they obliged to pay nearly $25/ month to pump the wastewater and that is also huge economic burden on them under the current difficult economic conditions. The need to address this critical issue is confirmed by PWA where it was reported in their study recently released that more than 31% of households have reported the presence of diarrhea cases where 56% of reported cases were children under five years old. It was reported that more than 40% of the reported cases was related to poor water quality where it was found that nitrate level in the water is almost 20 times higher than recommended level of WHO (45 mg/l) in some places where it reached 800 mg/l. As a result of the assessment which was conducted in the southern areas in Rafah and khan younis localities particularly for this project, different needs were identified and prioritized by the PHG staff ,CMWU and the municipalities representatives , which is considered as urgent and are requested immediately in order to improve the deteriorated situation in the localities, the main issue raised was the need for wastewater infrastructure improvement in the targeted localities listed as the following : 1- Upgrading and improvement of Al Forkan sewer system –Rafah , in which more than 6000 locals will benefited directly for the project. 2- Upgrading and improvement of sewer the systems at Al Salam, North Rafah, Msabbeh and Junina neighborhoods’ in Rafah, in which more than 6000 locals will benefited directly from the projects 3- Enhancing and enriching knowledge and experience of staffs of municipalities/CMWU as well as locals in the targeted areas in water and wastewater filed in which expected that more than 2200 persons will benefited form the CB and the awareness program.

2 - Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated and coordinated with the below parties: - CMWU, local municipalities and OXFAM to avoid and overcome work duplication, as well this cooperation and coordination will be maintained and improved at implementation stages at all levels with all stakeholders and organizations. The approach of implementation for this project will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women , municipality , local committees , the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any(local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the field particularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning . the design of the systems will be done under the supervision of PHG , and expected to be supported by CMWU ,PWA ,Funder representative, and member form the community whom will be as members in a steering committee of 3-5 persons , they will be updated/reporting back in periodically meeting by PHG engineer / head of office /representative for the progress and obstacles faced through implementation , and recommendations form next periods , the SC will support and help to find solutions to improve work and progress . the tendering process will go through the PHG procurement system , procurement plan will be designed for this purpose. The M& E officer at PHG , will follow implementation stage by stage through the M&E module and will insure achieving outputs and will measure indicator on regular bases . The main activities that will be implemented under the project which will target the neighborhoods of Alforkan, Al Salam,North Rafah, Msabbeh and Junina neighborhoods in Rafah can be summarized as follows: 1- Supply and installing of about 4500 meters of UPVC sewer pipelines with different diameters, 2-Supply and installing of about 200 concrete manholes with different sizes in the targeted areas 3-Supply and installing of about 350 house connections the targeted areas. 4-Conduct of capacity building and awareness campaigns in which 50 workshops focusing on different related subjects in water wastewater, hygiene management and knowledge. The aim of this activity is to address the problems identified under the needs assessment which is related to poor water quality and water borne disease infections especially for children as well as those obtained from the women and men interviews during the assessment.

3 - Indicators

The indicators are: - 2,150 households in the targeted areas are connected to a sewage network. - Waterborne diseases affecting children under 5 years old and women in the targeted areas is reduced by 15%. - Knowledge of 2,200 locals / staff including women on WASH related issues is improved. - 4.5kms of sewer pipelines, 150 manholes and 250 house connections are installed in the targeted areas. - 50% of cesspits (125 cesspits) are closed in the targeted areas. - The financial burden required to emptying the cesspits will be reduced by 300$ a yr. - Public health and personnel hygiene for 2,150 families (12000 people of which 5,640 is women) is promoted. PHG will conduct risk and community assessments prior to project implementation to verify each indicator of the project to ensure cost-effective and timely project implementation. Moreover, PHG will conduct interviews with relevant stakeholders, municipalities, CMWU and PWA staff for verifying baseline data and baseline indicators. End line surveys will be also conducted following project implementation in order to measure the impact of the project on targeted communities especially the impact on women and children. The results will be reflected in the final project report. Data on monitoring progress to realize the outputs will be secured from the weekly and monthly technical progress reports as well as financial reports that will show project expenditure and compare it with progress. Management will be informed in case of any deviation from the planned targets to enable them from taking timely action to modify plans and make needed consultation with donor and other relevant stakeholders in case there is any big deviation. Moreover, monitoring progress will be ensured through adequate reporting on the established activities and indicators in the project document by using information from the following sources: a) Baseline data collected and reported. b) Data from feedback and complain mechanism created by the project to ensure compliance and accountability c) Monitoring data coming from direct monitoring and follow ups from frequent monitoring field visits conducted by PHG teams. This includes all information, observations that would be gathered during these visits, will be documented , and processed according to PHG internal result monitoring system. d) Inputs and feedback from project Committee meetings on the overall implementation of the project. PHG will document all information, observations that is gathered during these meetings and use it to ensure better informed decision making and project planning and implementation. e) Gathered data segregated by gender and analyzed to assess the levelof project impact on gender and to make sure that target number of female beneficiaries are met during the implementation. f) End line data collection and analysis to report on the project impact on the target beneficiaries. g) Monthly meetings with local stakeholders to follow up on the implementation of activities and to address any potential obstacle during implementation. These tools together with baseline and end line data analysis and KAP assessment performed by PHG project team will be used to derive activity monitoring and reporting to management, donors and stakeholders on monthly, midterm and final project reporting periods or as established in the project agreement. PHG team will liaison with all stakeholders including WASH cluster, PWA, CMWU for any arising issue during implementation and will take proper actions to address these problems. Finally PHG will ensure timely reporting both narrative and financial to donors and relevant stakeholders as per the project agreement and will ensure that reporting quality is met through its quality management system. This will be ensured through the Monitoring and Evaluation officer who will follow up with project manager to ensure compliance with the quality and timely reporting.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PHG aim with all of its projects is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. In conjunction with this policy, PHG has ensured these practices by focusing on female heads of household, empowering the most vulnerable of families. By doing community outreach, PHG is able to help normalize female participation in public spaces. By providing home visits involving female evaluators, women can feel more safe in their own homes.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposed activities were developed through regular contact with beneficiaries and recent needs assessments, in coordination with the Palestinian Water Authority, CMWU and Area Focal point in Rafah (Islamic Relif- IRPAL).. In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected population, PHG is engaging households in the intervention design. As women and girls in sanitation bear most of the burden of carrying, installing sewer systems and connections to the household level, their involvement is crucial to effective WASH and sanitation projects. Women also have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children. Moreover, inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Community-based WASH Committees and PHG staff will ensure that the installing of the sewer systems in Rafah will include adequate safety, dignity and Do No Harm.The approach of implementation will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women, municipality, local committees, the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any(local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the fieldparticularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning .During the awareness sessions PHG team will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as a “women issues”. In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actually engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level. PHG staff and project committee role throughout the whole project will that that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of eachindividual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female farmers, female-headed households, households with people with disabilities).Beneficiaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized by PHG staff and project committee on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practice
