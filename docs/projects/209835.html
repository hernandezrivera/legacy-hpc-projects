Project Summary

Muzun will provide shelter rehabilitation services for 350 shelter units, serving an estimated number of 1750 participants (family size 5). This service will be conducted through a combination of direct implementation and through contractors. Muzun will target IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host community households, living in undignified shelter units; these include damaged and unfinished shelters that do not meet Sphere minimum standards and have a level of damage G1 and G2 as identified by the Shelter Cluster. Participants will be selected using a vulnerability scoring system, ensuring that the inclusion of PWD, the elderly, the chronically ill, pregnant or lactating women, and orphaned children (from another family) is an essential part of the selection process. In addition to considering the economic status and family status (host/IDP). Muzun will work with clusters, other NGOs, Local Councils, and community leaders to ensure the needs of these vulnerable populations are addressed in the shelter rehabilitation activity. In parallel with the vulnerability assessment, Muzun’s field engineers will conduct technical scoring for each shelter unit, targeting damage levels of G1 and G2. Meanwhile, a Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) verification process will be held in coordination with Local Council and community leaders.
Furthermore, Muzun will hold orientation sessions for local council and community leaders that will provide an overview of the project stages (announcement, participant registration, assessment, participant selection, implementation, and closeout) to ensure effective communication with the targeted communities and establish a collaborative and participatory approach from the onset of the project.
Muzun will also ensure equal access to safety, community participation, and feedback, application of protection mainstreaming, and monitoring of protection risks in shelter rehabilitation activities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Muzun plans to rehabilitate 350 shelter in sub-district Maaret Tamsrin. The community selection process will consider both the vulnerability factors and security situation in coordination with the Shelter cluster. The program will be publicized to the community upon selection, communicating duration, project selection criteria, and CARM channels in coordination with the local council. A variety of registration methods will be available, as direct registration, referrals, and local council data. Through the process, Muzun will make sure that participants are well aware of how to fill out forms and prepare other required supporting documents (identification documents). Furthermore, preliminary participants will be asked to provide their HLP documents, which will be checked by the local council, community leaders and Muzun’s HLP specialist; in case the HLP status of the shelter is not confirmed and if the owner has not provided consent for the works, the shelter will immediately be considered ineligible to the project.The participant households will be considered eligible if: 1) they live in the areas of operation; 2) inhabit a shelter consisting of damage level G1 and G2; 3) have Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) verification; and 4) meet the vulnerability criteria. In addition, the affected household intends to live in the shelter for at least one year from completion of repairs. Only participant households meeting the eligible criteria will move to the next stage.The type of documents identified that can be used for the verification of identity and ownership may include a passport, birth certificate, national identity card, family book, property title or deed (Tabou), land registry record statement, court document/decision, power of attorney, lease contract, etc. If these documents are not available, in some cases key community witnesses can be used to verify identity and/or ownership. Muzun will start a door-to-door assessment where teams will be composed of both males and females. The assessing field engineers will evaluate the overall physical structural status and integrity (columns, beams, structural cracks, spalling, loss of material, holes in walls and slabs, etc.) and verify if the shelter does not exceed Grade 2 on the damage scale.Family vulnerability status will be assessed according to a set of criteria which includes families led by a single breadwinner (parent), the elderly, children, teenagers, families that include pregnant/lactating women, the elderly, and disabled or chronically ill members. also consider families that host other families from the same community or displaced families (crowded homes), large families of eight or more members, and families with very low or unstable incomes. During the participant identification, technical staff will create an estimated BoQ and Scope of work with the needed supporting documents for each shelter unit with the needed rehabilitation work.
