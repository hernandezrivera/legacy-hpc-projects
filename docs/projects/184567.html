Project Summary

According to the WHO-UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme (JMP) 2020, 37 per cent of people in rural areas have access to water supply and 40 per cent of existing water sources in rural areas are non-functional. This is attributed to weak water supply management models, high operational and maintenance costs, and lack of spare parts and technical limitation of service providers. Groundwater provides 80 per cent of the domestic supply, but due to high water salinity , water is of poor quality. 39 per cent of vulnerable people in Somalia do not have access to sustained, improved and safe water source for drinking and other domestic uses, while 84 per cent still walk for more than 15 minutes to the nearest water source. Lack of water containers is a barrier in accessing water with 92 % households not having access impacting their regular access to water. An estimated 1.3 million people in 24 drought hotspot locations hugely depend on water trucking during drought due to frequent breakdown or and dry-up of strategic water sources. Consequently, majority of vulnerable population in these priority locations survive on less than 7.5-15 Lit/per/day. Communities in rural areas are now looking at alternative water sources to support rain water harvesting to counter the challenge on water salinity issues in Several districts in Nugaal, Banadir and Gedo regions
Generally the IDP population remains the most vulnerable impacted by multiple challenges with links to WASH i.e Malnutrition, GBV related risks when accessing services and facilities due to distances covered (toilets being more than 50 m from the shelters and water collection points beyond 500m), Poor hygiene and sanitation. Internal rapid assessments by NCA affirm these challenges. Current drought conditions have exacerbated the situation with government through state administrations , calling for humanitarian support. Pastoralists are relying on water trucking, moving away from their traditional habitats in search of water and pasture. Districts in target locations rely on trucked water from sources located 20-120 km away due to drying up of traditional water sources. Reinfestation of locusts has also been reported in areas of Puntland and Gedo following short rains experienced and are a general threat to the well being of the affected communities.
WASH response will address life saving needs of disaster affected groups with rights deficits on lack of/inadequate access to clean and safe drinking water of appropriate quality and quantity, health risks resulting from poor hygiene and sanitation practices. Emergency water trucking will be considered as a life saving option.WASH facilities will be rehabilitated/constructed in communities and institutions as part of durable solutions. Facilities will adapt to meet community preferences as part of adapting to local context, with gender and disability considerations included to reduce GBV concerns as well ensure leaving no one behind. Gender-balanced WASH committees will be supported to maintain facilities. Solar-powered water systems will improve access and reduce costs as environment friendly solutions. Participatory Hygiene promotion approaches will promote knowledge improvement. WASH installations will use CFW as appropriate to inject cash into communities and markets. Where feasible, restricted CVA (following market assessments) will allow choice of hygiene items to ensure access to preferred MHM/ incontinence items. Emergency guides/ tools for rapid onset crisis (WASH emergency stocks, Sphere, cash toolkit) will be adopted for implementation. Links with WASH cluster will provide comprehensive services using nexus approaches, where appropriate, to promote resilience and building back better using DRR modalities in the design of facilities. Community Engagement, Inclusion and Accountability are some of the frameworks that will inform this response ensuring that Feedback (CRM) provided is actioned and used for improvement

1. - Projects activities

According to JMCNA 2021, an estimated 6.2 million people need emergency lifesaving WASH assistance where 4.2 million need safe drinking water, 3.2 Million on sanitation services and 5.2 million require hygiene supplies. 16 per cent of these are females, 64% children and the elderly for 4 per cent. Severe drought condition, seasonal flood, diseases outbreak and conflict related displacement are contributing to the increase in the number of people in need. Rural areas and hard to reach districts have shown a significant increase in the number people in attributed to increasing vulnerability due to limited access to water and sanitation among rural areas and IDP sites.The project will ensure safer access WASH services for 43,300 people (8660 men, 10825 women, 10825 boys and 12990 girls) through; community mobilization and engagement sessions and considering feedback from needs assessments. Through this process, the intention is to rehabilitation of 16 berkards, Rehabilitation/maintenance of 3 strategic borehole in Daynille Gedo and Nugaal with 30 water points and elevated water tanks or 40m3, construction/ rehabilitation of 10 shallow wells in Gedo solarization of 4 water systems in Nugaal and Daynille. The training of water committees and provision of operation and maintenance tools will also be undertaken in this location with upto 30 Water committees targeted. Construction and rehabilitation of the water systems will be done Through CFW modalities in as much as possible through Red rose system where possible and mobile banking. Conditional and restricted transfer modalities will be our main approached in cash-based programming. Use of vouchers will be used during emergency water trucking exercises targeting parts of Nugaal and Gedo regions as part of accountability. WASH facilities will be constructed with to ensure access for all including PLWD. During Cash activities Vulnerable groups of the elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers will be consulted on how best they can be supportedOn sanitation rehabilitation of 6 twin latrines for health facilities and 10 in schools and rehabilitation /construction of 200 emergency latrines including provision of 500 solar lamps will be undertaken in Gedo, Nugaal and Banadir as part of improving beneficiaries protection environment. Construction of 15 solid waste management pits will be done to uplift environmental sanitation in project locations. The latrines will be lockable both inside and outside to improve security and privacy for users and adapted to people with disabilities Hygiene promotion through distribution of 3000 hygiene will be undertaken through use of vouches while hygiene messaging will adopt use of mass media including door to door visits. Covid messaging will remain integrated in hygiene promotion to ensure that gains made are maintained. Ensure women, men, girls & boys have enough knowledge to protect themselves against health-related risks especially Acute Watery diarrhoea by: Establish & train hygiene clubs in school; Men, women, boys and girls including people with disabilities will be consulted on their preferences to encourage use of facilities upon completion. As part of Building back better, our structures will be constructed with DRR consideration to ensure their resilience to climatic conditions. Age and disability will also be considered to ensure that our WASH facilities are accessible to all in the spirit of leaving no one behind. We shall as much as possible work in collaboration with the cluster, partners and Government stakeholders as part of the localization agenda in planning, implementation and monitoring of project initiatives in target locations

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

According to CaLP (Cash Learning Partnerships) using Cash or vouchers gives beneficiaries the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities. In allowing beneficiaries to control what goods and services their households need; cash transfers are considered by many as more dignified than receiving direct distribution and recognize beneficiaries as active participants in providing for the welfare of their families after a disasterThe availability of cash gives households a sense of restored power over their immediate situation. In addition, there is evidence that receiving cash can at least temporarily empower women within the household. Access to financial structures such as bank accounts may empower beneficiaries, in particular women, in the longer-term as well.Injections of cash or the use of commodity vouchers with local traders benefits the recovery of trade and the local economy. Cash-based support for livelihood rehabilitation can potentially also provide a link between relief and longer-term economic recovery and may offer the opportunity for beneficiaries to increase income and create savings.Most households depend on cash to some extent. When aid is given in kind, households are often forced to seek cash in ways that may be detrimental to longer-term livelihoods, for example selling productive assets, or giving children to the care of relatives. Evidence shows that cash transfers reduce the negative coping strategies used by beneficiariesBy stimulating local markets, cash-based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend.When goods are available and accessible locally, cash or voucher-based interventions have the potential to be more rapid than bringing supply from out of country. When beneficiaries use local markets, agencies also eliminate logistical and import costs. In the WASH programme CVAs will be used in ensuring access of communities to basic needs including meeting WASH outcomes on access to water supply, sanitation and hygiene. CFW interventions are also aimed at reducing dependency and strengthening community’s ability to be self-sufficient to some extent in supporting interventions that enhance their access to services. Cash Transfer Programming will be used as a delivery mechanism using local service providers and will support local supply of financial services.Cash for work activities will promote employment opportunities that will enhance household abilities to meet their basic needs. NCA will scale up and use of its Redrose platform to manage MBP/CVA interventions where possible. Use of mobile transfers will also be considered on case-to-case basis.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

In August 2020, NCA participated in the Joint market monitoring initiative that has largely informed this response in terms of what activities can be linked to CTP. Internal rapid market surveys have been undertaken to determine commodity prices but this is subject to change with the changing market trends resulting from disruption of normalcy pegged on climatic conditions and conflict displacements limiting access to markets in some area as a result of floods/conflict etc. NCA is also a part of the Cluster led forum on MBP with the aim to share experiences, learning and improve generally cash programming across cluster partners

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Joint program planning, implementation is central to this intervention. The involvement of the communities strengthens their ability to manage the project and ensure that needs prioritized locally are responded to. Empowering/capacity development of the different committees will ensure increased management and sustained access protection services. Through the partnerships, the different and complementary roles of local authorities, committees and local population will be strengthened. This together with building the capacity of the committees will enhance community development in a manner that communities are responsible of their own needs. At the end of the project, community capacities and coping mechanisms are expected to be strengthened on different fronts, constructed/rehabilitated infrastructure will remain in place, and hopefully, capacities for peace and stability will be enhanced Include gender sensitivity analysis in all assessments; integrate gender sensitive perspectives into NCA's staffing policies and routines; include gender considerations in partner assessments - in terms of both partners' capacity and routines; engage all stakeholders in target intervention areas to ensure women's and girls’ inclusion in the initiative; include women and girls in a meaningful way into NCA and partners activities; establish necessary mechanisms to avoid increased exposure to discrimination or exclusion based on gender. NCA has a good standing with communities where we work. On the basis of this, NCA has a fair understanding of the current sensitivities surrounding ‘conflict prevention’ in Somalia. Recognizing these complexities and sensitivities, NCA has developed risk mitigation strategies that have been utilized to enable project implementation. First, NCA employs national staff and works with partners who understand the local context and are well known and respected by local authorities. NCA national staff can access locations that non-Somali nationals cannot access, hence ensuring continuous project implementation and monitoring even in challenging contexts NCA also works with locally based partners who are located within the communities we serve. Further, NCA also employs remote monitoring systems to follow up and monitor projects where physical access is a challenge. Second is a deliberate approach to project branding: selecting language that will explain the project fairly and accurately, in a way that will be acceptable to stakeholders. Third, is an approach of open, ongoing stakeholder engagement involving NCA/partners, local communities and government. Regular security updates will be sought from International NGO safety organization, NCA and partners’ security teams to ensure that staff are well informed on security situation for necessary precautions and arrangements. NCA also has a complains handling mechanism in place that will give beneficiaries the freedom to raise concerns they may have on the project. DO no Harm considerations are also put in place to ensure that no further harm is targeted on the already vulnerable groups being supported as part of our interventions
