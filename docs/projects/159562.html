Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the 2018-2020 HRP Strategic Objective 2 ” The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law” and Strategic Objective 3 ” The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The project aims at providing emergency support and winterization assistance to vulnerable Bedouin communities living in the Gaza Strip. Bedouin communities are among the most vulnerable communities living in the Gaza Strip. Most of them live in temporary houses built out of metallic sheets which are not suitable to stand harsh weather conditions. They lack basic infrastructure and services, such as electricity, water and sewage networks. With the help of the local partner, PARC, the project will increase the access to safe and secure shelters for vulnerable Bedouin households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns in the Gaza Strip. It will enhance the households’ capacity to cope with the risk of deterioration of living conditions by improving their shelter standards. In total, 450 HHs will be assisted with winterization assistance and NFI. A total of 2,673 beneficiaries (2,565 people befitting from shelter intervention, 90 volunteers and 18 Community-based Protection Committee's members) will benefit from the project.

1 - Needs

The Bedouin communities are considered as one of the poorest and most marginalized communities in the Gaza Strip. Many of these communities are not registered with the municipalities, hence, do not receive any kind of public service. Moreover, no previous detailed needs assessment or baseline study have been conducted to address the living conditions of the Bedouin communities living in Gaza. In October 2019, ACTED and PARC conducted a rapid assessment aimed to identify Bedouin communities, collect information about their shelter conditions, social and economic status. After reviewing secondary data and conducting KIIs with chieftains of tribes, the assessment identified 9 Bedouin communities in Gaza: Um Al Nasser “Al Qaraya al Badawiya al Maslakh” (North Gaza); Al Malalha Bedouin in Al Zaitoun, Al Moghraqa Bedouin, Johr Al Diek Bedouin (Gaza governorate); Al Sawarh Bedouin, Wadi Al Salqa Bedouin (Middle Area); Al Nemsawy neighborhood Bedouin, Al Fukhkhari Bedouin in Al Fukhkhari (KhanYounis); Al Sekka Bedouin (Rafah). The assessment team conducted 3 Focus Group Discussions; the first meeting was organized with 7 of the community representatives in Um Al Nasser, among those 7 interviewees the Chieftain of the tribe was present, the second event was with 7 of the community representatives of Al Moghraqa Bedouin, among them the previous Mayor of Al Mughraqa, and the Chieftain of one of the biggest tribes in the area, The third FGD was held with the heads of 6 Bedouin households from 3 areas (Rafah, North Gaza, Al Moghraqa). A household-level questionnaire rolled out with a sample of Bedouin households in each community. In total, 140 households have been randomly selected and assessed. The total number individuals of the assessed households is 1,155, with average of 8.25 member per household; 363 (31.4%) adult males, 367 (31.7%) adult females and 425 (36.7%) Children. Among these, 20 PwD “person with disability” (14%) and 5 female-headed households have been identified. The data analysis showed that 99.2% of these HHs live below the extreme poverty live and 44% do not receive any UNRWA assistance as they are not registered as refugees. 72% of the surveyed HHs are living on governmental lands, 24.2% are living on their owned land and 2.8% are on rented lands. In terms of shelter condition, 57% of the assessed housing units are rickety and in bad condition, 62.8% have WaSH facilities which do not meet the minimum standards and 61.4% use nylon and other materials to close the windows while the rest do not have aluminum and glass windows. Finally, 96% of the HHs are in high need of new sleeping mattresses and reports lack of winter covers. Finally, all the visited HHs suffer from rain leaking in winter and make use of nylon sheets or abandoned rooms of the housing unit which have huge leaking. All the participants of the Focus Group Discussions emphasized the urgent need for winterization intervention. In terms of protection, 78% of the HHs have no doors and they lack privacy and protection means, especially for children and women. 100% of the participants reported the absence of services and utilities for PwD in the surrounding areas. Through this assessment, ACTED and PARC have identified urgent and emerging need in terms of shelter rehabilitation, winterization and NFI assistance as well as protection and privacy measures for all the family members.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 -Establishment and capacity building of 1 CBPC and volunteers network. 1 Community-based Protection Committee (CBPC) will be established. The CBPC will be formed of 18 representatives from Bedouin communities. It will be supported by a network of 90 volunteers who will participate in announcing the launching of this project to targeted communities, and will provide support in kits distribution and support the beneficiaries in the installation, maintenance and use of the items . The CBPC and the volunteers will be reinforced throughout monitoring visits, guidance and a training delivered by ACTED’s protection officer (protection principles, shelter reparation, capacity building). A referral mechanism to relevant stakeholders and organizations will be implemented in close coordination with the Shelter Cluster, for the households in need for other specific interventions. Activity 2 - Update and/or validation of the vulnerability and technical assessment. ACTED and PARC will reach out to households heads through announcement in order to select the most vulnerable households. ACTED and PARC will announce to the targeted communities the launching of the project and its objectives. The implementation process will be displayed, and the ACTED Protection Officer, CRM and CBPC operations will be detailed. ACTED and PARC will prepare the Bill of Quantities (BoQ) using the assessment and households visits to confirm the assessment outputs. Eligible households will then be examined according to a clear set of technical and socio-economic vulnerability criteria. The team who will conduct the visits will include ACTED and PARC engineers, the Protection Officer, members from the CBPC and volunteers. Activity 3 - Technical capacity building to the selected HHs. PARC engineers will provide on-the-field capacity building trainings to female and male households’ representatives in order to maintain their shelter and use efficiently the NFI items, with the support of the volunteers and the CBPC. Women, elderly and PWDs from the targeted household will be highly encouraged to participate to enhance ownership on the action. While using the Shelter minimum standards, the technical capacity building will emphasize the urgent shelter needs of households and will be tailored as much as possible in order to foster the household’s engagement and ownership. All the 450 households will be trained during an individual visit by the civil engineers and will be provided with technical advice. This will increase the households’ resilience and protection in which risk reduction and coping strategies are integrated to improve the emergency assistance. Activity 4 - Provision of winterization assistance and NFI kits. 450 vulnerable HHs will be provided with winterization and NFI kits in order to enable them to ensure durable protection of their shelters against adverse weather. It will allow the selected HHs who live in critical conditionals and in shelters which cannot be upgraded to minimum standards, to carry out a slight emergency winterization maintenance and to receive NFI kits, especially those items which can expose them to harsh weather and protection risks. The targeted HHs will be provided with NFI to meet the additional burden of the impact of the disaster on the local economy and access to such items. NFI will include, but not limited to – tarpaulin, rope, nylon, mattresses, blankets, winter covers, LED light systems, hygiene kits, winter clothes and any other item or tool the household is in urgent need of. Emergency assistance will help assisted HHs to improve their living conditions, reduce potential displacement, lower the risk of exposure to extreme weather conditions and decrease threats to the security of children, women, elderly and PWDs. Following the upcoming assessment, ACTED will coordinate with the Shelter cluster to ensure that the modality of assistance is the most adequate.

3 - Indicators

Indicators are selected based on clusters' indicators. In addition to those, ACTED and PARC will measure the following additional indicators: % of assisted children/ women/ PWDs/elderly reporting less concerns about safety and security at the end of the intervention (Target 80%; Means of Verification: baseline and end-line assessment); % of HHs reporting that the winterization assistance/NFI is appropriate to mitigate the risk they could be exposed by natural hazards (Target: 80; Means of verification: satisfaction surveys with representative sample of the HHs receive NFI kits); % of female members of Community-Based Protection Committee (CBPC); Target: 40%; Means of Verification: CBPC ToR and list of members, Training attendance sheet); % of CBPC members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the refresher capacity-building training (Target: 80;Means of Verification: Pre-Training Assessment (PTA), Post Training Evaluation (PTE)); % of the hardship cases and shelters need major rehabilitation, referred activity (Target: 80; Means of Verification: referral database). Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects and anticipate and mitigate any challenge. The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. In particular, ACTED will conduct a baseline and endline satisfaction, and draw lessons learned from the project. Those activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and to measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups (female-headed households). Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. M&E mechanism will have two main points of action ensuring complementarity. ACTED AME Officer will be responsible for supervising and monitoring the ‘’hard components’’ throughout the development of the action. ACTED’s AME Manager will be responsible for supervising and monitoring the ‘soft components’’ throughout the development of the action. The monitoring will focus on the respect of shelter protection principles and minimum standards, as well as the accountability through the already established and functioning Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM).

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be firstly ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including Palestine. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of the activities. Community members will be informed and trained on CRM in order to share informationwith the community and encourage beneficiaries to report any issues or concerns during the time of project and the drill. Several channels will be available to beneficiaries to file a complaint or raises concerns: through a CBPC member (who will then refer it to ACTED) and ACTED dedicated phone line open 24/7 (incl. via SMS/WhatsApp), dedicated email address, complaints box (within ACTED and governorate offices), AME monitoring visits. All beneficiaries of the action will be explained their right to provide complaints in confidentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance, and will be provided with small pamphlets summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth stressing that the CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, field paper forms, complaint tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanism, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries and non beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanism features several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBPC members, ACTED protection officer to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). ACTED analyzes protection risks, anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported and referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. Strong Monitoring and Evaluation and continuous communications with stakeholders, including community leaders and local authorities (with whom ACTED works with as well under other projects) as well as beneficiaries, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict. During the project, ACTED will raise awareness of the Community-based Protection Committee and volunteers about humanitarian and protection principles. The committee will be composed by 18 members from Bedouin communities. The committee members will also select 90 volunteers to be part of the project. ACTED will conduct an exhaustive needs’ assessment regarding Shelter needs in Bedouins communities. The needs’ assessment will be an essential tool to prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on affected populations. The volunteers will support the beneficiaries on the shelter maintenance and provide support for kits distribution. They will be also in charge of displaying Protection and Humanitarian principles in the framework of their participation in the project. ACTED solid experience in implementing similar Emergency projects in Iraq, Syria, Nepal or Haiti, enable teams to benefit from the capitalization of lessons learnt which contribute to building ACTED expertise in Emergency assistance and inconstantly improving projects’ design and implementation modalities. Finally, the establishment of a Community-based Protection Committee will develop and strengthen links to local communities, ensuring that feedback is shared and communicated to ACTED project team whenever new needs/concerns or implementation’s gaps are identified throughout the project. The 90 Volunteers will be trained on GBV, child protection,protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), BLS and PFA. They will focus on particularly vulnerable households, that may contain disabled or elders for example. Engagement and acceptance of community members is key component in the design, implementation and monitoring of ACTED projects. The project will be implemented in compliance with ACTED internal security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

A community-based protection approach, established by ACTED under previous projects, will be followed throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee a better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for highly vulnerable groups such as Bedouin communities. The kits allocation and distribution will be done on a needs-based approach. During the first stages of the project implementation, a needs assessment will be conducted in order to ensure that most vulnerable people and most urgent needs are being addressed. This will guarantee a safe, gender-integrated and impartial response. To deliver an equal response, the 90 volunteers will be in charge of displaying the protection measures. The goal is for vulnerable communities or people who would be the most affected by the crisis to be aware of this project and his objective. This will allow people to reach out in case of urgent need during crisis. These measures will ensure that the project addresses the most urgent needs and most vulnerable people. It will also allow to provide access to aid to disabled people and elders, by mainstreaming a protection-integrated approach.
