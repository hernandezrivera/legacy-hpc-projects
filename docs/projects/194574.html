Project Summary

The project will be aligned to Health Cluster objectives. It will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties, realizing an effective coordination and integration between development and humanitarian actions which will be functional to narrow the health care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most neglected groups. This will be done by proposing a range of different services provision modalities (static and mobile), expected to be able to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current health care coverage at all level of the health system and/or resulting from emergency events and therefore to respond to the essential needs of the target population, in particular of those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) makes them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging. For this purpose, health related activities will be integrated with those pertaining to the protection sector, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both clinical care and psychosocial support. In general, project impact will be maximized through a multisectoral approach, with the result of services being highly comprehensive, as addressing health conditions and the inter- related complications (ie malnutrition), and offering quality care, being informed by hygiene promotion and IPC principles, becoming the first setting for an effective prevention of the main infectious diseases. At this regard, the project will guarantee timely a2nd effective prevention, containment and management of outbreakes (COVID19, EVD). Surveillance and early warning will be strenghten, with the engagement and coordination of various stakeholders. In general, the project will promote the proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities and this will facilitate the selection and implementation of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable intervention options, focusing on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Local actors technical assistance and operational support will be one of the core strategies informing the proposed action, contributing to its effectiveness and to the quality of supported services and activities. The project is expected to reach 94722 people, out of whom 16% will be U5 children and 48% female, with at least 1% of patients accessing outpatients services being people with disability (456).

1 - Needs

LAKES STATE is considered priority humanitarian setting, with all Counties in severity level 3 and expected to degenerate to level 4 by mid2023 (IPCOct22). People in need are about 630.000 (PIN2023). Lakes is being heavily affected by flooding (in 2021, 4/8 Counties have been targeted by emergency interventions under SSHF) and this, coupled with the persisting high internal conflictuality, have hampered provision of and access to quality basic services and lead to noticeable migration, for more than 200.000 individuals (Mobility Round Track12). Matter of concern is IDPs burden over hosting families, whose resources are limited, because of raids/lootings and environmental factors. This is more concerning when considering that, due to the continuous fightings, the majority of households are headed by women, more exposed to violence and likely to be food insecure. This makes women and their children vulnerable to diseases, but inadequate HFs number, distribution, conditions (staff number and skills, equipment, supplies), with insufficient funding for sustaining routine services, fail in offering the due preventive and curative functions. Lakes health indicators are low (DHIS2 2021): ANC4 30% (ANC1 85%), institutional delivery below 30%, curative consultation merely reaching 1. Malaria/Diarrhea/Pneumonia are U5 main diseases, favored by poor WASH: households practice open defecation, do not have soap... In such conditions and given the low EPI coverage (even lower now that some antigens went repeatedly out of stock and COVID19 vaccination campaigns have disrupted routine vaccination), outbreaks have always been quite common, calling for State wide improvement of both prevention and surveillance sectors. The situation will worse further as soon as the main donors supporting MoH will step back or drastically reduce funds, leaving out of any kind of support even main referral sites, in spite of them being providing life saving functions. The reduction of resources allocated by the main donors left several communities without any HF in their area and the dropping out of State Hospital from main funding system put at risk system responsiveness to the main health related emergencies.Although often in lower emergency levels, WESTERN EQUATORIA STATE should be still considered a critical area, especially because often left behind by the main donors. In GREATER MUNDRI, HFs can now count on qualified personnel, but poor building conditions, lack of equipment and limited availability of drugs affect quality and continuous care (QOC quarterly report). Services demand is acceptable, with ANC1 being accessed by more than 80% of targeted population and number of consultations per persons going above 2, but utilization still low (not reaching 50% for ANC4), as well as institutional deliveries coverage (DHIS2 2021). Food security is at stress stage. Fragmented vertical nutrition interventions hamper malnutrition response (alert level). The unusually high prevalence of neglected tropical diseases (as Nodding Syndrome) and mental health issues (with epilepsy reaching almost 4%) result in a noticeable group of vulnerable individuals and households whose needs are not properly addressed because of lacking of resources on regular basis. Concerning mental disorders and disabilities, their data are not captured by the HMIS, understanding the size of the problem site by site and tailor the intervention to account for their necessities requires complementary data collection and monitoring activities. Few information on SGBV, mainly affecting women (97% of cases); although related data are now captured in the HMIS, victims do not speak up

2 - Project activities

Activities align to Health Cluster orientations to achieve its sectorial objectives and respond to the needs illustrated above. They will be implemented in selected PHC Facilities/Hospital and villages, agreed with Health Cluster, after needs assessment and discussion with beneficiaries. Priority will be given to those areas where existing services are not sufficient to cope with care demand, as neglected by common funding mechanisms or overloaded because of IDPs/outbreak/flooding/violence. Under SO1, the focus will be on the availability of basic life-saving services. Essential preventive/curative actions as per BPHNS will be provided at selected HFs and through mobile health team and community/boma health workers. Mobile/community based services will complement/replace static ones when HFs functioning/accessibility will be compromised by insecurity/adverse environment and to respond to outbreak/displacements. The project will link with the existing BHWs, expanding their geographic scope and sustaining their service delivery performance, with particular attention to health education. This, coupled with community based referral system and County wide free of charge ambulance service for obstetric/pediatric emergencies, should contribute increasing the number of people accessing the due attendance. The health-nutrition integration (GM, Screening, SAM complication treatment) will ensure the timely identification and response to the consequences in terms of malnutrition of wide food insecurity. PHCCs/Hospitals will be supported to provide BEmONC/CEmONC, as well as trauma care. All services will beusuable by PWD, offering them both generic assistance and tailored care to their specific needs. The various services deliveries modalities will make them more accessible, entry point for people with mental disorders and GBV survivors, receiving first aid (CMR, MHPSS) and referred to dedicated services, as per pathway locally defined. All this will be realized by addressing the main gaps reported above as hindering services provision: hiring/rewarding/training/supervising health staff and BHWs/CHWs; procuring/distributing essential drugs and supplies buffer stock, complementing what provided in kind by other actors; replacing old/ looted equipment and repairing damaged infrastructures to restore enabling conditions for services provision; ensuring transport means availability/running. CHD will conduct regular supportive supervisions to targeted sites. Under (SO2), focus will be on outbreak prevention, investigation/detection, integrated response. EPI coverage will be boosted, with static and outreach routine immunization and mass campaigns, as prevention and containment for infectious disesases. UNICEF/WHO will be advocated for regular antigens provision and cold chain maintenance. Link with WASH Cluster will promote IPC at community/facility. Surveillance continuity/consistency will be sustained (distribution of GL/tools, surveillance officers training/mentoring), strenghtening IDSR. In specific hot spot and circumstances (EVD or COVID19 alert), suspected/confirmed cases tracking and first line management will be done, as per National Task Force orientations, activating RRTs and focusing on case management (staff training and supplies provision). Under SO3, a multidisciplinary supervision/coordination mechanism for stakeholders’ concerted actions will be put in place. CUAMM will integrate the emergency intervention to its development program, without duplication, to promote synergies and optimise use of available resources, for services longer term provision. The quality of implemented actions will be guarantee with technical assistance and operational support to CHD, while services responsiveness/acceptability will be promoted engaging beneficiaries (community feedback mechanism). In general, disaggregated data collection and reporting will guarantee tailored implementation to address all age/gender/disability related needs

3 - Project indicators

The project will be monitored through the indicators proposed by the Health Cluster and other indicators jointly defined to ensure careful control of the specific mentioned activities progress and outcomes. The related data will be collected as much as possible through the existing Health Management Information System (DHIS2) and through already adopted integrative tools (as the Washington Group Short Set of Questions (WGSS)), avoiding creating parallel channels and increasing health staff workload but contributing to health system own monitoring and evaluation enhancement. At this regard, CUAMM Technical Assistance to targeted Counties authorities, staff and stakeholders will concern data collection/reporting/analysis as well, to promote prompt evidence based decision making process. Supportive supervisions will be functional also to verify the completeness and reliability of available data. For those indicators not found in the DHIS2 and specifically meant for project activities monitoring, CUAMM and Cluster will agree on the specific tools to be used, choosing an user friendly design to facilitate field team and health staff work. When possible, CUAMM will encourage data disaggregation by age and gender, for being able identifying eventual cases of unbalanced access and utilization of supported services. The disaggregation is being considered at project design phase as well; it is expected U5 to represent the 16% of all beneficiaries and female beneficiaries being the 48%, with U5 female being the 7% all beneficiaries and O5 female the 41%. The use of data collection tools meant to track people with disabilities access will help in disaggregating data considering their peculiar situation as well. It is expected PWD to be at least 1% of all those accessing outpatients services. Distinguishing between host and displaced communities is being challenged by the fact IDPs are often integrated withing residents households. At the time of implementation, CUAMM and Health Cluster will see the possibility to offer an even more detailed disaggregation. The proposed activities are expected to contribute to data production for each of the suggested indicators. Further, some of them implies the set up or strenghten of process for the collection of complementary information, both quantitative and qualitative, as those related to community feedback mechanism (interviews, questionnaire…).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CUAMM and CHD are already working to ensure the above mentioned Health Committees gender balanced and inclusive composition and attendance, making them as participatory spaces women and other vulnerable individuals can be part of, an empowerment driver, offering public engagement opportunity, usually very limited for certain groups, and making these latter aware of their own value, to be enhanced through a virtuous circle of increasing participation. Similarly, Review Meetings and community based activities will be open to CSO representing women, PWD, and other vulnerable groups. Boma Health Workers or other community based health providers will bring services as much close as possible to beneficiaries, minimizing access constraints faced by who is not in condition to freely move because of physica, social and economic dependance, as women and PWD and offering these people the opportunity to provide their feedback and inputs. The anonimous Clients Satisfaction Survey has been designed and it is administered in a way that makes it accessible to all kind of patients, indipendently on their education background (usually poor for the most vulnerable and neglected as women and PWD) and without requiring time wasting and further movements

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project will be driven through existing community consultation mechanism, to avoid overlapping and confusion and to contribute to strenghten them even further. The same mechanism will be used to get feedback and complaints, to make easier for the beneficiaries, already used to them, to give their contribute. CUAMM is used to maintain a constant dialogue with communities representatives, from those belonging to the Health Facilities related Health Committees to those from community based associations, which are invited to take part to the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level and which ensure a wider perspective on community needs and expectations and can report on services responsiveness and acceptability. The quarterly Client Satisfaction Survey offers the opportunity to get feedback on provided services by the direct beneficiaries. It is administered at service delivery site, meaning it is easily accessible, it is filled by literate volunteers so that it can be administered to any patient, without discrimination based on education level (which would have left behind the most vulnerable) and it has been piloted to confirm all questions being easy to understand and to be answered by people with different background. Finally, CUAMM is supporting local health authorities in managing County wide network of community based health providers (Boma Health Workers), operating at grossroots level, directly in contact with beneficiaries and therefore in the position to inform them about the project and to get their feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CUAMM has its own PSEA policy. The related communication strategy consists in integrating PSEA information dissemination into the ongoing activities aimed to sensitize people on Gender related matters, including Gender Equality, Gender Empowerment, GBV. These activities includes health staff and authorities training, community meetings, IEC materials production and events…activities which are part of CUAMM on going intervention and which will be included in the project as well, to reach a wider audience. Integrating PSEA information in this framework is expected to facilitate its understanding and to promote its acceptability. CUAMM PSEA policy is completed by a toolkit aimed to assist the field team in this process

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be collected through the above described community consultation mechanism, already existing and regularly used to get population perspective and perceptions on services responsiveness and acceptability. This consultation mechanism has been designed to get continuous inputs on the situation on ground and to be enabled doing timely adaptations of the proposed activities/supported services. As already used to do, CUAMM field team will assist the local health authorities in collecting, consolidating and analyzing the various feedback and to translate them into action points to be integrated into proposed project work plan and overarching strategy, making them more tailored and focused to respond to the needs and to address the issues beneficiaries perceived as priorities in that specific moment. This is also functional to ensure project constant update and alignment to the reality, narrowing the gap created by the time passed from project writing and project implementation

8.4 - Health services will be victims entry point to specific assistance (clinical, psychosocial, legal). CUAMM will sensitize and train health staff on how to identify and handle this kind of patients, offering first line care while encouraging their adherence to the following therapeutic steps. Health authorities will be updated on protection actors and supported in linking with them, establishing effective referral pathway for victims comprehensive attendance. CUAMM will advocate for Health authorities and staff to promote county wide sensitization and education on this matter, for violence episodes drop and victims stigma fear reduction, encouraging them to speak up and to look for proper assistance8.5. Marginalized are usually living isolated, fearing being harrassed while moving, with limited resources and therefore with few possibilities of getting assistance. Mobile modality for Clinical Services provision will enable them to access health care, close to their living places, free of charge. Community representatives/BHWs will provide Health authorities with information on these groups locations and status and for promoting their acceptance whitin local population. Right based approach will inform capacity building/training activities for health staff and authorities, for all patients being attended, indipendently on their belonging. Mechanisms for community involvement in health system are in place but members are mainly men, as per traditional habits. CUAMM and CHD will promote gender balanced composition of HF/Boma health committees through community wide sensitization. Women inclusion will create a virtuous circle of increasing awareness on the importance of all groups contributing to health system running. Health services enhanced accessibility will make women healthier and released from their caretaker role, enabling them to engage in public functions and tasks8.6. Proposed project complaint/feedback mechanism will coincide with that already in place and which targeted communities are already familiar with. Further, health authorities and staff will be advised and assisted in taking advantage of each contact with beneficiaries to ask and get inputs on activities/support responsiveness and acceptability. For instance, client satisfaction survey will be done at the same place/during the same moment people are getting services, in a way educational background will not constitute an obstacle to a wide participation in it. Further, community based and mobile service delivery will increase the number of contacts between health providers and beneficiaries and expand the area these beneficiaries belong to and therefore the possibility for them to offer their feedback will be enhanced. 8.8. CUAMM is working since several years with targeted Counties health authorities, providing the needed technical assistance and mentorship for the development of evidence based decision making culture and approach. This constitute a solid ground to prevent external interference in the identification of project beneficiaries, which will take place through a wisely conducted field needs assessment, with the direct involvment of local health authorities and community representatives and the use of validated tools, to be confirmed with the competent Cluster

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will contribute minimizing displacement by guaranteeing the provision of basic health services in the areas affected by emergencies events and therefore giving affected population a reason to remain on ground or at least addressing and narrowing some of the gaps that would lead people migrating somewherelse. Similarly, it will complement existing services, making them more responsive to inhabitants needs and therefore preventing them for looking for other places where to leave.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will support essential preventive and curative health services, promoting them as entry point for protection ones, especially for women and children and people with disabilities. The project will enhance health services accessibility, by providing them as much close as possible to beneficiaries, overcoming those geographical/economical/social barriers which are more penalizing for vulnerable people and groups; in this way, health providers will be in the best position to capture these latter and to facilitate their entrance in the essensial services net. Health staff will be trained to promptly identify patients with physical or intellectual impairment and/or who experienced GBV and provide them with the most suitable first aid, while arranging for their referral or for more structured treatment, according to facility/health provider capacities and competencies. The staff will also learn how and will be given the means to make health facilities as well as mobile health stations as much accessible and acceptable as possible, starting from infrastructural barriers reduction up to developing a right based approach in receiving and treating patients, creating an enabling and comfortable environment for those who are used or fear to be discriminated/stigmatized because of their conditions. CUAMM and health authorities will ensure the due coordination and cooperation with protection sector actors and stakeholders, creating structured and effective referral pathways for people reaching health facilities/being reached by health providers to have prompt access to all other services they are in need of for a comprehensive management of their conditions

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project does not directly address food security, however it will contribute to minimize its immediate consequences in terms of malnutrition and its complication. On one side, the project will support the provision of essential care as per Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services, therefore integrating, through staff training and mentorship and equipment/supplies provision, nutrition component main functions into the health offer, as growth monitoring and/or MUAC screening for children accessing OPD and EPI and for PLW at ANC/PNC and consequent referral to SAM/MAM treatment program. In this way, health services will become the entry point to nutrition services for malnourished children, who will be timely identified and transfered to receive the due care. Further, health providers will include nutrition related topics into the health education sessions program, contributing to malnutrition prevention. Finally, health authorities will get a good amount of data on patients nutrition status to take informed decision on how to improve it.On the other side, the project will guarantee children overall health improvement, promoting their access and utilization to quality preventive and curative health services and therefore it will make them less vulnerable to malnutrition. The already mentioned health education session will transmit and spread good behaviours and practices as those related to wash and hygiene, which are functional to protect from both water borne diseases and malnutrition
