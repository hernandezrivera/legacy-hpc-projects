Project Summary

The unique socio-economic conditions of blockade, with its movement restrictions and destruction of productive capacity, are further compounded by repeated cycle of conflicts and political instability and ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. UNRWA opened the 2021-2022 academic year with full face-to-face learning modality following the Ministry of Education decision, providing quality education to 291,100 students (140,492 girls and 150,608 boys, including 6,300 students with disabilities), but it is unclear if this approach would remain the same depending on the epidemiological situation and the measures taken by the local authorities on the ground. In order to ensure that no child is left behind and that access to education is available to all students regardless of their available resources under this circumstance, UNRWA will provide: (1) catch-up classes through remedial education interventions including supporting materials and back to school kits, (2) self-learning support to assist students’ learning experience at home, (3) application of preventive measures including school hygiene requirement to be compatible with the WHO and MoH regulations related to COVID-19, (4) support to special education for children with disabilities through Community Based Rehabilitation Centers, (5) structured recreational (after-school and summer) activities, and (6) mental health and psychosocial support to students and parents by experienced school counsellors through in-person and tele-counselling.

1 - Needs

In Gaza, the economic blockade, which entered its 15th year in June 2021, continues to push into deeper poverty and despair, compounded by the continued COVID-19 pandemic and recurrent hostilities. Since the COVID-19 outbreak in March 2020, the provision of adequate basic education has been largely interrupted due to the preventive measures adapted to contain the spread of the pandemic. Following the military escalation in May 2021, the schools remain closed until the end of the 2020/2021 school year. To mitigate the psychosocial pressure, poverty and environmental degradation affecting many children in Gaza as a result of conflict, UNRWA launched a summer school activity to strengthen their physical, mental and emotional well-being and to build their resilience. Of the 98,500 children participated in activities, 8.7% required some types of MHPSS sessions. The Ministry of Education decided to open schools with full face-to-face learning for the 2021/22 school year, yet it is still unpredictable if this decision will hold in 2022. According to the HNO 2022, in the previous academic year 2020-2021, 1.3 million children have lost at least three months of schooling and have not been able to engage in summer and extracurricular activities. On top of that, around 65 per cent of schools in Gaza have already been operating on a double-shift bases to accommodate increasing number of students, which resulting in reduced hours in core subjects and foundation learning (HNO 2022). The MSNA (July 2021) data shows that 31 per cent of the interviewed households in Gaza indicated having trouble meeting essential education needs due to the financial difficulties in the last 30 days prior to the data collection. The situation is further aggravated for children with disabilities. According to the PCBS, 20 per cent of the overall people with disability in Palestine are children of which 46 per cent between the age 6-17 years old are not enrolled in schools (HNO 2022). Furthermore, 25 per cent of households with children with disabilities responded that at least one child with disability was not attending school (MSNA 2021). For the rest of the children enrolled in education, they face higher risk of dropout, due to gaps in adapted facilities and assistive devices at schools, adequate transportation means, and staff capacity to accommodate for their needs. The MSNA (July 2021) data shows that 71% of the interviewed households with children with disabilities in Gaza reported that they are facing challenges in accessing education services. In such circumstances, the Education Cluster identified that 579,173 Palestinians (56 per cent females), of whom 96 per cent school-age children including 13,398 children with disabilities across the oPt, of which 74 per cent live in the Gaza Strip, will need of humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022.

2 - Activities

In 2022, UNRWA will continue to provide basic education to 291,100 elementary and preparatory school students in Gaza, providing a quality, inclusive and equitable education to Palestine refugee students. The Agency continues to focus on the delivery of its Education in Emergencies (EiE) approach, which was designed to ensure the continued provision of education to those children impacted by crises such as the COVID-19 pandemic and recurring hostilities. In order to adjust to the challenges created by the COVID-19 pandemic, UNRWA will maintain a flexible approach, applying different learning methodologies based on the epidemiological developments and circumstance on the ground, including through a full classroom learning, a blended learning, and a full remote learning. In order to respond to the at least 3-months loss of schooling during the 2020-2021 school year, UNRWA aims to provide support for students through catch up classes. These classes support students who have missed regular classes due to their inability to attend school because of conflict or other emergencies. These classes target students to meet minimum academic standards in the core subjects: Arabic, Mathematics, Science and English. Furthermore, in light of deepening poverty due to socioeconomic situation, many parents struggle to secure the learning and wellbeing needs for their children at school. To safeguard children’s right to education, UNRWA aims to provide 174,660 students (60 per cent of total number of students) including 84,295 girls and 90,365 boys with the necessary back to school supplies including students’ stationery, and school bags. To ensure the adequate access to education in on-going emergency in terms of COVID-19 and possible escalations, sSelf-learning programme will be activated when blended or remote learning modality is implemented. UNRWA will provide printed self-learning materials according to the students’ needs to ensure access to education by all students regardless of their ability to access online. UNRWA will also continue to enhance its Digital Learning Platform (DLP) that provide online learning materials such as self-learning cards, explainer videos and self-assessment cards. Responding to the on-going COVID-19 pandemic, UNRWA will ensure that enhanced hygiene and health measures are implemented in all premises. These include the continued availability of cleaning materials. Guidance is also provided to school staff to manage suspected or identified COVID-19 cases in UNRWA schools. Access to education will be extended to children with disabilities as part of inclusive education to alleviate further challenges they face. To this end, UNRWA will continue partnering with Community-Based Rehabilitation Centres (CBRCs) to provide basic education to 545 Palestine refugee children with disabilities including 225 girls and 320 boys, whose educational needs cannot be provided in UNRWA schools. UNRWA will implement MHPSS interventions to assist children in mitigating the impact of the repeated hostilities through activities facilitated by trained counsellors, aimed at strengthening their psycho-social resilience, coping capacities and mental well-being. This support will be provided in UNRWA schools by way of after-school extracurricular activities and the organization of summer fun weeks targeting up to 100,000 students, integrating COVID-19 preventive measures. MHPSS is accessible by all students and their parents in person or remotely via dedicated helplines, targeting 13,000 students (6,250 girls and 6,750 boys, including 300 children with disabilities) for individual counselling and 11,500 students (5,500 girls and 6,000 boys, including 250 children with disabilities) for group interventions. The counsellors will be using a holistic and integrated approach to child and family well-being, providing consultations and guidance for students, parents and teachers and will continue to be in contact with parents to enhance their partnership in PSS for children. Counsellors will also be available for remote consultations with parents, either by phone, through school online platforms or through group sessions in person or virtually as the COVID-19 situation allows. GFO's MHPSS unit plans to conduct 800 group sessions in 2022 for parents and plans to publish educational videos and other material on official Agency platforms. The counsellors will be following up with existing cases on their files as well as responding to newly emerged cases using holistic and integrated approach to child and family well-being. School counsellors act as case manager, and support can be sought internally from other UNRWA programmes or referred externally if the case requires interventions that UNRWA cannot provide, such as serious protection cases.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. UNRWA regularly assesses the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. UNRWA schools are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do no harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; and (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. UNRWA provides basic education to protect the safety and well-being of the students. UNRWA schools are equipped with child-friendly tools providing space for boys and girls including students with disabilities to study comfortably eliminating the risk of getting harmed or exposed to any danger as much as possible.The pandemic resulted in an increase in the protection threats facing groups with multiple overlapping vulnerabilities, which includes those at risk of domestic and intimate partner violence and the elderly. The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse may have also increased, as evidenced in other crisis situations. The Agency is ensuring that protection principles are mainstreamed and integrated in the response, and that special attention is paid to the needs of the most vulnerable, including children. Providing support to children who were forced to adjust their learning routine due to COVID-19 will protect their right to education during this challenging time.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA welcomes all Palestine refugee children from grade 1 to 9 in its schools across the Gaza Strip. The location of schools takes into consideration of safety concerns, especially for girls, including the accessibility, potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. Furthermore, schools are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Most of UNRWA schools are equipped with elevators to ease access for students with mobility difficulties, and some schools also implemented a child-friendly design. Educational staff, who are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection and gender mainstreaming, are able to refer specific protection cases to MHPSS counsellors for follow up. In COVID-19 emergency, students were forced to switch their learning routines from face-to-face in classroom to self-study at home. UNRWA provides self-learning materials to all students in order to support their learning experience at home. Community Based Rehabilitation Centres provides education to children with disabilities in order to ensure that these vulnerable children are not left behind.
