Project Summary

The intervention aims to provide wide-ranging support for household and community-based agricultural, livestock, and livelihood assistance in linkage with the value chain during 2023. The project will target the most affected people in NWS and engage them to effectively attempt to be self-reliant in the best possible way. The intervention plan anticipates that this will lead the families and their youth to think constructively and be self-reliant in the medium to long term. It will target all populations of IDPs, returnees, and host communities and take into consideration the most vulnerable people such as the women-headed families, elderly-headed families, and child-headed families. Moreover, the project will take into consideration the PWD.
The project is designed be implemented over 2022-2023 years to increase the self-reliance for both HH and communities through:

3 - Needs

Based on the last version of HNO-FSA report 2023, The food security crisis in Syria is indeed ”a protracted crisis”, and remains characterized by very complex drivers and contributing factors, from economic worsening and weakened local currency, to drought-like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity and limited energy supply and population movements. .It is important to note that the sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was not only driven by weather anomalies but also other drivers including issues with water scarcity and availability and disrupted services. The decline in agricultural production output was not only limited to rainfed food production systems but also to irrigated systems such as cereals, legumes, barley, and vegetables. Livestock has not also been spared from the impact of the prevailing water shortage and scarcity, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed, and the generally unfavorable weather conditions. The unfavorable 2021 – 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development, increased demand for fodder, limited supply, and high prices.Substantial harvest losses combined with chronic economic deprivation and displacement have contributed to mounting food insecurity and associated negative food and livelihood coping strategies for vulnerable Syrians.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the project implementation, the following protection risks may arise and actions to mitigate the risks are also outlined. 1. sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers(risk of harassment) of women and girls. Mitigation: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on a code of conduct and code of conduct in all staff contracts. Address any PSEA complaints. Recruit female staff and train them. The field MEAL staff with monitor the situation by conducting PDM. 2. violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurs (crowd control). Mitigation: Identification of suitable sites/ time. Set up several distribution points on sites to reduce crowds. make a joint distribution with another partner 3. Diversion of aid. Mitigation: Coordination meetings withcommunity leaders/gatekeepers/local councils on targeting criteria. Sign MOUs with local authorities 4. The Cash/voucher overspent on non-food items negatively impacts food security outcomes. Mitigation: Provide vouchers or e-cards, not cash, vary the payment days. MEAL staff will continue vendor monitoring systems (i.e. the selected shops). 5. targeting for IDPs versus Host communities. Mitigation: From localbenefeciaryselection committees with equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities.Set clear guidance to the Community Focal Points (CFPs) on the beneficiary selection process and criteria.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The current security situation that tends to be stable in most areas inside Syria will facilitat the intervention implementation to achieve the planned impacts. in addition to th good understanding and cooperation from most of the local stakeholders, some risks beyond QC control may affect the intervention implementation: 1. A huge number of DPs may reach the targeted areas if the East Euphrates operation started, so the demand for the food will be increased suddenly. Mitigation: Keep close coordination with the local councils and active organizations in the targeted area in order to harmonize the intervention and advocate through the FSL cluster to the donors to address the gap and big demand for the food. 2. Risk of Delayed deliveries due to closure of borders or closed internal roads between targeted areas for unforeseen reasons. Mitigation: QC will keep close coordination with the Turkish government since the humanitarian aids have exceptions, otherwise the procurement will be shifted to Syria. Close coordination with the local councils and main actors on the ground in order to facilitate the aids movements between the different areas and arrange warehouses in the different targeted areas. 3. Risk of New conflict may start in Idleb areas between the Syrian regime and other army groups or between different army groups themselves. Mitigation: QC safety and a security officer will be continuously following up and monitoring the security situation and update the safety plan.
