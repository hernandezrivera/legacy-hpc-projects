Project Summary

Despite the peace and stability that South Sudan is currently enjoying, a pocket of insecurity caused by armed youth due to cattle raiding and revenge killing, economic crisis and the rocketed commodities prices in the market is still facing the country. As the year began, people in South Sudan continued to suffer with high humanitarian needs. Some 8.3 million over two-thirds of the total population are estimated to need humanitarian assistance in 2021 according to the findings reflected in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview. This is an increase of 800,000 people from 7.5 million people in need in 2020. Some 5.82 million people, almost half of the population, are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worsening acute food insecurity in January. COVID-19, sub-national violence, and flooding continue to
strain access to the very limited basic services. More than 56 per cent of health facilities are non-functional. COVID-19 placed an additional demand for health services, supplies and health professionals. Health workers and other frontline workers faced stigmatization due to their COVID-19 related work. Mayendit and Panyijiar counties in southern part of Unity state were submerged by flood in July 2020 which has caused a massive displacement of the local populations, destroyed the crops and increased the prevalence of water borne diseases, raising the demands of healthcare services high in the two respective counties. A SMART survey conducted by IMC/SP in Mayendit county in Oct 2019 shows a GAM rate of 13.6% (10.4-17.5), while in Panyijiar county a SMART survey conducted by IMC in Dec 2019 shows a GAM rate of 15.4% (12.4-18.9). Over 9,000 returnees from Sudan were reported to have arrived in Manyo county last year and IDPs of about 26 households also were reported to have arrived in Manyo county from Akoka payam after the communal fighting between the armed youth of Nasir and Akoka counties. With this increased population in Manyo county, the demand to scale up healthcare services has highly increased. The 2020 HNO projection has shown Manyo, Mayendit, Panyijair and Cueibet counties among the 22 counties which are in IPC phase 4, most likely to face crisis or worsening acute food insecurity in Jan 2021. This project is aiming at enhancing access and strengthening the provision of emergency lifesaving healthcare services in Manyo, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Cueibet counties through the provision of PHCC, PHCUs and mobile clinic response. This project will benefit all gender groups including, 16,317 men, 16,983 women, 4,079 boys and 4,246 girls in all the four targeted counties of Manyo, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Cueibet respectively. UNIDOR will also support the psycho social and basic mental health services through case identification, first aid counseling, treatment and referral of the clients to nearby county or state hospitals. The project will also address GBV issues through community awareness, psychological counselling, treatment/management of GBV survivors and refer those who may need further management at the county or state hospitals. Lack of women participation in decision making including health choices due to cultural issues, low education level and discrimination has disadvantaged women against men. UNIDOR will ensure that women issues are considered in the project and their involvement and opinion incorporated in decision making for project improvement. The project will also response on COVID-19 pandemic through raising of awareness in the community and health facilities levels on COVID-19 preventive measures, surveillance and risk communication, case management and referral.

1 - Needs

Despite the efforts made by health actors in the provision of primary healthcare services in four selected counties, there is still a wider gap yet to be filled in Manyo, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Cueibet counties respectively. COVID-19, sub-national violence, and flooding continue to strain access to the very limited basic services. More than 56 per cent of health facilities are non-functional across South Sudan. The arrival of returnees and new IDPs with few health partners available in the large Manyo county divided by rivers and swamps, the healthcare services remained insufficient with an estimated 20% of the local populations in some hard-to-reach areas are not able to access or benefit from the existing health services in Manyo county. Hence the demand in the provision of healthcare services is very high. In Mayendit county, the effects of devastating flood which has displaced over 80% of the local population mainly from southern part, destroyed livelihoods and increased the prevalence of water borne diseases especially Malaria, AWD and skin infections has raised the demand of healthcare services in Mayendit county. In Panyijiar County, over 5 PHCUs submerged by water have been abandoned and the displaced population have confined themselves in few high grounds sharing the limited healthcare services in only few health facilities. The flood has increased the prevalence of water borne diseases, making the need to scale up healthcare services high. In Cueibet county, the continuous displacement of the local population due to communal fighting in the county has deprived or limited movement of people to health facilities because of fear of being killed. The people from several payams in northern part of Cueibet county including Panliet and Duony payams are not receiving adequate healthcare services and medical supplies because the SMOH and its one health partner supporting the provision of primary healthcare services is not able to cover the entire county considering its limited resources. A SMART survey conducted by IMC/SP in Mayendit county in Oct 2019 shows a GAM rate of 13.6%, while in Panyijiar county a SMART survey conducted by IMC in Dec 2019 shown a GAM rate of 15.4% Over 9,000 returnees from Sudan were reported to have arrived in Manyo county last year and IDPs of about 26 households also were reported to have arrived in Manyo county from Akoka payam after the communal conflict between the armed youth of Nasir and Akoka counties. 2 - Project activities

 UNIDOR will enhance access and strengthening the emergency lifesaving response by establishing Mobile clinics, Support PHCUs and PHCCs. The PHCCs will provide normal clinical packages and at the same time support referrals of SGBV survivors, CEmONC or surgical emergency cases to county or state hospitals.  More than 28,500 (11,172 A5 male, 11,628 A5 Female, 2,793 U5 male, 2,907 U5 female) will benefits from OPD curative consultations.  UNIDOR will target 150 pregnant mothers for safe deliveries at health facility by SBA.  CHWs/MCHWs, EPI Vaccinators, Nurses, Midwives, Clinical Officers and support staffs will be trained on IMNCI, Infection prevention and control measure on COVID-19, CMR and Quality of Care (QoC).  About 2,200 males under 1 year and 2,500 females under 1 year will be targeted for both routine and outreach EPI services. UNIDOR will strive to reach 75% coverage with Penta3 in Manyo, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Cueibet Counties.  UNIDOR will target 10,550 (M 5,170 and F 5,381) above 6 years and adults to be reached with health education awareness sessions on COVID-19 preventive measures especially on WASH and IPC, timely health seeking behavior, six months exclusive breast feeding, benefits of routine vaccination, environmental and personal hygiene etc.) during outreach and health facilities levels.  Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases and enhance weekly IDSR and EWARS reporting.  Prepare to respond to epidemic outbreaks (Malaria, Measles, Meningitis, Cholera and COVID-19) and integrate WASH and nutrition to healthcare services at PHCUs and PHCCs supported.  Improve resilience among vulnerable populations including disabled groups and mentally ill persons through psycho-social support services.  Integrated response for health/Wash/Nutrition/ food security and protection including Management of SAM with medical complications.  Forum for information sharing on services gaps and achievements for planning and accountability to affected population  Quarterly joint assessments and monitoring of services through the M&E and senior health team.  Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through joint supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services. Ensure to support community-based healthcare service provision (Boma Health Initiative) linking to nearby health facility.

3 - Project indicators

UNIDOR is committed to regular monitoring and evaluation (M&E) using indicators and targets as priority one to effectively track the progress of project to ensure implementation is guided by a results orientation and continuously informed by lessons learnt and beneficiary feedback. UNIDOR will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan and indicator tracking table to measure progress towards the expected results. The Indicators on the tracking table will include those in the log frame corresponding to on-going and planned activities. The Monitoring tools (indicator tracking template) will include the Departmental Questionnaires, online reporting tool, DHIS2 and EWARS, program tally sheets, and pictorial evidence especially during health facilities visits and trainings , will also use FGDs with the healthcare workers and the local Authorities to collect views on how the project is impacting on their lives. Data will be collected by the and M&E and health team and weekly/monthly/quarterly reports will be produced for internal monitoring. UNIDOR will submit quantitative data, disaggregated by sex, age and IDP/host when relevant both progress and final programmatic and financial reports online in line with donor reporting templates and Health Cluster donor requirements within the stipulated period as will be evidenced in the contractual documents . A joint quarterly supervision visits to all health facilities will be conducted together with our CHD counterparts using QoC/QSC tools in order to see the HFs compliance as per South Sudan BPHNS.The project indicators below will provide a successful measurement of project implementation.  # of men, women, boys and girls served through OPD curative consultations across the four selected counties. # of deliveries by skilled birth attendant conducted. # of healthcare workers trained on IMNCI, Infection prevention and control measures on COVID-19, CMR and Quality of Care (QoC).  # of individuals from 6 years and above reached with health education awareness sessions on COVID-19 preventive measures especially on WASH and IPC, timely health seeking behavior, six months exclusive breast feeding, benefits of routine vaccination, environmental and personal hygiene etc.) during outreach and health facilities levels. # of children under1 year (male and female) vaccinated against childhood preventable diseases with 75% Penta3 coverage achieved.  75% suspected cases investigated to detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases and enhance weekly IDSR and EWARS reporting. # of individuals treated or managed with mental illnesses and psycho-social support. # of quarterly join supportive supervision visits to improve quality healthcare services conducted. # of PHCCs and PHCUs linked with Boma Health Team to ensure that community-based healthcare service is implemented correctly.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNIDOR being a zero tolerance organization on SEA and with it staff well trained and signed SEA policies, the project commits to ensure that robust awareness is created to the beneficiaries and the community at large on PSEA through routine mobilization and sensitization session. Further, all the staff working with this project will sign code of conduct with the PSEA safeguarding principles

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will initiate a focus group discussion with those individuals living with disabilities to listen to their concerns and feedback on the project. The project will engage its local employees to conduct focus group discussions with those illiterates’ individuals using their local languages.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

From the inception of the project through the implementation cycle the community will be consulted and involved in design including the duration of the projects and donors funding the project, roles and duties of the community and the organization. Decisions and opinions will be sought through avenues such as community sensitization and mobilizations during focused group discussions with the focal persons. Project team will conduct monthly stakeholder meetings to discuss or review the project. Lastly, UNIDOR will also place suggestion boxes in the respective health sites and public place such as markets, schools and churches for easy access to all beneficiaries and members of the community. The project also will use Boma Health Team (BHT) and Village Health Committee (VHC) to share feedback with beneficiaries on the project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Upon receipt of any feedback from the beneficiaries , the project team will always evaluate the feedback for consideration and adaptation where possible and give back feedback to the community. In a scenario where any feedback does not give any tangible changes on the project design , then UNIDOR will have internal deliberations and take a step further to consult with the donor on the actions of implementation.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

UNIDOR staff will sign a code of conduct with commitment that they will never use their positions to take advantage of the beneficiaries served by the project. There will be continued awareness to the beneficiaries that services are free and ensure that the health sites are accessible to all. UNIDOR will work closely with the local authorities to monitor the security situation and dangle services if the beneficiary and staff safety is not assured. The terms of reference for recruitment will be shared with the local authorities to avoid leaders pushing to employment of unqualified staff as quality as paramount. The BHC/VHC will be asked to provide views directly to the organization or community leaders like chiefs and lead mothers. Community healthcare workers will be recruited from the respective villages of the project target to enable community members give feedback to people that can understand there languages as well as trust since they lives in the same villages. UNIDOR will also place suggestions boxes in all facilities for those that can read and write. This project will ensure that any factors that hinder beneficiaries from accessing services like distances or flooding are resolved by bringing services closer to people through outreaches and also ensure timely provision of services to enable beneficiaries get home on time thus minimizing the risk concerns along the home terrains
