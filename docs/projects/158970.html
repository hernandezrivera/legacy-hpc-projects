Project Summary

The project “Combatting De-Palestinization of Education in Occupied East Jerusalem” addresses: i) the need for effective protection of the right of Palestinians in occupied East Jerusalem to preserve and develop their educational system in accordance with their own culture, values and national identity, strengthening the advocacy and monitoring of violations the research/study on the issue if curricula in East Jerusalem and the different Israeli plans and policies. and, ii) the right of East Jerusalem children to freely learn, express and be educated in respect to their Palestinian identity, culture, language and values. Palestinians, including children, hold these rights as protected persons under IHL, and as individuals and a people entitled to self-determination under IHRL.
This issue is addressed because of: the broad concern about the systematic manner in which Israel has undermined these rights; the failure to obtain redress in the past through repeated but uncoordinated efforts; the international community including governments and UN and international organizations need to acknowledge the serious violation of IHL and human rights; and, the serious consequences for Palestinian society in East Jerusalem, in particular its youth.

1 - Needs

With almost 53 years of occupation of the West Bank, Gaza, and East Jerusalem, Israel continues its process of ‘Israelization’ or ‘de-Palestinization’ of education in occupied East Jerusalem. The plan proposed is a five year strategy that aims to gradually increase the number of students using the Israeli curriculum beginning from the first grade with specific emphasis on English and math. The first year would add 15 classes, followed by 18 in the second, 21 in the third, 24 in the fourth, and 27 classes in the fifth. Another goal of the cabinet is to continue to increase the number of East Jerusalem Palestinian students who would earn the Israeli matriculation exam (bagrut).The ex-Bennet, the Israeli education minister: was quoted saying, “the time has come for East Jerusalem to also study the Israeli curriculum from first grade. Jerusalem needs to be united in deeds, not in words. The political context of the problem raises additional concerns such as: the failure to obtain redress; the reluctance of foreign aid agencies and governments to engage in what is perceived to be a “political matter”, rather than one IHL and human rights; and, the serious consequences for Palestinian society in East Jerusalem, in particular its children and youth. Part of Israel’s policy of ‘de-Palestinization’ has been aimed at schools in East Jerusalem and the push by the occupying Authorities to introduce the Israeli curriculum which can be seen clearly through the Israeli five years plan foe education in East Jerusalem. With the world wide health crisis because of the corona virus which has been effecting many countries including Palestine and the Palestinians people. As a result of the corona, with the Coved-19, which as effected the whole life of the Palestinian people specially children mainly education, were schools, have been closed and distance learning has been the main mechanism education, which faced several challenges, including:- Access to quality education. - Lack of facilities and equipment needed. -Lack of experience among teachers in distance learning experience. -Lack accesses to distance learning. -Lack of quality educational environment. - Lack of distance teaching experiences among teachers. This situation increased poverty among Palestinian as a whole and in Jerusalem in particular. As well, the situation has an impact on children whom they were looked down, they can’t go out, which increase the stress among them.Consequences: -Higher schools dropout rates. -Psychological problems and needs.-The education disruption will continuous and extend beyond education, such as food security, economic instability.-Increase of violation in the family specially against women.-The need for more budgets and financial support for the education system. Gender: The Israelization of Palestinian education has profound and specific impacts on both boys and girls within the school systems. Boys specifically experience higher rates of dropout, while the combined dropout rate of both boys and girls in East Jerusalem schools is as high as 36% by the 12th grade. Girls also experience unique disparities as a result of the controlled Palestinian education, such as early marriages (prior to 18), which remains to be a form of gendered based violence and limited self-determination. Protecting the right of education among East Jerusalem Palestinian schools diminishes the negative impacts that specifically target boys and girls in distinct ways, which exist due to the Israelization of Palestinian education. Any reference to the Palestinian identity, culture and heritage will be erased, and all the expressions regarding the Palestinian history and contemporary narrative will be removed. This is a great concern for the next generation of Palestinian Jerusalemites; as this will affect the level of nationalism and might promote what Israel is hoping for, i.e. the Palestinian Identity and narrative will vanish with the demise of the old generation and that it will be forgotten by the coming younger generations. This will have a great gendered impact in the context of the Palestinian society which is still remains deeply patriarchal and is strongly marked by traditional gender roles. As gender disparities remain significant in numerous aspects of life, including political participation and access to decision-making positions, resulting in discrimination against women in terms of access to opportunity. As such, while boys will continue to have access to alternative sources of national narrative through their free social movement and participation in the social and national organizations girls on the other hand will not enjoy the same access and participation through cultural and social restrictions imposed on them. As well as it will impact the role of women from promoting the national identity including narrative, history and culture and will create a problematic gap in the family in particular and the society as a whole. The end result would have serve the Israeli ultimate aim of erasing the Palestinian National Identity. Within Jerusalem, 84% of Palestinian children live below the poverty line, and thousands of children are considered to be “at-risk.” With a shortage of classrooms and striking discrepancies in resources allotted to Palestinian versus Jewish Israeli citizens, many of these children lack access to adequate education, including 24,000 children who remain unrecognized by the Jerusalem Education Administration. Drop-out rates are high among the Palestinian students, and for those who graduate, acceptance into Israeli universities is incredibly challenging . The dropout rate for Palestinian students in East Jerusalem in 12th grade – when students are 18 years old is 33%, nearly 24 times higher than the dropout rate in the Hebrew education system, which stands at 1.5%, and despite forming 37% of the population and paying residential and commercial taxes – only 13% of the overall budget
