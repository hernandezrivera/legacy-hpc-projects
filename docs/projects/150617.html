Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the 2018-2020 HRP specific objectives in Food Security Sector: “The resilience, food security and productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected” and “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.”
Based on recent ACTED assessments, extensive consultations with key stakeholders, and following the recommendations of the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and Food Security Sector (FSS cluster), the project will aim at providing emergency support to the most vulnerable households of Rafah, Khan Yunis and Middle Area, including ARA (total of 2,800 beneficiaries).
Specifically, ACTED will:
• Restore the productive capacity of 305 vulnerable farmers (Activities 2&3), whose livelihood is located in the communities of Al Shokat (Rafah governorate), Fukhari (Khan Yunis) and Wadi As Salqa (Middle Area), with a specific focus on the ARA which were damaged during the incidents occurring at the border since the March of Return in March 2018. The project will contribute to improve and restore farmers’ productive capacity, households’ access to food security and contribute to increase communities’ resilience capacities, through cash-for-work implementation/cash-for-asset
• Increase access to food security of 195 vulnerable households (Activity 4) located in the same communities, through the creation of an emergency food security chain benefiting
• Strengthen 3 existing Community-Based Agriculture and Protection Committees (CBAPC) in each community, which were established by ACTED under former projects, through capacity-building activities.
In total, 500 households will benefit from the action (2800 individuals).
Eventually, the project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on women and vulnerable groups, and a strong Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) component. In particular, beneficiaries will be heavily involved in the action through community based empowered committees, and a comprehensive Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) will be deployed. Gender principles will be mainstreamed during the action and Committees will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases with a particular focus given to women and children issues such as related to GBV. An ACTED protection officer will be focal point of the committees to receive and follow-up on cases.

Needs

Succession of wars, existing restrictions imposed on cultivable surfaces and on import/export, lack of financial capacity, led to the output of Gaza’s agricultural sector declining by 31% since the Operation Protective Edge in 2014 (“Sowing for sustainable future in the Gaza Strip” Relief web, 18/02/2018). According to the HNO 2018, a total of 541,000 people in Khan Younis, Middle Area and Rafah (including nearby areas) are identified in need of food security (p.18), and 80% of Gaza population depends on food assistance (p.8). Rural households, households with large number of vulnerable individuals and female-headed households are more exposed to risks of food insecurity. Losses of the agricultural sector in Gaza since 2014 had a significant impact on women’s employment, due to their limited share of agricultural holdings and limited economic opportunities to generate income.In particular, the ARA, where the buffer zone takes up to 35% of agricultural land (“Gaza, ten years later”, UNSCO, 2017), has been targeted over time to prevent or discourage access, and farmers are therefore unable or reluctant to invest in land in ARA, though around 95 % of this land arable but inaccessible due to Israel's "open re" policy (Fact Sheet " Gaza Strip: Attacks in the border areas and their consequences ", PCHR, 2015). Loss of land, scale of damages on agricultural fields, lack of investment capacities, policies restricting cultivable lands, exportations and importations of raw material or agricultural equipment, have affected both the ability of farmers to produce and to furnish the local market (thus increasing need for imports and ultimately price of available products). From March to September 2018, the Gaza Strip has witnessed a significant increase of Palestinian casualties and damages in agricultural lands located in the ARA, where demonstrations are still happening in the frame of the Great March of Return. In consequence, many farmers and households, whose agricultural assets and lands were damaged or fully destroyed, lost their main means of livelihood, in particular in ARA Al Shokat (Rafah governorate), known as “hot” demonstrations‘ spots during the March of Return. Approximately 867 agricultural dunums owned by 217 farmers (Palestinian FSS, Quarter 2-2018 update, FAO/WFP report) suffered significant damages due to mass demonstrations in the targeted ARA since March 2018. During consultation meetings held in September by ACTED, the MoA confirmed that there is currently a total of at least 1000 food-insecure families in these governorates who do not yet benefit from any assistance. In 2018, ACTED conducted two FSS needs’ assessments in the Gaza Strip: - A market survey in Gaza which shows that food prices, especially for 6 out of 7 core seasonal products (potatoes, peas, green beans, zucchini, onion, cucumber) have progressively increased since the beginning of the year. Such inflation is the result of a decreased production and reduced availability of goods from outside Gaza, following the closure of Karem Shalom border- A qualitative assessment based on a participatory approach which consisted in the facilitation of three focus groups (total of 35 participants, including 21 farmers whose lands are located in ARA), in Shoka, Wadi as Salqa and Al Foukhary coupled with the interviews of key stakeholders (Head of Agricultural Department Agricultural advisor and Head of Irrigation Department of MoA). This assessment confirmed constraints encountered by farmers: poor irrigation and/or water quality, water scarcity, high costs of seeds and seedlings, unavailability of certain types of fertilizers, poor capacity to use machinery and material (fertilizer, pesticides). Both assessments have confirmed the urgent needs that ACTED will be addressing in the proposed project, which will participate in resuming production activities of most vulnerable farmers in ARA, thus favoring access to food security of their households.

Activities

Activity 1 – Identification of project beneficiaries through vulnerability and technical assessmentsIn coordination with the MoA, beneficiaries will be identified through:- A list of vulnerable farmers identified and provided by the Ministry of Agriculture- A call for applications that will be widely advertised in public spaces across targeted areas, including supermarkets, administrative buildings and mosques, with the support of local CBOs, cooperatives and existing or newly established ACTED Community-Based Agricultural Committees (CBAC).Vulnerability and technical assessments will be conducted in the targeted communities by ACTED and ACAD teams to identify most vulnerable farmers (and most urgent needs of rehabilitation) as well as vulnerable households to benefit from food baskets distribution. Beneficiaries of the provision of emergency food baskets will be identified through vulnerability assessments conducted on a list of vulnerable households identified and provided by the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD).Activity 2 – Urgent rehabilitation of agricultural water networksACTED and ACAD will launch a tender for the rehabilitation and maintenance of water assets in the selected farmers’ lands. In total, rehabilitation of 35 water ponds and 4 water wells to store water and minimize farmers’ dependency on main water sources will be implemented by hired contractor. Urgent rehabilitation of irrigation networks will be carried out in case of significant damages. Integrated solar systems for all ponds and pumps will be installed to minimize the impact of the electricity crisis. In total, 35 farmers will benefit from water ponds rehabilitation and 120 from wells reparation.Activity 3 - Urgent rehabilitation of 370 donums of land and existing agricultural assets185 farmers will be supported in the rehabilitation of their land (2 donums/farmers) through a mechanism of cash assistance:- Cash for assets: this cash assistance will be conditional and restricted to the purchase of agricultural inputs needed for rehabilitation of 2 donums (small irrigation network components, fertilizers, seeds…). - Cash for works’ implementation: each farmer will receive cash for work of 312 USD to support land rehabilitation and informal employment of community members. The amount will be calculated based on the equivalent cost of the kit installation by the Cash for Work modality, in line with the agreed cluster rates.Activity 4 - Creation of an emergency food security chainIn total, 150 households will be identified during the activity 1 to benefit from bi-monthly food baskets. The activity will include:- Procurement of farmers’ products: ACAD and ACTED project teams, with the support of the CBAC, will directly purchase products of farmers’ beneficiaries, thus creating a direct food chain between producers to consumers- Renting of warehouses in targeted governorates: ACTED will rent warehouses to enable farmers to deliver their products - Cash-for-work activity to employ 45 workers, including at least 40% female head of households and 10% of female head of households: under ACTED and ACAD supervision, they will be in charge of collecting, packing and distributing the food baskets once products are delivered in the warehousesActivity 5 - Capacity-building of Community-Based Agriculture and protection Committees3 CBAC, established and trained under former projects, will be strengthened through training on protection principles, food security, livelihood and SPHERE standards, best agricultural practices, risks of ERW/UXO (with UNMAS). CBAC will be involved throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and guarantee a better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female headed households, disabled, elderly…). Women were particularly encouraged to join the CBAC as active members.

Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects and anticipate and mitigate any challenge. ACTED AME Unit AME Unit will be in charge of measuring project outcomes related to the specific project objective falling under the 2018-2020 FSS HRP goals: “The resilience, food security and productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected” and “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.” and the 10 selected indicators: #of farmers, herders receiving inputs or services; # of dunums made productive or with improved production capacity; # of water wells rehabilitated or provided; # of m3 of water storage rehabilitated or provided; # of KW of renewable power supply system; # of beneficiaries supported through non-agricultural livelihoods; # of beneficiaries receiving food in-kind; # of individuals benefiting from Cash for Work; # of individuals benefiting from other conditional cash support. In particular, the AMEU will conduct a baseline and end line assessment to measure progress against pre-identified indicators, as well as beneficiary satisfaction, and draw lessons learned from the project. Those activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed data on beneficiaries and measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups. ACTED will share the findings and results with the FSS so as to contribute to the wider humanitarian community’s understanding of food security challenges and issues in the ARA of the Gaza Strip, and disseminate best practices. Satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-Beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM complying with HAP standards and ACTED’s SoPs. The mechanism will feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBAC members, ACTED protection officer and monitoring staff from ACAD, to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. During the project, the CBAC will be responsible for monitoring the works and ensuring that protection standards are upheld and concerns are addressed or referred to the ACTED PO. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to ACTED, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, ACTED PO, with the support of the CBAC, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process through regularly visiting all beneficiaries to ensure that protection concerns are being respected and addressed through the works. Finally, ACTED Project Development, Finance and Audit Department will closely follow-up project activities and ensure the action is implemented in conformity with the HRP guidelines, IHP, as well as locally defined food security cluster recommendations. Using ACTED’s global Project Cycle Management tools, project team will report monthly to the PDD, on project implementation, outputs and data to ensure that all indicators are on track, delays are prevented and any difficulties are identified and recorded. Similarly, the Finance and Audit department will ensure a constant follow up of all procurement, expenses and transactions. Those monitoring activities will ensure the highest level of compliance and transparency.
