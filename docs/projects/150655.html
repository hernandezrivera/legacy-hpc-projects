Project Summary

The intervention will target Al Amal area (Beit Lahiya) and Al Shoka area (Rafah) to address bad WASH situation (76% of the budget) with a complementary component that will address the related waterborne and nutritional health diseases for the children under 5 years (24% of the budget).
The activities will include installation of sewer lines, HH connections, repairs for HH WASH facilities, hygiene promotion and material support, distribution of water storage tanks and treatment for children who suffer from waterborne diseases.

Needs

In September 2018, DCA/NCA partners, MA'AN and Al Ahli Arab Hospital, had conducted an assessment including 490 families in two areas (204 families in Al Shoka and 286 Family in Al Amal area in Beit Lahia). This aimed to address the socioeconomic vulnerabilities, the accessibility to safe water, adequate sanitation and hygiene practices, the waterborne diseases of children under 5 years and the women concerns related to the accessibility to basic WASH services. The assessment included 500 home visits, interviews with women, men, children’s caregivers and elderlies, hemoglobin, heights/lengths/weights tests, 3 focus group discussions, 3 key informant interviews with the municipalities in charge and consultations with the actors operating in the areas. It is revealed that 97.1% of the target populations are abject poor. The areas' population includes 21.5% children under 5, 2.2% elderlies, 3.0% people with disabilities, 12.9% women headed HH’s, 5.1% disabled headed HH’s, 8.6% elderly headed HH’s, and 2.9% pregnant or lactating women. Assessment shows that 50.4% of the families have poor/unsafe water storage tanks, 27.6% have poor/unsafe potable water storage tanks, 93.7% have toilets in poor conditions which are neither healthy nor safe, 69.0% have kitchens in poor conditions which are neither healthy nor safe, 18.6% don’t have kitchens, 26.1% are connected to the public sewage network having 9.4% of the connections are neither safe nor functional, 60.8% are using cesspits having 85.5% of the cesspits are in poor/unsafe conditions, and 3.6% are using Septic Tanks having 100.0% of the Septic Tanks are in poor and unsafe conditions.The Cesspit flooding in the target locations occurs regularly resulting in waste water pools spilling onto roads throughout the year. The dangers related to these practices were documented in recent PWA’s studies which demonstrated the high levels of nitrates in the groundwater, which exceeds WHO’s limits.The bad WASH conditions are associated with infectious diseases especially diarrheal disorders and parasitic infestations which lead to malabsorption of nutrients and consequently malnutrition. It is revealed the history of diarrheal episode in the previous month was present in 62.8% of targeted children, while the prevalence of anemia was 56.2%. The infection with Entameba histolytica is present in 62.8% of targeted children, while the infection with pin worms is present in 95.3%.Upon assessment, 46.5% of participants wash their hands after cleaning child backside and 12.8% of them wash their hands before child breastfeeding. 37.6% of the participants wash their hands before preparing food and 41.1% wash their hands after eating. Only 33.3% of children wash their hands before eating as reported by mothers. The detergents are unavailable in 83.3% of participants’ homes. Hygienic aspects in the targeted areas are poor in terms disposal of solid waste and wastewater and most importantly, cleanliness and personal hygiene practices.The bad hygiene conditions are manifested in sites that accumulate stagnant water—such as sinks, toilets, waste pipes. Poor hygiene conditions of kitchens, utensils, bathrooms, combined with shortage of hygienic material inside homes were recognized. The economic burden is one of the main causes of the bad hygiene situation among the surveyed sample. The women in 16.3% of the HH’s reported concerns about children hygiene, safety and security due to the conditions of WASH Facilities. All families who include disabilities and elderlies reported concerns due to the conditions of WASH facilities that are not adequate for the use of those vulnerable groups. The poor WASH conditions affect adversely the psychological wellbeing of women. Women have the responsibility of emptying the cesspits at time of sewage flooding which overburden women. Supporting the access to safe and adequate WASH services is a priority for women to enjoy well-being by living with dignity.

Activities

WASH Activities: - Installation of 3700 meter (10 Inches) of sewer lines (2500m in Al Shoka and 1200m in Al Amal) - (350 HH’s - 2100 individuals including 570 girls, 587 boys, 486 women, 457 men, 45 elderly)- Installation of 151 houses sewage connections targeting 151 households in the targeted areas (911 individuals including 227 girls, 231 boys, 213 women, 220 men, 25 elderly)- Rehabilitation of Sanitation facilities including 459 toilets, sinks and water connections (2768 individuals including 743 girls, 761 boys, 650 women, 614 men, 57 elderlies)- Distribution of 247 Roof Storage Tanks (1000 liters’ capacity) - (1507 individuals including 424 girls, 422 boys, 333 women, 328 men, 20 elderlies).- Distribution of 135 Potable water storage tanks (200 liters’ capacity) - (690 individuals including 191 girls, 184 boys, 155 women, 160 men, 9 elderlies.- Hygiene material support and awareness raising (1000 families – 5912 individuals including 1577 girls, 1622 boys, 1392 women, 1321 men, 130 elderlies). The content of the hygiene kits will be determined in consultation with the communities. It can include Plastic Squeegee, sanitary napkins, Liquid and sponges for dishes washing, Dettol and Hand washing soap liquid. Nutrition Activities: - Screening activities for 3900 children under 5 years (to identify children with waterborne diseases– (1911 girls, 1989 Boys)- Treatment for 1400 children suffering from waterborne diseases (686 girls, 714 boys)- Nutritional health awareness targeting children’s caregivers (7500 individuals including 5250 women, 2250 men) The intervention will mitigate the protection risks manifested in potential health hazard resulting from the deteriorated WASH conditions and will address women needs as the main responsible for rationing water inside the home and other hygiene related responsibilities. HH’s include children under five who suffer from waterborne diseases will be prioritized.The maintenance works will address women security and privacy, child protection, supporting the wellbeing of elderlies and people with disabilities. The communication material for both hygiene and nutritional health awareness will be tailored to consider the different roles and responsibilities for men, women and elderlies inside the HH. Awareness raising will focus on households’ safe waste water disposal to prevent water borne diseases. Specific support will be provided for women in identifying and applying best hygiene, sanitation and nutritional health practices for activities specific to their roles and responsibilities such as safe water handling, safe personal hygiene, and safe children excreta disposal, handwashing, etc. The hygiene material support will consider the specific hygiene needs for women and girls particularly teenagers, pregnant, elderly and women with disabilities. Special attention will be made by trained female staff to deal with GBV incidents, where detected cases will be referred to specialized services through referral pathways of the protection cluster/CPWG.The intervention has been coordinated with CMWU, municipalities, WASH and Nutrition clusters including area focal points and ACF. ACF implements WASH interventions targeting entire Gaza strip, where further coordination related to beneficiary selection will take place with ACF to avoid duplication and ensure complementary. MAAN will be implement the WASH activities in both areas. NECC will implement the nutrition activities in Al Shoka, while AAH will implement the nutrition activities in Al Amal. DCA/NCA will ensure proper coordination among the implementing partners to ensure harmony.The intervention responds directly to the WASH cluster objective #1 and is in line with the health cluster priorities: Ensure access of most vulnerable children under the age of 5 to adequate lifesaving health and nutrition interventions.

M&E mechanism will be established to follow up the progress of the process, to ensure the matching of the identified indicators and the expected outcomes of the project. It will compile:a. Full coordination and involvement of relevant stakeholders during all project phases.b. Ensuring the gender mainstreaming in all project’s phases and outputs.DCA/NCA team is equipped with the relevant capacities to support partners and ensure optimal implementation of the project. The indicator measurement tools will include baseline/ end line surveys (i.e. KAP), beneficiary lists, monitoring field visits and perception surveys with beneficiaries.Results-based monitoring is going to be applied considering the indicators of achievements and means of verification. FGD’s will be conducted with the target group (including women, men, youth, elderlies and children caregivers) during the implementation phase to assess the quality and accessibility of the response provided and take the proper corrective measures as needed. Partners have specialized, mostly female, data collection and analysis team who have been trained on the tools and techniques of gender-sensitive data collection and analysis. DCA/NCA and its partners will establish a community engagement committee in order to ensure effective and accountable participation of the target community in the project implementation and monitoring processes. The representation of various community groups in the committees will be ensured including women, youth representatives for PWD’s, children caregivers and elderlies.An accessible complaints and feedback handling system will be developed in consultation with the community engagement committee. Community meetings will be conducted to introduce the project to the community, as part of information sharing mechanisms.
