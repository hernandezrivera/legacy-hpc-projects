Project Summary

Mercy Corps aims to strengthen our protection interventions to support the overall protection cluster objectives towards the provision of quality integrated protection services. Mercy Corps will focus on community based approaches and target vulnerable adolescents girls and boys and young women and men. Mercy Corps will expand on its commitment to integrating protection outcomes into other sectoral interventions including cash, food security and livelihoods, WASH and social cohesion. Mercy Corps will do this through direct implementation and local partners in northeast Syria. Mercy Corps projects aim to ensure vulnerable adolescents and young people exposed to acute and/or chronic crises are safe, protected and more resilient and have the capacity to address root causes of inequalities. This will be achieved through three pillars of integrated programming, 1) structured psychosocial interventions, life skills and mentoring to improve positive coping, functioning, self-efficacy and well-being whilst being provided with opportunities in livelihood and resources including cash grants or cash assistance; 2) Individuals, couples, and community leaders are supported to unlearn harmful values and social norms and to adopt new values and attitudes and become more committed to equality and diversity 3) Adolescents and young people who are survivors and marginalised understand their rights, exercise self-efficacy and are referred to specialised actors. These interventions will complement existing sectoral programming where Mercy Corps or its partners are operating in and will not be standalone. Mercy Corps will also continue to ensure protection mainstreaming is embedded across all our activities and programming. This will include strengthening data collection and ensuring it is disaggregated to inform programming; referral systems and service mapping are in place to refer identified protection cases to specialised actors; Mercy Corps staff and partners are trained in protection mainstreaming principles and approaches, protection risks analysis and safeguarding/PSEA; Mercy Corps community accountability feedback and response mechanism are designed in consultation with communities and feedback received are use to track red flags; community awareness sessions to inform the communities of their rights and entitlements, complaints and feedback mechanism and the code of conduct of all individuals providing aid.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis has disrupted water services, social services, strained coping mechanisms and community safety nets and resulted in psychological distress. HNO suggests that across Syria, 27% of children in households continue to display signs of mental distress. Mercy Corps’s 2021 assessment showed a rise in suicides and drug use. Mercy Corps Gender analysis suggested increased levels of violence towards women and girls. Girls are increasingly engaged in child labor due to family economic situation, they are at higher risk of sexual harrassment and exploitation in these jobs. According to 2022 Voices for Syria, early marriage percentages remain high. HNO indicated that boys face elevated risks linked to arbitrary detention, forced conscription and explosive ordnance. The UN Special Rapporteur report in 2021 indicated that children continue to be detained in overcrowded conditions, with limited services and are mostly housed with adults, putting them at high risks of abuse and are in need of psychosocial services. HNO and HNAP reports indicate that girls and boys, including adolescents and children with disabilities are also at more risk of other forms of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. All of Mercy Corps interventions will include community awareness sessions on prevention and response to child protection and GBV issues including a list of specialised actors that cases can be referred to. Mercy Corps will use the SC GBV awareness toolkit.

5 - Implementation of activities

Interventions will target vulnerable adolescents girls and boys, and young women and men using an integrated approach.. Regular monitoring and baseline and endline (i.e. functioning and KAP) will be collected. 1: Adolescents and Youths will be provided with structured psychosocial interventions, life skills and mentoring to improve positive coping, functioning, self-efficacy and well-being. 2:At risk adolescents and youths will be provided with facilitation skills, mentoring and material support to design their own activities that will benefit other children. Mercy Corps will use our youth engagement curriculum.3: Community based interventions addressing harmful social norms (such as violence against women and children, early marriage) will mainly be carried out through social behaviour change interventions. Mercy Corps will implement Mercy Corps CATALYST and Bridge approaches: Mercy Corps will provide inclusive MPCA to households directly referred by specialised protection, health and disabilities actors. Participants will have an identified protection, disability and/or health issue who meets the MPCA eligibility criteria. This may include households with survivors of abuse, blast survivors, and individuals with chronic illness. Mercy Corps has established MOUs and SOPs with these agencies. 5: Through training local authorities and humanitarian actors will understand psychological first aid, child protection minimum standards, safeguarding, and responding to GBV.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Interventions include different community actors increasing risks of SEA (high impact, medium likelihood). Mitigation: 1) on-boarding and training on PSEA and COC 2) community awareness on rights & entitlements, COC of aid workers 3) feedback & complaints mechanism designed in consultation with community 4) proper recruitment procedures 5) service mapping and safe referral are in place. Recruitment of children into armed groups (high impact, medium likelihood). Mitigation: 1) identify safe locations and timing in consultation with community 2) train staff to identify risks, refer to specialised actors 3) build capacity of adolescents & young people 4) monitor red flags 5) parents understand risks and red flags 6) regular mentor-parent check-ins. Mental health is highly stigmatized (High impact, medium likelihood) and poorly understood. Mitigation: 1) existing relationship within targeted community & community consulted 2) program uses wellbeing instead of mental health 3) program widely targets from different groups. Promotion of gender equality and rights of marginalised groups and changing gender roles casemay excerbate GBV (High impact, medium likelihood). Mitigations 1) updated gender analysis 2) consult M, W, B, G and marginalised on project design 3) ongoing monitoring of red flags through audits 4) strengthen feedback and complaints mechanism 5) staff trained on supporting survivors 6) SADD data collected and analysed.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Approval from local authorities (high impact, medium likelihood) Local authorities restrict activities due to lack of support for protection interventions or access issues. Where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavor to support partners in their intervention methodology and engagement with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. Increased insecurity (high impact, medium likelihood) Situations in the northeast and remain volatile. Due to insecurity Mercy Corps might experience issues in accessing certain communities and lead to project delays. Mercy Corps security team and humanitarian access team to scenario plan and adjust the project and intervention to minimize impact and risks to affected communities. Covid-19 pandemic (high impact, high likelihood) Ongoing covid- 19 pandemic had resulted in delayed delivery of services and project adaption to ensure public health measures are observed. This is expected to continue in 2022.
