Project Summary

In 2004, the International Organization for Migration began its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) program to better support the Government of Sudan and its humanitarian partners. DTM was introduced in response to the need for accurate information on displacement and the identiﬁcation of beneﬁciaries for the delivery of humanitarian aid that arose in Sudan follow a series of crises that have affected Sudan since 2003. Following the implementation of DTM, IOM now has an established, accurate data collection system that provides displacement data in the areas most affected by conﬂict and disasters.
IOM has been working in close collaboration with the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) and partners in the ﬁeld to establish a uniﬁed and systematic system to better track internally displaced populations and returnees in seven states in Sudan. Until 2018, IOM Sudan’s DTM program focused mainly on registration activities in response to requests from the Government and humanitarian partners to identify beneﬁciaries to better inform the delivery of humanitarian assistance (i.e. food vouchers by WFP). These registration activities have positioned DTM in Sudan as the key reference source of displacement data in the country.
As crisis levels within Sudan gradually reduce, and the situation broadly stabilizes the majority of the conﬂict-affected areas, there is a need to restructure the DTM program. Whilst there will continue to be a need to DTM to track displacement resulting from unpredictable events such as the ﬂooding in 2019, there are increasing requirements in tailoring the operation to better suit the current situation of protracted displacement and return scenario. More speciﬁcally, there is the need to update displacement ﬁgures more frequently, comprehensively, systematically, and at regular intervals to ensure that data is continually collected, provides a meaningful resource and is available through IOM’s DTM system.

1 - Needs

Ongoing conflict between government armed forces and rebel groups continues to cause widespread displacement in Sudan, while at the same time challenging climatic and socio-cultural conditions have led to high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition that have further encouraged high internal mobility. According to the DTM’s mobility tracking baseline zero – a compilation of all veriﬁed data registrations for both IDPs and returnees, commencing in 2010 and 2015 respectively – a total number of 2,086,934 IDPs (473 locations) and 543,353 returnees (1,060 locations) have been identiﬁed acrossseven states in Sudan: namely, North, East, South, West and Central Darfur, as well as South and West Kordofan. Initially, in 2020, MT will be implemented in these seven states but intends to expand its operations to cover the whole country in order to gain a geographically comprehensive understanding of displacement in Sudan. Enumerators will revisit each location and interview a broad network of key informants to update locations and verify population presence on a quarter-yearly basis. The population groups under observation are: IDPs, returnees from internal displacement (permanent and seasonalIDP returnees), returnees from abroad, and foreign nationals; and the unit of reference is the location (smallest administrative unit: village, neighbourhood, camp). Updates will be communicated regularly, and data collected in quarterly “rounds.” The information collected will include displaced population estimates (households and individuals), the year of arrival of IDPs, returning IDPs and foreigners, as well as the location of origin and/or previous displacement, reasons for displacement and return, and type of displacement locations and shelter type, among others. At the end of each round, information products (maps and narrative reports) as well as datasets will be released on the DTM webpage and distributed to IOM’s partners. Over the course of 2019, DTM has tracked 48,399 ﬂood-affected individuals across Sudan through its Emergency Event Tracking (EET) component. Floods are an annual occurrence during the rainy season in Sudan, and affected populations tend to ﬂee their homes to seek safety with their relatives and/or in high places, until the receding water levels enable their return. DTM aims to expand its EET component over the upcoming year to feed into its MT data information ﬂow, as well as to provide early warning systems to improve emergency preparedness for potential displacements. Finally, since June 2018, DTM has been conducting multi-sectoral location assessments (MSLA) across the state of South Kordofan, generating a comprehensive and detailed data source on essential key indicators for the facilitation of humanitarian and development programmatic design and implementation: namely, population presence, shelter, food security, education, health, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), protection and other services. To date, it has covered 185 villages within three localities and each exercise consists of a visit to the location followed by the physical surveying of public infrastructure, interviews with key informants, and group discussions with affected members of the community. DTM will continue to visit each location in South Kordofan, with the intention of surveying all affected communities across Sudan and subsequently feeding this data into its humanitarian response planning.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Main Output: Accurate and reliable DTM data information on affected populations is disseminated to the humanitarian agencies, sectors and other relevant stakeholders. Activities: 1. Gathering target population estimates through a broad network of key informants at the lowest administrative level available, for tracking and monitoring key trends in population movements over time. 2. Assessing demographics and vulnerabilities of the target population groups. 3. Identifying sectoral needs, gaps, and service provisions with respect to the target population groups. 4. Producing return and recovery program designs and plans, which are reﬂective of the evolving scenario. 6. Building local capacity for tracking and analysing internal displacement and mixed migration inside Sudan through joint implementation with government stakeholders. 7. Utilising mobility tracking estimates as an evidence base for operational planning and programmatic design for a more targeted humanitarian response . 8. Using DTM’s emergency tracking system to monitor ongoing emergencies or known high risk locations where population movements occur regularly/are predicted to occur. 9. Conducting routine and emergency location assessments at the site of the displacement or return to identify the urgent needs of the affected population and to support programming of life sustaining assistance in transition and recovery. . 11. Providing trainings, workshops and refreshers on standard DTM tools and protection practices for IOM enumerators. 12. Producing regular DTM statistical reports to be shared with GoS, humanitarian actors, relevant sectors and sector partners.. Based on IOM’s global methodology, but adapted to reﬂect the context and the population movements speciﬁc to Sudan, DTM aims to enhance its past operational approach and conduct the following activities over the upcoming year: Mobility Tracking (MT): The new methodology will implement a periodic baseline assessment to facilitate the collection of multi-layered information on the different target groups within deﬁned locations and update such ﬁgures at regular intervals. As a result, IOM will be able to provide a complete and up-to-date picture of displacement and other forms of mobility in Sudan. These ﬁgures can be used by the Government and humanitarian and development partners to better protect, assist and advocate on behalf of these populations. Additionally, MT aims to produce an evidence base for strategy and programme design to support humanitarian transition and recovery operations within Sudan. and will help facilitate joint assessment and response planning and joint funding appeals. EET: As a subcomponent of MT and activated on a need’s basis, EET will be deployed to track sudden displacements and population movements and provide more frequent updates on the scale of displacement and quantify the affected population when needed. It is activated within 24-48 hours after an event (i.e. ﬂoods) to assist in rapid response to meet lifesaving needs. The tool captures best estimates of populations and the populations in need. These ﬁgures will be subsequently veriﬁed through the upcoming round of baseline assessment. Multi- Sectoral Location Assessments: MSLA will be designed in coordination with the IMWG, ISCG and other partners to gather additional information on multi-sectorial needs and the availability or gaps in basic services at each location to alert relevant responsible parties on the location of the most acute and vulnerable populations. The MSLA will be developed through demand and identiﬁed information.
