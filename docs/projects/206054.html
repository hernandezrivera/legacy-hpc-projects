Project Summary

Samaritan’s Purse (SP) proposes an integrated multisectoral humanitarian assistance with the aim of providing life-saving assistance and improving resilience by increasing access to health and nutrition services, increasing access to safe water supply and storage, and improving household food security through promotion of sustainable farming systems, and creation of alternative livelihoods to provide a comprehensive approach to addressing the underlying causes of food insecurity and malnutrition in Mayendit county.
Sustainable behavior change requires longer-term engagement but is key to enhancing resilience within beneficiary communities. In the proposed project, SP will work with community nutrition Care Groups (CGs) and key social leaders to create awareness, promote and support positive behavior change on issues of maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN).
The proposed project is designed to build on SP’s experience in implementing nutrition sensitive projects in Unity state and incorporate lessons learnt into the design and the implementation of this project. The proposed action will capitalize on existing local community and government strengths and targeting weaknesses in health systems. The proposed activities will focus on capacity building and community engagement and education to ensure that inputs are sustainable and there is a clear exit strategy for SP. Instead of creating inefficient and unsustainable parallel systems of health services provision, project activities will work within and strengthen existing government structures.
SP will collaborate with the county health department (CHD) and other health and nutrition implementing partners in Mayendit to support 8,324 children under five (U5s) and 2,898 Pregnant and Lactating women (PLW) through direct treatment of malnutrition. With support from WFP, SP will reach 3,525 children under two years and 2,938 PLWs through blanket supplementary feeding program (BSFP). Furthermore, 10,471 PLWs will be targeted for MIYCN activities through the Care Group Model. Additionally, SP will train 16 health care workers and 1,120 Care Group Members in skills essential in strengthening the implementation of high-impact nutrition interventions.
SP will continue providing community-based malnutrition preventive services to all children less than five years, and pregnant and lactating women, ensuring improved capacity in nutrition information systems and knowledge-generation.
Samaritan’s Purse will employ a phased approach with full project support at start-up and allow the responsibility to shift to local structures towards the end of the project period. Specific activities will be focused on building the community’s ability to maintain project gains. The project will invest in local structures such CGs, WMCS and lead farmers through capacity building, mentorship and input provision to empower them to carry on with the knowledge and skills gained beyond the life of the proposed project, in an effort to sustain the resilience building initiatives.
SP believes that building synergies with a wide range of partners enable us to achieve the aspired multi-sectoral outcomes with limited resources. In its commitment against food insecurity and malnutrition, SP established healthy working relationships with various partners and MOH at both national and local level coordinating its nutrition programming with the CHD who is in charge of service provision and supervision of health facilities. In the greater Mayendit County, SP will coordinate its activities with all the organizations present supporting health and nutrition, including UNIDO, IRC and CASS.

1. Needs

Despite sustained humanitarian response, Mayendit county continues to struggle with food insecurity and poor WASH services that leads to malnutrition. Malnutrition rates have remained high in Mayendit county; results from the April 2023 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey in found a critical Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 20.5% and a Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate of 4.3%. This rate is above the emergency threshold per World Health Organization (WHO) standards (above 15% GAM). The same report revealed an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 82.3%; Minimum dietary diversity (MDD) OF 36% and Minimum Adequate Diet (MAD) of 16.6%. According to South Sudan Acute Malnutrition Situation Projections for December 2023 - March 2024 and April - July 2024; Mayendit is classified as critical (IPC AMN phase 4).Poor hygiene practices among community members and the lack of safe water and sanitation facilities in the community also pose a threat to the health status of individuals. The April 2023 SMART found that only 11.7% of respondents had access to a latrine and only 8.8% practiced appropriate hand-washing with soap. These conditions leave communities at high risk for water-borne diseases and diarrhoea. Malnutrition and diarrheal disease are cyclically related as malnutrition weakens the body’s defences therefore increasing the risk and severity of diarrheal diseases, while diarrheal disease affects the body’s ability to absorb nutrients which poses great danger for populations already affected by malnutrition.Hence, there is a need to scale up nutrition programs in this area with integrated multi sector response through WASH, FSL, Protection to reach vulnerable communities.

2. Project activities

Nutrition Activities: The proposed project will work towards sustaining gains achieved and address the increasing humanitarian needs by continuing to provide lifesaving nutrition interventions of affected populations through coordinated, multi-sectoral and integrated response to acutely malnourished children under 5 (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women, ensuring improved capacity in nutrition information systems and knowledge-generation. Key activities will include: early detection of malnutrition through family MUAC approach; strengthening referral mechanisms; Formation and training of Care Groups on MIYCN; Biannual Vitamin A and deworming campaigns for CU5; and kitchen gardening activities through Care Groups. In addition, training of health workers and Care Group members on CMAM and MIYCN will be done. For treatment of acute malnutrition, SP will identify and refer malnourished cases for treatment to the existing nutrition partner UNIDO who is providing treatment services in Mayendit county. WASH Activities: the integrated interventions will target the “caretaker – severely malnourished child” and HH level, with the aim of breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle. The project will further aim at reducing prevalence of WASH-related diseases and risk of outbreaks through distribution of water treatment items for households and chlorination of boreholes including monthly water quality monitoring; engaging with target communities to construct their HH latrines; conduct hygiene promotion and distribution of WASH kits to SAM cases at OTP sites. FSL activities: Samaritan’s Purse’s FSL lifesaving intervention will focus on provision of dry season fast-growing vegetable kits and rainy season crop kits for immediate and long-term benefits. The integration approach will increase targeted community knowledge on climate-smart production of nutritious staples and vegetables through regular trainings and adaptive group learning methodologies and encouraging year-round cultivation of vegetables through a central demonstration learning approach for continuous skill transfer. To address the underlying causes of malnutrition, SP will provide nutrition sensitive approaches in agricultural production by integrating key nutrition sensitive approaches across all activities to enable the vulnerable farming communities to link their production to their HHs’ nutrition. High yielding nutritious crop varieties will be selected for diversification based on productivity and the potential to improve income in the local market. SP will also increase vulnerable HHs’ access to improved seeds which can increase crop yields, and training will be provided on good agronomic practices, crop pest and disease management, and skills for and knowledge of local seed selection, multiplication and storage. The interventions will adopt the Do No Harm principle to protect the environment through environmentally friendly agronomic practices, strategies to improve soil fertility, and the promotion of water conserving crops and drought tolerant crops. With the availability of fishing grounds and market potential, SP will also support fishing activities, as fish is a first-class protein. Gender Based Violence (GBV) activities: Samaritan’s Purse will integrate GBV in its project implementation focusing on prevention and risk mitigation. This will be integrated into the community awareness-raising activities to be undertaken through CGs and staff capacity building on safe programming, including safe and confidential referrals. Since SP is not providing specialized GBV services, it will ensure that survivors get access to required services through identified referral pathways.
