Project Summary

Due to the decline in agricultural productivity, contributing to food insecurity, the EFSVL team will prioritize supporting small scale farmers with the restoration of sustainable agriculture, taking into consideration climate change impact on Syria’s agriculture and ensuring its project designs do not negatively contribute to climate change. Through the proposed project Oxfam will ensure the highest possible level of integration with the local market systems. Therefore, Oxfam aims at supporting the agricultural production and food security levels through improved access to agricultural inputs (e.g. seeds, tools), basic rehabilitation of critical infrastructure, and support to the diversification of high nutritional and market value crops.
Oxfam will provide technical, financial and business support to farming and kitchen gardening to encourage food production and increase locally produced food supplies; it will facilitate back-up plans for farmers who cannot access their lands by providing alternative means of food security and income such as poultry and fodder, vocational training and start-up kits, e.g. in food processing. In close cooperation with local stakeholders, Oxfam will rehabilitate agricultural infrastructure to bring to basic functionality Oxfam will provide all the needed technical capacity building to ensure sustainable functionality of the rehabilitated infrastructure. Oxfam will rehabilitate damaged bakeries in crisis-affected areas to enable people to have better access to bread at affordable prices in the local markets.
Oxfam will utilize learning from the above programme, alongside research activities, to better understand the impact of different food security activities and the associated benefits, including positive protection outcomes, of different interventions, to inform future programmes.
Oxfam will ensure that each stakeholder involved in the project can safely participate in its activities. Safe programming (or protection mainstreaming) is implemented as part of the project cycle. Oxfam’s staff, volunteers and implementing partners are trained in safe programming and risk assessments. This includes the identification of the risks, possible targets, and risk prevention, mitigation and management measures: this is then implemented and reviewed regularly. Particular attention will be paid to addressing risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse and aid diversion, and a conflict sensitivity approach will be adopted.

4 - Needs

Oxfam performed several assessments in Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Deir ez Zor and Daraa, the team found that crisis affected families are facing serious difficulties obtaining sufficient bread to meet their daily requirements. There is severe lack in the supply of bread as the limited number of functioning bakeries have very little capacity. Relying on bakeries in other geographical areas is not always possible because of the transportation costs. Women reported protection risks as they walk for long distances at night to reach bread distribution points. As coping mechanism, some families started producing bread at home, however the costs of bread flour and fuel or gas are very high. This additional economic burden is negatively impacting the lives and well-being of families whose livelihoods have been severely affected by the conflict. Further compounding food insecurity are the limited livelihood opportunities. Oxfam found that farmers have a desire and energy to restart their farming businesses, however they are unable to irrigate their lands and to avail the needed agricultural inputs. For example, prior to the conflict in Deir ez Zor, farmers were dependant on the Euphrates river for irrigation. During armed conflict, farmers were unable to access their land while incurring damage to and loss of their assets by ISIS weaponizing the irrigation system , for example, further preventing farmers from reinitiating their livelihood practices. According to information received from Directorate of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform in Deir-ez-Zor, out of the 616 pumps that used to supply irrigation water to 49,326 farmers, only 47 pumps are working currently (132 pumps were damaged, 303 were stolen, 45 were vandalized, 89 were destroyed). This has left approximately 45,500 farmers with limited or no access to the irrigation systems, severely affecting their farming capacity, and thus local markets.

5 - Implementation of activities

Based on Oxfam’s approach on developing resilient livelihoods, which integrates humanitarian relief, socioeconomic development, and empowered advocacy through improving social protection/safety nets and strengthening market system, and in line with the FSA sector log frame, Oxfam proposes to implement the following activities: Distribute agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment; and provide related trainingSupport small-scale food production (horticulture, poultry-egg laying hens, market gardens)Support income-generating activities including vocational trainingSupport rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructures through appropriate modalities, including Canals, irrigation systems, markets, storage facilities, bakeries.Policy research comparing the impact of different food security interventions. Oxfam in Syria has developed a Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism (FCRM) to allow for lodging complaints or make suggestions to the organization. The mechanism functions under the PMEAL Manager/team and has close coordination with the programmes/operational team to resolve complaints in a timely and consistent manner. The project's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) system will be in line with Oxfam's Common Approach to MEL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) which will be adapted to the project context according to the Alternative Operation Procedures (AoP) for Syria. The project will complement primary data where possible with the use of information from secondary sources (e.g. UN agencies, local authorities, SARC, etc.) to inform ongoing situation and needs analysis and to triangulate monitoring data.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Occurrence of gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse including of minors: Oxfam staff, volunteers and partners are child protection gender and GBV aware; GBV is strongly mainstreamed in this project; Safeguarding accountability and reporting mechanisms are in place in Oxfam and partners.• Women may be harassed to get access to the rehabilitated community infrastructure: during the project implementation Oxfam will conduct awareness sessions on GBV and the role of women in their communities, besides to te extent possible Oxfam will ensure women participation in the management of the rehabilitated infrastructures• The project could be perceived as taking a side in the conflict due to favoring specific communities or social groups: for every project Oxfam will perform conflict sensitivity analysis in order to make sure that the project is enhancing connecting factors and minimizing dividing factors in the targeted locations

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Safe access is possible for Oxfam team, partners and contractors to asses needs and implement the planned activities;- Adherence to the humanitarian principles of independence, operational neutrality and neutrality- The selected most vulnerable farmers, especially women, are willing to restart their farming activities.- Needed approvals are obtained from Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor, Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform and the Ministry of Local Administration and Environment-Fund is received into the country- Impact on the budget due to the fluctuation in local currency is manageable Risks: - Security hampers access of staff, volunteers, service providers and supplies and services to project locations. Oxfam has a security management plan and security protocols which all staff are trained on and required to adhere to. Oxfam conducts continuous assessment of the situation; security protocols are frequently updated and adapted to ensure risks are minimized. In cases where prolonged security affects operations or the project as a whole, Oxfam will update OCHA and discuss scenarios for continued or adapted implementation. - Perception of breaching humanitarian principles: Carry out a monthly evaluation against Oxfam’s red lines with critical analysis of impartiality, voice and safety. Ensure implementation if needs based program serving the most vulnerable communities, conduct validation and triangulation of data to- Beneficiaries data protection is compromised : Conduct awareness sessions to staff, volunteers and partners on the legal compliance with the GDPR regulations, require all staff to take the online GDPR training, extend awareness discussion to partners and other key stakeholders and finally seek beneficiaries consent before sharing their info- Government approvals: delays in project approvals leading to delays in implementation. Oxfam has designed its project workplan making allowances for potential delays
