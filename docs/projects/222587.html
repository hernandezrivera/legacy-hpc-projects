Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) proposes to implement integrated preventive, curative, reproductive and mental health activities in Akobo County, Jonglei state and Abyei region in Abyei Administration Area through outreach, mobile and static sites. An estimated 6.3 million people will face acute food insecurity among which 1.71 million will be facing critical levels of food insecurity (IPC Report 2024). The situation is projected to worsen during the lean season with an estimated 2.53 million people facing critical levels of food insecurity of which women and children are most vulnerable. Akobo county and Abyei region are among the 49 counties facing critical levels of food insecurity and both have been categorized as priority locations for humanitarian assistance. Additional to food insecurity, interruptions in humanitarian aid delivery caused by forced disruptions due to conflict, insecurity and climatic shocks such as flooding contributing to low access of health services. Both counties have experienced an increase in disease burden with disease outbreaks e.g. measles, hepatitis E largely contributed by poor sanitation practices. The proposed integrated project seeks to reduce excess morbidity and mortality particularly among under-five children, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable groups by increasing access and utilization of quality integrated services for communicable and non-communicable diseases including reproductive health, SGBV prevention and mitigation, clinical management for rape survivors, immunization, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), screening for malnutrition and surveillance and control of epidemic prone diseases and response to public health emergencies. SC will support the State Ministry of Health (SMOH) and County Health Department (CHD) with delivery of the project and will continuously collaborate, coordinate and engage with the health cluster and other implementing partners in strengthening delivery of a coordinated health response to supported health facilities and communities. SC will also coordinate with the RRC in needs assessments and analysis aimed at prioritizing locations with unmet needs and delivery of client centered and age-appropriate interventions to the most vulnerable people. The project will implement activities aimed at achieving the following strategic objectives; 1. Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs; 2. Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action through AAP, protection mainstreaming and monitoring and; 3. Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is increased, and durable solutions to root causes of their vulnerability and shocks are enabled by humanitarian action.
The project will target 99, 855 (64,765 in Akobo County and 35,090 in Abyei region) people in need comprising of returnees, host communities and IDPs. Supported activities will include provision of curative consultation, antenatal care for pregnant women, safe delivery through skilled birth attendants at health facility, postnatal care for lactating women and their newborns, family planning counselling and services, nutrition screening for under-five children, PLWs, psychological first aid, clinical management for rape survivors and community-based service delivery through BHWs. Competencies and skills of health care workers will be continuously strengthened in collaboration with the CHD through supportive supervision, trainings, on-job coaching and mentorship. Communities will be engaged through consultative meetings, focus group discussions and client exit interviews in order to receive feedback and develop action points in improving the quality of services delivered.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces a complex humanitarian crisis that heavily impacts its health sector. Intercommunal conflict, insecurity, economic instability, effects of climate change including flooding and numerous disease outbreaks have contributed to the significant health challenges. Akobo county and Abyei region are among the counties in South Sudan largely affected by seasonal flooding, intercommunal conflict limiting accessibility of essential services including health leaving its communities in need of humanitarian assistance. Limited functionality of health services, poor coverage and limited capacity of qualified personnel have made it difficult to meet increasing humanitarian needs. Women, adolescent girls, under-five children and newborns are particularly vulnerable to the limited health, nutrition and WASH services. Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world with 1,223 per 100,000 livebirths and 98 children in every 1,000 livebirths die before their fifth birthday. Teenage pregnancy rate is estimated at 30 percent among girls between 15- and 19-year-olds with a very low contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods at 6 percent. Gender inequalities and GBV are widespread and perpetuated by several factors including socio-cultural norms and armed groups presence in the communities. The Sudan conflict saw over 855,000 refugees and returnees settle in South Sudan by October 2024 of which an estimated 44,917 and 35,000 were reported to have resettled in Akobo County and Abyei region respectively. The presence of returnees and refugees has placed a significant strain on an already weak and stretched health system and its limited resources in the counties. The average consultations per clinician ration, in the two counties, has increased from 40 to 80 consultations per day which is above the sphere standard. Diseases such as cholera, measles, meningitis, malaria, hepatitis E and other outbreaks remain a major threat. Malaria accounted for the highest disease burden at 39% followed by diarrhea at 10% among the curative consultations. Coverage of immunization services remain low with penta 3 coverage at 36%, measles 35% signifying the need for active case finding and the risk that under-five children have of suffering and dying from vaccine preventable diseases. Adolescent girls, pregnant, lactating women and their newborns continue to face challenges in accessing reproductive health services as basic emergency obstetric and newborn care services are not adequately provided in hard-to-reach areas due to limited functionality and reduced funding of health facilities. Coverage of antenatal services remains low with only 21% of pregnant women attended ANC clinics more than four times and received comprehensive ANC services through skilled personnel. The low coverage of comprehensive services predisposes pregnant women to risks during pregnancy as they are not adequately assessed for pregnancy risks and appropriate interventions such as prevention of malaria in pregnancy are untimely delivered. Coverage for deliveries assisted by a skilled birth attendant was only 14% as most of the deliveries were conducted in the community signifying for more effort in ensuring that safe and respectful services are provided to women in labor and their newborns. Many health facilities in the two counties lack even the most basic water, sanitation and hygiene services. This lack of services compromises the ability to provide basic, routine services and the ability to prevent and control infections. The lack of safe water, functional toilets, hand washing facilities, and waste management systems in healthcare settings pose significant health risks to patients, healthcare workers, and nearby communities. Availability of sustainable water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services is essential to the quality of care and infection prevention and control in healthcare facilities.

2. Project activities

SC will work with the State Ministry of Health (SMOH) and County Health Department (CHD) to support delivery of essential and life-saving health interventions in outreach, mobile and static facilities and through the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) volunteers aimed at increasing access and utilization of services among the underserved and vulnerable communities. The support will include technical, managerial, material, financial to enable the facilities provide quality client centered, age-appropriate and safe interventions to affected people. Additional staff will be deployed to the PHCC and collaboration with CHD will be strengthened in setting up and operation of planned mobile clinics as well as supporting community-based disease surveillance. The project will deliver its activities aligned to the health cluster strategic objectives. Strategic Objectives (SO): SO1: Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance meet their needs. SC will continue to support 6 PHCCs, 7 PHCUs and 8 mobile clinics in Akobo and Abyei to provide curative consultations for underfive children and adults, routine immunizations to children and women of child-bearing age, ANC, PNC, SBA, CMR for SGBV survivors, psychological first aid, nutrition screening, FP and SRH services to girls, boys, women and men. At community level, the trained BHWs will be engaged to work with health care workers, CNVs, M2MSG, TBAs and vaccinators to support with defaulter tracing of under-five children and PLWs requiring immunization, ANC, labour and delivery services through skilled personnel from the communities and refer them to health facilities. SC will focus on strengthening competencies and skills of health care workers to provide quality services through trainings, on-job coaching, mentorship and supportive supervision. Regular skills assessments will be conducted with health workers in promoting adherence to IPC, MNH standards, national guidelines and protocols when delivering health services. Post-natal care will be provided to lactating women and their newborns with focus on family planning counselling among women. SC will further engage with case workers and protection colleagues in strengthening safe recognition and appropriate referral of survivors of rape to services such as MHPSS and protection. Coordination with WHO, UNICEF and UNFPA will be maintained to secure drugs, medical supplies and equipment through the humanitarian pipeline for use in the outreach, mobile, static and bomas serving vulnerable communities with health services. SO2: Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action through AAP, protection mainstreaming and monitoring. Health care workers working in iCCM sites and PHCCs will be trained to safely recognize children with protection needs, adolescents and women surviving sexual abuse and refer them to appropriate service providers. The health workers will further be trained on basic skills to assist them recognize signs of common mental health conditions in children, adolescents, PLWs, women and men in Humanitarian setting. This will enable them provide basic support and recognize when to issue referrals for additional MHPSS care. Client exit interview and FDGs will support community feedback. SO3: Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is increased, and durable solutions to root causes of their vulnerability and shocks are enabled by humanitarian action. SC will collaborate with CHD to plan, assess and respond to acute emergencies, disease outbreaks, significant increases in morbidity, population displacements and other disasters. SC will work with CHD in providing complete and timely IDSR reports through the EWARNS and DHIS2 platforms.
