Project Summary

Key figures:
 9 350 PLHIV
 1 050 people living with TB
Total: 10 400 people living with HIV and TB
 12 monthly rounds of food assistance via CBT
 CBT value of UAH 825 per person per month to ensure 75 percent of daily energy requirement
 Total CBT value for beneficiaries estimated at USD 3 677 143 (applied exchange rate of 28 UAH/USD)
 Total implementation costs – 250 678 USD
Key results and activities:
 Improve food security of 10 400 people living with HIV (PLHIV) and people with TB residing in the conflict-affected areas of Donetsk and Lugansk regions GCA with high level of internally displaced persons (IDPs).
 Prevention of interruption of ART treatment among food insecure PLHIV and treatment of TB due to lack/absence of food.
 Promoting adherence to ART and TB drugs regimens among food insecure patients with low adherence to ART or TB and patients who have recently started treatment or are about to start ARV/TB treatment.
 Achieving UNAIDS goals in the fight against the spread of HIV epidemic 90-90-90.
 Improved TB treatment outcomes for increased number of people.
 Prevention of multi-drug resistant tuberculosis (MDRTB) because of interruption of TB treatment.
 Make it easier for food insecure conflict-affected PLHIV (with special focus on women, girls and all young children) to access nutritious foods and supplements.

Needs

• The situation for people living with HIV (PLHIV) and tuberculosis (TB) in conflict-affected Eastern Ukraine is critical. Most of them are severely food insecure and are living in extreme poverty. Many, especially women and children have been displaced by the conflict and lack access to social services and adequate housing. Health and HIV facilities were relocated and the quality of services in many facilities remains poor. Eastern Ukraine has the highest HIV and TB prevalence rates in the country and the conflict is driving new infections.• The Donetsk and Luhansk regions have the highest burden of IDPs - 497,546 people have been officially registered in the Donetsk region, in the Luhansk region - 219,442.• Prior to the outbreak of hostilities, the region carried a high burden of HIV infection - 644.5 per 100,000 of population: hence, 13645 PLHIV are officially registered in Donetsk oblast, GCA, and 2176 PLHIV in Luhansk oblast GCA (while the estimated prevalence is doubled); whereas the registered cases of TB in both oblasts are about 1900.• In Ukraine, there is still a high level of stigma and discrimination towards PLHIV, TB, including potential employers. Because of the military actions, many enterprises stopped their work, the influx of IDPs increased the burden on the infrastructure and oversaturated the labor market, and the chances of finding a job (taking into account the state of health) were significantly reduced, specifically for women-headed households. Moreover, according to assessment provided by NGO “Positive women” in March 2016, 35% of HIV positive women who are residing in the conflict areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblast GCA have faced gender-based violence.• Due to frequent refusals to IDPs to rent housing and employment, we can talk about the double stigmatization of PLHIV / IDPs by society. In 2017, cases of denial of ART from PLHIV-representatives of the host community and PLHIV-IDPs-were documented, both for lack of money for food, and to travel to the point of ART issuing.• The continuation of the military conflict, the poor economic situation in the regions such as closed enterprises, the destruction of the socio-economic infrastructure, resulting in high unemployment (the highest unemployment in Ukraine recorded in Donetsk and Lugansk regions (15.2% and 17%, respectively)) and low economic activity of the population are risk factors for the survival of PLHIV and the spread of the HIV epidemic in the region.• Conflict continues to exacerbate food insecurity and threatens adherence to ART among PLHIV.• Food insecurity levels remain high: 0.9 million moderately and/or severely food insecure persons ― 13,000 severely food insecure in government-controlled areas (GCA), and 103,000 severely food insecure persons in non-government-controlled areas (NGCA).(FSLC Trend Analyses, March 2018)• According to behavioral study of PLHIV, 63% of PLHIV can be classified as food insecure: 22% - not enough money even for food purchase, 41% - barely enough money for food purchase, but not for clothes and other goods (Study conducted by GFK Ukraine on request of All Ukrainian Network of PLHIV in Aug-Nov, 2016). Lack of food in the cold season and food security is one of the biggest problems in the territories of conflict zone. The risk of dying is 2-6 times higher in group of people with HIV, who are hungry in the first months of treatment. Hunger is also a major barrier to long-term adherence to therapy.• Lack of food security may hasten progression to AIDS-related illnesses, undermine adherence and response to ART, and exacerbate socioeconomic impacts of the virus. During implementation of joint Network-WFP HIV response in 2017-2018, direct correlation between adherence to ART and food assistance was identified. АRT interruptions decreased 4 times as compared to the period prior to the project beginning. In a number of locations, zero dropouts were observed since the launch of the food assistance.

Activities & outcomes

All-Ukrainian Network of PLHIV is created and works for HIV-positive people, as well as for patients with TB, Hepatitis and representatives of key affected populations.Our every action and decision are aimed to overcoming of the HIV/AIDS epidemic in Ukraine. The Network works with patients and for patients. We represent the interests of PLHIV in 25 regions of Ukraine and are struggling to ensure that 100% of patients with HIV have 100% access to treatment.Within the suggested scenario, Network plans to continue same activities, started in 2017 together with WFP, although would expand coverage to 10 400 food insecure conflict affected people living with HIV and those living with TB in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts of Ukraine (with special focus on women, girls and all young children).Beneficiaries would receive 12 monthly rounds of food assistance via CBT in the form of electronic vouchers, over 01.01-31.12. 2019.The almost USD 3 927 821 worth intervention would serve as transitional stage before planned scaling up the project in 2019 and expanding food assistance also to members of HIV/AIDS- and TB-affected households.Area of operation: conflict-affected GCA of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts of Ukraine• Evaluation of food security in the target group (at the beginning, after 6 months and at the end of project). Screening questionaire will be used for selection of beneficiaries. Criteria for entry: high food insecurity (food consumption score <21). Duration of intervention: until indicator of food security improves (FCS>35), but not more than 12 months.• Signing and/or extending agreements with local retailers to purchase vouchers in accordance with existing procurement procedures and based on feasibility, geographic coverage, and identified needs. The vouchers are made with no personal information, either supermarket staff or others can know beneficiaries status. The beneficiaries’ personal data is registered only in the Network central office! • Network plans to coordinate with other humanitarian organizations working with the same target groups, ministry of health and FSLC• Distribution of e-vouchers among beneficiaries in 12 rounds (1 per month) through four NGO (Charitable organization "Club"Svitanok”, Charitable organization "Vybir", CO “Nasha Dopomoga” and Lugansk regional branch of Network PLHIV). Monthly entitlement is 825 UAH, in line with FSLC recommendations. Network coordinate their work, receive and audit distribution as well as financial reports on a monthly basis. • Consultation for beneficiaries provided by medical/social workers (based on AIDS centers and ART sites - places where beneficiaries receive their medical services and treatment) , dissemination of information materials on healthy and adequate nutrition in conditions of limited financial resources;• Assessment of adherence to ART and evaluation of treatment effectiveness (at the beginning, after 6 months and at the end of project);• Monitoring, data collecting and reporting. Narrative, financial and distribution reports according to donor requests or submitted on a quarterly basis. • Monitored beneficiary's CBT expenses on a monthly basis;• Evaluated food security index of beneficiaries on a semi-annually basis; • Prevented interruption of ART treatment among PLHIV and treatment of TB due to lack/absence of food.• Promoted adherence to ART and TB drugs regimens among patients with low adherence to ART or TB and patients who have recently started treatment or are about to start ARV/TB treatment.• Improved TB treatment outcomes for increased number of people.• Prevented cases of multi-drug resistant tuberculosis (MDRTB) because of interruption of TB treatment.• Simplified access to nutritious foods and supplements for food insecure conflict-affected PLHIV (with special focus on women, girls and all young children).
