Project Summary

The project aims to respond to 16,900 HH with a total of 96,000 disaggregated roughly as (21,120 men, 23,040 women, 25,920 boys, 25,920 girls) of the most vulnerable conflict-affected IDPs, and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, and prioritizing households headed by female, child, disabled, or elderly, then households with severely ill or disabled members, children below 5, elderly, pregnant or lactating women; with special attention to those recently displaced or living overcrowded.
The project contributes to providing life-saving and life sustaining shelter & NFI assistance through the following activities:
1- provide 6,300 HH with seasonal and supplementary NFIs and assistance including winter clothing, stoves, and fuel in Al-Bab, Afrin, and Azaz subdistricts, and camps. Each household will receive voucher of 150$ and prioritize its needs of these items accordingly. Moreover, 2000 HH will also receive their assistance through in kind modality.
2-Provision of emergency shelter through instalment of 3,500 tents with associated infrastructure in (Azaz, suran) in Aleppo governorate, (Dana, Maaret Tamsrin) in Idleb governorate.
3- Upgrading of 500 unfinished building with average cost of 850$ per unit in Aleppo (Al Bab, Suran, Daret Azza), Idleb (Harim, Salqin, Iskat, Kafr Takharim, Dana).
This project will also Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing through rehabilitation of 700 of partially damaged houses with an average cost of 750$ per house in (Harim, Salqin, Iskat, Kafr Takharim, Dana) in Idleb governorate, (Azaz, Al-Bab) in Aleppo governorate.
4-Construction 400 transitional shelter (concrete blocks) for 400HH in Azaz and Al-Bab districts.
Needs assessment was conducted early this year to identify the project locations.
In all activities SDI considers the protection of the beneficiaries, so the distribution centres and the shops provide the assistance will be prepared with functional firefighting equipment, brochures of safety and security practice will be distributed to beneficiaries, Moreover female staff will be recruited and trained to ensure more protection from exploitation and sexual abuse for girls and women and to deal with any GBV cases may encounter during the project.
Most importantly SDI pays high attention to people with disabilities, elderlies, and people with special needs and for those assistance will be delivered to home.
Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights of shelter sites, shelter beneficiaries, and house owners will be considered throughout all stages of shelter repair interventions, and agreement will be signed to guarantee the rights of the beneficiaries. Coordination with SNFI cluster and other active members is essential to ensure geographical separation and avoid overlapping/duplication.

4 - Needs

without adequate resources and due to the general economic deterioration in northwest Syria, people in need have continued to resort to negative and harmful coping mechanisms. According to SNFI HNO 2021 summary report, Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute. A significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement. In addition, returns have begun to some areas, and new IDPs swell already overburdened communities, especially in North-east and North-west Syria, resorting to living in unsustainable settlements with poor access to basic services. Adequate shelter with appropriate hygiene facilities is critical for people’s living standards, physical health, mental well-being and protection needs. The severity of shelter needs has increased slightly across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from2020 by four per cent to 5.88 million people in 2021.Access to NFIs is seen as lifesaving, especially during winter when vulnerable families are unable to access basic items to keep them warm, such as heating fuel, blankets and winter clothes. Despite the improved stability, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 37 per cent from 2020 to 4.69 million PiN in 2021 Within these affected populations, the MSNA data also shows that female-headed households, youth, older persons, and people with functional difficulties have reduced access to basic services, including shelter, as well as reduced livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to NFIs and adequate shelter. This places them at further risk of developing negative coping mechanisms as they may have to take on debt or resort to unsuitable living arrangements to meet their basic living needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

SDI will engage with local communities through holding meetings and/or focus groups with prominent figures, older persons, and women in order to inform them of the project goals, increase awareness to the project activities and coordinate beneficiary selection process to reach the most vulnerable persons and ensure impartiality. SDI will engage with local communities through holding meetings and/or focus groups with prominent figures, older persons, and women in order to inform them of the project goals, increase awareness to the project activities and coordinate beneficiary selection process to reach the most vulnerable persons and ensure impartiality. In 2.1.1 Rehabilitation of damaged houses and 1.4.2 Upgrading of unfinished buildings, SDI announces the project and open a registration office to receive applications directly from house owners and beneficiaries and check the HLP documentations, MEAL staff will conduct community HLP verification to 100% of houses. The technical staff, will conduct a technical field inspection of all shelter units to verify that they are safe and repairable, prepare BOQs and scope of work needed in each unit, and closely supervise the works of contractors to ensure quality rehabilitation works as well as media staff will document the works in each house .Beneficiaries will be hosted in delivered houses after signing house owner- beneficiary free tenancy agreement. In activities 1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuel, SDI will receive the beneficiary lists and pay visits to a random sample of beneficiaries to verify they meet the project vulnerable criteria. The modality of delivering the assistance is restrict voucher, cash and in-kind.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Beneficiaries are at risk on being exposed to any sexual misconduct by the humanitarian workers and they might not report the misconduct due to the following reason: 1. lack of knowledge of reporting mechanisms, especially for people with disabilities and women living in faraway areas. 2. feeling afraid of retaliation. 3. fear of stigma. 4. fear of deprivation of assistance. 5. being unable to understand written banners for being illiterate and/or for having a visual disability. 6. for children, language might be complicated To mitigate these risks, SDI will:1. aim at clearly announcing the hotline number and email specialized for PSEA of SDI and UN numbers. through many means, like, banners, social media, and messages to beneficiaries’ phones through telegram channel to ensure reaching to the biggest number of beneficiaries including women living in faraway areas and people with disabilities. 2. Conduct PSEA training for all staff members and contractors/suppliers dealing with beneficiaries and make them read and sign CoC that state Zero Tolerance towards SEA violations. 3. Conduct awareness raising sessions on PSEA for beneficiaries to encourage them to report any violations and stress on the confidentiality and on eliminating any other fears that they might have.4. Conduct awareness raising sessions for children in an appropriate language. 5. complaint boxes6. Using PSEA field and organizational checklists tools.
