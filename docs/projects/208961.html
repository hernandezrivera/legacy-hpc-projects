Project Summary

The escalation of violence in April 2023 had forcibly displaced at least 3 million children and Over 19 million school-aged children are expected to stay out of school in the 2023–2024 academic year because of the displacement. Unpaid teachers, and schools’ infrastructure damage resulting from the conflict. This number includes 6.4 million school-aged children directly affected by the conflict. Children will continue to miss out on urgent psychosocial support, protection from physical threats, social-emotional learning, and recreational services. Lack of education will also increase protection risks for children, exposing them to the risk of early marriage, child labor, trafficking, forced recruitment, and GBV. Teacher shortages were prevalent because of salary instability, inadequate training, and a lack of capacity to cater to all children’s needs. SC through this one-year action purposefully designed in response to the above challenges and needs to contribute to the impact of the conflict in Central Darfur, Gadarief, Sinner, Aljazeera, and River Nile states and provide access to safe, inclusive, and quality education to newly displaced vulnerable, crisis-affected children and new returnees aged 6-18 years, as well as children at risk of dropping out, and ensure retention for girls and boys including (CWDs). The project aligns with the 2024 HRP strategic objectives and priorities by responding to sector objectives (1) and (2) through formal and informal education, this project is designed to target 630,000 school-aged children (50% Girls), of which 15% are CWDs. Activities include the provision of teaching and learning materials, recreational and hygiene kits accompanied with hygiene messages and waste disposal, provision of safe drinking water stations, establishment of temporary hand washing facilities, rehabilitation of damaged classrooms, and WASH facilities following the universal design to accommodate all vulnerable children, including CWDs. Teachers will be capacitated through teachers' training in psychosocial skills and will be aligned with CP subsector guidelines, which focus on the provision of teachers and children's well-being for a positive enabling environment. To ensure the continuation of education services and investment following the crisis, teachers will be incentivized through payments to continue teaching; the incentive payments will consider conflict sensitivity and promote safety, equity, and inclusion to avoid tensions that result from the influx of IDPs to host communities. This action will provide training for PTA members and teachers on safe school, GBV issues, MHPSS, the provision of social-emotional learning to the crisis-affected children, children’s rights, and violence-free learning spaces, the establishment of referral pathways to support the most vulnerable children and will relate to schools with a diversity of PSS services for the children. The selection of targeting states is based on accessibility, severity, and protection situations and aims for 100 percent coverage of the accessible states, 60 percent in partially accessible, and 30 percent in hard-to-reach areas when the security situation allows. This project will ensure inclusivity and equity considering equal access to boys, girls, and (CWDs) gender-appropriate needs of diverse needs of IDPs, host communities, and returnees.SC will follow a complementarity approach by building on the GPE program in Aljazeera, and ECHO in CD. The team will work in close coordination with UNICEF and other INGOs/NGOs during implementation to avoid duplication and create synergy and complementarity. SC will follow the conflict sensitivity approach throughout the project time frame and the selection of beneficiaries will consider the diversity of IPDs, and host communities.SC will partner with the State High Council of People with Disabilities to ensure that CWDs learning and well-being are adequately met.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The main objective of this action is to protect the physical, emotional, and psychosocial well-being of affected children. This will be achieved through a set of activities that target teachers, PTAs, and caregivers. To achieve this goal, schools will be located near the children's communities and awareness sessions will be delivered to all stakeholders including local partners, to inform them about Save the Children's child safeguarding policies. To minimize the risk of protection threats on the project beneficiaries, teachers and PTA members will be trained on the code of conduct, positive discipline, GBV, and PSEA. The relevant community protection structures will be strengthened, and gender-inclusive community protection focal points will be selected and actively engaged in project activities at the community level to increase the voice of vulnerable people. They will also be involved in decision-making discussions to increase ownership and participation of communities. Additionally, a letter of commitment will be signed to hold them accountable for children's safety. To ensure the protection and safety of all children, including those with disabilities, policies for safe programming will be delivered.

4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will establish a feedback and reporting mechanism that will empower all beneficiaries, including children, to directly voice feedback and complaints, which will then be taken up by the program team to amend the implementation approach as needed.SC and its partners will consult the affected populations to determine their preferred accountability mechanism among FGDs, hotlines, open-end free text, feedback boxes, one-to-one meetings, and focal points will be assigned through the SC accountability system. The reports will then be shared with relevant and, reports on PSEA and abuse will be treated with confidentiality to safeguard focal person. The safeguarding focal person will also coordinate with other agencies if any reports and concerns are related to them in the same operational areas. Monthly accountability reports will be shared with the assigned project staff to review feedback and respond effectively which will contribute to the improvement of the activities.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Save the Children will conduct awareness training and deliver capacity capacity-building sessions for partners, teachers on PSEA, and child safeguarding, and ensure everyone understands their roles and responsibilities, which include establishment and management of inclusive feedback and complaints mechanisms, preventing PSEA, responding to complaints and ensuring confidentiality and protection of affected people. Regular assessments will also be conducted to identify gaps and areas for improvement in the feedback and accountability mechanisms. This includes surveys, focus group discussions, or other participatory methods to gather feedback from affected populations and stakeholders.
