Project Summary

South Sudan has been through very long and sharp shocks as a result of armed conflict and other cooperating factors such as economic crisis, climatic variability, lack of access to basic services and instability of systems that supply food to markets.
According to November 2015 Resilience Context Analysis (RCA), some of the key shocks identified included high food prices and economic shocks, insecurity as a result of violent conflict, hydro-meteorological shocks such as flood or drought, human, animal or crop disease outbreaks . This web of interrelated factors continues to widen the level of vulnerability as well as affecting household’s ability to resist food insecurity, malnutrition and hunger. Leer has experienced huge wave of conflict crisis with massive displacement to swampy areas further South of the State and Bentiu PoC. This crisis has caused extreme depletion of livelihoods resulting into extreme level of food insecurity. According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (September 2018-March 2019), populations in leer and Mayendit in former Unity State were expected to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). This situation has not changed and according to IPC (May –June 2019), food security situation continues to deteriorate with lean season characterised by food shortages, high food prices, presence of IDPs and returnees. This is a very threatening position in terms of food security and livelihoods and if this situation goes unabated, it might lead to further catastrophe in the short run.
In the context of Revitalised Agreement on Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) signed September 2018, it is expected that more stability will be restored across the country and this will create an enabling environment for possible return to areas of origin and communities will once again begin re-build their livelihoods as well as strengthening their absorptive capacities to deal with future shocks and stressors.
Against this backdrop, RCDI proposes to provide livelihood support to 4,965 households through direct distribution of emergency livelihood kits to food insecure households in former County of Leer, Southern Unity. This intervention aims at providing emergency livelihood support to the most at risk population and protecting vulnerable households affected by the conflict crisis against hunger and malnutrition. It will also contribute to improved food/nutrition security as well as diversification of sources of income. This intervention contributes to FSL Cluster Objective 2 (Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable, crop seeds and fishing kits and livestock support).
In terms of targeting, RCDI shall adopt Gender with Age marker’s framework in it’s entire process of the project intervention i.e. by considering both age and gender so as to ensure gender equality. Men, women, boys and girls will be targeted in the process with special attention placed on standard vulnerability criteria i.e. targeting severely food insecure households, persons with disabilities, child- and women-headed households, households, elderly people and above targeting of beneficiaries will also be based livelihood category of the beneficiaries (agriculturalists, agro-pastoralists, riverine/fishing). This process will also take into account geographical areas in terms of selection of Bomas and this will be done in collaboration with local authorities, RRC, women and youth representatives and local chiefs. RCDI and RRC and community leaders will conduct joint beneficiary verification exercise including physical verification of the households and the final verified list would be used as basis for distribution of vegetable and fishing kits.

1 - Needs

According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (September 2018-March 2019), populations in leer and Mayendit in former Unity State said to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). IPC (May –June 2019) indicated that food security situation continues to deteriorate due to number of multiple factors such as armed conflict and climatic variability continue to limit the efforts of increasing food production for example, in 2019, the county has experienced erratic rainfalls as wet season started late in June and this, resulted into late planting. Trade flows into and out of Leer have always been disrupted by insecurity and poor road infrastructure. There is high likelihood of early depletion of little available food stocks before the next lean season given the reality of other cooperating factors such as possible returns of the displaced population from other areas of displacement such as Bentiu PoC- that might exert more pressure on limited resources. Livelihoods have been extremely depletion and capacities to cope have been eroded and if this situation goes unabated, it might lead to further catastrophe in the short run. Against this backdrop, RCDI proposes to provide livelihood support to 4,965 households through direct distribution of emergency livelihood kits to food insecure households in former County of Leer, Southern Unity. This intervention aims at providing emergency livelihood support to the most at risk population and protecting vulnerable households affected by the conflict crisis against hunger and malnutrition. It will also contribute to improved food/nutrition security as well as diversification of sources of income. This contributes to FSL Cluster Objective 2 (Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable, crop seeds and fishing kits and livestock support).

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Community Mobilization and sensitization at County/Payam/Boma levelRCDI shall mobilize the communities in Leer on the purpose of the project, quantity of kits to be distributed and this shall involve conducting preliminary meetings in the selected Payams/Bomas with local community leaders, elders, including local authorities both at county and payam/Boma levels. In this preliminary process all stakeholders shall review and build consensus on identification and selection of beneficiaries as shall be specified in the distribution Plan. RCDI will provide SSHF with detailed report of the consultative meetings and decision reached on identification and selection of beneficiaries. Activity 2: Beneficiaries identification/registration RCDI shall target food insecure households for emergency intervention and it will target all categories of the affected population i.e. host communities, IDPs and returnees in the designated areas. All necessary arrangement shall be made to ensure that gender and age dimensions are well integrated into the process, meaning both men and women, boys and girls of various should be equally targeted by the intervention.Activity 3: Collection and distribution of emergency livelihoods kitsRCDI shall collect inputs from designated hubs and transport the inputs to the targeted locations for distribution. All required procedures and process shall be duly followed such as signing of all waybills, certification of goods received. All efforts shall be exerted on engaging the partner for early delivery of inputs to the hubs, which will also ensure timely delivery and distribution of inputs to the beneficiaries. The targeted HHs shall sign distribution forms and copies will be shared with SSHF/FSL Cluster responsible focal points. Activity 4: Rapid Post-Distribution Monitoring Immediately after direct distribution, RCDI FSL Project Manager and M&E Officer shall conduct Post-Distribution Monitoring targeting at least 10% of the beneficiary households. The exercise shall focus on obtaining rapid feedback from the beneficiaries in relation to timing of the inputs, quantity and quality of the inputs.Activity 5: Submission of Narrative Reports (Progress and Final) on implemented activitiesRCDI shall submit to SSHF progress and final reports on completed and achieved activities, especially on community mobilization and sensitization, identification and registration of beneficiaries, distribution activities. All required copies both hard and electronic shall be submitted to SSHF for review and approval.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The need to deliver strong, comprehensive agriculture sector programmes will has been a crucial strategy for the Government of South Sudan. The government has recognised agriculture as key engine for economic growth and employment for local population. This response will contribute towards food security as well as diversification of sources and eventually created linkage to market through sales of vegetables and fish.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The Government of South Sudan through its developed strategy (South Sudan development Plan as updated) advocates for sustained increase in cereal crop production, and overall production increase of other major foods. The strategy has also been geared towards the reintegration of returnees and the Country’s wider recovery, resilient, and development efforts

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

With September 2018 R-ARCSS it is expected that the current peace deal will result into comparative level of stability and hence, providing opportunity to communities to engage in productive activities. Therefore, this intervention will response to immediate needs of affected communities thus, enabling them to improve their resilience to recurrent shocks as well as creating a transition from chronic dependency on free humanitarian assistance to sustainable food security, livelihoods and development. Providing emergency livelihood kits through distribution of vegetable and fishing kits, will contribute to closing short hunger gaps and finally, such intervention sits within the broader global strategy for achieving zero hunger (Sustainable Development Goals No.2).

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

RCDI will employ key strategies for successful transition, which will include strengthening the capacities of local stakeholders, farmers, fishermen and women to sustain the benefits generated by the project. Through orientation on vegetable and fish production, communities will be empowered to continue with similar activities even after the end of the project. RCDI will create and develop partnerships with private and public sectors and other donors and will mobilize both external and local resources.
