Project Summary

The Project will provide multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to 3,360 acutely vulnerable households to allow them to meet their basic needs. Both emergency and regular MPCA will be provided depending on the needs and context. Emergency MPCA comprised of 1 to a maximum of 3 rounds will be provided to 1,920 vulnerable households to meet their most urgent needs immediately after a shock. Regular MPCA comprising at least six rounds will provided to 1,440 acutely vulnerable households faced with multiple needs especially in underserved communities where there is no regular food assistance support. Across NES, devastating water scarcity, weakening local currency, the continued impact of the Covid-19 pandemic, and a bad 2020/2021 agricultural season has drastically increased humanitarian needs with many households struggling to meet their basic needs. The cumulative impact of these factors has led to a high prevalence of acutely vulnerable households that the project will aim to specifically target with MPCA assistance to allow these households to meet their basic needs. Acutely vulnerable households will be identified through multi-sectoral vulnerability assessment with targeted questions to identify whether cash is the most appropriate form of assistance by looking at beneficiary preference, market accessibility and safety. This follows guidance produced by the Cash Working Group (CWG). Post distribution monitoring will also be conducted to ensure beneficiaries preferred cash over other types of assistance and did not face any barriers or protection risks as a result of receiving cash assistance. Market impact assessments will also be conducted to monitor the impact that the cash transfers have on the availability and quality of core staple items in the local markets of intervention and to ensure that cash transfers don't negatively impact the local economy.
The project will target the following acutely vulnerable households:
Head of household: Female, child, elderly or disabled
Households with two or more persons with a disability or chronic illness
Food security profile: severely food insecure
Income profile: Households with low to very low income
Livelihoods coping strategies: Crisis or emergency

3 - Needs

After over a decade of violent conflict, humanitarian needs across Syria remain severe with the continued need for emergency humanitarian assistance to meet the needs of over 13.4 million in need (HNO, 2021), over 6 million of whom are IDPs living in protracted displacement (HNAP, IDP series, 2021). According to 2022 HNO figures, in NES, a total of 282,150 individuals live in last resort sites, including formal camps (86,622 individuals), informal camps (43,372 individuals), collective centres (24,382 individuals) and informal settlements (127,774 individuals). The collapse of the Syrian pound combined with the secondary and tertiary impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic has had a devastating impact on households across Syria with many struggling to meet their basic needs in turn leading to continuous displacement trends as households move to camps and sites due to the inability to pay rent in urban centres. 84% of IDP households assessed by HNAP across NES reported that monthly income was insufficient to meet basic needs with householdsincreasingly reliant on harmful negative coping mechanisms to survive. Moreover, the onset of the triple water crisis caused by a lack of rainfall and the alarmingly low level of the Euphrates throughout 2021 has drastically impacted access to WASH services and led to significant losses in agricultural production, the effects of which will be felt in the coming year (HNO, 2022). Overall, needs are increasingly multi-sectoral.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project identifies key protection risks around MPCA distributions processes and the possibility of causing tension between IDP and host communities. Mitigation measures will include the following; - distributions to be conducted early in the day to ensure beneficiaries can come and go with daylight. - Women and men will have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female field workers. - the duration between assessment and distribution is kept to a minimum to avoid large distributions and to help deliver assistance to beneficiaries as soon as possible. - Whenever possible and appropriate staff from the area will carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks and good community acceptance. It ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness to the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. The NGO will hold consultations with both host and IDP communities to ensure social cohesion and reduced tensions


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project prioritises the importance of PSEA by explicitly addressing the issue in its Programme Participant Protection Policy and staff Code of Conduct, which together define the project's expectations of staff and partners, their responsibilities as well as a mandatory duty to report in cases of potential abuse. These policies are backed up by a Community Response Mechanism open to and accessibly to staff, beneficiaries and the general public to provide multiple means of reporting. In the specific case of Syria, The project is committed to supporting and engaging with the current Inter-Agency Standing Committee PSEA Expansion Plan for the Whole of Syria - to date three staff have been trained as internal focal points for PSEA and are involved on rolling out PSEA training and awareness sessions for the rest of The project's staff.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access.Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations.
