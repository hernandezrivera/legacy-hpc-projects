Project Summary

World Vision proposes an Emergency Shelter and Non Food Items (ES/NFI) intervention that seeks to respond to the needs of 40,000 very vulnerable newly internally displaced persons as a result of armed conflicts and the expected returnees in various locations. The primary objective of the response is to protect and save lives of the internally displaced persons and returnees. The project will seek to help these vulnerable individuals with coping mechanisms that are combination of in-kind distribution, cash transfers and the use of locally available resources to better their lives. Where possible, the use of locally available materials will be encouraged as these materials are considered to be more sustainable than the in kind distribution, they will be done alongside the cash interventions in selected locations. With the Signing of the peace deal by the warring parties, it is expected that the country might expect a lot more of returnees than new displacements. Most of these are expected to return to their previous homes with limited materials and therefore would need support to ensure that they become self-sustaining and building of their livelihoods. Therefore, the 2019 project will seek to respond to the needs of these vulnerable IDPs and returnees with the view of making their shelter needs and livelihoods more self-sustaining than depending on continuous donor support. This however would only be possible if these locations of response experience sustainable peace. The response will be done within the prioritized locations and any other locations that might experience new displacements or returnees during the project period. The organization seeks to achieve this through the utilization of its frontline mobile team and the experienced static staff in its various operational areas within the country. WVSS will continue to be part of the responses that are multi-cluster and multi- agency. This will ensure that the needs of the affected populations are served in a more holistic and shortest possible response time.

Needs

The warring parties in South Sudan just signed a peace deal on 12th September 2018 after a 5-year armed conflict that has seen over 4 million people seek refuge in neighbouring countries and thousands displaced sometimes multiple times within the country. The resulting effects of this civil conflict has been a very high need for shelter and other essential non-food items for the affected populations. In 2019, if the peace deal that has been signed holds, the county is expected to see a lot of returnees in various locations. These returnees are likely to be a combination of those who are able to fend for themselves and those who are very vulnerable in need of assistance for them to cope. The other possibility of people who will need support are those who have previously been in highly militarised locations where humanitarian actors hardly reach. These people will need assistance as they are likely to be accessible as a result of the whole country being peaceful. The third category will be, in case some parts of the country continue to experience conflicts, the people who are likely to be displaced in those locations. The shelter needs for displaced populations is key in protecting lives from extreme weather conditions and disease attack.In 2018, a lot of conflicts were reported in the Equatorias, Jonglei and Unity states. These locations are the states with the highest needs of Emergency shelter and Non Food Items. These areas could also see a rise in the returnees but could also possibly continue to have conflicts and therefore are expected to be the states with high needs. Western Equatoria saw a lot of armed conflicts and displacements in 2018 with access challenges to many locations in need of assistance. These locations are likely to see access restrictions eased up and people who were displaced in those locations returning to their original homes, following the recently signed peace deal. World Vision will then access locations which were highly militarised in Western Equatoria and be able to provide assistance to the very vulnerable populations. Locations like Nagero which had seen almost the whole population displaced might also have people returning in 2019. This is likely to be so in many locations within Western Equatoria. However, this is only going to happen once the signed deal stands and all warring parties abide to the agreement. Jonglei and Unity states are the other locations that experienced increased armed conflicts resulting in many civilians being displaced in 2018 and the previous years in general. Upper Nile saw less displacements in 2018 but this was not so in the previous years. In some of these locations the affected populations were displaced multiple times over the five-year conflict period. With the signed peace deal, there is a possibility of these locations seeing an increase in the number of returnees who might need support with the aim of increasing their resilience. Returnees are expected in Equatoria regions and the Greater Upper Nile locations in general. WVSS will respond in the proposed locations as the past trends have indicated that these locations will continue to be highly affected by the ongoing conflicts or the likely hood of high returnees in various locations of the county. In order to ensure that the most vulnerable people are supported in these prioritized locations, World vision will conduct needs based ES/ NFI assessments before any response is recommended in those locations. All the support that WVSS will render to the affected populations will be on need basis considering the limited resources that are available compared to the country needs. The project will ensure internal synergies with other WVSS programs such as cash programming for areas where the markets will be viable and accessible. Protection concerns within the affected areas will be identified and referred to the protection team for their information and support. This will be the case with other main sectors.

Activities

1. Conduct needs assessments, (direct and inter agency) to identify beneficiaries in need of Shelter and NFI (focusing on particular needs of women, men, girls and boys).2. Carry out verification, registration and 3. Distribution of ES- NFIs to identified beneficiaries (taking into consideration the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys)4. Deploy mobile teams to conduct emergency response in deep field locations as and when required5. Conduct rapid monitoring /post distribution monitoring (PDM)6. Set up community decision making groups in static and accessible locations where NFIs/shelters will be distributed. These decision groups will also be the medium for community feedback in support to the feedback that will be given by individual beneficiaries.7. Lead the NFI state-level cluster coordination in Western Equatoria.8. Co-lead the ES-NFI cluster at national level9. Submit Monthly stock/distribution reports to the cluster/state cluster coordinators10. Ensure internal synergy with other sectors such as WASH, Nutrition, Protection, Cash programming, and FSL. This will involve active referral of cases to relevant departments for their immediate response.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will be based on protection risk analysis considering the specific needs of different population groups. In focus group discussions with the affected populations, 50% of people interviewed will be men and 50% women. Women groups will be interviewed by female team members to ensure free community and sharing of protection concerns with the project team. Community based project committees will include women and men at equal proportions to ensure both sexes and genders are represented equally. World Vision South Sudan has an experienced Gender and Protection adviser whom the team will consult from time to time to provide insights on ways to handle protection and gender concerns in the communities where the project will be implemented.The project will identify specific protection risks in each area of intervention and aim to tailor activities to address those risks. For each risk that will be identified, a level of severity, impact and likelihood will be ascertained and managed accordingly.
