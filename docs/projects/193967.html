Project Summary

Starting from March 2021, Sudan hosts over 1 million refugees from the South Sudan newly borne country. Same of refugees live out - of - camp settlements, with host communities and urban and rural areas, while others stay in 13 refugees' camps ( in Alliat locality -ND ) This in addition to the high annual inflation rate in Sudan, which reached over 300 per cent in January 2022, according to the Central Bureau of Statistics, and shortages of basic commodities, including fuel, food, medicine, and other products. The Sudanese Pound continues to depreciate rapidly, further eroding families ' purchasing power and the ability to provide necessary hygiene and sanitation kits for themselves. IPC's estimates that 7.1 million people (16 per cent of the population) were acutely food insecure in December 2020. The COVID - 19 pandemic also hit Sudan in 2020, resulting in at least 19,747 recorded cases and 1,301 deaths as of December 8 , 2020 , COVID - 19 is having a devastating impact on the health and wellbeing of people across Sudan including South Sudanese Refugee to severe economic , social , public health and WASH implications. Meanwhile, other disease outbreaks, food insecurity, malnutrition, and climatic shocks continue to affect many Sudanese 'lives and livelihoods'. However, In Alliat locality of North Darfur, currently total refugee stands at 29,679 in 13 camps. The refugees have poor access to the limited basic services including water supplies facilities, water coverage is renaming below desired standard of 20/1/p/d. While an inadequate number of latrines and access to potable water remains a challenge. In addition, there are the need for provision of sanitary & dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age and hand washing soap to improve the general conditions through support operation beside rehabilitation and upgrading of water sources, solarisation of exiting water sources and construction of a hybrid family / household share latrines as well as the facilities centres such nutrition’s, health units. Moreover most of the refugee have poor access to safe drinking water and improved sanitation and hygiene services & support, the dire economic situation resulting in fuel shortages. Most of the water supply interventions in North Darfur -Allait has focused on the rehabilitation of existing water facilities to ensure adequate water supply. These however, do not meet the minimum standards in most cases, therefore the drilling of new boreholes in the areas with high potential underground water and rehabilitations and construction of new Water Source to increase water supply is needed to serve both the refugees and the host communities. Average water coverage across most of site hosting refugee is stand at 14L/P/d which is below UNHCR desired standards of 20/1/p/d. Open defecation is widely practiced among the SSR communities. To promote best hygiene practices, hygiene promoters trained in some SSR location and host community need to continue encouraging proper use and maintenance of sanitation facilities. In terms of sanitation maintained, it has been observed that communal latrines are not used properly and not being used by the users, resulting in high operational cost , and continuing widespread open defecation in North Darfur compared to other states with refugees. Since mid - 2017, there has been a shift of the approach from communal latrines to family / household shared latrines. UNHCR in consultation with partners and line ministries has made a strategic decision and moved away from communal latrines to household latrines and family shared latrine where there is space challenges to foster a sense of ownership to the users and makes usage, cleaning and maintenance a responsibility of those households sharing the service.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The long terms conflict and continuation displacement of SSR in Darfur has made children, girls, and women more vulnerable to various forms of abuse and exploitation. In their daily life, women and girls face a significant risk of abuse. Women and girls travelling to far places for various reasons, such as to collect water and fire wood, and even to distant market places, face different kinds of abuse. Domestic violence is also common. For SAHARI understanding how men and women are affected differently by conflict and displaced of SSR and hosting in the affected existing communities, and designing the right programs and projects that address gender issues is a priority, with the aim of preventing and responding to GBV risks. As a result of this, SAHARI has rolled out the DO NO HARM APPROACH in numerous contexts in addition to CLTs approach amid to let community-to lead total sanitation methodology using specific participatory processes and diagram tools which aim to give women as well as men more control over their lives as the basis for individual, household, community and organizational development. As part of this project, SAHARI trained its staff as (TOTs) to support the targeted community to adapting the context during implementing the WASH project, we will take all kinds of design consideration especially in the construction of sanitation facilities, latrines so as not to cause any kind of risk to women, girls, boys and people with special needs. SAHARI will construct more number of sanitation facilities keeping reasonable distance from the IDP, SSR families, host community, and returnees, this way SAHARI can minimize the risk that women and girls potentially face during night time while walking to sanitation facilities, and also will enable children to access latrines at night in close vicinity of the house. In addition, awareness rising sessions on gender-based violence will be conducted for women, men, boys and girls. While implementing the hygiene promotion sessions, the community (especially women) will be given awareness on HIV/AIDS and early or self-testing on breast cancer to increase their own concerns related moreover, the COVID-19 protocol will be followed according to MoH mitigation measures, women will actively participate in all forums of the project intervention actvities such hygiene campaigns, health promotion committees, water management ,IWRM/ PRA, CEAP approaches will be applied during the project implementation which give an space for women to empowering, more over all the services providing by this project are protection friendly approaches , however the women will be participate at least 1/3 per each actvities committee members
