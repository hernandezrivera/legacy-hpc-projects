Project Summary

Please provide a short summary of the intervention, describing its purpose, key activities and targets. – Limit 4000 characters
This project is designed to improve on the poor education services for children aged 3-17 with limited access to quality education in the areas of Yei, Rumbek North, Awerial and Pibor. The project mainly targets IDPs, Refugees and host communities in the said areas. A joint assessment report carried out by OCHA, UNICEF and INGOs-Plan International inclusive in September 2019, indicates that Yei has high rates of returnees coming to the county. Urban parts of Yei have registered white a sizable number of IDPs who fled the rural areas due to conflicts as well as need for essential services including education, health and water. Meanwhile Lakes state particularly Awerial is having influx of IDPs from Bor who fled their land because of conflict; the IDPs have settled in camps and others are within the host community. This has led to inadequate education facilities as the little that exist is shared between the IDPs and the host communities. Rumbek North county has experienced continued interruption of education services as a result of inter communal fighting/intentional revenge killing and cattle raiding. While for Former Jonglei state, in particular Pibor county has low enrollment, retention and completion of basic primary Education as a result of harmful cultural practices (early marriages, source of wealth, the traditional belief that if a girl is above 15 she is very old to get married) affect Education for school going age, in particular girl child.
In all the locations mentioned above, the outstanding challenges strongly calls for immediate joint interventions by the government ministry of general Education, Private Sectors, INGO partners and other stakeholders respectively in the provision of Educational services for the IDPs, Returnees and the Host Community children; boys and girls.
The project specifically will focus on helping children without access to Early Childhood Development Education (ECDE) and basic primary education facilities and services and more attention will be paid to Out-of-School children (OoSC), particularly girls child and children with disabilities. Children with impairment will be identified and supported through the provision of basic scholastic materials, psychosocial support, provision of Child friendly spaces, as well as facilitating referral pathways for severe impairments. To ensure quality education provision, Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) will be established in schools identified and supported with adequate classes. The establishment of the TLS will take into consideration children with disabilities through creating rams to enable classroom access. Furthermore, Temporary Pit Latrines that are of gender segregated quality will be established and supported in identified schools as these will create access, retention and completion of ECDE and basic primary Education to girls experiencing menstruation cycles as most families cannot afford menstrual hygiene materials to their girls. The project will distribute package of the Sanitary materials to girls in supported schools at the beginning of the project and the girls will be trained to make reusable sanitary pads. This will not only increase enrollment but also ensure sustainability by girls providing their own locally made Sanitary pads and maintaining the provided Pads, in addition to the peer training being conducted in the supported schools.
To enable OoSC gain access to education, Children and Adolescent clubs composed of 50% girls and boys will be formed per supported school to conduct drummers and community sensitization on the importance of education with specific focus on girl child . In addition, Champion of Change/peace clubs will be formed and trained

1 - Needs

Character limit already exists. According to UNESCO study, significant number of Children in South Sudan are out of school; The study reveals that about 2.2 million school going age children do not access education opportunities currently in the country due to continued conflict that has displaced children as well as recruiting the said children into armed forces. These scenarios have denied children rights to basic needs education not exceptional and it is estimated that the OOSC might likely raise to 2.4 million children in 2020 if the government of South Sudan and Humanitarian actors fail to stage measure against the pull factors. To make matters worse, about 50% of the OOSC are girls and even then the girls are married off in favour of education given the appalling economic crisis in the country where girls are valued as family wealth through payment of bride price after getting a husband. Although the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) has showed commitment to quality education through provision of Child-Friendly Schools, the education standards across the Country has remained very poor, very few teachers qualified and trained to provide quality teaching in schools; moreover, even the teachers trained have no sufficient knowledge to handle children with disabilities (MoGEI, 2015). The low education standards in South Sudan is further aggravated by poor pay of teacher salaries, inadequate teaching-learning supplies and lack of school facilities including classes and wash, hygiene and sanitation in most parts of the country. This has highly contributed to OOSC as well as low learning outcomes in learning institutions (Nation Wide Education Cluster Assessment, Nov. 2018) Humanitarian intervention remains critical to improve the declining standards of education in South Sudan. The quality of education can only improve if access issues like establishment of learning spaces, construction of gender segregated latrines, awareness campaigns on education rights of children specially girls, capacity building of education managers and delivery of teaching-learning materials is adequately done in schools. Nonetheless, other factors like wash and poor hygiene, poor childcare practices, Infant and young child feeding practices, poor access to primary health services accompanied with low levels of community knowledge, driven by low adult literacy rates particularly among women fuels the vicious cycle of disease and malnutrition. Access to health and nutrition services is needed to prevent under nutrition and morbidity in children. The status of education in Central Equatoria pauses more concern as IDPs and returnees are abundantly in parts of Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Kajokeji; yet education facilities have either been looted or destroyed during the 2016 conflict. Moreover, some schools are still occupied by armed forces to date; which further hamper children access to learning facilities (Joint Partner Assessment field report, Sept 2019). According to Sept 2018 IPC analysis, 73% of Lakes population are in Crisis, Emergency & Catastrophe of hunger and food insecure which is the highest of all the states. From October to December 2018, Lakes state is predicted to have 57.60% population in Crisis, Emergency & Catastrophe phases. This state of emergency with worrying figure and percentages paused threat to education access and quality as children cannot go to school on hungry stomach. To make matters worse, the situation has affected the IDPs and host communities since they cannot afford buying basic education materials to their children as well as sending them to schools. This has led to abandoning of education

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Plan international is committed in ensuring education programme design has taken into consideration potential protection, GBV and safeguarding risks related to this humanitarian education emergency response project in Rumbek North, Awerial, Yei and Pibor counties. Different risk mitigation measures will be in place to reduce/avoid the beneficiaries’ risk of being physically assaulted or exploited or raped and even sexually assaulted while at or on their way to and from school. To mitigate this risks, PI will sensitize staffs and beneficiaries on GBV and protection issues, available reporting mechanisms if problems are identified and learners will be advised to travel in groups to schools during safe hours. Parents will be sensitized to prioritize protection of their children against abuses including sexual harassment and rape. The children will be trained on life skills especially girls to talk about protection concern and their rights in the communities.
