Project Summary

It has been just over six years since the Anglophone crises broke out which has impacted the lives of 3 million people in 2 severely affected regions (North West and South West). The impact on communities has been enormous with lost lives and destroyed physical, social and government infrastructure, which has led to a drastic reduction in basic services and livelihood opportunities causing previously marginalized groups to become even more vulnerable. The economy of these regions is highly dependent on agriculture (crops, poultry and livestock, fisheries and forest products). Two thirds of agriculture communities have been severely affected, losing crucial tools/equipment. About more than one million tons of crops have been lost over an estimated area of about 900,000 damaged hectares.
The humanitarian community has been providing relief assistance in the form of emergency shelter, food assistance, water and sanitation, health care, education, and protection
Without rapid provision of agricultural inputs as well as immediate support to livelihoods in the form of intercropping support, seeds and seedlings, farming tools people will continue to remain in a vulnerable situation reliant on the support of humanitarian assistance.
As relief activities move on, the focus must now be on providing the most vulnerable with short-term support to boost income so people no longer need to rely on emergency aid in a way that supports longer-term recovery. This will be essential to bridge the gap before longer-term activities especially in the agricultural sector where hunger is going to be the next pandemic not that farmers and other farm families are unable to go on with their activities either because they have been displaced or their homes have been destroyed. Inland, farming is the most common livelihood with about 60 per cent of household relying on farming or agricultural wage labour as their main source of family income. Women agricultural workers are particularly vulnerable. Rural women contribute to 80 per cent of food production, but are much less likely to be part of support programmes or be able to exercise control over land or water resources—leaving them with fewer coping mechanisms as the crises rage on.
With relief activities ongoing and involving different actors, there are still large numbers of vulnerable people dependent on humanitarian assistance. With the longer-term recovery programmes of the Governement which is slow or even yet to start, there is a significant risk of a resurgence of acute humanitarian needs unless immediate action is taken to fill the gap in restarting livelihoods and the delivery of basic social services until Government recovery programmes kick in fully.
NYDO wish to concentrate some effort on livelihoods and sustainable agriculture because with the deteriorating situation, food insecurity and the current global crisis, a lot of people are facing severe hunger and the number keeps increasing by the day. The agricultural sector is facing a decline as those who carried out such activities are now left at their mercy with little or no support for them to effectively carry out their activity. If more effort is not concentrated in this sector, hunger will become the next pandemic and many more children will suffer malnutrition and starvation which could be slowed down on time. The population in the urban areas are beginning to experience food shortages especially with the hike in prices of basic commodities. Almost a third of households in affected areas have borderline or poor food consumption level
Target
This projects target young people who are most vulnerable and involved in different forms of irresponsible living as a means of sustainability.
AIM
1. To meet the immediate food needs that is on the rise, avoid nutritional deterioration, and build food security in ways that are sustained through stimulation of markets and production, and access to life-saving community-based nutrition services.

1 - Besoins / Needs

NYDO seeks to empowering and supporting the youths in vulnerable communities especially those affected by the ongoing anglophone crises to voice their needs and address root causes of poverty and suffering. This project will not only address the immediate causes of food insecurity but also build on structures to address the underlying causes of food insecurity caused by the crisis through capacity building of the locals and also integration with other sectors such as nutrition.Since 2010 NYDO programmes are aimed at addressing the short-term needs in emergencies, the medium term needs through rehabilitation programmes and the longer term strategic needs of the youths through our long-term development interventions. Our emergency interventions stabilise young people who do not, in the short term, have the capacity to meet their basic material needs. For the most part in our operation area, this has meant targeted programmes, distribution of quality seeds and input tools. Rehabilitation and longer term development aims to build the capacity of the youth to enable them to take responsibility for their own development needs – to make a sustainable impact on poverty. Through working in chronic food deficit areas and in the most disadvantaged areas of the North / South West and the Northern regions, NYDO ensures that its programmes target those who are most vulnerable in the most disadvantaged areas. These programmes include food security and livelihoods, Health and Nutrition, Water and Sanitation, Child-welfare and other special programmes. The project will group identified beneficiaries in at least 5 members per group and support them with: 1. Seeds and Seedlings 2. High breed Fertilized eggs and or chicks 3. High breed Piglets 4. Feed and drugs 5. Fertilizer and other farm tools.
