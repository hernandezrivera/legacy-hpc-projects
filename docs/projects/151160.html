Project Summary

Humanitarian situation in Mudug, Galgaduud, Lower Juba and Sool regions of Somalia is a critical due to the prolonged drought and conflict which reduced income base of the affected households and considerably reduced their food consumption score. The situation has been further exacerbated by limited humanitarian intervention promoting drastic deterioration of food security. HOD seeks to address this prolonged food insecurity through integrated food security packages which will respond to both immediate and the long term needs of the affected population. HOD will provide cash relief to households in crisis and emergency phase in order to help them meet their immediate food and non food needs and close the widening food consumption gap. HOD will also support households in stress and crisis phase with food voucher in order to stabilize their situation and enhance access to food. HOD will similarly provide support to food insecure households with able bodied individuals with cash through cash for work. The goal is to create communal asset and reduce dependence on external support. HOD also seeks to support and invest the livelihoods of poor households in the targeted regions through livestock restocking and treatment so as to strengthen their resilience and capacity to withstand and respond to shocks. The proposed intervention seeks to support 1,730 households with total population of 10,380 persons (Women 1,980, men 720, boys 3,220, and girls 4,360). The transfer value will be based on the current regional MEB. The proposed intervention is cost effective because 81.7% of the project budget will directly go to the project beneficiaries and in that regard the project is cost effective. Accountability on cash tracking will be achieved through the use of vouchers which will contain the name and unique identifiers for each beneficiary. A competitively selected Money vendor will also be engaged to process payments for beneficiaries in order to reduce risk associated with the cash and to enhance accountability in the cash transfer process. A post distribution assessment for the beneficiaries will be done to verify how much cash each received and the use they have put the cash to. A feedback mechanism/grievance mechanism will also be put in place for beneficiaries to reach out for any support. Frequent spot checks will be done to verify whether beneficiaries are receiving the right amount. Market data will be collected on regular basis to monitor cash impact on the market.

Needs

According to FSNAU report September (2018) Food security situation still remains at a critical level in the Somalia. Large scale famine has been averted in 2017/2018 through sustained humanitarian assistance coupled with good performance of the Deyr which was generally average. This project is designed to respond to the food security needs of the affected population in Mudug, Lower Juba, Galgaduud and Sool regions of Somalia where Malnutrition levels still remains above pre crisis level. GAM rate above 16% and SAM rate of 6% have been observed in most parts of these regions. IDP camps host the largest number of malnourished cases. The 2017/2018 drought badly affected livestock which is critical livelihood asset for pastoral households. 35% of the livestock head size has been lost. Household income has also being severely constrained due to growing debt levels. Food expenditure at the household level has reached at 61%. Floods and the effect of the cyclone Sagaar further aggravated the already grim food security outlook in the area. The targeted regions have combined number of 610,000 peoples in stress phase (IPC 2), while 331,000 are in crisis (IPC 3). Additionally 12,000 people are in emergency Phase (IPC 4). The proposed intervention seeks to support 1,730 households with total population of 10,380 persons (Women 1,980, men 720, boys 3,220, and girls 4,360). The action will support 750 households with total population of 4,500 persons (800 women, 380 men, 1800 girls and 1520 boys) with food each for 4 months at voucher value of $USD 70. The voucher will contain 30kg rice, 10 cowpeas, 10 wheat flour and 3 liters of cooking oil. 310 households with a population of 1,860 (330 women, 120 men, 750 girls and 560 boys) will be assisted through unconditional cash transfer of USD$ 100 for 3 months. 450 rural households with total population of (2,700) persons (500 women, 130 men 1,320 girls and 750 boys) through CFW. The transfer will be based on the current MEB

Activities

1.1: Mobilize, identify and register and transfer unconditional cash transfer to 310 households with a population of 1,860 (330 women, 120 men, 750 girls and 560 boys) with malnourished children, lactating women, and pregnant women, elderly, sick and new arrived IDPs, identified by community leaders and women groups to prevent a deterioration of the situation from (IPC 3 to IPPC 4). Each household will receive USD$ 100 for 3 months 1.2: Increase food availability and food access to 750 households with total population of 4,500 persons (800 women, 380 men, 1800 girls and 1520 boys). Through voucher food voucher. With each household receiving food voucher valued at US$70/ for 4 months. Female headed HHs will constitute approximately 60% of the beneficiary HHs. 1.3: Support 450 rural households with total population of (2,700) persons (500 women, 130 men 1,320 girls and 750 boys) through CFW so as to increase food access to vulnerable households and rehabilitate community productive assets and enhance water availability for 48 days intensive CFW. Each beneficiary will receive USD$100 per month.1.4: Provide restocking and veterinary services to 220 pastoralists’ households representing 1,320 persons (350 women, 90 men, 390 boys and 490 girls) with each household receiving 10 lactating goats. The aim is to replenish household head size for those who lost their animals during the drought in order to boast their resilience and improve access to nutrition and reduce dependence on external support.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed intervention seeks to address the immediate food needs of the most drought affected vulnerable household through the provision of dignified food security packages. It also attempts to strengthen the capacity and the resilience of the poor and food insecure households by investing their livelihood assets. 450 households will largely benefit from CFW activities which will help them to access cash while also creating assets and ensure water availability. 1060 households will be supported through conditional and unconditional cash transfer to enhance access to food during the hunger gap period. On the other 220 households will be assisted through livestock restocking and treatment. Each household will restocked with 10 goats to improve access to nutrition and increase household herd size. The project will also organize training for the project staff, community members to enhance their capacity in the areas of management, water conservation and hygiene promotion. This will help in creating local capacity that will be long term asset for the targeted project areas.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

HOD intends to employ an inclusive community based targeting which considers the needs of the socially marginalized groups such as minorities and displaced persons. They will consider becoming part of the decision making cycles such as community project committees so that they do not become only beneficiaries but also actors of the project by participating in the process of design and implementation and monitoring. In order to ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries HOD will adopt non discriminatory and impartial ways in giving out assistance

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Major exclusion risks anticipated on the basis of gender and age is gender based violence which in some cases remains largely unreported. Economic and social inequality especially among and girls due to cultural constrain that prevent from being active in mainstream economic activities and hence entrenching further marginalization and alienation. Early marriage especially among girls is a risk factor that considerably increases the risk of child malnutrition both the mother and the child. In issues of gender over phasing of the girl child priority and needs exposed total neglects to the boy child. This project is designed in a manner that will give equal rights to both men and women in terms of opportunities and decision making and participation of the project activities. Consideration will be given to socially marginalized groups, child headed households and women headed households. This will give equal space to all gender and age categories and hence will promote equality in accessing resources and decision making resulting in greater empowerment of the local community.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Insecurity remains number one concern of the targeted population resulting massive displacement exposing them to the risk of exclusion from accessing humanitarian assistance. The project will employ deliberate efforts to ensure full participation of the vulnerable groups in the project activities. The project will open an environment of collaboration with community members to foster peaceful coexistence and advocate for behavior change. Proper mapping will be carried out to ensure distribution centers are safe and protected. Beneficiaries feedback and complain mechanism system will be established so as to capture the concerns of all the beneficiaries. Constant field visit will be made in order to establish and address issues of discrimination, abuse and exploitation against women and girls.
