Project Summary

2022 Pilgrims of Hope's intervention will utilize an integrated approach to address the food security and livelihoods needs of the population in the two States of Central Equatoria (Kajokeji and Juba counties) and Unity States in (Panyijiar, Mayendit, and Leer counties). The main overall objective of the interventions will be to reduce food insecurity of the above selected Counties sustainably and having as main targets food insecure communities classified in IPC 5, 4 and 3 with special attention to single mothers, returnees and vulnerable households. FSL service delivery will integrate emergency to reliance interventions, aiming to strengthen the productive bases of agricultural communities in order to enhance overall production. while targeting the most vulnerable by integrating them into active roles within more-developed groups and production systems. More importantly, this model also does use the “model-farmers” approach, where by successful local farmers set the example for other community members. Activities will target both the local institutions [with a strong component of capacity building and awareness] and the local communities [with direct support, capacity building and access to improved agricultural inputs and services]. At the institutional level [county and local authorities], the interventions will focus on creating and strengthening monitoring systems during implementation so that IDPs/returnees and ‘host’ communities are both considered in order to avoid conflict. POH will ensure that, activities focus on their integration/interaction through agriculture activities, enhanced access to quality and improved agricultural inputs (seeds and tool kits, capacity building in agriculture using the Farmers Field School (FFS) approach. Essentially, the diversification of livelihoods activities will be supported (eg. fish farming, vegetable production, agro-forestry among others). Special attention will be given to climate change adaptation and smart agriculture practices with the aim of fostering a sustainable increment of production with constant attention to preservation of natural resources and restoration of lost resources especially trees planting. With the lessons learnt from compounding effect of COVID-19 pandemic to already existing challenges, several revised/new guidelines and approaches shall be adhered and implemented in 2022 to ensure continuity of equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services in the context of COVID -19. The project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programming with other relevant sectors such as nutrition, WASH, health, protection and food security through effective coordination mechanism that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to multi-sectoral basic service which will further reduce food insecurity and increase resilience. Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring that programming of FSL services approach is community/people centered in such a way that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation to ensure that the intervention is responsive enough to the arrays of the changing needs accordingly.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, People’s humanitarian needs continue to be mainly driven by years of conflict, increasing underlying vulnerability which has been further compounded by the impacts of climate change and the COVID-19 pandemic. The already critical humanitarian situation has been compounded by the third consecutive year of severe flooding in 2021, with 835,000 people reported as affected between May - December 2021. Critical infrastructure was destroyed and limited humanitarian access to heavily affected populations, while simultaneously limited the ability of people to migrate to access services. Climate change is aggravating the risk as well as cascading and compounding the effects of hydro-meteorological hazards. As their severity, frequency and complexity increases, the level of disruption to socioeconomic systems and of destruction to humans, livelihoods and infrastructure rises. The 2021 Global Climate Risk Index, which rates the impacts of weather-related loss events ranked South Sudan as the 8th most affected country. Besides these, FAO assessment findings has indicated that 65,107 Ha of land planted with cereals were damaged, with an estimated loss of 37,624 tonnes of grain in the flood-affected areas. Therefore, the effects of floods and climate change call for urgent actions, measures and approaches that will help farmers sustainably increase their agricultural productivity and incomes. High levels of food insecurity drive humanitarian need in most parts of the country. And an estimated 8.3 million people are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022, an increase of 7% from 2021. High inflation is persistent with high basic commodities prices despite the fiscal reforms. The South Sudan JMMI for Jan 2022, recorded that the cost of a food basket has risen to 31,996 ($73.55). People’s food security situation is expected to further deteriorate as a result of continued violence that disrupt people’s livelihood activities and trade, the impacts of flooding, ongoing economic crisis and seasonal effects. Localized dry spells at the critical time of crop growth also poses the risk of low agricultural production and thus further deepens the food gaps. Food insecurity negatively impacts people’s health and nutritional well being, and their ability to carry out livelihood activities. Those identified as most affected are children, people with disabilities (PWDs), older persons and women-headed households. These people are the most vulnerable whenever affected by increases in food insecurity. The FNSMS+ identified Food security and livelihoods as the most needed forms of assistance for men in 55% of assessed households; similarly, food and livelihoods were recognized as the primary priority need for women, in 52% of assessed households and in the case of children, food and livelihoods was the second most frequently identified pressing need, as reported in 29% of assessed households, following education in 48 per cent. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs overview projects that Leer, Panyijiar and Mayendit will have significant populations that have slipped back to IPC 4 and 5, and with Mayendit recording 9% increase of People in need in comparison to 2021. A recent SMART survey conducted Panyijiar by IMC recorded that Prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition for children between 6 and 59 months, is at 15.7% which is very low as per the WHO recommended rate. The plight calls for timely life-saving and life -sustaining Food security support through conditional and unconditional safety nets interventions as well as restoring already weakened and fragile livelihoods and further strengthen resilience of the most vulnerable households to cope with the current and future shocks. POH will respond to needs by delivering farm in-puts including fishing kits assistance while ensuring sustainable agricultural practices through community and resilience-based agricultural interventions accordingly.

2 - Project activities

1. Emergency food production: - The distribution will target vulnerable beneficiaries HHs; IDPs, returnees and Host community. Beneficiaries registration undergo a process of identification, verification and registration with involvement of department line government officials among them RRC, Agriculture Payam extension workers and local authorities for transparency and accountability to the community. - Provision of evidence base technical guidance to beneficiaries on vegetable production through setting of demonstration plots at Payam level or OTP sites for nutrition sensitive agriculture. - Training of beneficiaries both males and females as TOTs who shall then act as extension works/community agriculture facilitators within the community. - Training on animal traction/ox-plough to willing and selected beneficiaries to increase areas under cultivation and improving on the farming technologies, hence extensive farming. use in the appropriately by the beneficiaries in the various locations - Gender mainstreaming; during implementation cross cutting such as rights of gender based survivors (GBV) and most vulnerable HHs such as the women and child headed HHs will be advocated for. - Monitoring: Post distribution monitoring will be conducted by the M $E team two weeks after distribution of inputs to ensure inputs are physically in good conditions. Post planting will be done One month after distribution by the implementing/FSL and the M&E team to make sure that the inputs are in use appropriately by the beneficiaries in the various locations. 2. Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits/ vegetable kits; rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) - Identification/selection, verification and registration of beneficiaries for both vegetable and fishing groups - Distribution of short term/quick maturing vegetable seeds and fishing kits - Training of Fisher folk on fish the use of the kits, fish handling processing, preservation, and marketing. - Setting demo plots for conducting session on vegetable production to vegetable kits beneficiaries 3. Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonosis - Vaccination of animals in the three counties of Leer, Mayendit, and Panyijiar - Training of CAHWs and other stakeholders (traders) for the implementation of cost recovery system, facilitation opening of vet shops by CAHWs at Payam level. - Conducting awareness campaigns through dramas, community dialogue to educate the local communities on the importance of vaccination and treatment of livestock, create community and livestock owners’ self-alertness for disease early warning campaigns, zoonosis and TADs that includes anthrax, brucellosis, Rift valley fever, Tuberculosis, hemorrhagic septicemia, black quarter. - Support in the eradication and control of livestock disease within the Cluster Areas - Training of Community animal health works (CAHWs) in sampling, testing, cold chain management, and reporting using an approved training curriculum (covering the “One Health” approach) & functional in support of a national network cool chain management at County and Payam level. 4. Natural Resource management and agro forestry - Formation and training of natural resource management committee. - Establishment of fruit tree seedling. - Conducting community level sensitization on natural resource mapping and utilization with minimal conflict. - Encouraging use of improved cooking stove reducing the amount of emission into the atmosphere and training on Agroforestry technics.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

POH has in place a tested and working system of four years multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 300 questions (including question on FSL, nutrition, GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a good number of interviews more than 300 HHs that are capable of providing detailed reference at the Counties and Payam levels, an analysis centered on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge becomes the internal foundations by POH staff and thus the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. Finally, the coordination bases that POH has across the targeted communities are equipped with phones of hot-lines, suggestion boxes; focal point protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complaints whenever it occurs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

With protection, POH will work in collaboration with other actors including UN agencies, INGOS and NNGOs, FBOs and CBOs that are involved in child protection issues to safeguard the delivery of protection services in the schools and communities within these locations. POH will be accountable to the beneficiaries by ensuring that they participate in the project stages beginning from the planning, implementation, and then evaluation phase. In addition, appropriate and preferred mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints will be set up. On regularly or daily basis, POH conducts community engagement meetings that includes awareness campaigns, innovative youth events that can allow open and equal participation over the quality of progress of the activity being conducted. POH embrace a last-mile approach through field-based personal and daily visits to the field and conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which later are analyzed to distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention and beneficiaries barriers to access services. It is important that projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Pilgrims of Hope has time and time placed protection and gender issues at the center of every intervention by engaging directly with the host communities’ children, IDPs, returnees and CWDs so that their needs, their priorities and their rights are addressed within the scope of the project. On this therefore, POH will always create conducive and interactive environments that that not only allows them but also encourage them to express their needs, rights and priorities. Apart from the active involvement of the rights holders in the project, POH will ensure that the duty bearers (Government, PTAs/SMCS, institutions and non-state actors) play an effective role to promote the rights of the CWDs and girls. Activities will be developed with the active participation of women and parents in all stages of the implementation process, to ensure that girls’ priority needs are taken into consideration and that WASH infrastructures meet the right gender standards (IASC, 2015, Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action: Reducing risk, promoting resilience and also aiding recovery accordingly. More importantly, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at service access points (schools, health facilitates, distribution points and at the community. But also ensuring that, safety audits are conducted and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of beneficiaries and reducing risks. Community members, staff and stakeholder’s capacity will be strengthened through specific PSEA trainings accordingly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The continuous presence that POH has in the communities targeted by the intervention and local Staff recruited locally among people recognized by the communities as focal points in every location and also including senior positions working in the field and being used extensively and this ensuring that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the project cycles. All project’s beneficiaries (female and male IDPs and returnees’ children, CWDs and host communities’ children) will be considered as capable and active resources rather than as passive recipients who do not have a say in the project. It is therefore very vital applying localized approach with POH staff regularly present and usually coming from the same community of project locations, and with such, accountability is pursued also directly through its staff that can act as bridge between top management and beneficiaries. Besides this, considering the purposely field-oriented management team (officers and also project managers are field based), beneficiaries and affected population at large as constant possibility to interact and file complain to staff at different level. quarterly, POH conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, whereby the county and payam officials and traditional leaders Boma and sub Boma Chiefs forms the basis for community representation. In such meetings, POH explains to the community its strategic plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. POH field extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, do establish a case specific dialog where POHs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in the communities in order to evaluate possible challenges and intervention failure chance if any. Beside the surveys, POH’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings of which they provide platform to address issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can find the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported and such do increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

POH will ensure initial involvement of women and local women groups representatives in AAP committees and during the planning activities implementation; at project implementation they will be provided with information about available GBV services and collecting feedback from women and girls about their needs/safety concerns; at the time where community workers are going door to door especially during the covid -19 pandemic, the project will ensure CNVs are trained/oriented on how to safely respond to a disclosure and that they have the most up-to-date referral pathways. 12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will contribute to the collective outcome 3 acting on different levels. The emergency activities will focus on the most vulnerable especially of Unity State, among which flood related IDPs represent a priority group; these activities, such as emergency distribution of agricultural inputs and basic trainings, will help such flood affected population in finding relief from the emergency situation while building the basis for a new system of self-reliance. Along with this, POH will work with such Host Communities to strengthen the support network that take care of those displaced ensuring they have all the support needed. POH intervention aims at building the strong collaboration between host communities and displaced people as a synergic tool to ensure the growth of the whole community at large and the reduction of internal conflicts over the use of natural resources.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Particularly in Central Equatoria State, the activities will focus much in the field of food security and livelihoods with the main aim of reducing the proportion of population facing famine, emergency and or crisis situations. The activities will be oriented to delivering basic agricultural assistance to the most vulnerable while building the capacity of the communities towards an ever-growing resilience and independence from humanitarian aid. Working to improve on production and utilization of nutritious locally available food at household for optimal MIYC feeding, interventions will integrate nutrition to food security component by establishing sustainable solutions as demonstration kitchen gardens at household level, at nutrition sites, in schools and churches among other institutions. Continuation in engaging in cereals largescale production through progressive farmers’ groups and enhancing the mechanization of cereal production in the targeted areas with active farmers who have large coverage of ready and well prepared land under cultivation and supporting in production management and post-harvest methods. Facilitating in cooking demonstration to enlighten the mothers, fathers, and caregivers on preparation of nutritious foods harvested from the kitchen gardens and encouraged to replicate the same at home is another priority. Mothers and caretakers of children with acute malnutrition will be link to community general food assistant to improve on household food security. People with chronic illnesses and mothers of children admitted in SC for management of severe acute malnutrition will be link to institution feeding program to receive food assistance accordingly.
