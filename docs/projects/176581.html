Project Summary

The overall aims of this project are to address the Provision of Emergency Protection services and mixed solutions to the most conflicted affected people in living as Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees, and host communities in Fangak, Akobo, and Uror, in counties in Jonglei state, and Nasir/Luakpiny in Upper Nile state, with 40,000 Direct and indirect beneficiaries. (15,000 Boys, 15,000 Girls, 5,000 Men and 5,000 Women. in South Sudan through improving the protection services to the conflict-affected communities, and ensuring that all efforts geared towards reducing vulnerability and risks for Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV), scale-up life-saving protection services for both, female and male GBV survivors and community members at high risk of GBV, provide Capacity building within the community to support the provision of quality and comprehensives case management services through community-based approaches.
CAO intends to continue responding to the needs of children and Adolescent youth, engaging them as parts of the ongoing intervention in the targeting Counties with UNHCR and SSHF support, through training and other youths activities as part of its mandate as they have been exposed to armed activities during the crisis in the 4 counties by referring them for further action to the relevant working groups addressing protection risks issues, through a response strategy which includes: Focus on provision of quality Case management program, Capacity building support to communities and staff, strengthening adolescent and youths Centers, Community-based PSS mechanism and approach to respond to their needs when necessary, provide dignity kits and life-saving material assistance to the vulnerable adolescents, an elderly people and people living with disabilities, strengthening referral system for GBV cases;
CAO will establish Women and Girls-friendly Spaces in an environment that is sensitive and responsive to the needs of women and children within the host, refugee, and IDP community. These will also improve the efforts to ensure the Protection and achievement of durable solutions for persons of concern in the selected Counties, CAO will collaborate throughout with the local authorities, networks, and other stakeholders to build and maintain a local environment that promotes peaceful co-existence between persons of concern, Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and their host's communities through Initiatives/activities that will help to foster adaptation, recovery, growth and promote dialogue, survival, and protection.
CAO will ensure the integration of protection and COVID_19 messages to reduce cases of COVID 19 transmission, Increased identification and assistance of persons with specific needs among the refugee, IDP, and returnee population. The project will ensure that all stakeholders in the project locations (Akobo, Fangak, and uror) are involved in the project cycle (planning implementation, monitoring, and evaluation) to mitigate gender exclusion and promote ownership of the project outcome. increase the capacity of women and girls through awareness creation and training on protection risk prevention and mitigations well as strengthening their local leadership structures and networks to be able to critically engage and participate in local decision-making processes. Through local advocacy campaigns around protection, CAO plans to put women's rights at the center of local decision-making around peace and security so that their protection needs can be enhanced.

1 - Needs

Conflict and instability in South Sudan have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement since the December 2013 crisis of over 4 million individuals. In September 2018, the Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) was signed between the major warring parties. Whilst overall there have been improvements in security since and returns are ongoing. fighting and communal clashes (frequently linked to cattle raiding) continue to trigger new displacements and the situation remains volatile. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. This situation is compounded by the lack of critical protection services, reduced Participation of the community in SGBV prevention and response, provision of basic needs and essential services, Support to persons of concern with specific needs, and protracted violence. CAO intends to address the protection risks, through a response strategy that includes: provision of quality and comprehensive case management, identification and referral of cases to the appropriate service provider for the response. Conducts monthly protection monitoring and assessments at community and household level, and prepare the respective reports and share with protection cluster on a timely basis, provide protection clinics in communities, for community-level support; capacity building of the community structures; GBV case management; referrals, and response to GBV including the provision of PSS. Support SGBV International commemoration activities such as 16 Days of Activism and International Women’s Day, provide targeted assistance/support to identified PSNs, maintaining and updating PSNs data for the respective project locations including having a PSN Database for updated PSN data on daily basis, conduct a comprehensive assessment of IDPs, returnees and spontaneous refugee returnees needs for basic and domestic items (NFL's. CAO will also improve the provision of basic services to the vulnerable members of the community, Provision of psychosocial support, and other specific services to persons of concern. Inclusive participation is improved among the community, organized association/Forum will be provided training on decision making, and women empowerment conducts all kind of awareness and people attending counted as individuals. Sports and games for peace: Organize quarterly sports and games such as football, volleyball whereby IDPs will play with the Host Community to promote peaceful coexistence.Conduct monthly community outreach and recreational activities aimed at promoting peaceful coexistence in the community, and provide quarterly support to community networks and strengthening capacity (ensuring these networks are all-inclusive) CAO will derive a strategy to endure that women and girls are part of these networks (Gender aware and inclusive strategy), and Coordination mechanisms established and strengthened including joint assessment, planning, and evaluation exercises. increasing capacity building programs to staff and communities to support the children during the exit of the organization, to continue improving the well-being of the children that have gone through difficulty and in the community’s centres to complement the current multi-sectoral interventions with mainstream CP and GBV into Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health, and FSL Referral pathways will also be established to strengthen and support the implementation of case management and link children to other services and address the gaps in coordination within the targeted counties were CAO operates.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAO will use a community engagement and accountability approach, to put communities at the center of the project. In this regard, CAO will integrate the issue of sexual exploitation and abuse systematically into information campaigns, training, and meetings with affected people, with particular attention given to vulnerable groups.CAO team will Strengthen the community through awareness-raising, in Critical activities targeting women, girls, boys, and men in communities of concern as well as with other stakeholders (including implementing and operational partners, government officials, civil society organizations, and companies in the private sector). CAO will prevent SEA effectively, to all staff and workers must make sure that PoC is aware of their rights and entitlements. Beneficiaries should know that they are entitled to assistance, that they should never be expected to provide any form of sexual favor, and that demands for such favors are strictly prohibited.CAO will provide community members with information about existing codes of conduct for personnel, as well as where to report sexual exploitation and abuse committed by staff assisting. Beneficiaries shall be also oriented on how they should report SEA incidents safely and confidentially when they occur. CAO will sensitize the community/Beneficiaries on SEA on the project and Establish (safe and accessible) community reporting channels to report SEA. In many communities there exist already “community feedback/complaint mechanisms”, “Child Protection committees”, Women Centers, PSEA focal points”, protection Desk or other protection reporting channels. These existing focal points and channels need to be sensitized to be able to identify and report SEA.CAO shall ensure appropriate training is provided for staff and partners on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. Prevention, training, and information campaigns shall be put in place for all CAO staff. Training and information campaigns will include the distribution of information sheets, posters, and videos, or community meetings, focus group discussions, etc.

CAO will research gender dimensions of food security, collecting sex-disaggregated data by sex, age, disability, and other relevant vulnerability factors and assessing physical security and other associated risks. They allow people to report concerns without risk of stigma from the community (as people might use the help desk to do anything from asking about distribution times to report sexual abuse). Help desks can also build trust if they are frequently open and staff is helpful. Not suitable for persons with visual or certain mobility impairments if they cannot get there.Community or children’s feedback committee members would need to be recruited through a participatory process that builds trust. Projects monitoring systems and reviews that gather information about beneficiaries and their perceptions, views, and responses to the interventions. 12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The proposed project will significantly contribute to a decrease in 2021 GAM prevalence in South Sudan by ensuring the following is adhered to • Raising awareness & participating in activities that will increase the food consumption among children and raise the Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) and Minimum Meal Frequency (MMF) among children 2-23 months from 17.2% and 25.8% respectively to an acceptable %. Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) among children 6-23 months is 3.7%, according to the State level FSNMS estimate • Community action organization will promote Child practices in our operational areas with the aim of increasing exclusive breastfeeding coverage from the current 32.3 % to above 50 % which will, in turn, play a big role in the reduction of the global acute malnutrition prevalence rate among children boys and girls under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4. • CAO will in collaboration with the health partners present in our area of operation participate in Immunization campaigns and ensure children under 5 Boys & Girls are vaccinated against measles and provided with Vitamin A supplementation. This will play a big role in reducing malnutrition-related mortality and Morbidity. Access to a sufficient quantity of clean water will also be encouraged to curb diseases which later have an impact on beneficiaries Nutrition status.
