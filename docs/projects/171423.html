Project Summary

The intervention is designed according to a comprehensive assessment that addresses people’s access to equitable, safe, and sufficient water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services and examined the related risks of Gender Based Violence (GBV). The assessment (conducted in 2020 by DCA/NCA partner, MAAN) revealed that Poor access to adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation services plays a significant role in promoting gender-based violence against women and girls including those with disabilities. 65.8% of the surveyed women confirmed that this has a negative impact on the behavior of the male head of the family towards women in the family including different forms of psychological and physical abuse. For instance,
the substandard bathrooms were found to undermine privacy, safety and security that increases the risk of women being subjected to abuse. The lack of drinking water from the home makes women unable to meet the different needs of their family members, which often leads to strong criticism and heightened tensions within the family.
In response, DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid (DCA/NCA) and its partners, MAAN Development Center (MAAN) and Culture and Free Thought Association (CFTA), propose an integrated multisectoral WASH-GBV project that aims at improving the access to safe and equitable WASH services and appropriate GBV response mechanisms during emergencies in accordance with the dignity of the underserved and most vulnerable communities. The response will target Der Al Balah (Abu El Ajin Sanajira, Jafarawi, Al
Athar) and Khanyounis (Qa’al Qurin, Al Mawasi).
The objective of this action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the HRP as such:
• For WASH component, it is in line with HRP objective 2: "The basic needs of vulnerable
Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic
services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected
persons under IHL",
• For GBV component: it is in line with HRP objective 1: “The rights of Palestinians living
under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are
protected, respected, and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL”
In addition, it meets WASH Cluster 2021 HRP, objective 2. "Ensure equitable, affordable,
and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most
vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank". The
intervention is in line with the objectives of the Protection Cluster 2021 HRP, objective 2:
“Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the
occupation and conflict related violence. Outcome level Indicator: # of women survivors of
violence in need of support who received GBV services (health, legal aid, MHPSS,
economic empowerment, DK, shelter)
DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaints handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality ( either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism or using the partners complaints mechanism)

1 - Needs

During 2020, MAAN conducted a study assessing the relation between GBV and the poor access to WASH services. The study concluded that poor access to WASH services plays a significant role in increasing GBV against women. Upon the study, 53.8 % of the women reported that the bathroom in their homes is free of safety and security standards. 90.6% respondents’ women experienced psychological pressure and distress during rashes, while they were forced to close windows to avoid unpleasant odors and mosquitoes. 65.8% of women confirmed that they are subjected to violence in times of sewage rash. 79.5% of women are unable to purchase detergents. 68.4% of women reported that lack of domestic water exposes them to violence. Psychological violence came in the forefront of the types of violence against those women by 53.46%, followed by physical violence with 11%, and deprivation of resources by 3.15%, then sexual violence is 0.79%. The specific WASH needs of the prioritized target group and locations were assessed and validated (April 2020) through a survey that outreached 120 families, involving the related municipalities, community engagement committees and WASH cluster focal points. Among the assessed families, 96.0 % are abject poor and other 4.0 % found poor. The assessed population included 10.9 % female-headed households, 1.0 % of elderly headed households, 2.3 % people with disabilities, and 9.6 % pregnant/lactating women. 91.3% of the assessed families have poor and unsafe storage tanks for domestic water. 82.6% of the families do not have potable water storage tanks. 95.7% of the families have toilets in poor conditions that are neither healthy nor safe. 91.3% of families have kitchens in unhealthy/unsafe conditions. The results showed that 91.3% of the families are connected to the public sewage network, and 8.7% are using cesspits which are in poor and unsafe conditions. 66% of the visited cases of people with disabilities reported the inadequacy of the WASH facilities to their needs. The assessment revealed that 92.6% of the families could not secure the basic and essential hygiene materials, specifically the COVID related materials. Poor hygiene conditions of kitchens, utensils, bathrooms, combined with shortage of hygienic material inside homes were recognized. DCA\NCA partner, CFTA, has conducted an assessment to address the prevalence of GBV concerns and women's access to protection services. The assessment (September 2020) involved 100 women including 7 women with disabilities (19+) and 20 teenager girls (12 to 17 years old) including 5 girls with disabilities through interviews, FGD’s and analysis of the data from the Management Information System. Poor access to GBV services were reported by women either because of it is unavailable within their areas or due to lack of information about available services. It is revealed that majority of the consulted women & girls including those with disabilities reported facing an increased stress or/and violence during the quarantine period. Women and girls indicated that psychosocial services and needed prioritized items were not available including food, hygiene disposables and dignity related items. majority of women face psychosocial disorders during the quarantine and that they face difficulties in accessing psychosocial support services Women and girls with disabilities indicated they lack access to assistive devices, hygiene and dignity materials. and girls. Women involved in the assessment reported that they are continued to be blamed for all constrained families are facing including male violence committed against them within the household and in public. The assessment process addressed the GBV occurrence through observations and interviews with women. 24.8% of the surveyed families reported the occurrence of at least one GBV incidents in the last six months.

2 - Activities

The activities considered the needs and market assessments, consultations with the target beneficiaries. WASH: 1- Rehabilitate sanitation facilities for 100 households to meet SPHERE standards (age, gender, and culturally sensitive WASH facilities, and adequate to the needs of people with disabilities) - (592 individuals: 148 men, 135 women, 157 boys, 152 girls).2- Distribution of 70 water storage tanks for domestic use (70 tanks - 1000 liters’ capacity each) and for drinking (100 tanks - 200 liters’ capacity each) - (1007 individuals: 250 men, 227 women, 268 boys, 262 girls)3- Distribution of 1000 hygiene kits addressing COVID-19 - (5920 individuals: 1472 men, 1332 women, 1578 boys, 1538 girls).4- Hygiene awareness campaigns - (10000 individuals: 2492 men, 2252 women, 2663 boys, 2593 girls).5- Installation of sewer lines (1000 meter) - (474 individuals: 118 men, 107 women, 126 boys, 123 girls). MA’AN will conduct an environment and social impact assessment to ascertain the environmental and social impacts of the infrastructural subcomponent.6- Installation of house sewage connections (50) - (296 individuals: 74 men, 66 women, 79 boys, 77 girls). GBV: 1- Awareness sessions to be conducted on women’s rights, early marriage, GBV risks, and protection mechanisms. 900 women, 100 girls, 100 boys and 200 men will be reached. 20 women and men will be engaged in community led initiatives2- Case management and referral services (estimated at 200 cases). This will follow the Standard Operational Procedures coordinated by the protection cluster, making sure that survivors are accessing confidential, safe, and timely services3- Psychosocial counseling will include 200 Individual counseling cases, 5 group counseling sessions (80 cases), 2 peer groups (20 cases) and 500 cases through other recreational and stress release activities. 4- Distribution of 500 dignity kits (300 women and 200 girls). The kit content will be identified in consultation with women girls through FGDs. The kit may include soap, lady slippers, underwear-bra, underwear for women, sanitary pads, absorbent cloth towel, toothbrush, toothpaste, shampoo, wipes, bath sponge, hand cream, socks, deodorant, etc. 5- Provide legal counseling, mediation, and court representation to women. The cases will include divorce, custody rights, inherent rights, etc. (200 cases).CFTA will use TAMKEEN for psychosocial empowerment that was developed by the Palestinian Counselling centre in Jerusalem and based on the Knowledge, Attitude, Protection. It provides three life skills: stressed management, finding alternatives and applying them through initiatives at the family, community, and society level. Each group will include 12 sessions and coaching while engaging in the initiatives. The rehabilitation of HH’s WASH facilities will address women security and privacy, child protection, supporting the wellbeing of elderlies and people with disabilities. The communication material for hygiene awareness will be tailored to consider the different roles and responsibilities for men, women and elderlies inside the HH. Specific support will be provided for women in identifying and applying best practices relevant to their roles such as safe water handling, safe children excreta disposal, handwashing. The hygiene material will consider the specific hygiene needs for women and girls particularly teenagers, pregnant, elderly and women with disabilities. DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA training to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities.The intervention has been coordinated with CMWU, municipalities, PWA, WASH and protection clusters. DCA/NCA will ensure proper coordination among its partners to enhance complementarity.Cash assistance will be provided to GBV survivor to enable them afford access to remote services modalities (i.e. internet top up).

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA will support partners to increase the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. This will be ensured through engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. Beneficiaries will have access to both DCA/NCA and its partners complaint mechanism. DCA/NCA and its partners will consider the beneficiaries feedback and recommendation through direct contact and consultation with them. The complaint mechanism will provide the chance for all beneficiaries especially women and people with disabilities to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities through web site, hotline, complaints boxes, directly to the field monitors, and directly to partners’ or DCA/NCA offices. The applied complaint mechanism considers specific standard operational procedures in case of gender sensitive complaints. The received complaints and feedback will be documented and analyzed by the M&E and program staff to feed into the decision-making process and inform possible corrections during implementation that ensures a better response to the needs of the vulnerable groups. Community engagement committees will be formed to build community confidence and networks and ensure efficient and continuous participation of the community in the implementation and monitoring processes and to inform decision making. The project will organize regular focus group discussions with the community members to collect feedback on the provided services. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The projects focus is to enhance protection of GBV survivors and other vulnerable groups by providing a multisectoral/integrated WASH – GBV response. The project design recognizes the rights and needs of beneficiaries in terms of safety, security, and dignity and proposes activities that foster the realization of these needs and rights. It adopts gender-sensitive and culturally responsive approach to implement the project activities which will highly contribute to addressing the social and cultural risks that might undermine the participation of the beneficiaries, especially women in the project activities. DCA/NCA and partners use culturally responsive approach which involves respect for the cultural backgrounds of all right holders, particularly women and people with disability and fully recognize and understand differences, norms, taboos, and stigmas surrounding social and cultural backdraughts of the right holders. During the inception period, the project elaborates an environment and social impact assessment to ascertain the environmental and social impacts of the infrastructural subcomponents; and to manage, mitigate, and monitor any possible negative impacts.All safety and security measures will be considered when implementing the project activities to prevent incidences and reduce safety and security risks. DCA/NCA and partners will keep close monitoring for the security situation and the COVID-19 developments and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. CFTA recruited young interns with health backgrounds to ensure that staff and target groups are complying with the safety measures. In case of closure of any targeted locality because of COVID, CFTA is ready to move with the work online. CFTA will coordinate with the relevant authorities to deliver all items needed by women and girls including those with disabilities under quarantines. DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency (www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population. PSEA mainstreaming will be part of this project in the following ways:• DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA.• DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaints handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality ( either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism or using the partners complaints mechanism)• DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA training to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities.• DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism.• DCA/NCA’s PSEA local focal point is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. Accordingly, DCA/NCA will continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information especially on available referral pathways for sensitive complaints and will continuously seek support from the available network services when needed.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will ensure equitable access to WASH and GBV services for the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable families. DCA/NCA and partners adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions considering the humanitarian principles. Partners undertake need assessments to select the project beneficiaries based on clear selection criteria. The collected data is analysed and scored following an accurate statistical methodology and using Excel to ensure the accuracy of selection. The final selection results are verified and approved by partners’ management and DCA/NCA. All selected beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society.A principled humanitarian response will be implemented: Humanity: The project is addressing the most prioritized needs of the target groups which are designed based on participatory assessment engaging the target groups. The interventions will contribute to reduce the human suffering at the targeted areas in order to protect most vulnerable target groups while respecting human rights and values. Neutrality The project is designed to ensure neutrality far away from any external influence through pre-set criteria that are transparent and will be shared with all stakeholders. The criteria considered vulnerability as one of the main tools to select the target groups.Impartiality the project is designed to be implemented on the basis of the pre-recognized needs by the assessment; in addition, it will give priority to the most urgent cases received by the staff with no external influence. Independence DCA/NCA and partners are legally registered and are fully autonomous from any external political or social influence.
