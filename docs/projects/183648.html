Project Summary

The project has two main components:
1) Strengthening of WASH services for vulnerable households in North Gaza. This intervention is in line with WASH Cluster objective 2 “WASH Cluster Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians. The project will improve the hygiene conditions for vulnerable through rehabilitation and upgrading of WASH facilities including improving the water storage capacity for 180 HHs in North Gaza (Al Amal, Al Shayma ,Al Salateen and Izbit Beit Hanoun).
Moreover, the project will improve the hygiene knowledge and practices for the target HHs and will provide hygiene materials through e-voucher mechanism.
SCI partner will distribute 180 family hygiene kits to the target families with the most
vulnerable girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. SCI will develop hygiene awareness messages in coordination with the WASH cluster. The project partner will deliver the awareness messages through home visits in parallel with the rehabilitation activities inside homes . Beneficiary selection will prioritize vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, HHs with malnourished children and HHs with dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, injury or disability, children suffering from multiple child protection concerns and those receiving case management services. SCI will consult the Community Protection Committees (CPCs) and youth group in the selection of the beneficiaries and the planned working modalities with the targeted HHs.

2) In addition, the project will implement flood mitigation interventions at community infrastructures level in Gaza. This activity is in line with Objective 3 “WASH Cluster Objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks”.
Under this component, SCI will construct of 950 m of new storm water pipeline in Beit Hanoun and 700 m of storm water pipeline in Beit Lahia. The intervention will help approximately 10,000 people in the most vulnerable group to enhance their ability to survive, prevent or alleviate the risk of deteriorating their health and dignity.
To ensure full integration with other WASH actors, SCI will closely coordinate with the WASH cluster and Hygiene Working Group in designing the awareness messages focusing on hygiene and flooding management at the HHs level to strengthen their mitigation abilities to these kinds of risks. To ensure no-harm, SCI will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions amongst community members. The overall project implementation will be adapted to the recent COVID-19 national and international guidelines to minimize the risk of transmission. SCI commits to Do No Harm through ensuring all areas of SCI’ work are designed and resourced appropriately to minimize the opportunities for perpetrators to act exploitatively and to maximize the safety of those engaging with our
programming and those involved with SCI.

1 - Needs

More than 50% of land in the Gaza Strip is residential due to high population densities, where overcrowding and land scarcity skew the pattern of urban development towards housing and away from basic public infrastructure. Due to the absence of storm water network in most of Gaza residential areas, communities use the sewage network to draining the rain water, putting heavy load on the networks and the sewage pumping stations. In many cases, this pressure caused breakages in sewer pipes, causing storm water mixing with sewage water that later on over-flooded residential areas and sewage pumping stations. According to HNO -2022 “Almost 94,000 Palestinians reside in low-lying areas at risk of flooding events during winter due to stations heavy rains and overflow of storm water facilities and sewage pumping-station. More recently and due to climate change, flooding became more frequent and have caused significant losses of assets displacement, disrupted access to livelihoods and services, and exacerbated the risk of waterborne diseases. Beit Hanoun center has been affected repeatedly by storm water floods, and more than 1,500 people where their homes are directly affected by the flood, while the flood caused restriction to the movement of 5,000 persons. In addition, the flood affects their access to the only hospital in the town and access to schools in the area. In Beit Lahia , Alshima neighborhood is on flooding critical area where the rain water flooding affect the life of more than 5,000 individuals.In addition, the low HHs income, together with the shortage in water, leads to poor hygienic conditions of the HHs and increased their vulnerability to health risk. More than 10,000 households (4,000 in the West Bank and 6,000 in Gaza) are suffering limited access to adequate sanitation facilities. They are adopting several coping mechanisms, including sharing latrines with other families and using shelter spaces, other than bathrooms, for handwashing or bathing WASH-(WASH-HNO-2022). The inadequate household WASH facilities increase the challenge of cleaning the house and add house chores on women and girls. This is also resulting in limited privacy and insecurity for women and adolescent girls. In 2020, SCI partner Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA) conducted 650 home visits in deferent areas in north Gaza to update the assessment of HHs hygiene condition and the health impact on the children. The results show that visited families suffer from inadequate WASH facilities inside their homes, increased parasites, and diarrhea cases among the children. Malnutrition is strongly linked to recurrent infections such as gastroenteritis and respiratory tract infections. As a result, there is a clear link between poor WASH conditions (e.g. quantity and quality of drinking water availability, presence of waste disposal facilities) and malnutrition. It is vital to ensure that all health and nutrition interventions integrate key elements of water, sanitation and hygiene (e.g. promotion of handwashing, provision of soap and latrines). The result of home visits to 268 malnourished children in north Gaza conducted by SCI health partner Ard el-Insan showed that only 6% of their homes have adequate sanitation facilities. In October -2022, SCI conducted 2 FGDs with 10 men and 10 women in Beit Lahia (Al Shamia) todiscuss the impact of flooding in the area and the community needs. The participants indicated that the flood causes a health risk to their families due to the mixing of sewage water with rainwater. Families are sometimes forced to leave their homes during the flooding. The flood causes financial losses to many families due to damage to their furniture. The flood increases the burden of cleaning the homes and clothes on women. In the rainy days, movement becomes difficult in the area, especially for children and elderlies. Flooding also poses health risks for children who often get sick, and mothers would have to take care of them adding to their burden

2 - Activities

The project is designed to be in line with WASH Cluster objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank. SCI, in partnership with Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA), will work to improve the hygiene condition of the vulnerable families through rehabilitation and upgrading of WASH facilities for 180 HHs in north Gaza (Al Amal ,Al Shayma , Al Salateen and Izbit Beit Hanoun), and improve the HHs hygiene knowledge and practices and provision of family hygiene kits. The project will also respond to WASH Cluster objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks. The project will contribute in eliminate the flooding risk on approximately 10,000 people in Beit Hanoun and Beit Lahia, SCI will work in coordination with CMWU and PWA to improve the storm water drainage capacity in two Flood prone areas. Key activities will include:1) Rehabilitation/upgrading the HH’s sanitation facilities in 180 homes (BLDA): SC and BLDA will develop the selection criteria based on the vulnerability and the level of risk. BLDA will publicize the project among the target communities. BLDA to apply for benefit from rehabilitation of WASH facilities. BLDA will use its’ database to encourage families for apply. SC will cross check the list of applicant with MoSD to ensure the vulnerability. BLDA outreach workers will conduct verification visits to the nominated HHs ensure the assess the level vulnerability and health risk based on the agreed criteria. BLDA in coordination with SC will screen the applications based on the results of home visit and develop the list of beneficiaries. SC will crosscheck the list with MoSD to ensure the level of vulnerability. BLDA will conduct technical assessment to in the selected HHs and develop BoQ of required rehabilitation works in each home. BLDA in coordination with SC will develop the technical specification for required materials, safety conditions and all bid documents.Through a public bid ,BLDA will select a qualified contractor to implement the rehabilitation work the selected 180 homes. The rehabilitation work will include maintenance of toilets, kitchens taking into consideration the special needs of people with disabilities in target households. It will also include, provision of HHs water tanks and installation of HH water and sewage connections. 2) hygiene promotion (BLDA) :SCI and BLDA outreach workers will conduct home visits to the target families to assess the hygiene negative practices. Based on the results of home visits, BLDA in coordination with SC and WASH cluster / hygiene working group will prepare the design of hygiene promotion messages and proper tools to deliver the messages BLDA outreach worker will deliver the hygiene messages to the target HHs.Provision of hygiene items for the target families:Through the home visits BLDA will conduct meetings with Boys, girls, men and women to identify the specific needs of hygiene items for each group. SC will discuss the results with WASH cluster / to prepare a list of allow hygiene items in E-voucher 5) SC will provide the hygiene items to the target families through 2 top up of E-voucher mechanism 3) Construction of storm water pipelines :SCI SC will coordinate with Costal Municipality Water Utility (CMWU) and PWA to review the plan for mitigation of flooding risk in two flooding critical area ( Biet Hanoun –Biet Hanoun hospital and Biet Lahia – Al Shaima ) . SC will coordinate with CMWU in developing the engineering design and technical specification for construction new storm water pipelines (950 m ) in in Beit Hanoun and (700 m) Biet Lahia. SC will advertise the work to select two contractors to implement the work bids. SC in coordination with CMWU will supervise the implementation of work.BLDA roles:1- Rehabilitation of WASH facilities for 180 vulnerable HHs including selection of beneficiaries, technical assessment and hiring contractor to implement the work 2- Identifying HHs negative hygiene practices , develop awareness messages , deliver the messages to the target HHs .The selection of beneficiaries (HHs) will be based on vulnerability through socioeconomic and protection assessment, and verification of home visits. Beneficiary selection will prioritize vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, HHs with malnourished children and HHs with dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, injury or disability, children suffering from multiple child protection concerns and those receiving case management services.BLDA will conduct a pre-intervention survey to identify health and hygiene needs to develop the intervention plan with community-based organizations and active community members.. In integration with other SCI programming, the local partners, will coordinate with the two existing Community Protection committees (CPC), composed of 50% female members, and CBOs in the target areas . The CPCs and CBOs will facilitate coordination with the community, and facilitate raising the target communities' concerns and urgent needs. To ensure full integration with other WASH actors, SCI will closely coordinate with the WASH cluster and Hygiene Working Group in designing the awareness messages. SCI will coordinate with CMWU and PWA to construct the new storm water. Beneficiaries, both children and parents, will be asked about the process. SC MEAL team will conduct Post-distribution monitoring for hygiene items.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The Project is designed through a child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse from staff/representatives (including volunteers). We ensure that activities engaging with children and their care givers have informed consent, are non-exploitative and do not increase risk of harm or vulnerability. From the child-safeguarding angle, we ensure that all concerns, allegations, and incidents are reported, recorded and investigated according to our local country procedures and hierarchy of reporting within 24 hours. Moreover, SCI embed with CSG the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), our complaints handling mechanisms are robust enough to elicit and deal with PSEA related reports and we do regular awareness training with our staff, partners and community on abuse and the duty to report. SCI will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions amongst community members. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits and cluster consultation. SCI will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed. The project will address adaption guidelines for implementation, to reprogram/adjust programming at country level due to COVID-19 to minimize the harm.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to both women and men according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create women only groups to facilitate their participation. The selection criteria for rehabilitation HHs will focus on female headed HHs, families with member with disability. SCI, together with project partners, will identify the problems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse. Moreover, rehabilitation works will include adaptation of WASH facilities for the needs of PWDs. Also, the rehabilitation will improve sense of security and privacy for women and adolescent females in target households. SCI Gender Advisor, supported by the SCI Health Advisor, will ensure gender mainstreaming in the assessments and interventions. SCI will ensure that the selection criteria prioritizes the needs of pregnant/lactating women and children with malnutrition and/or CWDs from larger and women headed HHs. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI’s policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities in communities to assist in their access to basic services.
