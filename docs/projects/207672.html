Project Summary

Cameroon has been facing for several years now many security challenges with both endogenous and exogeneous causes including: (i) Management of Central African refugees arriving on its eastern border (in particular the East and Adamaoua regions) as a result of the various armed conflicts caused by the multiple socio-political crises in CAR, (ii) Abuses of the terrorists armed group Boko - Haram which rages in the Lake Chad area causing huge displacement of populations within the country, particularly in the Far North region (iii) and the socio-political crisis which degenerated into armed conflict in the North West and South West regions. Since 2017, clashes between the government defense forces and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) pursuing a separatist objective have resulted in a humanitarian crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions on one side and the neighboring West, Center and Littoral regions on the other side.
According to UNHCR statistics in October31, 2023, there are 2 196 969 individuals victims of forced displacement in in Cameroon, among them 475 729 refugees, 9 240 asylum seekers, 1 066 254 IDPs and 746 returnees.
This population is facing protection issues ranking from discrimination (8%), child marriage (10%), family separation (14%), GBV (18%), drug abuse (22%), economic exploitation (24%), kidnapping (26%), early pregnancy (29%), arbitrary detention (37%), lack of civil documentation (40%). In the GBV, sexual attacks stand at 29%. Showers, latrines and water points are among the most insecure places in these regions. The security concern affecting women include risk of attack within the community (27%), sexual violence (29%), lack of safe spaces (30%), the risk of attack when travelling out of the community (53%).
Women and girls are particularly affected, as they suffer the great responsibility of the household charges because the men having greater risk of abduction by armed forced have limited opportunity for conducting income generating activities. The women also face double jeopardy as in one hand they suffer consequences of GBV and in other hand are prevented from seeking for help to the state services by non-armed groups.
From the need assessment conducted in the NW/SW and Far North regions, the top priority needs ranked by the men and women include health care, food, WASH, Shelter, education and protection including GBV prevention needs.
This intervention will focus on protection, with the aim at increasing the resilience of women affected by the crisis through GBV management and economic empowerment. This solution is the most important as it is going to tackle women and girl’s vulnerabilities and provide them with means to care for the other needs expressed such as health care, food, WASH, Shelter and education.
Objectives:
This project intends to Increase the protection of women and girls affected by the crisis in the North West, South West and Far North regions, in order to better resilience for their autonomy.
Beneficiaries: The project targets 10 Councils in the Northwest, Southwest and Far-North regions. With 30000 direct beneficiaries and 60 000 indirect beneficiaries, among vulnerable women and girls, from IDPs and host communities.
The project will be implemented from January to December 2024, with the budget of US$ 3,175,000.
Outcome 1: Women and girls are protected against gender based violence
Output 1: Increased access to quality services for women and girls GBV survivors or survivors of crisis related sexual violence;
Output 2: Strengthened community mechanisms for reporting and preventing gender-based violence;
Outcome 2: Women and girls affected by the crisis have increased capacities and leadership to care for their needs
Output 1: Facilitation of livelihood and economic empowerment of vulnerable women and girls
