Project Summary

SCI will meet the most vulnerable families’ basic needs in GCA and NGCA by using a multi-sectorial approach and distributing coal, winter NFIs, hygiene NFIs and multi-purpose cash, based on the families most critical needs. Lifesaving activities are prioritised in areas affected by the conflict and COVID-19, especially in times of difficult economic situation aggravated by COVID-19 pandemic. Check Points remain limited for crossing for more than 6 months, and in addition to internal COVID-19 related restrictions people have no opportunity to cross the LoC to get access to their pensions and social benefits. The situation in GCA with zoning system (green, yellow, orange and red) remains tense and numbers are increasing day by day, posing an increased pressure on population, especially the ones living across the contact line with limited movement opportunities in case lockdown is activated. The intended aid will alleviate burden for vulnerable HHs with children.
SCI has been implementing similar activities for years with great success and at high levels of quality. SCI will target 14,700 beneficiaries in GCA (0-5km) and Donetsk NGCA, with a total budget of USD 1,500,000. SCI has been operating in Donetsk NGCA since 2015, having a strong team of professionals, and will be able to deliver the proposed activities in partnership with local organisations on time, on budget, with compliance and quality.

1 - Needs

7 years into the conflict that erupted in eastern Ukraine in 2014, vulnerable families have exhausted their coping strategies and require assistance to meet their basic needs. In both GCA and NGCA along the contact line, the COVID-19 pandemic and related disruptions to employment, school, childcare and health systems have further pushed families into conflict- and pandemic- induced poverty. The quarantine imposed by COVID-19 has had a disproportionate impact on women and female-headed households, due to a rise in GBV, higher exposure to the virus in the health sector, lack of access to education and childcare preventing work, as well as higher unemployment in sectors where women make up the largest workforce (OCHA, 2020). The persisting closure of the contact line during the pandemic has further limited movements and increased economic exclusion, in addition to elderly who are not able to receive their pensions in GCA and are often their only source of income. It is estimated that among the most vulnerable households are households with 3 or more children, single parents (in particular female-headed households) with children, and households with children below 3 years of age. The draft 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 3.2 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, including 1.53M in GCA and 1.62M in NGCA. According to the HNO’s overall severity scale, areas along the contact line of Donetsk NGCA are the most in need with overlapping vulnerabilities. The Shelter/NFI Cluster estimates that 168,000 people are in need of assistance, including 56% women and about 25% children. The main needs are related to winterisation. The WASH Cluster estimates that 2.9M people are in need, including 1.8M people with low awareness of COVID-19 prevention measures and availability of hygiene and sanitation facilities. According to REACH’s Multi Sector Needs Assessment (2020) covering NGCA, unemployment and high prices are among the key concerns expressed by surveyed households. Save the Children’s assessment “Listen To Us: Girls’ and boys’ gendered experiences of the conflict in eastern Ukraine” (2020) documents reports of girls having to resort to survival sex to support their families in contact line areas of GCA. According to Save the Children’s assessment ”COVID-19's impact on children in Eastern Ukraine” (draft, 2020), 72% of children surveyed know that COVID-19 is dangerous and only 19% are not worried about it (children with disabilities were less likely to report being worried, pointing to a possible lack of information). Adults, and in particular adults with disabilities, reported difficulties finding and affording food and hygiene items during the quarantine. PPE items in particular were highly requested. In the proposed project the needs of beneficiaries will first be analysed and the most appropriate form of assistance will then be provided.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

SCI will implement life-saving activities to enable the most vulnerable families of GCA (0-5km) and Donetsk NGCA (in line with cluster recommendations) to meet their basic needs for cash, hygiene, winter items (including clothing and fuel). These activities are particularly critical in a pandemic that restricts movements, pushes conflict-affected families deeper into poverty and threatens their lives. Coal and winter NFIs will indeed be critical before winter when vulnerable families lack the resources to adequately heat and dress themselves when temperatures drop. SCI has been implementing such activities in previous years from its main office in Sloviansk GCA as well as its office in Donetsk NGCA. In Donetsk NGCA, SCI has 4 permanent staff that work alongside locally accredited organizations to deliver humanitarian assistance. SCI will deliver activities in line with best practice and Cluster technical recommendations, such as the CWG recommended amount for MPC transfers, Shelter/NFI recommendations for winterization etc. All the activities will nevertheless be tailored to the age and gender groups being targeted (eg. diapers for children, menstrual hygiene etc.). Vulnerability criteria: HH with children and members with disabilities, female headed and elderly headed households with children, HH with young children, etc. Outcome 1: 2,700 (900 HHs) conflict affected boys, girls and their families have increased capacity to cover their essential basic needs strategies through provision of multi-purpose cash assistance in GCA (400 HHs) and NGCA (500 HHs) (Cash Working Group) The transfer amount for one household is 10,350 UAH (1,150 UAH/person/month multiplied for 3 persons of the household for 3 months of assistance) (based on previous project implemented by SCI). MPC in NGCA is to be delivered under support of local partners and can be compromised by COVID-19 contextual restrictions Activities: Registration and verification of beneficiaries; Disbursement of first wave of cash transfers through national post office and/or banks; Intermediate verification of cash utilization; Disbursement of second wave of cash transfers through national post office and/or banks activity; Post distribution and endline monitoring; Lessons learned session Outcome 2: 6,600 (2,200 HHs) conflict-affected boys, girls and their families’ needs for winterization are met in Donetsk NGCA (Shelter/NFI Cluster) Activities: Registration and verification of beneficiaries; Procurement procedure to select supplier; Distribution of winter NFIs; Distributions of coal; Post distribution and endline monitoring; Lessons learned session Outcome 3: 5,400 (1,800 HHs) conflict-affected boys, girls and their families’ needs for hygiene are met in GCA and NGCA (WASH Cluster) Activities: Registration and verification of beneficiaries; Procurement procedure to select supplier; Distribution of hygiene NFIs (including related to COVID-19); Awareness raising on COVID-19; Post distribution and endline monitoring; Lessons learned session Protection and gender mainstreaming: in line with SCI policies (Child Safeguarding, Adult Safeguarding, Anti-Harassment and PSEA), SCI will ensure that protection risks and gender equality concerns are duly taken into account while implementing activities, and partner’s capacity will be built on these topics.Coordination: SCI will engage with the CWG, Shelter/NFI and WASH Clusters at all levels. SCI works bilaterally with agencies engaged in similar activities to ensure geographical coordination and avoidance of overlapping, while ensuring referral mechanisms across organisations. SCI coordinates with the Donetsk NGCA and informs them beforehand about program activities, demonstrating transparency and accountability.Partnerships: SCI will implement through its existing local partners in GCA and NGCA. SCI selects only qualified and accredited local partners, who have been trained, having sufficient capacity, knowledge and expertise.

3 - Indicators & targets

Targets: # of individuals assisted (MPC) (target - 2,700: (313 male, 659 female, 78 elderly male, 165 elderly female, 772 boys, 713 girls)) # HHs receiving solid fuel and heaters for winter (Shelter) (target – 1,400 HHs: 454 male, 1,058 female, 113 elderly male, 265 elderly female, 1,201 boys, 1,109 girls)) # individuals receiving NFIs (clothing sets, bedding sets, and other general NFIs) (Shelter) (target – 2,400: 259 male, 605 female, 65 elderly male, 151 elderly female, 686 boys, 634 girls) #of people benefiting from the provision of hygiene items (WASH) (target – 5,400: (626 male, 1,318 female, 157 elderly male, 329 elderly female, 1,544 boys, 1,426 girls)) #of people reached with hygiene promotion or COVID-19 messages (WASH) (target – 14,700: (1,652 male, 3,640 female, 413 elderly male, 910 elderly female, 4,204 boys, 3,881 girls)) 70% of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age) reporting that humanitarian assistance is safe, accessible, child-friendly and age- and gender appropriate All indicators will be disaggregated by sex and age. M&E: PDM will be conducted to assess the outcome of the activities and monitor proper utilization of assistance provided. In NGCA, monitoring will be done jointly with local partners. Accountability: Conflict affected people (male, female, boys, girls & elderly) will have access to mechanisms (social media, hotline) to give feedback and will be consulted during the project cycle. Information will be shared regularly in an accessible way. Using SCI's Accountability Framework, SCI will apply a range of gender-responsive and age-appropriate mechanisms (proactive and reactive) to ensure accountability to the affected population, and child-friendly feedback mechanisms. Beneficiaries are systematically informed about SCI’s Child Safeguarding Policy and mechanisms in place to share complaints, what can they expect from SCI staff and partners in terms of behaviours towards beneficiaries and that Save the Children is safe for children. Dialogue with beneficiaries, to the extent possible, is also a way to ensure conflict sensitivity and that the intervention is not fuelling further tensions. A feedback hotline is operational and receiving calls daily from all areas of eastern Ukraine, including NGCA. Additionally, beneficiaries can contact SCI through social media accounts and emails if electronic communication is preferred. It should be noted that given restrictions on humanitarian assistance in NGCA, accountability channels are usually less accessible by beneficiaries than in GCA, however partners are also able to gather feedback through their own hotlines. Specific complaints and response mechanism (CRM) provisions will also be made, such as placing suggestion boxes in places visible and accessible to children, adults, partner staff where feasible. Complaints are systematically shared with management staff to ensure prompt action. Analysis of the calls received on the hotline is discussed monthly with the management team to act accordingly. Programme staff and partners will be oriented on their responsibilities in collecting and reporting community feedback
