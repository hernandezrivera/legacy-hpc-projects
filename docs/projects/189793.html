Project Summary

In this project, CEN will target 5460 (6000 Girls ) pupils direct comprising of newly displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, host communities children and people with disabilities who have limited or no access to education services. Activities proposed include and not limited to establishment and or rehabilitation of learning spaces and gender segregated latrines with water points, hygiene promotion in schools, Provision of Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to beneficiaries, Provision of quality education recreational materials to the conflict and crisis affected children and youth, Train teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and Child centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children, Train and update teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms, Train teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health and nutrition, Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures and Organize Back To Learning (BTL) campaigns CEN has professional and qualified staff in Education, WASH, GBV, Peace building and Health who will enhance the efficacy of the project implementation. At present, CEN is supporting 223 schools in Upper Nile and Jonglei state where it is training and supporting volunteer teachers, School management committees and parent teachers associations. Through the intervention, 82,179 (37,073 Females) pupils have been enrolled in Early Childhood and Primary education and are being provided with learning, recreation and teaching materials. Also, CEN is engaged in establishment, improvement and renovation of child friendly learning spaces, water and sanitation facilities across the 223 schools. Further, CEN is actively conducting Back to Learning Campaigns through teachers, Ministry of Education and General Instructions, Community Leaders (Chiefs, Religious Leaders, Traditional authorities) and the local authorities. The intervention is being conducted through South Sudan’s Education Cluster at State and National Level. With a static presence in Melut, Baliet and Renk Counties, CEN is best placed to offer an integrated approach to this Education intervention.

1 - Needs

Majority of children especially girls in Baliet, Renk, and Melut Counties of Upper Nile State and Pigi County of Jonglei State dropped out of school in early primary-level due to cultural related factors such as early marriages, early pregnancies and demand for bride wealth. Pupils attending schools in the targeted Counties are showing some very poor educational performance that have been necessitated by the factors mentioned above especially the girls at which they are disproportionately most affected; other factors are that include insufficient school feeding programs for hungry children, inadequate teaching and learning materials, inconsistent teacher payment, inadequate teacher training, limited sanitation facilities at schools, low enrolment and attendance, and high drop-out rates, especially among girls. Existing community infrastructures such as school serves as a unique entry point for protection of children and other lifesaving services, including access to safe drinking water, food, AWD/cholera prevention, and protection mechanisms reaching one of the most vulnerable groups - children. In 2018, UNESCO, Ministry of Education and General Instructions and Global Partnership for Education released a study on out of school children in South Sudan. According to the report, 2.2 million children across the country were classified as out of school – a figure that has gone up due to factors related to conflict and Covid-19. Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4) includes a target stipulating that, by 2030, all girls and boys should have access to quality early childhood development, care and pre-primary education so that they are ready for primary education. In six out of ten states in South Sudan, more than 50% of five-year-old children – regardless of sex – are considered to be out of school. In some states, the rate range between 49% and 83% for boys and 63% and 89% for girls (differences of 34 and 26 percentage points, respectively). The highest percentages of OOSC of pre-primary age were found in the Jonglei and Upper states, where more than 80% of five-year-olds were out of school. Overall, each year, more than 300,000 South Sudanese children are estimated to miss the opportunity to prepare for entry into primary school. Given the magnitude of internal displacement coupled with the regular influx of refugees into the population, this figure represents a conservative picture of the situation. Factors which lead to children remaining out of school or dropping out include and not limited to the non-functionality of schools due to insecurity; school closures; the interruption of education days during the school year; attacks on School; limited accessibility to the schools and remoteness; poor school infrastructure, limited availability of WASH facilities and soap in schools, Low enrolment and attendance rates; and high drop-out rates, negative cultural practices where communities prefer educating boys and girls; and weak capacity of teachers and education support structures, lack of teaching, learning and recreational supplies and low morale among teachers due to delayed or lack of salaries. Further, female teachers are few and are either unpaid volunteers or untrained. This has a negative bearing on enrolment and retention of girls in schools. School-community partnership remains poor due to lack of sensitization and parent/child involvement in school development plans and other educational activities undertaken by the school administration. Failure to enroll and retain children in school means that boys will continue to be at risk of recruitment into armed groups, gangs and child Labour while girls face the danger of forced or early marriage.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CEN is committed to the protection of human rights, therefore it has established mechanisms to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). CEN is dedicated to upholding IASC 6 core principles on PSEA and will integrate PSEA sessions in all its activities, CEN will have a full time Gender Officer tasked with mainstreaming protection and creating awareness on PSEA to staff, beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on PSEA and their policies pertaining to PSEA during project implementation. The Gender officer will provide information about PSEA during inception meetings with community leaders, IDPs and host community representative, during focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls, and during selection of beneficiaries, during distribution of learning and teaching materials. Also, the Gender Officer will ensure PSEA messaging through banners, T-shirts and brochures during project activities. The Gender Officer will hold biweekly meetings with women and girls representatives to gauge their perception on protection concerns as well as seek their views on how best to safeguard their protection concerns. CEN gender officer will also play a prominent role through existing referral pathways in target locations. Necessary PSEA referrals will be done if and when they arise by the gender officer.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Information about the project (aim of the project, its objectives, targeted beneficiaries, project duration, project donors, and the kind of assistance and beneficiaries selection criteria) will be provided to community members, leaders and local authorities during inception meetings, review meetings and end of project stakeholders meeting. Project reports including progress reports, post distribution monitoring reports and end of project reports will be shared with community representatives, Education cluster at State and National level and with the community leaders in target counties. Information on feedback mechanism will be provided by Reach Organization’s Gender Officer during meetings, training, and selection of beneficiaries and at distribution venues. The Gender Officer will conduct sensitization and awareness about the type of feedback that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit and avenues of receiving feedback. As far as complaint mechanism, CEN will distribute a toll free number which will be under custody of Gender Officer and Protection Officer. The number will also be displayed prominently at registration sites as well as distribution sites. Complaint and Feedback desks will also be availed during selection of beneficiaries and distribution of shelter and non-food items.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CEN will create forums where project staff, its Gender and Protection Officer will conduct Focus Group Discussions, individuals and group meetings to obtain information from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on their perceptions regarding project implementation. In instances where a member of a family is disabled, CEN will conduct personalized meetings so that the individual or their caregivers can be able to communicate their preferences, submit complaints and received feedback. Also, CEN will seek to engage women in leadership positions so that their voices, concerns and preferences can be taken into account right from project planning, project implementation, project planning and evaluation. Further, Complaint and Feedback Data will be disaggregated by gender and age in order to measure the level of participation of females and those living in disability in the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CEN employs evidence-based programming in all its projects which will apply to this project through constant information sharing and feedback from the community. Project feedback and suggestions from the community will be gathered through multiple channels such as safety audits (on protection and GBV), focus group meetings, interviews and Complaint and Feedback Mechanism. The information gathered will be used to inform implementation process. The feedback/information gathered will also be used in advocacy for further actions from relevant parties in situation where CEN is unable to respond to the needs. Positive feedback will form part of documentation of the project success stories.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

On risks of beneficiaries being exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, CEN will conduct a safety audit on all registration and distribution points to map existing and potential risks. Safety Audit Findings will be embedded in project work plan and safety matrix to ensure all project activities are conducted in locations and sites where no risk is posed to staff, project supplies or beneficiaries. CEN will ensure registration and distribution points are near settlement sites to reduce on distance travelled by beneficiaries to collect their shelter and non-food items. On risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, CEN has a robust PSEA policy with all the staff properly trained on PSEA. CEN will conduct PSEA training targeting enumerators, community mobilizers and AAPs committees. Also, CEN’S gender and protection officers will conduct sensitization targeting women and girls, community leaders and local authorities on PSEA mitigation and reporting. On risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA, CEN’S Child Protection policy prohibits any form of child Labour and child exploitation. Gender Officer and the Protection Officer will conduct community sensitization and awareness to ensure child protection concerns are addressed and mitigated before they occur. Cases of Child Protection will be referred to appropriate child protection actors at project locations. On risks that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The project targeting criteria is clear and targets these category of beneficiaries. Community communication and sensitization will be conducted targeting beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in order to minimize of tensions arising from sections of the community who do not receive the assistance. To mitigate HLP related disputes and conflict, CEN through local authorities, protection partners and chiefs will conduct due diligence will be made on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. Staff, local authorities and AAPs will assist to clarify tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership prior to establishment of learning spaces and latrines.
