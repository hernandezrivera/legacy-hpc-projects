Project Summary

UNICEF will sustain the delivery of the basic nutrition services package including the therapeutic treatment to children affected by severe acute malnutrition in IDP camps and hot spot areas to prevent excess morbidity and mortality. The project will also deliver promotional and preventative package of services to targeted communities to increase demand for improved care of children, pregnant and lactating women. Additionally, the project will provide technical support for capacity building of local service providers including MoH in consistent with the Somali agenda of bridging the humanitarian and development divide

Needs

Results of 30 separate nutrition surveys between June and July 2018, indicate that the overall nutrition situation has improved due to a combination of improved food security, reduced disease outbreaks and sustained humanitarian interventions. However, despite this improvement, nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished in the coming months. The surveys indicate Critical levels of GAM (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed (7 IDP and 5 Rural) while Critical levels of SAM prevalence (≥4-5.6) were only seen among IDPs in Mogadishu and Guban pastoral. Similarly, the Crude Death Rate (CDR) rates are Critical (1 to <2) in 3/33 population groups surveyed while CDR rates are Serious (0.5 to <1) in 11/32 and U5DR rates (1 to 1.9) in 6/32 population groups surveyed, respectively. Furthermore, Morbidity rates are high (>20 %) in many parts of the country but highest among population groups surveyed in northeast and central parts of the country. Overall, the median GAM is 14 per cent which is an improvement from Critical in 2017 Gu (median GAM of 17.4 per cent). Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence is Critical (more than 4 per cent) only among Mogadishu Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) settlement and in Guban pastoral livelihood. From a protection perspective, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs are disproportionately affected with critical prevalence of acute malnutrition (Global Acute Malnutrition of more than 15 per cent) in seven out of 15 locations surveyed as well as some rural livelihoods. The seven locations include Bossaso, Baidoa, Doolow, Gaalkacyo, Garowe, Mogadishu and Qardho IDP settlements. The situation is a serious humanitarian emergency among Mogadishu IDPs where acute malnutrition rates are more than 15 per cent, associated with a crude death rate of over 1 per 10,000 population/day. Overall, UNICEF will target 75% of SAM and 34,480 MAM in areas without TSFPs

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project addresses immediate reintegration needs through available humanitarian resources, while mobilizing development funding to strengthen the longer-term resilience of displaced persons and displacement-affected communities (returnees, IDPs and host communities), through community-based approaches. The project is also consistent with the national commitment to Durable Solutions, as reflected in the Somalia National Development Plan (2017-2019).

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Sustained exposure to underlying causes of sub-optimal child care practices and drivers of malnutrition. Persistent high food insecurity, risk of escalating conflict and forced displacement increasing vulnerability.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Ethnic minorities and socially marginalized groups including IDPs are at high risk of exclusion from access to lifesaving services.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The nutrition program works with over 50 NGOs majority of them being local who understand the local context in hard-to-reach areas. In the event of escalating armed conflict, violence and displacement, the project will scale up to reach the affected target groups
