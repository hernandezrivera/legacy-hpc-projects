Project Summary

GOALPrime Organization Nigeria (GPON) intends to target a total number of 50000(60% women/girls and 40% men/boys) will be reached with motorized risk communication with messages on the prevention and control of cholera and 9 Health facilities will be supported health facilities to be able to provide Clinical Management of rap services and specialized Mental health services across camps and host communities in KalaBalge, Bama, Ngala and Dikwa LGA of Borno state. 20000 Information Education and Communication-IEC Materials will be printed and distributed to beneficiaries who will be reached with risk communication. The IEC materials will be developed in Hausa, Kanuri and English languages in collaboration with the Risk Communication Pillar of the Health sector and the Borno State Ministry of Health. 10% of the total number of beneficiaries to be reached will be people living with disabilities.
GOALPrime in collaboration with the Risk Communication Pillar will develop prevention and control jingles/messages on cholera which will be aired consistently on the Radio Station with coverage reaching Kala-Balge and Damboa LGAs. GOALPrime will also procure and distribute small transistor radios and batteries to the 2000 vulnerable household heads during the door-to-door risk communication to ensure that they listen to the prevention and control messages aired through the radio stations.
In Kala-Balge, GOALPrime will implement in IDP camp and host communities in Rann; in Damboa, GOALPrime will implement in Unity Camp, General Hospital Camp, Old Secretariat Camp, Lowcost Camp, and Hausari Host Community while in MMC, GOALPrime will carry out motorized risk communication in 20 camps and host communities.
GOALPrime through its ongoing Community Based Psychosocial support (CBPSS) and Lifeskills project in KalBalge and Damboa LGAs has identified the critical need for mental health psychosocial support, especially for adolescent girls and survivors of sexual assault as well as the need to deploy more health staff to both LGAs. GOALPrime will be fully operational in both LGAs all through the implementation of this project

1 - Needs

Approximately 2 million children need child protection services due to a breakdown or absence of child protection systems and community-based safety networks. Symptoms of child emotional distress were reported by 271 MSNA respondents, with a higher prevalence among IDP and returnee households in Borno state. DTM Site and Location Assessments estimate that 442,600 children have been displaced due to conflict, with a significant impact on emotional wellbeing. Multiple displacement leads to even higher levels of stress (around 10% of the sites/locations are occupied by IDPs who have been displaced more than once). This highlights the urgent need for multilingual MHPSS services. An estimated one in 50 children have been separated from their families due to the conflict and urgently require family tracing, case management and alternative care. Case management is critical for linking children with appropriate response services. Violence against children increases in emergency settings due to the erosion of social protection mechanisms, insecurity, and unmet basic needs. UNICEF estimates that 60% of children have experienced some form of violence in Nigeria. According to the DTM, an estimated 79% of displacement sites and 49% of host communities do not have safe shelter for at least half of the IDPs living in these locations, increasing the risk of violence, GBV and other hazards faced by children. In addition, food insecurity leads to an increase in school dropouts and child labour (these issues were raised by 1,590 households in the MSNA). Child protection needs will continue to be significant. Insecurity due to a consistent or projected increase in armed conflict, coupled with unmet basic needs and lack of essential services, will likely result in ongoing and/or increased population movements. This will lead to high levels of abductions/forced recruitment of children by NSAGs and family separations, increasing the number of unaccompanied and separated children, and forcing families into negative coping strategies that harm children. The need for MHPSS, case management, family tracing, and systems-strengthening services are projected to increase, putting additional pressure on an already under-funded sub-sector.There is a serious shortage of skilled health care workers, particularly doctors, nurses and midwives as the unpredictable security situation hampers the movements of health workers, drugs and other medical supplies. In Kala-Balge LGA, 112,405 (30825 women, 21752 men, 35,094 girls, and 29,734 boys) and Damboa LGA, 164,621 (37917 women, 32768 men, 51,121 girls and 42,816 boys) are people in need of multi-layered humanitarian assistance, (Source: 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/nigeria/document/nigeria-2020- humanitarian-needs-overview). the critical health needs have also been confirm by GOALPrime Organization Nigeria from the relevant community and camp stakeholders in both LGAs during GOALPrime’s rapid needs assessment (June 2022) through which GOALPrime gathered evidence of MHPSS/counseling for survivors of sexual assault, reproductive health life skills services, deployment of health staffs to the health facilities in Kala-Balge, Bama, Ngala and Dikwa; and Risk communication in all the targeted LGAs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

By integrating every strategy with the protection principles, GOALPrime will mainstream protection (GBV & PSEA) throughout the execution of this project and advance meaningful access, safety, and dignity of both beneficiaries and of employers and volunteers. Safety, decency, and avoiding any kind of harm to both beneficiaries and employees are priorities for GOALPrime. Additionally, GOALPrime will also make sure that the target population has equitable access to the service, free from impediments and prejudice, in accordance with their needs. GOALPrime will pay attention to people and groups who could be especially vulnerable or have a hard time obtaining help and resources, including those with disabilities and GBV survivors who have been identified. GOALPrime will integrate GBV Risk Mitigation efforts to reduce or decrease GBV risk to beneficiaries. GOALPrime will work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. Also, GOALPrime will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). All staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project will be given PSEA orientation. They will be familiarized with GOALPrime's policy on PSEA, and they MUST express their unwavering commitment by signing GOALPrime's Zero Tolerance to SEA Code of Conduct. Also, GOALPrime will also lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately, they occur. GOALPrime will also ensure that the identity of the reporter(s) is kept very confidential. When convenient, GOALPrime will encourage reports to be made on anonymous grounds. Also, being guided by the Inter-Agency mechanism for reporting SEA, GOALPrime will ensure a systematized engagement with affected populations including confidential complaints and investigations procedures at both the system and individual agency levels. As recommended by IASC Task Force on PSEA which identified inter-agency cooperation in the creation and maintenance of community-based complaints mechanisms (CBCMs) as a key component in the prevention and response to SEA. GOALPrime will continue to raise awareness to the target population that humanitarian assistance is free and never conditioned on sexual favors as well as to be informed on how to access the appropriate complaints mechanisms, and support to set up in the community if SEA occurs. Furthermore, GOALPrime will ensure that established CBCMs in target locations are effective and that there is inter-agency coordination to ensure consistent messaging and that access to the mechanism is as broad and straightforward as possible for potential complainants. GOALPrime will also be intentional about the environment and safety of the communities where we implement by ensuring that our approaches and tools do not contribute to the degradation of the environment. In addition, GOALPrime will also integrate climate change and provide routine sensitization of the project beneficiaries on climate change prevention. GOALPrime shall mainstream CP, GBV & SEA into all training contents as well as approaches

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In target LGAs, GOALPrime will create or improve new or existing procedures for complaints and feedback. In order to understand the project's impact on adolescents and their carers, GOALPrime will organize biweekly feedback focus group talks with girls and boys who are beneficiaries of the project as well as those beneficiaries' caregivers. Discussions in the Focus Group shall, however, adhere to the COVID-19 rules regarding keeping social distance and the use of face masks. In order to gather opinions and feedback from kids that can be used to improve project activities and strategy, GOALPrime will also conduct at least two kid-friendly surveys – one at the start of the project and another one at the end. Furthermore, GOALPrime will continue to file a complaint and If beneficiaries and their parents feel more comfortable making anonymous complaints, they can place them in suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations throughout the project area. They can also leave suggestions for how the project might better serve them. As soon as complaints are received and reviewed, GOALPrime will inform the beneficiaries, parents, and other pertinent community stakeholders. The program approach will be reviewed, and ideas will also be taken into account. Concerns raised through our established feedback channel will be addressed in a timely and effective manner. Lessons from previous projects have shown, however, that most concerns made through the complaint box system are unrelated to the current project. This will be addressed by GOALPrime as soon as complaints are received and reviewed, GOALPrime will inform the beneficiaries, parents, and other pertinent community stakeholders. In order for the target beneficiaries and members of the community to grasp the expected project outcomes and lodge pertinent project-related complaints, there will be an increased emphasis on project sensitization.
