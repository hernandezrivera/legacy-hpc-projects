Project Summary

Following the crises that has affected Sudan since February 2003 and, in response to the need for accurate information on displacement and the identification of beneficiaries for the delivery of humanitarian aid, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) began its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) program in 2004 to support the Government of Sudan (GoS) and its humanitarian partners in establishing an accurate data collection system and providing data on displacement in areas most affected by the conflict and natural disaster. DTM is a system to track and monitor displacement and population mobility. It is designed to regularly and systematically capture, process and disseminate information to provide a better understanding of the movements and evolving needs of displaced populations, whether on site or en route.
Data collection strategies and tools are based on the DTM global methodology but have been adapted to reflect context and population movements specific to Sudan. IOM has been working in close collaboration with the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) and partners in the field to establish a unified and systematic method to collect individual data on internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in seven states. Up until 2018, the DTM program in Sudan focused on registration, including biometric registration (since 2010), of IDPs and returnees. Registration was mostly carried out in response to requests from the government and humanitarian partners for the identification of beneficiaries and the delivery of humanitarian assistance (i.e., food vouchers by WFP), positioning DTM Sudan as the reference source of displacement data in the country. There remains a strong need to restructure the program and tailor the operation to better suit the current protracted displacement and return scenario, to in turn inform evidence-based programming by IOM and the wider humanitarian community in Sudan. Specifically, there is the need to further update displacement figures more frequently, comprehensively, systematically, and in regular intervals to ensure that data is continually collected, provides a meaningful resource, and is available through IOM’s DTM system. Since 15 April 2023, military conflict has erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) across Sudan. The fighting has been centralised in Khartoum, and has also broken out across the Darfur region (including cities such as Ag Geneina, El Fasher, and Nyala), as well as in the North (Merowe) and Kordofan (El Obeid). The conflict has led to rapidly increasing civilian vulnerability, significant losses and casualties across Sudan, the destruction of key infrastructure in affected areas (such as hospitals, warehouses, and markets), and the immediate suspension of humanitarian activities, widespread looting of UN facilities, and the evacuation of international staff and foreign nationals. Despite the notable limitations to DTM's logistical operations (given the deteriorating security and economic environments) and the connectivity and telecommunication challenges, DTM has been able to maintain key data collection operations, and has provided the UN community with preliminary estimates of displacement and return. These preliminary estimations have expanded from providing initial information on incidences and mobility (locations of origin to displacement), to capturing information on shelter typology, return intentions, and priority needs. In this way, data collection activities have adapted to the current context, establishing an operational framework for the resumption of Mobility Tracking Rounds. Given the pressing emergency context presented by this military conflict, DTM is best placed to provide essential and evidence-based information about the humanitarian context. DTM forms a core component of IOM’s ongoing response to the unfolding humanitarian crisis, among IOM’s other PC1 programming, in Sudan.

1 - Needs

Protracted and new displacement induced by localized intercommunal conflict, increasing protection of civilians, risks and threats due to the rise in criminality and insecurity in parts of Darfur and other conflict-affected areas, unprecedented spikes in acute food insecurity due to dry spells and erratic rains, high inflation and increased food, fuel, and commodity prices, floods and persistent disease outbreaks have resulted in record numbers of people in need of humanitarian assistance.Commencing in November 2020, DTM Sudan expanded its operations to implement Mobility Tracking (MT) across three additional states of Kassala, Gedaref, and Blue Nile, in addition to an initial seven states. In February 2021, DTM further expanded its operations to North Kordofan state and to Red Sea state in December 2021 respectively. Currently, DTM has been conducting its mobility tracking in 12 states and has also expanded its overall operations to all 18 States in Sudan. The MT methodology utilizes key informant networks to systematically track presence of mobile populations in locations of interest over consistent data collection rounds. Enumerators revisit each location and interview key informants to update the locations list and verify population presence on a quarterly basis. Mobility Tracking Round Five, conducted between January and March 2022, identified the accumulative presence of 3,779,487 IDPs, 1,181,419 permanent returnees from internal displacement, 55,045 seasonal returnees, 148,256 returnees from abroad, and 775,570 foreign nationals currently residing in Sudan. DTM will continue to implement MT throughout 2023 (completing data collection for Round 6 in the first quarter) to frequently update population figures and allow for subsequent analysis of trends. Throughout 2022, DTM captured the new displacement of an estimated 359,592 individuals (of which 98,952 were displaced due to natural disasters - floods or fires and 260,640 individuals due to conflict) using its Emergency Event Tracking (EET) tool. As a subcomponent of MT, EET will continue being regularly deployed to track sudden displacements and population movements and provide more frequent updates when needed. EET is activated within 72 hours – one week after an event (such as natural disasters or conflict) to assist in rapid response planning. The military conflict commencing 15 April 2023 has had wide-reaching implications for DTM’s operational context. This has in turn raised considerations which are reflected in our revisions to the budget for this project. Firstly, the new context will create a need for a tighter and more robust security monitoring of data collection operations. Secondly, DTM anticipates that the negative externalities of the conflict on the domestic economy will be profound. Damage to infrastructure and the looting of local businesses will reduce the pool of services available relating to the procurement of transportation. Field teams report dramatic increases in the price of fuel and consumable goods, as well as severe disruptions to internet connectivity and telecommunications services. These factors will also compound on the cost-of-living, which will in return place inflationary pressures on enumerator salaries. Thirdly, DTM anticipates that the displacement caseload is set to continue rising (increasing to approximately 1.8 million IDPs displaced within 6 months of 15 April 2023 - notwithstanding changes in context - as per DTM’s internal projections). These projected new needs and assessments will also require additional operational contingencies (such as expanding key informant networks).

2 - Activities/Outputs

Main Output: Accurate and reliable DTM data on affected populations is disseminated to the humanitarian agencies, clusters, and other relevant stakeholders. Activities: 1. Gathering target population estimates through a broad network of key informants at the lowest administrative level available, for tracking and monitoring key trends in population movements over time. 2. Assessing demographics (collecting sex and age disaggregated data). 3. Identifying priority needs, vulnerabilities, and protection gaps of the target population groups. 4. Building local capacity for tracking and analyzing internal displacement and mixed migration inside Sudan through joint implementation with government stakeholders. 5. Utilizing Mobility Tracking, Emergency Event Tracking and Situational Assessment estimates as an evidence base for operational planning and programmatic design for a more targeted humanitarian response. 6. Providing trainings, workshops and refreshers on standard DTM tools and protection practices for IOM enumerators. 7. Producing regular DTM narrative reports, maps, and graphs to be shared with GoS, humanitarian actors, relevant clusters and cluster partners.Based on IOM’s global methodology but adapted to reflect the context and the population movements specific to Sudan, DTM aims to enhance its past operational approach and focus on conducting the following activities over the upcoming year: Mobility Tracking (MT): The new methodology deployed in 2019 through periodic baseline assessments provides multi-layered information on the different target groups within defined locations and update such figures at regular intervals. As a result, IOM will be able to provide a complete and up-to-date picture of displacement and other forms of mobility in Sudan. These figures are already used by the Government and humanitarian and development partners to better protect, assist, and advocate on behalf of these populations. Additionally, MT aims to produce an evidence base for strategy and programme design to support humanitarian, transition, and recovery operations within Sudan; and will facilitate joint assessment and response planning and joint funding appeals. Emergency Event Tracking (EET): As a subcomponent of MT and activated on a need’s basis, EET will be deployed to track sudden displacements and population movements and provide more frequent updates on the scale of displacement and quantify the affected population when needed. It is activated within 72 hours - one week after an event (i.e., conflict or floods) to assist in rapid response to meet lifesaving needs. To supplement EET, DTM Sudan has designed and implemented Situation Assessments to collect data on populations in non-emergency settings or protracted situations caused by conflict or natural hazards. As with EET, Situation Assessments utilize a broad network of key informants to provide an overview of the context and inform response planning.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM's DTM activities are conducted with strict adherence to data protection principles and international best practices. The exercises draw on IOM's global expertise in conducting similar activities to ensure that information is non-identifiable, stored securely and only accessible, by request, by approved partners. Furthermore, DTM is committed to collecting information on main age and sex breakdown per location of assessment, which is the foundation to protection programming, providing insight to the demographic and specific gender nuance for protection focused programming.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The DTM program provides baseline figures that are then used to build more detailed and nuanced data collection exercises like the peace building fund profiling exercise. In addition, area-based indicators that monitor the progress towards durable solutions will be collected through MT and analyzed together at the individual level collected under different exercises to provide the full picture and measures of durable solution indicators and criteria. Yearly surveys are conducted with data users and partners to allow a continuous improvement and relevance of the data collected. Data is also shared during Information Management Working Group (IMWG) and Durable Solutions Working Group (DSWG) meetings, thus providing another venue to elicit comments and feedback from users. Information on AAP will be shared with the Inter-sector Coordination Group (ISCG) and IMWG in line with international data protection standards and established IOM principles. DTM intends to explore the possibility of conducting follow-up restitution meetings with affected communities.
