Project Summary

In 2022, Human Appeal responded for 20,400 individuals in Idlib camps by winter items in several areas, including Al-Dana, Ma'aret Misrin, Harem and Salqin, contributed to alleviating the suffering of the displaced in the camps, especially after the floods that occurred in January 2021. Also responded for Samidon camp for 30 tents equipment in July 2021 after they burned in the summer.
In 2023, the proposed intervention aims at ensuring Non Food Items for the conflict-affected population in NW Syria with focus on the IDPs as being among the most vulnerable people within the targeted population, the intervention largely target those that have recently been displaced in the first quarter of 2020 to areas near Turkish borders.
Residents of the targeted camps were also affected by the snowstorm and floods that came to northwest Syria in January 2022 in addition to the consequences of Covid-19 and Omicron The pandemic has resulted in thousands of people losing their jobs and being unable to afford minimal housing and warmth requirements. Moreover, the massive inflation of the local currency led to a price hike of about 90% for all the items that they could not buy to protect them from the harsh winter and summer heat, conflict-related conditions have also increased the suffering and instability of the target population.
The proposed intervention aims to target about 2000 HH / 10000 individuals IDPs in Idlib camps by:
1- NFI kits that designed according to SNFI cluster guidelines. A kit contain (1 plastic sheet, 1 insulator, 2 blanket, 2 sleeping mattress, 1 carpet, 1 jerry can) that is sufficient to reach their well warmth in the winter with good protection from the floods and rains, the insulator and plastic sheet are protect each family contains of 6 person from the summer heat especially for the women and children.
2- NFI Supplemetary Items for heating that includes (1 Stove with 900 kg Pomace) contributing to get well heat for one month and keep the camps population away from the winter disease.
Human Appeal Turkey/ Syria team implement their projects coordinating with cluster suggestions with reporting, also coordinate with the local councils with arrange some work on the field.
Our projects target the most needy people in the camps as (disabilities, widows, orphans, poor). HAT have an emergency response sector for any unexpected events as (burns, floods), provide them by tents equipment, additionally the case studies are response by some NFI kits.
Heavy snowfall affected north-west Syria on 18 January, damaging displacement sites, tents and people’s belongings. According to preliminary reports, 22 sites in Aleppo Governorate (mainly Afrin and Azaz districts) and nine sites in Idleb Governorate (mainly Harim district) were affected. As of 19 January, 362 tents were reported damaged and 2,124 individuals (429 families) affected. Abraz Camp in Ma’btali subdistrict in Afrin was badly affected and families were evacuated to safer shelter, including the camp’s administrative building, a nearby school and Abraz village The most pressing needs are reopening of the roads for uninterrupted access to the sites, moving the affected persons to safe places, securing means for heating, replacing destroyed tents, providing ready-to-eat meals and winter clothes for children and families.

3 - Needs

With the security environment remaining somewhat volatile, deterioration in economic conditions and a depreciating Syrian pound, have intensified the severe need for NFI support. As such, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 15 per cent to 5.7 million (from 4.90 million in 2022). Winter clothing, mattresses and blankets have become significantly inaccessible in 2022 compared to the previous year. In particular, clothing expenditure ranks as the highest monthly SNFI expenditure, i.e. three times more than rent and four times more than electricity. Such items are crucial to ensure the well-being of the most vulnerable, particularly during harsh winter seasons when access to basic NFIs is considered lifesaving. Nearly 85 per cent of the overall population reported a deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs, demonstrated through a significant increase in the inaccessibility of NFI items, such as clothing, mattresses, and blankets. Coupled with the deterioration of shelter integrity, increasing shelter adequacy issues (i.e. limited electricity/lighting and poor insulation from heat/cold) households are exposed to harsh weather conditions with a high risk of loss of life, particularly during winter. Adequate shelter conditions and access to basic NFIs is vital for maintaining good physical health, mental well-being, and to sustain learning and educational opportunities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoid causing harm: Concern for the safety and dignity of individuals and avoiding causing further harm is central to mainstreaming protection. The safety of beneficiaries should be the primary consideration in humanitarian action. 2. Ensuring meaningful access to assistance and services: Recognized as a crucial component of all humanitarian interventions, we all must seek to ensure that people have a meaningful opportunity to attain their rights by accessing available services and assistance. Barriers to access can exist in different forms, including logistical and social/cultural factors, insecurity and a lack of information on available assistance and services. 3. Accountability: Setting-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. 4. Participation and empowerment: The humanitarian community should strive to put affected populations at the center of decision-making processes that affect their lives so that services are choice-driven and client-focused. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

- Make sure that all staff sign the Code of Conduct declaration. - Train all staff in PSEA and be seen to take specific steps to prevent it. For example, discuss PSEA action plans in office meetings, meet with government officials to discuss prevention, discuss the issue with partners. - Provide specialized services in support of sexual exploitation by provide instructions to the beneficiaries and setting up phone numbers in the event of a complaint. - Engage in community outreach by provide people about the risks of sexual exploitation through many media resources. - Providing support to beneficiaries who have been subjected to harassment or sexual exploitation by educating them for the future, raising their morale, and ways to reach those responsible for their defense and protection. -Work to activate methods of control over field employees with the help of those responsible for the beneficiaries to ensure that the beneficiaries, whether women or children, are not alone and exploited. - Ensuring the rights of the beneficiaries always by adhering to the humanitarian standards of the United Nations and not tolerating any violations of field staff. - Programs identify risks of harm to children and women of beneficiaries and address them appropriately in project design the project proposals are accompanied by an assessment of the risks and harm inflicted on children and women in order to avoid these risks.
