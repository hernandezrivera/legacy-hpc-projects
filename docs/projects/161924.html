Project Summary

The project aims to enhance protection and resilience of Rohingya women and adolescent girls in the refugee camps, as well as promote social cohesion between host communities and refugee communities. It will also promote women and girls’ empowerment from both communities, as well as strengthening capacities of humanitarian actors, government, law enforcement, media and women's rights organisations/groups on gender equality and addressing GBV, essenitially towards ensuring: i)women and girls, both Rohingya and host community have access to key life-saving and protection services, are resilient leaders, and are able to address and build social cohesion through constructive joint participation and dialogue ii) technical assistance is provided to Camps-in-Charge (CiCs) and law enforcement officers so they are better equipped to address women’s protection and empowerment needs and ensure linkages with elected women, iii)potential women leaders are capacitated with the leadership skills with which they will be able to effectively carry out leadership responsibilities and contribute to community representation, iv) Community Security and gender-responsive policing is enhanced, and v) Capacity of journalists are improved in gender-responsive journalism for improving positive narratives in the media promoting social cohesion and women’s involvement in humanitarian response. In terms of key project partnerships with the Government of Bangladesh, these will build on existing ones with the Ministry of Women and Childrens Affairs (MoWCA) and the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commission (RRRC). UN Women co-chairs the Gender in Humanitarian Action Working Group (GiHA WG) under the umbrella the Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) and manages the ISCG Gender Hub, which both work closely with all sectors, especially the Protection Sector (and GBV Sub sector) and the PSEA Network, to link approaches that can achieve shared aims and be coherent, and therefore the project will be informed towards avoiding any overlaps or duplication of effort. Project reporting will ontribute to sector specific Monthly 5W reporting mechanisms. UN Women also contributes to the preparation of coordinated and harmonized strategic and analytical gender briefs, including recommendations, for the Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG). At a project level, UN Women as well as implementing partners will have dedicated focal points participating and contributing to the Protection Sector Working Group (and the GBV Sub Sector), towards aligning with the response to protection needs of refugees and host communities. Furthermore, all partnership agreements signed on between UN Women and Implementing partners include attention to “Special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse”, acknowledging that Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse are strictly prohibited. The targeted 40000 women and adolescent girl MPWC beneficiaries in addition to capacity building and technical support provided by the project to national partners such as CiCs, and Bangladesh Police are directly linked to the Protection Sector Objectives no. 3 and 5.

Needs

Pre-existing gender inequalities, gender-based violence (GBV) and discrimination are often reinforced, perpetuated and exacerbated during a crisis and this is disproportionately affecting women, girls and the most vulnerable and marginalized Rohingya refugee population groups. Gendered isolation and restricted mobility of women and girls according to traditional norms of Rohingya are limiting their access to life-saving assistance, services and information as well as their participation in decision making. Practices such as purdah are restricting the mobility of women and their access to lifesaving assistance, services and information (Gender Profile 2, ISCG, 2019). Adolescent girls, young women, and women heads of households are some of the most at-risk in this context. Humanitarian responses often miss opportunities to transform socio-cultural gender norms and relations through the leadership and empowerment of women and girls, as well as by promoting positive forms of masculinities — notwithstanding the fact that these are key to a right-based and effective response and to communities’ longer-term resilience and social cohesion. In 2019, consultations through several Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) were held with elected and non-elected Rohingya women, under the umbrella of the women's leadership task force, Gender in Humanitarian Action Working Group (GIHA), which further confirmed Women’s concerns are not being adequately met and addressed in camps and being and becoming a female leader poses considerable challenges. Women and girls, and other vulnerable and marginalized groups among the Bangladesh host population critically face barriers to accessing already scarce and overstretched social services, inflation, as well as labour competition and increase in protection and safety risks. There is a negative perception in the host community that the Rohingya influx is driving an increase in sex work, child marriage and illegal activities such as human and drug trafficking. A baseline survey conducted for a UN Women project on social cohesion in 2019 consulted 530 respondents (all women), consisting 292 (55%) from Host/Bangladeshi Community and 238 (45%) from Rohingya Community, where 36.3% of host community women mentioned that presence of Rohingya community is a burden for them. The increased capacity of the CiCs, Bangladesh Police as well as local women's rights networks to respond to the concerns of women, including protection needs and GBV, and women in decision-making structures will contribute to Grand Bargain localization commitments and greater women’s participation and leadership across the peace-development-humanitarian nexus. Misinformation and negative rhetoric about the Rohingya, and the negative portrayal of Bangladeshi women humanitarian staff and volunteers working for the refugee response in the media is adding to the overall hostility of the host community towards Rohingya, and stigmatization, harassment and security threats against them women humanitarian workers. These indicate the need to work with the media and journalists for gender-responsive journalism and promote an accurate understanding of the media of the role of women humanitarian workers and Rohingya refugees. it is also vital that the local civil society organizations, especially those that work for advancement of women’s rights, women’s leadership and empowerment, are strengthened.

Activities or outputs

Under SO 5:•Running existing five multi-purpose women’s centers (MPWCs) in camps 3, 4, 4e, 5 and 18 to provide life-saving and protection services to 25000 Rohingya women and 15000 adolescent girls. Establishment of 3 new MPWCs (targeting 9000 women and 6000 adolescent girls in host communities). Beneficiaries will be selected through a KAP survey, and as part of an M&E process, will use FGDs to instill a feedback mechanism. •Development of advanced Women's leadership training modules and roll-out across blocks in all camps and across villages in host community •1500 Community members and leaders, especially 300 men and boys (imams, majhis, CiCs, local authorities, community leaders), are sensitized on women’s leadership and participation in community decision-making processes. •Women leaders are coached (on the basis of a mentoring system engaging 8 Rohingya and 9 Host Community Volunteers, in collaboration with CiCs, Government authorities, IPs and humanitarian actors)•Women from host community trained in mediation and leadership, through whom key messaging on topics relevant to protection will be developed and mass communicated•Establishment of 2 women & children’s helpdesks, and Bangladesh Police trained on GBV response•Information sessions with Host Community and leaders on women's engagement in the MPWCs Under SO3: •Placement and training of gender field officers to support CICs to effectively address women’s needs and concerns•Recruitment and training of 36 Rohingya community outreach volunteers (5 per 5 MPWCs in camps + 11 volunteers to support 5 CiC gender officers) •Development of Gender training module for CiCs and their staff and trainings with SMT and UNHCR •Capacity enhancement training of CiCs and CiC staff on Gender, Human rights, Protection and participation •Capacity Strengthening and support to Women Peacebuilders and Groups•Dialogues with Community Policing Forums and host community, Rohingyas, etc. on gendered protection and security issues•Rohingya women leaders are linked to host community women, strengthening positive relations; together they initiate and/or participate in activities to promote dialogue, mediation of conflicts and social cohesion •Capacity building of local women’s rights/women-led NGOs/groups to promote IASC Gender Policy and Grand Bargain commitments• Capacity building of journalists on gender-responsive journalism for improving positive narratives in the media promoting social cohesion and women’s involvement in humanitarian response•Joint activities on socio-economic empowerment and gender/ women’s position by Rohingya women/girls and host communities to promote social cohesionUN Women operationalizes the principle of Do No Harm, by supporting (potential) women leaders and strengthening women’s links with trained and capacitated CiCs and making sure the mechanisms are in place to support women leaders from that level and deal effectively with violence directed at themThe construct of the existing MPWCs were reinforced by the WFP DRR unit to be more resilient during the cyclone and monsoon season - similar efforts apply to the new MPWCs. Information sessions on DRR will be conducted in line with agreed messaging by the EPWG and the CwC WG as well as linking women in the centres to the CPP volunteers. Locations of new MPWCs will be determined based on dialogue with the DCO and relevant local government authorities with due consideration of not contributing to further deforestation.
