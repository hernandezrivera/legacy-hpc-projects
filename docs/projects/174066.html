Project Summary

ProSPINE+ will ensure that vulnerable individuals in North-East Nigeria are protected in their communities and receive support when they experience harm. ProSPINE+ builds on previous and ongoing protection programming in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states. The intervention will target both IDPs and returnees through both joint, integrated as well as targeted specialised services to prevent and respond to violence against displaced communities to achieve the following outcomes: Outcome 1: Women, girls, boys and men are free from violence in their communities (prevention); Outcome 2: Women, girls, boys and men are protected from the consequences of violence (response); and Outcome 3: Women, girls, boys and men access rapid and equitable emergency relief services (emergency response). The intervention will target IDPs, returnees and host communities. Targeting criteria will include: newly displaced individuals/households; children affected by armed conflict or associated with armed groups; unaccompanied and separated children; survivors of GBV; children at risk of or who have experienced abuse, neglect and exploitation; children at risk of early or forced marriage; female- and child-headed households; households with elderly individuals or individuals with disability, chronic illness or reduced coping mechanisms and children with disabilities.

1 - Needs

• The need to address protection risks arising within the household. Through ProSPINE, we have seen high incidence of child-headed households, early pregnancy, forced marriage, domestic violence, child abuse and GBV. • The need to assist people who are returning to their communities and families who have been separated during their displacement or at screening sites, including more than 10,000 unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) in need of immediate assistance. • The need to provide support for survivors of gender-based violence. According to recent interagency GBV Information Management System (GBVIMS) trend analysis, women and girls are mainly seeking supported after suffering sexual violence perpetrated by known assailants or intimate partners, including armed actors during forced conscription or kidnapping. There is generally a delay of several weeks after the attack and the women or girl seeking support services. GBVIMS reports also point to a large unmet demand by the majority of survivors for livelihood support, as services are unavailable. • The need for livelihood opportunities to help mitigate protection risks. There is an increasing number of female-headed households with large numbers of dependents who lack means of livelihood. Through ProSPINE, we have seen first-hand that limited access to livelihood opportunities increases protection risks.• The need to establish protection structures and foster a more protection-sensitive culture amongst government actors and communities to create a safer environment for beneficiaries. To this end, stronger engagement is needed with government actors, through both advocacy and capacity-building support.• The need for greater availability of timely and reliable information on protection issues that is accessible to humanitarian actors.• The need to assist the many individuals lacking the legal documentation necessary to access services and exercise their rights to protection. Housing, land and property issues are prevalent, including loss of title documents, forced evictions, and lack of access to land.• The need to mainstream protection principles and promote safety and dignity of beneficiaries across all humanitarian services.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Plan International will operationalize protection mainstreaming across all humanitarian sectors to ensure promotion of meaningful access, safety, dignity, participation and empowerment of all IDPs. Community feedback mechanisms and regular sex and age segregated consultations will increase humanitarian actors’ accountability to beneficiaries, including improving safeguarding from humanitarian perpetration of sexual exploitation and abuse. Community groups and staff will be trained on safeguarding and protection mainstreaming action plans which will be monitored and adjusted throughout the project.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Plan International will integrate robust feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, older people and people with disabilities. These will include regular FGDs and listening sessions, including sessions specifically with women and girls; suggestion boxes at project sites, including within women’s safe spaces and protection information centres; a toll-free hotline accessible to illiterate beneficiaries, with notices in local languages with the hotline number posted prominently at project sites; and household interviews, which will reach beneficiaries served by mobile teams in hard-to-reach locations. Feedback is received through hotline handlers, who refer requests for assistance or feedback on programme quality to the relevant programme teams for action. Each point of feedback is documented and compiled in monthly reports allowing management to track the status of response and follow-up. AAP will be added as a standing item in the PMC, joint monitoring, review meetings and, selectively, in Steering Committee meetings, as well as in quarterly reporting to DFID. Collaborate with Translators without Borders (TWB) to translate protection messages into different languages and ensure communities access appropriate, confidential and accountable protection services in multilingual contexts. Specific innovative components include the use of internet-in-a-box technology to improve access to feedback and accountability mechanisms, piloting of improved communication with communities on Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and development and dissemination of mobile glossary apps for field-based protection terminology management.
