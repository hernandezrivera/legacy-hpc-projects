Project Summary

Under the general guidance of the Health Cluster strategic objectives and cluster activities for 2024, IOM is planning to facilitate meaningful access to quality Primary Healthcare Centres (PHC) and services for IDPs and crisis-affected population through fixed and mobile health clinics as detailed below: 1. Organizing and conducting regular and joint cluster monitoring and post assessments to assess health situations and identify urgent needs and gaps among the most vulnerable groups of IDPs, returnees, and host communities. Ensure IOM regular attendance in the cluster meetings to share key outputs and challenges for future programming. 2. Provide staff and logistical support to existing and functional health facilities through procurement and distribution of medicines, medical supplies and equipment, and rehabilitation. IOM will support state Ministries of Health (MoH) and 12 functional health facilities in the targeted states by providing essential staff incentives, running costs including fuel, printing of treatment protocols, Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials, registries, and logistical support, while being cognizant of the developing security situation. As a contribution to preparedness and response efforts, strategic and efficient procurement, prepositioning, distribution of medicines, medical supplies and equipment will be employed by collaborating closely with health partners, local health authorities, and stakeholders. Additionally, IOM will continue engaging with stakeholders on cross-border procurement. IOM will contribute to the rehabilitation and/or refurbishment of 18 selected PHC facilities including maintenance/installation of incinerators and solar units. 3. Deploy medical mobile teams in states with high IDPs caseloads to provide life-saving primary healthcare services (BPPHS). Services will include: a) Outpatient consultations and treatment for communicable and non-communicable diseases, support for EWARS reporting and disease surveillance. b) Reproductive health services including Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) to enhance the quality of antenatal and postnatal care and increase delivery services by qualified health professionals; c) Maternal and child health services with a focus on immunizations for under 5 and Pregnant and lactating Women (PLW); d) Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), with both individual and group consultations; e) Environmental health (e.g., waste and vector management); f) Referral and mobile medical team services to increase access for mobile populations and local communities facing limited physical access to health facilities; and g) Health promotion services. Health promotion activities will be delivered by encouraging direct participation of community members to tackle issues of malnutrition and negative health seeking behavior and practices (e.g., an establishment of community health management committees and mother-to-mother or father-to father support groups). RCCE will be emphasized to understand risks, perceptions, behaviors, and knowledge gaps. As a complementary support under this activity, provision of ambulance maintenance and fuel costs will be provided to support efficient ambulatory services identified by state MoH. Moreover, beneficiaries in need of referral from the mobile team will receive partial cash assistance to facilitate transportation and consultation fees further supporting referral pathways between mobile medical teams and specialized secondary healthcare services. 4. IOM will also strengthen the health system’s technical and operational capacity through the provision of relevant support including organizing and conducting capacity building activities: a) Train frontline healthcare workers at the targeted facilities on provision of quality services and community healthcare workers to provide essential health services. b) Strengthening public health interventions at and around PoEs and along mobility corridors.

Implementing Partners

Non-governmental organizations (NGOs), Civil Society Organizations (CSOs), state and federal Ministry of Health (MoH). Potential non-governmental partners will be selected through a competitive bidding process, including thorough due diligence. In emergency health response, IOM may provide direct support to some of the targeted clinics if there are no NGOs or CSOs with a physical presence and demonstrated a strong capacity in delivering emergency health responses. The interventions will be implemented by providing medical and healthcare service providers with capacity building including training, along with essential medicines, medical equipment, supplies, and furniture as needed. Direct interventions will be conducted for at least nine months to address the current emergency, after which IOM will prepare a clear handover plan to state MoH if the security situation permits.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As a measure of protection mainstreaming, IOM is providing health, nutrition, MHPSS services activities through multi-disciplinary mobile teams that has an integrated protection assistance team, assigning a case worker and a psychologist to provide protection assistance to the IDP gathering sites and host communities in catchment area. The project will continue to address the protection needs of IDPs, women, and other vulnerable groups such as returnees and other affected communities through strengthening the capacity of community to create a protective environment. The project will pay special attention to women of reproductive age, PLW, children U5, elderly and persons with disabilities who do not have adequate access to essential health services and are thus more vulnerable to large scale epidemiological events and disease outbreaks. The planned interventions will focus on the development of women’s capacities and strengthening women’s participation through their involvement in community committees and tailored services targeting women and girls. Additionally, the project will support interaction with communities to address negative health seeking behaviors and promote gender equality and reproductive health rights. In order to address the consequences of GBV, the project will seek to strengthen the coordination between the health service outlets (mobile clinics, multi-disciplinary mobile teams, and IOM supported health facilities) and GBV Sub-Cluster. This will be achieved by building the capacity of healthcare staff on CMR, psychological first aid (PFA), and referrals. In coordination with the IOM protection team, the project will seek to improve the linkage of GBV survivors to existing health and other multisector services as per GBV area of responsibility guidelines.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

A beneficiary satisfaction form will be administered to a representative random sample of beneficiaries to gain feedback on the quality of services provided. The form is available in both paper-based and electronic version using Kobo Toolbox, and is offered in English and Arabic. The feedback will be used to make necessary adjustments to healthcare service provision and operations and highlight local communities' health needs as required to ensure quality of assistance and AAP. As a continuous learning process, a 'lessons learned register' will be kept and regularly updated with experiences from the field teams on factors that equally hasten and hinder project implementation, with brainstorming sessions to be organized on recommended solutions. A final report of lessons learned will feed into after action reviews (AAR) to improve organizational learning.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional GBV in crises framework on GBV response during crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to refer GBV cases through the referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance. The project will implement key principles ensuring accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be considered starting from the first phase by assessing critical/ humanitarian needs of affected populations and local communities to the interventions. Efforts will be made to engage women (especially PLW), children, youth, and people with disabilities. The project will empower these groups by encouraging direct participation in community-based health awareness activities. As part of capacity development, CHWs will be trained as mentors and key messengers addressing negative health practices as well as protection threats such as harassment, sexual exploitation and abuse against women, girls, and persons with disabilities. Accountability will also be promoted through the capacity development of CHWs and health providers and NGOs/CSOs in the health sector as duty bearers to inform the cluster partners in health and protection of any protection concerns. Applying IOM's expertise in enhancing the protection of migrants, including IDPs and returnees, and crisis-affected populations through the provision of direct assistance services, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling, and medical assistance to victims/survivors of SEA and GBV in strong coordination with the cluster partners as needed.
