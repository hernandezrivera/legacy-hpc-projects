Project Summary

Acute malnutrition persisted high in Somalia, at the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three years. According to Food Security & Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) Post Gu Assessment in July 2021, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score). The drivers To address the high rate of acute malnutrition and considering gender inequality and all other inequalities in Somalia, ALIGHT will provide equitable access to quality preventive and life-saving curative nutrition services through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases, support curative and preventive nutrition interventions to treat children under five (U5), pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). The need assessment and proposed assistance is tailored towards all gender, age groups and those with disabilities. Services include nutrition screening, targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP) and outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) as well as management of SAM children with medical complications at stabilization centres. All interventions will be aligned with MoH, WHO, WFP and UNICEF guidelines/protocols and Somalia integrated management of acute malnutrition (IMAM) protocols through capacity building to the qualified staff and CHWs on IMAM, IYCF and CommCare COVID putting the COVID-19 protocols into consideration. ALIGHT will improve access to life-saving preventive nutrition services with an integrated health response and protection mainstreaming for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in an emergency, provision of supplementary products, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition services will be co-located at the MCHs to help ensure that children U5 across both genders (69498 males and 72335females) and PLW presenting for health services are screened for vaccination, nutrition and likewise those presenting for nutrition services can be referred and easily access primary, maternal, and child health services, as needed.
Considering all genders, all children U5 presenting at the MCH will be screened and if necessary, referred for admission to the TSFP or OTP per the IMAM admission criteria. SAM cases presenting with medical complications will be referred to the nearest stabilization centre (SC). ALIGHT is also planning to combine the nutrition and health nursing staff to foster more integration across health and nutrition activities at the facilities while undertaking protection mainstreaming. The linkage to WASH and health components will ensure a comprehensive approach to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with the compound effect of diseases resulting from poor sanitation conditions, lack of access to safe water, and malnutrition. To increase the impact, the nutrition project will be coordinated also with ALIGHT WASH, Protection and Livelihood projects. ALIGHT will undertake SMART or IYCF survey for evidence-based intervention and Strengthen nutrition situation coordination, monitoring and surveillance, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response Regularly updating the ONA and sharing 3W Matrix, ALIGHT will closely coordinate intervention with the cluster, MoH and respond in locations where gaps in nutrition services have been identified. ALIGHT will ensure the participation of the local communities, with special attention to the participation of women, in decision making throughout the project cycle to strengthen community acceptance and ownership. Throughout the lifetime of the project, ALIGHT will work with the MoH throughout the lifecycle of the project by sharing information and supporting joint support supervision to pave the way for a proper phasing out at the end of the project.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

ALIGHT is dedicated to safeguard that beneficiaries safely access services, devoid of harm, to promote meaningful access, accountability, and participation in the provision of nutrition assistance considering risks and barriers to access faced by persons with disabilities, maximizing the positive impacts of nutrition programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights. Establish proper information sharing protocol for the disabled in a manner they can understand and survivors of abuse to avoid exposing them to further trauma and that efforts are made to ensure confidentiality and wishes or comfort of the beneficiaries. ALIGHT’s needs assessment tools and frameworks will be adapted to strengthen understanding of all disability inclusion ensuring that persons with disabilities are involved in any key informant interviews and focus group discussion. A varied sample of persons with disabilities will be part of the focus group discussions including men and women, boys, and girls, of all ages and impairment types so that a diverse insight into the risks and barriers can be understood ALIGHT will keep beneficiaries and personal data records confidential and safe from unintended use for effective data protection measures. Clearly marked separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females accounting for persons with disabilities and children will be provided. For impartial and equal access of the basic services for all, ALIGHT will ensure proper documentation, collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, disability and diversity to effectively inform programming and ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable. ALIGHT requires all partners and contractors, to sign Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) policy at the time of agreement, and all staff, suppliers and consultants at the time of offer letter so that the contracts are not valid without a signed code of conduct. To strengthen participation and empowerment, ALIGHT will involve all categories, age, or gender group, disabled and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to needs. For purposes of transparency, ALIGHT will set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment, and assist in detecting misconduct by creating complaint boxes at nutrition sites, phone lines, setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that does not require the beneficiary exposing themselves ALIGHT teams will respond to complaints, immediately documenting lessons learnt, organize awareness-raising sessions so that people know how it works. ALIGHT will utilize the real time beneficiary feedback system, Kuja Kuja as a tool that captures the ‘pulse’ of customer satisfaction with services and to allow service teams, technical teams, and support functions both at the local and global levels to understand real-time issues being faced, see opportunities for immediate and strategic improvement to service delivery. Feedback mechanisms will be accessible to all persons including the disabled with support from the Kuj Kuja team members who are all times standby to provide necessary guidance Through the adoption of Do No Harm approaches, efforts will be made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs implemented to improve relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of conflict situations. ALIGHT believes that understanding the context in which it operates is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and avoid negative consequences. ALIGHT will assess the context and conflict dynamics, especially the role of dividers and connectors to link the conflict analysis with the program cycle helping ALIGHT to re-plan, implement and monitor the interventions.
