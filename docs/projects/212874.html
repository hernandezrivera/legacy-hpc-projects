Project Summary

Norwegian People’s Aid (NPA) established a humanitarian mine action programme in Syria in 2021. The project's primary objectives are to protect civilians from the impact of explosive ordnance (EO) to ensure the safe return of IDPs in Yarmouk Refugee camp, Damascus, and to ensure safe access to livelihood opportunities in Nashabiyeh district, Rural Damascus.
NPA’s intervention responds to these needs by ensuring the mapping, marking and safe removal of EO to international standards.
More than a decade of civil conflict in Syria has left the country heavily contaminated with EO in both rural and urban areas, with more than 150,000 people displaced from the Yarmouk Camp, and more than 2 million people in Rural Damascus requiring protection. From early 2013 to February 2020, UNMAS recorded more than 12,300 victims of EO accidents in Syria, with children making 25% of the victims. Men and boys are the most affected at 80% of the total number of victims reported. Almost 25% of the total are internally displaced persons (IDPs). Women and girls are also heavily impacted through financial liability, security and access to opportunities, due to the presence of EO.
This project will continue the deployment of the existing NPA capacity at the two locations. In each location, two Multi-Task Teams (MTT) and two Non-Technical Survey (NTS) teams are managed by an international Technical Field Manager, with one additional TFM as leave rotation. This gives the project a total of four MTTs, four NTS teams, and two international Technical Field Managers. Targets for the initial period of this project: 01 July 2024 to 31 December 2024 - NTS – 3,500,000 m2 – cost of $0.07 per m2, Clearance – 498,500m2 – cost of $1.40 per m2. Targets for proposed 2025 period of this project: 01 January 2025 to 31 December 2025 - NTS – 7,650,000 m2 – cost of $0.05 per m2, Clearance – 1,210,000 m2 – cost of $1.00 per m2.
The work of the teams is guided by strict NPA prioritization criteria, with full support of the National Committee for Mine Action. This includes accidents, proximity and blockages, in order to target those people most in need. NPA avoids discrimination in relation to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries. This is guided by severity analysis, needs assessments, analysis of accident/victim data, and inclusive communications with communities on prioritization of tasks and to manage expectations of response times. NPA staff are gender diverse and ensures the engagement of women and girls in project planning and implementation and have established proven feedback mechanisms. NPA has integrated Housing, Land and Property (HLP) risks into the project, including the control of, and access to, cleared land.
NPA’s Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct are essential elements of the ethical standards framework, together with a global Whistleblower system. The Safeguarding Policy focuses on preventing and responding to harm caused by Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) or harassment in and outside the workplace. Each NPA staff member is required to read, understand and sign the policy, while specific training is regularly conducted in Arabic and English. SEA risks related to NPA’s programme arise from engagement in communities when conducting NTS and clearance activities. To mitigate these risks, Prevention from SEA tools and processes are integrated at the project design, implementation and reporting stages. NPA works to ensure that affected populations, especially women and youth, are informed and aware of their rights, and how to access the complaints / feedback mechanisms in a safe and anonymous manner.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NPA has reviewed risks by including key staff with different expertise including compliance, legal, finance, logistics, communication and security in all project phases. NPA regularly monitors, maps and analyses all key risk categories that may have an impact on this project. A comprehensive, context specific conflict sensitivity framework is integral to ensuring that NPA’s programming causes no harm. A dedicated in-country Safety Manager ensures that all NPA security needs, policies, and procedures, are in place and observed.
