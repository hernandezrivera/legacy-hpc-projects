Project Summary

VSO- Bangladesh is Education in Emergency (EiE) project for FDMN and Host Community programme with the implementing partner Shushilan at Cox’s Bazar to provide equitable, accessible education and safe space for learning to help crisis affected Rohingya and Host community children. The main target is that 3-14 years aged children can learn continuously in high-quality, safe and protective learning environments. The programme aims to provide community based accessible Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) support through female caregiver (Big Sister/Mother) for Rohingya and Host community children and transitional learning opportunities for Rohingya children (aged 6-14) in a safe environment. VSO is also responding to COVID-19 situation within the project area by distributing hygiene kits and awareness activities. VSO’s intervention is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality. The different needs of girls and boys have been analyzed and integrated well in the activities and objectives. Sex and Age disaggregated data is collected to understand the different needs of the groups. The project will promote inclusive teaching methods and materials, ensuring participation of girls and boys by building the capacity of the teachers through using gender-sensitive methodologies. The project targets to increase coordination and collaboration among stakeholders to improve the quality of education provision for Rohingya community children that is aligned with education sector standards. Children living with disabilities are also enrolled in Community Based Centers. The project will continue identify the children with the disabilities (impairment) and other special needs who can access the ECCE in community-based centers. VSO highlights gender responsive programme through enrolment, recruitment material development, center management and teaching learning process. Social inclusion and gender’ are one of VSO’s core approaches, along with resilience and social accountability. VSO integrate technology-based education solution with ECCE intervention using VSO school app following the curriculum of MeshGuides, and VSO family booklet in partnership with Ustad Mobile. The app is to support learning and provides an innovative and portable way of accessing interactive content for facilitators with no professional experience of teaching.

1 - Identified Needs

Education is essential need in the humanitarian crisis context like the refugee camps in Bangladesh. Due to pandemic it has been more challenging situation. Several national and international NGOs and the government are working to secure the education for Rohingya children in such fragile context, but still more efforts need to be made to make education for these excluded children include girls, and children with disabilities. The nearby community are also affected by the influx. With Covid-19 pandemic from March 2020, education activities were paused thinking of the safety of the learners and still about to be fully resume. The enrolled children are out of learning for long time and few early aged learners are waiting to be enrolled and promoted to next level. Therefore, organizations need to come forward with different strategies and commitment to ensure the quality education service. VSO will continue work with families and volunteers to develop community-based early childhood education spaces, whilst training them to become community educators. JMSNA Factsheets (November 2021) it has been highlighted that sectoral Living Standards Gaps (LSGs) among households with unmet needs for education is 50% of the refugees in the camps. 47% of households with school-aged (6-18) children (girls 45% / boys 29%) reported at least one school-aged child as not having been enrolled in learning facilities pre-COVID. 51% of households with school-aged children (51% girls/35% boys) reported at least one school-aged child as not having regularly accessed home-based learning. Realizing the need and scarcity of available spaces in the camp, VSO will mostly work with 50 community based ECCE programme as an expansion; as for the including more learners, aged 6-4 for the learning centers, VSO already have 12 Learning Centers in camp 15. No doubt that the host communities are very much affected due to this influx and several children in the host communities have not received any form of education. Following the J-MSNA Report (November 2021) 50% of households with school-aged (6-18) children (girls/boys) reported at least one school-aged child as not having regularly accessed home-based learning. Following the same ECCE intervention at camp level, we are delivering this successful intervention for the children in the host communities given the extremely low number of schools going children there. J-MSNA reports mention that several children indicated availability of learning materials as one of the most significant challenges. Train volunteers to create low-cost, sustainable resources, so a lack of supplies will reduce barrier to learning. Alongside developing alternative and accelerated education programme to make sure young learners don’t miss out at this crucial time, we provide psychosocial support to help children cope with the stresses of their experience and environment. Creating safe spaces for learning and play helps bring a sense of normality, allowing those who’ve suffered trauma to regain their childhood. The project considers the child protection and mental health development issues as high importance as well as cross cutting components towards all interventions. Due to lack of awareness and some traditional harmful practices, the Rohingya children particularly girls are not being supported to continue their education. We are trying to engage female caregivers, facilitators and volunteers mostly to increase girls engagement with in our project. Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) are sensitive issue and have many stigmas and myths attached to it. Girls are not confident to share their concerns. In addition, educators may not feel comfortable discussing SRHR due to lack of knowledge or cultural reasons. Institutions may also not be prepared to include SRHR in their program due to the negative attitude attached to it. VSO developed a sensitive approach to address misconceptions and stigma related to SRHR.
