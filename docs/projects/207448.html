Project Summary

Plan International is a prominent international non-governmental organization (NGO) dedicated to child rights in Cameroon. The organization actively collaborates with civil society organizations and networks, particularly those focused on child rights, inclusive education, and the protection of girls and young women from gender-based violence (GBV). Plan International Cameroon holds membership in the national committee on human rights and actively participates in national working groups and emergency response coordination thematic groups, playing a crucial role in shaping the humanitarian and emergency crisis response in the country.
The project undertaken by Plan International is strategically designed around four outcomes with the overarching goal of providing support to children, adolescents, and youth (CAY) affected by the humanitarian crisis in the Lake Chad region. The primary focus is on adolescent girls and young women, aiming to empower them through gender-sensitive protection, sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), and nutrition interventions to help them realize their rights with dignity.
The programmatic framework of the project adopts a multi-sectoral and multi-level approach, addressing the needs of adolescents and youth at different levels, including individual, family, community, and institutional levels. The project emphasizes outcomes such as strengthening child protection and SRHR, promoting positive coping mechanisms, and ensuring gender equality. Key activities include conducting a multi-risk assessment, engaging religious/traditional authorities, involving parents/caregivers in life skills and nutrition sessions, revitalizing community structures, and establishing safe spaces for adolescents and youth.
To address child protection and SRHR, the project implements age and gender-appropriate life skills sessions, case management services for those at risk or who have experienced protection incidents, and involvement of religious/traditional authorities. Parents/caregivers are engaged through life skills and nutrition sessions, and community structures are revitalized to ensure prevention, identification, and appropriate referral of child protection, GBV, and malnutrition cases.
The project places a strong emphasis on psycho-social support, developing a training curriculum and tools for holistic case management. Safe Spaces are established or strengthened, with active consultation with adolescents and youth on their location, management, and monitoring, as well as the composition of dignity kits.
Service providers receive training on child protection, including standard operating procedures (SOP) and referral pathways, and community members are sensitized to their way of working through awareness sessions. The project aims to improve access to emergency services, particularly through the establishment of mobile units operating at the community level. Lifesaving SRH interventions are included, focusing on awareness sessions on GBV, timely referrals for adolescent survivors of GBV, and the distribution of SRHR supplies.
To address malnutrition, parents and caregivers are trained on the Family-Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) approach, and awareness campaigns and cooking demonstrations on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) are conducted. Children under five with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) have access to lifesaving treatment via community CMAM sites or through a clear referral path.
The project also targets youth aged 18-24, with a special focus on (S)GBV survivors, those at risk of recruitment into armed groups, married youth, and youth parents. Pregnant and lactating adolescents and young women are considered vulnerable groups, with efforts to address domestic violence and socio-cultural norms affecting them.

Implementing Partners

ALDEPA will be the main implementing partner. However, Synergies will be sought with relevant humanitarian and development actors and programmes to ensure coordination and address duplication. Linkages will be established with these actors to reference beneficiaries for longer-term services and ensure continuity of support. A strong coordination system with the Government and its Line Ministries (Ministry of Women Empowerment and the Family,ministry of Health, Ministry of Social Affairs) are integrated within the action. The protection and nutrition activities are built on the existing systems/programmes in target areas, while CP case management interventions are built on the National SOPs for working with Unaccompanied and Separated Children and other vulnerable children in the regions (signed and endorsed by the Government). The relevant Ministries will be strongly involved in the planning and oversight of the project activities, thus contributing to the action’s sustainability.
Also, Plan International will collaborate with relevant sectors to conduct a mapping of service providers using the service-mapping tool to develop a functional referral pathway in the project locations and where a referral pathway already exist, Plan International will liaise with existing actors to review and update the existing referral pathway to ensure quality and timeline service provision to project participants. Beyond the sectors, Plan withh coordinate with UNICEF, UNFPA, HCR, IRC, WFP, INTERSOS to ensure joint efforts to address the needs of targeted beneficiaries through synergies and complementarity.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Lake Chad region is affected by a persistent and multidimensional humanitarian crisis which is driven by more than 10 years of armed conflict, displacement, food insecurity and malnutrition, disease outbreaks and extreme weather events exacerbated by climate change. The persistent insecurity has resulted in displacement, devastated agricultural production and livelihoods, limited access to essential services and infrastructure and a protection crisis. According to IOM’s regional Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) dashboard as of December 2022, Lake Chad countries were hosting an estimated 5,752,539 affected individuals made up of 3,205,485 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), 2,239,055 returnees and 307,999 refugees. Over one million people were internally displaced within Cameroon, while 560,000 people returned to their area of origin, often finding their homes partially damaged or destroyed. Among displaced communities, the risk of SGBV has increased with the breakdown of family and community structures, changes in social and gender roles or responsibilities and increased socio-economic vulnerability. Children, adolescents and young adults, especially girls and young women, are severely affected by physical and psychological violence and restrictions on their rights. The risks of neglect, violence, exploitation and abuse of them are compounded by the dire humanitarian situation. Negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage are increasing significantly in regions like Diffa. Survivors of GBV lack adequate health and psychological support and virtually no legal recourse. Health facilities and specialized services are inadequately equipped and not attuned to the needs of children adolescent and youth. There is also a general lack of social and information services and support networks for them at critical stages of their development. In families and communities, the SRHR of young people, especially girls and young women, are suppressed. Sexuality is a taboo subject. The development deficits, few economic prospects, few opportunities for meaningful civic participation by youth and low trust in the state, as well as its lack of capacity to provide services or respect to human rights, are the main reasons that drive youth and young adults into violent extremism.In 2023, 4.7 million people will need humanitarian assistance in Cameroon; this is considerably higher in comparison to 2022, when 3.9 million people were estimated to need humanitarian assistance. This is mostly due to an important increase in the number of people facing acute food insecurity (HNO). Attacks against civilians, State security forces (SSF), including army, gendarmerie, and the police, persisted and non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and armed criminals continued to carry out lootings in crisis-affected areas. In addition, the region has also been affected by environmental changes, including droughts and desertification. These changes have led to reduced agricultural productivity and increased food insecurity, particularly in rural areas. Cameroon’s population is predominantly young, with half of the population under 19 (HRP). Children are at high risk of violence, abuse and exploitation, family separation, and have significant psychosocial needs. Due to insecurity and population movements, many children have been forced to flee their villages, sometimes being witnesses or victims of violence leading to high levels of stress and trauma and significant negative consequences for their cognitive and emotional development. Children are regularly exposed to violence, including kidnappings, abductions, recruitment, maiming, exploitation, and killing in addition to the consequences of negative coping mechanisms adopted by their family, such as child marriage and child labour.
