Project Summary

In 2020, despite the reduction of large-scale hostilities since March 2011, the Syrian population continues facing particularly severe cumulative impact on their mental health and psychosocial wellbeing as a result of protracted conflict, displacement, insecurity, uncertainty, compounded by COVID-19 pandemic and a deepening economic hardship. The population all ages groups, overwhelmed host communities, IDPs, returnees, and refugees, other vulnerable populations and marginalized groups are in a dire need more for proper support.
The major objectives of mental health programme are in full alignment with HRP 2021 strategic objectives and to be pursued through providing integrated and responsive mental health services in health facilities, community-based platforms and outreach mobile teams; development of strategies for promotion and prevention in mental health, strengthening of data management and information system for MHPPS and capacity building.
In 2020, WHO supported delivery of 221,951 MHPSS services at community level in areas under the jurisdiction of the MoH - mostly in areas of health sector severity scale 3 and above - through partnership with 17 NGOs. Management of mental health, neurological, and substance use conditions was delivered by specialized and/or trained and supervised non-specialized health care providers during 2020 44,292 consultations. In addition, 74,921 psychosocial support services were provided by trained health workers and psychologists during 2020 for distressed people, including psychological first aid and through referrals and linking vulnerable individuals and families with resources. The total number of reported psychiatric cases receiving inpatient care for management of mental disorders from specialized health care providers was 8,017, while the total number of treatment courses of psychotropic medicines delivered was 149,058. The total number of health facilities with integrated mental health services rose to 580; furthermore, 1,470 school teachers and counsellors were trained on school mental health package. WHO aims to increase the coverage in 2021. The established and updated referral pathways for MHPSS services will be expanded, effectiveness and usability of the tool to be evaluated, with the continuous adjustments and feedback received from partners in MHPSS TWG, and the inclusion of cross-sectoral services. Mental health promotion and prevention activities are carried out at community level, with full participation of community members throughout all phases of projects from design to implementation and evaluating the results. WHO has supported awareness-raising sessions in partnership with partner NGOs. Such interventions will be continued in schools, homes, community health settings, IDP camps and camp-like settings, and neighborhood and community groups.
In northwest Syria, WHO continues to provide support to all 169 existing facilities with MHPSS services, providing technical support and regular supplies of medication in 74 communities and 29 sub-districts. As of September 2020, there were 163 doctors trained on mhGAP, 473 Psychosocial Workers and 18 psychologists. For HRP project 2021 it is a great need to ensure the continuity of the existing MHPSS services, including support for 2 specialized MHPSS facilities in Idleb and Azzaz, respectively that serve as the main referral MHPSS facilities across NWS. Aiming to provide mental health support in hard-to-reach communities and camps, it is critically important to ensure operational support for Mental Health Mobile Clinics including responding to sudden displacements of people through provision of Psychological First Aid, and emergency response for any acute mental health care emergency across NWS. It is planned as well to continue supporting 6 non-specialized MHPSS facilities that are providing quality non-specialized MHPSS care and are helping to filling in the gap on MHPSS services, including supporting MHPSS Helplines .

4 - Needs

In Syria, mental trauma is widespread and under-assessed but certain to have long-term implications across all population groups. There is a huge need to sustain the supply chain of psychotropic medication to afford treatment courses for people suffering from MNS conditions and ensure receiving the proper continuity of care during COVID19 response. IDPs in different sites across Syria suffered from the psychological burden of being uprooted from their homes, being repeated displacement, witnessing destruction, loss of or separation from family members and assets. Children of IDP households face elevated mental and physical health risks. In 2020, rates of psychological distress amongst IDP children (31 per cent) have almost doubled since 2019 and are highest when compared to children of other population groups. One-quarter of all interviewed returnee households indicated that their children had shown signs of psychological stress in the past month, such as anxiety, sadness, fatigue, or frequent trouble sleeping. Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 23 % of surveyed households’ children such as anxiety, sadness, extreme fatigue or frequent trouble sleeping demonstrating the cumulative toll on mental well-being with immediate and if not addressed, lifelong consequences. Students and school personnel with psychological distress need access to appropriate services within their schools or through referrals to specialized services.The impact of the protracted emergency on the lives of people living in NWS, including the impact of COVID-19 has rendered several areas of concern for suicide prevention. The months of April- June 2020 saw a 57.7% increase in attempted suicide compared to January- March 2020.

Specialized, focused, community-based, and social secure needs, in addition to other type of services beyond MH and Health services.Implementations risks includes; Difficulties in delivering MHPSS services in newly accessible areas are due to severe damage in infrastructure, and in emergency response areas due to hardship in accessibility; maintain a sustained trend in funding community-based projects such as family wellbeing centres and MHPSS outreach mobile team; Restrictions of movements for IDPs and as well for the implementing partners staff due to local authority approvals; Limited access of vulnerable populations (including GBV) to seek services due to stigma and culture related concerns and Possible stigmatization of MH and GBV interventions at the community.mitigation procedures: expand and sustains the partnership in specific well-studied sites, with the focus on areas with severity scale more than 3 to establish family wellbeing centers and enable NGOs’ health and community care providers to provide evidence based community mental health services for more geographical coverage; Conduct raising awareness activities & campaigns on health and community level to reduce the stigma and discrimination which encourages people to ask for MHPSS services, and trend up help seeking behavior and Merge GBV curricula
