Project Summary

This intervention is aimed at improving access to and utilization of health services through five fixed primary health care facilities including Rirnyang PHCU in Ulang County, Kiechkuon PHCC, Makak PHCU, and Wandeng PHCU in Nasir and Jekow PHCU in Maiwut. To scale-up health care services in Nasir, Longechuk, Maiwut, and Ulang Counties. RI will also establish eight MMUs and ICCMs to serve the hard-to-reach population with lifesaving health care services. In total the project will target 99,094 vulnerable individuals.
RI will recruit and deploy qualified health workers including clinical officers, nurses, midwives, community health workers, and vaccinators to all supported fixed facilities and mobile medical units. Alongside this, Boma Health Workers will be selected within the targeted communities to implement ICCM activities.
The intervention will further focus on continuous screening and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through patient consultations. Additional services provided will include antenatal care, skilled deliveries, postnatal care, and clinical management of rape (SGBV services) in the health facilities and mobile medical units. The intervention will also integrate and strengthen mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and services for people with disabilities.
The project will continue streamlining and strengthening the referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), sexual and gender-based violence survivors and, maternal and child health emergencies. RI’s health team through this response will conduct routine and outreach immunization services to children under 59months of age.
This project will engage all communities in the targeted counties throughout the implementation stages to ensure the sustainability of the program. Beneficiary exit satisfaction interviews will be conducted at fixed facilities and MMUs and community feedback mechanism meetings will be conducted to facilitate feedback from the communities. This project builds on RI's existing static and mobile health interventions, are locations and static facilities not services by other actors - serving the most vulnerable, and hard to reach IDP, host and returnee community in Upper Nile State.

1 - Needs

The current scale and coverage of the humanitarian response across Upper Nile remains inadequate to meet humanitarian and health needs. Insecurity remains unpredictable; inter-communal fighting, including cattle raiding and revenge attacks, has caused spikes in tensions, displacement, and loss of lives. The protracted conflict and its aftermath in Upper Nile have reduced the availability of and access to health services, safe water, reduced food production, and disrupted livelihoods and markets, consequently exacerbating morbidity and mortality amongst the most vulnerable and remotely located communities, especially affecting women, children and people with disabilities. The population of Nasir, Maiwut, Longechuk, and Ulang Counties has limited access to essential health services due to insecurity, flooding, and the impact of Covid-19 measures. These counties have been classified as having emergency food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) which further impacted the health situation of the population. Despite improvements in health care services in Nasir, Malaria remained the highest morbidity (REACH Jan-Mar 2020 report). The report also highlighted that 43% of assessed settlements in Ulang County reported no health facility within walking distance. A Joint Rapid Needs Assessment in Nasir led by RI highlighted that the most common morbidities in Nasir were Malaria, ARI, and Diarrhea, and Skin infections. Children under 5years old (CU5) and PLW are particularly vulnerable to diseases. The majority of communities in Nasir, Maiwut, Longechuk, and Ulang counties are cut off by swamps have difficult access to health services. Lack of immunization, antenatal care, skilled deliveries, and postnatal care services to communities hard to reach. Covid-19 measures further impacted health services provision.There remain significant protection concerns in Nasir, Longechuk, Maiwut, and Ulang counties as a result of inter-communal conflicts and proximity to cantonment sites, with an extremely limited number of actors providing any form of protection services. Women, girls, and children extremely suffer the most as they are exposed to multiple protection risks including psychological distress, sexual gender-based violence(SGBV), and other abuses. Lack of CMR services due to limited access to health care services.To address the above needs, RI proposes to provide and improve access to and utilization essential services by the target population and hard to reach communities of, Nasir, Maiwut, Longechuk, and Ulang Counties to reduce excess morbidity and mortality. The intervention will scale-up the existing primary health care facilities (Kiechkuon PHCC and Makak PHCU) and three Mobile medical units (MMUs). The intervention will further increase the number of medical mobile units to reach the most vulnerable communities with no access to health services. Disease outbreaks will be detected early, responded to promptly and adequate surveillance systems will be in place through Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS). RI will provide health services adapting to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention and appropriate response activities are integrated while continue providing essential lifesaving health services. EVD prevention, surveillance, and response readiness to mitigate the impact of an Ebola outbreak will be continued as part of disease outbreak, prevention and response. Referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors, and maternal and child health emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and services for persons with disabilities will be integrated and strengthened. The project will continue providing curative health services to the population.

2 - Project activities

This intervention aim at improving access to and utilization of health services through five fixed primary health care facilities including Rirnyang PHCU in Ulang County, Kiechkuon PHCC, Makak PHCU, and Wandeng PHCU in Nasir and Jekow PHCU in Maiwut. To scale-up health care services in Nasir, Longechuk, Maiwut, and Ulang Counties, RI will establish eight MMUs and ICCM to serve the hard-to-reach population with lifesaving health care services. RI will recruit and deploy qualified health workers including clinical officers, nurses, midwives, community health workers, and vaccinators to all supported fixed facilities and mobile medical units. Boma health workers will be selected at the community level to implement ICCM activities across the four counties. RI will build the capacity of health workers to ensure the quality of health services provision. Topics to be trained include, integrated disease surveillance and case management, immunization in practice (IIP) and effective Vaccines monitoring (EVM), BEmoNC, Mental health Gap action program (mhGAP), SGBV/CMR, IMNCI, and use of malaria rapid diagnostic test (mRDT). At the community level, the Boma health workers will also be trained on case Management using Boma health initiative training materials and guidelines for effective ICCM management. The intervention will further focus on continuous screening and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through patient consultations. The intervention will also provide the management of injuries, antenatal care, skilled deliveries, postnatal care, and clinical management of rape (SGBV services) in the health facilities and mobile medical units. The intervention will also Integrate and strengthen mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and services for people with disabilities. The project will continue streamlining and strengthening the referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), sexual and gender-based violence survivors and, maternal and child health emergencies.RI’s health team, through this response, will conduct routine and outreach immunization services to children under 59months of age.Throughout the provision of health services, RI will adapt the current Covid-19 context and ensure Covid-19 prevention and appropriate response activities are integrated while continue providing essential lifesaving health services. All fixed health facilities, MMUs and ICCM will be equipped with IPC/WASH materials, PPEs, and IEC materials on Covid-19 to further strengthen the prevention. EVD prevention, surveillance, and response readiness to mitigate the impact of an Ebola outbreak will be continued as part of disease outbreak, prevention and response. The project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming to increase community mobilization and sensitization on health-related messaging including COVID-19 prevention, availability of immunization services, ANC, skilled deliveries, CMR services, and MHPSS. This project will engage all communities in the targeted counties throughout the implementation stages to enable the sustainability of the program. Patient Exit Interviews will be conducted at fixed facilities and MMUs. RI will also implement community feedback mechanisms such community meetings, feedback boxes and AAP groups to continue providing feedback and complaints. For the successful implementation of this project, RI will engage the communities during the selection of Mobile medical (MMUs) and community support for storage spaces for the essential drugs and MMU equipment. The selected MMUs and health facilities (PHCCs and PHCUs) will be equipped with essential medical equipment, drugs and test kits.All healthcare staff will be trained in the core protection principles, including 'Do No Harm'.”

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All RI staff and volunteers who are based in the four locations are trained on RI's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each RI field office has safeguarding champions who are given more intensive training on RI's safeguarding and PSEA approach. During project kick-off meetings with communities RI will inform communities of the PSEA policy, who the safeguarding champions are, what constitutes PSEA, emphasize that all project activities are delivered free of charge and that beneficiaries should not be asked to accept anything in exchange for any goods or services. RI will place posters and visuals outlining RI's policy and where community members can report at distribution sites. Additionally RI will have a help desk manned by RI staff at each distribution site, to collect complaints and feedback and to oversee distributions. Finally, RI's independent MEAL team and project management staff from Juba will conduct ad-hoc monitoring visits to project sites, meeting with community members and will reiterate the training and RI's position on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our integrated program (Health, Nutrition, WASH and protection) programs. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. Exit audit is conducted at the facilities and MMU to every beneficiaries and all these will capture feedback from individuals with disabilities and others. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1) Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2) Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3) Individual interview form, 4) Suggestion boxes installed at all supported facilities and communities. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. By using a variety of methods to receive feedback, RI will ensure that feedback from vulnerable and marginalized groups is captured. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites. IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the project leads through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RI will conduct sensitization and mobilization prior to start of project, through consultations with community leaders, both formal and informal. Chiefs, project management committees, and women/youth groups will be provided with full details of assistance including health and hygiene promotion activities. The project introductory meetings in all project areas will include explanation and orientation on how to make feedback and complaints and how the feedback and complaints will be analyzed, and when feedback on complaints and requests received will be given back to the community. Moreover; RI staff and local authority will be trained on how to orient, collect, record beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received, as well as how and when to respond. all staff will be trained on core data protection and confidentiality principals also. RI has already conducted an AAP study in each location proposed which asked a statistically significant sample size what the preferred means of both receiving information and giving feedback were. Overall, individuals felt more comfortable giving feedback verbally through RI staff or community leaders. Interestingly, females preferred female community leaders or direct to RI staff. Further, all participants stated they preferred to have information shared verbally from RI staff, and then community leaders. A significantly fewer cohort stated they preferred giving feedback to local authorities. The verbal nature of feedback and information was emphasized throughout and is unsurprising given the literacy levels of the wider population in all counties, and the limited/no access to electricity and mobile phone connection for more advanced feedback mechanisms e.g SMS, hotlines or email. RI will ensure to have the following community engagement throughout the project lifecycle:- Project kickoff and close meetings with Community Leaders, and womens leaders, local authorities, - Monthly FGDs with disaggregated cohorts of the target population- in-depth interviews with sample beneficiaries- patient exit interviews to measure satisfaction- case studies. - complaints hot-desk and AAP champions at trainings and mass health education campaigns

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RI will use the beneficiaries’ feedbacks to shape and adapt into project design and implementation. RI will undertake routine Patient Exit Satisfaction Interviews to get beneficiaries feedback on beneficiaries’ satisfaction on the services they received. RI will then address feedback received and will adapt its programme to address any concerns or gaps highlighted. During monthly consultation meetings, RI will then provide feedback to the beneficiaries on any changes made to the project based on feedback received. Additionally, RI's MEAL team will conduct quarterly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on RI's health project activities. RI's response to feedback will be shared back with community leader. Action plans will be developed with RI's project team and where appropriate actions will be followed up to improve the implementation of the project

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project will continue streamlining and strengthening the referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), sexual and gender-based violence survivors and, maternal and child health emergencies. RI has existing protection programs in 2/4 locations proposed under this intervention. These actions have established referral pathway mappings already in place - following extensive safety audits and service mapping. All project staff, regardless of sector, are trained in these referral pathways covering where possible all aspects of the referral pathway. in the other two locations, RI has already conducted service and referral mapping, as well as safety audits. all staff have bene trained on PFA and GBV basic concepts and are aware of the referral pathways available in each location. RI already has links with key protection actor in all location to refer any cases for structured case management and follow up. As RI is the main health actor in all locations, all SGBV referrals receive CMR from RI in each location. RI is able to conduct PSS and case management in 2/4 locations. In the remaining 2 - RI health staff are able to refer cases for this support. The team will provide screening, psychosocial support and referral of GBV cases to appropriate care facility. As this might require emergency intervention within 72hours for SGBV survivors, the MMU Midwife will be equipped with PEP kits and trained on its use before conducting the referral. The team will raise awareness to community about timely referral of SGBV case and where to seek care for the survivor. RI Protection's Team will train all healthcare staff on GBV basic concepts and GBV referral pathways, to ensure that treatment and referrals are conducted in safe, survivor centred approach.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

One of the key drivers of malnutrition in South Sudan is poor health status, and ,limited/lack of access to any form of primary or secondary healthcare for adults and children alike. By improving the health status of the population as a whole, through increasing access to curative and preventative health services - free of charge - RI is reducing morbidities and mortalities and enabling populations to continue working (in livelihood opportunities), and reduce likelihood of becoming malnourished as a result of disease and poor health status.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

RI’s proposed intervention focuses on the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, women and men. All activities are designed to address specific needs of different age and gender groups. To gain acceptance for activities targeting women/girls, and children, RI will involve men form the beginning through project kick-off meetings, community consultations on most appropriate time for activities and ongoing dialogue. Women and children have been disproportionately affected by concomitant protection risks, with IDPs and returnees at particular risk. It is well-known that women and girls are exposed to violence both within their homes and while undertaking day–to-day activities such as fetching water and collecting firewood, and those who are internally displaced or living close to cantonment sites are most at risk. RI systematically conducts a gender equality analysis at the planning stages of all projects in order to mitigate gender-specific risks and take into account key gender considerations for quality assurance and beneficiary accountability.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

RI has targeted interventions aimed at reducing infant morbidity and mortality in locations that are currently IPC 3 and 4, particularly amongst under 5s. This includes provision of support to vaccine preventable diseases (measles, polio, tetanus diphtheria), and health education and curative services particularly for diarrehea, Malaria and Pneumonia - the biggest killers of under 5s in South Sudan. These disease reduce appetite, ability to retain and digest food, and increase the risk that children will become malnourished. By addressing the health status of the population - both curative and preventative (immunizations, health education, etc.) targeting the most vulnerable individuals in IPC 3 and 4 locations, RI is reducing the risk of malnutrition in these locations, and aims to equip populations with the health knowledge to make positive health and hygiene choices that strengthen the health status of children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women.
