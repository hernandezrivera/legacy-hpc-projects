1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip very much remains in a state of protracted low intensity conflict in which Israeli military incursions and airstrikes are sporadic yet common place. People living in Gaza are exposed to perpetual uncertainty and political instability that could surface and become volatile with very short notice, or completely unexpectedly. Surrounded by the possibility of military escalation in the Gaza Strip, its residents and communities must be prepared for this eventuality and preemptively implement safety measures to mitigate the risks associated with armed conflict. By far the greatest conflict related risk threatening civilian life in Gaza is the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. Explosive weapons such as aerial bombs, missiles, projectiles, etc., cause three simultaneous effects: blast, heat, and fragmentation. All three of these effects can be projected over a wide area, killing and injuring people in proximity to the site of the explosion. In 2019 alone, Action On Armed Violence (AOAV) recorded a total of 29 confirmed attacks using explosive weapons in Gaza which resulted in 148 civilian deaths. Experiences from previous wars in Gaza clearly tell a story that local populations unable to seek refuge during use of explosive weapons have a better chance for survival through better preparedness. Safety and preparedness measures exist which can help civilians mitigate the risks associated with the three effects of an explosion and help to improve civilian survivability during and/or after an attack occurs. Knowledge of safety measures, risk mitigation techniques, proper evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, and basic First Aid can help civilians prevent loss of life either during or in the aftermath of an attack. Public awareness regarding Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) also plays a critical role in protecting civilians against ERW which can litter populated areas during a conflict and pose a threat long after an attack occurs. So far in 2020, for instance, there have been a number of accidents involving ERW in Gaza resulting in 6 casualties: two deaths (one boy, one man) and four injuries (two boys, two men). The ever-present possibility of renewed high-intensity conflict underscores the need to educate civilians about the threats posed by explosive weapons and ERW prior to such an event when our ability to reach and educate Gaza's residents is greatest. Despite the clear and present need to raise awareness about emergency preparedness and protection in Gaza, concerted efforts focused on improving individual and household risk mitigation measures have been limited. A field survey conducted in 2015 by NPA with 1,689 head-of-households (465 women, 1224 men) in vulnerable areas of Gaza showed that the vast majority of civilians (99.98%) in these areas had never received emergency preparedness and risk education training, many civilians had little to no first aid (84%) or fire safety (91%) experience, and the presence of ERW was still a perceived threat (93%). Civilians surveyed generally held many misconceptions about what constituted safe behaviour during a conflict and lacked basic information that could help improve the safety of themselves and their loved ones in the event of an attack. The implementation of Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) emergency preparedness and risk education training sessions in schools and communities in 2016-20 has proven effective at improving public awareness about conflict-related risks and informing people about the safety measures they can implement to improve their safety during an emergency. NPA’s pre-/post-training testing of both child and adult CPP beneficiaries has demonstrated that beneficiaries retain the information taught and are far more knowledgeable than the baseline (ie. untrained) regarding what actions and positions are safest to take during an attack, how to protect one’s head and body, how to breathe, how to safely evacuate a building, and what they should do if they encounter ERW. By emphasizing that there are indeed actions which civilians can take to better protect themselves during conflict, emergency preparedness training is helping to empower civilians, motivate them to seek adequate protection, and combat feelings of indifference or helplessness.

2 - Activities

As a leading global advocate against the use of explosive weapons in populated areas(EWIPA), Norwegian People’s Aid aims to remedy the existing gap regarding emergency preparedness risk education in Gaza and other conflict affected countries throughout the world. NPA’s CPP) initiative empowers civilians to mitigate risks associated with armed conflict and explosive weapons. This project will utilize NPA’s existing CPP framework, methodologies, and educational materials which have been effectively employed in Gaza since 2015 to expand the provision of emergency preparedness risk education training to adult heads of household and school aged children. To date, NPA has provided emergency preparedness risk education training to over 102,000 beneficiaries across the Gaza Strip in cooperation with the Ministry of Education and Higher Learning (MoEHL) in Gaza and its network of local Community-Based Organization (CBO) partners. Participants of NPA's CPP risk education trainings receive instruction on the following topics: home preparation and blast protection safety measures, conflict risk mitigation measures, safe evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) awareness, and improvised First Aid solutions. NPA’s CPP framework utilizes separate curriculums for adults and children, designed to convey safety messaging in a manner which is appropriate and effective for each age group. All training sessions incorporate high quality training aids, educational materials, and media to enhance the learning experience. This project aims to achieve its objective via the following activities; conducted in partnership with several CBO partners (TBD) and in coordination with the MoEHL, the UN Mine Action Service (UNMAS): Activity 1: Provision of CPP and ERW risk education training for children (girls and boys) in vulnerable conflict affected communities in the Gaza Strip; 1.1. Train 50 CPP trainers to deliver emergency preparedness and risk education training in communities and equip each with high quality training aids and informational print materials; 1.2. Conduct CPP emergency preparedness and risk education training for 20,000 children; 1.3. Raise public awareness of CPP principles, and safety measures, and EWIPA advocacy work through youth engagement and outreach; Activity 2: Provision of CPP and ERW risk education and support for adult heads of household (women and men) 2.1. Conduct CPP risk education training for 10,000 adult heads of household across Gaza’s five districts through community-based training sessions; 2.2. Provide emergency equipment and supplies (First Aid kits, basic COVID-19 mitigation materials, etc.) to households in Gaza’s most vulnerable conflict affected communities; Contingency Planning: While NPA’s preferred method of reaching beneficiaries has been through cooperation with the MoEHL and the implementation of CPP training in schools, the COVID-19 crisis may mean that this approach will be impractical given public health and safety concerns and the possible closure of schools. In the event that COVID-19 prevents NPA from implementing CPP trainings in schools for either part or the whole of 2021, NPA will partner with local CBOs across all five governorates of Gaza which have access to both children and adult heads of household in their respective communities and train/support them in delivering the required CPP risk education sessions in community centers or in the home, with proper COVID-19 mitigation measures in place, including the use of PPE by trainers and beneficiaries, social distancing, and the targeting of households/families in order to limit the exposure of beneficiaries to the trainer and those who they are already cohabitating with. During periods of lockdown, restricted movement, and prohibition of public gatherings as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, NPA will seek to deliver CPP training via remote means, utilizing online trainings, pre-recorded video training sessions, social media, radio, and television to reach beneficiaries unable to attend in-person training sessions. Target Locations: Specific project locations will be selected in coordination with NPA’s partners based on assessed need and coverage under past programming. All five governorates of the Gaza Strip will be targeted, with priority placed on high-risk communities in north, east, and south Gaza adjacent to the border with Israel. Gender/Age/Disability: While boys and men are most at risk of being a victim of an ERW accident, Explosive Weapons use in Populated Areas (EWIPA) is a conflict-related risk which affects all genders, with repercussions which vary across age/gender/disability groups. Since CPP training covers safety and risk mitigation measures against both threats, NPA tries to reach both female and male beneficiaries equally so as to not disadvantage any group and to encourage female beneficiaries to a leadership role in the protection of their respective households. As such, CPP training and outreach conducted under this project will be done in such a way as to ensure a proper balance of female and male beneficiaries. NPA will coordinate with its partners to ensure that an equal number of female and male beneficiaries are trained as CPP trainers to implement CPP training in their respective communities. If possible to implement CPP training activities in government-administered schools in 2021, CPP trainings will be targeted at children in grades 2-9, while trainings at secondary schools will target grades 10-11. Otherwise, NPA will work with its CBO partners to ensure a high female participation rate and a proper gender balance among both child and adult beneficiaries. All educational materials and media used in this project have been designed to be gender inclusive and to promote women’s empowerment. People with Disabilities (PwD) will be an important consideration as NPA prioritizes this group as particularly vulnerable to the effects of armed conflict.

In the event that the COVID-19 crisis and any resulting restrictions limits NPA’s ability to target schools, NPA will use local CBO partners and remote methods to deliver the required CPP trainings. NPA will report on the project's indicators to UNMAS and the Mine Action Working Group of the Protection Cluster on a regular basis. Reaching PwD with CPP training will be a key goal in 2021 and NPA hopes to target beneficiaries in such a way as to have PwD make up to 5% of the total project beneficiaries reached (500 girls, 500 boys, 250 women, 250 men) in 2021. NPA will also report project indicators through the HRP periodic reporting framework and update the Financial Tracking System as funding for project activities is secured.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Preventing harm to civilians is the core of NPA’s CPP programme and, as such,ensuring the safety and dignity of targeted communities is critical to our project plan.NPA is committed to the humanitarian principle of ‘do no harm’ and has designed thisproject’s activities to empower beneficiaries and mitigate threats against them withoutjeopardizing their wellbeing. Special considerations regarding age, gender, disability,and displacement, among others, will be considered at all stages of the project.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NPA’s CPP framework has been designed to meet the needs of those most vulnerableto the direct and indirect effects of armed conflict. NPA will prioritize targetingaccessible at-risk areas and communities most severely affected by past conflict, aswell as vulnerable populations such as women, children, people with disabilities (PwD)and caregivers, and refugees where possible. NPA will work with local partners with specialization in reaching specific disability groups and adapt its CPP its messaging andmethodologies to better meet the needs of PwD under our 2021 programme activities.
