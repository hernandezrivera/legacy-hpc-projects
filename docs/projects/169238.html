Project Summary

On August 4th 2020, a warehouse in the Port of Beirut, reportedly containing 2,750 tons of ammonium nitrate, exploded causing devastation and grave damages across the city. As per MoPH records, at least 190 people were killed, over 6,000 wounded, and over 300,00 people left homeless.
From August 5th 2020, AVSI has:
• Immediately responded with emergency shelter activities;
• Conducted a need assessment for 130 vulnerable households in the areas affected by the blast, considering the structural, physical damages and the socio-economic vulnerability.
• AVSI pre-identified households in need of immediate support.
• AVSI mobilized its private funds and the technical teams begun working immediately on repairs; in one week from 9 to 15 August, AVSI completed repair for 10 residential units. As a result, 30 people were already able to return home. Thanks to its fundraising campaign and granted fund from UNDP, AVSI IS CURRENTLY CONDUCTING minor repair for 115 residential units for a total of 375 individual supported.1 The total secured fund is 417,700$. MOREOVER, AVSI IS EXPECTING A FUND FROM ITALIAN DONORS EQUAL TO 110 000$ TO CONDUCT MINOR REPAIR FOR ADDITIONAL 50 RESIDENTIAL UNITS.
WITH MORE FUNDING, AVSI can still help more families to mitigate suffering through an integrated quick response in two priority areas: shelter, and restoration of essential needs.
WITH THIS FLASH APPEAL FOR A TOTAL OF 527,700$, AVSI FORESEE TO PROVIDE:
• MINOR REPAIR FOR 165 RESIDENTIAL UNITS includes fixing nonstructural damages, defined as first and second level damage according to the IA Shelter Strategy. THAT CAN BE AND NOT LIMITED TO: INSTALLATION OF GLASS, WINDOWS, DOORS, REESTABLISHMENT OF WINDOWS FRAMES, FIXING OF FALSE CEILING, ESSENTIAL PLUMBING AND ELECTRICAL WORK…. CONSIDERING THAT SHELTER SUPPORT WILL BE CUSTOMIZED AS PER THE NEEDS AND PRIORITIES OF EACH RESIDENTIAL UNIT. THE PROJECT MIGHT ALSO FORESEES THE PROVISION OF DOMESTIC EQUIPMENT DESTROYED BY THE BLAST. The aim is to secure houses and to make them habitable, and to ensure decent dwelling for vulnerable families;
• Protection mainstreaming in the assessments and response to promote holistic approach. The assessment and rehabilitation support provided to the affected beneficiaries will be used as an entry point for the identification of specific cases in order to ensure needed pathways for people needing case management and specialized services (psychological, medical, legal etc.): the aim is to mitigate the psychosocial distress that families are living after the chock, to promote fast recovery, and to foster the community and individual resilience.
AVSI is coordinating with Beirut Forward Emergency Room, with the Shelter, livelihood, protection and CP WGs to ensure synergies and avoid duplications. AVSI will particularly coordinate with the shelter WGs for the geo-split of response within the radius of the 138 Zones identified by the LRC. The priority will be given to zones at 1 to 3 Km from the blast and most vulnerable zones as per UN Habitat vulnerability map. The selection of beneficiaries will be based on: Sphere Protection Principles and Core Humanitarian Standards, the socio-economic vulnerability criteria coupled with the technical criteria in line with the interagency definitions of the emergency shelter.
AVSI will reach the beneficiaries through the daily presence of AVSI social and technical team on field and the established connection with community stakeholders (such as Municipality members, religious leaders, Moukhtar). Target group will be composed from: Lebanese and non-Lebanese, 60% female and 40% male, while 7% of the target group will be Person with Disabilities.
Direct beneficiaries are as follow:
• From minor repair: 165 residential units, with an average of 3 individuals per households, for a total of 495 males and females.
• Among these households’ members, those who are in need will be benefiting from protection services.

1 - Needs

After Beirut port Blast and as of August 13th, the secondary data from the Multi-sectorial need assessment lead by Lebanese Red cross revealed the high need for shelter repairs: out of 4194 HHs, 2066 are having damages in their shelter’ structures and more than 3484 are having minor damages, more than 50% of the population is having chronic diseases, and more than 40% are aged above 60 years old. Following the networking with local authorities, Since August 5th AVSI has been assessing residential and non-residential units in the following areas: Achrafieh (Geitawi and Rmeil); Mar Mikhael; Burj Hammoud; and Carantina. AVSI deployed a team composed of technical and logistic staff, social workers, education, protection, MHPPS staff; supported by a team tasked with management, coordination, monitoring. The Rapid assessment of the structural/ physical damages and of the social and vulnerability level of the population, aimed at providing immediate relief, shelter, support and referral. AVSI data collection system – based on KOBO and an ArcGIS Mapping of the assessed buildings are updated in real time with data entry; AVSI is immediately able to crosscheck the data. As of August 22nd, 117 heads of households were interviewed by AVSI to collect data on 247 family members (average of 2-3 members per family). Among the heads of households assessed: 95% are Lebanese and 5% are refugees (Syr and PRL); females constitute 53% of the head of HH interviewed. The vast majority (62%) of them are aged above 60. A quarter of the heads of household interviewed (25%) were injured as a result of the blast with medium to light injury severity. AVSI assessment conducted so far also evidenced: - Access to humanitarian servicesResults showed that access to humanitarian services is severely lacking with 78% of the visited households not supported to date by any other humanitarian actor. 81% of those supported received food, 12.5% received health and 6% financial assistance. - Health and protection- HH population assessed, composed by 70% of females- 7% are people with disabilities: of which 20% have physical impairment, 20% hearing impairment and 60% intellectual impairment- 24% suffer from chronic illness- 6% with life-threatening health conditions- 25% were injured by the explosion, among them 79% had mild injuries, 16% with moderate injuries and 5% were severely injured-5% are refuges exposed to exploitation, social exclusion, stigmatization, GBV and other risks-Majority of assessed households showed signs of high anxiety, distress and disrupted life routine especially those who lost their siblings and beloved ones. - LivelihoodsThe economic distress had already placed a toll on the Lebanese population, and the livelihoods of the household assessed were put further at risk as a result of the explosion: in total, 53% of the HHs are currently unemployed (and 94% of the Head of HH do not work) 27% are studying and only 20% are working and generating incomes. -Only 8% of the interviewees have a university degree, while 48% have only completed the primary education. -10 heads of households have registered damages to their business shops, which will further hinder their income. - Status of the Household InfrastructuresThe data analysis provided an overview on the status of units’ infrastructure. Based on the Interagency scale of the building damages assessment, AVSI has found that:- 76 % received minor damages such as broken windows, doors lock and false ceiling (level 1)- 22 % have damages compromising the safety and security of the households (level 2)- 1 % have major structural damages (level 3)- 5.7% of the screened houses do not have a livable environment

2 - Activities

Output 1: Access to adequate residential/ non-residential units for affected populations is increased1.1 Emergency shelter: Provision of minor repair for 165 residential units Following detailed BOQs prepared by AVSI civil engineers for the identified residential units, Small rehabilitations will be executed for first and second level damage (light and moderate) according to the IA Shelter Standard and include: the repair or reinstallation of doors, windows, locks, glass, electrical wiring, plumbing and painting. The main aim is to secure houses and to make them habitable. TO ENSURE THE SECURITY OF HOUSES BEFORE THE UPCOMING WINTER IS A TOP PRIORITY. AVSI then signs an engagement agreement with the head of HHs and on their behalf with the contractors, stating roles and responsibilities and the scope of rehabilitation and mutual commitment in line with the AVSI Standard and policies in humanitarian intervention and with the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS) In its immediate response, AVSI was able within a timeframe of two weeks to identify 117 eligible households, preparation of 45 BOQs and completion of 10 residential units. The average timeframe needed to finish the minor repair is within four days from the BOQ preparation date. AVSI immediate response relies on extensive shelter experience of its technical team, and the relationship with socio-economic fabric and economic actors in the areas. Therefore, AVSI is able to mobilize its network of local services, contractors and providers of raw material, and accomplish the repair work in a short timeframe. FROM THE CURRENT EXPERIENCE AND MARKET PRICES, The package of repair is worth an average of $1,500 USD. Thus, in addition to the provision of basic domestic equipment destroyed by the blast for the toilets and kitchens, as per each unit's needs. Output 2: Affected community assets and spaces reconstructed for enhanced safety and resilience2.1 Vulnerability and protection assessment, Identification and referral of affected households’ members to specialized services AVSI Needs assessment reveals that approximately 37% of the identified households' members are vulnerable and need protection assistance beside the shelter assistance (see health and protections paragraph in 1-Needs section above). Along with the intervention, all at risk individuals and families identified by the social and psychologist team will receive MHPSS (including PFA) and Protection support, and consequently are referred whether internally in AVSI for services offered by other projects or externally according to the available service in line with IA standards. AVSI will coordinate regularly with the IA and other partners in field. AVSI will use RIMS platform (established by DRC under ECHO fund) and the inter-agency form for external referral. In case of unavailability of pro-bono services, the action also foresees the support for adults or children in need for advanced specialized services, by the provision of financial subsidies for them to receive the service with the needed follow up.
