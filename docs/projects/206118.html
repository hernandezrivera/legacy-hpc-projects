Project Summary

This project seeks to provide critical live-saving and durable shelter solutions to returnees, internally displaced persons, refugees and vulnerable resident populations in Upper Nile (Baliet, Fashoda, Longchuk, Nasir, Maiwut, Malakal, Manyo, Melut, Ulang, Renk, Panyikang) and Jonglei (Pibor, Pochalla) reaching a target of 35,000 persons in need; internally displaced, returnees and vulnerable host communities. This action is based on the current displacement due to the ongoing fighting in Sudan and the dire protracted humanitarian situation of the IDPs and resident population in the target locations.
In conformity with the SNFI cluster strategy, HDC will capitalize on its static presence to reach population at-risk and promote integrated response by responding in target locations through both SNFI and mainstreaming of protection activities that promote centrality of protection. This will embed the strategic objective 2 of ensuring centrality of protection in all emergency responses and ensure dignity and protection of the most vulnerable groups among the internally displaced, host community and returnees
Essentially, the action adopts both in-kind and cash-based modalities (cash for NFIs and shelter construction) to meet basic Shelter/NFI needs of the returnees, IDPs and vulnerable resident population across the target areas.
HDC will rely on the core pipeline of SNFI Cluster to provide essential non-food items and shelter materials for the in-kind modality. Items provided through cash and voucher assistance will be procured through suppliers and vendors as CVA is dependent on functional markets.
The action contributes to all the three HRP strategic objectives;
(SO1) - Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs. This is possible through provision of life-saving essential NFIs and shelter materials for newly displaced persons and most vulnerable families
(SO 2) - Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have safe, tailored, timely and dignified access to appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action that promotes centrality of protection priorities. HDC integrates key protection principles and activities in SNFI responses to ensure centrality of protection
(SO3) - Vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions that respect their rights. Provision of cash assistance improves the lives of conflict and shocks affected persons as they employ ways to be more resilient through dignified means. It also improves on the economy by injecting cash into the market. The transitional shelters are more resilient to damage and improve on dignified living of the displaced persons.

1. Needs

The South Sudan HNO 2024 estimates about 2,06 million people require humanitarian assistance in 2024, constituting about 1.26 million South Sudanese returnees. Of these populations in need, the Shelter/NFI cluster estimated that 429,741 people require emergency and durable shelter solutions. The urgent need to access emergency shelters and NFIs will ensure that the population at risk has increased shelter for protection and resilience from recurring climate-induced risks through durable shelter solutions, and Community-Led Disaster Risk Reduction interventions. Of particular concern are counties in Upper Nile, Unity, and Jonglei states with nearly half the population remaining displaced. This situation is further compounded by the increasing influx of returnees and refugees, inter-communal clashes, and recurrent floods breeding increased protection risks, particularly for women, children, older persons, and persons with disabilities in camps, temporary settlements sites, and in the resident communities. In addition, across the greater Upper Nile region, there are limitedly available flood-resilient construction practices aggravated by the increasing depletion of local resources for transitional shelters, which have heightened the vulnerability of the population, particularly those living in camp-like settings, and informal settlements. The increasing returns and influx of refugees further add strain on the existing capacities and heighten the need for emergency shelters for newly displaced populations along with durable shelter/NFI solutions to facilitate safe and dignified returns and settlements. In line with S/NFI strategy, this project will provide in-kind and cash-based shelter/NFI assistance to 7,162 households at-risk- 42,974 individuals, particularly the new arriving returnees, IDPs and residents in Upper Nile (Malakal, Fashoda, Panyikang, Balliet, Manyo and Melut), and Jonglei (Duk, Twic East, Pibor, Pigi/Canal and Bor South)

2. Project activities

This project will provide emergency shelter kits, critical NFI materials, and transitional shelter assistance targeting areas of newly displaced population, areas of returns, and those in protracted displacement or crisis, with heightened protection risks and vulnerabilities. Implementation of this action will be closely coordinated with the S/NFI cluster, this includes technical, core pipeline support, S/NFI coordination meetings, and submission of 3 W reports. Where there are functional markets, HDC will deliver both in-kind NFI distribution with cash for shelter assistance. For the newly displaced population, HDC will provide emergency shelter assistance along with live-saving NFI kits in line with S/NFI strategy and approved standards. For those living in protracted crises and returnees, HDC will provide locally appropriate and sustainable shelter solutions such as semi-transitional and transitional shelter assistance. The response will be delivered through a coordinated structure, involving the returnees, the community leaders, local authorities, the state S/NFI cluster, WASH, CCCM, and Protection actors to ensure coherence and integrated service delivery. In all distribution and shelter assistance responses, HDC will establish Complaint and Response Mechanisms (CRM) to ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) throughout the project cycle. All interventions will be informed by contextual and S/NFI-specific assessments and needs analysis, and disaggregated data of the population targeted will be provided by age, vulnerability, and disability. HDC will provide transportation of items provided by the core pipeline to the project locations unless where required, transportation will be coordinated with S/NFI and logistics clusters. All S/NFI distribution will be accompanied by post-distribution monitoring and post-construction monitoring. To inform CVA programming, along with S/NFI specific needs assessment and analysis, HDC will conduct rapid market assessment and protection risk analysis to establish response mechanisms for Cash-based interventions or not. Cash-based S/NFI interventions will only be done in areas with functional markets and will target empowering the local communities and markets. Along with these core S/NFI activities, HDC will mainstream core protection activities such as Housing, Land and Property rights and legal act awareness raising, GBV risk mitigation, as well as prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, including awareness on the reporting protocols and service providers in the locations of intervention.
