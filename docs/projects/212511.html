Project Summary

Northwest Syria has seen 13 years of bloody conflict that has left the country in ruins and millions of people displaced. The 4.6-million-person population, most of whom are civilians, is burdened by:
Persistent hostilities: Although combat has decreased, intermittent skirmishes and transboundary shelling continue to affect security and stability. Economic collapse: Due to the war and other worldwide crises, inflation has soared to such an extent that many people can no longer buy basic needs like food. Massive displacement: Millions of people who have been uprooted from Syria and other countries live in unstable situations and frequently depend on humanitarian aid. Drought and destroyed infrastructure: While access to resources and markets is hampered by damaged infrastructure, agricultural production has been interrupted by war and climate change. This complex web of challenges has culminated in a dire food security situation, with 80% of the population in north-west Syria classified as food insecure. To paint a clearer picture, let's delve into quotes from the latest Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria 2024: Scale of the crisis: "4.1 million people in northwest Syria require immediate humanitarian assistance, including 2.7 million people who are severely food insecure." (HRP 2024) Deteriorating conditions: "Food prices have doubled in Syria in 2023, making essential food items increasingly unaffordable for millions of people." (HRP 2024) Reduction in aid: "WFP announced the suspension of its General Food Assistance programme for the whole of Syria in 2024 due to insufficient funding." (HRP 2024) Impact on families: "Many families are skipping meals or relying on cheaper, less nutritious foods, leading to increased malnutrition, particularly among children." (HRP 2024) The urgency of action: "Immediate and sustained humanitarian assistance is critical to prevent a large-scale famine in northwest Syria." (HRP 2024)
1,239,052 individuals are confirmed to be in dire need of food assistance, as reported by HNO 2024. The report also indicates that less than one percent of households in Atareb and ten percent of households in Dana can secure sufficient food. The distribution of food and supplementary would be a significant step towards alleviating the suffering of those in need. ATAA is looking forward to the resumption of food and supplementary distribution in the subdistricts of Dana and Atareb.
Idlib and Aleppo residents face a constant struggle against hunger. This project, driven by their needs, aims to deliver life-saving food year-round, reducing hunger and empowering resilient communities.
Key interventions will include:
- 12,000 food baskets every month in Harim, Jebel Samaan, and Jarablous districts for 10 months. "Jarablous, Jub AlKusa, ِAtareb, Sarmada, Aqrabat off-camps and Dana Camps"
- 2500 cash for food every month in Dana, Al-Bab, and Azaz sub-districts for 8 rounds the cost of CVA is 65$.
- Moreover, there will be 8,000 RTEs that will be distributed in emergencies.
ATAA will identify, verify, and profile target beneficiaries through local councils and camp management according to the vulnerability selection criteria:
o Head of household is a single female
o Head of household is a minor (17 years old and younger)
o High dependency ratio (more dependents than adults)
o High female dependency ratio (more dependents than adult females)
o Households taking care of one or more orphans
o Households with one or more persons with a severe disability, severe mental illness, or severe chronic illness that impacts basic functions and requires daily care
The total number of HHs will be 22,500 families, 112500 individuals.
ATAA will remain AAP through open dialogue, that accurate and timely information (including goals, outcomes, and any vulnerability criteria to participate) will share with them. We have now $1,28,350 as a secure fund and ATAA will make efforts to secure funds to carry out the project.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ATAA employs a scientific risk management approach by developing a risk matrix during project design that considers all potential hazards and mitigation measures. As part of the management of this risk, the S&S department depends on the strategy of achieving community acceptance during all phases of projects and building a trust relationship with the local authorities by clarifying for the local authorities the impact of the project on the region. Due to the unpredictable nature of conflicts, such as shelling or armed clashes, the S&S department closely monitors the project area, conducts daily risk assessments, and communicates potential scenarios and appropriate responses to the crisis cell. In addition, the department updates evacuation and contingency plans and provides field staff with training on expected scenarios and adherence to approved SOPs for a secure operational environment.ATAA is considering the PSEA in the top of priorities by active membership and participation in PSEA networks activities, and developed internal framework and control system to enhance PSEA including all the core standards, in addition to include the prevention measures in all the humanitarian interventions of ATAA.Additionally, ATAA created a solid response system starts from the active and accessible reporting channels, and investigation planning and procedures with qualified and trained staff and network of the focal points with gender balance to enhance access to survivors and provide the response interventions based on survivor based approach.Ataa is updating PSEA assessments with partners and received a UNICEF assessment result as full capacity low risk partner since mid of 2023.in addition, to be able to reach the following numbers:- 12,000 food baskets every month in Harim, Jebel Samaan, and Jarablous districts for 10 months.
