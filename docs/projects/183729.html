Project Summary

The overall objective of the project will contribute to Coordination Support Services objective 1: Humanitarian response is implemented through effective coordination mechanisms and supported by the timely allocation of humanitarian pooled funds and Coordination Support Services Objective 2: Provision of humanitarian assistance is enabled through the promotion of humanitarian principles and the rights of Palestinians living under occupation.
The project will ensure uninterrupted implementation of the core cluster coordination functions, including:
1. Supporting service delivery and coordination
2. Informing strategic decision-making of the HC/HCT for the humanitarian response
3. Planning and strategy development
4. Advocacy
5. Monitoring and reporting
6. Capacity building in contingency/preparedness planning
7. Accountability to Affected Population
In the context of the State of Palestine and especially with the recent COVID 19 pandemic, authorities lack national coordination mechanisms for addressing the enormous challenges facing the education system; each education system in East Jerusalem, Area C, and Gaza has different duty bearers and face different significant protection challenges. The Education Cluster will support the relevant duty bearers to ensure that children (age 8-12) 50% girls, youth (13-18), and teachers in the most vulnerable communities can safely access schools that meet minimum humanitarian standards and provide a protective environment in the context of protracted conflict. All interventions planned through the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) will be closely coordinated with local communities and meet the requirement of strong protection mainstreaming and community engagement, especially in the needs assessment and the implementation of activities.
The Cluster, in collaboration with the Humanitarian Gender Group, is also planning to strengthen the partners level of engagement on gender through joint Cluster needs assessments on gender-specific needs in the field, joint HRP mid review and related gender analysis, strengthening partners capacity on data collection and reporting of disaggregated data related to special needs of women, girls, boys or men. Partners will jointly identify the cluster priority interventions and responses based on the gender-disaggregated needs assessments, reports, and testimonies.

1 - Needs

An estimated 579,173 Palestinians (56% females) across the oPt, the vast majority (96%) of whom are school-age children including around 13,398 Children with Disabilities (CWDs), will need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022. Nearly 74 per cent live in the Gaza Strip and over 26 per cent in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Children from Area C, Hebron H2, and East Jerusalem continue to witness a non-permissive environment, incidents of settler violence, increased threats of demolition, and displacement that challenge their access to schools. Military harassment and settler violence against children on the way to school is the primary concern of parents in these areas. Girls face a higher risk of school dropouts due to parents’ fear for their safety during the school commute. Around 15,768 children and their teachers (9,160 males) are estimated to be in need of different protection services. In the Gaza Strip, the shortage of adequate school infrastructure is driven by a range of factors, including the longstanding Israeli closure, the internal Palestinian divide, and the recurrent escalations of hostilities. At least 331 education facilities in Gaza Strip, including over 136 schools, were damaged during the May escalation, affecting 121,092 children (59,008 girls; 62,084 boys). This has worsened the existing shortage of classrooms, where 65 % of schools are operating on double or triple shifts to mitigate the overcrowding of classes. The multiple school shift results in shortened school days of only 4 hours every day. The lack of adequate, safe, and accessible school infrastructure is also a major concern in vulnerable areas of the West Bank, especially in H2, Area C, and East Jerusalem, mainly due to restrictive planning regime set up by the Israeli authorities, which prevents the construction and upgrading of schools. There is a shortage of more than 3,000 classrooms in East Jerusalem for the public system. Many schools operate in private residential properties, which do not meet minimum standards. Nearly 60 per cent of schools lack playgrounds, libraries, or science labs, and at least half of the schools suffer from substandard physical conditions. The outbreak of COVID-19 significantly compounded the children’s vulnerabilities due to prolonged closures of schools and kindergartens and burdened an already strained educational system. As of the end of August 2020, 1.3 million children had lost at least three months of schooling. Many students from remote, disadvantaged areas of the West Bank and Gaza strip lack internet access, devices, and adequate digital literacy to effectively benefit from the online learning opportunities and manage learning loss. Only 44% of households with children (10-17 years old) who are currently enrolled in education have the required devices (desktop, laptop, or tablet) to enable their children to access online learning materials (PCBS 2019 household survey). Girls and children from poor families were particularly affected. Around 24,789 children (12,568 males and 12,221 females) need remedial education to enhance their literacy and numeracy skills and mitigate their risk of dropping out of school. Against the unique contextual backdrop of the situation in Palestine and the recent outbreak of COVID-19, there is a continued need for coordinated humanitarian response and advocacy. There is a high demand within the local humanitarian community, as well as in the broader international community, for support to develop a robust, detailed, evidence-based common situational analysis; for the coordination of advocacy and operational humanitarian responses; for the management and monitoring of country-based pooled funds; and for the development and monitoring of strategic needs analyses and response plans. With over 40 implementing partners participating in the 2021 HRP and financial requirements reaching over USD 20 million targeting 0.4 million people in 2021, there is a clear strategic need for continued coordination of humanitarian action in the State of Palestine to maximize achievements while reducing gaps and duplication in the provision of humanitarian assistance. Education Cluster's unique role in facilitating and coordinating these streams of humanitarian work continues, therefore, to be of critical importance, especially with the COVID-19 draining impacts and recent Gaza escalation on the education sector. Humanitarian access to education continues to be compromised due to protracted conflict and occupation. For half a million children (51% girls) in the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Gaza, access to quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment is impeded by the presence of checkpoints, the Barrier, frequent escalations in Gaza, military and armed group activities and incursions, settler related incidents, as well as the lack of infrastructure. The disruption of schooling affects education quality and brings about many psychosocial effects, such as excessive stress, fear, and distress. Through service delivery, advocacy, and capacity development, the cluster will seek to mitigate the effects of, ensure preparedness, and immediate response to emergencies such as COVID 19, a resurgence of conflict, forced displacement, and attacks on schools. The response will focus on advocacy efforts to address the increasing trend of education-related violations, where there is little or no accountability for perpetrators of violations. Authorities lack national coordination mechanisms for addressing the enormous challenges facing the education system. Each education system in East Jerusalem, Area C, and Gaza has different duty bearers and faces other significant protection challenges.

2 - Activities

The education cluster aims that girls, boys, and teachers in the most vulnerable communities can safely access schools that meet minimum humanitarian standards and provide a protective environment in the context of protracted conflict and COVID 19. All interventions planned through HRP will be closely coordinated with local communities, and meet the requirement of strong protection mainstreaming and community engagement especially during the needs assessment, project design, and implementation. Activities:1. Coordinate meetings, at country and operational levels, to ensure a common situational awareness and timely, appropriate and effective humanitarian response2. Facilitate and support coordinated gender-sensitive assessments and monitoring, data collection, and analysis of key vulnerabilities3. Lead national efforts to monitor and respond to education-related incidents and attacks4. Promote cross-cutting interventions in the humanitarian response including WASH, health, and child protection5. Engage with relevant education authorities in Education in Emergencies (EiE) Working Group and the higher-level Education Sector Working Group on the bridging of humanitarian and development actions6. Implement joint advocacy events among Cluster partners as required7. Support service delivery for acute vulnerabilities of populations in Gaza and West Bank according to humanitarian caseload through coordination of education partners including government authorities and development actors8. Support strategic decision making of the Humanitarian Coordinator and Humanitarian Country Team for the humanitarian response, while strengthening Sub-National Education Cluster Coordination in Gaza9. Contribute to planning and strategy development of the humanitarian response, including in particular the development of the HRP as well as developing Education Clusters Response Strategy. The Cluster will make sure that these documents and strategies take into consideration different needs of girls and boys including children with disabilities and responses are planned accordingly10. Strengthen the national capacity of relevant education authorities in contingency planning and emergency preparedness, as well as in integrating EIE in the education sector plan and budget11. Conduct joint monitoring and evaluation on the general education humanitarian response12. Monitor the tracking tool to follow on the progress of Accountability to Affected Population implementation throughout the HPC13. Conduct bilateral meetings and donors workshops to present the needs and the gaps in the EiE sector and advocate for increased funding for EiE sector in-country14. Update Education Cluster IM tools including the 4Ws on a quarterly basis15. Represent the education sector in the ICCG and actively engage in ICCG led initiatives16. Support the Humanitarian Pooled Fund through identification of education priorities, selection of partners, and monitoring of projects.17. Support Education Cluster partners in mainstreaming protection, gender, GBV, PSEA, and PWD in their projects through the development of protection action plan and gender, PWD mainstreaming checklist. In accordance with the MSNA results, the cluster will prioritize raising awareness among affected people on their rights concerning humanitarian assistance, aid workers' IASC code of conduct, and the CBCM mechanism. The cluster will advertise existing feedback and complaint channels, while will foster consultations with affected communities on their preferred communication channels (hotline number, email address, and WhatsApp application) in a culturally, gender, and age-sensitive manner. The cluster will strengthen its collaboration with the PSEA Network, also as means to rely on the Network’s common complaint channels and referral systems to cover for the gaps on partners with less capacities. Education Cluster interventions will ensure the mainstreaming of the five cross-cutting issues i.e. protection, gender, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), accountability to affected population (AAP), and disability, in line with the results of the MSNA that was conducted in 2021 as well as specific guidance/ results of assessments carried out by relevant working groups, including the Gender Task Force, the HCT PSEA Network, the Gaza Community Engagement Working group, and the RCCE. Equity monitoring will be continued through ensuring disaggregation of data along with gender, age, and disability, and other social determinants, as relevant. Partners will contribute to gender equality through gender-mainstreaming programming and addressing gender-specific vulnerabilities faced by men, women, girls, and boys. The partners will ensure the implementation of programmatic. The cluster will work closely with women associations to respond to the needs of women and girls in the education sector.The education cluster will ensure the response to the needs of children with disabilities through data collection, analysis, and project design and implementation. The cluster encourages partners to be disability-inclusive in all their project interventions, and has added a specific intervention are to respond to the needs of CWDs inside and out of schools.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The needs of individual beneficiaries will be met through response interventions that are not cash-based. Interventions are centered around improving coordination, response, and advocacy at the EiE sector level. Beneficiaries will be reached with direct services and therefore cash transfer is not relevant to the type of interventions proposed in the project. The cluster will continue to engage with the cash Working Group to find out how best to introduce cash programming in the EiE sector. This will be followed by partners' capacity-building and resource mobilization efforts.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The education cluster aims to provide a relevant and holistic education response by promoting and integrating all fundamental cross-cutting issues and principles. This will help ensure all interventions planned by partners are protective, inclusive and taking into consideration gender equity. The emergency education strategy in oPt is driven by protection-sensitive activities, which underpin the Sector Response. In addition to school-based psychosocial support, providing protective presence for students and teachers, legal aid and advocacy to help protect schools from attacks, the Education Cluster will work with the Child Protection AoR to link schools with child protection service providers to address protection needs in the most vulnerable schools

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Gender equality and gender-responsive programming is promoted through conducting gender analysis with due care to the different needs and protection concerns in health, protection and education of females and males. All data collected by the education cluster partners should be age- and gender-disaggregated. Projects will address gender discrimination in terms of access to education, as well as the diﬀerent risks that boys and girls face on their way to, from and inside, school. Partners will ensure equal participation of boys and girls in student clubs and ensure that school WASH facilities are gender segregated. Community engagement will be enhanced by the establishment of school-based safety committees, through existing community structures, in some targeted schools; to identify priorities, map existing resources within the community, prepare school-based contingency plans, and coordinate activities in the school. Parent-teacher associations will also be involved in key education advocacy, in addition to collecting feedback from community members to enhance accountability to aﬀected populations with due consideration of gender equity. Student groups will also ensure that students’ voices are integral to the design and implementation of emergency education activities. The Education Cluster will encourage partners to establish feedback and complaint mechanisms, where students and beneficiaries can raise their concerns and participate in projects’ design and share recommendations.
