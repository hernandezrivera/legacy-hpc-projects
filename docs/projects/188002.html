Project Summary

The project is design to respond to both winterization and shelter needs of most vulnerable groups in NWS. The project comprises of 2 pillars: 1- The activity will be self-implemented by WHH where it majorly targets Afrin as it is listed in the underserved districts with the most urgent needs and is aligned with strategic objectives 1 & 3. Previously conducted research has shown that IDPs &host community in NWS have increased expenditure during the winter months linked to additional needs. This is coupled with a decrease in the availability of income generating activities, which are usually more accessible in warmer months. The cash support for winterization to cover immediate seasonal needs of 1300 IDP families. The total value of cash will be $150 equivalent of TL based on Turkish Central Bank bid rate or agreed exchange rate per HH where they will receive the assistance one off distribution through a contracted cash agency in
November 2022, December 2022 and January and February 2023. As WHH has a well-structured system and well-designed Standard Operating Procedures in place for cash assistance delivery in Afrin, the activity will be a complementary to on-going project under grant “TUR-21/3559/SA1/FS-WASH-ER/INGO/18447“ in Habit Albaraka, Koblak and Tal Slur villages, Center 24-25, Hirsh Alzaidiah, Alpazar, Altal, Aldin camps. 2- WHH aims to respond to the most acute needs of the population in A’zaz and Idleb districts, focusing on the most vulnerable including newly arrived IDPs. The intervention will be implemented by WHH’s implementing partners Shafak and TAS where WHH will closely monitor their activities. For all activities, needs assessment will be conducted to identify the most vulnerable groups and their most urgent needs. Also, it will be ensured that key humanitarian standards and documents such as Sphere Standards, HLP Rights in Shelter Due Diligence Guidelines and Shelter and Repair Guidelines by SNFI Cluster will be taken into account. The activities will focus on upgrading basic and emergency shelters, complemented by road repairments and NFI assistance. 500 HHs residing in camp/s will be supported with tent insulation. 200 HHs living in unfinished houses will be assisted with house upgrading activity. In order to enhance the access to the camps for nearly 4000 HHs, 30.000m2 lenght of damaged roads due to heavy rain and floods will be graveled. Furhermore, unfinished houses and damaged houses will be repaired to accommodate 300 IDP famiies. In case HHs need to be moved to their newly repaired houses, transportation support will be provided, as well. Upon the completion of these activities, the HHs will be further supported by complementary winterization, food, and NFI assistance.

4 - Needs

The number of IDPs in need of shelter assistance is around 2.7 m (HNO 2021), shelter stands out to be the top 3rd priority (34%) among the surveyed HHs in A’zaz where only 2% stated they have received shelter assistance in the last month(WHH MSNA 2021).The recurring escalations of conflict in S.Idlib &W.Aleppo force many others to flee towards NWS, leading to increases in rental costs due to demand in the housing market.13% of shelters in NWS are reportedly damaged; HHs living in undamaged shelters report many different issues such as cold weather conditions, lack of heat, and space. 46% of HHs reported not being able to afford the necessary repairs in their shelter(HNAP,2021). Based on WHH HH Economic Assessment Report (Nov2021), HHs reported their top 3 highest expenses as food, shelter maintenance &rent. Therefore, providing shelter assistance in the area is of utmost importance. This project will be addressing the scarcity of shelter supply &the improvement of damaged shelters. It will ensure that the needs of diverse groups (such as women, PLW, PwD) are considered while repairing &building shelters through needs assessments. MSNA(11/2021)-WHH: 90% of HHs in surveyed locations in Afrin report either no income or an income below $50 which means that the living standard is way beyond the poverty line. This issue will be addressed by the winterization intervention.

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with SNFI cluster objective of providing lifesaving &lifesustaining shelter,WHH aims at responding to the most acute needs of the population in Azaz&Idleb through 2 implementing partners:TAS &Shafak monitored by WHH throughout the project.Initially, the targeted people will be identified with their urgent needs in shelter.The activities will be introduced to all stakeholders.In repairment of unfinished &damaged houses, HLP verification &technical assessment will be conducted for the houses. 200 HHs living in unfinished houses will be assisted with house upgrading activity.Unfinished &damaged houses will be repaired to accommodate 300 IDP HHs.500 HHs residing in camp/s will be supported with tent insulation.To enhance the access to the camps for nearly 4000 HHs, 30.000m2 length of damaged roads due to heavy rain &floods will be graveled. In case HHs need to be moved to their newly repaired houses, transportation support will be provided.Upon the completion of these activities, the HHs will be further supported by complementary food, and NFI assistance.Per winterization, WHH will focus on the most urgent unmet humanitarian needs like heating assistance.1300 HHs will be receiving 150 USD or equivalent value currency for the winterization support.Through cash vouchers, the vulnerable people living in Afrin can afford access to winterization needs and thus protect the targeted people from harsh winter conditions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WHH focuses on applying protection mainstreaming (both through the principled approach and in the technical areas of operations), primarily following the guidance of GPC. While implementing protection mainstreaming throughout project cycle, WHH is pursuing 4 elements:prioritizing safety &dignity, avoiding or at least mitigating effects of physical &psychological threats faced by affected population when accessing humanitarian services, arranging for people’s access to assistance &services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e. g. discrimination), setting up appropriate accessible mechanisms, through which affected populations can evaluate the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints in a participatory or involving way and finally supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights,.WHH adheres to CHS,, the Sphere Technical Standards, CoC of International RedCross &Red Crescent MovementIFRC and NGOs in Disaster Relief and to other international standards and commitments. The aim is to improve the quality and effectiveness of its humanitarian assistance and to support greater accountability to communities and people affected by crisis. WHH's mandate is to end hunger. Shelter activity will address cross-cutting issues such as gender, age, and disability. HHs headed by females, with PWD and PLW shall be able to benefit from the activity. WHH can also make direct or indirect referrals when necessary


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) are unacceptable breaches of fundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. The sexual exploitation and abuse of those who depend on WHH for assistance runs counter to all our personal and organisational values. It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassment of our fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has significantly stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, and taking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UN system, donors, and civil society organizations. Strategy covers both SEA and SH, bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. The Strategy also acknowledges the complexity and limitation to WHH’s authority in relation to SEA perpetrated by implementing partner personnel, including Government personnel. However, it does include measures to address and prevent these violations including supporting partners to fully integrate PSEA into programmes in both development and humanitarian contexts, creating a joined-up accountability culture that abhors, sanctions and prevents SEA and protects victims.
