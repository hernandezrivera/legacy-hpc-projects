Project Summary

UNHCR will work with the Government to give Rohingya refugees a legal identity through registration and documentation, which will serve as the foundation for protection, program delivery and solutions, while pursuing community-based protection responses and providing essential services. UNHCR will continue to facilitate access to effective justice mechanisms through its legal partners working in all 34 camps, including through legal counselling, representation in court, alternative dispute resolution mechanisms in non-criminal cases and targeted interventions for the release of refugees held for administrative violations.
UNHCR will remain a central player within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for the monsoon and cyclone seasons and to address remaining COVID-19 challenges. UNHCR will bolster inclusion with host communities to promote peaceful coexistence and social cohesion. Reinforcing accountable government leadership of the response will be an important component of inclusion, and UNHCR will continue to support capacity development across the different levels of engagement with government authorities from the camp level up to the RRRC’s office.
UNHCR will empower Rohingya refugees by building community capacities for leadership, self-management and engagement with the Government and the humanitarian community in 31 camps, including through the refugee representatives’ system in 4 camps. UNHCR will continue to involve refugees from all parts of the community in a range of community-based protection programs which builds their resilience while supporting the community and thus reducing dependency on aid. This will include refugee volunteer networks such as the Community Outreach Members (COMs), community groups, and religious engagement networks of imams and female religious teachers. Within the current framework, UNHCR will advocate for greater access to education and economic inclusion, so that young refugees retain hope and become a constructive force in the community. While promoting conditions needed for voluntary, safe, dignified and sustainable return in Myanmar, UNHCR will work to improve protection conditions and quality of life for refugees in Bangladesh pending solutions, building upon the framework for responsibility sharing provided by the Global Compact on Refugees.
UNHCR will ensure the centrality of protection through protection and gender mainstreaming, inclusive coordination, and leadership of the humanitarian response as the UN agency leading the protection sector within the ISCG. The Protection Sector will ensure strategic, policy and advocacy coordination of the response for effective delivery of quality protection services and results. Accountability to Affected Populations remains central to UNHCR’s work. UNHCR will support the expansion of the common feedback mechanism, and work with partners within the Protection Sector to further integrate an approach centred on age, gender and diversity across all areas of work to ensure that the specific needs of all individuals are taken into consideration in the response and in service provision. UNHCR mainstreams participation and engagement of refugees across programming and the operations management cycle, as well as through the protection focal points and their protection mainstreaming activities at camp level.
With a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, UNHCR deploys a variety of actions to ensure appropriate reporting, response and prevention activities are undertaken which target staff, partners, authorities, and refugees. This includes the application of SOPs on SEA complaint referral developed by the CXB PSEA network. There are multiple complaint mechanisms for refugees, such as face-to-face engagement, a hotline, awareness-raising posters, individual case follow-up and regular focus group discussions allowing for the safe reporting of cases of SEA. UNHCR ensures that PSEA is also addressed in all partnership agreements.

1 - Identified Needs

The COVID-19 situation exacerbated pre-existing protection risks, contributed to a deteriorated security situation, limited humanitarian presence in camps, stressed community protection structures and deepened inter/intra-communal tensions. 86% of households in the camps have severe or extreme unmet needs in the camps, with a portion of the population feeling less secure than they did 12 months ago. The lockdown hindered communities' access to life-saving protection information and caused a loss or disintegration of community structures, thus weakening community self-protection mechanisms. The 2021 J-MSNA preliminary findings indicated that restrictions on service provision posed a barrier towards accessing protection services, when needed. 65% of households unable to access protection services, reported unavailability of service/staff due to COVID-19. Women and girls, persons with disabilities, religious minority groups and gender diverse populations, continued to face heightened barriers in accessing services and information. More vulnerable households have less knowledge of and access to “recommended points-of-contact” for service provision, and thus rely on traditional, patriarchal systems. 64% of persons with disabilities faced barriers accessing services, mostly relating to the physical access of services. 76% of persons surveyed faced difficulty moving around camps. Female older PWD were found to be disproportionately affected. Meaningful participation of women and female youth volunteers remains a challenge, particularly given their limited access to phones, and the prevailing social norms that restrict females’ movement in public. Only 28% of surveyed households reporting that a female could go alone to a women friendly space according to GBV assessments conducted and 15% of women and girls not feeling safe in any space in the camp. Improving access to information, as well as meaningful participation and involvement in decision making through community representation and service groups requires strategic intervention to strengthen inclusive responses and shift refugees from being passive recipients of aid and turning to negative coping mechanisms, to drivers of change in their community. Married children were found at greater proportions in households with persons with disabilities and larger households, compared to less vulnerable households, thus highlighting the use of child marriage to mitigate vulnerabilities families face in unmet needs. The slowdown in registration activities during the lockdown created a backlog of cases. Continuous registration must remain an accessible process to ensure refugees have access to assistance and services. Major gaps remain in the civil registration and documentation of births in the camps. Refugees’ unrecognized legal status puts them at risk of unlawful detention and leaving them vulnerable to exploitation and abuse. Despite the presence of legal assistance services, refugees are reluctant to seek legal redress due to lengthy processes. Barriers to access justice have led to impunity and lack of trust towards service providers, law enforcement institutions and administration of justice. The protracted situation Rohingya refugees and decreased livelihood opportunities for both host and refugee communities has resulted in an increase in theft and violence, fueling inter/intra-communal tensions. The refugee population remains highly reliant on humanitarian assistance, and almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs.

2 - Activities or outputs

1.1: Continue border monitoring and advocacy with border officials/authorities to ensure access to territory and prevent refoulement, identify and refer new arrivals to the Transit Center for registration and documentation, provide information to new arrivals in the Transit center on their rights. 1.2: Lead protection monitoring/case management across the 34 camps through camp Protection Focal Points and Refugee Volunteer Protection Counselors. Support will be provided to PERU teams on emergency preparedness. 1.3: Provide legal assistance across the 34 camps through legal partners, supported by trained refugee paralegal volunteers. Legal services in the camps include counseling/advice, representation, mediation support and awareness sessions. 1.4: Co-chair the Anti-Trafficking Working Group, increase awareness on trafficking risks in camps and capacity to identify and safely refer VoT to adequate services, including legal assistance. 1.5: Maintain 7 registration sites covering 34 camps including the two registered camps. 1.6: Support national institutions to issue civil status documentation to refugees. 1.7: Co-lead the protection sector and ensure strategic and effective coordination of protection across the response. 2.1: Community outreach members (COMs) will work in 27 camps with communities to respond to protection and assistance needs of the community, including on protection and emergency responses. 2.2: COMs will work on protection issues aimed at behavioral change within the community through projects designed, implemented and monitored by them. 2.3: 144 Community groups, with inclusive and equitable participation will operate in 31 camps. 2.4: Support will be provided to elected refugee leaders, including female representatives, in 4 camps. With GoB approval, elections will be conducted within 4 other camps. 2.5: Engagement will continue with imams in 31 camps, and refugee female religious teachers in 29 camps to support protection, social cohesion and emergency preparedness related awareness raising. 2.6: Community centres and Information Service Centers will operate in 17 camps, with feedback and complaints systems in place. The Interactive Voice Response (IVR) system will be used for messaging on COVID – 19 and emergency preparedness. 2.7: Technical support will be provided across UNHCR sectors to ensure greater inclusion of persons with disabilities in service assistance delivery and programing. 2.8: Support will be provided to PSN through individual assistance, assistive devices, and strengthened disability inclusion in programs, including with COMS and community groups. 2.9: PSEA training will be conducted for staff/ partners, SEA assistance and tracking mechanisms will be strengthened, and PSEA focal points will be present in UNHCR/partners. •3.1: Protection capacity building of Police, Border Guard, Judiciary, and camp administration officials (CiC) to increase refugees’ access to justice and rights, incl. training on human trafficking. 3.2: Training/capacity building of protection actors at camp level, including partners' lawyers, protection volunteers/counsellors, and inclusive refugee community groups, on access to justice, protection mainstreaming, and human trafficking. 3.3: Support to the Armed Police Battalions (APBn) in the camps and the District Police to increase patrols, monitoring of the security situation in the camps, and access to justice.
