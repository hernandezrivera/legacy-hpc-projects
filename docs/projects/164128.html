Project Summary

The project will prioritize the most marginalized within the IDP camps and the communities who have exhausted their resources and coping mechanism, and at higher risk of losing or damaging their lives and dignity. Household vulnerability are interrelated, one aspect could lead further constrains, multiplying vulnerability of the marginalized groups.
Therefore, the proposed project aims at abbreviating household vulnerability through integrated protection interventions), addressing the particular needs of the most marginalized households within the IDP camps and communities that have recently experienced IDP influxes. The project places special attentions on outreaching the person with functional difficulties, that is now estimated to be 10-15 % of prevalence in the Syrian society. While their functional levels and capacities vary, the severely affected are considered not having adequate access to humanitarian assistance, and at blink of further deterioration of their conditions. The project ensures at least 1200 (20 % of the direct beneficiaries) are persons with functional difficulties and consequently, their families’ members also get benefits from the project, in total, over 6000 people will be reached.
As only few facilities are providing specialized service, the project will engage mobile outreach modality to provide specialized supports (physiotherapy, PSS, nursing, assistive devices) to outreach 700 persons living with functional difficulties in the camps and the communities.
In addition, the mobile outreach teams following survivors consent will conduct referrals to GBV specialized services if they come cross a GBV cases . Through in-house health referral mechanism to HIHFAD center that annexed to Maternity and Children Hospital as well as collaboration with other GBV actors, survivors of GBV will have access to service in a timely manner.
Case management supports 300 persons living with functional difficulties, including referral and vocational training opportunities for 25 beneficiaries.
Throughout the project, inclusion mainstreaming will be enhanced. The project will provide inclusion mainstreaming training to the humanitarian actors based in Syria, thus they can provide more accessible service across their on-going interventions.
In order to fight against social stigma, inclusion awareness sessions will be widely conducted in the communities and in camps, in addition to four community-based inclusion events will be organized with direct inputs from the beneficiaries. The project also establishes a peer support group among the caregivers of the children with functional difficulties, so they could exchange their experiences and create a mutual support network.
HIHFAD is committed to promote inclusive employment, six persons with functional difficulties will be employed, four within HIHFAD and two with other organizations with their salary substituted by the project.
This project also aims to enhance the psychosocial wellbeing and protection of 400 children and 400 care giver through conducting child resilience programme activities in the already existing child spaces (integrated with rehabilitation centers in Armanz and Albab that also provide Physical rehabilitation, PSS, Peer Support and Speech Therapy).

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 : Girls and boys of all ages still face violence and protection concerns in many life situations. Transition to adolescence signals a time when exposure to new forms of violence increases due to harmful coping mechanisms within the family Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, detained and recruited or to be involved in child labour, while adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage, cyber harassment and other forms of gender-based violence including sexual violence. Boys are also at risk of sexual violence, as are men, primarily in the context of detention .Women and girls are disproportionately affected by all forms of GBV in Syria. Widowed and divorced women and girls and those with disabilities continue to face stigmatisation, discrimination, sexual violence, and forced marriages. They face specific challenges in accessing assistance including but not limited to shame, and risks of sexual harassment and exploitation. People with disabilities are at increased risk of separation from their families, care providers, and the assistive products which support their independence. Relocation to a new environment can impede dignified access to basic services. Girls and boys with Girls and boys with disabilities are often at heightened risk of forms of violence.According to the needs assessment that was conducted by HIHFAD MDM-France that finished in Jan 2020: • Only 28% households with a member living with physical disabilityhave access to specialised services. •52% of households did not send their children to school in the 30 days prior the assessment. Child labour is highly prevalent in northern Aleppo governorate as reported by 78% of households. .• Intra-household violence and physical and/or sexual violence is high, as reported by 34% of households. • 85% of households do not have access to any psychosocial support and/or community space and 44% reported a lack of safe spaces for women and girls.
