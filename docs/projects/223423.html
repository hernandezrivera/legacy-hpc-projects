Project Summary

The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mozambique’s strategy focuses on real-time tracking and monitoring of population movements and stability, with a particular emphasis on Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees, and affected non-displaced families. This approach targets vulnerable and mobile populations across nine of Mozambique’s eleven provinces, where displacement is driven by both climate-related and conflict factors.
Key programmatic priorities include enhancing data tools to track the Stability Index and monitor progress toward durable solutions. This supports disaggregated data by age, sex, and disability, enabling more tailored assistance. The strategy aligns with cross-sectoral objectives by integrating Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN) considerations, including WASH, health, and protection responses, while building partnerships with other clusters. Embedded within all activities are protections for vulnerable groups, gender inclusion, and environmental safeguards. DTM Mozambique also deploys mobile teams to reach remote areas, ensuring inclusive, community-reflective data that supports multi-sectoral interventions and evidence-based planning.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mozambique’s strategy focuses on real-time tracking and monitoring of population movements and stability, with a particular emphasis on Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees, and affected non-displaced families. This approach targets vulnerable and mobile populations across nine of Mozambique’s eleven provinces, where displacement is driven by both climate-related and conflict factors. Key programmatic priorities include enhancing data tools to track the Stability Index and monitor progress toward durable solutions. This supports disaggregated data by age, sex, and disability, enabling more tailored assistance. The strategy aligns with cross-sectoral objectives by integrating Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN) considerations, including WASH, health, and protection responses, while building partnerships with other clusters. DTM Mozambique also deploys mobile teams to reach remote areas, ensuring inclusive, community-reflective data that supports multi-sectoral interventions and evidence-based planning.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

DTM Mozambique collects granular disaggregated data by sex and age and targets vulnerable populations across key demographic groups—specifically IDPs, returnees, and host communities—with a focus on women, children, and persons with disabilities, informed by the HRP and HNO 24-5. Geographic priorities include districts with high displacement rates and fragile infrastructure mainly Cabo Delgado, Nampula, Sofala and Zambezia. DTM’s data in Mozambique informs the Humanitarian Response Plan's goals, providing a comprehensive understanding of needs and ensuring that resources reach the most affected.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

All DTM Mozambique staff and partners will receive training on PSEA protocols, ensuring full awareness and integration into data collection and field operations. Data collection tools will be reviewed by PSEA experts to confirm alignment with PSEA standards and adherence to the Do No Harm principle.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

DTM’s monitoring strategy includes ongoing data collection and analysis on displacement trends, needs, protection risks, and the stability index. Regular community feedback mechanisms will evaluate the relevance and quality of the response, guiding sector-wide adjustments to enhance effectiveness and accountability. Information Management Working Groups, Inter-cluster coordination meetings, and Solutions Workstreams will serve as platforms to update sectors on National Displacement Profiles and track progress.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will identify people with disabilities by incorporating disability-sensitive data collection tools, engaging with local communities and organizations of persons with disabilities, and establishing inclusive feedback mechanisms, while removing barriers to access by ensuring physical accessibility, tailored program designs, and staff training on inclusive practices to create an equitable humanitarian response.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project’s impact on the environment is carefully considered, with potential risks such as waste generation from data collection materials and increased resource use in field operations being mitigated through planned measures, including adopting digital data collection tools to minimize paper waste, ensuring proper disposal of non-recyclable materials, and integrating environmental assessments into operational planning to identify and address specific environmental concerns.
