Project Summary

UNFPA commits to continue providing leadership to emergency GBV responses in the next humanitarian project cycle, in keeping with its mandate under the IASC. UNFPA therefore, working together with its implementing partners, and in coordination with the GBV AoR, will continue to provide and further expand quality life-saving GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services in Northern Mozambique in coordination with the respective provincial governments and government institutions in three provinces.
The proposed interventions aim to increase availability and accessibility of quality life-saving GBV prevention/response in conflict-affected and IDP-hosting locations where women and girls have limited access to survivor-centered GBV multisectoral services. Additionally, lack of privacy, crowded living conditions, inadequate lighting in compounds and shared spaces in host and IDP communities contribute to increased GBV risks for women and girls.
The project also aims to establish new and sustain existing Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFSs), offering comprehensive mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and GBV case management as well as GBV awareness sessions and socioeconomic empowerment activities.
It also aims to further increase access to integrated GBV/SRH services (with a focus on Clinical Management of Rape services) through mobile brigades and in Health Facilities.
UNFPA also commits to continue procuring Female Dignity Kits and post-rape kits to cover the needs of vulnerable women and survivors of sexual violence.
UNFPA also commits to supporting implementing partners and GBV AoR partners in reducing GBV including harmful social norms as well as sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) through improving humanitarian actor and community members understanding and access to victim assistance for SEA.
UNFPA as a lead of the GBV AoR will continue to provide strategic direction to the implementation of prevention, mitigation and response to GBV in Cabo Delgado as well as leading in GBV data collection and information management through the GBVIMS. This will be done through strengthening leadership by increasing human resources, strengthening the GBV Capacity of GBV AoR partners, and community resource persons.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

UNFPA commits to continue providing leadership to emergency gender-based violence (GBV) responses in the next 2023 humanitarian project cycle, in keeping with its mandate under the Interagency Standing Committee (IASC). UNFPA therefore, working together with its implementing partners, and in coordination with the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR), will continue to provide and further expand quality life-saving GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services in Northern Mozambique in coordination with the respective provincial governments and government institutions in three provinces. The proposed interventions aim to increase availability and accessibility of quality life-saving GBV prevention/response in conflict-affected and IDP-hosting locations where women and girls have limited access to survivor-centered GBV multisectoral services. Additionally, lack of privacy, crowded living conditions, inadequate lighting in compounds and shared spaces in host and internally displaced persons (IDP) communities contribute to increased GBV risks for women and girls. The project also aims to establish new and sustain existing Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFSs), offering comprehensive mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and GBV case management as well as GBV awareness sessions and socioeconomic empowerment activities. It also aims to further increase access to integrated GBV and sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR) services, with a focus on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services through mobile brigades and in Health Facilities. UNFPA also commits to continue procuring Female Dignity Kits to cover the needs of vulnerable women and survivors of sexual violence. UNFPA also commits to supporting implementing partners and GBV AoR partners in reducing GBV including harmful social norms as well as sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) through improving humanitarian actor and community members understanding and access to victim assistance for SEA. UNFPA as a lead of the GBV AoR will continue to provide strategic direction to the implementation of prevention, mitigation and response to GBV in Cabo Delgado as well as leading in GBV data collection and information management through the GBV Information Management System (IMS). This will be done through strengthening leadership by increasing human resources, strengthening the GBV Capacity of GBV AoR partners, and community resource persons.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project aims to increase the availability of and access to specialized GBV services across the northern provinces of Mozambique through static facilities and mobile teams. UNFPA will increase its support and services to female-headed households and adolescent girls, and widows and focus on empowering them through women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS) and integrated RH/GBV outreach services. Special attention will be made to increase the inclusiveness of GBV services to people living with disabilities. UNFPA will continue to lead the GBV coordination mechanisms and enhance the quality of the GBV response through building the capacity of member organizations, strengthening and updating referral pathways and SOPs. Provide lifesaving specialized quality GBV services including case management, mental health services and psychosocial support and establish and reinforce referral pathways throughout implementing areas. Where possible, legal advice will be provided. The capacity of service providers will be built to ensure quality GBV services are provided in a confidential, safe, respectful and non-discriminatory manner. Service providers will be trained on CMR, case management (CM), adolescent girl friendly programming and disabilities. UNFPA will engage in strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV. These include supporting the publication of the Voices from Mozambique qualitative assessments to inform interventions on empowerment and peacebuilding activities (adolescent girls’ role with United Nations Security Council Resolution [UNSCR] 2250 Youth, Peace and Security) , awareness raising, recreational and vocational activities and engaging men and boys in the prevention of GBV, through awareness activities on such topics as intimate partner violence, sexual harassment and early/forced marriage. Specifically,1. Individual GBV Case Management including support to existing Centros de Atendimentos Integrados (CAIs) or One Stop Centers2. Procurement and distribution of dignity kits3. Material assistance other than cash, voucher, or dignity kits4. Training case workers on safe and ethical management of GBV data5. Individual justice, legal aid, Clinical Management of Rape, MHPSS to GBV survivors including specialized care for conflict related sexual violence (CRSV) including GBV CM and MHPSS capacity building initiatives6. Construction, maintenance and repair of women and girls’ friendly spaces/community centers and psychosocial support services through WGFS7. Develop and roll-out economic empowerment initiatives to support resilience of women and adolescent girls8. Safety and risk mitigation activities through GBV Safety Audits, Referral pathway mapping and strengthening activities, GBV risk mitigation training and assessments9. GBV awareness raising initiatives (Small group sessions of a maximum of 20 persons). Public communication campaign sessions organized during mass awareness raising events such as International Women's Day (IWD), 16 days of activism against GBV and others (exceeding 20 persons)10. Provide awareness to humanitarian actors on victim assistance services and how to make referrals for SEA survivors

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Providing GBV services is critical to ensuring survivors are able to recover and heal, if these are not confidential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. As a result, UNFPA carries out thorough risks analysis of its interventions and works with communities to reduce risks of GBV. UNFPA strives to ensure that all services are confidential by building the capacity of its partners on the guiding principles of working on GBV so that survivors of GBV will feel comfortable accessing them but also to make sure that there are no negative repercussions in accessing them. Communities are always engaged when WGFSs are established to ensure their acceptance. UNFPA is leading efforts to mitigate the risks of GBV. As such UNFPA, as GBV AoR lead, will organize and facilitate trainings for a variety of humanitarian actors to ensure that actions to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV are included in their responses. UNFPA has established strong M&E systems. In addition to UNFPA’s regular M&E standard reporting practices, UNFPA conducts quarterly assessments at health facilities and WGFSs, key information interviews with service providers, and FGDs at WGFSs.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

UNFPA is committed to providing beneficiaries with quality services in line with their needs. To this end, activities proposed within this project are based on the interagency needs assessments, regular coordination meetings with actors providing GBV services through the GBV AoR coordination meetings, and regular communication with women and girls through the UNFPA IPs feedback mechanisms. Over the period of implementation of HRP 2022, UNFPA Implementing Partners have collected and provided useful feedback from communities and end-user needs and references that have informed the design of this project. In the 2023 project cycle, UNFPA, through its IPs, will seek beneficiary inputs through feedback boxes located strategically, for example, in the WGFSs, to validate the project ideas and monitor impact and needs for adjustments as well as through hotline numbers and client exit interviews. UNFPA will also continue to work directly with women and women leaders in displaced and host communities. In 2022, feedback mechanisms will include:A. Help Line number: Linha Verde: communities will be encouraged to share their needs, concerns, and complaints on the services provided.B. Using client exit interview (simple questionnaire) after receiving services at health centers or mobile brigade services where PSS services have been integrated.C. Focused group discussions with the different target population groupsD. Post Distribution Monitoring exercises

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNFPA Mozambique and its implementing partners will be guided by the UNFPA Social and Environmental Standards (SES) as outlined in its Environmental Efficiency Strategy (EES) 2021-2025. The thematic areas that most apply to the Mozambique Context are Biodiversity, Ecosystems and Sustainable Natural Resource Management, where UNFPA and its partners utilize natural resources responsibly to construct Women and Girl Friendly Spaces (WGFS) and recycle capulanas, old school uniforms for the socioeconomic activities within the WGFSs. Climate Change Mitigation and Adaptation and Disaster Risk Reduction: where UNFPA works with the Government of Mozambique’s Instituto Nacional de Gestão e Redução do Risco de Desastres (INGD) to ensure its community committees include women in trainings as well in the development of mitigation and disaster risk reduction interventions. Pollution Prevention and Resource Efficiency: UNFPA and its implementing partners employ methods to reduce carbon emissions such as planting trees/shrubbery or small farming plots within WGFSs as well as reducing the use of take away food boxes, to protect land and water resources from pollution.
