Project Summary

After cessation of violence and termination of the wars and crises in many regions across Iraq, the next step and challenge is supporting the resilience and stabilization process to assist a smooth return process for the people. With establishing this process, efforts should be spent to maintain the stable situation in the already existing camp locations and provide the assistance the vulnerable groups of populations in, and outside, the camps while supporting referral services.
Duhok governorate was among the governorates received the largest numbers of displaced people during the earlier stages of the crises and across the following time periods. In the current time, the governorate is hosting more than 300,000 IDPs settled in more than 21 camps. The health needs of these IDPs and their big burden on the infrastructure of the governorate, in addition to the limited capacity of the DOH due to the financial crises that is already exhausting the health facilities, necessitate continuous efforts to provide sustainable interventions to avoid any gap formation in the health service provision within the camps.
Heevie is proposing to provide equitable, safe and free access to full package primary health care for the vulnerable population within 9 (Nine) IDPs camps in Duhok and Ninewa governorates. The camp locations are Shariya, Mam Rash, Kabarto1, Kabarto2, Bersivy1, Bersivy2 and Qadiya IDPs camps in Duhok governorate and Jadaa3 and Hasan Sham U2 camps in Ninewa governorate. The project activities are planned to cover the period of 12 months, starting from 1st of January – 31st December 2019.
Heevie is aiming to provide 24/7 primary health care services within the static clinics of the mentioned camps, this involves provision of the below mentioned services:
1. Supervision of communicable and non-communicable diseases
2. Immunization services for under 5 year old children
3. Growth monitoring and nutritional services
4. Reproductive health care services (ANC, PNC and family planning)
5. Health awareness and health education services
6. Health information system strengthened and EWARN system
7. Referral services for secondary and tertiary care centers
8. Laboratory services
9. Radiological (Ultrasound) services
Heevie is already running the camp clinics of Shraiya, Mam Rash, Jadaa3 and Hasan Sham U2 camps and this project will be an opportunity to continue the service provision and avoid any gap or shut down due to lack of fund.
The clinics of the remaining 5 camps, Bersive1, Bersive2, Kabarto1, Kabarto2 and Qadiya camps in Duhok governorate will shut down by January 2019 since the current active partners in the camps have declared that they cannot continue beyond this period. Heevie, and after discussions with the DOH and the local health cluster, have started the preparations to run these camps PHCCs to provide the health care services for the IDPs especially women, girls, elderly, people with disabilities and adolescents from both genders.
Heevie Organization, as a national NGO, has contributed effectively in responding to the needs of the vulnerable population since the early beginning of the crisis especially when Duhok governorate received sudden waves of displaced people.
Heevie is aiming to have a clear exit strategy for the project in coordination with the DOHs in the corresponding governorates. Through the proposed project, Heevie will sustain the service provision in the selected locations to make the process of handing over of the targeted facilities smooth leaving no gap behind.

Needs

According to the national health cluster, more than 5.5 million people are in need for the services throughout Iraq, among them 0.8 million are in KRI. From this number, 2.4 million are planned to be targeted by the projects and interventions of the health cluster for the year 2019. From the 750,000 targeted IDPs, 350,000 are in KRI.Despite the stable situation in many crises affected regions across Iraq, the return process is still showing minimum numbers, according to an assessment done by UNHCR on the populations of 21 IDPs camps in Duhok governorate; 86% of them are not planning to return back to their original places in the near future. Many factors are contributing to this situation, from which the destruction of the infrastructure and the instable security situation are on the top.According to the assessments done by Heevie teams, many factors are affecting the health service provision in the crises affected regions; these factors can provide important indicators for determining the needs and the gaps in the health services in these regions. Poorly supported health facilities, displacement of health care providers, non-availability of essential medicines and equipment and the restriction of movement in many areas are still affecting the health service provision in regions across Ninewa governorate.Most of the camp habitants in Duhok governorate are from Yezidi ethnic minority from Sinjar district. The military operation in Sinjar and the surrounding regions in addition to the poor/no accessibility is the main factors for which people are not encouraged to return back. On the other hand, the people in Hasan Sham U2 and Jadaa3 IDPs camp, which are targeted by the current Heevie proposal, are mostly from the west bank of Mosul city which nearly 80% destructed according to many national and international evaluations.The apparent needs in the health sector are reflected in many aspects of the current situation in the war affected region across Iraq. Low immunization coverage, decreased access to growth monitoring and nutrition care, high infant and maternal mortality, minimum family planning achieved, low coverage for regular antenatal and postnatal care, increasing risk of pregnancy related complications, the increasingly vulnerability to gender based violence, poor care and support for the people with disabilities and outbreaks of the epidemic prone diseases are all among the main gaps in the health system and programs.Since around 49% of the affected populations are from the female gender, the needs of the women and girls have to be paid the required within any response plans. Care for the pregnant and lactating mothers, services for the women in the child bearing age, management of GBV cases, special programs for the girls involving the awareness services are of the main needs the female gender.The needs of the youth people from both genders have to be covered by any planed health program. This involve the special vaccination programs, metal health services, participation in the community based programs, awareness about the risks related to their age groups such the STDs, providing the job opportunities.. etcThe medical profile of the Iraqi population is showing increasing numbers of the chronic diseases especially diabetes, hypertension, stroke and malignancies. Guarantying provision of the treatment and good control and management of the complications is mandatory. Keeping the stable situation and maintaining the health service provision in the already existing camps is are of the main challenges facing the humanitarian community, efforts should be put in action to assure the continuity of service provision to avoid any gap that may arise due to lack of the financial support in a time that the local health authorities, especially DOHs in Duhok and Erbil, have declared clearly that they are currently unable to support the health in the IDPs camps.According to the national health cluster, “the primary focus of the health service delivery will be the IDP camps for as long as they are occupied”, under its prosed project, Heevie is aiming to perform the health service provision in 9 IDP camps (Jadaa3, Hasan Sham U2 and Mam Rash in Ninewa governorate and Shariya, Kabarto1, Kabarto2, Bersivy1, Bersivy2 and Qadia IDPs camp in Duhok Governorate) targeting the vulnerable populations from women, girls, elderly people and people with disability. The population of these camps exceeds 96,000 IDPs, Heevie is currently providing the full package of primary health care services in inside the clinics of Shariya, Mam Rash, Jadaa3 and Hasan Sham U2 camps while the other 5 are expected to have no partner providing the services by January 2019.Based on this, Heevie, and in coordination with the DOH in the respective governorates and the health sub cluster, have taken the initiative to provide the health care services in these camps’ clinics.

Protection Risk Analysis

The targeted health facilities are away from any actual or potential threats, all the clinics are designated to provide safe access to the health services, security issues for the beneficiaries and the staff are taken in consideration. Measures are put and actions will be taken to protect the clinic beneficiaries and the staff against any abuse or violence specially the GBV.The beneficiaries and especially those from the vulnerable groups like women, girls, children, elderly people and people with disabilities from both genders will have safe, continuous and equitable access to the health care services for 24/7 in all locations. Measures will be put to avoid any harm or abuse for the clinic visitors.

Mitigation Measures

The clinics are designated to provide safe access to the health services, security issues for the clinic beneficiaries is taken in consideration. The locations of the targeted health facilities are away from any threats or harms. The project is designated to ensure that confidentiality and privacy is respected in the consultations, counseling or personal information sharing. Each clinic is guarded by a guard 24 hourly. Pharmacy and warehouse are designed and guarded continuously. All the protection mechanisms are done in direct coordination with the local formal authorities.Heevie has already prepared the security and transportation documents for all the project administrative staff to have a safe and smooth access to the project locations especially in Ninewa governorate. in this regard, signed documents from Duhok gov, JCC Erbil and JCMC in Baghdad are available to facilitate the staff transportation. These measures will be applied on the new staff also to ensure safe transportation and access to the filed.Heevie will continue coordination with the camp management to ensure the safety of the beneficiaries.The waste products of the clinics are managed through the waste product management unit of DoH Duhok.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Heevie teams will perform regular monthly monitoring visits to the project locations and provide the project manager and the organization administration with detailed reports about the multiple aspects of the service provision in each location. The quality of care and the needs of each department within the centers, sample feedback from the beneficiaries on the services provided, the behavior and way the clinic’ staff deal with the visitors will be among the topics covered by these monitoring visits.Regular assessments will be done in response to any requirement or issue from any of the project locations in relation to the needs of the beneficiaries and the gaps existing. These assessments will be provided as reports to the project management and the organization administration to have the required steps. A phone number will be specified and announced for all the project beneficiaries to receive their complaints, respond to their needs and have their feedback on the service provision. The clinic will also be provided with a special fixed box to receive the complaints of the beneficiaries regarding the services provided.The feedback from the IDPs call center will be taken in consideration and followed up regularly to respond to all the complaints from the project beneficiaries.The patient registration form used in the project includes, in addition to the patient’s personal information, the phone number of the patient; this provides an additional tool to receive the feedback from the beneficiaries on the different issues related to the project activities.All the above mentioned procedures will be documented in a regular basis involving the actions taken and the solutions provided.The proposed project is designated to provide the health services to the vulnerable populations. IDPs and in particular children under 5, pregnant woman, woman of child bearing age, girls, elderly and people with disabilities are in a situation of particular vulnerability. Women and adolescent girls make a particular at risk group.According to the estimations of the national health cluster, most of the vulnerable populations are female gender and children. Based on that, the health status of the women, providing care for pregnant and breast feeding women and family planning are issue taken in consideration in the proposed project. Care for pregnant ladies, lactating women and care of women in child bearing age in addition to family planning services provided by the obstetrician doctor in the clinic.The project is designated to allow easy delivery of these services to all the mothers and other women within the project locations taking in consideration recruiting female staff to deliver services to assure the maximum benefit.Lack of access to vaccination and poor access to nutritional services, put the vulnerable people, and specially children, in a great danger of high rates of vaccine preventable diseases such as measles, mumps meningitis and diarrheal diseases and exposed them to the danger outcomes of malnutrition and its complications. Immediate action should be taken to do catch up vaccination and to have strict surveillance for any vaccine-preventable diseases that arise. The nutrition surveillance and monitoring remain a permanent and danger gap in the health system in the conflict affected areas, it is expected to find increasing rates of SAM and MAM among the vulnerable population. This project is designated to allow safe and easy access to the nutrition assessment services and valuable breakdown of the types of malnutrition to allow better surveillance, treatment and intervention for all the conditions.
