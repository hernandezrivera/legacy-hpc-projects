Project Summary

Concern proposes a WASH project to benefit 918 of the most vulnerable individuals in the blast-affected areas of Beirut. The final target locations will be determined in coordination with the Shelter Sector, but Concern intends to focus on Medawar, Bachara, Remeil and Borj Hammoud as they – particularly Medawar – were some of the poorest and most vulnerable areas before the blast.
Within those areas, the project will focus on households and individuals who were most vulnerable before the blast and who have therefore felt the greatest emotional, physical and financial impact of the explosion. Poor women, adolescent girls, older people, and people with disabilities or chronic illness will be priority targets. All nationalities will be targeted but specific attention will be given to migrants, Syrian refugees and extremely poor Lebanese.
The objectives of the project will be aligned with the Priorities 1 and 2 of the WASH appeal that aim to (1) mitigate COVID-19 transmission, (2) increase access to hygienic materials, and (3) repair plumbing systems within building plots.
The activities will include distribution of hygiene kits (200 households) and baby kits (120 households); repair and rehabilitation of plumbing systems within building plots (70 households); and distribution of PPE materials and support to other IPC measures for frontline workers (30 people).
Concern plans to implement the WASH project as part of a multi-sectoral response that also includes shelter and protection activities. The project leverages Concern’s expertise and experience working in these sectors in Lebanon since 2013.

1 - Needs

On August 4th, a warehouse at the Beirut port exploded causing widespread casualties and destruction. The Government of Lebanon has declared a two-week state of emergency in Beirut. The death toll has reached 220, over 7,000 people have been injured, and more than 300,000 have been left displaced. The impact of the explosions has caused ‘severe damage’ within six kilometers of the epicenter; ‘moderate damage’ up to 10 kilometers; and ‘light damage’ up to 20 kilometers. An estimated 30,660 individuals live in buildings with severe damage; 51,300 individuals live in buildings with moderate damage and about 89,313 individuals live in buildings with light damage. Some people have had the means to relocate to other, safe neighborhoods or municipalities while the majority, especially those in poorer areas, have had no choice but to stay in damaged buildings, facing severe risk to their lives and wellbeing.Water services in the neighborhoods close to the blast have been affected including connections to water and wastewater systems (both public and private) and water tanks. Figures are still being collected to identify the number of affected buildings and apartments in need of WASH rehabilitation, but given the fragile state of WASH infrastructure and systems before the blast, much is likely to be needed. Between 50 and 75% of houesholds do not have safe water tanks (no lid), according to a rapid needs assessment conducted by ACTED.Structural damage has been most acute in the neighbourhoods of Quarantina, Ashrafiyeh, Central district, Gemmayzeh, Rmeil, Marfa’a and Mar Mikhail. Other neighborhoods such as Bourj Hammoud or Bachoura have suffered less structural damage but due to pre-existing vulnerability are likely to have a high level of need. These areas are overcrowded, home to large extended families, and host many migrant workers, refugees, and informal sector workers who have less capacity to cope with emergencies. Migrant workers are of particular concern because many had become unemployed and homeless due to the economic crisis, as many employers cut them loose with no place to stay and often with wages unpaid. Many have faced increased racism as the economic situation has deteriorated, and many have no means of returning to their home countries. Large numbers of migrant workers lived in the highly affected neighbourhoods of Quarantina and Bourj Hammoud.The preliminary results of a Lebanese Red Cross (LRC) survey in the blast-affected areas show almost four out of five respondents have no savings and 37% reported no income. Approximatively 81,000 women of reproductive age, including adolescent girls, are displaced as a result of the explosion (UNFPA). Those living in temporary relocation and/or shared shelters with unrelated families are likely facing heightened protection risks as well as reduced access to water, sanitation and hygiene services, making it difficult to maintain basic hygiene – particularly menstrual hygiene – with dignity.The blast came at a time when Lebanon was already at breaking point, struggling with a severe economic crisis, political upheaval and ongoing COVID-19 outbreaks. COVID-19 cases were already on the rise before the blast. A total of 608 new cases were reported on August 22nd, bringing the cumulative total to 11,580 cases and representing more than a three-fold increase since the day before the explosion (WHO). Due to a lack of infection prevention and control (IPC) items, such as personal protective equipment (PPE), many affected by the blast are reluctant to access the services they need. First responders are also lacking PPE, and there is a general need for increased IPC measures across the response.

2 - Activities

Concern will work closely with local organisations, associations and volunteers who have been responding to blast-related needs of the most vulnerable, especially migrants. These organizations will help the project efficiently identify people in need of WASH and shelter support.To respond to Priority 1, Concern will distribute hygiene kits and baby kits in the affected zones assessed and prioritized by a Shelter partner, preferably in Concern’s own Shelter target area. As per the SOPs from the Water Sector, the households eligible for this activity are those eligible for Shelter kits and who did not receive any hygiene kits from another partner. The kits will be mainly provided by UNICEF, but Concern can use its own stock to top up as needed, ensuring the same composition of items. Each distribution will be reported through the standard WASH beneficiary list and reported on Survey 123. To respond to the Priority 2, Concern will conduct repair of plumbing systems at both the building and household-level according to degree of damage defined by the Shelter Sector. The rehabilitation will only take place in structurally sound buildings according to the UN-Habitat assessment. Concern will ensure the legality of the building by compiling all necessary documentation and that written agreement from the owner to conduct the rehabilitation is secured before starting any work. If possible, Concern will negotiate a rent decrease or freeze for the tenant depending on the level of WASH investment. Repairs will include, among others: replacement of damaged communal tanks; fixing leaks; repairing water and wastewater connections linking to public or private systems; rehabilitation of floor surfaces and hot water system in the toilets; and tiling in both toilets and kitchen. Owner-led approaches will be prioritized, if feasible, to increase community participation, but use of local contractors will also be a modality. Concern will use a cash for rehabilitation modality with an amount based on the type of rehabilitation and in line with the Sector guidance.This project will complement the shelter and protection intervention of Concern that includes minor repair of residential and non-residential buildings, rehabilitation of residential buildings, common area upgrades; response to and prevention of sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) and trafficking of exploitation of children and vulnerable populations; distribution of dignity kits; provision of psychological first aid and psychosocial support sessions; and emergency cash assistance to address urgent blast needs, including rent. Concern is already implementing a shelter response in Rmeil and Bourj Hammoud and is poised to expand. Concern has also been implementing a similar WASH project in North Lebanon since 2013. Concern will lead a multi-sectoral intervention in one neighborhood that brings together shelter, WASH and protection activities to promote a more holistic and lasting recovery of the affected community. The community support project will be designed jointly by Concern and the selected community. Preference will be given to locations with a high number of migrants. Prioritization of beneficiaries will consider socio-economic vulnerability, presence of specific needs based on gender, age, disability as well as other vulnerability criteria as they arise. Migrants and Syrian refugees will be specifically targeted. Beneficiaries will be fully integrated into the project. Concern will apply a protection lens across its activities to follow a ‘Do No Harm’ approach and mainstream PSEA. Beneficiaries will be referred to needed services and be capacitated on Concern’s Complaint Response Mechanism. Following a training on IPC in emergencies from MSF, Concern staff will train staff and volunteers on the same. Concern will ensure all program participants and partner associations are following COVID-19 IPC measures and are equipped with appropriate PPE during their activities.
