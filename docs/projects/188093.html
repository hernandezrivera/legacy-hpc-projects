Project Summary

The program provides life-sustaining shelter and NFI support and contributes towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure. Targeting both IDPs (70%) and host communities (30%) involves activities; 3,000 standard NFI kits distribution (3,000 HHs) and winterization components such as distributing (500,000 L) heating fuel to 1,667 HHs, and (2,000) stoves to 2,000 HHs and (25,000) winter clothes kits for 25,000 children both gender. As well as providing restricted cash for house rent services (1,000 units) for those IDP vulnerable groups and prioritizing female or child-headed households and elderly people and considering people with disabilities (1,000 HHs; 5,000 individuals).
This program includes 2,500 house rehabilitation activities which will target both IDPs and host communities (2,500 HHs; 12,500 individuals), this activity includes rehabilitating partially damaged units through ceiling repair, walls buildings and holes ﬁxing, doors and windows, infrastructure, electricity network, and solar panels, plastering, plumping and providing the water tank. This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by the SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensuring that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel with ﬁeld coordination with the relevant local councils
The program will seek feedback from both male and female beneﬁciaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access and equal opportunities.

4 - Needs

Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in significant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. The severity of shelter needs has increased slightly across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2020 by four percent to 5.88 million people in 2021. Of this, 3.89 million (66 percent) are in areas of severity three or higher. Geographically, these shelter needs are concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb, Ar-Raqqa, Rural Damascus Governorates, and camps in the northeast and northwest. At least 1.95 million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements. The overall NFI PiN has increased by 37 percent from 2020 to 4.69 million PiN in 2021. Of this, 2.70 million people (57 percent) are residing in areas of severity three or higher with a concentration in 73 out of 270 sub-districts.379 According to the latest assessment, 90 percent of families report diminished access to NFI’s with unaffordability being the main constraint. This is exacerbated by the deterioration of purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their needs during harsh weather with fuel and power sources remaining in high demand.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) considerations are overriding components of all activities. This project will take several steps (training staff, use of the feedback mechanism, recruitment of both male and female staff, and clauses in contracts) to work towards mitigating any susceptibility to such risks. QRC is an active member of the PSEA Network in Gaziantep and has supported the establishment of the inter-agency reporting mechanism to ensure procedures and materials are in line with community understanding and preferences. QRC staff sign a code of conduct, including protocols to ensure that sensitive beneficiary information is kept confidential. We have a feedback mechanism (FM) that includes a mobile phone and WhatsApp hotline number for beneficiaries, other stakeholders, and affected population in general, to provide feedback (positive or negative). Information about the mechanism is distributed broadly through activities (i.e., posters in public areas and during outreach). One dedicated staff is responsible for monitoring the feedback number, logging all messages, and alerting the appropriate team member about relevant feedback in a way that is sensitive towards the complainant, and taking necessary action to respond. Where necessary, QRC will modify program implementation to address concerns raised by affected people. QRC FM receives all reports, including SEA. If a SEA case is received, the receiving staff will immediately refer it to the QRC PSEA
