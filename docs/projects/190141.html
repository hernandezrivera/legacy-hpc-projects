Project Summary

1. Conflict in the Northern part of Ethiopia (Tigray and Amhara regions):
The conflict in Tigray has disrupted the livelihoods and the coping capacity of IDP, returnees & host communities. The DTM round 8 shows that there are about 1,814,284 IDPs (386,741 HHs) and more than 23,000 returnees in the region. According to the multisectoral seasonal assessment report, the annual crop production of Tigray region was reduced by more than 40% . The farming households were not able to prepare their lands timely, and essential farm tools including their oxen were looted and/or killed during the conflict. Hence, the food security and livelihoods of the people have been severely affected. According to the recent assessment by WFP, more than 80% of the people in Tigray remain food insecure. The Meher assessment revealed that each zonal administration has severely affected districts. In Eastern Zone, crop production loss was very critical in Erob, Sewha Sasie, Hawzen, Gulomekeda, and Ganta Afeshum woredas. This is because of the prolonged conflict in the areas, low agricultural inputs supply, low precipitation, and crop pest and disease become the biggest problem.
The conflict in Tigray has recently spilled over to Amhara National Regional State. Upon the withdrawal by the Ethiopian National Defense Forces (ENDF) from the Tigray region on 28 June 2021, Tigray forces (Tf) moved to adjacent areas within the Amhara region, resulting in the displacement of thousands of people and impacting access into the region. In Amhara, there are nine zones that have been directly affected by the conflict. These are North Gondar, Central Gondar, South Gondar, Waghemira, North Wollo, South Wollo, Dessie City Administration, Oromo and North Showa. In these zones, 76 woredas and 1,502 kebeles were directly affected.
2. Drought:
The Horn of Africa is experiencing one of its most severe La Niña-induced droughts in recent history following three back-to-back poor rainy seasons in Ethiopia and other neighboring East African countries. The October-December 2020, March-May 2021, and October-December 2021 seasons were all marred by below-average rainfall, leaving large swathes in southern and south-eastern Ethiopia. The trend of drought in Ethiopia is a vicious circle. With the difference in geographical coverage and effects on the community, it occurs on a yearly base. In 2008 there was a drought that affected more than 5 million with the soaring food prices as mentioned by the UNICEF. Similar hazards of drought occurred in 2016 & 2018 as reported by the government and humanitarian actors affected a large number of communities by diminishing their livestock. And in the same token recently in 2022 in Afar, Southern and Eastern Oromia and the whole Somali regions are under tough drought situations in which many livestock are dying and communities are suffering from food security and water shortage crisis. Ethiopia is experiencing a prolonged drought after three consecutive failed rainy seasons since late 2020 affecting 6.8 million people living in the Oromia, SNNP, Southwest, and Somali regions. several areas in southern and southeastern Ethiopia- Somali (10 zones), Oromia (8 zones), Southwest (1 zone), and SNNP (7 zones). The most severely impacted regions are the lowland areas of southern and eastern Oromia and Somali regions. In drought-affected areas in Oromia and Somali around 225,000 malnourished children and over 100,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women need urgent nutrition support. In 2022, projected an estimated 850,000 children will be severely malnourished across the drought-affected regions. Severe food insecurity would be expected if the March to May rains were significantly below average or failed. Without a scale-up of humanitarian assistance, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) area classifications would likely happen. to reverse the adverse impact of the drought ZOA proposes agriculture-related interventions in both Oromia and Somali regions.

Drought-affected populations Response Agriculture activities: Oromia and Somali Region.Output 2. 1: Provision of drought-resistant small ruminants:Mostly when the cows are deceased during severe drought the pastoralist community uses small ruminants, particularly goats for milk and meat. Especially milk of goats is highly nutritious and its drought-resistant nature highly advantageous for the pastoralist community in producing milk and meat. Also, it produces offspring within three months and even 3 to 4 kinds produce in a year. Therefore, the provision of small ruminants for drought-affected people highly contributes to nutrition as well as the maintenance of the livelihoods of the pastoralist communities. Activities:Activity 2.1.1 procurement and distribution of 6000 heads of goats to 2000 drought-affected female-headed households (1000HHs Oromia and 1000HHs in the Somali region). 3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

ZOA Ethiopia has strong coordination and collaboration with all relevant stakeholders at the national and regional levels of its program and project locations. Starting from the beneficiary targeting to the distribution process, ZOA is working together with all relevant actors, such as local authorities, representatives of IDP groups and host communities, humanitarian representatives, leaders, women and youth groups, and other relevant government bodies. The coordination issues in responding to the conflict crisis in Tigray and Amhara, there are several coordination platforms that ZOA is attending. ZOA is a member of all relevant clusters and an active participant in the Agriculture cluster both at regional and zonal coordination meetings. In addition to the cluster meetings, ZOA also attends the inter-cluster coordination group (ICCG), a platform open for cluster leads and co-leaders.Moreover, ZOA also is one of the three leaders of the Tigray Region Humanitarian International Non-Governmental Organizations (HINGO) forum and Amhara interagency working groups. The regional Emergency Coordination Center (ECC) is another extensive coordination platform that ZOA is actively participating in. Those coordination meetings have been helpful for ZOA to share its achievements, take lessons from the context, and get updated needs and responses to the humanitarian situation. Besides, ZOA’s presence and network in the Tigray and Tigray region help together and share information with others on the ground. The Bureau of Agriculture and Rural Development (BoARD) in all will be involved as primary stakeholders for planning and determination of the type and a variety/breed of agricultural inputs to be distributed to end-users, and generally, coordinate the intervention with other humanitarian and development efforts being undertaken in the selected locations. An MoU will be signed with sector bureaus in which duties and responsibilities will be defined. The zonal and woreda administrations will also be fully engaged in the provision of administrative support throughout the project. ZOA will fully get involved in Agriculture cluster and will update implementation status, lessons learned, and experiences. These government bureaus are also supported by and coordinated with the UN agencies namely FAO, WFP, UNHCR, EHF/OCHA, and other potential donors and implementers. ZOA actively participates in coordination meetings, at a national, regional, and sub-clusters level in Oromia and Somali regions. ZOA has been involved actively in the DRM ATF cluster, CWG, and other relevant clusters, coordination meetings, and participated in or coordinated various assessments to identify existing humanitarian needs in both regions. ZOA will closely work with respective clusters at different levels, line government offices, and actively follow the humanitarian situation in the area to ensure quality implementation, minimize duplication and achieve concrete impacts. The existing emergency, recovery, and resilience programs implemented by other NGOs in Oromia and Somali regions were mapped and taken into consideration in the design of this project. the participation that ZOA has in the drought-related responses in the country is strong. ZOA has a good reputation and participation in all agriculture-related clusters and working groups in Somali, Oromia, and Gambella regions. On top of this ZOA has implemented emergency response in Gursum, Midega tola, and Faddis woreda of the East Harerghe zone. Again, ZOA has operated in the Somali region in supporting refuge, IDPs, returnees, and hosting community through emergency response and recovery programs in the southern part of the region. ZOA has a satellite office in Moyale town, which will coordinate Borena and Dawa zones project interventions areas.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

ZOA has strengthened compliant feedback mechanisms (CFRM) in all areas at all levels. ZOA is committed to Accountability to Affected populations. This is manifested in the Accountability Framework of the Organization which clearly lays out the Complaints and feedback reporting and handling procedures. The complaint and feedback response mechanism will be established in consultation with the target community considering their level of literacy and access to the various mechanisms, especially for women, girls, and disabled community members. Once the mechanism is agreed upon, Complaints and Feedback Response systems that are safe and confidential will be established. The MEAL officer with the support of the Programme Area Manager will take the lead in the establishment and operationalization process. Complaints will be addressed at the field level in a timely manner by relevant staff in keeping with the confidentiality commitment. Our Code of Conduct (CoC) will be shared with project beneficiaries during information sharing sessions to ensure target communities are aware of the expected behavior of ZOA staff who are also trained on the CoC. Complaints on the breach of the CoC and other complaints that demand the attention of Country office senior management will be escalated using our internal complaints reporting mechanisms guided by our Integrity Framework. The CoC clearly indicates that there is Zero-tolerance for Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Any SEA case will be reported directly to the Country and Headquarters Integrity Coordinators for immediate investigation. Hence, it is guaranteed that beneficiaries' complaints will be properly handled at all levels in adherence to the appropriate age, gender, safety, and confidentiality considerations.
