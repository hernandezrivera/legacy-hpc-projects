Project Summary

Narrative Hub, a women-led organization, will provide life-saving WASH activities aimed at meeting life-threatening, severe needs of the population of host communities, IDPs and returnees. This project will be implemented in Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil South and Nasir counties which was affected by conflict, floods, high malnutrition rates, disease outbreaks and GBV threats. NH will have a static presence in both locations with flexibility to address needs across the whole counties, as part of crisis modifier strategy. This project aims to support the provision of access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases and aims to reduce gender-based violence-related issues. Since women and girls are the primary, direct users of WASH services, the project will contribute to GBV mitigation among the targeted population. This project will support life-saving integrated WASH and GBV activities, targeting 10,000 individual beneficiaries suffering from inadequate access to safe drinking water, inadequate sanitation and poor hygiene conditions.
The interventions will include but not limited to:
• Provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management), that will be supported at the institutional level and IDP sites. At the HH level the latrine construction with local materials will be encouraged with GBV prevention aspect taken into consideration for latrine design;
• Safe water provision through borehole construction and rehabilitation, ensuring access to water for the affected communities, where safe access to water points mitigates risks for girls, women and children;
• Hygiene promotion (handwashing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain, safe solid waste disposal and jerry-can cleaning campaigns);
• Capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs);
• Distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR or others) for the most vulnerable people in the community;
The activities will include a detailed multi-sector needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies of the affected population. The main priority is to provide emergency WASH necessary to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by WASH-related diseases, contributing to malnutrition and limited access to water. The project design aims to integrate with other projects in other sectors for a holistic response. This will include skills training and resilience building. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected population by ensuring that services are delivered in a gender-sensitive and responsive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, including people living with disabilities, etc. With the involvement of our M&E department, the indicators will track cooperation with communities through an existing beneficiary feedback mechanism, to address GBV and protection threats in WASH.

1. Needs

Nasir has been the center of two armed fights between government and Nuer Youths this year. Armed fighting between government and Nuer youth white army in Nasir first broke out in February, deteriorating the situation for women and girls. The conflict displaced about 29,500, some 15,000 of them women and girls, and more than half into Pandanyang (9,000), Torpuot (5,000), and Koat (8,000) alone. A 20th February 2024 tentative agreement reached at a meeting between the political leadership the community resolved that the government keep the army out of town for its own safety and for that of the population, allowing only soldiers’ wives out of the barracks. The resolution was regardless of the outcome of the community demand that the government relocates the barracks out of Nasir. But fighting again erupted in August 2024, affecting 40,000 households (Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessment to the Conflict Displaced and Flood Affected Population in Greater Nasir County, 2024). Women and girls lack hygiene kits. Reports of violence against women and girls are on the rise. Since the breakout of several conflicts in Nasir that resulted to displacement, women and girls’ vulnerability increased, according to the Rapid assessment which was conducted on 2nd may 2024, the findings shows high number of displacements, that has resulted to shortage of food, domestic violence at household level due to too much consumption of alcohol, drug abuse, lack of health, hygiene and sanitation facilities. The recent returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia due to war and recent flooding in Nasir has increased issues of mental and psychological challenges among women and girls. According to Humanitarian Situation Overview in Aweil East and Northern Bahr el Ghazal conducted by REACH South Sudan in October 2024, since April 2023, South Sudan has received more than 800,000 people displaced by fighting in Sudan. Although the large majority - roughly 80% - have crossed the border through a single town in Upper Nile State, intensified fighting in the neighboring Kordofan region of Sudan in 2024 has resulted in the emergence of another major entry area further west, in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State. The Majokyinthiu border crossing in Aweil East county is now the second most trafficked point of entry from Sudan nationwide, and Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State one of the most common settlement areas for new arrivals. Aid organizations have recorded more than 50,000 people crossing the border into Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State (Aweil North, Aweil South, and Aweil East), while tens of thousands more have arrived from elsewhere in the country. The growing use of “informal” entry points has complicated efforts to track people crossing the border, as established routes are increasingly deemed too unsafe for travel. Findings suggest that gaps in monitoring along the Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State border have left many South Sudanese “returnees” - who comprise the large majority of arrivals - without humanitarian assistance since their initial displacement. Food, shelter, and healthcare were commonly identified as priority unmet needs. Large-scale arrivals from Sudan, steep price increases, and atypically severe flooding are exacerbating an already critical humanitarian situation. Available data indicate that, in parts of Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State, there is widespread waterborne diseases including the recent cholera outbreak, rates of acute malnutrition have increased significantly over the past 12 months, while severe acute food insecurity is widespread. This population, particularly is in urgent need of WASH services. Most of the families came without any household items. Many are sharing spaces without any privacy to women and girls and there is open defecation too. A number of protection concerns were noted in the settlement areas.

2. Project activities

This project will focus on the following cluster objectives: CO1: - Contribute to reducing WASH- related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition; CO2: - Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties; CO3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation; and CO4: - Community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructure.Narrative Hub will provide the following services: • Provide or rehabilitate sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management). This will be supported at the institutional level and IDP sites. At the HH level the latrine construction with local materials will be encouraged with GBV prevention aspect taken into consideration for latrine design; • Provide safe water through borehole construction and rehabilitation, ensuring access to water for the affected communities, where safe access to water points mitigates risks for girls, women and children; • Conduct hygiene promotion (handwashing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain, safe solid waste disposal and jerry-can cleaning campaigns); • Provide capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs); • Conduct distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR or others) for the most vulnerable people in the community; • Provide training to hygiene promoters; • Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations; • Create awareness on appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases; • Provide gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers; • Provide safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis; • Provide safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention interventions; • Provide safe drinking water in nutritional centres (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM; • Provide WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC down to the household level; • Ensuring small stock water requirement and promote appropriate hygiene practices to prevent small stock open defecation at household level WASH in Health for outbreak preparedness, response and risk reduction; • Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community; • Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level. • Provide community engagement (AAP) sessions, complains and feedback mechanisms aimed at enhancing accountability; and •Participate in coordination and assessments at county, state and national level.
