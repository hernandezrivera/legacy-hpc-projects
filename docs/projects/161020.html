Project Summary

RAO will distribute lifesaving S/NFIs to 10000 vulnerable IDPs, host communities and returnees (6000 women, 3000 men, 500 boys and 500 girls) in Akobo, Nyirol, and Uror Counties in Jonglei State. For each county, targeted beneficiaries are as follows; Akobo: 4600, Nyirol: 2400, and Uror: 3000 County. The modality for distribution will be in-kind and will be conducted through locally hired and trained enumerators with the support of local authorities and community members. Prior to distribution of the items, affected communities will be assessed to verify the level of S/NFIs. Persons Living in Disability, Elderly persons, individuals with no community links, caregivers of orphaned children, child headed HHs, women headed HHs, and severely sick persons will be engaged in order to obtain specific S/NFIs needs facing them. A complaint mechanism will be put in place from the onset to ensure beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit complaints and receive feedback on time. Information on CFM will be provided during the entire process of implementation. Due to displacement, IDPs and returnees returning home are finding their land or property occupied by those left behind. In some instances, the IDPs and the Returnees may require to be apportioned community land where they can settle. This if not handled well may trigger tension or conflict. RAO will work closely with Chiefs, other partners implementing shelter interventions in target locations, Local landlords and authorities to address land and property issues that may arise during the intervention. To ensure adequate attention on HLP matters, RAO will utilize IOM Housing, Land and Property due diligence standard as a guide during the implementation process mitigate the risk that the construction of shelter contributes to increasing tensions and conflicts around land. RAO in partnership with chiefs, landlords, and shelter actors will establish a process that can anticipate and respond to land and property issues as they emerge. HLP messaging will be conducted among staffs and local community on connections between land rights and shelter. The intervention is integrated and will seek to link activities and beneficiaries to nutrition, health, education, protection, FSL and WASH. Protection partners will be engaged during GBV awareness and referrals, rapid assessments, safety audits, registration and verification of beneficiaries and distribution to ensure the exercises are conducted in the most conflict sensitive manner. Nutrition partners will be engaged when conducting trainings and distribution of NFIs such as mosquito nets to pregnant and lactating mothers. Joint messaging on Cholera, Malaria, pneumonia, diarrhoea prevention and proper usage of mosquito nets will be conducted together with health partners. FSL partners will be engaged in joint information sharing with fishermen and farmers groups to reduce incidences of mosquito nets being used for fencing gardens and fishing. To avoid duplication of activities, Project staffs will participate in S/NFIs meetings at the county, state and National level for information sharing. For durable solutions, community based S/NFIs committees will be trained and supported on effective, sustainable and equitable utilization of local resources to ensure community based shelter solutions. Women and girls will receive training on alternative ways of cooking (energy saving stoves) to ensure reduced consumption of firewood, which is associated with GBV risks especially when women go to the forest to look for firewood. RAO does not have an MOU with core pipeline. Applications will be made through State Focal Point Person (SFP) to ensure the MOU process is expedited before the project commences. In case the MOU is not obtained by the time the project commences, we will obtain S/NFIs items through partners having active MOUs with core pipeline. The intervention, information sharing and coordination will be conducted through S/NFIs SFP.

1 - Needs

The S/NFIs Cluster estimate that 2.4 Million persons will face S/NFIs 2020.Out of this number, 24% will be women, 17% persons living with disability and 54% children (HNO Draft,2020). Key drivers of S/NFIs needs include latest flooding whose impact will extend to next year, general and localized conflict, disruption of supply routes and markets, macroeconomic instability (hyperinflation), food insecurity that is leading families to spend the little they have on food than buying shelter or non-food items and time poverty. Some of the places that are likely to experience serious S/NFIs needs in 2020 include Akobo, Uror, and Nyirol Counties in Jonglei State. Other factors include inflow of displaced persons from IDPs and Refugee camps to their home of origin. The IDPs and Returnees are coming home to find shelters they left behind destroyed, looted or burnt as a result of conflict. Others find their original home occupied and have no means of generating income to establish new shelters. As a result, they are forced to sleep under trees, occupy abandoned structures and institutions, or share living and sleeping spaces with host community or relatives. In such circumstances, the affected individuals live in inhumane conditions, are exposed to elements of weather and protection risks. Women and girls sharing sleeping spaces with male counterparts or sleeping outside lack privacy and at risk of exposure to sexual gender based violence. IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in these Counties are currently exposed to severe flooding while those residing in Uror, Akobo and Nyirol being most affected (OCHA, South Sudan Seasonal Flooding Snapshot, October 2019). Rising water level continues to destroy shelters, sweep away household assets (cooking, serving and sleeping items) forcing families to abandon their houses to seek higher grounds. Also, as a result of the flooding, malaria has increased due to elevated levels of mosquito breeding, lack of mosquito nets, misuse of mosquito nets (for fishing) as households which have depleted their food stock seek alternative sources of food and livelihood. OCHA projects that the impact of the floods will last into next year necessitating provision of S/NFIs items to affected populations. Beyond flooding, according to the IPC Analysis Report, Uror, Nyirol and Akobo will remain in crisis (IPC3) or worse food insecure between September and December 2019. On the other hand, Duk County will remain in emergency (IPC4) during the same period. Come next year, the same IPC report classifies Uror as crisis (IPC3) food insecure or worse while Nyirol, Akobo and Counties are flagged as emergency (IPC4) food insecure or worse between the months of January to April 2020. Individuals or families facing crisis (IPC3) and emergency (IPC4) are most likely to utilize the little resources or money they have to buy food instead of shelter materials or other household items such as mosquito nets, cooking sets or clothes. Others are likely to sell the little items they have or exchange them for food. As a result of fighting, markets, supply routes, and livelihoods got disrupted. Vendors vacated insecure areas while transporters removed their vehicles from the road for fear of being attacked. Job opportunities shrunk as private, public and non-government organizations stopped or scaled down their operations. The cumulative impact such disruption has resulted in reduced production, collapse of markets, unemployed population that rely on aid and reducing purchasing power among populations. This would mean that essential shelter and non-food items are not found in rural markets, are too expensive or affected communities who rely on aid have no means of buying the items.

2 - Activities

ACTIVITY 1.1.1: Community sensitization and establishment of Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM): Project staffs will conduct preliminary meetings with community leaders and members and humanitarian actors in target counties to provide information on project’s aim, objectives, duration, modality of intervention, selection criteria, expected results and seek community support of project activities. During the initial contact with the community, a CFM will be developed and information regarding avenues of submitting complaints and receiving feedback well-articulated. ACTIVITY 1.1.2: Conduct Safety Audits: RAO staffs in close coordination with protection partners in target counties will conduct safety audits to obtain information on risks that are likely to affect project implementation. Safety Audit Findings will be fed into project work plan and will inform advocacy steps to be taken to ensure project staffs, S/NFIs supplies and beneficiaries are protected from risks. ACTIVITY 1.1.3: Joint rapid S/NFIs needs assessment: Assessments will be conducted in targeted counties to obtain information on level of S/NFIs needs and number of affected persons. The assessment will be conducted through FGDs, Observation, KIIs, transect walk and household visits. Humanitarian actors in target locations, community members and leaders will be engaged during the rapid assessment. Assessment reports will be submitted to S/NFIs cluster through SFPs and S/NFIs coordinator for approval. ACTIVITY 1 .4: Training of enumerators and staffs: RAO will train staffs and local enumerators on GBV Mitigation, HLP, AAP, CFM, PDM and on how to conduct selection of beneficiaries and distribution exercises. Training topics to include GBV Mitigation, crowd control, Data compilation and analysis, rights of beneficiaries, PDMs procedures and requirements, CFM Procedures and requirements. ACTIVITY 1.1.5: Selection of beneficiaries: Project staffs in coordination with community members and leaders will conduct registration, verification and selection of beneficiaries in target four counties. Selection exercise will be preceded with public information sharing on selection dates and venues, numbers targeted in each location, profile of beneficiaries to be selected and avenues to submit complaints for redress. ACTIVITY 1.1.6: Distribution of S/NFIs items to beneficiaries: Project staffs will distribute S/NFIs through enumerators and in close coordination with community leaders and member. Distribution will be preceded with setting of distribution venues (ensuring availability of water, disaggregated wash facilities, shade and space for elderly persons, women, pregnant and lactating mothers to rest), information sharing on distribution dates, locations and venues. Distribution will be conducted during light hours to allow for sufficient time for beneficiaries to arrive at distribution points and return home early. ACTIVITY 1.1.7: Reconcile S/NFIs Data: Project staffs, enumerators and chiefs will reconcile S/NFIs stock. A Joint report will be developed on items remaining, list of beneficiaries who missed on distribution and ways and dates intended beneficiaries should collect their items. ACTIVITY 1.1.8: Post distribution monitoring: Joint PDMs will be conducted with the participation of S/NFIs actors in target locations, community members and leaders to obtain information on appropriateness of S/NFIs distributed, quality of intervention and lesson learnt. PDM will be conducted using S/NFIs Cluster PDM template through FGDs, KIIs, Observation, Transect walk and household visits. ACTIVITY 1.1.9: HLP Messaging: RAO in coordination with S/NFIs partners, protection partners, chiefs, landlords, local authorities and community leaders will conduct HLP messaging and awareness to mitigate tension and conflict associated with issues of Housing, Land and Property.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The Intervention assumes participatory and rights approach in implementation. Women, Men, Boys, Girls, Persons living with disability, individuals with no community links and elderly persons will equally participate in project activities as staffs, enumerators, beneficiaries and local leaders without being discriminated on the basis of faith, age, gender and status. Community members will be provided with sufficient information on their rights to access S/NFIs assistance, submit complaints and receive feedback. Also, the intervention has mainstreamed protection of women and girls from any form of Gender Based Violence. Information and training on GBV mitigation, reporting and referrals will be availed to staffs, beneficiaries and community leaders to ensure the rights of women and girls are observed.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

RAO put in place a clear mechanism to identify the people it aims to assist and their representatives, referring to gender, age, diversity, and special needs. Apart from the ordinary consultations with the community leaders and local authorities, RAO has gone a step further to engage children and youth out of recognition that they have agency and input they can make to inform in the program design. This has been especially useful in design of the Youth resilience program (Brick making). As regards to assessments, communities have adequately and equally participated in separate and joint sessions to provide information on immediate needs they face and best way and modality to meet the needs. RAO has also taken active steps to be aware of and take into due consideration local culture, customs, beliefs, capacity and coping mechanisms and strategies as well as contextual factors that may enhance or inhibit free and open Complaint and Feedback on the part of separate groups. Engagement will outlast needs assessment and project design. Community members and leaders will actively participate in project implementation contributing time, talent, ideas and suggestion in project monitoring and evaluation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The Intervention is linked to community networks such as local community leaders, leadership structures of Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and Host Community, Chiefs, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission, Humanitarian actors in target locations and cluster networks. Activities will be implemented and coordinated with these structures to avoid overlap, duplication of efforts and to attain synergy.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The intervention is focused on meeting immediate Shelter and NFIs needs of affected communities. However, local capacity will be enhanced through establishment of S/NFIs Committees and Local Level, Training of Trainer of Trainers on durable shelter and NFIs solutions and training of women and girls on fuel efficient stoves. Equipping local community with skills and knowledge on durable S/NFIs solutions will empower the community to creatively begin to sustainably utilize local resources to meet their own immediate and long term shelter and non food items needs. Fuel Efficient Stoves is meant to reduce on amount of firewood used at household level. By extension, women and girls who are tasked with collecting firewood in the forest where they often get abused sexually will have reduced exposure to G.B.V risks. Also, F.E.S made using clay can be sold to earn income. As such, the project is aligned to social protection of affected communities.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project is based on speciﬁc risk information obtained through key informant interviews, updates from Protection Cluster, S/NFIs cluster operation updates, and access information obtained during Inter Agency Assessments in target locations. Referring to this sources, RAO has taken measures to identify who is at risk, how and why at the very outset of the project design, taking into account the speciﬁc vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons without community links. RAO has put in place measures to strengthen the collection, management and analysis of information to inform and adjust early warning and response. Also, it is anticipated that target locations will be accessible by road and air in order to deliver S/NFIs supplies on time to affected persons.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Project sustainability is ensured through training of youth, men and women on sustainable shelter and non-food items durable solutions. Whereas women and girls will receive training on how to make FES for sale and for home use, men and youth will be trained on making of bricks, household items such as chairs, tables, and beds using wood, ropes and animal hide. Durable solutions training will be accompanied by Environment Education to create awareness on need to preserve the environment and utilize natural resources in a sustainable manner. Protection of environment and enhancing the capacity of affected persons has multiplier effect and benefits that will outlast project duration.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To deal with barriers to accessing assistance, RAO shall actively seek out the most vulnerable members of the population including elderly persons, severely sick individuals, persons with no community links and support networks, unaccompanied children and minors, those living with disability and other vulnerabilities. These category of beneficiaries will be mapped with the help of chiefs, community leaders and enumerators to obtain information on their locations and contacts so that they do not miss their share of S/NFIs assistance. Women and girls will be encouraged to participate and/or lead in all steps of program cycle. During distribution of S/NFIs items, Project staffs will actively seek out girls, elderly women, women heading households, severely sick women, pregnant and lactating women gender minorities and ensure them equally beneﬁt without discrimination. Appointment of persons in groups, leadership committees etc. shall observe gender parity rule as much as practically possible. Negative social behaviors that denigrate one gender shall be barred from all community engagements where RAO will be facilitating. These actions will be done by RAO for all people groups regardless of age. To ensure protection of women and girls, project staffs will be guided PSEA Policy to mitigate all forms of exploitation, deprivation, coercion and sexual violence amongst the women and girls who are the most vulnerable members of the target communities. There shall be extensive and conﬁdential consultations in safe spaces for vulnerable members especially the children, women and adolescent girls and youth. Project staffs are adequately trained to identify salient protection threats within the community and respond appropriately. RAO in coordination with S/NFIs partners, protection partners, chiefs, landlords, local authorities and community leaders will conduct HLP messaging and awareness to mitigate tension and conflict associated with issues of Housing, Land and Property.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

RAO has a Feedback & Complaint Procedure that deﬁnes, documents and implements expectations and the processes through which it will enable women, men, boys and girls that it aims to assist, and other stakeholders, to provide and receive feedback. RAO has put in place a clear feedback mechanism that allows all members of the affected population targeted by this program to provide feedback both in the ﬁeld and in the coordination oﬃce.
