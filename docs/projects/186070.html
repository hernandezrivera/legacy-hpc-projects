Project Summary

This project is intended to enhance the capacities of school heads, community leaders, project facilitators in community-led child protection mechanisms in 16 of the 34 sub-divisions of the North West Region through;
- Establishment of 15 child friendly spaces to take care of teenage school drop outs (10-18 years)
-Provision of MHPSS to 12000 children in schools and child friendly spaces.
Provision of case management services to 1000 identified CP cases from schools and non formal learning spaces.
- Sensitize 25000 children and caregivers on the risks related to explosion (IEDs).
Identification of 415 most vulnerable persons in need of assistance.
• Sensitize at least 1500 parents, community focal points, school heads, care givers including traditional and religious leaders on the prevention of family separation.
- Train community facilitators and focal point in all the sub divisions to establish safe school protocols. This activity is intended to ensure that the ongoing schools remain a protected environment for children (safe and fulfilling for children’s welfare) through engaging the trained leaders to monitor violations and respond, sensitize communities and raise their awareness to monitor and identify violations, provide urgent psychosocial first aid and referrals services.
• Sensitizing and raising awareness in 15 subdivisions on prevention of child violation, child protection mechanisms using banners/flyers and posters, in schools, churches, traditional councils, women, youth and children’s groups.
In order to create a safer world for these children, we first need to strengthen the
mechanisms that can keep them safe (involving all key stakeholders to know children's rights, and know how to exercise these rights). This is why we need to reach at least 25,000 people in these communities through advocacy/communication with communities to equally promote social cohesion to rebuild disintegrated communities that would enhance survival, prosperity and reunification of separated or unaccompanied children and comprehensive child protection case management services
• Provision of support services to enhance Child Protection. Services here include (but
not limited to) civil status registration (especially enabling children to possess necessary
nationality documentation), enabling access to GBV, primary health care, referral
pathways and other required facilities.
GPA have the capacity and access to enhance project efficiency and efficacy coupled
with support partnerships with churches and intervention collaboration with CBOs in some key areas like Njikwa and Kumbo. In addition to the fact that GPA is UNICEF's main IP for education, access to these institutions are facilitated already due to our constant collaboration with the Delegations in charge of education in the NW and SW regions.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Through the implementation of projects in the course of the year 2021, GPA has identified several needs on the field pertaining to child protection concerns. Without the necessary means to meet these needs, GPA intends in 2022 to face these issues squarely. It should be noted that the ongoing emergency situation in the North West region has only exacerbated the existing challenges/consequences of chronic poverty that characterized most of these conflict affected communities, coupled with weak governance and a scarcity of supports for children affected by various forms of involuntary social exclusion – children who suffermultiple inequalities, deprivations and vulnerabilities; children in dire need of lifesavingmeasures; children in distress; children who have been deprived basic rights and havelost human dignity. The crisis has rendered masses of families homeless and largenumbers of children become unaccompanied or separated - mass unplanneddisplacements have subjected children to risks such as family separation while othershave been forced by circumstances to live in unstable structures in bushes or unsafehideouts in neighborhoods. GBV is not uncommon. Sexual exploitation and violence is avery common phenomenon. Several young girls (adolescents) have been found pregnantresulting from forced sex commonly practiced by members of both State and Non-State armed groups. These coupled with the fact that return to school is also a challenge because of the ban on schooling by some NSAG groups.
