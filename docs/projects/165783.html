1 - Needs

1. Technical Information(a) Emergency description: Haiti faces a political and socio-economic crisis that culminated in the “peyi lòk”, or country lockdown movement, causing a nationwide economic standstill in 2019. The crisis damaged livelihoods of the urban and rural poor as access to land for cultivation was blocked and/or roads to markets were either ‘locked’ or became dangerous due to criminal activity. Consequently, inflation has risen to 22.6% and led to decreased demand for farm labor, further deteriorating income-generating activities (IGA) in rural areas. The El Niño phenomenon in 2018/2019 continues to cause lower than average food production contributing to spikes in staple food prices. Although economic activity has resumed with markets reopened and stocked with essential foods, poor households (HH) lack the purchasing power to meet their nutritional needs because of limited IGAs, decreased subsistence production of food, steadily rising prices of staple foods, and depreciation of the Gourde. Indicators of acute food and nutrition insecurity in targeted geographic area: The targeted geographic region—the communes of Moron, Bonbons, Dame-Marie, Chambellan, Marfranc, and Abricots that make up a significant portion of the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) HT08 and HT07 zones in Grand’Anse (GA)—have experienced some of the highest food price increases in the country. The local HH food basket cost rose 25% between July and October 2019 while price increases of CNSA-identified essential food commodities ranged from a low of 20% for oil to 78% for sugar during the same period. The price surges are largely attributed to the isolation of GA as supply routes from Port-au Prince were cut off during peyi lòk. In December 2019, CARE in collaboration with ACTED and Solidarité International (SI) conducted a rapid needs assessment of the food security situation in GA. The assessment revealed the depth of the crisis and the greatly increased food prices. It also found most HH in GA could in July 2019 afford three daily meals, but in December 2019 could only afford one daily meal. At the same time, the assessment indicates the majority of the population in GA is rapidly depleting productive assets, pointing to a reliance on negative coping strategies such as selling livestock (41%), consuming seeds (39%), and buying food on credit (73%).

2 - Activities

Goal: Food security and nutrition of socio-political crisis-affected HHs in 6 communes of GA of Haiti improved. Purpose 1: Reduced the use of negative coping strategies by crisis-affected HHs. This will be achieved through two food vouchers—one for dry food and one for fresh food: (1) Dry Food Vouchers: CARE has been implementing a range of interventions, including food security assistance in the form of food voucher distribution to vulnerable populations, in GA for several years. The proposed action will build on this experience. CARE will reach 6,280 food insecure HHs (up to 31,400 individuals) with a dry food voucher worth USD66 per HH. Approximately 70-80% of target HHs will be IPC4 and 20-30% in IPC3, with variation across target communes. According to the CNSA, the size of a HH is estimated at five individuals.(2) Fresh Food Vouchers: CARE will distribute a fresh food voucher of USD 17 to allow target families to access additional fresh food, specifically items like fish, meat, eggs, and dairy products, to diversify their food composition. The total combined value of the two food vouchers will, therefore, be USD 86 (inclusive of the MT charges) per HH, equivalent to 75% of the food basked per CNSA in December 2019. Sensitization sessions under Essential Complementary Activities (ECA) will accompany the distribution of food vouchers to ensure purchased food is used for balanced food consumption.Purpose 2: Affected communities supported to access essential nutrition information, enhanced means of production, and livelihoods support. This purpose will be achieved through the implementation of three ECAs that support the targeted communities to achieve sustainable results in the area of food security/nutrition. In addition to ECAs, CARE will provide a one-off unconditional Multipurpose Cash Assistance transfer to targeted HHs to access other essential non-food items critical to sustaining their food and nutrition security.
