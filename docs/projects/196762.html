Project Summary

Through this intervention AVSI would like to support school children and, above all, out-of-school children (3-17 years old) in remaining in education as well as to promote quality education in Rural Damascus, by enhancing the learning environment of the schools with soft inclusive and green rehabilitation, by improving teaching quality and by supporting psychosocial resilience for crisis-affected children. This intervention has been planned and it will be implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Education, guaranteeing to AVSI the access to the area. The sub-district selected have an high severity scale (3). The project is based on the previous AVSI experience in a similar SHF educational project. In particular, we would like to target the teachers and children of 2 schools in 2023 and relative parents and children in the communities of At Tall. In At Tall, AVSI is already present, making needs assessment to develop complementary projects also in Livelihood to increase financial support to families with children. The total beneficiaries planned are 770 children, 60 parents (PTA meetings) and 24 teachers and volunteers (for 2023).

3 - Needs

The protracted nature of the conflict, economic duress and the Covid-19 pandemic continue to impact overstretched education services to children and increase the challenges facing the education process in Syria (psychosocial effect, increased drop-out rates, child labor, etc.), and these can lead to an uneducated, left-behind and lost-generation in the next coming years, if actions are not taken. 18% of school aged children are currently out-of-school (HNO).1- According to HNO 2022, the sub-districts of Al-Tall has an high severity scale 3, combined with very-high number of PiN, both children (91.302 in total) and education personnel (3.043 in total). Thus, this area is priority to AVSI education projects in Rural Damascus.2- Moreover, as per needs assessment conducted by CSO "Charitable Action Committee" for AVSI in 2021 in At Tall confirmed that school age children are dropped out since low education quality, the absence of safe and healthy school environment, limited teaching capacities, overcrowding, economic families' vulnerability. 3- Furthermore, according to HNO 2022 Education sector findings is estimated that there is one functioning classroom in Rural Damascus per 94 school-aged children (5-17) which is affecting the quality of learning process and its dangerous especially during COVID-19 pandemic. In addition to 72% of children in Rural Damascus reported lack of privacy and there is no separation between boys and girls toilets.

4 - Implementation of activities

Main activities:* Soft rehabilitation including WASH facilities and providing furniture and green-energy/lights to schools.* Create ECE space in each school and providing ECE courses.* Sanitizing schools against COVID-19 and provision of PPE materials.* BTL campaigns.* Training to teachers and education staff on Active learning (including ECE) and Psychosocial support* Provide teaching materials to schools and transport incentives to participation.* Provide most vulnerable children winter kits and transport incentives.* Provision of catch-up courses, remedial classes and ECE sessions.* Provide life-skills and Psychosocial support sessions.* Conduct PTA meetings.* Monitoring and evaluation will be performed thanks to AVSI MEAL officer (PDM after each distribution and satisfaction survey after each activities which will be reported on 4W).The project will be implemented in collaboration with MoE and by implementing in partner with SARC.The MoE teachers will implement the life-skills activities, remedial classes, catch-up courses and participate to PTA meetings, after conducting training for them on active learning and child protection. In addition they will support BTL campaigns to reach deeper the local community.SARC volunteers will be trained on AVSI international psychosocial approach and child protection minimum standards and they will implement the PSS sessions to children in school settings, BTL campaigns, awareness sessions on protection against COVID-19.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

One main risk is to have inappropriate and unsafe spaces for learning, and to mitigate these risks one of the first objective will be to soft rehabilitate classrooms and schools to make learning spaces safe and appropriate; moreover violence and bullying could be a threat to the correct development of FE and NFE promoted and this is why specific trainings (PSS/CP, inclusion) are planned for teachers and educational personnel, psychosocial support is provided for children and continuous monitoring and feedbacks/complaints mechanism will be in place. In order to guarantee safe, appropriate, and inclusive learning space, thus, soft rehabilitation of classrooms and schools will take place; moreover, specific training for teachers and education personnel (PSS/CP, inclusion, etc.) will be provided in order to avoid violence and bullying among children threatening the correct development of FE and NFE. Specific risks are linked with gender and disability both in term of participation and harassments. In fact participation could be hidden by lack of parents approval, being after regular schools, or lack of adaptation for the activities to their needs. Thus, we ensure to make the activities in suitable time for the participating children, taking into account the average distance and time from schools, sustaining transportation, and communicating with parents regularly. The activities are modelled to be as inclusive as possible according to gender and diversity which means adaptation.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

About possible harassment, Child Safeguarding polices and PSEA policies will be also in place. In particular, at the beginning of the project, a specific Child Safeguarding Risk Assessment Template will be developed to mitigate all the risks related to adults dealing with children and to avoid unnecessary direct contacts between adults and children. AVSI is ensuring that all staff and volunteers involved in this program are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Safeguarding polices, and the internal reporting mechanisms for CP and SEA cases, through the mandatory annual trainings and the signature of the code of conduct and the SG bulletin. AVSI has worked on enhancing its internal PSEA minimum standards through participation in trainings provided by UNICEF in 2020. For this project AVSI will reinforce field monitoring, outreach activities, case managers' presence and work (including reporting and referrals), awareness among personnel, beneficiaries and local populations regarding PSEA, limitations of the risks through support to girl’s transportation, specific girls and disables' toilets rehabilitation, etc. .


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AVSI promotes collaboration with local teachers volunteers for its educational projects, since they are available locally even in case of curfew. Furthermore, the excellent collaboration and coordination with the partner and the relations with the local authorities allow AVSI to further mitigate this risk, having the possibility to require special access authorizations and/or monitoring mission even in case of curfew. The risk of Covid19 infection both for the staff and the beneficiaries, which can prevent the implementation of activities in the schools, is mitigated by the adoption of AVSI procedures related to COVID pandemic including the use of PEE, disinfection of schools, social distancing, temperature’s measurement, early warning system, limited number of students in classes, etc.. Other risks concern the arrival of funds in Syria and the great inflation and volatility of the exchange rate. AVSI has already set up mitigation measures such as the change of the bank at HQ country level for sending funds to Syria, accelerated procurement plans, additional supervision of procurement process by the Regional Operations Manager and accelerated payment procedures to avoid any procurement delays. Moreover it has increased its local contingency funds both in SYP and in USD to balance any fluctuations. The risks of fraud or corruption are mitigated by internal procedures (DIGE 8/2020) which establishes high standards of responsibility, transparency and regulatory compliance.
