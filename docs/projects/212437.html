Project Summary

According to the 2024 Syrian Arabic Republic Humanitarian Needs Overview, 14.4 million people need Mine Action related Protection Interventions in 2024. This is significantly higher than the number of those in need in 2023. Additionally an din proportion, the number of identified People in Need is significantly higher in the Governates of Northwest Syria (Aleppo and Idleb). Research suggests that events like earthquake, flooding and severe winter significantly increases people’s exposure to the risks of injury and death as a result of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) (as these events can dislodge and displace explosive remnants). Northwest Syria has recently been exposed to such events, and there is significant reporting of new discoveries of ERW. Moreover, due to the ongoing and active conflict in Northwest Syria, there is new and dynamic risks of exposure to ERW associated with people fleeing their homes to new unfamiliar areas that are impacted by parties to the active conflict – and thus exposing those displaced people to ERW. Community based Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) and Awareness Raising activities are crucial to reduce the risk of injury from mines and unexploded ordnance by alerting children and adults to the risks.
NAS will respond to the issues raised above by:
A.Provide risk education sessions for beneficiaries through Explosive Ordinance Risk Education (ERW) mobile teams (three mobile teams, each team (6 staff each; 1 team leader, 5 male and female educators) using Mine action sub-cluster materials to raise their awareness and avoid any accident related to explosive hazards. ERW Mobile teams will target the beneficiaries in camps, streets, mosques, markets, health centres, nurses, farmers, and housewives. Each session is 45 minutes long using Mine action-sub-cluster materials. The session contains information on ERW definition, types of war remnants, evidence of war remnants, fire safety, mine awareness, warning signs and what we should do when we see any strange item. NAS team will work closely with older children (groups made up of older children and younger children) to help increase their participation and understanding of the instructions. NAS team will follow the method “Learn by playing” with a maximum of 20 minutes per session. The level of attention of children and adolescents is lower, and changes according to their age. Facilitators will determine the materials to be used according to the age and context of the participants, in addition to using purposeful videos and images during these sessions.
B). Train 72 (Teachers, local councillors, nurses and community leaders, humanitarian workers) as community focal points in Idlib and Aleppo to raise their capacity in delivering training and awareness on risk education, explosive hazards, mine awareness, and communication and facilitation skills (they will be able to conduct training on ERW and raise awareness of people in need or at risk like farmers and their children about ERW). NAS will monitor the focal point persons to ensure they are raising awareness as per the agreed action plans by requesting for list of participants for sessions conducted and cross checking with targeted communities. In addition, the NAS M&E team will conduct field visits to the locations where these sessions are taking place.
The ERW focal point persons will be from different segments of society (teachers, local authorities, community leaders and IDP camp facilitators…. etc.). The training focuses on information about ERWs and the best ways to report about any remnants, each focal point will be provided with MASC brochures.
C). Providing safe, accessible, child-sensitive reporting mechanisms are in place for PSEA.
PSEA sessions will be provided to boys/girls/men/women, and materials developed by the PSEA Interagency network will be distributed in the communities so that girls, boys, women and men will be aware on how to report SEA cases.
