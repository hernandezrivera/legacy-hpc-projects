Project Summary

FCDO/UNOPS funded and Christian Aid Led Consortium SHRP project will enhance equitable access to health primary & secondary care services through PHC facilities and Health post at camp 13 and camp 19 of Rohingya Refugee camps. However, current funding from FCDO/UNOPS is valid until end of December 2020. The PHC and Health post will ensure services on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases (NCD). At the PHC, project will ensure isolation beds for maternal delivery patients with suspected COVID-19 before referral and beds for Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD).
Comprehensive and specialty services will be ensured, including the needs of the gender diverse population for HIV, TB, palliative care, disability, emergency care, ensure infection prevention and control measures and timely response to all communicable diseases with outbreak potential, including COVID-19, enhanced referral systems. Availability and equitable access to preventative and curative services for all crisis-affected age groups for women and girls, men and boys, will be scaled up. Maternity care, long acting reversible contraceptives, facility-based deliveries, and comprehensive GBV care for survivors will be expanded. The Project during this time frame will provide capacity building for urgent care services, obstetric complications, trauma, acute medical emergencies, non-communicable and infectious diseases.
The Project will expand risk communication and community engagement through community health workers, who will inform refugees about medical services available, improve health awareness, build trust with health service providers at camp through Health Facility(HFs) which will increase health seeking behaviours, rational distribution of medications and decreased dependence on drug shops. Along with this, expansion of immunization sensitization will be continued to increase the coverage.
At the Health facilities, efforts will be made to ensure crosscutting issues. Efficient and accountable Information management, data collection and reporting will be maintained with Health Sector and government systems, and early notification of reportable diseases emphasised.
For Host community, the project will increase service availability, accessibility and community engagement same as for the refugee population. Our Camp HFs will continue to offer free services for host community members. Increased linkage to SRH services and referral systems, availability of FP services and commodities, will equally benefit the host community. In addition, we will participate in any emergency response such as Mobile Medical Team (MMT) through our expertise and medical supply both in the camps and host community.
The Project will support 25,000 participants through PHC and 10,000 participants through Health post for healthy living improvement of health seeking behaviour and utilization of essential service package by refugees and host populations.

1 - Identified Needs

COVID-19 have increased difficulties in access to health services and reduced access to crucial health services such as sexual reproductive & maternal health due to increased feelings of distrust, dissatisfaction and misinformation (Ref. In the shadows of Pandemic, the gendered impact of COVID-19 on Rohingya and host communities). Fears and rumours around COVID-19 deterred refugees from seeking care in health facilities including SARI ITCs in the first half of 2020. In April 2020, ANC visits dropped by 36.7%, and between January and June, institutional deliveries by 23%(Ref. MSNA, october,2020). The demand for MHPSS services increased further because of fear, isolation, and stigmatization. Critical gaps continue to exist for reproductive health including family planning. Long-acting reversible contraceptives are not widely available, with IUD devices offered in only 43% of facilities. Referral pathways for obstetric and other emergencies, and for specialty services continues to be a challenge. Services for gender-based violence are not yet offered at all facilities; less than 50% of primary health care centres offer comprehensive GBV medical services. The JRP 2020 was adhered to quality and respectful implementation of the minimum essential service package, but the arrival of a pandemic to the camp setting created additional challenges to service provision. There is a need to enhance timely childhood immunization uptake and awareness to protect against vaccine preventable diseases; in 2020 only 25% of children completed measles 2 vaccination. Access to rehabilitation services for those recovering from the long-term effects of COVID-19, or other debilitating NCD-related illnesses, will complete the health network continuum of care, from acute inpatient, to discharge, to community-level care for chronic conditions or palliative care. The needs in the host community were equally compounded in 2020 by the pandemic with 5,304 individuals becoming infected across Cox’s Bazar district.By midyear, October, MSNA Health sector Survey, revealed that 84% of persons surveyed reported not knowing what COVID-19 was and movement restrictions and closed facilities were a further barrier to health seeking behaviours. Of those with an illness serious enough to seek medical care, only 35% sought services at a government or NGO facility, with the majority (41%) self-treating at a pharmacy or drug shop. Persons ill enough to require services but did not, reported the same fear of COVID-19 as within the camp setting, resulting in fewer presenting to facilities for timely care. Further aggravating health seeking behaviours, 15% of households reported having to walk more than one hour to reach health-related services. Out of pocket payments are an additional barrier to care. Many of the needs in the camp community are reflected in the host; households with disabled members pay considerably more for health care, and while health was considered one of the top two priorities for women and girls, only 28% of women surveyed could access sexual and reproductive health services. Social and cultural barriers to family planning services, including access to contraceptives need continued vigilance. Epidemiological surveillance, testing and treatment facilities require continuation. In addition to response to COVID-19, essential services require continued attention at host and camps.
