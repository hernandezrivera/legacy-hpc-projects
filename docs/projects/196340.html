Project Summary

"Save the Children (SC) aims to continue education services for children and adolescents, through access to safe, equitable, inclusive, competency-based, and age-appropriate learning opportunities, within a child-friendly and protective environment inside the camps and host communities, under Ukhiya and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh.
Activities will focus on maintaining and upgrading Learning Centres (LCs), establishing new Community Based Learning Facilities (CBLF), Peer Education Programs (PEP), Distance Education (DE), and Cross-Sectoral Facilities (known as Girl Friendly Spaces) for early learning and basic education for children and adolescents. SC will implement LCFA, MC, CBLF, ECD, and interventions in Rohingya and host communities covering children and the age group 3-18 . The intervention will be implemented in the Camps and host: Total LC in RR: 97, Camp 04 (Block: E, G ), Camp 10 (Block: A, C), Camp 13 (Block: A,B, C,E, F,G), Camp 14 (Block: A,E, I,Q), Camp 15 (Block: E, F ), Camp 16 (Block: D), Camp 18 (Block: C), Camp 19 (Block: B), Camp 1E (Block: A, B,C), Camp 25 (Block: D ), Camp 26 (Block: D,F, H), Camp 8W (Block: A)), CBLF-RR: 154 (Camp 07 (Block: A,B,C,D,E,F,G ), Camp 9 (Block : A, B, C, D, E,F,G), Camp 13 (Block: A,C, D,E, F,G), Camp 14 (Block: A,B, C, D,E), Camp ,15 (Block: C,D, E,F,G,H), Camp 21 (Block: A,B,C) and Learning centre in host community 3 and all are in Jaliapolong Union under Ukhiya Upazila.
Interventions also include enhancing the knowledge of parents and caregivers on how to support their children’s learning, development, and social well-being. Besides, activities will aim to improve teaching quality through continuous teachers’ professional development support for all education sector partners. Inclusive aspects of programming include Mental-Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), gender-responsive, child-sensitive, psychosocial support, and disability-friendly as well as integration between sectors, including child protection and health will be mainstreamed in all activities to ensure that different needs of girls and boys are responded to, and they are equitably benefitting from the interventions.
Moreover, special attention will be paid to the capacity building of relevant staff members on key teaching-learning based on the new Myanmar Curriculum (MC) and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP), as well as Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) issues. All the learning facilities will be equipped with gender-segregated WASH points, including Menstrual Health Management (MHM) services. In addition to that physical infrastructure, materials, and facilities will be strengthened to ensure access and equity for children living with disabilities. Being a sector co-lead, SC will continue to play a key role in advocacy, increased quality, and sustainability of education services for the displaced Rohingya and adjacent host communities.
Furthermore, to combat the sudden pandemic and other unprecedented crises, SC plans to continue the caregiver-led education modalities, such as Interactive Radio Instructions and web-based & E-learning resources to accelerate and support remedial learning. Besides, SC will continue to advocate for the continuation of education as an essential service and the permission of technology-based interventions in the camps, so that the technology/remote learning activities can be ensured. Additionally, consulting with the District Primary Education Office and Upazila Office, SC will support the lower-performing government primary schools to ensure quality education among the learners.
The project and programmatic initiatives are and will be enhancing social cohesion within the host and Rohingya communities. Besides, they are increasing inclusion and mutual understanding, creating a sense of belonging, promoting trust, and offering its members the opportunity for upward social mobility through different day observations, cultural events, awareness sessions, and meetings.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Significant progress has been made over the years by the education sector’s over the years for reaching and enrolling the learners into the LC over the last 5 years, specially for the age group of 6-14. However, the JRP-2022 data set along with education sector’s 5W MIS reports shows that there are still a huge gap specially for the ECCD and adolescent learners. There are still 19% gap in terms of reach for the JRP 2022 considering the overall sector. The education sector’s interactive dashboard clearly shows that, as of Nov 08, 2022, there are 32% gap for the age group of 3 to 5 years old, 4% gap for the 6-14 years old and 57% gap in terms of the adolescent girls. The UNHCR Population factsheet of Sep, 2022 shows that there are a little over than 100k children of the age of 3+ to 5 years old and the number of the adolescent group between the is 11 years to 18 years is nearly 189,842. So, it is apparent that more focused programing on ECCD and adolescent groups are essentials to cover all the learners of the Rohingya camp.According to the 2021 J-MSNA report, enrollment in education remained low, especially among adolescents, especially adolescent girls. Educational attendance of adolescent girls remains very limited, both in camps and in host communities. In camps, girls aged 11 to 14 were less likely to attend school than boys, and this trend increased with age, with the largest proportion of out-of-school children being Rohingya adolescent girls. I'm here. It is estimated that 86.8% of Rohingya youth between the ages of 15 and 24 have no education or training. A limited range of educational and economic opportunities exposes them to exploitation, increases protection risks, and can lead to negative coping mechanisms.Meanwhile, in 2020, Rohingya youth and adolescents faced new challenges due to the COVID-19 pandemic, which resulted in the closure of educational institutions in the camps. There is currently limited data on the number of Rohingya youth and adolescents participating in pre-primary programs, especially educational opportunities involving life skills. The camp has had some success in recruiting a high percentage of female teachers, with her 56% of teachers being female. However, most of the teachers are Bangladeshis from host community, and Rohingya female teachers make up only 25% of total. This is a challenge as more female teachers with adequate Myanmar language skills need to be recruited and trained when introducing the Myanmar curriculum. Children with disabilities also face significant challenges. They attend learning centers at a much lower rate than their peers in all age groups, both girls and boys. In addition to inadequate skills and training of educators, this is fueled by a lack of awareness, negative attitudes and stigma towards children with disabilities attending the same learning centers as children without disabilities. increase. Learning facilities often lack ramps, accessible seating, toilets and water supplies, and the surrounding terrain is often rugged and uneven, making mobility difficult and assistive devices difficult to obtain. The lack of comprehensive learning and materials, including materials suitable for children with learning disabilities, is an additional barrier."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

SC is currently working with the standard package for the service delivery of education activities: Early Childhood Development (ECD) and Myanmar Curriculum (MC)/ Accelerated Learning Programme (ALP)/Learning Competency Framework Approach (LCFA): Areas of focus of a standard package for education service delivery is also ensured by the SC, aligning the direction of education sector. SC will apply sector’s minimum standards for sector activities: design of LFs, standard remuneration for teachers/facilitators/head teachers etc. and to comply with the minimum technical standards agreed by the Education Sector. SC will try through its implementing partners to ensure that geographic overlaps and gaps are avoided; mapping of response areas will ensure there is no overlapping by taking into consideration block presence of partners. Mapping out of partners will be done based in collaboration of the education sector and CiC office. According to the rationalization principles, there will be no other agencies where SC will be working. So, there are extremely low chance of duplication with sector partners. However, to avoid duplication for the internal programming, SC is and will continue maintaining the database along with progress ID (which is unique). Data duplication-checking methods will be applied here to find any possible duplication. Like other education sector programme partners and Implementing partners, SC is and will be Appling Education sector minimum standards along with Child safeguarding policy and PSEA policy and sign the code of conduct of Education sector. When SC and its Ips will move their operations to a different geographic area, the handover of relevant facilities, case files, and other elements to new partners will be done in a responsible manner. As sector co-lead and an Active member of the education sector’s SAG and TWiG, SC was involved in camp rationalization guideline and is committed to work with sector,CIC, RRRC.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Emphasizing on creating a gender-sensitive learning environment through capacity strengthening initiatives for the staff and partners on Gender and inclusion to support improving the sensitivity on child education, especially for girl children. Considering the protection concern of the girl child, community-based learning facilities formed and rolled out which is supporting girls access to education intervention. Ensuring Sex segregated WASH facilities for the learning centers. Ensuring gender-sensitive data collection process by engaging both women and men. Besides, ensuring a survivor-centred approach at all levels for the best interest of the children and adult survivors is another layer of Safeguarding protection. This is being mainstreamed by including the above elements in the general project management as central to delivering humanitarian aid. And incorporating into project cycle management through protection mainstreaming indicators for different activities.

3.2 - Gender

Emphasizing on creating a gender sensitive learning environment through capacity strengthening initiatives for the staffs and partner on Gender and inclusion to support on improving the sensitivity on child education especially for girl child. In addition to that, members from community education committee and parenting meeting being part of different discussion which is getting community acceptance in improving awareness on importance of education for children including girls and children with disabilities, proper parenting, reproductive health, prevention of child marriage and teenage pregnancy etc. Considering the protection concern of the girl child, community-based learning facilities formed and rolling out which is supporting girls access to the education intervention. Ensuring Sex segregated WASH facility for the learning centers. Moreover, SC integrating age, gender, and diversity components in different assessment like outcome assessment and monitoring process.

3.4 - Diversity

SC education team is conducting assessment following child functioning module of Washington group questions. Along with that, SC is collaborating with Age and Disability working group (ADWG) to ensure necessary technical support on rehabilitation and assistive device which facilitate meaningful participation of children with disabilities in the learning activities. Moreover, SC is conducting training on disability inclusion and accessibility consideration for the staffs and doing different safety and accessibility assessment of the learning facilities to identify the barriers children with disabilities may face to access the facilities so that the barriers can be removed. SC has identified that the usual learning materials is not always useful for the children with different disabilities and there have multiple barriers to access the learning facilities and started process to consulting children with disabilities, their caregivers, and teachers to come with possible way forward.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

For this project, multiple accountability mechanisms will be applied to ensure voices to children and communities and create an opportunity for community members to influence relevant decisions affecting them. SC will support accountability mechanisms (16 mechanisms for Adults and Children as community meetings, exit interviews, hotlines, helpdesk, children’s consultation, child-friendly mechanisms and post-distribution monitoring) providing an inclusive platform for community members to raise their complaints in a safe and dignified manner. SC uses this as a means of continual refinement and improvement in the design of future programs. Feedback and Complaints (F&Cs) received by SC are processed, analyzed and referred to relevant sectors (ISCG), site management sector, camp-in-charges, etc.) every week (within 24 hours for Child Safeguarding). SC generates monthly reports on Accountability Snapshot. Besides, Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) are followed throughout its responses.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The Project area is geographically located at a high risk of various disasters. Cyclones, storms, heavy monsoons, drought, landslides, flash floods, and fire incidents are some of the major disaster risks that could happen in the camp. Mandatory Disaster Risk Management Training will be provided to all staff, teachers, and volunteers. The community, Parents, and learners will get a regular briefing on disaster preparedness and discussion sessions to raise awareness regarding natural disaster issues.
