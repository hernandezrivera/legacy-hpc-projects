Project Summary

Overall, the situation across the Far-North region is projected to remain unchanged in 2019. With the majority of populations in need being women and children being internally displaced, returnees, but also women and children from communities hosting IDPs and returnees, the child protection response need to be maintained in order to continue to assist new children returning to Far North Region taking into account that separation from family members continue to take place and affect children., protect all those vulnerable from violent extremism and gender based violence but also assist in their reinsertion into their communities while mistrust, stigmatization and suspicion of collaboration and affiliation with Boko Haram are still present.
The key result of the project which is in line with the objectives of the protection sector, is that 133,636 conflict affected children (5-17 years) among whom 73,500 girls and 60,136 boys, are protected against violence, abuse and exploitation and live in a protective environment in the targeted localities of in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava, and Mayo Tsanaga departments of the Far-North Region of Cameroon for a twelve-months starting 1st January 2019.
The project builds on previously established dynamics and community structures and will provide psychosocial support to 133,636 children, 73,500 girls and 60,136 boys, in 72 child friendly spaces or other functional safe spaces.
During the child friendly spaces activities, partners will continue to identify unaccompanied and separated children (based on previous trends and on partners capacities, the project plans to assist around 1,340 UASC including 60% of new cases, ensuring and providing those children with psychosocial support, alternative care arrangements with the capacity building of foster families, reunification with their families of origin and reinsertion in their communities. All will benefit from close individual follow up. In that context, a total of 65 community-based child protection networks will be set up and reinforced. Child protection networks are expected to identify and refer to social workers a minimum of 780 vulnerable children.
To address both social norms but also to ensure smooth community reinsertion, the project will work at strengthening community mobilization and will organize community activities around prevention of gender-based violence including activities to abandon child marriage, intergenerational dialogue, social cohesion. Based on the projections, a total of 74,760 persons (women, men, girls, boys) are planned to be reached through community mobilization activities. Partnership with religious and traditional leaders, and chiefery including female stakeholders will be an important channel to display messages and address complex issues. In complement, the work on youth empowerment and youth participation will enable to merge with the other members of the communities and promote prevention and protection of children from violence and exploitation.
Following up on strategic planning with Far North regional decentralized and deconcentrated authorities, the issue of conflict-affected children not having their birth certificates came out strongly.
45,360 persons (women, men, girls, boys) will be sensitized on the importance of birth registration.
A planned number of 1,200 children suspected of association with armed groups are expected to be identified, will receive psychosocial support, and will be supported for family and community reintegration.
UNICEF will continue to closely work with civil society implementing partners such as ALDEPA and IMC, providing them with capacity building, supporting the setting up SOPs for case management and referral. UNICEF will also strengthen the coordination mechanism led by the Regional Delegation of Social Affairs ensuring a merging with justice and law enforcement authorities.

Needs

Based on the DTM round 15 run in 2018 in the Far North Region of Cameroon and reports, the major issues children are facing were identified as separation from family and the newly identified issue on child marriage. In addition to these, it is also important to mention the issue of children formerly associated with non-state armed groups, as well as those suspected of association, without forgetting all those at risk of association and use by non-state armed groups. With more than 75% of interviewed persons from the six departments of the region, both within internally displaced population and within host communities, acknowledging child marriage as a customary practice in Far North Region, the issue is considered as critical. The results of the study revealed that the departments with the highest rate of positive response towards child marriage practice were Logone-et-Chari (70%), Mayo Tsanaga (67,77%) and Mayo Sava (67,77). The highest rate of positive response was found within the returnees’ populations confirming that many child marriages are happening with the IDPs and returnees’ populations. Respectively 56,3% and 32,5% of the out of refugee camp; 32,5% and 33,8% of IDPs; 27% and 23,0% of the returnees; and 25,4% and 38,1% of the host communities’ respondents of the departments of Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga acknowledged the presence of separated and unaccompanied children within their communities. Displacement of populations following armed attacks by armed groups, kidnapping and killing of populations, and the children’s survival instinct are among the main direct causes of separation of children from their families. Kidnapping and use of children (boys and girls) by armed groups particularly during suicide human bomb attacks remain a critical concern in the Far North Region. Some have been able to escape and return. Since then, they are victims of stigmatization and are rejected both by their communities of origin and their own families. Some have been raped during their captivity and have been victims of ill-treatments. Girls, often used as sexual slaves, return with young children or pregnant. The percentage of bordering communities having witnessed child recruitment or child kidnapping cases perpetrated by armed groups is estimated at 80%. Between January and August 2018, a total of 287 children were identified as suspected from association or associated with armed groups. 90% of them received holistic support and psychosocial assistance. With populations that are reluctant to collaborate in the identification and support to these children fearing reprisals and pursuits by the armed groups, it is even more important to work with the traditional leaders to sensitize the communities.More specifically, the various needs are essentially of:• Holistic, integrated and community-based care for formerly associated children, ex-hostage children, and children suspected of association with Boko Haram and other non-state armed groups;• Psychosocial support to internally displaced children, host community children, returned children, children affected by the conflict including children victims of gender-based violence, and unaccompanied and separated children;• Support for the identification and family tracing for the reunification of unaccompanied and separated children with their families using a community-based approach;• Assistance in the delivery of birth certificates to children not registered at birth and to children whose birth certificates were lost during displacement; • Within the scope of the gender-based violence, children are in need of protection from child marriage in the Far-North Region, including children already vulnerable due to the crisis and who are at even higher risk of being married;• The Project will also address the needs of teenagers and adolescents who are vulnerable and need to be protected from the risks of being associated and involved in violent extremism actions.
