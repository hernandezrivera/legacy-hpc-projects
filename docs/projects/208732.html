Project Summary

The crisis in Northeast Nigeria continues to exacerbate protection risks and needs for internally displaced populations (IDPs) and host communities. While in some locations the situation became more stable, in many locations the unpredictable and volatile security situation continues to threaten the populations’ daily safety and health. By fleeing insecurity, the population is deprived of basic services and often left without any means of livelihood and access to basic protection and MHPSS services. The project aims at continuing and strengthening IOM’s protection response in Northeast Nigeria through the implementation of protection activities by interdisciplinary Psychosocial Mobile Teams (PMTs) comprised of social workers, lay counselors and community mobilizers, and including government staff such as from the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development. IOM will prioritize coordination with protection actors working in other local government areas and government partners including the National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons (NAPTIP) and the State Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States.
The following areas of intervention are to be implemented under this project: 1) Capacity building of key stakeholders, including partners and government officials, on protection mainstreaming, counter-trafficking and community-based mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS); 2) Increased protection risk assessments to measure and report on trends related to general protection risks (i.e. a situation where an individual or group are at risk of or suffering from violence, coercion and deprivation) and risks of trafficking in persons; 3) Provision of specialized protection services to (potential) victims of trafficking (VoTs), including case management, reintegration support, and psychosocial services, as well as to other vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities and older persons; 4) Increased protection advocacy to the government and other key stakeholders incorporating findings from protection risk assessments, as well as advocacy for increased action on trafficking in persons among protection sector partners; 5) Additional training for IOM Women’s and Men’s Committees on identifying key protection risks and trends and making safe referrals for people in need of specialized protection services; 6) Developing reports and other information products on identified protection risks and trends and sharing these with key stakeholders, including the Protection Sector in NE Nigeria and; 7) Targeted livelihood support to (potential) victims of trafficking to support their reintegration.
The implementation of planned services will be guided by the Guidance note on how to mainstream protection across IOM crisis response (IN/232 Guidance note on how to mainstream protection across IOM crisis response (or Migration crisis operational framework sector of assistance), the IOM Manual on Community-Based MHPSS in Emergencies and Displacement (IOM Manual on Community-Based Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergencies and Displacement International Organization for Migration), the IOM handbook on direct assistance of victims of trafficking (IOM handbook on direct assistance for victims of trafficking, 2007, IOM handbook on direct assistance for victims of trafficking), and the Caring for victims of trafficking guidance note for health providers (Caring for trafficked persons: guidance for health providers, IOM, 2009, Caring for trafficked persons: guidance for health providers).

1 - Needs

The crisis in Northeast Nigeria has impacted women, men, girls and boys, and people with special needs differently, and their vulnerabilities, as well as coping mechanisms, vary. Displaced populations face grave protection risks and an increased risk of abuse, such as exploitation, family separation, restrictions on freedom of movement, and insecurity. Vulnerabilities of the crisis-affected population are exacerbated by pre-existing, and emergency-induced problems faced by the affected population, notably for the more vulnerable sub-groups of the population, including the elderly and chronically ill, persons with disabilities, female, and child-headed households, unaccompanied and/or separated children, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant and/or lactating women, persons with mental health conditions and people returning from captivity. The population displaced due to the conflict is facing additional stressors created by the displacement itself. Stress factors include family separation, diﬃculties accessing (basic) services and sometimes denial of access to services, limited livelihood opportunities, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, and secondary and repeated displacement. The above factors impact the conflict-affected population’s mental health and psychosocial well-being. Further risks relate to the lack of access to safety measures and protective livelihood opportunities. Many IDPs returned to areas that are still experiencing significant security risks and threats that endanger the lives and well-being of community members, as well as serious limitations in services provision and livelihood opportunities. Many people choose not to return to their areas of origin because of these risks and challenges, opting instead to stay in Maiduguri and other safer locations in informal settlements or integrated into the host community, where humanitarian assistance is limited. Therefore, both those who stay in host communities and informal settlements and those who return face an array of serious protection risks and need targeted assistance, including MHPSS and protection services. Trafficking in persons by armed groups manifests in brutal forms such as the use of children in combat roles, the sexual enslavement of women and children, forced marriage to combatants of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), suicide bombers, and other ancillary activities such as cooking, cleaning, and farming. Various data sources (2020 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), 2021 MSNA Bulletin, 2022 MSNA and Protection Analysis Update, April 2021) reveal that IDPs and host communities in captivity are being exploited in several forms including, but not limited to forced labour, forced begging, organ removal, sexual exploitation and forced marriage. Victims who are released or escape from these trafficking situations suffer from stigma and possible rejection from the community upon their release, as well as insufficient attention, care, and support. Meeting their medical, MHPSS, and other needs is crucial for successful integration into the communities and for the fulfillment of their rights and psychosocial needs. Failure to do so may result in increased mental health, and protection risks such as re-trafficking. However, the limited response to Trafficking in Persons (TiP) in northeast Nigeria makes it difficult to provide comprehensive support to victims of trafficking in persons. Through already existing capacities of IOM’s MHPSS/Protection Unit and supported by new funding through this project that will allow to expansion of the services provided by IOM (e.g., additional services focusing on specialized activities for GBV survivors and children in need), the following activities will be provided.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Direct assistance such as case management, referrals, reintegration support, and direct mental health and psychosocial support (lay counselling, psychological first aid, basic emotional support, sociorelational, recreational, and cultural activities, referral to specialised mental health care) to the distressed population including (potential) victims of trafficking (VoTs), at-risk groups and vulnerable persons affected by protection concerns. Activity 2: Provision of specialized protection services including direct assistance to vulnerable persons, including older people, persons with mental health conditions and persons with disabilities and their dependents, to address core needs and foster positive coping mechanisms. Activity 3: Carry out key advocacy activities with 1) the Protection Sector Northeast Nigeria and related protection partners for increased focus on counter-trafficking and 2) government authorities to support Counter-Trafficking (CT) initiatives. Activity 4: Organize training sessions for existing women and men’s committees across the BAY states on identifying key protection risks, trends, and existing referral pathways and how to conduct safe referrals for (potential) VoTs and other survivors of protection incidents. Activity 5: Conduct outreach activities and dissemination of protection-focused messages, including trafficking in persons, with at-risk groups, community leaders, parents and other stakeholders. Activity 6: In coordination with CCCM sector, carry out protection assessments at reception centers and review of existing tools and strategies for improved protection mainstreaming with the reception center operations and organize protection mainstreaming training for IOM field staff and relevant national NGO and INGO partners of CCCM, Shelter/NFI and WASH sectors. Activity 7: Improve data collection and reporting on information about trafficking in persons risks and trends, including monitoring the trafficking risks associated with displacement as a driver of irregular migration through the development of tools, in close cooperation with the state level anti-trafficking in-person task forces. Activity 8: Provision of small-scale livelihood support (such as vocational skills training and start-up kits) to promote psychosocial well-being and increase self-reliance among women and men including potential VoTs seeking IOM’s psychosocial support services. Activity 9: Training of IOM PMTs on core protection risk assessment activities in line with the protection monitoring framework and tools of the Protection Sector Northeast Nigeria. Regular reports will be developed with information from protection risk assessment exercises carried out by IOM PMTs and Women and Men’s Committees, and these reports will be shared with PSNE and other relevant partners for action. The collection and usage of information and data will be in line with relevant guidelines such as the Principles of Protection Information Management and the IOM Information Management Guide on Counter Trafficking in Emergencies. Activity 10: Train anti-trafficking taskforces including NAPTiP officials on how to input assessment findings into a centralized database for more streamlined reporting on trafficking trends; and deploy NAPTIP officials to support on the needs assessment, including identification of risks, monitoring trends, supporting awareness raising, identifying cases and reporting and supporting advocacy for the reduction of protection risks. Activity 11: Conduct training of trainers and facilitate cascaded training for the relevant law-enforcement agencies (Nigeria Police Force and Nigerian Security & Civil Defence Corps) on TiP prevention, response and community engagement using existing training manuals.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Through this project, IOM will prioritize engaging its staff and other humanitarian actors including government agencies through awareness raising and training on PSEA, GBV risk mitigation, child safeguarding and protection mainstreaming incorporated in all other training sessions. Additionally, IOM will support in reinforcing two main aspects of a prevention model: 1-Through the dissemination of harmonized GBV and PSEA messages to the community and ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups.; 2- Support in strengthening existing pathways for any referral activities to ensure appropriate access, observing do-no-harm principles and appropriateness of the response. IOM will ensure PSEA posters are printed and put in place in offices and community level as well as disseminate PSEA key messages are disseminated to community members including information on reporting channels. IOM staff are trained to ensure that sensitizations, focus group discussions, and other key services and activities are inclusive of people of different ages, genders, and persons with disabilities. Staff are also trained to consult directly with a variety of people from affected communities (including women and girls, youth, and persons with disabilities) in the process of identifying needs, gaps, strengths, and resources while designing specific activities. IOM teams are gender-balanced to ensure all voices are heard and promoted and allow for gender-disaggregated activities as necessary

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population through satisfaction surveys, collecting complaints and feedback during regular activities, carrying out post-distribution monitoring, as well as actively engaging women, men, boys, girls and persons with disabilities and diversities in assessment to understand needs of different groups and specific vulnerabilities. Focus group discussions will be conducted with IDP representatives ensuring all sub-groups of the community are represented and Persons with Specific Needs. Project beneficiaries can share feedback and complaints via the MHPSS/Protection unit’s Psychosocial Mobile Teams, as well as through a hotline number.
