Project Summary

In response to the immense needs resulting from the conflict in the Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) regions in Cameroon, CRS is implementing fourteen-month project - Anglophone Crisis Emergency Response (ACER IV). ACER IV builds on the success and learning of the previous phases of the response. Changes and adaptations are based on community consultations and input, needs assessments, coordination and harmonization with other humanitarian actors, and changes in the security context and accessibility.
The goal of the ACER IV project is to improve food security and alleviate suffering of displaced and host households in the Northwest Region of Cameroon. The project seeks to achieve this goal through two purposes: (1) vulnerable displaced and host households in the Northwest meet their basic food and NFI needs, and (2) vulnerable displaced and host households in the Northwest improve poultry production. In partnership with the Archdiocese of Bamenda, CRS is delivering an integrated package of assistance, providing vulnerable displaced and host households with poultry production training and support, as well as emergency food and NFIs. The project is using market-based approaches to support 10,800 people in 1,800 households over 14 months.
The Project's Theory of Change is, Assuming that markets and vendors’ supply chains remain accessible and functional and the security situation remains stable, if households have improved access to sufficient quantity, quality and diversity of food; and if they purchase essential non-food items (NFIs); and if they raise poultry to improve their livelihoods and generate income; then they will improve their food security and experience reduced suffering; because the first three phases of the ACER project have demonstrated that market access to food reduces household hunger scores, broader humanitarian evidence shows that food assistance leads to improved food security, and poultry interventions in Cameroon’s ACER III program have demonstrated the potential to improve income and facilitate more sustainable access to food
ACER IV responds directly to the normative and felt needs of the affected population. In the International Organization for Migration (IOM)’s multisector needs assessment (MSNA) conducted in August 2022, food and non-food items (NFIs) continue to be priority needs. IDPs, people with disabilities, older people, and female-headed households are disproportionately impacted by the crisis. In the NW, nearly one million people are food insecure and cannot meet basic household needs. In response, ACER IV will provide food assistance and NFIs using an e-voucher modality that leverages local markets and stimulates the local economy. In CRS community consultations, affected populations have expressed interest in receiving support to restart their livelihoods and generate income so that they do not continue relying on humanitarian assistance. Poultry production was among the most cited and viable livelihoods options considering the context – difficult access to agricultural land and a tradition of a booming livestock sector including egg production. In ACER IV, CRS will scale up the poultry assistance to reach more participants and locations -a subset of the food assistance participants. The poultry pilot intervention includes training, input distribution and strengthening collaboration and engagement with the Ministry of Livestock, Fisheries and Animal Industries (MINEPIA) and Livelihood Field Agents (LFAs).
ACER IV will cover 5 Divisions -Momo and Mezam from previous phases and Ngoketunjia, Boyo and Bui (newly added) in the Northwest, where there is demonstrated need and major gaps in humanitarian assistance. ACER IV will continue to work in Bamenda I, II and III, Santa, Bafut and Batibo sub-divisions (old locations) and will expand to Bali and Tubah in Mezam, Mbengwi in Momo, Belo in Boyo, Balikumbat in Ngoketunjia and Oku in Bui.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Violence continues in the Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) regions, leading to disrupted livelihoods and food insecurity. The number of food insecure people is increasing across the NW and SW: Over 980,000 people were severely food insecure between June and August 2022 compared to 955,000 people in the same period the year prior. Roughly 29% of Cameroon’s food insecure people live in the NW, one of the most food insecure regions of Cameroon. Over 77% of households in the NW were moderately or severely food insecure as of October 2022. Access to food remained significantly low, particularly for IDPs, female-headed households, and those with disabilities. Many households are facing a consecutive year of little-to-no harvest. Households that used emergency strategies to cope with food insecurity increased from 46% in May 2022 to 58% by October 2022. Displacement and insecurity have significantly limited people’s ability to earn income. Accessing farmland outside of the urban areas can be insecure, while suitable land and access to water is limited in urban areas. Providing training and inputs to initiate poultry production will increase the availability of animal-source proteins at the household level and in the markets, creating spillover effects. People selling poultry products will increase their income, enabling them to meet their household needs in the medium termIDPs and vulnerable host households lack the proper shelter and NFI resources they need to adequately survive in the NW. According to the 2022 HNO, 348,000 individuals, including both IDPs and vulnerable host individuals, need shelter and NFI in the NW/SW. The August 2022 MSNA revealed high needs for shelter and NFIs in assessed villages in the NW/SW with shelter/NFIs ranked as both 2nd level and 3rd level priorities.Based on the analysis of households’ most critical and lifesaving needs, the social and economic dynamics driving the need, areas of reliable humanitarian access, and the anticipated ongoing toll of the conflict, CRS will address food insecurity and the lack of basic household items in Mezam, Momo, Boyo, Ngo Ketunjia, and Bui Divisions in the Northwest region, to support vulnerable displaced and host households to meet their basic food and NFI needs. CRS will address the causes of food insecurity and households’ inability to meet basic NFI needs – limited access to sufficient diverse foods due to reduced incomes and inability to access farming lands, lost household assets, and high NFI prices in the market – by providing unconditional food assistance and shelter and settlement (S&S) NFIs using e-vouchersACER IV will scale the poultry production intervention to 110 total households in 4 sub-divisions surrounding Bamenda. These peri-urban settings allow adequate space for poultry production. Households will be selected from among the food assistance caseload according to their vulnerability, capacity to maintain a healthy flock, availability of sufficient space, and previous experience raising poultryACER IV will reach 10,800 people in approximately 1,800 households with monthly food assistance through vouchers and a one off voucher assistance to households to purchase core relief items. 110 households will receive chicks and poultry raising inputs alongside capacity building to support income generation.
