Project Summary

The overall aim is to enable immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations including those in hard-to-reach areas of leer and mayendit counties of south sudan. The interventions in relation to GBV in the humanitarian context thereby expanding access to GBV response services provide life-saving protection assistance through specialized and integrated protection programming to address the protection needs of the most vulnerable individuals and households at risk and affected by conflict, displacements and other shocks (including women, children, elderly, disabled and survivors of sexual and gender-based violence) in hard-to-area's. This project will also strengthen prevention and mitigation of GBV in Mayendit and Leer TPA. The project will target 11,800 beneficiaries with a total budget of $350,000 and implemented between 1/1/2019-31/12/2019.

Needs

According to WHO (2018), The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains volatile and unpredictable since the beginning of the crisis now in its 4th year. The latest round of peace talks took place in Khartoum and permanent ceasefire agreement signed. However, despite this, it is apparent that the fighting leading to loss of lives has continued unabated. The economic crisis with hyperinflation, food insecurity, and continued fighting has put lives of millions South Sudanese at risk. Peace agreement - On 27 June 2018, Khartoum Declaration of Agreement signed by parties to the conflict. Agreement includes permanent ceasefire to take effect 30 June 2018-however, same was violated hours after taking effect. Insecurity and attacks: On 3 July 2018, 3 SPLA-IO fighters were killed and four others wounded when they clashed with government troops east of leer and north of mayendit County of Unity State, allegedly, SPLA forces attacked the SPLA-IO base in Gadur and Naya-giny east of Leer and Rubkuay and mirmir of MayendiAccording to Amnesty International (2018) in late April 2018, armed combatants attacked villages in opposition -held areas of southern Unity state and committed crimes under international law and other serious human rights violations. During these attacks, both government forces and allied militias deliberately targeted and killed civilians, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. While many civilians were killed by gun fire, others were burned alive in their homes, hung from trees and rafters, or run over with armored vehicles. Civilians were also hunted down after fleeing into nearby wetlands, or rivers, as soldiers shot indiscriminately into areas where they were hiding and carried out attacks on islands where civilians had sought refuge. During these operations, these combatants systematically abducted and raped women and girls. Some women sustained serious injuries as a result of being gang raped. Many of those who resisted were killed. Abductees were forced to carry looted goods long distances, and while held by their captors, made to carry out domestic chores. Amnesty International also received information that men were captured and detained, and in one instance, held in a shipping container on a military base in leer. Their fate and whereabouts are anonymous. These unknown gunmen moreover, engaged in a widespread campaign of looting and destruction, systematically setting fire to civilian homes, looting and burning food supplies, stealing cattle, goats and other livestock, as well as civilian property. Even trees and plants were uprooted. These abuses appear to have been carried out with the purpose of forcibly displacing civilians and making villages uninhabitable in the future. Attacks on food sources, including the deliberate destruction and burning of food stocks, moreover, seem to have been carried out with the intention of making the civilian population living in opposition held areas of Leer and Mayendit food insecure.

Activities

Comprehensive case management- Psychosocial support (PSS)- Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services- Women & girls friendly spaces programming, including strengthened integration with child protection to support child survivors- Community-based protection (activities may include: Establishment of women's safety committees; Strengthening participation of women’s groups and women-lead organisations in protection coordination structures)- Community awareness raising and sensitization on women’s rights, etc…- Protective livelihood programming for women (example: safe alternative fuel projects)- Programming targeting female and male youth as agents of change for gender equality (Activities targeting adolescent girls and young women (as priority): empowerment, self-esteem, rights awareness and collective power; Activities targeting male youth (transforming discriminatory attitudes and behaviors that contribute to inequality; negative masculinities, accountable practices, women's rights); Conducting gender analysis of needs and aspirations of female and male youth in local community- Peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence (activities include: Capacity building local women's groups/women's organisations and women leaders on mediation and peaceful resolution of conflicts; Involving women in decision-making in Community Dispute Resolution, conflict mitigation, mediation & peacebuilding; Conduct gender analysis of community relations and norms affecting peaceful co-existence,support football and valley ball tournament league in the area that target boy and girls.)

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. The project location has witnessed in the recent past serious Social upheavals/ political instability. This has been due to clashes/Inter-ethnic clashes/Clashes between returnees and resident community members. All these have posed serious protection challenges in these areas. Hope Restoration as the leading local agency in general protection continues to provide life saving services to the PiN/ PSN in these locations. However, with the signing of peace agreement, there s faint possibility that 2019 may have low clashes/ violence witnessed in these areas. Women and girls are at high risks should the status quo remain. Persons wit disabilities especially the physically challenged who may not be able to run away during the violence's are at eminent risk should the events of April/May 2018 resurface in 2019. Rape in these areas has been rampant especially for young girls/ children. Assuming that the process comes to fruition all these will come to pass.
