Project Summary

The gender inequality in Pakistan is widespread and the country ranks 133 out of 189 countries in terms of gender inequality (2018 UNDP Report). District Umerkot and Tharparkar has witnessed high rate of suicidal incidence among women and young girls in last 2-3 years and concerns shown by all segments of society on this issue. The area has witnessed consecutive droughts and also affected by recent floods. Being already poverty stricken districts with highest malnourished population in Sindh, droughts and floods aggravated the situation of poverty. Besides this, current COVID-19 pandemic has also contributed in vulnerability of these districts as hundreds of youth who were earning their livelihood in big cities like Hyderabad, Karachi and other have lost their jobs and unemployment has contributed a lot to violent behaviors of bread earners among family. Gender-based violence is exacerbated by humanitarian crises as institutions and systems for physical and social protection are weakened and destroyed. Women and adolescent girls are more vulnerable in these situation especially when they head the household. The area is also known for early child marriages as a strong tradition that also cause of gender based violence leaving severe impacts on physical and mental health of girls.
According to Pakistan Demographic & Health Survey (2017-18) 28% of women age 15-49 have experienced physical violence since age 15, and 6% have experienced sexual violence. 34% of ever-married women have experienced spousal physical, sexual, or emotional violence. The most common type of spousal violence is emotional violence (26%), followed by physical violence (23%). Five percent of women have experienced spousal sexual violence. 26% of ever-married women who have experienced spousal physical or sexual violence have sustained injuries. Cuts and bruises are the most common types of injuries reported. 56% of women who have experienced any type of physical or sexual violence have not sought any help or talked with anyone about resisting or stopping the violence. Rural women (30%) are more likely to have experienced physical violence than urban women (24%). According to Annual Report of Aurat Foundation (2017) Sindh has second high rate of reported cases of gender based violence with 14.37% of reported cases in Pakistan.
The proposed project will support provision of psycho-social support, case management and referral of sexual violence and other forms of gender-based violence; sensitization on GBV issues for the affected population, by establishment of 08 Women Friendly Health Spaces 04 in each district. The “Women Friendly Health Space” is referred to as a place where women and adolescent girls can come and spend their time in a safe environment, access information and services and further linked to other required support. The project will provide (1) Life-saving reproductive health services for GBV survivors including medical first aid, counseling on birth spacing, and information on danger signs of pregnancy, prevention and referral on Sexually Transmitted Infections treatment. (2) Case Management services which includes: psychological first aid by trained providers, psychosocial support by psychologists, and referral for specialized mental health services. (3) Sensitization of the communities on issues related to reproductive health and GBV and their after effects upon the survivor their children, family in particular and society in general. Community sensitization campaign will be conducted to sensitize Men and boys where as women and girls will be provided such services at health facilities and women friendly health spaces. (4) Survivors of gender based violence will be provided with the required support including appropriate referrals and case management. The WFHS will have key role in the referral pathway. Establishment of Women Friendly Health Spaces (WFHS) and provision of dignity kits will be done through the WFHS.
