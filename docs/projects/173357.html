Project Summary

This project will be implemented in 4 health districts and 8 health facilities: HD Njikwa (IHC Konda and Oshie), Bengwi HD (IHC Bamunka and CMA Babessi). Stabilization centers in District Hospitals will be support.
Nutrition:
Maternal, infant and young child nutrition in emergencies:
In order to ensure that good nutrition practices do not deteriorate and that poor practices are improved, IMC will conduct a mapping of existing activities and platforms that can be used for strengthening and improving Integrating Maternal Infant & Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices; set up mother support groups as a platform for improving MIYCN practices (including identification and training of lead mothers and CHWs to supervise and support the lead mothers); conduct nutrition education sessions within communities to improve nutrition practices; carry out IYCF IEC during ANC/PNC visits; conduct individual counseling for mothers with breastfeeding or complementary feeding problems by a trained counselor; train of health staff, CHWs and lead mothers in MIYCN practices and education techniques; and conduct cooking demonstrations.
b. Sub-sector 2: Management of acute malnutrition
IMC proposes to implement a CMAM program to ensure quality treatment of acute malnutrition following the Cameroon national protocols and international recommendations for providing treatment of acute malnutrition in the COVID-19 context.
Treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications in the Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP): Treatment of SAM will be implemented in OTP sites in the revitalized health centers and nutritional supplies for the OTPs (RUTF) will be provided by UNICEF. The program will be strengthened through technical support and capacity building activities such as training and supportive supervision to enable the OTP program sites and staff to treat cases appropriately. The OTP will take place on a bi-weekly basis due to COVID-19 and will use ready-to use therapeutic foods (RUTF) and routine medication as per Cameroon protocols (antibiotics, vitamin A, folic acid, anti-helminths, and anti-malarials if required).
Treatment of SAM with medical complications in the Stabilization Centers (SC): SAM cases with medical complications will be referred to Bamenda where SCs are fully functioning with support from different humanitarian actors. IMC will cover costs related to the referral including transport, medical bills and feeding during hospitalization.
Outreach in the community: CHWs will inform the communities about malnutrition and the available treatment programs, conduct routine screening of children aged 6-59 months, trace absentees and defaulters and conduct home visits for problematic cases in order to reduce defaulter rates and increase access in the communities. The CHWs will also be responsible also for facilitating the distribution of iron/folic acid, vitamin A supplementation, MYICN education sessions, cooking demonstrations, and participation in national deworming campaigns.
Health:
International Medical Corps’ implementation strategy for health activities will target four health districts in the North West region and provide support to eight public health facilities with basic essential services. The choice of the Mbengwi, Njikwa, Ndop and Fundong health districts and the targeted health facilities have been motivated by the number of those in need (IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host population); the needs and gaps identified at the health facility and community level, including access as well as the presence or absence of other partners and NGOs. As insecurity and the influx of IDPs have reduced access to quality health care in the targeted health districts, IMC’s focus will be to improve the provision of primary care through training, formative supervision, deployment of staff, identification and support of CHWs and donations of medical equipment and drugs.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Health:Basic primary Health Care:International Medical Corps will support the provision of basic primary health care in the 4 targeted health facilities in order to increase access to primary health care (PHC) services, including reproductive health and HIV/AIDS services to IDPs and vulnerable host communities.Provision of Curative consultations: PHC services will be provided at three levels: at the community level through outreach on prevailing diseases with a network of trained CHWs, at health facilities with the deployment of staff, and at the district level through referrals to secondary and tertiary health facilities. Beneficiaries within a 5 kilometer radius from a health center will be supported by IMC staff deployed in these health facilities while those located more than 5km from the health facilities will be reached through outreach activities (advanced curative and preventive consultations, awareness and sensitization activities...). In line with the MOH and other health partner protocols, IMC will support the provision of a minimum package of activities consisting of curative consultations, disease prevention, EPI services, mental health care and nutrition. Community Health Interventions: IMC will support the surveillance of diseases of epidemic potential including vaccine preventable diseases through a detect-prevent-respond strategy at the community level. CHWs will be trained to implement other health promotion activities including C4D (communication for development) sessions on the key health issues, nutrition and hygiene topics such as the use of mosquito nets, proper hand hygiene techniques and optimal IYCF practices. Management of mental health at the primary health care level: IMC will provide mental health services to new clients and conduct follow-up consultations for MHPSS clients in the health facilities. In addition to mental health consultations, other services such as psycho-education, support groups and peer support sessions will be conducted by social workers for persons living with mental health conditions and their caregivers. IMC will continue to support health facilities in the management of mental health conditions through skilled psychiatrist nurses and psychologists and support of the psychotropic and neurotropic drug supply.
