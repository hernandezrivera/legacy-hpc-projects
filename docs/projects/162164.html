Project Summary

IOM’s approach promotes self-reliance initiatives through integrated skills development & links to opportunities. Skills & in-kind assistance support enable adolescent youth (boys & girls), & adult Rohingya men & women to maximize their participation in emerging camp set up economic networks & adjoining host communities of informal markets to which they are connected to enhance their well-being.
Food & nutrition security of both refugee & host community remain is a key concern. Vulnerability is shown by the Fill-the Nutrition-Gap Analysis (WFP oct 2019) which indicates 1 of 2 children in immediate host communities are malnourished. IOMs existing programmes support collaborative production initiatives through the provision of tools, fertilizers, improved vegetable seeds to groups of Rohingya & host community households.
A Social Cohesion Trends & Dynamics Baseline conducted by one of IOM’s partner, United Purpose, has attributed rising crime to joblessness among youth. Concerns over threats to safety of Rohingya in certain camps have led to relocation of Rohingya households (United Purpose, June 2019). Similarly, IOM End Line Assessment (October 2019 shows 40% of respondents attribute tensions to gaps in access to livelihoods & life-saving support.
While facilitating continued emergency & life-saving support to the Rohingya, IOM invests in building human capacities of refugees to cope & maximize market environments within camps set ups & immediate co-existing host communities. In host communities, the adoption of market-oriented livelihoods strategies is a dimension IOM seeks to promote sustainable incomes, household capital & market linkages. This will be implemented in the following ways; -Promoting the development of vocational/technical skills for Rohingya in camps to tap into the opportunities for social & economic well-being while fostering peaceful coexistence for Rohignya and Host Communities immediately next to camps.
-Promoting collaborative production initiatives through,
a) Supply of tools, basic production equipment, improved seeds & functional literacy & market skills development of 3700 affected host community households coexisting with Rohingya in camps & in adjoining locality of camps. This will aim to maintain production from homestead farms, fish ponds, & vegetable crops; as well as livestock initiatives.
b) training & distribution of assets & tools to 5,000 Rohingya (mainly 3,000 women & 2000 male) to participate in production of vegetables & basic agriculture produce on available land alongside their host counterpart, as well as small scale assets transfer for livestock micro initiatives;
-Promotion of Peaceful coexistence initiatives:
This will involve the support to 25 networks of 250 women Social Entrepreneurs in camps & in adjoining host communities for promoting sustainable practices. The objective is to enhance Women’s self-help & promote their central role in conservation as primary users of land & natural resources. This initiative will recover eroded lands through adapted tree plantation on 1000 hectares of land. Both Rohingya & host community women compete in gathering of fuel wood, cultivate most of the vegetable products on available lands in & around the camps & therefore are key agents of knowledge sharing towards positive community attitudinal change. This activity builds on lessons of IOM’s Women Income Generation Support Project (WINGS) which has shown that women empowered by exposure to market-oriented trainings often use their skills & creativity to build their own models of economic & social empowerment while contributing to social harmony (IOM WINGS Oct 2019).

Needs

1) 23,000 Rohingyas (13,123 female; 13,449 male) in need of improved skills for self reliance and coping strategies ; This project aims to increase resilience, self-reliance and socio-economic empowerment of Rohingya and adjoining host community adolescent boys, girls and, women, first through enhanced integrated skills training, and the start up support and market linkages to create incomes and sustain well-being. For the Rohingya, the target women and adolescent boys and girls will receive integrated life skills support through the multipurpose learning and innovation centers. The integrated skills approach is a mix of vocational, technical and business skills, with an emphasis on the creation of products and the marketing opportunities through regular r exhibitions and linkages to host community. The improved well being of the target 23,000 Households in camp set up will positively impact the well being of children, boys and girls. 2) Similarly, a target of 12,000 individuals (8,000 female and 4,000 male) of affected populations within and closer to camps ( adolescent boys and girls and women ) will benefit the integrated skills development aimed at assisting them to create small businesses and markets linked to camp economy. These products include small homestead vegetable growing, poultry, food and fish processing. This will directly improve food security and nutrition levels of children of these affected communities. 3) Construction and equipment of 8 learning and innovation centres; Additional learning and innovation centers will be created in 8 camps and adjoining host communities to provide spaces for learning and exhibition of products of both communities . People with disabilities (PWDs) are also catered for according to need and will be trained in beads products, embroidery, soap making and other artifacts the preliminary needs assessment has identified. The pilot center is located in camp 24 and 20 extension and planned construction and equipment of 8 additional in 8, 9, 11, 12, 14, 16, and 22 and 23 where the interaction of host and Rohingya populations are much higher and provide opportunities for communal production and peaceful coexistence. 4) sponsorship of 5 farmer and food markets; 4) sponsorship of 24 product exhibitions with market linkages(-eCommerce platforms and value chains; 5) support 10 Quick Impact projects for community rehabilitation, cash-for-work and Cash-for-exhibition initiatives
