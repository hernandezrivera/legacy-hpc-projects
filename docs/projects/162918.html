Project Summary

The humanitarian response aligns with UNHCR’s strategic directions 2017-2021 by emphasizing protection; accountability to the people we serve; participation; mainstreaming of age, gender and diversity; and increased focus on the promotion of durable solutions. In line with the Strategic Objective of the 2020 Refugee Response Plan, Save the Children will build self-reliance among the CAR, Chadian, and South Sudanese refugees by strengthening community-based structures and integrating livelihoods. As a cross cutting issue, environmental protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle.
Save the Children has articulated the following objective for 2020: To provide basic services and assistance for refugees in South Darfur, South Kordofan and Centeral Darfur, in line with humanitarian standards, and promote durable solutions.
The project will target 66,921 (17052 girls,15740 boys, 17406 women, and 16723 men) individuals (3500 Chadian refugees, 12144 CAR refugees, and 37145 SSR) in need living in 6 localities within 3SCI priority states including Centeral Darfur, South Darfur, and South Kordofan The project will be focusing in provision basic health care services and nutrition assistance to affected people and children with severe and moderate malnutrition, provision of water supply at community and institutional level including construction, and contribute to elimination of open defecation within our operation areas Provision support of UASC, provide support for families of EVIs children, training for newly animators and CBCPNs, establishment and strengthen the CMS. Rehabilitation and construction of basic and secondary schools and provision of teaching and learning materials to targeted schools to support school retention. The project will be focusing also on supporting refugees and hosting communities to restore their livelihoods and integration process.
The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $2,475,000.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, Over 90 per cent of refugees in Sudan are reliant on food assistance, and suffer from a lack of dietary diversity, rations gaps and lack of sufficient income and means to supplement household food stocks. Refugees’ food insecurity has contributed to ‘critical’ and ‘serious’ malnutrition. Refugee camps in Sudan are very congested and are well above capacity, undermining, water, health services and other assistance, especially shelter which further undermines the dignity and physical safety of refugee families. Refugees lack of access to land, productive assets, financial services and other means of production in Sudan, limiting their access to livelihoods, income and education, and undermines their capacity for self-reliance over the long-term. There are approximately 400,000 school aged refugee children living in Sudan, including 308,000 primary school aged (77 per cent) and 92,000 secondary school aged (23 per cent). About 67 per cent of primary-school aged refugee children remain out-of-school, with estimates above 90 per cent out-of-school in more remote refugee-hosting areas outside of camps among South Sudanese refugee children. Over 90 per cent of refugee children eligible for secondary school are out of school. Food insecurity and a lack of dietary diversity within refugee locations compounds health issues. According to recent Standardized Expanded Nutrition Surveys (SENS) for major refugee locations in Kassala, Gedaref, White Nile, South Kordofan, West Kordofan and East Darfur, refugee communities face high rates of anemia among refugee children and pregnant and lactating women, with low coverage of Vitamin A supplementation. Approximately 30 per cent of refugees in Sudan still lack access to primary health care services. Refugees living outside of camps and in urban areas are increasingly unable to afford health services. Approximately 30 per cent of refugees in Sudan still lack access to primary health care services. The majority (88per cent) of refugees and their host communities live in remote and often hard-to-reach areas. Nutrition status in out-of-camp locations are equally concerning, with SENS data from Kordofan states indicating as high as 19.6 per cent GAM and 4 per cent SAM in El Meiram, West Kordofan, and 17.6 per cent GAM and 4.2 per cent in El Leri, South Kordofan. interventions. Among these, 140,374 are children below five years old presenting malnutrition, 91,702 pregnant and lactating women in need of treatment, 32,605 infant and young children and 22,919 other caseloads. There is a need to strengthen quality, access and coverage of community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) programming targeting refugee locations, especially for refugee settlements in out-of-camp areas. 1.15 million refugees living in Sudan, lack of documentation is a key driver of refugee vulnerability, especially for refugees living in out-of-camp settlements, as those without sufficient documentation are unable to access social services where they are available, have limited access formal and stable livelihoods opportunities, and face movement restrictions. About 10,000 boys and girls are living outside of a family environment as either unaccompanied or separated children in addition to 13,000 unaccompanied or separated refugee children (50.15per cent being female) who are in need of protection support. Out of the 7.6 million people in need of WASH assistance, the most affected population groups in Sudan are IDPs, returnees, non-displaced host communities and refugees. Out of the 7.6 million people in need, 5.3 million lack access to improved water sources, 6.2 million people lack access to improved sanitation and 7.5 million people lack access to Hygiene services. 5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project , and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities , prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy frame work. The plan mitigation will through effectiveness of existing disposal mechanism as preventive measure for disease caused be insufficient or unsafe water supplies, poor drainage and uncontrolled diseases , Plan the location of emergency waste disposal sites with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odors. Healthcare and other forms of hazardous waste will be disposed of using appropriate methods including Sachet and cartons of RUTF/RUSF, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent spread of communicable diseases. Water source will be project focused to determine sustainable levels of water use, an early assessment of the presence, quality, and quantity and recharge rate of groundwater sources will be done. Take care to locate latrines downstream of wells, at least 30m from groundwater sources and at least 1.5m above the water table as sanitation measures. Carefully will consider location of shelter and new building to minimize pollution and damage to sensitive natural environment. Appropriate storage of food supplies to Protect health of workers and food consumer (e.g. tablets used for fumigation) and food provision will have appropriate soilid waste management plan. Availability of Standards tools and guidelines, Standards, tools and guidance documents will be available to assist humanitarian responders in managing environmental impacts and risks.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The preposition complaint and feedback mechanisms will be used to gauge the satisfaction level of beneficiaries against services provided by SC. Accountability phone is standing by to receive feedback and complaints from the project beneficiaries and stakeholders, for beneficiaries without phones they report to a community focal point. The call-in number will be printed on posters and displayed at service outlet centers to be easily access to callers when interested to deliver their feedback. The MEAL team alongside with program staff will conduct focus group discussions and community meetings to share project information with direct and indirect project beneficiaries and collect their views. All feedback and complaints received will be logged into the Complaints and Response system, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. To ensure accountability to the affected community, SC will consult communities, government authorities and other relevant partners in the design of this project. The consultation with communities will involve both women and men as well as children. The accountability mechanism will provide opportunity to the affected population to raise their concerns and claim their rights. The project considers conflict sensitivity by understanding the situation, examining how each activity may influence the conflict and work to maximize the positive and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions.
