Project Summary

WFP will provide unconditional food assistance to the most food insecure, crisis-affected populations. Life-saving general food assistance (in-kind food and cash-based transfers) will be delivered to IDPs, returnees, and host populations in North-East Nigeria (Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa States). Early recovery and resilience to shocks will be promoted through asset creation and preservation and increased livelihood opportunities. Joint multisectoral assessments and Emergency Food Security Analyses will be conducted regularly to monitor the food security, and market situation in crisis-affected states.
A gender-transformative approach that meets the needs of women, men, girls, and boys will be adopted. WFP will mainstream protection throughout its work, supported by a solid understanding of the operational context and protection analysis. Training and capacity building of staff and partners in the protection and humanitarian principles will include on-the-job coaching. WFP will ensure that affected populations (women and men) will be consulted throughout the programme cycle; community feedback mechanisms will be tailored to their preferred means of communication. Beneficiaries will continue to be empowered with knowledge of their food assistance entitlements and their rights, including WFP’s zero-tolerance policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be embedded in M&E tools, community feedback mechanisms, and training of partners and staff.
WFP will continue the collection of key information on bene ciaries and assistance provided as part of its M&E activities. Monthly monitoring will include distribution and activity site management; beneficiary and entitlement verification; and ensuring adequate protection and gender measures. Lessons will be regularly drawn from monthly process and biannual outcome monitoring and reporting, and periodic reviews and evaluation to inform strategic and operational decisions.
WFP will further consolidate partnerships to enhance national and state-level emergency preparedness and response systems, including food security and nutrition assessment and monitoring, early warning, protection, gender analysis, targeting and registration processes, safety nets, cash-based transfer capacities, and supply chain.
Where security and access to land allow, increasing livelihood activities will complement food assistance to support access to productive assets, income-generating activities, and self-reliance in the long term. Assistance will be coordinated with government distributions to ensure food assistance is complimentary with other ongoing assistance from other clusters with no gaps.

1 - Needs

The persistent and widespread humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria continues to cause repeated displacement, protection concerns, and unrelenting food and nutrition insecurity. Millions of children, women, and men bear the brunt of the conflict, which has decimated livelihoods, crops, livestock, infrastructure, education, and basic services. According to the October 2024, CH, towards the upcoming 2025 lean season (June to August), 33.1 million people are projected to be food insecure. Lingering conflict, widespread flooding, and rising prices of food and non-food items are the contributing factors behind the country's rising food insecurity situation. In the BAY states, the number will increase to around 5 million people who will need food assistance in the 2025 lean season, of which 218,958 will be in the ‘emergency phase’ (Phase 4). About 456,544 food-insecure people are IDPs, of which 84,681 are in Phase 4. When compared to the March 2024 analysis, the overall population of food-insecure people in BAY states has increased by approximately 200,000, from 4.8 million. The continued deterioration in the food security situation is the result of compounded vulnerabilities and inadequate food consumption associated with increased prices of staple food commodities and a difficult macroeconomic environment, which diminishes the purchasing power of market-reliant households. Notwithstanding, the continued assistance by the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community to deliver food and nutrition assistance, livelihood support, access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets, and livelihood activities remains limited. Given the current levels of food and nutrition insecurity, below-average crop production disrupted livelihoods, and high staple food prices, the food security, and nutrition situation remain extremely fragile. Large populations in the northeast depend on humanitarian assistance to meet their food and nutrition needs. WFP regularly conducts protection analysis including Do No Harm, protection risk assessments, and protection monitoring within the context of the provision of food assistance. Protection and gender-specific questions have also been integrated into vulnerability food assessment tools and rapid intersectoral assessments to ensure that protection and gender are captured in food security assessments. Depending on the context, one or more of the following targeting criteria can be considered in agreement with the communities: heads of household with a disability; households headed by older people with no support; child-headed households, separated and unaccompanied children; heads of the household taking care of a large number of children or relatives; households headed by females with no support; households headed by widows with no livelihood source/support; households with a large number of dependents; households with no livelihood activity/source; households reliant mainly on risky income-generating activities such as firewood gathering; newly displaced individuals (less than 3 to 6 months) residing in a camp and not integrated with communities; households headed by people who are chronically sick; households found to be adopting negative coping strategies such as beggars (when considered by the community as an abnormal consequence of shock).

2 - Activities

In 2023, WFP launched its second Country Strategic Plan (CSP 2.0) to implement humanitarian interventions. The CSP targets addressing underlying root causes of food and nutrition insecurity while simultaneously meeting humanitarian needs and strengthening resilience, thus reducing risks. In 2025, it is planned under food security, to reach up to 2,244,000 beneficiaries with GFD (in-kind food, CBT) in the BAY States. Food assistance will be maximized from the beginning of the year up to the lean season and will decrease during the last quarter of the year (post-lean season and harvest period). Supporting the technical capacity of the federal, state, and local actors in Information Management Systems (IMS), vulnerability assessment and mapping, M&E, safety net management, supply chains, nutrition, and Emergency Preparedness and Response will contribute to FSS objectives 1, 2, 3. To continue focused support to the most vulnerable conflict-affected communities and LGAs, WFP leverages evidence from the Cadre Harmonise and other supporting information such as the ENA and market and livelihood assessment findings. Multiple factors are considered with priority given to the most food-insecure LGAs (in CH Phase 3 and above), including the most vulnerable people living in camps and entirely dependent on WFP support, and accessibility and coverage by partners. The criteria below are used to classify beneficiaries that receive lifesaving and livelihood support in order of priority: IDPs beneficiaries living in camps; newly arrived displaced people; vulnerable IDPs with local communities’ returnees and host communities. WFP will provide livelihood and income-generating support to IDPs and host communities using food assistance for assets (in-kind food and cash-based transfers). The activities undertaken shall build resilience and be shock-responsive in nature. The activity selection process, targeting and prioritization criteria, and transfer modality selection ensure social cohesion aligned with the “do-no-harm” principle. The selection of both IDPs and host communities to receive resilience-building shock-responsive support assures overall community integration in the end. The selection of activities and transfer modalities are informed by operational context, level of vulnerability, and beneficiary choice. Gender and protection analysis also informs the abovementioned selection/approach. The appropriate geographical targeting is also informed through the FSS and the CH results, complemented by WFP food security assessments and IOM DTM. Targeting and prioritization of beneficiaries and activities are collectively derived through the participation of Partners and the communities to avoid duplications and minimize assistance gaps. WFP collaborates with IOM through a data-sharing agreement that assures a harmonized registration approach via electronic data exchange.

The project outcome indicators are informed by the feedback collected. WFP accountability to affected populations is a key channel employed to minimize and establish measures to mitigate any potential risk and increase awareness of the purpose of the interventions, and the intended recipients. To enhance consultation and provision of information WFP and its partners establish community-based project management committees that provide a two-way channel of communication between WFP and affected populations.
