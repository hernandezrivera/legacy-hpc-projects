Project Summary

Women Aid Vision proposes an Education in Emergencies project that will respond and provide improved access to safe, inclusive and lifesaving education among 2,935 vulnerable displaced and host population 935 male and 2000 female in Rumbek North (Madol,Malueeth and Maper payams) and Cueibet (Citcok,Duony and Pagor payams) counties by improving the quality and equitable access to education to ensure continuity of relevant education services among targeted children/ youth, strengthening the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth.
To achieve this WAV proposes scaling up already achieved activities through current UNICEF and NORAD education funding and sustaining the momentum of activities by establishment/ rehabilitation of 10 learning spaces/ classrooms, provision of school meals, establishment/ rehabilitation of 10 gender disaggregated latrine facilities equipped with 12 hand washing facilities, prepositioning through UNICEF/ Cluster pipeline and provision of scholastic learning and teaching materials/ EiE kits, capacity building and trainings of 60 teachers and volunteers, trainings which will encompass community focal points and authorities on psychosocial support, trainings and equipping of SMCs, PTAs on school governance and management..
WAV has an office in Rumbek town with adequate and experienced staff, with existing strong grassroot networks, that will scale up current UNICEF EiE programming and sustain momentum of activities.

1 - Needs

IPC indicates the effects of Covid-19,Inter and Intra communal conflicts, cattle rustling, food insecurity levels, and the impact of flooding on livelihoods as key drivers in exacerbating an already poor economic environment with 9.3 million people in need in South Sudan comprising of 64% or 5.4 million children (0-17 years). Rumbek North in (Madol,Malueeth and Maper payams) and Cueibet in (Citcok,Duony and Pagor payams)Counties of Lake state now have an extra burden; with many schools in the Counties affected and damaged by the floods and violence which has been recurrent and some of them occupied by IDPs. This has delayed the re-opening of schools in many areas is limited and much of the service infrastructure was damaged, destroyed or closed in 2021.During an inter-agency IRNA in Rumbek North and Cueibet Counties in November 2021 found out that the floods had affected upwards with 12940 people being displaced and 9840 in Cueibet counties . Schools were found to be occupied by the displaced population as well as livestock. Most Teaching and Learning materials were completely damaged and Learning infrastructure including Temporary learning Spaces and latrines had collapsed. WAV also conducted a rapid school-based WASH assessment in January 2022 and established that 5030 children (53% girls) in 8 schools of Rumbek North and 4,730 children (40% girls) in 6 schools of Cueibet lack adequate TLS and WASH structures for schools to reopen. WAV basic assessments on Children living with disabilities found out that of the forms of disabilities existing in schools, 30% of the disabled children are physically disabled; 20% said the children are blind while the smallest proportion of 10% of the teachers said children are deaf. WAV Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2022, therefore, seeks to increase equitable access to quality education through participation of 10000 vulnerable children and youth aged 3-17 years (4500 boys & 5500 girls) in Rumbek North and Cueibet counties improve quality of teaching and learning; bridge the gender gap in access to education; improve access to quality education for 2000 (1500 girls and 500 boys) children living with disabilities; and improve management of education service delivery in Rumbek North and Cueibet. This target targets 10000 of Education Cluster’s 2022.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All feedback regarding project-related matters will provide self-reflection and providing actionable plans to ensure the project is adjusted to suit recommendations provided during consultation sessions. Feedback obtained from project activities will be consolidated and analyzed at the state and Juba level offices and feedback into the program unit used to realign activities to planned standard.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Prior to this application participatory assessment through focused group discussion (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) was conducted in Rumbek North and Cueibet counties with 30 participants involved drawn from different ages ,gender and diversities to ensure there is commitment in placing accountability to people affected by disaster and conflict at the core of its emergency policy and practice, from preparedness and the onset of an emergency throughout the phases of the programme cycle. The current education concerns being faced by the vulnerable marginalized groups (child headed HHS and children living with disability) patterns and and EiE risk factors have been identified through beneficiary feedback mechanism. WAV will continue to ensure that this project utilizes a people-centered approach, sensitive to the dignity of all human beings, the varying needs of different segments within the community, and the importance of ensuring that women, men, girls and boys can equally access and benefit from education services provided. During the selection of the beneficiaries there will be community consultative meetings to identify the vulnerable beneficiaries to be considered for support with education services. WAV will follow participatory assessments to identify, verify and profile targeted beneficiaries. The selection criteria will prioritize vulnerable children, including children living with disability ,IDPs, host communities and child headed HHs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will Include GBV mitigation as part of staff training & conduct. The project will Identify and minimize the risk of harm to children and adults from staff. In order to provide beneficiaries with information about PSEA, all training will incorporate elements of PSEA, indicating the engagement levels of the project staff with the community, reporting avenues available, their rights to benefit without being compromised, and more. The beneficiaries will be reminded that WAV staff interactions with children or with adult beneficiaries, or with personal data about such persons, will comply with the attached WAV's Safeguarding Behaviour Protocols. - Any incidents of harm or risk of harm to children or to adult beneficiaries will be reported immediately through channels that will be explained and demonstrated to the community. - Beneficiaries will be reminded of all issues related to PSEA including reported cases being treated with utmost confidentiality; investigated and action taken. All actions taken will be discussed with the complainant after a specified time (feedback).

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

WAV feedback mechanism will be used for this project to guide in service provision to the beneficiaries .A complaint mechanism desk will be set up for information collection at the learning spaces and in classrooms where different opinions will be collected and solutions generated.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

This project will contribute to outcome 2 where GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response will be rolled out in the 3 payams of Cueibet and 3 payams of Rumbek North where the case workers will be trained on comprehensive case management to ensure they provide 1st Aid PSS where they will be supporting the schools to identify the cases that will need the service . Through the existing community protection committees and the case workers WAV will ensure this 2 structures conduct awareness raising on available GBV services in schools and dissemination of referral pathway to ensure there is effective referrals.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The 2 Lakes state counties of Cueibet and Rumbek North have been prone to floods between the months of October and November this project will ensure we engage other stakeholders in the private sectors and government to come up with long term interventions such as construction of dykes and also strengthen the early warning systems to ensure we reduce the affected population by the floods to 25%.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The current project has prioritized Rumbek North and Cueibet counties which are currently in severity classification 5 during the implementation of this project WAV will ensure the number of people in IPC 5 reduces by ensuring there is establishment of safe learning schools where there will be recreation and skill building activities with a component of individual 1st Aid PSS. There will also be trainings of the PTA's and SMC's on governance and school management done at the payam levels to ensure there is an increase in enrolment and pupil retention in schools. WAV will rehabilitate the damaged sanitation facilities to provide a clean and conducive environment for the learners .All this interventions will result in reduction of people in IPC 5.
