Project Summary

This project is an extension of a previous project that started the last two years aiming at integrating primary health care, community health, and mental health services.
The Qatar Red Crescent provides primary health care services with integrated psychosocial support services and community health services within 9 primary health care centers in Syria. Health services include reproductive health, maternal and child care, IMCI, communicable and NCD, reproductive health counseling, outreach community health rising awareness services, mhGAP and PSS scalable psychological intervention; PM+, routine immunization, medications, internal and specialized clinics such as dental, ophthalmology, and this is classified as comprehensive health care centers Level 4, which provides BEmOC services such as delivery and temporary observation (inpatient) and other basic services and life-saving, which aims to stabilize the health system inside Syria and protect it from collapse during the protracted crisis. During this project, QRCS aims to target 402,479 beneficiaries including IDPs and host communities as well as the project will continue to target women, men, boys, and girls. Locations of the project in Idleb governorate close to Turkish-Syrian borders and rural Aleppo.

Monitoring Plan
The MEAL Staff dedicated to this project in cooperation with the health specialists in QRCS will implement the following activities throughout the project life cycle:
At the beginning of the project:
1- Conducting a baseline survey to determine the current related Health situation in the targeted communities before the intervention of the project, the base-line findings well be used as a benchmark to be compared with the endline findings to measure the improvement and Impact achieved (taking in consideration the last achievements of the project components as they are ongoing activities).
• Conducting spot-check visits to the project sites (PHCs and community centers) to verify the readiness of the facilities and that they are fully supplied with the necessary equipment and supplies in compliance with the established standards and ready to deliver the planned services.
During the project implementation:
• Monitoring all the services provided by the project according to the type of service and evaluating them in terms of quality and effectiveness. This will include conducting regular visits to the project sites and meeting randomly selected samples of beneficiaries and measuring their satisfaction with the services provided, the findings of this activity will be triangulated with the direct observation findings of the field MEAL officers and FGDs + KIIs with the other stakeholders in the targeted communities which ensure the credibility of information.
• Maintaining compliance and feedback mechanisms will increase the involvement of beneficiaries in the project implementations and enhance community engagement and accountability toward the affected populations.
At the end of the project:
• Conducting the end-line and final evaluation of the whole project to measure the achievements and overall impact, then documenting the lessons learned and best practices to contribute to the organizational learning and knowledge.

Implementing Partners

This project will implemented by Qatar Red Crescent Society (QRCS).
QRCS was established in Doha, Qatar in 1979 as a humanitarian organization that aims to assist and empower vulnerable individuals and communities without partiality or discrimination. In 1981, QRCS became a member of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement, which consists of the International Federation of the Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) in Geneva, the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), and 190 National Societies. Accordingly, QRCS adopts the Movement’s universal principles as a guiding framework for all its activities whereby our slogan, Saving Lives and Preserving Dignity, reflects our commitment to the universal principles of humanitarian work.
As the humanitarian crises in Syria escalated, QRCS sought a comprehensive response to the emerging humanitarian needs. To that end, we established 4 Missions in Jordan, Lebanon, Iraq and Turkey. Our Missions in Jordan, Lebanon and Iraq focus on Syrian refugees in these host countries; whereas Turkey Mission focuses on cross-border activities to relief IDPs inside Syria.
QRCS Turkey Mission was established in 2012 and operates through a main Syria Office located in Ad-Dana and branch offices inside Syria in Idleb, Hama, Homs and Deraa. Our activities inside Syria are under two main departments; the Health Department and the Programmes’ Department.
Our health programme is well-established in various locations within Syria and seeks to builds partnerships with existing governance structures to facilitate knowledge transfer, create ownership, enhance social participation, improve access to the most vulnerable areas and groups, and increase the efficiency of response. Hence, all our Programmes are developed and implemented with local partners, e.g. Syrian NGOs, Local Councils. This approach has enriched our local knowledge and ability to assess humanitarian response needs and methods which resulted in local acceptance and trust of QRCS activities within Syria. It consists of the following units:
 Primary Health Care Programme
 Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Program
 Health Preparedness and Emergency Response Programme
 Community Health Programme
 Third-Party Monitoring Programme on immunization campaigns
QRCS is actively engaged in international coordination mechanisms in Turkey hub; specifically, we are active members of the following clusters: health, nutrition, FSL, CCCM, WASH, ER, Shelter, and Protection. We are also on various advisory boards and technical review committees. QRCS is currently a member of the Partnership Advisory Group that advocates and promotes sound partnership approaches between international and local agencies in Turkey hub to contribute to a strong Syrian civil society capable of becoming a strong vehicle of transition to post-conflict Syria as well as QRCS is active member in Strategic Advisory Group (SAG) in health and nutrition clusters from 2017 until now.
QRCS has received funding via UNOCHA THF, WHO, and GIZ since 2015 for 15 projects directly and 3 as implementing partner of main IP. 14 of the 15 projects have been successfully completed and, and 13 have been audited by the donor.
Furthermore, QRCS is currently implementing integrated health services into primary health care and community centers project in northern in rural of Idleb and Aleppo. Through our project, we are adopting a comprehensive approach that incorporates health care workers, mhGAP doctors, data entries, community health workers, field supervisor, CHWs team leaders, medical and nutritional supplies, medications, detection & referral of illness, malnourished and Mental health people as well as training for health workers and community health workers at PHC level, in addition to supporting referral to secondary and services. 4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, the Provision of health services continues to be disrupted by ongoing hostilities. Attacks on health have rendered 27% of primary health facilities in Syria is not functional and 21% partially functional, while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained and predictable access to populations in need. Sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need across the country. An estimated 15% of deliveries and 15% of newborns will require life-saving emergency intervention. Mothers need outreach support to properly care for the newborn at home, including feeding practices for infants and young children; Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are critical: prolonged exposure to conflict is believed to increase the prevalence of mental health conditions above the WHO global prevalence of 15%. however, the infrastructure and referral system that remains functional after close to eight years of crisis is inadequate to cope with existing needs. Recent MSNA Household Survey, 2019, indicates the need for improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs, particularly for non-communicable diseases (NCDs), which are estimated to account for 45% of all deaths. Over the last year, QRCS has been able to provide a package of essential emergency assistance to vulnerable people in Syria and then demonstrated its capacity for expansion and replication of service provision in vulnerable districts. QRCS supported 513,829 primary healthcare consultations. In fact, as QRCS is one of the humanitarian actors able to effectively provide essential services through integrating maternal, child health and MHPSS services with engaging the community as a stronger approach to enhance health system, achieve UHC and improve health status in the NW Syria despite the unstable political and security environment, vulnerable groups outside of QRCS target districts seek services at QRCS' support PHCs in Idleb& rural Aleppo.

5 - Implementation of activities

Support 9 Health Facilities in targeted areas to provide Quality of Primary health care services as an integrated approach as followings: Maternal and Child health services: Improve the quality of primary health care services to accelerate reductions in the morbidity and mortality of men and women, boys, and girls in all clinics in the facilities. Providing free basic medications to patients in the center: Delivering supplies including pharmaceuticals, commodities, and supplies & Monitoring of supplies distributed. Monitoring and evaluation of the services provided through field visits. M&E field staff interviews with beneficiaries. Activating referral system from community health program and PHCs. Provide basic reproductive health services including family planning, pregnant care, safe delivery, and prevent sexually transmitted diseases. Provide children with suitable health services including integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI). Providing basic services for the treatment of non-communicable diseases and chronic diseases Strengthen Health information system: reporting by using DHiS2 on monthly basis. MHPSS Services: Enhance access to improved and quality MHPSS interventions at PHC level by trained doctors and psychosocial workers. Adapt and disseminate MHPSS standard protocols to ensure minimum standards are achieved at focused PHC services and its integration with existing community activities. No-specialized health professionals receive training and supervision in the WHO mhGAP Program Intervention Guidelines. Psychosocial workers receive training and supervision in the WHO PSW Standardized Training Manual and Problem Management Plus (PM+). Health service providers will receive training on RMNCH-NCD-MHPSS topics. Community-based services: Promote awareness and practices of basic community health topics regarding WHO - TWG guidelines and manual. QRCS will continue to coordinate with other health partners through the Health Cluster.

Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1. ensure training packages are in line with child protection minimum standards and consider remotely managed context; 2. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules; 3. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision; 4. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. 2-Protection risk: 1. Lack of commitment to integrating and enforcing risk mitigation measures; 2 lack of accountability; 3. Sectors may put survivors at risk if they do not follow correct procedures Likelihood: Medium. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for the code of conduct and PSEA.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Access to targeted locations and beneficiaries. - Continued funds based on an approved plan. - The security situation does not deteriorate. - Acceptance and cooperation by stakeholders. 1-Targeting centers by direct shelling or changing areas of influence and the multiplicity of military bodies controlling. Severity: High. Mitigating measures are taken: Assessment of the safest and least vulnerable areas. Training all workers on first aid and evacuation of wounded. Ensure that centers are equipped with full safety supplies such as fire extinguishers, first aid kit, etc. 2-Limited availability of qualified personnel that limits the ability of centers to provide services. Severity: High. Mitigating measures are taken: To provide the program with a comprehensive assessment of the current availability of staff, doctors and staff in and around the centers to ensure a realistic, feasible and appropriate management and financial proposal. 3-Limited implementation of project outputs and objectives technically. Severity: Average. Mitigating measures are taken: Training materials and executive protocols that emphasize the need to monitor and record observations in the health situation affecting women, men, boys, and girls. The Qatar Red Crescent will, as far as possible, request or advocate the use of data and information disaggregated by sex and age to monitor variables in access to health services that disproportionately affect one or more gender groups or age groups
