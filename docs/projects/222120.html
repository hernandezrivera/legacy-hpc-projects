Project Summary

WASH situation in a country plays a critical role in the well being of the community and poor water sanitation and hygiene situation can lead to adverse effects in the community. Contaminated water is a major cause of water borne diseases that contribute to malnutrition and other diseases like cholera and acute watery diarrhea.
The situation of WASH in South Sudan has remained precarious with over 70% of the population in the country lacking key WASH services and very few have access to safe and clean water for drinking and for domestic use to the SPHERE standards. This has led to sporadic disease outbreaks in the country that can be attributed to contaminated water. Currently the country is experiencing a cholera outbreak and that is directly associated with contaminated water and according to WHO IDSR bulleting week 45 2024, over 33 people have lost due to the Cholera Outbreak in Renk County.
The WASH infrastructure has remained very poor primarily due to years of conflict and other factors like flooding and displacements that has contributed significantly to the destruction of WASH facilities. Hand washing practices and other hygienic activities are not well practiced among the communities and open defecation is practiced in over 80% of the population in South Sudan (UNICEF 2023).
Acute food insecurity has also affected the delivery of WASH services with communities prioritizing food rather than WASH services.
This project will therefore address the challenges faced by the communities in 5 counties of Rubkona, Aweil East, Kapoeta east, Bor South and Pibor with WASH services.
Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) will offer integrated wash services by ensuring that rehabilitation of water points and sanitation is done after safety audits to ensure that women and girls are safe to get this very essential commodity. Community participation in identification of the facilities that will be rehabilitated will be done to ensure that communities have full ownership of the project activities.
The organization will create awareness on WASH services though robust community engagement using the human centered design that focuses on community led interventions that will reduce harmful practices like open defecation and contamination of waterpoints. This will be done in collaboration with hygiene promotors and also focusing on involvement of persons with disabilities in all the project activities to ensure inclusivity.
Water management committees will be formed and trained to ensure that they are able to support the sustainability of the water points and help the communities maintain the rehabilitated waterpoints. This will include training of the water pump mechanics at the community level to support repairs of hand pumps and boreholes.
CRSF will also train the health workers on minimum WASH package activities to ensure that they are able to support the communities with key WASH messages thus improving hygienic practices and thus reduction of water borne infections in the community.
Distribution of WASH NFIs will be done in the communities that are most vulnerable with CRSF setting the eligibility criteria depending on the vulnerability and ensuring that the elderly, children and mothers with malnutrition, persons with disabilities, displaced persons, children headed households are given priority. Accountability to affected population committees will also be formed to ensure that community representation is adequate during the project implementation.
This project will target 11000 beneficiaries in 5 counties. The beneficiaries will be segregated by age and gender with 3000 male, 3500 female, 2000 boys and 2500 girls targeted by this project. 1550 people of all the project beneficiaries will be persons with disabilities.

1. Needs

South Sudan Communities have severe limitations in accessing water with only about 50% of the population in South Sudan has access to safe drinking water to the required SPHERE standards and rural areas are particularly underserved. Many people rely on unprotected water sources, which are often contaminated due to open defecation and other factors.Ongoing conflict in Sudan and some internal conflicts and displacement exacerbate water access issues, especially in areas where refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs) are concentrated. Access to functioning water supply systems in such areas is often disrupted. The Sudan crisis has led to influx of returnees and refugees thus constraining the already very limited WASH facilities in the countryKapoeta East County face natural water scarcity, especially during the dry season, making it difficult to maintain a reliable water supply. This affects the quality and quantity of water available for communities to use and thus exposing the communities to unsafe water for drinking and for domestic use.According to UNICEF only 10% of the population have access to improved sanitation and therefore 90% population lacks access to improved sanitation facilities (latrines or toilets), which is one of the primary causes of public health problems like diarrheal diseases. Currently South Sudan is experiencing an active Cholera outbreak that can be attributed to poor WASH situation (WHO 2024). Many areas still practice open defecation due to a lack of latrines, particularly in rural and conflict-affected regions. This contributes to the spread of diseases and poor hygiene.There is very poor waste disposal mechanisms and even in urban areas, waste disposal systems are often inadequate, with limited capacity for managing waste in a way that prevents contamination of water sources.Hygiene behaviours, such as handwashing with soap, are not widely practiced, partly due to cultural factors and the lack of infrastructure to support hygiene promotion efforts. Poor hygiene practices contribute to high rates of waterborne diseases such as cholera, dysentery, and typhoid, which are common in areas with poor WASH infrastructure. This has been a major cause of morbidity and mortality in south Sudan and especially to children under 5 years.South Sudan’s WASH infrastructure is underdeveloped and has been severely impacted by conflict. Many water pumps and sanitation facilities are broken or require rehabilitation. The local authorities capacity to deliver and maintain WASH services is limited, both in terms of technical expertise and financial resources. Rural and hard-to-reach areas often receive less attention in terms of WASH interventions, leaving populations without access to basic services.South Sudan is vulnerable to both flooding and droughts, which further complicates water access and sanitation efforts. Floods often damage water sources and sanitation infrastructure, while droughts can reduce water availability. According to the flood snapshot November 2024, over 1.4 million people have been affected by flooding on over 22 counties. (Flood Snap shot Nov 2024)Poor access to clean water and sanitation exacerbates the spread of diseases that contribute to malnutrition, particularly in children under 5. According to IPC Nov 2024, more than 2.1 million people are in emergency food insecurity calling for efforts to support access to improved WASH facilities thus reducing water borne infection to the already vulnerable populations.

2. Project activities

1. Integrate WASH and Nutrition In the counties which are having High GAM rates and highly food insecure by doing the following activities:• Rehabilitation of water points in the Nutrition centers• Hygiene promotion for caretakers of children admitted with SAM and MAM in Nutrition facilities• Distribution of WASH NFIs and Hygiene kits to couples (Caregiver/child) admitted with SAM and MAM in nutrition centers.• Distribution of water treatment commodities to caregivers of children admitted with SAM and MAM in the nutrition centers.2. Integrate WASH in health facilities in the five counties and building resilience to flood affected populations by doing the following activities• Rehabilitation of semi-permanent water points after safety audits in health facilities and in the community affected by floods.• Formation and training of water management committees that will support resilience building and sustainability of the water points• Identification and training of water pump mechanics at community level for sustainable repair of broken down water points.• Rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in health facilities and communities affected by floods• Hygiene promotion in the communities with key focus on handwashing and reduction of open defecation.• Training health workers on minimum WASH activity package activities to give then knowledge on how to support WASH activities in the health facilities and nutrition centers.3. Mitigation of gender based violence through WASH interventions by doing the following activities• Safety audits before rehabilitation of latrines and water points.• Distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable and affected women and girls.• Safe distribution of WASH NFIs by ensuring early distribution and taking the distribution sites as near to the communities as possible.
