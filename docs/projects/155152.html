Project Summary

This project aims to provide Rohingya refugees with shelter and NFI support. Main focus will be on transitional shelter e.g. shifting from emergency shelters to more stable and durable shelters. Furthermore, for host community this project will focus on providing semi pucca housing and improving road infrastructure.

Needs

Under the leadership of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), the Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) Sector needs analysis and strategy has been developed, also in coordination with other Government counterparts at various levels to ensure that shelter approaches are informed and supported. The Shelter Technical Committee, established and led by RRRC, which is inclusive of the Deputy Commissioner’s Office, reviews strategy, interventions and evolving shelter issues. FDMN are residing in makeshift shelters that are severely below basic humanitarian standards, overcrowded, and will not withstand seasonal climatic conditions. The low quality of the shelters and poor terrain as well as the lack of privacy within these shelters has a serious impact on the physical and psychological well-being of the refugees, and especially for women, children and older FDMN and FDMN with disabilities. Upgrades to shelters and site improvements are urgently required prior to the next monsoon and cyclone season. Space in settlements is critically limited. Lacking adequate land, FDMN have built shelters on hills which are at risk of landslides and in areas prone to flooding. The sector’s joint needs assessment revealed that 40% of surveyed people in host communities paid rent for land or houses. The same assessment revealed that essential household needs vary considerably according to distributions to date and geographical location of HHs. FDMN are requesting support with replenishment of items which have been distributed before as well as some non-typical NFIs. Clothes, lighting, stoves and kitchen sets are amongst those commonly requested items. Until recently, the Government of Bangladesh restricted construction of semi-permanent shelters in refugee camps and settlements. As makeshift shelters and upgrades have a limited lifespan and do not meet standards to withstand high wind events, there is a need to adopt more viable solutions. Since starting we along with other organization provide Upgrade shelter support but those are not durable and also not addressing the privacy and security. Considering the above we are proposing to provide Semi Pucca House at Host community, Construction of Road including maintenance and Conatruction of Bridge/ Culvert with in the year 2019 under the guidance of UNHCR Shelter Team and ISCG. Fuel support is also needed for the FDMN due to scarcity of fire wood in the locality. As per Need Assessment report of CARE, 1st Choice 38% respondent said that they need kitchen item, then 28% with blanket and 14% with other materials. As per Joint Need Assessment Report of ISCG, there is also concern that there are reduced job opportunities and the unskilled wage rate has decreased with additional labour available in the market. In general host communities do not consider themselves economically better off than the refugees and many (over 20%) are living below the poverty line. Current estimation is 100,000 refugees for Teknaf and Ukhia HC. The needs of the long established Rohingya are assumed as similar to those of the Bangladeshi host communities.

Activities or outputs

"Onward, emergency Shelter/NFI preparedness and response remains high priority. The coastal area of Bangladesh is susceptible to cyclones. The Cox’s Bazar district is situated by the Bay of Bengal has witnessed devastating cyclones over the past decades. The 2018 response witnessed wind gust speeds that did not exceed 30 km/hr while wind gust speeds reached 65, 130, and 148 km/hr in the years 2017, 2016, and 2015 respectively. The existing shelters in camps, excluding transitional and mid-term shelters, are estimated to resist wind speeds up to 40 km/hr. Bamboo provided in upgrade shelter kits in 2018 are deteriorating, further reducing the overall strength of shelters.For the small number of refugee households that are relocated to newly developed camps or to areas of existing camps that are redeveloped and therefore less congested and able to meet the covered living space standard, mid-term shelters will be constructed. The mid-term shelters will be part of a structured site plan. Due to the nature of redeveloped areas, the mid-term shelter option will often require skilled labor to carry out the structural elements of the shelter, which may include raised floors or stilts. The mid-term shelter is not a final product yet one step closer to reaching a “durable home/shelter”. Note that despite their differing modalities, both transitional shelter assistance and mid-term shelter approaches offer a vast increase in shelter durability and will undoubtedly contribute to reduced suffering, disaster risk reduction, and enhanced protection, dignity, and safety. The Shelter and Site Management partners are required to innovate, shelter designs and site plans that adhere to international standards while minimizing shelter plot shifting and community disruption. Considering RDD existing shelters in communal sites/host communities will be upgraded and surrounding site conditions will be improved building Transitional Shelter, mobilisation of skilled/non-skilled labours, and building DRR capacity in the refugee/host communities providing training session for them. NFI – Voucher and Cash: The support of cash and vouchers in lieu of in-kind NFI is evident amongst local authorities. LPG interventions and solar light home systems will continue to be provided in-kind as the market is not yet mature to offer items with the required specifications and quantity. BRAC recognizes that this influx of Rohingya population had an adverse impact in the lives of host community of Cox’s Bazar, exacerbating the pre-existing challenges of poor health, nutrition, and poverty status. In addition to this, the surge humanitarian response, largely focused on the new influx of Rohingyas, created further dissatisfaction and creating tension between host and Rohingya community. To address this tension and address critical needs, BRAC will also provide targeted non-food items to the most vulnerable households in the host community. The service provisions for the host community- • NFI kits to 10,000 household include: culturally-appropriate clothes, bedding, solar lamps, water containers, kitchen materials, etc. (5 persons per HH) = 50,000 individuals • Provide DRR Training to 48,000 Community individuals to save their life and asset • Construction 400 Transitional Shelter for FDMN (5 persons for each HH) =2,000 individuals • Construction/reconstruction of 900 Semi Pucca House for Host community (5 persons for each HH) =4,500 individuals • Construction/Re-construction /Maintenance of road 2,000 meter at Host community• Construction/Re-construction of 200 meter pucca Bridge at Host community In the next phase, BRAC will incrementally move towards the provision of the durable shelter solution from temporary shelters, which will last for years than months, for the Rohingya community.
