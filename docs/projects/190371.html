Project Summary

The conflict in Tigray that erupted on November 24, 2020 has been driving displacement, disruption of services, destruction of infrastructure, loss of livelihoods and household assets. The most life-threatening consequences of the crisis include: widespread displacement and protection threats, severe food insecurity, heightened exposure to deadly disease outbreaks, and increased risk of malnutrition. Women and girls are at heightened risk of gender-based violence (GBV), as indicated in the UNFPA Tigray Crisis Preparedness and Response Plan, Tigray. Since the beginning of this protracted conflict between the federal and regional forces in Tigray more than 5 million people have been displaced across the three regions of Tigray, Amhara and Afar with thousands fleeing to surrounding countries like Sudan. UNOCHA’s February 4, 2022 update on Northern Ethiopia humanitarian situation indicated that 695 boys and 772 girls under the age of 17, as well as 4,685 women, and 3,044 men respectively, engaged through awareness raising and messaging for GBV prevention and risk mitigation in Tigray. Moreover, the same report indicates that more than 5,412 children, including 2,671 girls received mental health and psychosocial service in Mekelle, Enderta, Maichew, Adigrat, Adwa, Axum and Shire. At least 1,262 women and 20 men also received mental health and psychosocial support. However, UNOCHA’s report indicated that provisions of protection services at IDP sites across northern Ethiopia, including child protection, gender-based violence (GBV) and Sexual GBV (SGBV) prevention and response, and mental and psychosocial support have not yet been fulfilled and were identified as gaps. UNFPA December 2021 Situation Report2 states that “data on the prevalence of sexual and gender-based violence across Northern Ethiopia is limited due to the absence of Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GVBIMS), fears of stigma and retaliation, as well as lack of access to comprehensive care, the various reports suggest many women and girls, men and boys endure sexual violence although women and girls were disproportionately affected.” There are 8 existing Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) which are ideal for conducting our case management activities with minimum cost. At the moment FHE has field presence and experienced personnel in Mekelle and Shire who will ensure timely implementation of activities, regular monitoring, learning and reporting. FHE has put in place a community feedback mechanism that is open to the beneficiaries to bring up their complaints and grievances confidentially. The project will focus on adolescent girls and women in the IDPS who are affected by gender-based violence in existing 8 WGSS in two IDPs, in Mekele and Shire. The main targets are women and girls with special focus on GBV survivors, adolescent girls and women who are vulnerable to GBV (including peoples with disability, female headed households, and unaccompanied children).
Objective 1: Improved utilization of community-based Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) Services for vulnerable conflict-displaced women and girls of reproductive-age in Mekele and Shire.

1. - Projects activities

The planned emergency response activities will include:PHASE 1: Objective 1: SRH supporta) Information and awareness raising and community outreach and engagement: Train, coordinate and disseminate key messages on sexual, reproductive health, access to SRH and GBV services. b) Distribution of 6000 dignity kits in 8 IDP camps in Mekelle and Shire. Distribution of 60 CMR kits in Mekelle and Shire (4 per WGSS)c) Conduct Training for community health workers, health volunteers and midwives, on COVID-19 prevention, SRH and peer education skills c) Conduct community mobilization, sensitization and peer education outreach for SRH.e) Development of SRH IEC materialsObjective 2: GBV Prevention and Response:a) GBV (Gender Based Violence) is provided through case management provision, counseling, Psychological First Aid (PFA), health provision including SRH, and referral to legal services. Management of women and girls safe space activities implementation. b) Strengthening of referral networks with other GBV-related services such as legal services, psychosocial support and shelter, SRH, FP, rights and responsibilities, etc.c) Development of GBV sensitization campaign including the production of IEC materials.d) Capacity building training for community health workers, health volunteers and midwives, in GBVIMs (case management), and reporting in post conflict situations, harmonizing data collection by GBV providers etc.e) Establish 8 Women and Girls Friendly Safe Spaces for GBV response and coordination of activities.PHASE 2: Objective 1: SRH support: a) Promotion of maternal and child health through FH’s Emergency Care groups focused on WRA (Women of Reproductive Age) and PLW (Pregnant and Lactating Women). b) Conduct mobile outreach sessions, providing SRH clinical services to the vulnerable conflict-displaced communitiesObjective 2: GBV Prevention and Response: a) Conduct life skills and Peer education training for youth, women of reproductive age and adults on GBV, FP, rights and responsibilities, etc to be able to manage and plan their life effectively.Objective 3: MHPSS: a) Build Families’, Communities’ and Children’s Emotional Resilience, Recovery and Mental Health Care & Psychosocial Support through Tutapona’s Trauma Healing and recovery- a journey to emotional healing through mental health services for people impacted by war or conflict using Heroes’ Journey for children and GROW methodology for adults.b) Conduct individual and Group Clinical psychological counselling to affected children and adults Conduct Psychological First Aid (PFA) to staff and beneficiaries.c) Conduct effective referrals, ensuring that those referred are supported to access the services and monitored through the process of healing.d) Conduct Sports for Psychosocial support for both adults and children as part of traumatic healing and recovery.
