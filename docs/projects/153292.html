Project Summary

Based on the current security situation in South Sudan, multiple displacement of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) is likely to continue. IDPs are expected to continue living in with host communities, facing the direst living conditions without an alternative coping mechanism with host families. Families are often displaced without substantial household items. Also, given the long rainy season, the limited scope to earn a livelihood, the lack of access to markets and the often-protracted nature of displacement, need for provision of basic and domestic items remain a priority. Therefore, stock needs to be replenished on a regular basis so that UNHCR can respond quickly to the identified vulnerable persons as part of the protection responses. Through the provision of core relief items, UNHCR’s material assistance will continue to be targeted and protection focused, aiming that the most vulnerable displaced households are not left without basic material support and specific needs of woman and girl are catered for.
While UNHCR aims to reach some 42,000 households, corresponding to some 30% of the overall target population, as priority, UNHCR plans to assist the most vulnerable 128,430 individuals with selected core relief items, covering only 13% of the comprehensive target population in spite of life-saving nature of the material assistance and their relevance for protection interventions. In the context of further budget reductions, delivery of assistance will continue to be based on a targeted approach of supporting the most vulnerable PSNs identified during protection assessment and protection monitoring process. The items to be distributed will be selective, taking into account the most urgent protection needs of persons of concern that can be addressed via provision of NFIs, availability of other actors, logistical and security constraints / feasibility. Resources allowing, UNHCR also plans to provide dignity kits (sanitary cloths, soap, and underwear) and solar lanterns to some 2,000 households as complementarity to CRIs and targeted protection activities especially for women and girls.
Due to the fluid and unpredictable nature of the IDP and security situation in South Sudan, the type of shelter support required varies as IDPs are moving from one place to another. Despite the efforts by humanitarian agencies, shelter needs of IDPs outside of POC sites remain a challenge. Returning IDPs and refugee returnees often face the additional challenge of finding their former housing damaged, destroyed and/or occupied by other households. The need for decent shelter in areas of potential return are critical to realizing UNHCR’s solutions oriented IDP strategy. UNHCR will assess the HLP and conflict sensitive issues in at least three location. UNHCR shelter strategy is mainly on protection response to support the most vulnerable IDP and returnee families to reduce the protection risks and associated vulnerabilities. Training and capacity building of partner staff, in collaboration with the community, will greatly contribute to the successful identification and assistance to 286,500 PSNs in 2019. In some locations, shelter support will focus on reconstruction of damaged housing, while other locations will focus on transitional shelter and on emergency shelter. Resources and security conditions allowing, UNHCR will continue to provide support to the persons of concern in terms of rehabilitating the local infrastructures such health facilities and learning shelters to enable access to basic services like medication and education.
UNHCR will assess the HLP situation in at least three location.
UNHCR will remain an active member of the ES/NFI Cluster, particularly at the field level, and will maintain its cooperation with the Cluster actors to advocate for protection mainstreaming and enhanced vulnerability assessment, and optimize the available resources and focus and timely respond to the most critical needs of vulnerable IDPs among other affected populations.
From earlier years’ experience, UNHCR has realized that UNHCR and its partners are playing complementary role where UNHCR has strong ability to response quicker and where UNHCR and partners are static in ten key locations in South Sudan. UHNCR will follow the agreed SOP between S/NFI cluster and UNHCR for the emergency assistance of S/NFI.
For HRP Plan only 49% of total UNHCR Plan in South Sudan is included.

Needs

IDPs continue to face life threatening risks and violations due to widespread conflict caused and compounded by multiple shocks and stresses from food insecurity, destitution, disease and absence of essential services. Risks remain high among women, girls, boys and persons with specific protection needs. By the end of 2018, an estimated 1.9 million persons are internally displaced in South Sudan. The population inside UNMISS POC sites remained at about 220,000 across the country with limited freedom of movement and fear/risk of being attacked by armed elements within and outside the PoCs, forcefully recruited or abducted or being subjected to sexual violence with limited psychosocial support. New displacement were reported with the Equatorias, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity State maintaining the greatest levels of displacements. Throughout 2018 clashes in multiple locations, including Jonglei, Central and Sothern Unity, Western Bahr-el-Ghazal, Central Equatoria, Western Euquatoria and Greater Upper Nile region have caused additional population displacement, exacerbating intra-communal tensions, cattle raiding, overall criminality and other negative coping mechanisms, expanding the scale of needs. The priority for UNHCR intervention in 2019 will be addressing the needs of the vulnerable populations. Protections needs analysis indicated serious protection risks including threat to physical safety, serious human rights abuses such as sexual violence, killing of civilians, rape and gang rape and incidents of abduction of women and girls particularly in Southern Unity, perpetrated by different groups. Around 61% of IDPs are children while women and girls make the most vulnerable among the displaced. The prolonged conflict coupled with new displacements and lack of access resulted to severe food insecurity, according to IPC analysis released in September 2018. The number of IDPs living in hard-to-reach areas remained high, requiring mobile response, integrated missions and rapid assessments as priority. In 2019, synergies with all other clusters will ensure an integrated and solutions-focused response to the needs of the populations.Despite the foregoing, there were spontaneous returns in 2018 indicating possibilities of IDP returns to some secure locations in 2019. The Solutions Working Group in Bor facilitated voluntary returns of 221 IDPs from Bor POC to Akobo. It is expected that with the revitalised peace agreement, an increasing number of IDPs, including those of POCs, may opt to voluntarily return to places of origin, relocate elsewhere in the country or seek to integrate in places of displacement. In 2019 UNHCR and its partner will enhance population profiling to identity solutions needs of the populations to support them to exercise their options. Temporary and spontaneous returns of refugees from neighbouring countries will expand the scale of the needs. Full prioritization is required to implement strategies to decongest POCs in Wau, Bentiu, Malakal and Bor and comprehensive solutions pursued for IDPs in the two POCs and urban settlements in Juba in coordination with relevant actors. Operationalisation of area-based strategies led by Solutions Working Groups will by key to implementing durable solutions.About 88% of IDP population continue to live in host communities and spontaneous settlements, many in remote areas with limited infrastructure and accessibility. Through an expanded operational coverage and strengthened mobile response, protection monitoring and participation in RPAs, IRNA and RRM; UNHCR plans to reach about 50% (955,000) of the projected IDP population for 2019. Among the target population, an estimated 30% (286,500) are persons with specific needs; UNHCR estimates to reach 211,430 IDPs through Shelter and NFI interventions both by complementing with additional items for S/NFI Cluster partners or fully sourcing the items. For HRP purpose only 49% UNHCR plan is included.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

IDPs were consulted through interagency participatory assessment (PA) exercises conducted by UNHCR, its implementing and operational partners countrywide. The PA process aimed to gather information on specific protection risks faced by IDPs, analyse risks jointly with them, understand their capacities, and to hear their proposed solutions through structured dialogue. The principles of age, gender and diversity, a rights-based, and community-based approach guided the process. PA ensured that IDPs are put at the centre of the decision-making concerning their protection and welfare as well as ensured accountability to affected populations. Qualitative data collection was done through FGDs and KIIs and feedback sessions were held with communities to present findings and recommendations.

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring will measure access of beneficiaries through consultations with communities of all ages and gender and diverse groups to provide feedback and ensure accountability to affected population. Community based complaint mechanisms will be established to provide feedback on service provision and ensure that the voices of beneficiaries are factored in needs assessments, design, implementation and monitoring of programs.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project will ensure that women, girls, boys and men of different ages are provided with assistance and targeted interventions in coordination with other sectors to enhance community resilience. The ES/NFI intervention will be complemented by the Protection and other targeted intervention by UNHCR. For instance, protection from SGBV will require integration of prevention and response with other sectors such as livelihoods and WASH; targeted programs for the youth will be implemented both in POCs and hard to reach locations. It is proposed to establish multipurpose youth centres that provide life skills to the youth and other market assessed skills that will lead to jobs and self-reliance
