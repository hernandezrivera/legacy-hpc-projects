Project Summary

Following the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN) approach that IOM strongly supports and actively promotes in Cameroon, and building on the programmes and activities implemented in the recent years, IOM plans comprehensive emergency interventions for 2023 focusing especially on coordination, shelter, mental health support (health), psychosocial support (protection) and WASH interventions.
IOM’s multisectoral response aims at contributing to reinforce life-saving assistance and to improve well-being of displaced and the most vulnerable populations affected by the crisis in the Far North region of Cameroon.
Critical data on displacement situation, alerts as well as humanitarian needs following our DTM methodology will be provided to the humanitarian community in the frame of the Coordination Sector. IOM will also distribute shelter and NFI kits using cash-modality solutions when possible (bearing in mind that lack of civil documentation poses a huge challenge) and help the construction for most vulnerable people. In the WASH response, IOM will drill and rehabilitate boreholes, construct water points, latrines and other infrastructures and conduct hygiene sensitization and promotion activities. Finally, IOM multisectoral assistance will also include mental health activities including direct MHPSS assistance using PMTs, reinforcing referral mechanism and capacity building of health actors (Health sector), while also directly providing psychosocial support to populations impacted by crisis by reinforcing safety and social network as well as strengthening community capacity to provide psychosocial support to victims of GBVs (Protection sector). These interventions are complementary to other programmes IOM implements in the region, focusing on DDR or social cohesion and by creating synergies within such initiatives, aim at achieving the HDP collective outcome set by the National HDPN Taskforce of Cameroon : “the most vulnerable populations in the convergence areas or in other regions affected by the crises recover indiscriminately their fundamental rights and improve their physical well-being and social welfare.”
The project will target various localities in the Far North region considering the level of needs and vulnerabilities observed and consulted with coordination with each sector of planned interventions. When feasible and appropriate, participative targeting workshops involving local community members and Humanitarian, Development and Peacebuilding partners in the localities will also be held.
IOM respects its procedures to handle complaints on SEA starting with a focal person to receive and handle complaints and the building of knowledge of IOM staff and IPs about IOM’s policy and mechanisms to prevent SEA, including training on the proper conduct to handle SEA disclosures. IOM will also make usage of the Far North GBV referral pathway to refer SEA cases.
Through activities, direct feedback from beneficiaries and community members will be gained via IPs and also directly by IOM staff during field visits and visits of the M&E/AAP independent team in accordance with the wider IOM AAP emergencies workplan. AAP focal points will be assigned in Maroua and Yaounde respectively, with clear SOPs to be in place to capture complaints from beneficiaries and to take any appropriate action. Members of IP will be trained on topics such as the proper handling of disclosures of gender-based violence (GBV) incidents, aspects of confidentiality, and respectful communication in high-stress environments.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Humanitarian needs in Cameroon remain high. According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) published in April 2022, approximately 3.9 million people (or one in six) are in need of emergency assistance, and an increase in number is estimated in 2023 as the country faces three humanitarian fronts (Far North Region, North-West, South-West (NWSW) Regions and East refugee crisis) plus climate-related crisis to cope with, in addition to the war in Ukraine which has suddenly increased instability with higher food prices that have compounded community pressures and pushed vulnerable persons further into poverty and hunger. Since 2013, ongoing hostilities and violence in the Far North Region continue to trigger lower magnitude displacements and community shocks due to sporadic attacks by armed groups designated as terrorist organizations (AGDTO) such as Boko Haram and the Islamic State West Africa Province. Attacks are not only limited to clashes with Government security forces but are carried out on nearby communities, with as a result more than 573,184 displaced persons uprooted as of August 2022. Furthermore, intercommunity violence between different ethnic groups over lack of resources and competing livelihoods is occurring more periodically, most recently in the Logone Birni arrondissement. Insecurity in this region is exacerbating the already limited access of displaced populations to essential social services as looting and destruction of goods and public infrastructure are becoming more recurrent, further harming community cohesion and leading to higher inter-community tensions. Currently, all humanitarian response actors are struggling to meet the large humanitarian needs due to a low level of humanitarian funding and an overall low visibility. Low funding has meant that there is a greater need for the reinforcement of a Rapid Response Mechanism which requires reliable and quickly available data on alerts. IOM’s last multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in the Far-North region (August 2022) showed a variety of needs relative to individual sectors.Shelter/NFI was identified as the second priority need after food, with overpopulated shelters (42% of the respondents), in very bad conditions (38% of the respondents), damaged or destroyed shelters (28%) as the main problems. This was coupled with the findings that more households lacked a series of basic NFIs such as bed mats (66%) as well as housing construction materials. For the health sector, 61% of respondents noted that they had difficulty accessing health services, with the principal reasons being that they were too costly (77%), lack of specialized services (45%) or health facility is physically inaccessible (25%). As outlined in the protection sector response plan, reduce GBV risks, reduce exposure of children to arm aspects of war and improve protection and respect of fundamental rights for all the persons affected by crisis are the main issues to be addressed. In particular, there is a very limited capacity in the health centers in terms of mental health, and IOM has been providing capacity building to staff and specific drugs to these centers. Women and girls were the most impacted by lack of access to health services (74%) followed by elderlies (71%). And in terms of WASH, there are serious limitations in terms of access to potable water with 34% of the interviewed people mentioned that the drink water from the river, lake or mayo, 28% from a non-protected well and 13% take water from other non-protected sources. Access and use of latrines is also an important constraint, with insufficient number (67% respondents), lack of privacy (34%), far distances (26%) and danger to use them at night (21%) being the main obstacles.
