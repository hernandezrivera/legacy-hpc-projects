Project Summary

Founded in 2006 in Nyala, South Darfur, Great Family Organization (GFO) was the first NNGO which was engaged in IDPs protection since 2007. GFO coordinated with OCHA and HAC to Co-Manage El salam IDPs camps through coordination the humanitarian services in the camp.
Reception of the newly displaced in 4 reception centers constructed by GFO through CHF funding in 2010 which provided protection of new IDPs including reintegration of families, referring those who are traumatized to health clinic, GBV and child protection.
With Unicef, SMoW and SCCW, GFO had a comprehensive program in Street Children and children out-of-school. GFO is still coordinating with other actors in protection issues related to vulnerable including IDPs, host communities and returnees.
This project is designed as result of November 15th, 2020 Inter Agencies rapid assessment report: East Jabal Marrah, Mersheng and Elwihda localities witnessed the most aggressive confrontations between the Gov. of Sudan's Army and the Oppositions' fighters and also between the fractions of the opposition. These problems reflected to huge number of displaced person in between these tree localities: East Jabal Marrah, Mersheng and El Wihda
This project is designed to address the need of protection services to protracted and newly IDPs including women, children and elders beside the people with disabilities, in gathering accessible locations in North of South Darfur State, namely: Mersheng and Manawashi, Kidinir, Jawa, Yara and al Malam. The activities will cover: the provision of psychosocisial support to traumatized women and children targeted 1500, creation of 6 women centers in the six villages, formation of 21 women groups of 30, conducting 90 visits of social workers to evaluate individual situation of the beneficiaries, 60 training on S_GBV, integrating separate families particularly separate children, FGA awareness raising, early marriage of girls, children labor. The project will facilitate and disseminate the Rule of Law through the organizing workshop on human Wrights. Organization of community festivals and exchange visits between the targeted locations in the three localities.
Every activity in the project is designed to ensure protection mainstreaming during implementation.

1 - Needs

In East Jabal Marrah (Sharg Aljabal) locality 78,000 people are projected to need protection assistance in 2020/2021 in which a number 16,000 are most protection need . The ongoing conflict has resulted in grave violations of human rights, including brutal attacks resulting in death, injuries, sexual violence, abductions, early and/or forced marriages, arbitrary and extended detentions without trial, disappearances, and deprivations, denial of services, family separations and forced recruitment by armed groups. Conflict-related psychological trauma is widespread, meanwhile mental protection services are inadequate. Psychosocial support and mental health interventions are needed, including specialized care for survivors of gender-based violence (GBV). Mental, neurological and substance abuse and related disorders account for an estimated 20% of the global burden of disease . In emergency settings, the rate of common mental disorders quatripled. There are only no protection center or health facilities in the region referral to secondary health care is through ICRC. Primary Medical assistance are provided free of charge by humanitarian actors. But accessibility is still difficult, and referrals are done only for most severely affected population (transport by road is difficult. Gender-Based ViolenceGender-based violence (GBV), is widespread among IDPs living in host communities and camps or camp-like settings. Female-headed households, widows, women with disabilities and adolescent girls remain the most at-risk groups of sexual violence and other types of GBV. Data shows that, among the GBV survivors seeking assistance, 40% are children, of whom 65% are girls, and 43% reported having suffered from sexual violence . About 60,000 people are estimated to be at risk of GBV . Despite increased rates of GBV, services to support survivors are still very limited. Only 18% of fully or partially functioning health centers in Jabal Marrah locality and surroundings can reportedly provide GBV survivors with integrated clinical management services. Child ProtectionChildren in the area of Jabal Marrah are extremely neglected and abused, particularly girls under 18 years are facing threat, abuse and sexual exploitation or early marriage.Boys under age are forced to hard work and took responsibility of the family or recruited by the armed group, or flee out the area to become street children in towns (Nyala, Kass).No protection center available in all the area of Jabal Marrah, and more than 87% of the children are out of school.

Provision of trainings to ensure clinical care for sexual assault survivors is available and accessible in health facilities in new locations- Strengthen local level coordination on GBV and improve the functioning of GBV referral pathways to ensure women and girls have timely and confidential access to life saving servicesOutput 1.2 Vulnerable populations, including women and girls face reduced risks and benefit from an improved protective environment through community engagement, advocacy and risk mitigation activitiesActivity 1.2.1 Conduct community outreach targeting 4,000 community members to raise awareness on services and facilitate confidential referrals to GBV response services-GBV1 - GBV: Construct temporary Semi permanent Women Centers: Training women on GBV including sexual exploitation, early marriage, meeting with women leaders, provision of support according to the GBV SS Partner.GBV2: Running of Women Centers include recreational activities, life skills activities, individual and group counseling (non-specialized), Psychological First Aid (PFA) and skills building/vocational activitiesActivity 1.2.2 Conduct safety audits and community discussions to identify risks faced by the community, in particular by vulnerable/marginalized groups within the society, and develop strategies to address these in collaboration with local communities and humanitarian actorsActivity 1.2.3 Conduct weekly protection monitoring in consultation with affected population to monitor violations, discrimination, exclusion, coping mechanisms, and access to services, analyze the findings, refer urgent protection cases and share protection monitoring reports with clusters and other humanitarian actors to inform advocacy and recommend adjustments to programmingOutput 1.3 Communities are engaged and trained to improve GBV prevention and response services in their own communities (GBV services awareness, identification and referral)Activity 1.3.1 Establish Community Based Protection Networks (CBPNs) in each location with the aim of identifying the types of GBV and CP risks confronting women and children, supporting and promoting the genuine participation of women/girls and children in decision making, participating in community mobilization on GBV and CP Concerns.Activity 1.3.2 Train CBPNs on GBV and CP issues, identifying, monitoring and reporting violations in their communities in order to equip them with the relevant skills to undertake their activities.The members of the established CBPNs will be involved in the identification of violations and GBV cases which will be reported to GFO case workers and community mobilizers. Activity 1.3.4 Dissemination of key messages on GBV prevention, awareness of GBV consequences and referral to services particularly for sexual violence (including the need to access medical assistance for rape within 72 hours) – 5 awareness sessions per each location with at least 20 participants per session.- Conflict affected children have access to safe spaces and appropriate existing services and support

This displacement of population who used the tree-cut for their living needs: housing and food preparation which affected directly the green belt in Jabal Marrah.The project will create sustainability among the population and minimize the tree cutting disseminating through the environment awareness raising and importance, participating in tree replanting and convince UNHABITAT for SSB production of environmental house and other GFO will train women in efficiency stoves to reduce dependancy on the charcoal.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries is a very important aspect of this project. GFO is alive to the fact that protection and safety issues are a big challenge for many affected populations in South Darfur.GFO will involve all the affected populations in the response and their views will be taken into considerations too. The choice of distribution sites, access to those sites and distance will be taken into account to ensure safety of the affected populations. Where applicable locations with waiting structures, shelter, water and toilets will be preferred for distributions as long as they are safe for them. Violations against civilians have been widely been reported in many locations including Gender based violence and sexual abuse mainly for women and girls. In some locations children especially boys have been recruited as child soldiers. GFO will look out for such cases and report them to relevant protection partners in those locations or where possible use the GFO protection officers to manage these cases. When insecurity occurs most displaced populations move to safe locations with almost nothing. This increases their vulnerability and in most cases would need to be protected from various risks. This project will seek to ensure these communities are provided protection services to secure their living conditions. All vulnerable and deserving beneficiaries will be included on the list of beneficiaries and no segregation for whatsoever reason will be allowed during this response.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The affected populations will be involved from inception (through holding FGDs and community meetings within various gender categories (Males, Women and the Youths). Participatory assessments will be conducted at project inception to identify locations for interventions. During assessments, the households, the community, and the local leadership will provide protection inputs to the most needed in the locations as well as indicate who the most vulnerable people are. Household interviews to the affected populations are also conducted and their feedback documented. Through these means of interaction, the affected populations have a say in the project before they receive assistance. They indicate what their urgent needs are and what assistance they prefer. Their involvement continues at various stages of the project, including during the verification of the neediest, during implementation . During the registration and verification of beneficiaries the community and beneficiaries will receive the guidelines on the selection criteria and they will be part of the selection of the deserving beneficiaries. During these stages, all the casual staff that will be used to carry out the various roles will be from the affected population’s community.To start implementing, the affected population will be requested to choose the preferred the location they prefer (comfort and safety). The help-desk will be accessible at all sites and the beneficiaries will provide their complaints and feedback through this. A report will be compiled and findings shared in cases were immediate solutions are not available. Relevant feedback/ report shared internally, with the cluster and the donor. GFO will seek to strengthen the coping mechanism of the community and ensure that it does not erode the way the community structures and their coping mechanisms. GFO will seek to understand the context of the displaced people and offer appropriate and relevant assistance. GFO will ensure that in its response and accounting to the affected population, the organization does not do any harm. In all the locations that the project will respond to, the local authorities are critical as they are the first point of contact when the teams arrive. The team leaders will meet with these authorities explaining the objectives of each mission and the target groups with emphasis on the limited resources available. The teams will also have meetings with the local chiefs and community leadership as they know the communities better and the challenges they face. However, it’s the responsibility of the team leaders to explain the beneficiary selection criteria. Through these meetings GFO will emphasize the need to conduct independent needs assessment and beneficiary verification exercises.
