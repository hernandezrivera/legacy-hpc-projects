Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen the protection and social safety net and resilience of children, caregivers, and communities in the face of chronic exposure to domestic and conflict-related violence by: (1) providing community-based and specialized child protection case management and psychosocial and mental health services to children and their families, (2) strengthening protection preparedness and response in targeted communities through engaging youth, community counselors, CBOs and other front liners in professional training and coaching session and (3) providing a specialized package of prevention and response services, including legal aid and MHPSS, to GBV survivors and girls and women at risk. This project targets Palestinian children directly and indirectly exposed to violence, abuse, and conflict-related violence in most affected communities in the West Bank and East Jerusalem. War Child Holland (WCH) and partners – SAWA, Torture, and Rehabilitation Center (TRC), and the Palestinian Counselling Center (PCC)- will respond to the identified needs through a comprehensive and holist package of services that will engage children, families and their communities in enhancing access to protection services, including GBV. The partners will target 15 CBOs distributed in Area C, Hebron H2, Eat Jerusalem, and north West Bank (Nablus, Jenin). The areas were selected based on the previous experience of consortium partners in those areas and the needs identified as well as on the OCHA Vulnerability Profile.
War Child caseworkers will closely coordinate with MOSD child protection officers on the governorate level and regularly attend coordination meetings in the field, including the CPN monthly meetings at the governorate level. War Child and project partners are well coordinating with the MHPSS, CP, and GBV actors in the project-targeted areas to ensure effective coordination, and complementarity and avoid any overlapping. War Child and partners in the project will be coordinating closely with the CP/MHPSS WG, GBV sub-cluster, and Protection Cluster for referrals to external and other services not provided through the project activities to cover the gaps. Through this ongoing coordination and collaboration, project partners will ensure that the response is more effective and that the project interventions are comprehensive. Case management and Child protection services and referrals made during the project will ensure adhering adherence and high commitment to Child protection minimum standards, ISAC, and GBV standards to ensure the safety of the project participants and do no further harm.
Case Management services are implemented according to the case management interagency guidelines and the Child Protection minimum standards and in line with the national referral system/MOSD Index of Networking and Referral of Child Protection against Violence, Neglect, and Abuse. Data protection protocols and information-sharing agreements will be in place among partners. The service directory will also be regularly updated and in line with CP/MHPSS coordination mechanisms.
Through this ongoing coordination and collaboration, project partners will ensure that the response is more effective and holistic. The project intervention is comprehensive and will be implemented according to humanitarian standards such as CPMS, ISAC, and GBV standards to ensure the safety of the project participants and do no further harm. In addition to specific Protection interventions, War Child and partners will mainstream PSEA, disability inclusion
Through the project life cycle, PCC will be focusing on the provision of PFA services through youth emergency groups and will be referring the children and parents in need of further psychological support to TRC for the provision of group and individual counseling, SAWA for GBV case management and legal aid to GBV survivors and WCH for Child protection case management service provision.

1 - Needs

This project addresses MHPSS and protection risks stemming from domestic, community, and occupation-related violence. It complements and fills gaps in community-protection mechanisms in the most vulnerable communities of the West Bank. The project’s gaps, needs, and objectives at a high level were identified through the analysis of 2022 MSNA and HRP 2023, in addition to consultations with key stakeholders, including the CP/MHPSS partners, GBV cluster, MoSD, and MoWA. The project-specific interventions and plan were defined jointly with the local communities in the targeted localities to respond to War Child’s commitment to Global SO4, which aims to improve Community Participation and AAP, with a special focus on PwD and most marginalized groups. The protection needs have increased due to the high level of violence, mainly domestic violence, and occupation-related violence. According to the HNO, children in oPt face high violence at home, with 90 percent facing some form of violent discipline at home (boys 92.3 percent, girls 97.9 percent). In addition, 24.3% of (married or previously married) women in the West Bank between 18 and 64 experienced violence in the 12 months preceding the survey (PCBS). With an estimated 630,000 people in the West Bank assessed to be in need of humanitarian assistance, the MSNA 2022 reports alarming data on the level of MHPSS distress among children and adults, the impact of GBV on girls and women living in targeted areas, and the reduced access to services reported by HHs. In WB, 12% of HH reported that at least one HH member showed signs of psychosocial distress or trauma in the past year. Out of those 12% HH, 88% reported at least one adult member, and 29% reported at least one child showing psychosocial distress. Whereas the MHPSS needs are huge, the available services are insufficient and associated with access challenges, mainly for females and PwD. In addition, GBV continues to be a challenge in the targeted communities, whereas 12.3% of the HH reported that women and girls avoid certain areas in their locations because they feel unsafe. Furthermore, the data reflects that high school-aged children dropouts during the current schooling year; 13.6% of girls and 19.8% of boys. The top three reasons associated with dropout were; political/protection risks (36.1% for girls and 54.8% for boys), school closure (29.3% for girls and 53.6% for boys), and lack of interest (17.8% for girls and 12.6% for boys). Palestinian families in EJ still face the threat of forced eviction from their homes by the Israeli authorities, including in Silwan and in Sheikh Jarrah, due to court cases initiated by Israeli settler organizations. In EJ, at least 218 Palestinian HH comprising 970 people, including 424 children, are facing eviction cases pending in Israeli courts (HNO 2022). According to OCHA, 64 Palestinian communities located in EJ are within close proximity to Israeli illegal settlements; with a distance of 1000 meters or less. Across WB, 199 communities close to settlements that host 534173 inhabitants suffer from Israeli forces’ violence, settlers’ attacks, and injustice. The needs have also been informed by War Child’s ongoing data from the child protection case management information system, whereby between March and October 2022 in Northwest Bank, Area C, and Hebron, 107 children have been experiencing critical protection risks; 70% experienced emotional abuse, 26% experienced neglect and 4% physical abuse. While boys and men continue to be directly exposed to occupation-related violence, girls and women are also affected- according to the 2022 HNO, women and girls with disabilities, Bedouin women, internally displaced women, and women and girls living in refugee camps are particularly at risk of violence. The HNO 2022 confirms that the GBV response system is not sufficient to meet the needs of GBV survivors. Among the most pressing issues are the lack of available and accessible services for all community members, including PWDs, the lack of quality GBV care, and the lack of a functioning referral system. While the needs for protection and MHPSS are increasing, the current care system is overstretched and cannot respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of girls, boys, and their caregivers, including the PWDs. Through this project, War Child, YMCA, and TRC will provide high-quality, integrated, and coordinated Child Protection, Case Management, and MHPSS services.

2 - Activities

Activities contribute to Objective 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place. Specifically, the project is in line with HRP standard activities as follows:HRP Standard Activity: CHILD PROTECTION and MHPSS: Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programs:(1)Case management services for 120 boys and girls (12 WD). Data protection protocols and information-sharing agreements will be in place among partners. The service directory will also be regularly updated and in line with CP/MHPSS coordination mechanisms. WCH community caseworkers in Area C will provide basic counseling, psychosocial support and individual case management for low to medium-risk cases, and refer high-risk cases to the CPN. Detected children will receive integrated quality services, through appropriate safe referral to specialized mental health services provided by PCC under this project and external mental health and other service providers, in coordination with CPWG and the CPN at the governorate level. 3 - Indicators

War Child has established a global monitoring and reporting framework and uses standardized and globally validated means to assess program effects on the well-being of participating children and families and the quality of the participant experience and satisfaction. Through WCH’s monitoring and evaluation methodologies, the quality implementation of programming and delivery of care will be ensured. Project partners will integrate monitoring at all levels (activities, outputs, and outcomes) in their regular work, and mechanisms will be put in place to facilitate early detection of any negative unintended results of the project. Regular field visits and supervision of all activity sites will be conducted, not only for accountability purposes but will also serve to learn and support adaptive programming. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through the engagement of CBOs and community facilitators. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

War Child has an overall Integrity Framework in place at a global level, consisting of: Child Safeguarding Policy, Code of Conduct, and an Anti-Fraud and Anti-Corruption Policy. PSEA is embedded in our Safeguarding Policy, which refers to the UN bulletin on PSEA and commitment to respond to its breach. War Child is committed to the PSEA and incorporates the concepts to prevent any potential violations perpetrated by staff, suppliers, vendors, or community members. War Child is an active member in the PSEA network set by UNICEF and endorsed the Standard Operating Procedures for the Community-Based Complaint Mechanism in oPt. War Child assures that the community members are oriented on the AAP procedures, including FCRM and PSEA, which is planned at early stage of each project through designing, printing and distributing relevant materials and employing the different project interventions to promote the relevant channels employed. The feedback received through the FCRM are recorded on War Child’s FCRM system, categorized and responded to immediately, or after referring the case to relevant staff within the organization. The system provide timely report visualization using PowerBI application. The FCRM thematic analysis help the project team in employing adaptive management and generate learnings. In any case that require handling beneficiaries’ data, War Child ensures adherence to Data Protection Protocols that uphold the privacy and confidentiality in all cases. Access to beneficiaries data is kept limited to the professionals working on the specific case. When the case is closed, War Child the replace any identical information with codes and archives the case data. Those mechanisms are key part of generating organizational learning, as it helps capture the participants’ feedback to reflect on the quality of the processes and make informed decisions and changes to the programs

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

In-line with War Child’s Strategy 2025, particularly for Strategic Objective P4: Improved AAP and Community Participation, the organization programs are devoted to maintain people-centered approach where members of communities, including children, PWDs and marginalized groups, have a clear and visible input into the projects design and influence the implementation of interventions carried by War Child and partners. War Child employ an advanced computerized FCRM system to assure the implementation of APP, effective implementation of the Integrity Framework and full compliance with the CHS. War Child is aligned to the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child as a guideline, and to the Child’s Participation and Protection Rights. These underlying principles and standards, alongside the commitment to do-no-harm and quality programming, are our impetus to have functioning accountability and feedback mechanisms everywhere we operate. War Child prioritize the capacity building and training of all staff and partners on the FCRM SoPs, including the reporting mechanisms, promotion of FCRM channels, usage of the standard forms, and monitoring the quality implementation of the employed feedback mechanisms. Furthermore, War Child is committed to improve the community participation along with the different stages of the projects, including the design, planning, implementation and after action reviews.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The FCRM SOPs suggest that all projects employ several feedback channels that include but not limited to: Hotline, SMS, WhatsApp, Helpdesk and Child-friendly complaint boxes. The project team works with the community stakeholders and general community to verify the relevance of the employed channels and decide on adding more channels in case the basic set of available channels are not sufficient, this include defining new channels that better fit the preference of PWD or marginalized groups needs. The validity of the FCRM channels consider the following aspects: community location, age groups, gender, age, literacy, disability and physical and social barriers to guarantee equal access to FCRM channels. FCRM is properly designed to assure the access of different vulnerable groups including PWDs, elderly, children and females. For example, when the project is targeting individuals with mental- related disability, additional mechanisms with skilled experts are employed to meet the beneficiaries in-person to collect their feedback regularly.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

War Child considers the specific needs and vulnerabilities of boys and girls of varying ages, as well of men and women, recognizing their roles in the communities, while trying to empower those who tend to be left out of power. War Child aims at being accountable to the children and families of this project and has taken a systematic approach to ensure that Accountability to Affected Population, Protection, Conflict Sensitivity, Do No Harm and GBV are mainstreamed into War Child’s project implementation. Practical applications of the Do No Harm in the proposed project will encompass: *accountability measures in line with War Child Integrity Framework, including community-based and child-friendly complaints and feedback mechanisms for all services, and participants’ satisfaction assessments using WCH tools and FGD with women, men, girls and boys • Identifying vulnerable groups across communities to foster social stability, avoid exacerbating tensions within the community and promote the equitable distribution of services across identity groups; • Reaching out and including vulnerable children within War Child intervention as per locally contextualized vulnerability criteria; • Analyzing existing community power dynamics to ensure that the needs of individuals and groups at risk of marginalization are voiced (also through the feedback mechanism); • Do No Harm principles included as a component of child protection and child safeguarding competency development of partners and communities; • A strong focus and commitment on child safeguarding and confidentiality in all interventions • With regards to case management services, case workers consult with the child and caregiver as to where they feel most comfortable to receive the service – for some they prefer in their homes, for others they prefer in the case worker’s office • Timely referral to specialized referrals to MHPSS, CP, and GBV professionals *Meaningful Access through outreach to children and caregivers with disability; *promoting participation and empowerment of boys and girls, and men and women; *applying a gender age marker in the design and monitoring of activities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure meaningful access and participation, vulnerable children and caregivers (including people with disabilities) will be identified through outreach (including home visits), awareness raising sessions in the community, structured PSS programs, then referred to case management for assessment. The case workers are based in the community where we work, making it easier to identify vulnerable children and caregivers. Following a comprehensive assessment, case workers will refer children who are under 18 years old and their caregivers to the needed internal services or external services. Access to external MHPSS services in the targeted communities are limited, therefore requiring children and their caregivers to travel to the nearest city which may be several hours away. As the travel and transportation costs may at times be a barrier to access, War Child in these instances will provide transportation support and case workers will accompany the children (including children with disabilities) to the specialized services to ensure access to care. In addition, given the overall and high need of children and families affected by conflict- related violence, selection of participants in community-based PSS activities and case management services will be based on specific vulnerability criteria (such as being acutely affected by or at highrisk of conflict- related violence, family or community violence/abuse/neglect, disabilities, child labor, COVID-19,etc), which will also be well publicized through community meetings. PSCCW will guarantee outreach to women and girls with disability in their GBV activities. CBOs and community facilitators will regularly engage women, men, girls and boys, including with disabilities, to tailor the community-based PSS and CP interventions specifically to their needs. Also recognizing that the participation of children with disabilities can be a challenge due to social-cultural norms, the project will actively identify person with disabilities through awareness raising in the community and coordinating with the CPN network and Protection Cluster partners such as the PRCS and YMCA who provide services for children with disabilities in the targeted areas if needed. CP Case workers will also coordinate with the CPN officers in their governorates who oversee protection cases for children with disabilities to ensure that children with disabilities are referred to relevant PSS activities as needed and coordination will be closely done with the available services provided by Protection Cluster actors such as the YMCA and the PRCS for children with disabilities. The non- specialized activities are provided by community members themselves, trained and supported as part of the project. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through engagement of CBOs and community facilitators who will regularly engage women, men, girls and boys (including persons with disabilities) in defining their needs and tailoring the community-based PSS interventions. Case workers and community facilitators will also receive an orientation training by War Child staff on inclusion principles in order to skillfully adapt PSS interventions to ensure inclusivity, including for children with disabilities.
