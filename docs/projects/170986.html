Project Summary

Médecins du Monde – France (MdM-F) is operating in Gaza since 2002, working mainly in the fields of emergency preparedness and response, mental health, and psychosocial support in addition to reproductive health. Gaza is facing a deep economic and humanitarian crisis, which stems from 13 years of blockade, continuing internal political division, and the accompaniment of chronic electricity and water shortages and recurrent escalations of hostilities. As a result, access to health for this population has witnessed almost a reversal.
MdM is an active member of both the Health and Nutrition cluster and the Protection cluster. Among the last years, MdM contributed to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and in this case more specifically to the HCT Response Plan.
The proposed intervention is contributing to the health cluster objective 1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt related to the strategic objective 2.
MdM-F is focusing its intervention on strengthening the capacities of the health system and the community to deal with emergencies. The proposed intervention aims at enhancing the mental health and medical emergency services at the hospitals and community levels.
At the hospital level, the intervention will aim to increase the capacity of the health staff at 5 emergency departments on the mental and medical flow chart that was designed and validated by the Ministry of Health (MOH) in previous MdM interventions. MdM will ensure the continuation of the mental health integration at the emergency department of Al Aqsa hospital (both medical staff and 5 psychologists) as part of the MOH strategy and following the approved WHO approach as it was previously agreed between WHO and MOH that MdM will on WHO approach using the mhGAP . At the community level; MdM will work with a local partner to ensure the delivery of MHPSS services to the communities in Beit Hanoun through technical support taking into consideration COVID-19 adaptations, needs integration, and mainstreaming in all level of interventions.
MdM is in working with both MoH and Health cluster to coordinate its intervention and to ensure the effectiveness of our actions as well as to avoid duplication
This intervention complements the Palestinian Ministry of Health (MoH), as the main provider of health services in Gaza they are responsible for the supervision, regulation, and control of all health services. Finally, MdM – France, MdM - Switzerland, MdM-Spain , MAP UK, and WHO have coordinated their proposals in order to avoid any duplication.

1 - Needs

Gaza is characterized as a complex emergency situation, characterized by various risk factors, central of which is a continued risk of armed escalations which can compound and exacerbate preexisting crises in social service provisions, such as health care. Based on the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, the already challenged healthcare system in the oPt is experiencing added strain from the COVID-19 outbreak. The current surge in need takes place against a backdrop of an overall extremely precarious humanitarian situation in the Gaza strip. The health care system in Gaza is suffering from a chronic shortage in drugs and disposables (around 40%), the allocation of health staff to respond to COVID-19 needs will have a negative impact on the day to day provision of medical services especially at emergency departments. The MoH has stressed the importance of continuous improvement in the quality of medical emergency services through the dissemination of medical and mental health emergency flow charts among the main five emergency departments in the Gaza strip and the training of dedicated staff for its application. The charts were developed by MdM in a previous project as a first step and validated by MOH, the proposed intervention aims to continue the intervention to complete the dissemination and capacity building of relevant health care staff, through that MdM will contribute to the enhancement and the quality of medical emergency services and enable staff to access flow charts tools through an online training platform. Mental health and psychosocial support remain a critical cross-cutting issue at all levels. Up to 2020, MdM has worked on the integration of mental health services at Al Aqsa hospital by establishing a mental health unit, managed by Al Aqsa hospital and technically supervised by the mental health directorate. During the period from January 2019 to August 2020, the mental health unit provided 7,738 one-time consultations with an almost 100 % increase in consultations between 2019 (2,163) and 2020 (4,462). Out of the 7,738 individuals, 68% were diagnosed as suffering from anxiety related to health condition, 18% from acute stress and the remaining were either agitation/psychoses, conversion disorder, depression, panic, suicide/homicide, and substance use. Due to the current COVID-19 pandemic, it is expected that anxiety, especially anxiety related to health conditions, will increase and the need for continuation of MHPSS high-quality services will be essential. MdM’s intervention aims to continue the support to the mental health unit at Al Aqsa hospital and strengthen their capacities in terms of COVID-19 mainstreaming, remote support technique, Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and specific needs of Persons with Disabilities (PWD). Based on Al Aqsa mental health unit statistics, out of the 700 cases that were referred to specialized services, 50 % were female adults which reflects the high vulnerability and need for specific services for women. The intervention will support the unit in establishing a feedback and complaint mechanism specific to the unit and allow them to offer more outreach services through community engagement activities. On the other hand, during the last 12 months, MDM through partnering with one active and experienced community-based organization (Ataa Association) has positively contributed to raising the awareness of 5000 beneficiaries living in Beit Hanoun in regards to important psychological symptoms, self-care, life skills, and much other resilience building and well-being promoting topics. MdM through this partnership has shed the light also on the need to strengthen coordination and referral pathways between different services providers and continuation of services.. Moreover, during MDM field visits, MDM field team has reported the increase in the needs and demands from the communities to more extended services. AL Atta association is considered a safe and easy entry point for MHPSS services as it is highlight accepted by the community.

2 - Activities

General objective: To contribute to increase mental health and medical emergency preparedness and response including COVID-19- mainstreaming at community and secondary level. Outcome 1: The capacities of MoH staff at the emergency department of five hospitals have been strengthened Activities related to outcome 1 (Target: 5 emergency departments) : 1.1 To strengthen the capacity of the MoH staff of 5 emergency departments on the medical and mental health flowchart prepared in the previous phase of the project, the training will be conducted by MdM technical team and the national committee that validated the flowcharts. The training will target 200 staff with at least 80 female staff working in the main emergency departments in the Gaza strip (Shifa, Andonis, Al Aqsa, Naser, and Abousef Al Najar).and will coordinate with WHO, ICRC, and Map-UK, in order to ensure the complementarity of action where MDM will be focusing on medical protocols and not on the triaging system while WHO is focusing more on mass causality management and ICRC focusing on Trauma. For sure MDM, is continuously showing flexibility to adapt their intervention to a most suitable methodology based on the dynamic changes in the health needs of Gaza strip and with close coordination with MOH. 1.2 To form, with the MoH, a technical supervisory committee formed of MoH consultants and MdM staff to supervise the quality of the flowchart implementation. 1.3 To provide “mental health gap” training to 50 ( 20 female and 30 males) medical staff at al Aqsa hospital as part of the continuation of the prior agreed methodology of intervention with WHO and MOH to complete the national integration program of MOH to integrate Mental health at the hospital level using the mhGAP approach. Moreover, within the targeted two facilities MDM will conduct the mhGap general training and will coordinate with MDM-Switzerland the part of self- harm to be implemented by them. 1.4 To work on the creation of online flowchart training tools and ensure the establishment of a remote training platform. 1.5 To support the emergency department at Al Aqsa hospital with the provision of needed mental health and medical emergency related drugs and disposables. Outcome 2: The resilience of the population living in the North , Gaza and Deir el Balah governorates is empowered through the improvement of the mental health and psychosocial services in both governorates. Output 2.1: The mental health and psychosocial services provided at Al Aqsa and At Al Rantisi hospitals are enhanced and strengthened through the continuation of the full functionality of the mental health unit at Al Aqsa hospital. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

MdM accountability system is based on sharing information in a regular, timely, and accessible way with the different actors involved in the project. The permanent presence of MdM teams in the field makes this flow of information continuous and bidirectional, establishing a communication system that allows the participation of the health staff at MoH in the definition and adaptation of MdM activities. In addition to this, tools for seeking the feedback of the staff of the services supported are included in each MdM project. In the same way, regular communication is kept with the International Cooperation Department (ICD), PHC, and Hospital Directorates of the MoH to ensure full information sharing, participation, and feedback on MdM interventions. Part of the MdM intervention is to support the mental health unit at Al Aqsa hospital. MdM will work closely with the unit to establish a feedback mechanism as part of the activities. As a best practice, MdM will ensure to dedicate a phone number for feedback, complaints, and suggestions to the patients receiving the MHPSS support, the information coming through this number will be discussed amongst a committee from MoH and MdM to be addressed, the committee will work according to a TOR that will be developed.At the local partner level, MdM will ensure to strengthen the partners existing complaint and feedback mechanism, the proposed methodology will be: When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed first by a prior identified focal point per the organization who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular feedback/complaint and sensitive feedback /complaint. The user(s) who provided feedback will be continuously updated about the process and final decision through official communication channels.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The form of humanitarian assistance that MdM is providing does not further expose people to physical hazards, violence, or other rights abuse. One of the components of the present proposal aims to increase access to quality services taking into consideration age and gender, and first of all safety for healthcare providers. MdM is taking into consideration the specificity of the current health situation in Gaza Strip with the presence of COVID-19 as well as the mental health wellbeing of the patients. Within the proposed intervention, MdM from a human rights perspective is combating the stigmatization of Mental health including COVID 19 through an awareness campaign as per its consideration to be a violation of human rights and impacting dignity of the patients. Within its intervention, MdM is following specific rules for the data collection, gathering, and publishing based on MdM 2010 GUIDELINES “Ethics in the Field”. As a rule, all MdM team signed the MdM confidentiality charter, MdM is keeping safe storage of the beneficiaries’ data and information. When providing feedback to communities and stakeholders, maintaining confidentiality is critical for MdM and its team. The interest of beneficiaries is always protected, and individual names are not revealed in public or group meetings.We will ensure that medical and mental health teams from MoH and the local partners who are providing medical and MHPSS services to vulnerable community members are well-trained, supervised, and gender-balanced within their distribution, they are part of the local communities and are updated about the cultural, religious, context. We will ensure that the communities are also aware of the protection and complaint mechanisms in place if needed. MdM interventions at the community level are provided by Al Atta association team through outreach to the households which reduce the exposure of communities to harm and provide equal access, including easier access for people with disabilities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender, and inclusion are adopted by MdM within its intervention in Palestine. During MdM’s intervention in 2020, MdM reinforced the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff on gender, GBV, and inclusion of PWD.MdM is addressing the needs of the local community based upon gender mainstreaming approach (gender-specific health), for instance, developing different IEC for women, men, and children, developing different protocols based upon gender-based different manifestations) and also addressing all the barriers preventing PWD to access to services. By its outreach program, MdM can reach the majority of the victims affected by the critical events, by the support provided by a well-prepared Logistic department. MdM MEAL process is taken into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities, and targeted locations. By 2021, MdM will add PWD to all its monitoring, evaluation, and reporting process. Gender mainstreaming is a basic consideration for all the MdM projects that are all following MdM gender policy. Gender analysis is integrated into all the phases of project management such as diagnostic elaboration, objectives, results, activities and indicators design, implementation, and evaluation based on MdM gender policy and PSEA policy. For MdM, gender equality is one of the primary conditions for social sustainability in cooperation for development and emergency interventions. Project activities encourage women to take on decision-making roles and ensure they are participating in project activities.As per the nature of the intervention, the provision of drugs and disposables will be delivered to MoH central drug stores and distributed to the facilities and after to beneficiaries based on the health needs. For MHPSS support, the specific needs per gender will be taken into consideration with the provision of services, a female provider will be available to service patients, age and gender of children also will be taken into consideration while providing the service to their caregiver.
