Project Summary

Since July 2024, rainfall has increased across South Sudan following a slow start, contributing to average to above-average cumulative rainfall as of October 2024 across Jonglei state. This has significantly increased flooding, so far affecting over 470,000 people in 26 counties—mostly in parts of Unity, Jonglei, and causing rising displacement, crop damage, livestock diseases, property and infrastructure damage, and increasing the risk of disease outbreaks. River levels also remain high, at or surpassing Alert and High thresholds as monitored by the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation. The combination of these high river levels, ongoing releases of large volumes of water from Jinja Dam in Uganda, low outflow further north and ongoing above-normal rainfall has led to severe flooding that has caused grave darmage to the food security and livelihood in the country.
RCO seeks to provide Agricultural support to populations affected by crisis in Jonglei (Duk, Uror, Nyirol),counties. The support will be delivered through provision of main and seasonal crop kits and agricultural tools to food insecure and displaced households and with an additional input of either vegetable or ﬁshing kits depending on the suitability of the additional inputs to speciﬁc locations. This will be coupled by capacity building on modern agronomy practices as well as capacity building of fish handling and preservation whilst formation of farmers’ producer groups and formation of fisher folk groups.
The project will target 10,000 HHs (15,000Men, 15,000Women, 15,000Girls & 15,000Boys) vulnerable populations by building their resilience and restoring their assets that were depleted by drought, floods, or conflicts. This will be done through supporting the identified beneficiaries with conditional cash-for assets activities, including cash for restocking. Target group will also be supported with agricultural inputs including Crop and Vegetable kits and tools taking into consideration the seasonal calendar activities whilst riverine beneficiaries will be supported with fishing kits.
RCO will also promote the use of improved agriculture technologies through the distribution of early maturing nutritious dense vegetable kits, high yielding, drought-tolerant & disease tolerant crop varieties to gain short term results coupled by capacity training on improved agronomic practices and ﬁsh preservation/handling techniques and training of farmers on land –harvest, storage and market linkages that will develop communities’ knowledge and skills that will improve food access, increase and build their resilience and future self-reliance by creating and expanding delivery models that support community empowerment and accountability.

1. Needs

2024 South Sudan’s concurrent shocks including a deteriorating economy, soaring prices, high returnee burden, continued conflict and insecurity, and increased flooding are driving high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan. In August, 8-9 million people (>65 percent of the population) are in need of food aid amid escalating flooding affecting hundreds of thousands. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes are prevalent in over half of the counties, mostly located in Jonglei state. Amid ongoing humanitarian food distributions from Julyto November 2024, some households with low asset base and extremely limited coping capacity and restricted access to humanitarian assistance in remote and insecure parts of Jonglei, as well as among returnees and refugees – both those newly arrived and those sheltering in areas of transit are likely facing Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and require urgent assistance. Since July, rainfall has increased across South Sudan following a slow start, contributing to average to above-average cumulative rainfall as of mid-August across most of Jonglei state. This has significantly increased flooding, so far affecting over 470,000 people in 26 counties—mostly in parts of Unity, Jonglei, and causing rising displacement, crop damage, livestock diseases, property and infrastructure damage, and disease outbreaks like cholera. River levels also remain high, at or surpassing Alert and High thresholds as monitored by the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation. The combination of these high river levels, ongoing releases of large volumes of water from Jinja Dam in Uganda, low outflow further north at the Jebel Aulia dam in Sudan, and ongoing above-normal rainfall has led to severe flooding that has submerged crops, and drowned livestock. As of 25 September 2024, the flooding in South Sudan had a devastating impact, affecting more than 735,000 people in 38 counties and Abyei Administrative Area. More than 76,000 people were also reported to be displaced due to the impact of floods. The situation worsened the existing humanitarian crisis in Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Warrap states, causing extensive damage to homes, crops, and critical infrastructure.9 This has further disrupted education, nutrition, and healthcare services for vulnerable communities, exacerbating their hardships. The flooding, combined with economic challenges, food insecurity, intercommunal conflict, and disease outbreaks, has heightened the vulnerabilities of these communities, isolating them and hindering access to essential markets and humanitarian aid. (South Sudan: Flooding Situation Flash Update No. 5 As of 25 September 2024)Given the high levels of severe acute food insecurity in the target counties, Food insecurity is expected to be more severe during 2025 as concurrent shocks persists and macroeconomic conditions further deteriorate. It is in light to this that Relief Corps Organization (RCO) proposes for a project that will immediately scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the targeted counties, particularly those with a high share of populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. Further, the project seeks to build resilience support and enhance a disaster risk reduction (DRR) strategy to mitigate the effects of climate change. All this will be in line with the FSL HRP cluster objectives 2025.

2. Project activities

1. CLFSC/CO1 Post Distribution Monitoring and Evaluation of the Project. 2. CLFSLCO2. Beneﬁciary identiﬁcation, registration and distributing of staple crop kits and nutritious-dense early-maturing, high-yielding and drought-tolerant vegetable kits and tools to 10,000HH in target Counties. 3. CLFSLCO2 Identification, registration of riverine beneficiaries coupled by distribution of fishing kits to 3,000 Households in target counties. 4. CLFSLCO2Establishment and maintenance of 20 demonstration plots and kitchen gardens at nutrition facilities in the target counties. 5. CLFSLCO2With the aid of the trained CAHWs, distribute 10 solar powered cold chain facilities in the targeted locations and vaccinate 30,000 livestock in the target locations. 6. CLFSC/CO3 Identification and training of 100 extension farmers on improved agronomic practices post-harvest management, pest control, preservation/storage, sales and market linkages. This training is aimed at improving their agricultural production and productivity hence strengthening their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption, mitigate irreversible coping mechanisms and build their resilience to withstand future shocks. 7. CLFSC/CO3 Training of 100 extension farmers on pest control including scouting and Fall Army Worm and desert locust surveillance, detection, prevention and mitigation. 8. CLFSC/CO3 Formation and training of 5 farmers’ producer groups of 20 members each on to oversee post-harvest, storage and sales and marketing in the target Counties. 9. CLFSC/CO3 Capacity development and training of 100 CAHWs on livestock handling, treatment and vaccinations and safeguarding basic animal health. 10. CLFSC/CO3 Training on 100 fishermen in the target counties on fish handling procedures processing and preservation methods.
