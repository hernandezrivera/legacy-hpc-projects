Project Summary

HLSS With the proposed intervention intends to respond to the humanitarian needs identified in the HRP prioritized locations of Bor south and Twic East counties of Jonglei state, Magwi county of Eastern Equatoria state and Lainya county of Central Equatoria state. The Humanitarian needs continues to grow in the proposed locations throughout 2020 which got hit by the flooding in Bor south and Twic East for which over thousands of people were displaced, livestock’s destroyed and household properties damaged, and Magwi and Lainya counties with the continued impact of the conflict compounding with the hit by COVID19 crisis which has grossly affected the economy of the country which continues to put the population in a dare need of humanitarian assistance, thus the proposed locations continue to indicate minimal responses from humanitarian actors covering the whole affected population in the prioritized counties. HLSS intends to reach to 21,430 individuals with GBV responses and prevention intervention (Boys, Girls, Women and Men). The total number to be reached with the response is inclusive of persons with disabilities.
With the proposed intervention HLSS will focus on the scaling GBV prevention and response activities in the 4 proposed counties of Bor South, Twic East, Magwi and Lainya, and these shall include the following activities: 1. Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery,
2. Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support. 3. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support. 4. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment
HLSS with the proposed intervention will establish 4 women and girls friendly which will support in facilitating women and girls coming together to run activities such as skills buildings and as well use the women and girls friendly space as a point of sharing experiences and building community based copying mechanism and resilience strategies to crisis. 5. Provision of livelihood and economic support including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectoral GBV response 6. Implement community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle, with the support of hiring community mobilizers, conduct awareness raising/information dissemination to the community about existing services for women and girls in the proposed project intervention sites, this shall promote timely response to GBV cases and also coping mechanisms for women and girls who have undergone any form of abuse by accessing the WGFS established. 7. Engage men and boys for accountable practices to transform harmful social norms that perpetuate gender inequality, and to promote the health and safety of women and girls 8. Provide capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to frontline service providers, HLSS with the proposed intervention shall conduct capacity building training to frontline service providers on GBV basic concepts, Case management, Caring for child survivors, community outreach services and GBVIMS. These trainings will improve the capacity of the front line service providers in delivering services with ethical considerations and putting in place global standards and protocol of GBV service delivery.

1 - Needs

Two years after South Sudan signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. Protection concerns continued flagging throughout 2020. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. Furthered with communities having got hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19.1.6 million people continued to remain internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labor and child marriage. The economy continued to spiral downwards, pushing people to the brink, especially in urban areas. Access to essential services, including health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, continues to be limited and much of the service infrastructure damaged, destroyed or closed in 2020 due to the different situations (flooding, .Humanitarian assistance delivered to more than 6 million people kept many communities from falling into deeper need, however increased violence against aid workers and assets and operational interference prevented hundreds of thousands of vulnerable people from predictably accessing the support they needed. People’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms continued to deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020. (According to South Sudan HNO 2021). Lainya County in particular experienced confrontation in Dec/2020 this left some people killed and other injured, livestock (goats and chicken) and properties looted, the elderly were tortured and some families displaced to Lainya town. After the above incident, it did not take long when crises erupted between the Mundari pastoralists and the Pojulu community erupted on the Jan/2021 where some people were killed, some injured and as well cattle’s raided and properties destroyed. The crisis affected the Payams of Lainya, Kenyi, Kupera and Wuji. All those who are displaced continue to be of dare need to humanitarian assistance of protection, Health, WASH, NFI and Food. (according to the inter-agency assessment conducted on Jan 2020)Flooding and inter-communal violence continues to threaten and worry the population in Twic East. Given the situation of insecurity, heavy rainfall and flooding that led to shortage of food, displacement of people, loss of properties, and lack of access to basic services, among others. Accessibility for intervention becomes so challenging. The raiders take advantage to terrorize the communities living in those affected areas. The continuous attacks and flood has led to increased vulnerability compared to limited humanitarian assistance. Similar to Bor south County the heavy rainfall resulted into flooding causing displacement, hindering accessing to humanitarian services, loss of properties and live stocks this continues to present protection concerns for women and girls, child and the early. (according to IRAN assessment report July/2020). Looking at the above different drivers of the needs for the humanitarian stated, HLSS with the proposed intervention will scale up GBV prevention and responses services for the affected population in the four counties.

2 - Project activities

With the proposed intervention HLSS will focus on the scaling GBV prevention and response activities in the 4 proposed counties of Bor South, Twic East, Magwi and Lainya, and these shall include the following activities:1. Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery, Psychosocial support helps to address a person's emotional, social, mental and spiritual needs – all essential elements of positive human development. It helps an individual or a group of people to physically, economically, educational, health wise and socially have reliance build. Psychosocial support also helps build resilience of crisis and incidents one goes through. It helps individuals and communities to heal the psychological wounds and rebuild social structures after an emergency or a critical event. It can help change people into active survivors rather than passive victims. With this intervention HLSS will ensure individual and group PSS shall be provided in all the proposed project locations. 2. Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support Case management is “a collaborative, multidisciplinary process which assesses, plans, implements, coordinates, monitors and evaluates options and services to meet an individual’s needs through communication and available resources to promote quality, effective outcomes.” Thus through this intervention HLSS will ensure that safe and confidential spaces will be created and as well train GBV case workers to support survivors of GBV with case management. This will help survivors achieve wellness and autonomy through advocacy, comprehensive assessment, planning, communication, health education and engagement, resource management, service facilitation, and use of evidence-based guidelines or standards. 3. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support. A Referral Pathway Guideline facilitates primary duty bearers and actors with information on how to respond to GBV cases and to guide the victims/ survivors of GBV on where to seek assistance and what services are available at different referral points. HLSS will establish new a referral pathway in the proposed locations which do not have referral pathways, first a service mapping shall be conducted to establish the existing partners with services on ground and then pathway developed, meanwhile in locations where there will be exiting referral pathways HLSS will ensure to strengthen and update the exiting referral pathways and this will help to outline the various support from partners and have active referral services, that involve ongoing support from a case worker, and monitoring and review through regular case conferences including all who are involved. At the end of time this helps survivors to have a comprehensive support through the referral system developed and established 4. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowermentA Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) is a formal or informal place where women and girls feel physically and emotionally safe, comfortable, and able to express themselves. HLSS with the proposed intervention will establish 4 women and girls friendly which will support in facilitating women and girls coming together to run activities such as skills buildings and as well use the women and girls friendly space as a point of sharing experiences and building community based copying mechanism and resilience strategies to crisis. These spaces further provide women and adolescent girls with a safe entry point for services and a place to access information. Safe gathering points also offer them an opportunity to engage with each other, exchange information, and rebuild community networks and support. In this way, safe spaces can be a key way of building women’s and girls’ s

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HLSS has PSEA reporting mechanism in place in all its project locations, which include hot line for reporting, email address for ethics and integrity. HLSS further has embedded PSEA in all staff contracts and code of contacts which all staffs sign from the time of joining the organization and as well whenever staff’s contract is being renewed. HLSS further conducts PSEA training to all staffs and this is conducted bi-yearly every year and every staff is to adhere to the polices and there is zero tolerance of PSEA for all staffs at work place and as well during service provision for the beneficiaries. Therefore during service delivery HLSS will ensure to conduct awareness to the beneficiaries making that all the services are at no cost and they are never attached in return of any goods from them in order to receive the said services, secondly HLSS will ensure to aware the beneficiaries on the importance of reporting any staffs that has asked them in return of the services they are reciving or attached conditions of sexual in nature for services to be recived and letting them know what ever is reported will be treated with confidentiality and managed in safe manner by investigation team.HLSS will further ensure that the poster containing the hotline and email address for reporting will be put in all the service points and educate the beneficiaries on the use.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HLSS will ensure that PWDs are always considered with 5% of all services to be provided and thus separate feedback meetings will be conducted with them and responses acted accordingly throughout the implementation period. Further HLSS will ensure material assistance with be in place support the vulnerable women and girls and there feedback captured and incorporated in all the activities. Lastly HLSS will ensure that the project design will factor all categories of beneficiaries even those that are not literate and they will be engaged in other hand activities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HLSS with its current projects have a strong AAP mechanisms established in all the field locations and this has been proved effective with the implementations in line of engaging the communities in the project activities such as, AAP meetings conducted with the community leaders both male and female representation, Feedback meetings conducted with beneficiaries (women, men, girls and boys) at service points, client satisfactory survey as well conducted with survivors to ensure they are engaged on the various services they receive and well HLSS factors their feedback. Post distribution feedbacks are always been conducted with the beneficiaries when dignity kits are distributed and as well IGA seed funds. These post distribution feedback meetings with beneficiaries help in keeping the community engaged and feedbacks collected to improve on the service delivery. Monthly feedback meetings conducted by HLSS with the community which basically looks in to the responses of the feedback received from the community in the course of the month. HLSS will ensure to have all these rolled out in the intended proposed locations for this project to keep the community engaged throughout the project implementation period.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback that HLSS will receive from beneficiaries during the project implementation shall be adapted during indicator reviews and as well factored during activity selection at the Women and girls friendly spaces.
