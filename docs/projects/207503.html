Project Summary

The IRC’s proposed project anchors on four key areas: comprehensive service delivery for GBV survivors; transformational prevention programming; raising awareness and mitigating GBV risks; and capacity building and coordination to reduce the risk, promote resilience, and aid recovery. Aligned with the IASC Minimum Standards for GBV in Emergencies and the 2024 JRP strategic objective, the project will ensure comprehensive GBV response and prevention in camps (5, 13, 14,15,16 18, 22, and 25) and Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong, Raja Palong, Ratna Palong, Unions in Ukhiya Upazila, and Nhilla, Sabrang , Baharchhara Union in Teknaf. Component 1 Transformational prevention programming. Promote positive social norms and behavior change with EMAP+, targeting adolescent boys and men. Incorporate women and girls’ voices to inform the curriculum, fostering male allies, and challenging norms contributing to violence. The AWARNESS and SUPPORT phases of SASA! Together will be implemented to address root cause of GBV. Implement SASA! Together's AWARNESS and SUPPORT phases to address root causes of GBV. Implement the Girl Shine early marriage curriculum to prevent and respond to child marriage, equipping girls with skills for a successful transition into adulthood. Introduce Girl Shine Caregiver Curriculum for a healthy family. Enhance well-being with Safe@Home, addressing co-occurring IPV and child maltreatment. Develop an integrated SBCC strategy, seamlessly integrating with other sectors. Component 2: Comprehensive service delivery for GBV survivors, Ensure availability, accessibility, and acceptability of quality GBV case management within the WGSS & integrated protection centers to:- a) enhance access to knowledge, skills and a range of services including SRHR; b) improve psychosocial well-being and create social networks; c) access specialized services for GBV survivors and; d) feel safe and encouraged to use their voices and collectively raise attention to their rights and needs. Collect, store, and analyze case management data in-line with the information sharing protocol using GBVIMS+(ver:2). Provide dignity kits, and lifesaving assistance to diverse women and girls at risk including GBV survivors. Component 3: Raising awareness and mitigating GBV risks. Conduct safety audits and risk mapping with community groups to identify potential GBV risks. Women Support, Community Watch, and Self-help Groups prepare and implement risk mitigation plans to respond to, prevent protection risks and avoid negative coping mechanisms, for a safe & protective environment, and build social cohesion. Conduct awareness campaigns and sessions, targeting men, boys, women and girls, Gender diverse people to transform gender norms and foster respectful relationships. Commemoration of major events, 16 Days of Activism against GBV, and other days. Economic and Social Empowerment (EA$E) will be implemented to reduce IPV, increase women's decision-making authority, and decrease tolerance of violence. Women Rise, a group PSS curriculum that fosters a supportive environment for women to address stress, develop coping skills, recognize their strengths, and collaborate on problem-solving. Component 4 Capacity building and coordination. Enhance government and structural coordination for a safer environment using a socio-ecological framework. Strengthen inter-departmental coordination, leading the case management group working to facilitate timely access to services. The project focuses on 200 gender-diverse populations and 11,669 individuals with disabilities, providing comprehensive GBV services, prevention, and awareness. IRC's partner, Bandhu, builds capacity among community members, service providers, and law enforcement, addressing gender, sexuality, and challenges faced by gender-diverse populations. IRC collaborates with MOWCA and DSS, coordinating GBV services and PERU at Coxs Bazar and camp levels.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

With 965,467 Rohingya refugees currently stateless in Cox’s Bazar, the Rohingya Refugee Crisis is a protracted crisis. With no solution in sight, crucial gaps still exist in provision of and access to GBV programming in the camps and host communities; 95% of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. IRC GBVIMS Annual Report 2023 revealed, GBV is a significant issue, with intimate partner violence (IPV) being the most frequent incidents (93.02% as reported to IRC facilities). The top three types of GBV reported were physical assault (54.8%); denial of resources, opportunities, or services (22%); and psychological abuse (19.1%). most incidents occur at survivors' homes, rendering it an unsafe space for women and girls. Contributing factors include oppressive gender norms, increased hardship, overcrowding, heightened aggression, and limited privacy. Gender norms rooted in patriarchal culture led to pervasive IPV. Economic insecurity, such as food scarcity, triggered tensions, and violence at home. (Gender Analysis, IRC September 2023). Women and girls’ risk of GBV is heightened by disasters such as cyclone, flooding, and fire which the camps and host communities are prone to (2023 safety audit report, IRC). Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (JMSNA) ,2023 by the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) highlights protection concerns as a top priority in the refugee camps (48%), along with low access to services (17%). According to the JMSNA, Host community largely favor community-based dispute resolution mechanisms (49%), while refugees community rely heavily on CiCs 58% and Majhi 54%, and only 14% and 10% will refer someone who has experience GBV to health and women friendly space facilities respectively. Access to SRHR for women and girls in Cox’s Bazar remains low. With 24% of women within the reproductive age bracket, only 46% of deliveries take place in healthcare facilities. The maternal mortality rate in the camps remains a concern, with a rate of 319 deaths per 100,000 live births reported by WHO in February 2023 (the baseline rate of 326/100,000 live births). According to IRC GBVIMS data 2023, Women and adolescent girls face enormous environmental, social, cultural, and psychological barriers when reporting GBV incidents, suggesting the real level of violence against women and girls in camps and host communities is much higher. Marginalized groups including persons living with disabilities (PWD – 12% of the camp population) face specific vulnerabilities and are subject to high rates of emotional, sexual, and physical GBV. Power and gender norms stemming from patriarchal culture permeated and perpetuated violence in the home. Women were expected to obey men in all situations, even when men were not fulfilling their traditional gender roles. Harsh punishment for girls was attributed to potential impacts on the family’s reputation and more violent treatment of boys was attributed to beliefs that males are stronger and thus able to withstand tougher physical punishment than girls (IRC Gender Analysis in camp and host, October 2023). In this report, economic insecurity was identified as a risk factor for GBV. Evaluation the Effectiveness of Protection Structures report (conducted by IRC October 2023) in Cox's Bazar's host community found that, Transgender individuals are vulnerable and at risk. Girls are particularly at risk due to cultural norms leading to issues like child marriage and early pregnancy. Both girls and boys, especially from marginalized communities, face mobility restrictions and safety concerns, hindering education and posing risks of sexual violence. Comprehensive gender analysis report by GiHA in March 2022 found that there is not enough protection, support, and awareness for gender-diverse groups, especially the marginalized Hijra community, in humanitarian efforts. This lack is causing increased vulnerability and discrimination.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The IRC’s proposed project aligns with rationalization principles, including Principle 3 (integrated approach), Principle 6 (transparency of information), and Principle 4 (predictability and continuity). Adhering to these principles, GBVSS completed the rationalization process, preparing map based 5W data, agency priorities, fair distribution, a complete GBV full package, service gap analysis, service audits, and discussions among agencies. Contributed and shaped the rationalization process in GBVSS as a member of SAG. Covered camps 5, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 22, and 25, along with host communities in 2023. IRC prioritizes integrated protection (GBV, child protection, Protection rule of law) and DRR services; Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong, Raja Palong, Ratna Palong Unions in Ukhiya. Established integrated protection centers (multipurpose) offering child protection, GBV, and legal services in those locations. In all WGSS, case management and integrated Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) services including CMR services, SRH and information sessions will be delivered by midwives. IRC’s education sector provides basic literacy and numeracy training in all WGSS in 2024 and integrate life skill services with concern worldwide livelihood programs in camps 13, 14, 15, 16, and 22. The integrated Safe@Home program improves family well-being by addressing intimate partner violence and child maltreatment. EMAP Plus, targets the prevention of Violence Against Women and Girls by engaging men and boys in behavior change. IRC's rationalization integrates services, minimizes gaps and is very cost effective. Program stability is ensured through reliable fundraising strategy with additional funding sought for programs. Sustain services in new locations (Nnila) by addressing gaps in GBV full package. The focus is on active participation in camp-based coordination, maintaining relations with the government, especially CiCs and MOWCA, and ensuring seamless coordination among service providers.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project is committed to prioritizing the protection of affected populations and contributing to protection outcomes in humanitarian actions. The project will employ global protection mainstreaming toolkit and SPHERE standards to incorporate protection principles throughout the project cycle, including non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety & dignity, ‘do no harm’, accountability, participation, and empowerment. continue to strengthen the technical capacity of partners' protection mainstreaming focal points (PMFPs). This includes a protection risk assessment, project-specific protection mainstreaming action plan (PMAP), and mitigation measures. The scorecard and PMAP will be completed in the first six months, with quarterly provide remedial support to partners as necessary to mitigate risks and promote client accountability and participation by adopting industry and DG ECHO protection mainstreaming indicators, tools, and client satisfaction surveys on a quarterly basis. The project will also adhere to IASC guidelines on the inclusion of Person with disabilities in humanitarian action. The project will focus on protecting diverse women, girls, and GBV survivors by designing, implementing, and monitoring all activities while considering protection risks and potential violations. Particular attention will be dedicated to protecting the affected population from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation. Activities enhance rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming, considering gender, age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness. Additionally, all activities are designed with a protection-mainstreaming lens, promoting safety, dignity, meaningful access, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries across the response.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will strengthen GBV analysis and program design, aiming to understand and address their experiences, needs, and risks. It will ensure safe and dignified access to GBV services through activities such as service mapping, the recruitment of diverse staff and volunteers, tailored case management training, and monitoring of service access. Meaningful participation of specific needs of Persons with Disability, the project in conducting safety audits to identify attitudinal, physical, and institutional barriers to access assistance and services, and create more scope for the empowerment through the protection interventions. The project is designed to be inclusive and responsive to the safety needs of women who are older and adolescent girls, recognizing the intersection of identities. It integrates a safeguarding policy into every program phase and engages the affected group in meaningful consultations regarding decisions affecting their lives. Age-sensitive psychosocial support (PSS) and referral mechanisms will be developed based on consultations to ensure access for girls and women of all ages. Adopt a gender-sensitive approach with specific interventions aimed at moving towards gender-transformative programming for all gender groups. It will strengthen the capacity of stakeholders to transform norms, address underlying protection risks, and enable local partners to deliver integrated and inclusive protection services for diverse groups. IRC's Signature programs, such as Girl Shine's New module and EMAP Plus, will be contextualized and structured based on the gender and age of participants to facilitate value-driven discussions and skills development. To meet diverse need the project will address gaps, inequalities, and exclusions for various women and girls, including those with different ages, abilities, sexual orientations, gender identities, ethnicities, and religious affiliations.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IRC and all implementing partners have robust Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) Frameworks in place both globally and specifically in Cox’s Bazar. The project's AAP approach aligns with the IASC’s commitments on AAP, emphasizing the integration of AAP principles and practices into all aspects of humanitarian programming. Consult with diverse women and girls during GBV assessments, design feedback and complaints mechanisms, and ensure AAP-related questions are incorporated into templates. IRC's primary goal is to comprehensively understand the level of satisfaction with services, identify community needs, concerns, hopes, and preferences, and incorporate these insights into program design to enhance intervention quality. The participation of affected populations, including marginalized groups, is an essential and ongoing component of the program, involving in-depth participatory research and frequent community consultations. An effective and accessible Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) is already established, allowing communities to provide feedback, raise complaints, improve referral mechanisms, and facilitate two-way communication between IRC/implementing partners and the communities. Prioritize strengthening the CFRM for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Maintain PSEA Network membership and provide PSEA training to all staff, volunteers, and contractors. The project will regularly collect community voices to support advocacy, allocate budgets for AAP activities, assess SEA risks, and ensure affected groups can register complaints and provide feedback during implementation. Information on accessing services will be widely disseminated, and dignity kits will be customized to diverse needs. GBV referral pathways updated and shared within PSEA networks and program teams, and conduct orientation on AAP-related actions and principles, including PSEA for all.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IRC is dedicated to mainstreaming Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within the project through a comprehensive framework that places the rights and well-being of individuals affected by PSEA at the forefront. A survivor-centered approach will be maintained, with strict adherence to confidentiality at all stages of the process, ensuring that the survivor's safety and dignity are always prioritized during reporting, investigation, and assistance. IRC will strengthen the complaint referral procedures with multiple channels for reporting. Channels designed in line with age, gender, disability, culturally, and contextually appropriate. IRC will ensure that whistleblower protection is in place, safeguarding staff members against any retaliation for reporting misconduct, with clear whistleblower policies and practices communicated to all personnel. IRC will collaborate with GBV Sub-Sectors and PSEA Network and relevant organizations to ensure that survivors have access to appropriate services and assistance, irrespective of the investigation's outcome. Investigation and follow-up for PSEA cases remain the responsibility of the concerned organization, based on its internal procedures. IRC will provide assistance in identifying appropriate resources for the investigation, either from within the PSEA Network or accredited regional resources. IRC is committed to enhancing the capacity of its implementing partners through tailored training and support to ensure effective project implementation and adherence to PSEA and ethical standards. All IRC’s local implementing partners will report SEA incidents. The appointment of safeguarding focal points will further strengthen IRC's commitment to PSEA and support the successful implementation of policies and the creation of awareness. IRC will ensure accessible and culturally appropriate channels for SEA survivors and fostering awareness to reinforce community-level PSEA mechanisms.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will incorporate Disaster Risk Management (DRM) and climate change measures to reduce the disaster risk at the individual, community, and institutional level. DRM will be integrated to strengthen GBV risk management through project’s gender & diversity sensitive GBV analysis and program design, to better understand and addressing the context specific. GBV assessment and safety audits will be conducted incorporating disaster risk identification, and the project's budget will allocate funds for DRR/DRM-related activities. GBV case management in emergency training will be tailored by covering multi-hazard preparedness and response, including Fire Safety Assessment & Drill. Customize dignity kits, strengthen referral pathways for reducing GBV risk during disaster. Provide onsite PSS support to women and girls by the mobile case workers during emergencies, and the project will closely monitor service access, incorporating DRR indicators into the results framework. empower women and girls to become agents of change, promoting resilience, sustainability, and equitable development in the face of climate change and disaster. The DRM integration will mainly emphasize reducing protection risks and negative coping strategies, such as child marriage and hazardous child labor in consequence of disaster impact. Provide training to caregivers and parents to support. Train stakeholders on gender analysis and assessment for disaster preparedness and response to identify the needs of affected women, girls, and children and empower individuals and households to address climate challenges and improve GBV objectives effectively. These efforts will be facilitated through community structures and develop comprehensive disaster risk mitigation plans specific to the emergency protection response. The WGSS women's committee, self-help group, and GBV watch group will be trained on multi-hazard preparedness and response.
