Project Summary

ARC proposed activities are in line with the Outcome 2 in the Humanitarian response plan (HRP), i.e. Displaced populations, refugees, returnees and host communities meet their basic needs and/or access basic services while increasing their self-reliance. The integrated primary health care interventions will be provided with key objective to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with disease among the children under five years, men, women, adolescent boys and girls, women of child bearing age, elderly men and women and people with disability.
ARC has been implementing comprehensive integrated primary health care life-saving interventions supporting 25 primary health care centers; 10 PHCCs in East Darfur and 15 PHCCs in South Darfur. Under this project, ARC will continue provision of basic primary health care services; Treatment and diagnosis of communicable diseases, reproductive health, support of referrals for both medical and obstetric emergencies to tertiary level, emergency preparedness and response, prevention activities through health promotion activities by community volunteers, routine immunization, provision of essential drugs, medical and non medical supplies, clinical management of rape to gender based violence survivors and provision of Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) services. ARC will also strengthen community-based approaches to promote positive health seeking behaviors, recognition of illnesses, and care-seeking practices through trained community structures, community relief development committees and health, nutrition and WASH volunteers network.
The project will strengthen institutional capacity through on job training and mentoring to implement sustainable and affordable health interventions that meet the needs of men, women, boys, girls, and children <5 and will utilize the network of WASH, Health and nutrition volunteers to enhance the disease surveillance system including early detection, preparedness and response to emergencies. These activities are in line with the cluster objectives, which are to provide the basic minimum package of primary health care services, including maternal and child health, emergency referral services, training of human resources for health emergencies, and monitoring of health services
In coordination with line ministries, ARC will ensure the health facilities adhere to approved treatment protocols/guidelines, provision and availability of vaccine and vaccine supplies for routine immunization, support during the acceleration campaigns, national immunization days and celebration of global health international events. In addition to improve the quality of services and build the capacity of the health frontline staffs and line ministries, ARC will conduct refresher trainings and joint monitoring visits with State ministry of health (SMoH) respectively.
Through a well-structured Health Information System (HIS), ARC will capture data and submit weekly epidemiology reports to the SMoH including key health indicators on a monthly basis. Data collected will be dis-aggregated by sex and age.
This project will target the IDPs and host communities in South and East Darfur State. Total population target will include 535, 770; 341,172 IDPs and 194,598 host community. 17% (91,081) will include children under five years and 21,431(4%) pregnant and lactating women including the elderly and people with disability.

Needs

The protracted displacement has depleted the communities’ coping mechanisms and strained limited resources and available services. Despite the humanitarian interventions, Darfur continues to be affected by high morbidity rates of communicable disease with frequent occurrence of disease outbreaks and high mortality. The line ministries responsible for service delivery do not have sufficient capacity and resources to cover the huge needs for basic services in Darfur. This situation, coupled with the significant decrease in the resources allocated to address the humanitarian needs of the IDPs in Darfur, puts further pressure on existing donors and implementing organizations, including ARC, to take more responsibility to address the critical lifesaving humanitarian needs of the target populations. Sudan, which is home to some of the worst health indicators in the world, has a maternal mortality (MMR) of 311 deaths per 100,000 live births. In South Darfur, MMR is 334 deaths per 100,000 live births. Limited access to lifesaving primary and reproductive health services and acute shortage of medical supplies contribute to these poor indicators, which are exacerbated by a shortage and inequitable distribution of health workers, as well as a weak health information system (HIS). According to ARC health facility data report from January 2018 to January 2019, 533,352 communicable diseases were diagnosed and treated at 25 ARC supported health facilities. Out this 57816 (11%) were diarrheal cases with 206 (0.4%) as acute watery diarrhea (AWD). Acute respiratory infections (ARI) represented 11% and Malaria 8% among others. For the purpose to reduce morbidity and mortality among the men, women, girls and boys, elderly women and men including people with disability, ARC will continue in close collaboration with line ministries to strength the disease surveillance, health information system, mentor and coach frontline health workers to improve provision of quality of services.

Activities

- All PHCCs will provide minimum basic health package, include outpatient consultation, communicable disease management, maternal and child health services.- support referral to secondary and tertiary level facilities for complications, including emergency obstetrical care, that cannot be treated at the PHCCs.- provision of essentials drugs, medical and non- medical supplies, vaccine, reproductive health supplies through collaboration with WHO and SMoH.- Supports SMoH through salary coverage and capacity building of SMoH seconded health facility staff. - Conduct joint supportive supervision with SMoH.- ARC will work with the health, nutrition and WASH joint community structures, the CRDCs, and community volunteer network to increase the role of community participation.-support SMoH to conduct accelerated immunization campaigns and NID for Polio and Vitamin A and tetanus toxoid (TT) supplementation. ARC will conduct monthly immunization outreach activities in facilities that do not have cold chain.- participate in the emergency preparedness and response planning for disease outbreaks.ARC actively coordinates with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH), WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA and other local and international agencies. ARC participates in the weekly Health and monthly Health cluster coordination meetings. ARC collaborates regularly with SMoH on program staffing, joint monitoring of project activities. ARC receives anti-malaria treatment and ICT from SMoH, and rapid response kits from WHO. ARC Sudan understands and seeks to prevent, mitigate the potential risks, including all forms of violations on the affected persons through continuous interactive analysis of risks and threats. ARC will continue to identify measures to reduce protection risks, including stopping and preventing violations, avoiding and reinforcing existing patterns of violence, abuse, coercion or deprivation and working towards restoring safety and dignity to people’s lives.
