Project Summary



The purpose of this project is to promote optimal nutrition practices, prevent and treat acute malnutrition in the most vulnerable population groups like children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers. These aims will be fulfilled through implementation of community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) interventions in Rumbek Centre, Wulu, Cueibet counties of Lakes state. The project interventions, such as prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition in children under five years, MIYCN, are in line with the MOH South Sudan national health policy (2016-2026) focus areas, health needs, and priorities and South Sudan’s Humanitarian Response Plan 2022. The project implementation period will be from January 1, 2023 - December 31, 2023 and shall benefit the most vulnerable groups of the population of IDPs, returnees and host population. This project will focus on the management of children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM), pregnant and lactating mothers with acute malnutrition and promotion and prevention of acute malnutrition through maternal infant and young child nutrition interventions. Furthermore, preventive activities like MIYCN counselling, health, nutrition and Wash education /promotion, setting up demonstration gardens, complementary food cooking demonstration and distribution of wash and other preventive NFIs (like soap, aqua tab, mosquito net etc.) will be done at nutrition sites and at community level. As part of treatment protocol, all children with MAM and SAM will be screened and treated for malaria according to the national protocol / or referred to nearby health facilities. Furthermore, preventive kits (jerry cans, Mosquito nets, and soaps) shall be provided to mothers /caregivers of children who are enrolled for treatment in OTP or TSFP or SC sites as these children are at higher risk of being infected and to promote proper personal hygiene and sanitation practice. All children who will be discharged cured from OTPs will be enrolled to TSFP programs for full nutritional recovery and those deteriorated will be linked to SC. Besides, if a child in TSFP is nutritionally deteriorated, he/she will beenrolled/referred to OTP/SC program. Those who recovered shall be given protection ration for two weeks and referred to other (FSL, BSFP) services. When there is deworming and vitamin A supplementation campaign, this project will support to ensure children under five years old in the counties get the services in collaboration with SMOH/CHD, UNICEF, WFP and the health partner in the county. Furthermore, PI will participate in FSNMS, SMART and other joint assessments that may happen in the counties of operation. PI will implement OTP and TSFP programme together in Rumbek Centre (21 nutrition sites), Wulu (13) and Cueibet (14) counties and Stabilization centres in Rumbek Centre (1) and Cueibet (1) counties. In addition to the aforementioned interventions, PI will implement BSFP in Boma, Labrab and Maruwa payams of Pibor County and Rumbek North and Cueibet counties of Lake State. In total, this project will benefit 141786 populations (boys: 59892, girls: 62337, women: 19557, people with disability: 1418) in different services like treatment for acute malnutrition, MIYCN counselling, Vitamin A supplementation, deworming, blanket supplementary feeding and other interventions.

1 - Needs

According to the latest IPC report (Published on November 3, 2022), about 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54%), are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse between October and November. Of those, 2.2 million people are experiencing worse conditions in Emergency(IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and an estimated 61,000 people in Catastrophe (IPCPhase 5) acute food insecurity in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Akobo of Jonglei State; PiborCounty in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area. The most food insecure states between October and November 2022, where more than 50% of their populations are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, are Jonglei (68%), Unity (66%), Northern Bahrel Ghazal (62%), Upper Nile (58%), Warrap (57%), and Lakes (57%). In the post-harvest projection period of December 2022 to March 2023, an estimated 6.31 million people (51.0% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 33,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo and Fangak of Jonglei State; and Pibor County of Greater PiborAdministrative Area. During this period, an estimated 1.97 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2023, an estimated 7.8 million people (63% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo, Canal/Pigi and Fangakcounties of Jonglei State; and Leer and Mayendit counties of Unity State. Between July2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children un- Acute Malnutrition | January -December 2023 Projected Acute Food Insecurity | April - July 2023 der five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) and program admission trends. Approximately 345,893 will need SAM treatment, while another 1,057,703 will need MAM treatment. Acute malnutrition burden will be highly concentrated in the four states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap in, about 61% of the total burden. As for the severity of the situation, during July and October, 30 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical), 15 counties in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious), 19counties in IPC AMN Phase 2 (Alert) and 13 in IPC AMN Phase 1 (Acceptable). Overall, in the post-harvest period, counties in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) and IPC AMN Phase 4(Critical) will likely increase from 45 to 56. The lean season will likely see a further deterioration in all counties, thus increasing the number of counties in IPC AMN Phase 3(Serious) and IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) to 59 counties. In July 2022, Plan International (Plan) conducted a rapid needs assessment using Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) in children 6-59 months in Lakes (Cueibet County). The assessment found that 10% of those screened (five of 50 children) were malnourished and the majority (80%) were enrolled in nutrition centres. 56% and 88% of children under five years screened were dewormed and received Vitamin A supplements in the past six months. Given the high rate of acute food insecurity coupled with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macro-economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, and low agricultural production, it will be mandatory to continue providing preventive and curative nutrition services in the three counties.

2 - Project activities

The key activities in this project are: - Nutrition, health and WASH education in side nutrition sites and at outreach sites- Providing Maternal, Infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to mothers/caregivers- Screening for acute malnutrition- Admission and treatment of children 6-59 with sever and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM) and Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with acute malnutrition • - Regular collection (from WFP and UNICEF) and delivery of nutrition supplies to nutrition sites- Doing Exhaustive MUAC assessment - Participating in FSNMS and other assessments like IRNA - Establish/promotion of kitchen gardens at nutrition sites and in the community. - Distribution of preventive items like soap, aqua tab, mosquito net- Training of Nutrition officers, nurses, Clinical officers, community nutrition workers and community nutrition volunteers (CNV) on Community management of acute malnutrition(CMAM) , MIYCN, child protection, Gender, GBV, WASH- Supporting Vitamin supplementation and deworming campaigns- Train the mother support groups on Maternal infant and young child nutrition, nutrition screening, identification of malnourished children and PLW and referral •- Start-up and close up workshop - Printing and distribution of visibility materials- Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes, suggestion books and regular meetings with beneficiaries in the SC and OTP/TSFP sites- Training of CNWs and CNVs and other nutrition cadres on identification of children under five with Acute watery diarrhoea (AWD), treat using ORS and Zinc as a conjugate regiment- Construction/rehabilitation of latrines, water points, nutrition facilities (screening areas, waiting area, storage facilities) hand washing facilities and garbage pits

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Pl will apply high quality risk management standards to manage all possible risks of the project in all the three counties where this project is going to be implemented. The key identified risks and mitigation measures are listed below. Insecurity: PI has a Security and Access Manager at Juba level and a Security Coordinator in Pibor and Lakes who continuously monitors and updates staff on security issues to ensure safety when moving between locations in all project areas including. Pl has also conducted a security assessment of its operations in GPAA and Lakes counties and has in place security SOPs and response measures for identified risks. Furthermore, PI shares security information with different partners and UNDSS operating in the counties/states. When there is real or perceived security threat and limited accessibility to target locations staff will adhere to the security policies and procedures. PI’s policy of recruiting majority of staff and volunteers from project communities provide an additional layer in managing security risks at the local level. Financial: Financial risks, including fraud, will be managed by applying strong controls in procurement, payment and documentation. All procurement and financial transactions will be subjected to relevant checks and scrutiny based on delegated levels of authority. Contractors will be thoroughly screened against required watch lists to ensure regulatory compliance. Operational: Operational risks such as access and logistical difficulties are analyzed and managed by a dedicated project manager and the Program Area Implementation Manager (PIAM). Weekly operational reviews will be held between the PIAM and the project manager to ensure that new challenges are attended to with speed. Where needed, additional operational support will be provided from relevant PI staff in Juba. Personnel Management: Personnel related risks will be managed through line managers and specialized teams within the human resource department. An HR officer has been stationed in Pibor to help in managing personnel risks. Weather conditions – rainy season in Pibor hinders access and running out of supplies in nutrition sites. This will be mitigated by prepositioning supplies and strategically planning activity periods. Wrongful conduct (e.g. fraud, Sexual Abuse and Exploitation) by staff, local actors and / or others: - This could result in harm to beneficiaries and negatively impact on the efficiency and effectiveness of the operation. Plan International staff will sign the child protection policy and a code of conduct making commitments to refrain from wrongful conduct and enhance PSEA, anti-fraud and anti-terrorism practices as well as follow protocol reporting system. The organization will exercise absolute zero tolerance to any breach. Remind Plan International’s staff on these through several instruments / documents including the sub-agreement and the purchase orders contain these at the back Looting of food affecting project performance and beneficiaries not getting service: - PI will work closely with local leaders and community to ensure nutrition supplies are kept safe and if they fail to secure it is difficult to provide service to malnourished children and PLWs. Further, PI will employ enough security guards after background check and fences nutrition sites and any construct storage space when possible.
