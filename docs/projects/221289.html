Project Summary

This project in Belo, Jakiri, Ndop, Babessi, and Bamenda II seeks to mitigate (PPFA, PSS, livelihood, sanitation kits,) the effect of GBV incidences on GBV survivors and reduce further harm on them. Protect (identification papers, local/national/international policies, sanitation facilities, partnerships among actors to provide quality inclusive services, risk reduction, Self organized community women groups empowerment etc.) IDPs and vulnerable women/girls in host communities against harmful gender norms, GBV, exploitation and exclusion of any form in the process of services access. Quantified outputs include 5 safe spaces, 5 community of practice (surveillance bodies), 2 sanitation facilities, 10000 women and girls IDPS and from host communities targeted, 125 GBV survivors and those at risk, 125 duty bearers, 125 Mayors/councillors/staff. The strategy includes (i) building partnerships horizontally (among IDPS and host community members) and vertically (among IDPs, host communities and duty bearers). (ii) Increasing access to/utilization of multi-sectoral services among GBV survivors, IDPs and vulnerable host women/girls’ population. (iii) Strengthening awareness/understanding of GBV, gender, gender mainstreaming, inclusive service delivery, lobbying for reforms at all levels among Stakeholders for IDPS and host communities. (iv) Strengthened leadership/decision making skills of community women groups for their protection and holding Duty bearers accountable, (v) Strengthened existing Community protection mechanisms on strategies to promote GBV prevention and protection of IDP against any form of community exploitation, exclusion, and stigmatization with hate speech. Principles and approaches will be strictly respected in the implementation of the project. GBV work principle, PFA action principles, humanitarian principles, Do no harm principle, leave no one behind principle and human rights-based approaches, Survivor center approach, Age and diversity-based approach and must importantly the Human security approach.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The North-West and South-West socio-political crisis started by teachers and lawyers turned into an armed conflict between state secured militia and Non-State Arm Groups (NSAGs) resulting to forced displacement of populations to secure areas. About 65,500 people to Nigeria (UNHCR, Nigeria: Registration of Cameroonian refugees, 30 June 2021) and 712,800 IDPs in Cameroon. About 80% are IDPs in the West (162,726) and Littoral (80,925) regions (OCHA, IOM and CHOI, MSNAs August and September 2020). The IDPs are exacerbating existing social, economic, and political stress and they are experiencing livelihood challenges and in competition for scarce jobs, especially in lower skilled and lower-wage jobs with host population in the 5 municipalities of Belo, Jakiri, Ndop, Babessi and Bamenda II in the North West. IDPs in these new environments are confronted with economic problems as they have limited access to finance, capacity building all through with entrepreneurial spirit. The shock of displacement and lack of economic power particularly among women and girls is exposing them to varied exploitation (labor, shelter, market, and sexual etc.), gender-based violence, non-social cohesion and discrimination as well as generating a lot of insanitary environment and poor personal hygiene and pressure on the environment posing varied risk (land degradation, shelter in risky zones, etc. All these result to different types of trauma experiences. Employment and decent work among IDPs and host communities can help contribute to shock recovery and social cohesion hence WAG is proposing an all-inclusive context specific driven GBV prevention and livelihood activities that are people and IDP centered (Human security-based approach) that protect, build resilience, and transition them to autonomy. Stakeholders in Disaster Risk Reduction in line with their gender strategic plan will be train and engaged to increase women’s security and access to quality services as most conflicts are a result of excluding vulnerable peoples from accessing services and resources; women and girls safe spaces will be created or strengthened to function as safe entry point for accessing life-saving services including Psychosocial support, and a platform to engage with each other, exchange information and leverage on community networks and support. All these will help reduce women/girls’ risk, further harm, and displacement. Women and girls’ participation in the safe spaces will help them to speak out, lead, participate in their security, empowered to meet their specific rights/needs, and recover from displacement shock. This proposal will be achieved through the following outcomes.Outcome 1: Vulnerable women and girls IDPs and host population are locally supported and live protected in a safe environment.Outcome 2: Vulnerable women IDPs and host population live in good health and their dignity restored in a sanitized environment.
