Project Summary

En dépit des problèmes de sécurité, l'UNICEF continuera à soutenir la stratégie nationale pour la santé dans les situations d'urgence et de santé publique ( plan national multisectoriel de lutte contre les épidémies et catastrophe. Ce plan national est basé sur un ensemble de mesures sanitaires intégrées ayant un impact important sur la réduction de la mortalité maternelle, néonatale et infantile, y compris des campagnes de vaccination, des réponses aux épidémies et des interventions communautaires sur la prévention des épidémies sporadiques de paludisme, le traitement des cas courants et la prévention et l'atténuation de la violence sexiste en situation d'urgence, la lutte contre le VIH, ainsi que le renforcement de la résilience par la fourniture d'équipements, de médicaments et de produits de base aux établissements de santé et des interventions de prévention et de contrôle des infections dans les communautés et les établissements de santé. L'UNICEF soutiendra également le gouvernement et poursuivra sa collaboration avec les ONG sur le terrain pour mettre en œuvre le programme de santé intégré dans les zones de conflit, y compris les interventions à fort impact telles que la vaccination, la santé infantile, la santé maternelle et infantile, la réponse rapide au paludisme, les maladies vulnérables aux vaccins pour atteindre zéro dose d'enfants et les enfants manqués et les femmes enceintes, les nouveau-nés et les enfants sous-couverts.
Programme Targets
1, 831,040 individuals (1,356,326 children under 5 and 474,714 pregnant women) will benefit from the integrated health package and community-based interventions in the 6 regions of Mopti, TBCT, Kidal, Menaka, Gao, Taoudeni
2,183,930 children under 5 will receive measles vaccines and 14149957 under 5 will be vaccinated during the polio outbreak response in Mopti, Gao, Tombouctou, Kidal, Menaka, and Taoudenit regions

1 - Contexte

Mali is witnessing an unprecedented escalation of conflict and violence. Attacks against civilians have been increasing in northern and central Mali. The departure of the United Nations peacekeeping mission in Mali started on 1 July 2023 and is continuing up to 31December 2023.10 Since August 2023, non-state armed groups have created siege-like conditions in several towns in the regions of Timbuktu, Gao, and Ménaka by blocking main supply routes. The deterioration and spread of the conflict, including the entry of new armed actors into hostilities, is expected to lead to serious humanitarian consequences if it continues.11 Also of concern is the diminishing presence of non-governmental organizations in some areas due to insecurity and operational restrictions. The combined impact of climate-related risks, persistent political and socioeconomic crisis (heightened by inflation in the prices of certain food products), insecurity, and internal displacement is affecting the most vulnerable people. An estimated 2.3 million people in Mali were food insecure in 2023 and an estimated 275,700 children suffered from severe wasting.12 More than 2,500 people face catastrophic levels of food insecurity (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 5) in Ménaka, while 76,000 people there are experiencing emergency levels of hunger (IPC phase 4).13 Nearly 5.1 million people14 required emergency health care in 2023, among them 3.2 million children. Routine vaccination coverage (DPT3) among children in Mali stands at 77 percent. The country has experienced periodic epidemics of polio and dengue fever, and cases of measles have been reported in 32 of the country's 75 health districts in 2023. Children's access to education in Mali is precarious, with 1,545 schools reported closed16 as of June 2023 due to insecurity and attacks on educational infrastructure and staff. This deprived more than 460,000 children of access to learning and affected more than 9,200 teachers. Children continue to be exposed to protection risks including family separation, gender-based violence, and recruitment into armed forces/armed groups, leading to dire consequences on all dimensions of their lives including on their mental health and development. From January to March 2023, 260 grave violations against children were verified,17 mostly in Timbuktu, Gao, Ménaka and Kidal Regions.18 Women and children are also at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. The Children’s Climate Risk Index rating for Mali placed the country in the high-risk category, and the impact of climate change (drought, floods) makes crises more common and harder to recover from.19 An estimated 3.2 million people,20 including 1.7 million children, lack access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities.
