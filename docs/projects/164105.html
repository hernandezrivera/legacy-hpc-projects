Project Summary

The hostilities in northwest Syria continue to heavily impact the lives of civilians, who have endured nine years of crisis, violence, multiple displacements, and economic downturns. The humanitarian situation, particularly in the Idleb de-escalation area, continues to deteriorate as a result of the hostilities that escalated in late 2019. From December 1st, 2019, almost 1 million people were displaced in northwest Syria, mostly from the southern parts of Idleb governorate; 80 percent of whom are women and children. People were forced to flee their homes within a matter of hours and without most of their belongings. The majority of the newly displaced have stayed within Idleb moving to either urban centers, such as Idleb city of Ariha, or to areas where vast IDP populations have already settled, such as Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, and Atareb. Approximately 45,000 of newly displaced individuals chose to move to areas in northern Aleppo governorate to seek safety and to reach services.
Through this project, IR Syria plans to reach 2,290 families (11,450 individuals) who are newly IDP in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The targeted families will be assisted with:
• 2kg of baked bread daily for 60 days, and
• Monthly food parcels for four months.
The following table illustrates the targeted areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorates that have been identified in close coordination with the local councils, camp management, and other humanitarian organizations working in the area. These areas host a large number of new IDP families and there are gaps in food assistance. Therefore, the project will support extremely vulnerable families to meet their basic food needs.
Through this project, IR Syria will respond to the urgent food needs of the newly displaced people in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate. This project aims to reach 11,250 individuals with food support. The overall project objective of this financially secured project is to reduce suffering, increase and maintain human dignity, and save the lives of vulnerable IDP in Harim and Jebel Saman Districts of Syria.

4 - Needs

Despite the huge efforts from the Food Security Cluster members, the increasing number of newly displaced people continues to have dire implications on food assistance. The gap in food assistance by Humanitarian partners has been recently estimated as 94,000 individuals. The Food Security Cluster calls for additional support to meet the food needs of the newly displaced 94,000 people by delivering frontline response (cash, cooked meals, baked bread, and ready to eat rations) for the next weeks till the situation on the ground calms down. Later, these families need to be integrated into regular monthly food assistance programs. Analysis of the overall food security status of beneficiary households in NWS showed that 18.3% were categorized as ‘food secure’; 53.0% as ‘marginally food secure’; 25.8% as ‘moderately food insecure’; and 2.8% as ‘severely food insecure’. Thus, the majority of beneficiary households are in the borderline marginal or moderate groups, which indicates a key result as these households are at high risk to drop to greater food insecurity without assistance. The vast majority of households (89.6%) still rely on some form of negative livelihood coping in order to meet their food and essential needs, while 36% of these households engage in strategies that are detrimental to their resilience and current or future well-being. Female-headed households are more food insecure than males despite receiving similar types of assistance at similar frequencies. They have worse food consumption and dietary diversity, and they are more economically vulnerable. Furthermore, female-headed households rely more on negative coping strategies than males in order to meet their food and other essential needs. For example, compared to their male counterparts, women sell more humanitarian assistance and withdraw more children from school. Households with members who are chronically ill or have a disability are also less food secure than other households.

5 - Implementation of activities

In close coordination with the local council, camp management, local community and other humanitarian organizations active in the field, the geographic areas where a large number of new IDP families arrived will be mapped. Also looking at the FSL cluster gap analysis, the villages/camps where there is no other humanitarian organization working will be identified. The main selection criteria would be new IDP families who have not received any support from other humanitarian organizations. Baked Bread Support: the newly displaced families who are residing in the camps, unfinished buildings, informal settlements and have not received baked bread (staple food commodity) will be provided with daily baked bread. A total of 2,290 families (11,450) individuals will receive this support. IR will identify the bakeries in close proximity of the targeted families. Through the IR standard procurement process, IR will identify 2-3 bakeries that are able to produce the appropriate quantity and quality of the baked bread daily. The IR team will distribute the bread to the families. Each targeted family will receive 2kg of baked bread (20 loaves of bread) daily for 60 days. A total of 137,400 bread bags will be distributed. This activity will be implemented in the initial two months of the project. Food Parcel Support: As recommended by the FSL cluster, after the initial weeks of displacement the families need monthly food parcel support to sustain minimum food security requirements without adopting harmful negative coping strategies. Each of the targeted families will receive one food parcel monthly for a period of four months. A total of 9,160 food parcels will be distributed. Monitoring activities will be carried out over the course of the project phases. The MEAL team in the field will monitor the actual actives against the planned activities and will report monitoring outcomes to relevant departments and individuals.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Diversion of aid due to armed groups in the targeted location: IR ensures to follow a clear beneficiary selection criteria. All stakeholders are fully aware of IR impartial and independent way of working and hence IR use the local communities, local councils and local leaders to ensure IR work as per the beneficiary selection process to avoid aid diversion. Deterioration of security situation in target area (increase in the bombardment and airstrike): IR team may have to suspend the operations or evacuate the staff. The activities may be suspended temporarily or completely as per the situation. IR Security team will continue to evaluate the security situation and will advise the programme team to implement the project activities accordingly to ensure the safety and security of the staff and the project deliverable. In case of temporary or permanent inaccessibility to a certain area, IR may follow up the project activities though local screened partners.Security risks for IR staff during project implementation & monitoring: IR staff will abide by internal safety and security procedures. All the light vehicles will be equipped with first aid kits and fire extinguishers. Furthermore, the staff will be trained on first aid and medical evacuation. Mistreatment of beneficiaries by the project staff or the stakeholder staff: All the project staff abide by a code of conduct and will be provided with an induction workshop (refresher) that includes protection of the beneficiaries and showing respect to all the stakeholders. New staff will be trained on the ‘do no harm’ principle and all partners will be sensitized on the principle. The MEAL and program team will also visit the target areas and collect feedback from the beneficiaries. In case of any complaints, appropriate actions will be taken as per IR policy.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Fraud/corruption: IRW is committed to ensuring transparency and accountability through many mechanisms like the CRM and the whistle blowing mechanism.Challenges in money transfer from Turkey to Syria: IR will ensure to have cash flow plan according to which money will be transferred from Turkey to Syria. In case of any difficulties, alternative solutions will be identified. Such as use of the Turkish banks/money transfer agents available in Euphrate shield area (Northern Syria).Exchange rates and material/service costs remain reasonably stable throughout implementation period of the project: IR will ensure that all the financial transactions in Syria are conducted in hard currency to avoid any major exchange fluctuations. Following a competitive procurement process, IR will ensure to procure the best quality items at reasonable prices. In case of an unexpected major change in the price, IR will inform the donor and make any changes required.Attacks on IR warehouses and offices: IR warehouses and offices are currently in a safe area with very low records of security incidents. Supplies will be divided and stored into multiple warehouses if needed. IR will try to keep the items in the warehouses only for a short period before transporting to the distribution points. All warehouses and offices will be equipped with fire extinguishers. Evacuation protocols will also be in place for any emergency event. The closure of Bab al Hawa border crossing between Turkey and Syria: IR will use a second border crossing i.e. Bab al Salamah. This will slightly increase the transport cost of the deliverable.IR will then also asses the possibility of buying materials from inside Syria.No access to certain target area: IR can cancel the inaccessible areas (as usually when there is an intensification of hostilities, people immediately move from the area to other nearby areas) or reallocate the activities to other areas, where IDP needs remain high.
