Project Summary

Improving equitable, safe and sustainable access to essential WASH services and enhancing preparedness capacity to mitigate and prevent floods are considered immediate needs for the most vulnerable communities and institutions of the West Bank. The project interventions were built upon the WASH Cluster eligible actions for HRP 2021, targeting the most vulnerable communities and institutions according to WASH 2020 vulnerability mapping. The interventions are also fully in line with HRP 2021 Strategic objective 2, complementing WASH cluster strategic objective 2 “Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians” and in accord with Strategic objective 3 and WASH strategic objective 3 “Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, to reduce threats and cope with emergencies”. The action has been endorsed by PWA and the Ministry of Education. It is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) and in compliance with the Water Master Plan for Area C. The project complements another WASH initiative submitted in partnership with UNICEF, AAH and MA’AN for the purposes of the HRP 2021. The action is also in line with the SDG 6 “Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”. WeWorld-GVC in partnership with MA’AN aims to targets the core vulnerabilities of people in need by:
1) increasing access, availability and quality of water at HH and community level, connecting 5 communities in Area C (1,666 beneficiaries) trough installation/rehabilitation of water networks, transmission pipelines and HHs connection; increasing water storage capacity for 3 communities in Area C (469 beneficiaries) trough cisterns construction and provision of PE water tanks; improving water quality trough provision of 12 Chlorination systems for 12 communities in WB (5,442 beneficiaries)
2) guarantying a safe and adequate level of sanitation and hygiene services at household level, through the installation of mobile and concrete latrines in 4 communities in Area C (398 beneficiaries)
3) enhancing access to WASH services in institutions, through the construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 15 key Health Centres in WB (17,822 beneficiaries) and 21 schools in West Bank and East Jerusalem (8,473 beneficiaries)
4) promoting floods mitigation and prevention, by the construction/rehabilitation of stormwater networks and drainage systems in 18 communities in WB (9,923 beneficiaries)
WeWorld-GVC and MA’AN ensure that all the proposed interventions will trigger a positive effect on the reduction of the WASH vulnerability, increasing the resilience of the community under threat of forcible transfer in Area C and increase meaningful access to WASH services. Moreover, the action would enhance the protection of the communities and institutions, supporting preparedness and response on combating COVID-19 outbreak direct and indirect effects. The action will be implemented through different response modalities, encompassing primarily cash transfers, whenever feasible, through WeWorld-GVC Community Implementation methodology and through hiring local laborers supervised by MA’AN technical team; otherwise by services delivery through contracting. The entire project cycle - including need assessments, designed strategies, proposed interventions, set indicators, outcome measuring procedures – takes into account the need of the most affected groups such as women, children, PWDs and elderly, in order to achieve gender and age-sensitive relief through responsive assistance that meets the material and protection needs of specific vulnerable groups, ensuring adequate and equitable participation and access to humanitarian goods and services, and eventually preventing/mitigating potential negative effects of the action on different gender/age groups.

1 - Needs

Access to essential WASH services in the West Bank continues to be a significant challenge. This is true for 49,286 HHs that are either unconnected to a water network. Especially in Area C, water consumption rates can reach less than 35 l/c/d – less than half of the 100 l/c/d WHO’ recommendation; communities are mainly dependent on unsecured water sources and/or tankered water, and lack of sanitation and hygiene facilities triggers sanitary risks and protection concerns. Furthermore, according to WASH cluster vulnerabilities mapping 2020, in the West Bank 141 schools and 122 Health care units count with inadequate or insufficient WASH facilities. Moreover, 39,459 people in the West Bank are suffering frequent flooding during winter with severe impacts on their livelihood, preventing communities' access and thus leading to isolation. The COVID 19 pandemic outbreak worsened an already compromised reality, and it is in fact the most vulnerable communities and institutions with inadequate WASH services that cannot adhere to the WHO recommendations of frequent hand-washing and environmental disinfection and end-up being at a greater risk of contagion. This project is based on thorough evidence-based assessments identifying communities and institutions lacking WASH services and floods prevention and mitigation measures, and therefore proper coping mechanisms to withstand the protracted threats and the recent COVID 19 pandemic, with a high risk of displacement. Those assessments are : - Community Based Protection Approach (CPA), initiated in 2015, updated on yearly bases, in the framework of the ECHO WB Protection Consortium, where WW-GVC collected quantitative and qualitative data, including a specific WASH sector-analysis for 71 communities located in Area C, H2 and EJ for the development of Protection Community profiles and Protection response plans; - An in-depth field assessment conducted in September 2020 by WW-GVC and MA'AN on the most vulnerable communities of WB, based on WASH cluster vulnerability index 2020, identifying at HHs and community level, needs of WASH facilities, gaps of water service providers in terms of provision of high standard quality of water, and highlighting the communities in need of urgent interventions regarding flood mitigation and prevention; - A WASH in School needs assessment completed by MA’AN (September 2020), with the support of MoE WASH cluster, PWA, in Tubas Salfit, Jenin, Jerusalem, Qalqilya and Ramallah, identifying major lacks of WASH facilities and protection concerns that threaten the safe and dignified access of communities to WASH services. - Needs assessment for QCs and HCs in the WB, elaborated by WW-GVC in coordination with MoH and all governorates in July 2020 (updated October 2020), identifying WASH vulnerability and gaps on Health Care facilities; - Water master plan for Area C targeting 247 communities, developed by WW-GVC and AAH in 2017 in coordination with PWA. The identified communities are dependent on the direct purchase of water for most of the year, failing to match Humanitarian Benchmarks of 60 l/c/d, and even the critical minimum of 30 l/c/d. Water expenses can account for up to 50% of HHs monthly expenses with a negative impact especially on women who prioritize the use of water for domestic uses rather than personal hygiene, and men who prioritize water for their livestock, generating negative protection impacts increasing the risk of displacement and contagion of COVID and having a direct correlation with the increase of GBV cases. Increased access to WASH facilities, such as water network and latrines, stands as the humanitarian response to improve communities living conditions. Lack of access to adequate WASH facilities affects the most vulnerable populations within the targeted communities, particularly women, girls, and persons with disabilities. It affects the hygiene and dignity of women and girls during their menstruation cycles, critically jeopardizing their sense of privacy and personal safety and affects the safety of persons with disabilities who lack the safe, appropriate, and dignified WASH facilities to which they are entitled. Most of the targeted communities do not have functioning latrines for all the inhabitants, which leads to open defecation, which poses health hazards due to the propagation of water-borne disease. Moreover, the provision of WASH services in Schools is essential to ensure an adequate and safe learning environment; all the 21 identified Palestinian public girls’ schools are in need of urgent rehabilitation of their WASH facilities. Health Centres in the WB are at the forefront of COVID-19 response, and 15 institutions have been identified in extremely immediate urge of WASH facilities rehabilitation and construction. Finally, the increasingly frequent extreme weather events in winter, together with the poor maintenance of stormwater networks and drainage systems, pose the identified 18 communities under high risk of displacement and destruction of their properties, with even worse consequences if stormwater mixes with garbage and/or overflow wastewater. With all of the above-mentioned protection concerns considered, WW-GVC and MA'AN developed an integrated WASH response dealing with the source of WASH vulnerability at community and institutions level, such as water availability and support to service providers, adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities at HHs, schools and HCs level, and flood mitigation and prevention.

2 - Activities

WeWorld-GVC and MA’AN will organize meetings with stakeholders to coordinate the activities. Governorates, Municipalities and Village Councils (VCs) would be involved as local partners for the collection of data related to the baseline and the endline. In order to avoid overlapping, proper coordination will be performed with PWA, WBWD, local authorities, and major stakeholders active in the WASH sector on the target communities. The project will primarily encompass, when feasible, the adoption of WeWorld-GVC Community Implementation methodology (CI), entailing the creation of a Community Project Committee (CPC) composed of representatives of the communities/CBOs (gender-balanced), which are the local implementing partners. CPCs are responsible for the design, implementation, and maintenance of the infrastructures and will receive direct costs through conditional cash transfer modality. Direct costs (which includes material and cost of labor) is therefore paid to them upon receipt of a payment request with a progress report (prepared by the sector responsible and verified by Technical Coordinator and Project Manager) that certify the work accomplishment along with the payments amount eligibility. Capacity building and monitoring will be done by WW-GVC along with the intervention. Prior to any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community and the Project Committees will be conducted. Specific SOPs are followed during implementation to minimize/mitigate risks and therefore Community Implementation will not only endorse accountability and community empowerment but also reduce the risk of confiscation and other potential obstacles related to the coercive environment of Area C, thus enhancing the commitment and ownership of the beneficiaries. WASH in school response would be delivered through services contracting supervised by MA’AN engineers and in coordination with the department of buildings and health in the directorates of education. While the provision of inputs and installation would be via subcontracts, when relevant, hiring of local laborers will be promoted and their work will be supervised by MA’AN engineers. Activity 1 - Community Implementation: the creation of a Community Project Committee and, prior to any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community and the Project committee. Activity 2 - Need assessments: at the beginning of the action, together with the CPC and stakeholders, a needs assessment will be carried out to verify any change of needs and/or objectives, as well as to assess the security situation to enable the interventions to be conducted according to plan. Activity 3 - Improvement of access to water at HHs and community level. 3.1 Provision of 12 Chlorination systems in 12 communities of West Bank: 3.2 Installation/extension/rehabilitation of water networks for 4 communities in Area C (Jenin governorate: Khirbet al Muntar ash Sharqiya, Al Hafira, Nazlat ash Sheikh Zeid; Ramallah governorate: Kobar bedouins); 3.3 Installation of 1 transmission pipeline in Isfey al Fauqa in Hebron governorate in Area C;3.4 Construction of 5 cisterns in A Seefer in Hebron governorate in Area C;3.5 Provision of 71 PE water tanks 1.5 m3 in Al Baqa’a (50) and Khirbet Zanuta (21) in Hebron governorate in Area C; Activity 4 – Improvement of access to sanitation and hygiene, especially for the most vulnerable groups (women, children, and PWD).4.1 Provision of 59 HHs latrines (mobile and concrete) in 4 communities in Area C of WB: (Nablus governorate: Khirbet Tana (4), Ramallah governorate: Al Mu’arrajat Centre (25), Ras al Tin (27); Tubas governorate: Al Hadidiya (3)); Activity 5 – Improvement of access to WASH services in institutions.5.1 Construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 15 HCF, including kitchen and latrines construction and maintenance;5.2 Construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 21 schools in the WB and East Jerusalem, including the construction of 6 toilet and sanitation units in 15 schools and construction of drinking water fountains in 6 schools; Activity 6 - Flood mitigation and prevention 6.1 The construction/ rehabilitation of 18 storm water networks and rainwater drainage systems in 18 communities in the West Bank, including small storm drainage tunnels and rainwater drainage networks.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Build upon WW-GVC Community Protection Approach (CPA), the proposed project ensured community participation -promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community such as people with disabilities, women and children - since the identification of the needs and the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in increased empowerment at the individual and community level. This approach is built from a multisectoral perspective, which supports the communities in assessing their vulnerability and in mapping appropriate protection responses, aiming to increase the capacity of the community to make informed decisions about their safety and dignity. Particular attention was given to input/feedback provided by women, whose participation is strongly encouraged in any decision-making process; further, the action takes also into account the indications provided by WW-GVC “Gender and WASH toolkit for Palestine”, which provides applicable and feasible suggestions on how to mainstream gender in WASH projects in the Palestinian context. WW-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint response mechanism system (WW-GVC CRM) allowing beneficiaries and the affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will in turn influence management decisions and reinforce accountability. Whenever possible, the implementation will be led by community members through a participatory approach, nevertheless, the project will also instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measures to meet “Do No Harm” requirements, especially in light of COVID19 ongoing outbreak, by using all the preventive measures and equipment to minimize the risk of contagion. In the case of using a cash transfer modality, protection precautions are put into place to ensure that the activity will be aligned with WW-GVC’s Child-Protection policy which prohibits child labor and that the activity does not trigger harm or hostility within the community. Finally, all staff involved in the projects is aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in WW-GVC Code of Conduct and related policies including Child-Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; AntiTerrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project, in line with the sustainable humanitarian approach, aims to provide immediate relief, ensuring equity, sustainability, and impartial access by conflict affected communities and institutions of WB and East Jerusalem to decent WASH services. The project aims to increase access to water, sanitation, and hygiene, as well as the efficiency, predictability, and equity of the WASH service and has been designed considering the direct and indirect impact on women, girls, boys, men with or without disabilities. The planned interventions will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children and PWDs to safe water and sanitation through the access to safe and equitable water, increasing the HH storage capacity, access to adequate and adapted sanitation facilities, rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools and Health Care Centers and action towards flooding mitigation and prevention. While most public schools in the West Bank need extensive infrastructure work, due to girls’ personal hygiene needs, girls’ schools are the most in need of such services and will be primarily targeted by the intervention. The strategy, activities, and materials used for implementing the project were developed and selected to tackle the assessed needs, together with and in the best interests of the beneficiaries, building upon lessons learned from similar projects implemented in the past. As a consequence, the project is expected to positively impact all community members and institution users, but in particular, women, as there will be a noticeable decrease in time and effort spent in fetching water at the household-level, especially for girls and women who are usually in charge of this task; therefore, fewer tasks will be imposed on girls and women who will not have to face long walks to collect the water, reducing exposure to the protection risks related to their trips to the water source (i.e. attacks and harassment from Israeli Forces and settlers). In the case of families with PWDs members, access to water will help to alleviate the work of the caregivers and help to overcome difficulties. Finally, a specific design of WASH facilities (i.e latrines) will guarantee their ease of access during use and maintenance to all members of the community and institutions, including girls, women, boys, and men, with or without disabilities of all ages.
