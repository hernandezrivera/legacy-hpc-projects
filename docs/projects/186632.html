1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in North Eastern Nigeria continues to have a profound impact on the population, leading to displacement both within and out of the region and high rates of unmet needs among internally displaced persons. The sudden influx of arrivals add strain to the existing infrastructure of the humanitarian response, resulting in high rates of unmet needs, overcrowding in camps, and exposure to protection risks for vulnerable groups. Borno state remains the epicentre of the crisis, containing over 80% of the region’s over 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), with a majority living in urban host communities or informal settlements further complicating service delivery. Indeed, regarding the CCCM sector, the camp management tracker reports indicate that over 60,000 households live in makeshift shelters, up to 1,000 are living in the open with no shelter, while over 2,000 are sharing shelter. Furthermore, the REACH MSNA shows that over 60% of Northeast Nigeria’s estimated 2.1 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) reside out of camp, in host communities. Due in part to their dispersion over large geographic areas, displaced populations residing in host communities can be more difficult to identify, access, and target than those in camps. As a subset of host communities, informal sites and settlements (ISETs) are particularly vulnerable. Residents of ISETs live in hyper-dense areas characterized by extreme poverty and marginalization, with limited access to basic services, and are often not receiving humanitarian assistance. In addition, many vulnerable displaced persons face difficulties in accessing humanitarian assistance due to security and access constraints, which is seriously curtailing shelter and NFI partners from providing assistance required. Furthermore, there are 235 informal camps in the BAY states, and 42% of these sites remain without dedicated or mobile site facilitation. In Borno, 83% are without facilitation, 20% in Yobe, and 4% in Adamawa. Among the formal camps, only 44% have facilitation in Borno and 2% in Adamawa. In terms of shelter’s needs, the report reveals that 44% of the IDPs are living in emergency shelter, 35% in makeshift, 11% in collective communal shelter, 9% in public facilities and 1% in transitional shelter. Furthermore, 10% of shelters are damaged, 89% of HH are in a form of shelter only, and 1% of HH is sharing shelters. The Prolonged closure of schools due to COVID-19 has shown to negatively impact the well-being of children, and to weaken their protective environment. Severe needs remain, as out of the 7000 children present in Stadium Camp, around 3000 are out of school. Although the huge majority recognized education as being a strong concern, the main challenges identified for children attending school vary significantly. Indeed, male adolescents and SBMC staff reported a lack of support from parents and caregivers, while women mentioned COVID19 as a significant hindrance, along with poor infrastructure (e.g. tables and chairs). As for adolescent girls, it has been reported that many children attending school resort to hawking.

2 - Activities

REACH support to an evidence-based humanitarian response will enable actors to plan and implement an appropriate crisis-wide response to the priority needs of displaced populations in target countries. Moreover, ACTED's approach to mobile and static CCCM support will ensure that forcibly displaced populations in and out of camp contexts have improved access to basic services and dignified living conditions through the provision of basic life-saving activities, including emergency response, and benefit from the set up/strengthening of coordination platform to optimize humanitarian assistance. Building on its experience with the COVID-19 Child Protection (CP)/ Education in Emergency (EiE) strategy, Tdh will continue to implement a flexible approach retaining the good practices REACH information management activities:- Provide assessment, information management and analysis support to relevant humanitarian coordination structures- Joint Market Monitoring- Emergency Prioritisation through Rapid Assessments - Population Movement Assessments and Research ACTED Activities:- Establishment/ strengthening of coordination fora and platforms (at the camp, informal site, area/settlement, subnational or national level) to improve the quality of life, safety and dignity of displaced people through coordination activities- Capacity building of local/national actors on CCCM/ planning of services to displaced populations for a more efficient and locally led displacement response.- Improve safety and protection for the affected population through site improvements/maintenance activities and support to site planning - Monitoring and addressing risks and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys and girls through regular Safety Audits.- Support participation of affected population to the humanitarian response through the assessment/set up and strengthening of inclusive and representative camp/site level governance structures- Establish and/or maintain two ways communication channels with the affected population- Provision of WASH, S/NFIs, FSL and HLP services to affected population to address humanitarian gaps in service provision. ACTED to construct 400 Bama shelters and provide 400 NFI kits to affected populations. A substantive CCCM response can address the above issues through improved site planning and coordination, to minimize the risks associated with congestion. The daily presence of CCCM site monitors in camps enables the collection of regular, accurate data on camp conditions, thus informing more targeted and evidence-based response activities by humanitarian actors, and improved advocacy on critical gaps in services and priority needs for site residents. With the severe restriction of land availability, an area-based coordination and planning at the site level is needed to ensure optimal usage of the available site. Such an approach maximises the intelligent planning and coordination of efforts among multiple aid actors in congested and limited spaces and is critical to ensuring adequate coverage of needs. The daily facilitation and management of IDP sites by CCCM teams can therefore improve alert systems to critical risks faced by site residents, including protection, fire and sanitation risks, and efficiently coordinate to address the most critical risks in a dignified and timely manner. This contributes to the sector objectives of enhanced displacement management in camps and out of camp settings to ensure a protective environment through an effective flow of information to facilitate coordination and quality of integrated service provision. Moreover, the support to community governance structures contributes to enhancing resilience and improved ability of communities and local partners to cope with displacement and ensure local ownership and self-governance through inclusive participation, gender mainstreaming and engagement of displaced persons.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. ACTED will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the wellbeing, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to CCCM assistance, and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. All ACTED staff will uphold protection principles at all times, as signatories to ACTED’s Code of Conduct, including ACTED’s Policy on PSEA. Moreover, as a leading CCCM agency in all proposed target sites, ACTED will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer protection cases identified through the CRM. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: 1) Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: ACTED will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. ACTED’s CCCM team will ensure that community meetings and sensitization campaigns are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. Gender-segregated queues with mandatory COVID-19 social distancing measures and available disinfectant materials will be mandated to all camp-level communal and distribution sites. Under the CCCM component, ACTED will integrate gender and protection mainstreaming, ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups in decision-making processes. Strong referral mechanisms will be enacted, enabling direct referral of any vulnerable IDPs to relevant service providers, including victims of assault or GBV. 2) Ensure meaningful and equal access: the CCCM Team will strengthen participatory camp governance structures, facilitating direct inclusion of beneficiary groups into decision-making processes, facilitating greater equitable and meaningful access to assistance programs. ACTED will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy. 3) Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: Beneficiaries will be members of camp committees, ensuring strong participation in camp governance, channeling ideas and influencing the wider humanitarian coordination and response planning, and play a vital role in the implementation of site maintenance activities, providing foundation for community-led camp management and interventions. Daily laborers will be recruited from the IDP community to provide employment opportunities, with hiring process reliant on a strict equal opportunities basis and considerate of protection and gender aspects. Accountability will also stem strongly from the CRM, ensuring timely and appropriate response to complaints. Moreover, PDM exercises will assess beneficiary satisfaction and inform further programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED’s established Complaints and Response Feedback Mechanism (CRM), mainstreamed throughout all ACTED-led interventions in Northeast Nigeria, facilitates safe, accessible and transparent feedback channels for affected populations. ACTED’s CRM enables beneficiaries to voice concerns, complaints and suggestions, while informing ACTED’s partner coordination efforts at camp-level and thereby increasing overall accountability of service providers and the wider humanitarian response. CRM mediums include complaints boxes, complaints desks, a telephone hotline and email. ACTED’s independent AME Unit will maintain responsibility for collecting and responding to all reports, addressing complaints through protection-centered case-management support in a timely and transparent manner. ACTED’s CRM provides an effective two-way communication channel, ensuring direct links to target populations, and inclusion of beneficiary and broader community voices in program implementation. Further, information gained through the CRM is looped back into the design of program approaches, as ACTED continuously adjusts intervention activities according to feedback received. Moreover, in ACTED-managed sites, Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) are operational. As opposed to the CRM, the CFM is for site-wide feedback related to all partner activities, rather than ACTED implementation only. This allows ACTED to gather feedback on all activities in the sites and ensure beneficiaries have an avenue to express their feedback on all site-related issues. Finally, ACTED is committed to humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and ensures the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by monitoring impact of project activities to ensure activities do not inadvertently cause tensions among targeted populations. All ACTED staff will be strictly compliant with the code of conduct, and any breach of the code of conduct will be responded to with serious measure.
