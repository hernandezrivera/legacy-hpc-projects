Project Summary

The proposed project in Unity State and Juba is designed to provide protection services through strengthening the protection capacity and resilience of the most vulnerable individuals in targeted communities of Mayendit, Panyijiar and Juba (Rejaf) of South Sudan. The project will have a 60% protection mitigation and response component and a 40% access to justice and HLP rights component. The protection concerns of the communities across South Sudan and specifically in areas where there is little to no permanent protection actors remain high. Communal violence, including cyclical revenge killing, tribal conflict, and cattle-related violence, have continued across the country, fueled by negative coping mechanisms resulting from multi-generational trauma and militarized community response structures. Repeated shocks have weakened individual and household capacity and community-based structures plus consistent services. Government continue to exhibit and perpetuate a weak system that compels the civil society to fill that gap so as to address protection and HLP needs of people in need. In light of that, durable remains a pipe dream for displaced and conflict affected people in Mayendit, Panyijiar and Juba Rejaf. Through an integrated protection and lifesaving support mechanisms (collaborating with IRC WPE, CP, ERD, Health and Nutrition actions coupled with interventions by other actors plus linking with service provider alliances from the government), this action intends to provide protection mitigation and response activities including responsive protection, return/ post return and HLP monitoring, comprehensive protection case management, targetted individual assistance including emergency cash assistance to the most vulnerable groups of people, legal counseling and assistance (coining a fine nexus with HLP support activities and referrals from WPE and CP), two-way communication and information system (linking with and integrating risk communication services), incident profiling and reporting, and provide a platform for discourse on HLP and durable solutions. This activity will be complemented through the strengthening of community-based protection networks plus establishing a community of paralegals that will assist in the identification of vulnerable beneficiaries and the provision of support provided to individuals with protection, legal and HLP concerns in their communities. Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights is becoming one of the most critical issues for IDPs, and returnees in Panyijiar, Mayendit and Juba (including host of displaced communities). As such this action will promote community-based monitoring of HLP issues, establish HLP trends to inform response and advocacy, raise awareness on HLPs and build the capacity of duty bearers at county level and community level on their role and need to prioritize HLP concerns as human rights issues. IRC will deploy a roving legal protection team to support access to justice and HLP programming. In Panyijiar and Juba, IRC will scale up its protection and rule of law activities that empower communities to influence their recovery and protection - placing the centrality of protection across all the activities, and taking into account conflict sensitivity aspects. Protection activities will be conducted through static and mobile teams in Mayendit and Panyijiar. The action will also endeavor to create links between protection actors and peace committees in the target locations to implement peace building activities.

1 - Needs

The current situation in South Sudan (SS) is characterized as a protection crisis as a significant portion of the local population is unable to secure fair, equitable and consistent access to rights, leading to increased vulnerability. This action seeks to ensure that people are safe in their communities and that they receive support when they experience harm. Repeated shocks have weakened individual and household capacity and community-based networks (CBPs) plus quality service delivery. Since beginning of 2022, Mayendit experienced high rate of active fighting involving IG-affiliated youth and IO. Limitations to access to basic services (plus protection) continue to push families to adopt negative coping mechanisms that expose the vulnerable people, including women, girls, and people with disability in Mayendit, Panyijiar and Juba. The targetted areas continue to experience compounded vulnerabilities and are affected by ethnic and/or political marginalization, conflict and flood-related displacement, and localized insecurity and armed conflict. Women, girls, boys, and men in target locations continue to face access to quality protection services, including MHPSS, legal HLP and social, and community spaces. Further distress is caused by targeted and indiscriminate killings, where men account for 76% of the direct victims (UNMISS, 2021). According to SS HNO 2022, about 5.6 million (including 53%F and 15% PWD) people need protection services in SS. More than 100 thousand people are in severe need of HLP services in target locations. An additional, 254, 800 people face severe general protection needs in the three target areas. COVID-19 challenges continue to drive humanitarian needs and protection concerns in SS. Movement of people from both formal and informal IDPs settings to any destination in SS, is just a matter of shifting protection concerns and risks from one point to another. Therefore, durable solutions will remain hard to achieve without proper intersectoral needs analysis and implementation of integrated humanitarian assistance, protection, and development programs in the three targetted areas. The expected return of refugees and IDPs to their areas of origin and dynamics during transit drives HLP concerns, and protection risks especially for women. The voices of women and girls, PSNs, and PWD are not really heard as are result of weak government and community systems and a times due to a vacuum in protection services. Flooding has continued to increase vulnerability and exposed communities, particularly children and women, to multiple protection risks, in the target areas. REACH SS integrated needs tracking sheet has placed Mayendit and Panyijiar counties at the highest quintile in overall needs severity, and one in five children was already affected with wasting before the flooding submerged 80% of the land, displacing thousands of people, impacting people’s access to essential services, eroding coping mechanisms, and exacerbating vulnerability. Tough economic situations in SS impacts negatively on people’s livelihoods compounding to more protection concerns. Continued displacement has weakened the protective environment, undermined resilience, and challenged recovery due to the extensive destruction of property that accompanied these crises. A key challenge in addressing protection needs in this context is that up to 80 per cent of displaced households in SS are female headed, raising concerns about access to housing, land and property, income-generating opportunities, and physical security. Communities face access to justice challenges that has a bearing on their access to civil documentation, HLP resolutions that disproportionally affect women’s rights. Based on the high need for protection mitigation and response and HLP assessment and programming in the area, IRC will provide responsive protection activities (prevention and response) including protection monitoring and support to community networks.

2 - Project activities

1) Protection, HLP and Return Monitoring for Action: General Protection (GP) will conduct protection monitoring at target locations to identify displacement patterns, HLP concerns, protection incidents, and accessibility barriers/facilitators to active response mechanisms. This ongoing protection analysis will inform PSN profiling and humanitarian response. The action will engage and strengthen community protection networks to inform protection interventions and peaceful coexistence activities. 2) Comprehensive Case Management (CM): The action provides CM targeting individuals that face heightened protection risks and cannot benefit from specialized child or GBV CM services. Action targets persons with disability, elderly displaced by flooding, and at-risk people facing MHPSS challenges. CM will include linkages to services, PSS, and development of individual action plans to mitigate the protection risks. IRC Technical team will provide technical backstopping for quality programming in accordance with applicable standards. 3) PSS Activities- To complement CM, GP will support adults to manage stress through a WHO developed, evidence-based curriculum Self-Help Plus (SH+) for people both with and without mental disorders in hard-to-reach conflict areas, in target areas. SH+ is a group-based self-help intervention that combines a five-session pre-recorded audio course with an illustrated self-help book designed for low literacy populations. 4) Access Justice and HLP Interventions: Access to justice activities will include legal counseling, two-way legal information services and customized legal assistance through community paralegal networks at location level to focus on referrals from GBV and CP caseloads and adding value to addressing HLP related challenges and access to documentation for displaced affected people. IRC will provide mentorship to community paralegals. 5) Two-way Information Services: IRC will use two-way information services to provide vulnerable communities, including IPDs, returnees and other people living in crisis, with the knowledge and resources on rights, HLP and access to justice to protect themselves and prevent transmission of COVID-19. The action strengthens community interactive platforms and networks to deepen community engagement and build trust while countering misinformation with local, contextualized information that is sourced according to communities’ self-expressed needs. That includes localized integrated information awareness. The information service approach is coordinated closely with other stakeholders, with the potential to harmonize information across the humanitarian response in the targeted locations. IRC will develop actionable protection trends informed by protection monitoring and two-way communication services to inform response. 6) Targeted Individual Assistance (TIA): TIA, one-off response (cash-based assistance or in-kind) will be implemented for at-risk people to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity and reduce their vulnerability and exposure to risks and promote meaningful access and participation to services. 7) Strengthen Community-based protection network: The action engages and strengthens community structures, through community risk reduction and mitigation sessions, to identify risks, prioritize solutions, mobilize existing community resources, and inform protection interventions at community level. IRC will promote integrated CBP networks that will cover a wide spectrum of protection issues including CP, GP and GBV to enhance access to protection services for at risk groups including PWD. 8) Peaceful Coexistence activities: IRC will promote peaceful coexistence through linking community protection network with existing peace networks in the target areas. Protection Mainstreaming: GP will lead and promote protection mainstreaming practice across multi-sectoral areas through training and client action planning and working with community structures.

3 - Project indicators

Success will be measured using the IRC’s IMPACT standards which identifies a set of monitoring actions across the project life cycle. The project will dedicate funds to recruit monitoring and evaluation staff that will be charged with setting up project M&E systems and check points to track project implementation progress and to document learning. The project will be implemented in line with the overall strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Protection Cluster strategic objectives to ensure that activities align with the overall prioritized needs of affected populations. The indicators tracked will be in line with the Protection Cluster priorities and will be disaggregated as much as possible by age, sex and disability. This will further inform access and utilization of project assistance by vulnerable groups in the community. Quantitative and qualitative data will be collected during project implementation for protection monitoring and analysis, comprehensive case management, PSS activities, Access Justice and HLP interventions, Two-way Information Services, Targeted Individual Assistance, Community-based protection network and Peaceful Coexistence activities. Data will be collected using standardized tools used by the Protection Cluster and reported on a monthly basis. Regular review meetings will be held at the project locations where project staff and partners will review data on all indicators and use the data to make critical decisions about how to best deliver on the project outcomes and make necessary adjustments. Project cycle meetings will be held on a monthly basis to discuss performance across service delivery sites, share experiences and learnings and make high-level evidence-based decisions geared towards strengthening the project delivery mechanisms and success pathways.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The proposed cash for protection and legal support is based on context-specific market assessments conducted by IRC and through the Cash Working Group. IRC is an active member of the South Sudan Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) hosted by WFP through REACH and conductsmonthly market price and commodity surveys which inform the Inter-Agency Cash Working Group (IACWG) Country Multisector Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSMEB). Further, the IRC is active at the national and subnational Inter-Agency Cash Working Group, and both a strategic committee member and a technical committee member of the South Sudan Cash Collaborative Delivery (CCD) network, to which provides strategic and technical leadership on harmonization of cash delivery and safety nets programs in South Sudan. The cash transfer process will be guided by the IRCs standard cash operating procedures (SOPs) and the Safer Cash tools aligned with the South Sudan IACWG guidelines. Response shall embed COVID 19 mitigation measures. A prequalified cash distribution agent will be engaged to distribute cash to clients. The transfer mechanism that will be selected be based contextually upon the local financial regulations, government restrictions, programmatic requirements, and budget availability. Currently according to the IRC CROPS, the modalities used include cash in envelope, use of cash agents, vouchers etc. Selection of selection of modality, delivery mechanism, and service provider will be based on comprehensive market assessment, IRC CROPS, PIN and PCC that inform safer cash programming including safe and dignified referrals. More specifically, IRC will analyze access to goods and services with an AGD lens and compare this information with protection needs assessments. Also there will be an ongoing analysis of market systems related to protection, e.g. alternative care, health, legal services, transport, education, birth registration. Overall, analysis of the potential protection risks and benefits of market interactions in the community and among traders will be prioritized.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Based on current IRC experience and wider Cash WG assessments and market monitoring cash is meeting all the pre-requirements to be feasible. The action will provide multi-purpose targetted individual cash assistance to enable most vulnerable individuals (including at risk persons with legal and HLP challenges) to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity and reduce their vulnerability and exposure to risks and promote meaningful access and participation. Cash provides clients with additional flexibility and in line with a strength-based approach empowers clients to make decisions to meet their identified needs in a dignified way. IRC will refer cases for clients to ERD and other FSL members in the location like SP, to enable them to meet their basic needs and economic well-being needs. In addition, IRC will have another bucket of cash for protection to meet emergency needs for people facing protection challenges including those that need medical transport and specialist medical support, plus referrals from WPE and other actors. Protection staff will ensure that any risks clients might face if they receive cash will be mitigated. IRC will implement the cash program based on the IRC Cash Relief Operating Procedures, and Safer Cash approach. IRC will conduct cash feasibility assessments, cash distribution and post distribution monitoring to inform safer cash for the most vulnerable at risk persons. Cash for protection committees will be established and strengthened to enhance accountability by the IRC to the clients and the community leaders. Two-way communication systems will include help desks, focus group discussions, one on one assessments of cash beneficiaries and other reactive and proactive channels selected for the project. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We will collect, analyze and use feedback from beneficiaries regardless of their age, nationality, gender, residency status, education status, disability status and other diversity factors. We will use the Washington Group Short Set of Questions to disaggregate feedback and complaints by disability status. The IRC’s feedback channels will disaggregate feedback and complaints by gender, age, nationality, residency status and disability status to enable us to understand the needs and perspectives of different clients, including the most vulnerable members in the communities we serve. We will pay special attention to the most vulnerable and gather their feedback through key informant interviews, focus group discussions and community meetings. The community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements include persons with special needs to ensure their needs are into account.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be informed on the scope of the project, timelines, key decision gates and community accountability mechanisms and systems during project opening meetings, project review and coordination meetings, stakeholder and community forums, information boards and using IEC materials. IRC has established community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements who will inform community members on the available client feedback and complaints mechanisms that they can use to provide feedback about project assistance. The community-led feedback management committees will also follow up with IRC on actions taken on the feedback and complaints provided to ensure that the feedback and complaints received from clients are included in adaptive program management. As a client-centered organization, the IRC has a well-structured client responsiveness and accountability system in place where clients’ complaints and feedbacks are collected, analyzed, and used to inform program decision making. We will use both the proactive and reactive feedback channels to solicit client feedback that will guide project implementation and strategies for improvement. This feedback will be used to inform decision-making and adapt interventions to achieve better outcomes for beneficiaries in line with their priority needs and preferences.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Through various Project Management in Partnership approaches, clients will be involved at all phases of the project cycle management. An updated needs assessments will be conducted where basic data will be collected and used to determine the scope of sexual exploitation and abuse in the project locations. Project start up workshops or meetings will be conducted to clients in which they will be educated on the dangers, negative impacts, how to early identify and refer sexual exploitation and abuse cases, referral pathways of the victims among others. A continues monthly/quarterly review meetings with the beneficiaries and other stakeholders will be held in which clients will be briefed on the achievements in terms of control of sexual abuses, challenges faced and the proposed solutions and end line client’s satisfaction survey will be conducted which will measure to which extent have the clients been satisfied with the services of protection against sexual exploitation and abuse through the project cycle and the feedback from this survey will be used to improve the quality of protection against sexual exploitation and abuses incase of scale up to other location or better services delivery in the next project cycle.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Experience and research has shown that if beneficiaries do not know what has become of their feedback, then they will lose trust in the feedback process. At the IRC, we respond to beneficiaries’ feedback by asking them how we should respond, selecting the appropriate channels, identifying the timing for the response, planning and allocating resources, recording and keeping track of client feedback, and monitoring and reviewing our feedback mechanisms. Complaints and feedback will be analyzed and presented during weekly field site meetings and monthly project cycle meetings where practical actions to address them will put in place with clear timelines and responsibility allocated. These actions will then be followed up in subsequent meetings to ensure they are implemented and to measure the results. Key actions to adapt project assistance will include, but not limited to, making programmatic changes on assistance delivery, changing policy, provision of goods and services, providing information on available assistance, providing linkages with other projects, providing external referrals and including the needs of clients in project funding proposals.
