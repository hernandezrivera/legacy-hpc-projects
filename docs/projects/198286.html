Project Summary

The Boko Haram insurgency that started in Nigeria spread quickly in the Far-North Region of Cameroun early in 2014, with abduction and killing of civilians, destruction of villages, attacks on military positions, and massive violation of human rights. This led to an relentless movement of populations, both internally and externally, including the arrival of refugees from Nigeria. The protection situation in Far-North Region is a mixed situation, complex and marked by violence inflicted upon the most vulnerable civilians. Besides the armed conflict, the region is still recovering from the recent intercommunal conflict which caused mass displacement and tensions between different communities. Both conflicts have as consequence severe human rights violations include increased gender-based violence, attacks on property, illegal or arbitrary arrests, thefts, lootings of properties and limitation of the freedom of movement due to the loss of civil documentation. All these conflicts are added by flooding which affected around 238,000 people in the last months.
The continued insecurity has resulted in multiple population multiple displacements in Far-North of Cameroon. In its Round 23 Report on displacement conducted in the Far-North Region in June 2022, IOM indicated that more than 377, 800 persons are internally displaced in the Far-North Region due to armed conflict and intercommunal conflict. According to HNO analysis, more than 85% of these displaced persons need protection response. There are over 120,000 Nigerian refugees are living in the Region, both in Minawao camp and communities in the region.
To contribute to the protection response and fully play its role as the provider of last resort as well as the one of Protection Cluster co-lead lead, UNHCR identified 4 key priorities in 2023: 1) strengthening the centrality of protection, 2) coordination of lead sectors 3) emergency preparedness and response and 4) securing solutions.
Through a community-based approach, UNHCR intends to secure protection and provide solutions through a comprehensive response. This includes reinforced and expanded protection monitoring and strengthening communities’ ability to contribute to their protection, to respond to protection incidents as well as to participate in the search for solutions. The interventions will focus on community empowerment initiatives, responding to critical GBV and child protection incidents, legal assistance, and support for civil documentation as well as psychosocial services. In-kind support, Cash-Based interventions, and referrals to available services in the identified communities will be an integral part of the response.
The use of an area-based approach will consist of selecting specific areas in the 04 districts most affected by the displacement and working with all stakeholders in the priority locations including displaced, host community, returnees and refugees, local authorities, civil society, agencies working in the areas. The approach will ultimately contribute to build sustainability of key services and consequently prevent displacement as well as guarantee greater accountability to affected populations (AAP).
