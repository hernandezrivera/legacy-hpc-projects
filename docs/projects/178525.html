Project Summary

UOSSM is proposing to implement CP programming which aims to address the CP concerns through the following interventions: in order to enhance the capacity of CP staff to respond adequately to the identiﬁed CP concerns NW Syria, training will be done on CPMS, CPCM, FTR and PFA. Moreover, child protection networks (CPNs) will be established and will receive basic training on child rights, community mobilization, awareness raising and follow up support. Women, men, youth and adolescent boys and girls will be members of these CPNs. They will be equipped with the right skills through training, coaching and mentoring to operate as entry points for CP awareness raising and referral mechanism in the targeted communities and to enhance community mobilization on CP rights, which will improve reinforcing the social structures for child protection. The project provides focused psycho-social support services through child friendly spaces (CFS). Caregivers, the youth and community leaders are encouraged to become involved. Generally, girls and boys including adolescents in the CFS will participate in different kinds of activities to develop their personal and social skills such as cultural skills, life skills, and recreation activities. Similarly, parents and caregivers will be targeted with structured activities through parenting programs to help them understand, prevent and respond to protection risks for children. In conjunction with the CFS, mobile teams will also be established to conduct recreational PSS activities as well as focus on mobilizing communities to better protect their children. Moreover, they will conduct service mapping and coordinate with the humanitarian actors in the targeted areas to establish and strengthen of localized referral mechanisms to specialized child protection services and other services. Case workers will also link with the CFS and mobile teams to ensure provision of specialized child protection services through mobile outreach, counselling and case management services addressing children and caregivers’ vulnerabilities that are related to disability, physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, lack of appropriate care, family separation and immediate risks of child labor, child recruitment and child marriage aiming to support children to reach their full potential. UOSSM will support Child Protection Networks (CPNs) in identifying entry points to access Child protection cases. Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAFAAG) cases will be assisted to access formal and informal education, and livelihoods opportunities that are commensurate to local economy. Active family tracing will be required for cases that need reuniﬁcation and follow up visits conducted to ensure reintegration is going according to plan. Children with disabilities, especially children with intellectual disabilities, will be provided with special needs rehabilitation program to support their integration with their peers. USSOM will use the harmonized massages developed by the CP sub-cluster to raise awareness on some of the issues (family separation, physical harm, child labor) as well as sharing information with the affected communities on the available services in the targeted areas. UOSSM will conduct the following training for the staff and CP networks (PSS and Psychological First Aid (PFA), Communication skills, Group facilitation skills, GBV core concept, child protection minimum standards CPMS, Family Tracing and Reuniﬁcation, CP case management. Community-based child protection interventions are available for girls and boys in targeted locations -By establishing the 3 CP centers in Azaz, Maaret- Misreen and Harim (Dana), after hiring gender balanced staff who will be chosen from the IDPs and HC. through female staff will deliver the service to girls and male staff will provide the service to the boys. Facilities will be rehabilitated to assist access to PwDs and be provided with separated toilets.

4 - Needs

Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. According to HNO 2021, Children comprise almost half of the affected population. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused. Separation from caregivers is a persistent issue across Syria. Death of a caregiver15%boys & girls,economic need 25%boys,13 %girls, child marriage11% boys, 44% girls and child recruitment10%boys are among the top cited reasons for family separations. Absence of a continuum of alternative care options, including formal options, is a concern.Recruitment of children by parties to the conflict has become a normalized practice in some parts of Syria. In 2020, the UN verified 813 incidents of recruitment and use of children96% boys, with 99% used in combat roles,7% under age of15. Financial and material incentives, family and community influence, as well as the need for protection, survival,status and lack of meaningful alternatives remain the main drivers of recruitment. According to CPSM Annual Report–2020:85.64% of the KIs reported that children are engaged in types of work and that prevents them from going to school and 38.97% of the KIs reported child separation in their locations. the largest percentage of the needs reported during this year within the protection services was PSS services because of school closures and the children's increased need for these services, and many KIs mentioned the urgent need to support the separated children institutes and specialized services for children engaged in labor.

5 - Implementation of activities

Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to child protection concerns in Syria: By establishing the 3 CFS in Azaz, Maaret- Misreen and Dana, after hiring gender balanced staff. Each CFS will be equipped with CFS to provide safe space where children will receive focused PSS activities. Each CFS will receive 10Gs(4Gs under 10 ,3 adolescent girl’s Gs &3 adolescent boy’s Gs) each week for 8 weeks. the curricula will be used to be age- appropriate and aims to create a safe space and focus on fostering resilience, self-esteem and dignity. Pre-post evaluation to measure the impact of the intervention.-Parenting skills will be conducted through the CFS and outreach activities -CPNs will be established from parents, teachers &CLs. Youth will be engaged in CPNs to increase leadership skills and engagement of youth in the project activities. -Survey communities in areas covered by our services with coordination with CPMTF on monthly basis, Awareness raising campaigns in the community through home, camp and schools’ visits for all age groups will be conducted with coordination with CPNs. -Specialized CP services (CM). The case management team will be linked to each center with coordination with outreach teams and will respond to children with or at risk of CP concerns thorough assessment tool the needs, risks, and protective factors for each child will be identiﬁed.Monitoring plan:1. Include monitoring of community tensions related to services in M&E tools.2. include monitoring of access to services by sex, age, host/IDP status, and by people with reduced mobility3. Regular review of context and patterns of hostilities as part of considerations in programming.4. Include in M&E review of trainings conducted and frequency.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The threat of poor security situation in the areas can directly affect the activities; hence, the activities should be suspended in the high threat, and must be moved to new locations with better security situation if it took long time. Assumption: The team will receive training in PFA, staff care. UOSSM will mitigate the risk by daily communication with the security and safety team, following security advisories, recommendations, updating contingency plans, coordination with other service providers, close coordination with the protection cluster to implement the emergency response plan, provide PFA to the new IDPs and activate the mobile teams. Finally, exit strategy will be planned and put in place and revised regularly with close coordination with beneﬁciaries, other service providers and community leaders. -The risk of disruption of activities due to change of dominant forces on the ground Apply the exit strategy based on recommendation of safety and security and with close coordination with the community leaders, CPNs, clusters and other service providers. Activation of safe evacuation and destruction of documents and posters the lessons learned and the challenges that have been faced with the implementation of the project will be shared with the concerned managements for future use in designing of similar projects.
