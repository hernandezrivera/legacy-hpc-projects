Project Summary

UNRWA plans to provide up to 420,000 Palestine refugees with cash assistance to meet their basic needs, including food, non-food items (NFIs), and WASH needs. The Agency will continue to provide this assistance based on a targeted approach introduced in 2019, prioritizing refugees assessed as most vulnerable with a higher cash transfer. In 2022, it is expected that around 145,000 Palestine refugees will be assessed as most vulnerable based on the four vulnerability categories used by UNRWA (female headed households, families with persons with disabilities, families headed by an older person and unaccompanied minors/orphans). The remaining 275,000 vulnerable Palestine refugees will receive a lower cash transfer value as explained below.
Our cash transfer values were calculated while designing our Emergency Appeal 2022 intervening during the last quarter of 2021. The values were based on discussions with the CWG and in consultation with other humanitarian actors and are aligned with CWG recommendations, and more specifically the suggested MEB transfer values. Based on these discussions and recommendations, our cash transfer values were set to SYP 312,500 per month for a family of five members belonging to the most vulnerable categories and SYP 225,000 per month for a family of five members belonging to the remaining caseload of vulnerable Palestine refugees.
UNRWA plans to provide its monthly assistance to Palestine refugees covering twelve months over three rounds of assistance in 2022, with each round covering four months of cash assistance. Ideally, the rounds would start on 1 January, 1 May, and 1 September. In reality, UNRWA might adjust the start date and duration of each round depending on fund availability and taking into account cash flow considerations and donor requirements for the different grants.
UNRWA plans to conduct two post-distribution monitoring exercise in July and December 2022. UNRWA also has a result-based monitoring (RBM) system which tracks a number of indicators for all programmes and interventions including cash assistance. Quarterly management review (QMR) meetings involving relevant staff, including senior management, are held to track and assess the indicators. Mid-year result review (MYRR) and annual result review (ARR) are also conducted with departments at HQ to add another layer of scrutiny and to provide an overall overview of achievements and challenges.
It is worth mentioning that UNRWA also plans to provide in-kind food assistance to all 420,000 Palestine refugees through three distribution rounds of food baskets. Each food basket includes wheat flour, rice, bulgur wheat, sunflower oil, sugar, chick peas, lentils and milk (aligned with WFP). This intervention is designed to help Palestine refugees in addressing the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, coupled with the socio-economic crisis and the increase in prices of basic commodities, which have severely affected the food security status of Palestine refugees and depleted their comping mechanisms. The details of this intervention are included in a separate project submitted to the Food cluster.

4 - Needs

According to UNRWA assessments, 420,000 Palestine refugees (96%) are in need of humanitarian assistance and 145,000 Palestine refugees (35%) are categorized as most vulnerable (female-headed households, families with members with disabilities, families headed by an older person, and unaccompanied minors/orphans). Palestine refugees struggle to secure basic food, shelter, NFI, a WASH needs. According to UNRWA crisis monitoring report on the Socio-economic Situation of Palestine Refugees in Syria (May 2021), 82 % of Palestine refugees live with less than USD 1.9 per day (at the rate of USD 1= SYP 3,250) after receiving UNRWA cash assistance. A total of 54 per cent of families reported having taken debts within the last 3 months. Initial findings from PDM conducted by UNRWA in September 2021 found that 50% of respondents reported the purchase of food items as first priority, 16% shelter and housing, while the remaining 34% ranked other needs (NFIs, WASH, health and education). For most vulnerable households, the first indicated priority were food items (81%), non-food items (13%), education (3%) and health (2%). With regards to purchased foods, 16% of households reported purchase of vegetables, followed by oil and fat (15%), pulses, rice and cereals (13%) as well as sugar (13%) and bread and flour as well as milk, dairy products and eggs (12%). Protein rich foods (meat and poultry) were only purchased by 6% of households.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA distributes cash through BBSF Bank and Al Haram Company in addition to its own cash distribution centre in Damascus. The UNRWA’s distribution centre is established to exclusively distribute cash to the Beneficiaries who are not able to collect cash assistance from FSPs outlets due to lack of official IDs. UNRWA has an electronic registration module to generate lists of eligible beneficiaries and share it with the FSPs ahead of the starting date for planning purpose . UNRWA communicates the start date of the distribution round to the FSPs in order to ensure the availability of sufficient SYP fund at the outlets. UNRWA sends SMs to the beneficiaries to communicate to them the outlet’s location, the distribution date and time and, assigns dedicated technical UNRWA staff to each of the cash outlets, allowing the Agency to ensure strict compliance with cash assistance SOPs.Targeting criteria are based on UNRWA’s vulnerability assessment released in 2018, which found 91% of Palestine refugees in Syria to be under the poverty line. Based on this assessment , UNRWA introduced a targeted approach to cash assistance in the second half of 2019: Based on this two-tier system, female households headed by older persons and unaccompanied minors (orphans) receive a higher social transfer value, while the remainder of the vulnerable caseload receives a reduced amount of cash assistance. Availability of food and NFI is assessed through area offices and WFP surveys.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Abuse and exploitation: Potentially, the Beneficiaries face the risk of abuse an exploitation when travelling to and from distribution points or when withdrawing their cash assistance entitlement from the FSP outlets. • Mitigation measure: UNRWA ensures the proximity of cash distribution to the beneficiaries considering (1) a big network of FSPs outlets and (2) UNRWA gives the opportunity/flexibility to the beneficiaries to collect cash assistance from the nearest FSP outlet while are invited to attend another outlet. Risk 2: Exclusion (request of documentation): Beneficiaries who do not have official IDs are not able to collect cash from FSPs outlets.: UNRWA established a distribution Centre in Mazzeh to exclusively distribute cash to the Beneficiaries who are not able to collect cash assistance from FSPs outlets due to lack of official identification documents. Risk 3: Exclusion (selection criteria not understood). Some beneficiaries may be excluded and not considered vulnerable. Mitigation measure: UNRWA has a CFM to receive, assess the concerns of the beneficiaries, provide feedback and implement corrective actions. Risk 4: Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion), UNRWA has a CFM to capture GBV risks and staffs have read/are aware of the Code of Conduct. RISK 5: CP: a delegation system is in place to not exclude unaccompanied children.

UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risks linked to CVA implementation e.g. further inflation, liquidity issues, economic deterioration, impact on the market, etc. Mitigation: UNRWA is engaged very closely with the UN Country Team in its negotiations with the Central Bank of Syria to address the spread between the informal and formal exchange rate. UNRWA continuously assesses the socioeconomic context and remains open to considering alternative options and modalities, including in-kind assistance, to address the volatile conditions and the increasing inflation. With regards to liquidity, UNRWA provides cash assistance in local currency. Liquidity crisis for the local currency will be solve in discussion with Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and PRs.
