Project Summary

In line with the FSL cluster strategy for 2022, ACTED will contribute towards improving food security of over 712,536 beneficiaries through provision of in-kind and cash food assistance, enhancing emergency food production though distribution of livelihood kits and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs through resilience building activities such as training and market support functionality. Millions of South Sudanese remain displaced across the country following years of violent conflict that resulted in one of the most devastating humanitarian crises in recent history. Based on the latest IPC analysis and projections until July 2021, 60% of the population living in South Sudan is critically food insecure with 7.2 million people estimated to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above). Specifically, 4.668 million people are facing crisis (IPC Phase 3), 2.413 million people are facing emergency (IPC Phase 4) and 108,000 people are facing catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) food insecurity As such, the needs across South Sudan have severely increased, driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The project activity will be in line with the cluster Objectives “CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)
CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5)
CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (implemented across all 78 counties)

1 - Needs

With sever food insecurity expected to increase by around 7% from 2021, the needs surrounding Food Security and Livelihoods continue to be an area of intervention requiring significant attention. With food assistance expected to decrease by 10-20% from already reduced levels in 2021 coupled with increasing humanitarian emergencies such as consecutive floods and ongoing violence point to the fact that projected resources available for 2022 will only be sufficient to cover eight months of need, with the impacts predominantly being felt by women, children, individuals with disabilities and other vulnerable groups. According the South Sudan HNO 2022, the main drivers for this (1) a combination of shocks, such as climactic, conflict and economical (including, inflation, disruptions to livelihoods, floods and droughts); and (2) household idiosyncratic stressors, such as GBV, lack of access to basic essential services, deaths caused by diseases or deaths of family breadwinners. Likewise, certain cross-cutting issues such nutrition, WASH – particularly in food preparation – and protection concerns, such as GBV, displacement and conflict further exacerbate stresses on food security. Although the international focus is shifting away from Food Security and Livelihood interventions, the severity of food insecurity is expected to worsen in 2021/2022 compared to 2020/2021. Ongoing conflicts and persistent natural disasters that are increasing in severity, along with the inability to access markets and a continuing economic crisis have significantly impacted regional food security. Likewise, food prices continue to increase based on drops in supply caused by droughts or year-round waterlogging of croplands as a result of heavy rains in Ethiopia and high-water levels in lakes in Uganda. To address these needs, ACTED will continue to play a role of a strong FSL partner and conduct activities in line with the following cluster objectives: CO1 : Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5)

2 - Project activities

To achieve these objectives, ACTED will 1. Undertake general food distribution or unconditional cash/ voucher transfers for the most severely food insecure (IPC 4 & 5, refugees and POCs), with blanket supplementary feeding for children under 5 and PWLs in Wau, Jur River, Awerial, Bor South and Maban counties. 2. Provide conditional assistance in the form of food through Food for Assets activities for RES in Jur River, Bor South and Maban counties. 3. Distribute fishing & rapid response livelihood kits as part of an integrated response 4. Protect livelihoods through main season seeds & tool and fish & vegetable kit distribution in Wau, Jur River, Awerial, Bor South, and Maban 5. Implement activities to help sustain community coping mechanisms and build resilience in; measures to combat: Post harvest losses (PHL); pest surveillance & control especially FAW and DL in IPC 3. 6. Support market functionality: scaled up support for market dependent households in Jur River county and WBeG and above HHs; -Continue with COVID IEC materials & messaging with adherence to distribution SOP/ guidelines for in kind/ cash/ livelihood kits; and identification of PSN

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher programming is used as complementary to the in-kind food aid provision in ACTED’s programming. Cash and voucher modalities will be preferred where possible. This includes preference from beneficiaries and availability of items in local markets as well as capacity of the market to absorb such transfers as in areas directly affected by disaster, functioning markets are either not available or slow to recover from the shock with limited availability of goods due to hampered access or damaged infrastructure. ACTED will implement both conditional (cash for work) and unconditional (emergency needs for the most vulnerable households). The conditional modality, which is cash for work for market rehabilitation construction, will have a two-fold benefit; supporting the households financially while improving access to market. The unconditional modality will facilitate access to a wider range of items and empower the beneficiaries to choose the specific items that their household needs, in locations where the markets are functional

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

• Market analysis is a crucial part of ACTED’s cash and voucher activities and are incorporated into the response. Before undertaking any cash or voucher activities, ACTED will conduct a detailed market analysis and routinely monitor prices to ensure the market is functional and has the sufficient capacity to meet the needs. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ACTED counts on a robust internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA. All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which includes ACTED’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy and Child Protection Policy. The staff will receive refresher trainings on these policies on a regular basis. ACTED will take the necessary measures to ensure that the communities receiving WASH items will have safe access to distribution sites by selecting an accessible location. Vulnerable and/or marginalized groups will be included in community consultations to ensure they are included in decision-making processes and receive the assistance provided by ACTED. ACTED will conduct inception meetings with external stakeholders including local authorities to ensure that the scope of the project and any selection criteria is widely understood.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The proposed action will incorporate gender, age and diversity into the full project management cycle in order to prevent and mitigate protection risks and ensure all activities are inclusive. Where possible, gender, age and (to some extent) diversity disaggregated information will be collected to further inform the programming and advocacy. The integrated protection response proposed adopts a twin-track approach by ensuring protection is mainstreamed throughout the project cycle (awareness, attitude change, integration and inclusion) and by aiming at empowering communities. ACTED will focus on the empowerment of affected populations and particularly women in its approach. Prevention and mitigation of risks related to GBV and gender inequalities will be integrated into the interventions through GBV/Gender mainstreaming. The incorporation and monitoring of the protection principles in all project activities and sites’ locations will be assessed in terms of safety and accessibility.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Climate change is one of the major issues in South-Sudan as flooding massively affects populations. Floods exacerbate the vulnerability of communities and force people to flee their houses, increasing their basic needs. Given the climatic situation in South-Sudan, ACTED will ensure in each of its interventions to strengthen the resilience and self-reliance of communities to reduce the impacts of recurrent shocks. As an example in this project, ACTED will strengthen the communities to adopt strategies to increase their resilience to climate and disaster-related shocks/stressors, including flooding, through participatory, community-driven planning and resource management.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

To support households in meeting their essential food and nutrition needs, and to reduce negative coping mechanisms that can lead to increased protection risks, ACTED intends to support the targeted communities through life-saving food assistance. The assistance will include the new arrivals to address their urgent food needs. Where needed ACTED will utilize cash-based transfers as to not to disrupt the functioning of the markets available to the populations. ACTED will also integrate resilience-building activities into its programing to ensure holistic support to the communities.
