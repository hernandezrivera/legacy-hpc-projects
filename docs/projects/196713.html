Project Summary

The Project will improve children feeding practices to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality among infants and young children in NWS & NCS by ensuring access to safe, adequate and appropriate preventive and curative nutrition services.
Based on the monthly Nutrition Surveillance reports issued by assessment technical working group in NWS in the targeted locations by community surveillance activities, the coverage of nutrition services is low, and about 62% of malnutrition cases are not receiving any malnutrition treatment, which means there is a need to increase the numbers of OTPs / RRTs, at the same time increase the coverage of nutrition community surveillance activities in NWS.
In order to address these challenges, WV in collaboration with sub implementing partners (IPs) proposes a project to enhance access to quality nutrition services and foster Resilience through Multi-Sector Emergency Support to the affected community. The target locations are areas with high GAM rate, and host high number of acute food insecure population and each RRT will provide the main preventive nutrition services and treatment of malnourished children 6 – 59 months and treatment of malnourished PLWs for communities and camps, in addition to performing referrals for sever acute malnutrition cases with medical complication to nearest in-patient care. The project will provide treatment of malnutrition cases (SAM & MAM) in 4 Outpatient therapeutic Program (OTP) centers in Aleppo and Idleb (In Jarablus, Daret Azza Ar-Ra'ee, Jsr-Ash-Shugur) and these centers will be integrated with existing Primary health Care services. 3 community surveillance teams will be covering Jsr-Ash-Shugur, Janudiyeh, Badama subdistricts in Idleb. 30 RRTs will be supported under this project and distributed in Aleppo and Idleb based on the need and in close coordination with nutrition cluster and other actors.
In North-Central Syria, WV through implementing partner will be providing nutrition screening services, IYCF services, and treatment of moderate (MAM) and severe malnutrition (SAM) cases with referrals for SAM case with medical complications in Ras Al Ain.
Nutrition staff will be trained in PSS skills to support mothers who are in distress or unable to engage with their infants, and to recognize when a mother needs further, specialized mental health support and also other red flags such as signs of gender-based violence. If Child Protection needs are identified during the assessment, or following, families will be referred to the CP CM program. The intensive counselling and education will be supported by information, education, and communication (IEC) materials that have been developed by or in line with the Nutrition, Protection and FSL clusters.
The activities will be implemented in the North West & North Central Syria in Aleppo, Idleb, and Al Hasakeh. The project will ensure the intentional target of Women or children with disabilities to support with assistive devices, mainstreaming interventions with access to services and resources in Health Care facilities. Severe acutely malnourished children with complications will be referred for inpatient care at the existing stabilization centers. The project will provide training in nutrition screening and referral services. The project will also ensure that nutrition teams are equipped with minimum supplies needed for nutrition screening activities, (e.g. MUAC tapes, registration and reporting format, referral forms, guidelines etc.), promoting and supporting community mobilization and sensitization activities towards nutrition screening program. Nutrition supplies will be received as in-kind donations from UNICEF and WFP and other supplies will be procured under the project.
The total catchment population 2,556,970(2,452,970 in Idleb, 99,000 Aleppo, 5000 Al Hasaka-Ras AlAin), 2-3% are disabled children under 5 - as per findings of nutrition community surveillance teams in NWS.

3 - Needs

According to the last SMART survey conducted by PAC in July 2022 in NWS, the prevalence of combined Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in NWS was 5%, and the combined SAM rate was 1.7%, the chronic malnutrition (stunting) among children 6 – 59 months is still one of the main public health problems in NWS. The SMART survey further informed that the prevalence of the stunting was 22.3%.The wasted children over stunting had a higher death rate than only wasted children. Among PLWs, the prevalence of acute malnutrition across northwest Syria is 12.96%. Also, the micronutrients deficiencies and Anemia are the main public health problems between children and Women of Reproductive Age (WRA), the anemia rate between children was 36% and the anemia rate between Women of Reproductive Age was about 52%.Across all populations children under 5, adolescent girls and PLWs have relatively high nutritional needs and are the most vulnerable groups receiving support through the nutrition sector.HNO 2023 indicates approximately 5.9 million people including 3.75 million children (2.2 million girls and 1.55 million boys) and 2.1 million women are in dire need of nutrition assistance in Syria, an increase from 5.5 million people in 2022. Syria is faced with a triple burden of malnutrition- high chronic malnutrition depicted by high prevalence of stunting, increasing levels of acute malnutrition and emergency levels of anaemia among children and women of reproductive age.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project adopts CMAM with interventions to address malnutrition that are both preventive and curative. The preventative approach will address the causes of malnutrition and identify mitigation measures through community sensitizing on malnutrition among children under 5 and PLW. The project will educate PLW and caregivers on infant and young feeding practices to promote optimal infant feeding practices, including exclusive breast feeding. The curative approach will screen, identify and manage the already malnourished cases through OTP and TSFP based on the criteria of admission based on CMAM guideline. The project will engage local NGO partners and their Community Nutrition/Health Volunteers who will provide pre-distribution nutrition education as well as, post distribution monitoring. This project will establish, train, and support Mother to Mother Support Groups for the successful implementation of Maternal Infant & Young Child Nutrition. RRTs will be functioning using the SOP developed by Gaziantep nutrition cluster. Project monitoring will take place at all levels, including community, local council, and governorate levels. The project will submit monthly updates and formal reports every 6 months to disseminate activity progress and challenges with validation and triangulated with the clusters (4W), partners. An accountability mechanism will also be there.Cash or Vouchers will be delivered for families of malnourished children and PLWs to buy diverse healthy foods.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.The likelihood of Safety and security risks is high as there is always a geopolitical, religious crisis and economic downturn sometimes. WV will continue monitoring the situation and adjust the programs based on the context change. 2.Unequal access to services for women, girls and other at-risk groups like children with disabilities. A high likelihood of low decision making capacity in the family leads to child marriage and adolescent pregnancy leading to malnutrition. WV will emphasize gender mainstreaming in the programs and engagement of men for some interventions.3.Increased risk of GBV due to lack of global and national sector standards related to protection, rights and GBV risk. Although a medium likelihood WV can build awareness on GBV with supportive linkages to GVB case management. 4.Lack of participation by vulnerable group in project in age, gender & culture appropriate way. This is low likelihood as WV plans to engage community at all stage of project cycle management. WV will initiate program accountability to listen to the community.5.The need to advocate for and support the deployment of GBV specialists within the nutrition sector. WV will keep special eyes on the equity of feeding practices, complementary feeding, adolescent (girls) nutrition, vaccination, etc. to ensure the wellbeing of women & girls. Another focus can be given on sensitizing education sector on equity for nutritional aspects.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WV adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards Sexual Exploitations & Abuse. The support will be provided for the PSEA mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding and PSEA available policy review and development with partners. The ToT training will be provided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in addition WV field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community in proper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists which are used by WV field staff which all is constantly monitored through the project lifecycle. Furthermore, WV has its own safeguarding incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WV uses a standard online platform named Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response. WV will ensure this hotline information portal is available to every location to ensure quick complains and will be responded by a third party investigator to remain unbiased, fair management of the complains.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Prices remain generally stable or fluctuate within the buffer allowed for inflation. The indicators can be high inflation rates, significant increases in the cost of implementation and led to deteriorating the economic situation and increase the risk of food insecurity. Project budget has taken into account the possibility of price fluctuation and will closely monitor monthly spending reports and market prices. The budget will be revised as necessary. Covid-19 Pandemic and cholera outbreak may create the uncertain situation if cases increase . The indicators can be high cases of C-19 reported increase positivity rate daily, high death rate due to C-19 or cholera. WV will continue interventions with online modalities using phones or other available devices maintaining health measures. WV will support the beneficiaries with adaptive interventions by giving in kind supports as needed for survival. No logistical constraints beyond WV’s control. The indicators can be mon renewal of cross border resolution. WV will closely monitor the supply and price of goods available through networking with other organizations and multiple suppliers. Additionally, during the heavy rain season, WV will coordinate with partners and identify alternative solutions to transportation challenges and ensure enough lead time for deliveries. Security condition significantly disrupt project implementation like threats, attacks, violence. WV will monitor the context on the ground and adjust activities.
