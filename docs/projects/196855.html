Project Summary

The project aims to improve the financial situation for the most vulnerable families in northwest Syria by providing live-saving humanitarian assistance for 1000 vulnerable families (6000 individuals: 1287 men, 1353 women, 1649 Boys, 1711 girls) That will be achieved through:
- Distribution of cash for 1000 vulnerable families (6000 individuals: 1287 men, 1353 women, 1649 Boys, 1711 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates while each family will be supported with monthly e-vouchers (100 USD balance) to address their needs for continuous 8 months.
The e-voucher (NFC) modality provides beneficiaries with the freedom to receive the cash from several cash distribution points.
BINAA will prepare a vendor map and provide the selected vendors with devices and training on how to use these devices. in addition to conducting orientation sessions on using e-voucher cards for the beneficiaries.
BINAA will take the COVID-19 precautions in all project phases starting from beneficiary selection till the bread distribution according to BNAA COVID-19 Guidance
We are targeting both males and females in this project including all ages. We will also take the elderly and people with disabilities.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO2023, The Syrian economy continued to contract with severe macroeconomic deterioration throughout 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal spending, and economic sanctions have plunged additional population sectors into humanitarian need.People in Syria are sliding deeper into poverty and finding it overwhelmingly difficult to make ends meet.The inability to meet basic needs is evident across the whole of Syria, with only minor differences between population groups, and despite 94 percent of households having an employed head of household. Most often, households cite the combined impact of high prices and lack of income as the main reasons for their inability to meet basic needs. BINAA conducted a rapid need assessment in July 2022. The assessment covered 51 camps in 6 sub-districts (Aghtarin, Ghandurah, AlBab, Suran, Azzaz, Jarablus).The main objectives of the assessment were to Identify needs, affected groups, and geographical areas and interventions requiring immediate assistance and develop an evidence-based strategy and implementation plan for improving the situation for the vulnerable people in these communities. A semi-structured questionnaire with Key Informants was designed to address the above objectives. The assessment results indicate an urgent need for interventions, especially support by MPC to families who cannot secure their daily needs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points, or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm to some women and may cause domestic violence.- Likelihood: M. Impact: H.- Mitigation: -We will not distribute at a late time of the day. -We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute in each village separately. -During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties.- The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers.-Likelihood: w. -Impact: H.-Mitigation: -Choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. -In case any exploitation happens, BINAA will take appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation where allegations of SEA/GBV are reported involving our personnel (suspending staff till the investigation is conducted, referring the victim to PSS, and even witness protection program if needed) and investigate allegations in a timely and professional manner.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: The risks of SEA can be compounded during project implementation Likelihood:L. Impact: H. Mitigation:1-Ensure effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by integrating PSEA in BINAA policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA or GBV.2- BINAA has a PSEA officer who is the PSEA focal point, and he will make appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing prevention strategies. This could include collecting and analyzing information on actual/potential risk factors for vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse and elaborating measures to address them. Make sure that PSEA materials and posters are visible and in place in all targeted locations. Receive complaints, reports, and questions about alleged acts of sexual exploitation and abuse committed by the organization’s personnel. And track all PSEA-related activities for the Organization and ensure that all information on PSEArelated activities is shared with relevant persons in the Organization.
