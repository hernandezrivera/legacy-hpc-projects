Project Summary

Armadilla has been working in Syria within the health sector since the year 2005 with a specific focus on persons with disabilities. The Syrian crisis in March 2011, has dramatically worsened the situation, and since then the Organisation developed and implemented projects in the Damascus Area, in strict cooperation with different local actors (both NGOs and health specialised centres), in coordination with the Ministry of Health of Syria. According to the World Bank, over one billion people, or 15 percent of the world’s population, live with disabilities; in Syria, 3.7 million or 27 percent of the total population (aged 12+) have a disability, confirming that prevalence and negative impacts of living with a disability are more pervasive in developing and crisis-affected countries. According to the “HNAP, Disability: Prevalence and Impact. A Nationwide Household Survey Using Washington Group Methodology”, 2019, 26% of resident population in Syria suffers one or more form of disability, while the percentage among IDPs raises up to 29% and around 25% among the returnees. Distribution of people with disability among the national territory is somehow consistent, having a peak in the Rural Damascus Area (32%) and the less prevalence in As-Sweida (19%), even if the difference among the different territories may have different explanation, including difficulties in certain areas in collecting useful and fair data, more or less presence of International Humanitarian Aid, etc.
What is certain is that disability in the whole of Syria is one of the first concerns that humanitarian actors should considered, especially since the capacity of Syrian actors (health, education, social welfare) in assisting and supporting the needs of them has been hugely reduced by the conflict, and it is a condition that shall impact the capacity of families and communities along the time.
In this framework, Armadilla developed a 360-degree approach twin track approach to respond the crisis, with projects and actions aim to:
a) Deliver immediate assistance to people with disability, and women (reproductive health services).
b) Develop and implement activities with the aim to increase the capacity of local actors in working about the disability topics, through training cycles for professionals of the Centre for Prosthesis and Rehabilitation of the Ibn Al Nafis Hospital and delivering teachings for the university degree in occupational therapy at Manara University of Latakia and Public University of Damascus.
In collaboration with the Syrian association Zahret Al Madan, which among the different activities implemented in the Damascus and Rural Damascus area manages a clinic in the Yarmouk Camp, Armadilla will establish a service of Reproductive Health, providing the necessary resources for the clinic, and delivering awareness sessions for women and the community on RH topics. This service, according to one feasibility study developed in the Autumn 2021, is totally absent in the Yarmouk area. The medical service will be provided by a doctor who will be present in the clinic two days a week for the entire period of the project (spaces, equipment and medicines will be made available by the clinic).
Furthermore, the project here presented is integrated with activities proposed by Armadilla under the umbrella of Protection sector, which include assistance to children with disability and protection of women.

3 - Needs

In the whole of Syria health care systems for trauma, suffers for lack of equipment and, above all, health workers are insuﬃciently trained. The Prosthetic and Rehabilitation Centre (CPR) of the Ibn Al Naﬁs public hospital is a public hospital located in Rukk el Din, Damascus where people form the whole country is referred to receive prosthesis, orthosis, and rehabilitation therapies pathways. The CPR assist people of all age, with born or acquired disabilities. Two huge gaps hinder the capacity of the Syrian actors in delivering quality services to final beneficiaries in the field of disability. 1) techniques taught to students of physical rehabilitation and prosthesis are old and do not fit with the present problems deriving (also) by the consequence of the war, and b) a chronic lack of equipment and consumables. the responsible of the services. As data collected by Armadilla in November2021, the director of the CPR pointed out as as there is still a huge lack in equipment for therapy pathways, and, again, of specific expertise among the Centre’s staff. Especially, the CPR would need to improve its services, among others, in the field of occupational therapy, speech therapy, rehabilitation after stroke, pain therapy. Regarding the Occupational Therapy (OT), has been observed that the practice is totally absent in Syria. Finally, the clinic of Zahret al Madan in Yarmouk requested a support in order to activate reproductive health services for the women of the community.

Project activities will be implemented by the Organisation in strict co-ordination with local partners (CPR of Damascus, Zahret Al Madan, and Aamal/University Manara of Latakia+Univeristy of Damascus).At this aim, Armadilla already established general Memorandum of Understanding with the partners, which are willing to cooperate to the project. Moreover, Armadilla since the year 2006 has signed a protocol of cooperation with the MoH and the MoSAL. Activities will be monitored by Armadilla’s staff in Syria and abroad, which also will take care of the logistic needed for the realization of the project. Activities to be implemented:1. Establish a reproductive health services in the Yarmouk clinic of Zahret al Madan. A staff composed by medical doctor, psychologist and nurses will be available twice in the clinic and for house visits. The staff will deliver also awareness sessions for the community.2. Support the capacity of CPR in equipping the new building addressing people with disability coming from the whole of Syria3. Training to CPR staff on several subjects to improve their capacity in working with PwD4. Teachings to students of University on OT topics, delivered by Italian professors Training at CPR and for students and professionals’s experts in Occupational Therapy topics will be performed by professors of European Universities.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSA will be implemented starting from principles and methodologies in use in the Organisation.Identified risks related to the proposed actvities are solely for the activity of Reproductive health, which is te only kind of activites that may be affected by the problem.Risks will be prevented:1) activity will be implemented in safe space provided by local associations and managed by Armadilla and local association staff2) staff assigned to the actvitiy is composed uniquely by women3) visits will be performed always in pressence of minimum 2 staff (doctor+psychologist or doctor+nurse)4) patient should register in advance the visits, hence may be accompanied to the centre by relatives/friends who will serve as deterrent for any potential abuser.Regarding awareness campaign/infodays:1) They will be performed in safe places2) Volunteers of the local association will be always present to guarantee a secure environment3) Campaigns will share information regarding the risks of SEA and mitigation/prevention measuresFor all the other activities no risks were detected, considering that beneficiaries will be reached in safe environments and are health professionals participating in trainings.A specific matrix for the mitigation of risks will be drafted before the start of activities and updated in the course of the time.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to the Area of the activities: Locations where the project will be implemented, Centre for Prosthesis and Rehabilitation (CPR) of Damascus, University Al Manara of Latakia and Yarmouk clinic, are areas so far considered totally secure and accessible. However, the Organisation will be in continuous communication with the relevant security actors in order to organize activities and movements only after consultation with them. Availability of goods: Goods will be purchased on the local market, looking for the best vendor in terms of economy and capacity of guarantee the provision of service. A back-up list of potential suppliers guarantees the possibility of ﬁnding the goods in case of problems will be drafted. Lack of permission to enter in Syria for expatriate staff: Armadilla is not experienced problems in obtaining by authorities visas for expatriate staff/experts, however to avoid any delay and obstacle in implementing the activities, monitoring and logistics activities will be realized through Syrian staff of the Organisation, supported by remote by international experts. Experts and professors: until the end of the year, training delivered by international experts should be done through remote teaching, therefore no risks are detected.
