Project Summary

The proposed action aims to improve the access of boys and girls to safe and inclusive caring environments in Derna and Benghazi through capacity building of child protection and education actors, the provision of PSS and recreational activities for children and caregivers, awareness-raising and sensitization sessions and the provision of case management for children in need of specialized child protection services.
In Benghazi, ACTED will strengthen the capacity of child protection and education actors as well as non-child protection actors – including Libyan national/local authorities and CSOs - by providing capacity-building trainings on child protection and PSS. The training topics will mostly cover PSS to children, child psychology, children’s rights, child development and children’s well-being, including child protection and the identification of child protection risks and concerns. ACTED will also deliver dedicated sessions to address the topic of inclusion, non-discrimination and access to services for non-Libyan populations, life skills and social emotional learning as well as case management and referral pathways. Through these sessions, ACTED will help strengthen participants’ capacity to identify, monitor and respond to child protection issues.
ACTED will also conduct PSS recreational activities in Benghazi, responding to children’s wellbeing needs through an integrated approach combining education and protection interventions. These activities will also be group-based and individualized PSS support will be provided where needed to the most vulnerable children and their parents and caregivers. In a context where both parents and teachers noted signs of distress and psychological consequences from the conflict and because of the recent COVID-19 outbreak, these activities will respond to the children’s wellbeing needs. The activities will also seek the participation of parents and community members to contribute to reinforce family and community support systems and foster an enabling and supportive environment for young people, aiming at building resilience and social cohesion.
ACTED will engage in both cities, with local communities to conduct awareness-raising sessions for parents and caregivers and children on key issues pertaining to child protection, psychosocial support, as well as to support building acceptance of the project and participation of beneficiaries. Awareness-raising sessions will also focus on topics related to children enrolment in schools, COVID-19 and the importance of prioritizing children’s education.
In both cities, ACTED will incorporate a case management system for children in need of specialized services or assistance. The child protection case management system will address the needs of vulnerable children experiencing or at risk of abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. In the context of Benghazi and Derna, ACTED’s case management unit will pay particular attention in identifying children-at-risk, children with disabilities with protection concerns, children working in hazardous environments and exploited, child and adolescent survivors of SGBV, as well as adolescents who were formerly recruited by armed groups.

1 - Needs

The scale and intensity of the protracted humanitarian crisis continues to increase in Libya, substantially affecting the well-being and mental health of children exposed to severe protection risks. The 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview reports that 160,000 children in Libya face severe protection needs, with more than half being IDPs and 26% being refugees and migrants. IDP, refugee and migrant children continue to face barriers in accessing basic services such as education due to lack of documents, discrimination and language barriers. According to the 2020 MSNA, 15% of HHs reported at least one child protection concern. Verbal and sexual harassment, robbery and fearing kidnapping being the highest concerns for girls, while fearing being robbed or kidnapped and fearing being injured by an explosive hazard being the highest for boys. 36% of HHs also reported believing that safety and security risks for children have increased since the COVID-19 outbreak, Benghazi being the top mantika with HHs reporting this (52%). Safety concerns for children attending schools was also reported by HHs (14%) in the 2020 MSNA for both cities. In Benghazi, ACTED’s 2019 Protection Monitoring Factsheet highlights that 46% of interviewed households reported that children 6-17 years old were attending school. Public schools were reported to be overcrowded with children facing risks such as violence in and out of schools, bullying, drug abuse, proliferation of small arms and travel routes to/from schools. ACTED’s EiE Needs Assessment conducted in December 2019 reveals clear gaps in education service provision as well as child protection needs in the wake of the armed conflict in Derna. 22% of households had one or more school-aged child out of education, with the cost of education constituting the most important barrier to education. The psychological impact of the conflict in Derna on children is evident. 87% of households with children stated they had noticed signs of distress in their children during the conflict. Parents, teachers and students spoke to the ongoing psychological impact of the conflict on children after the end of hostilities, who also lack recreational spaces and activities. IDPs face the highest level of HH vulnerability with a lower ability to meet basic needs and higher adoption of negative coping strategies.

2 - Activities

In Benghazi, ACTED will strengthen the capacity of child protection and education actors as well as non-child protection actors by providing capacity-building trainings on PSS to children, child psychology, children’s rights, child development and children’s well-being, including child protection and the identification of child protection risks and concerns. ACTED will also deliver dedicated sessions to address the topic of inclusion, non-discrimination and access to services for non-Libyan populations, life skills and social emotional learning as well as case management and referral pathways. ACTED will also conduct a range of appropriate, structured, age and gender-specific recreational activities in Benghazi. Awareness raising and key child protection messages will also be incorporated into the activities. These activities will also be combined with group-based PSS activities and individualized PSS sessions where needed to the most vulnerable children. ACTED will organize meetings for parents to improve their resilience, contribute to reinforce family support systems, engage with them in discussions on child protection and promote positive parenting. ACTED will conduct awareness-raising sessions with local communities in both cities on key issues pertaining to child protection, PSS, as well as to support building acceptance of the project and participation of beneficiaries. In both cities, ACTED will incorporate a case management system for children in need of specialized services or assistance. This system will address the needs of vulnerable children experiencing or at risk of abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

ACTED dedicated security staff continuously monitor conflict developments to anticipate access concerns and modify procedures in advance. ACTED has in place standard operating procedures designed to minimize the exposure of staff and beneficiaries to security threats. Should changes in conflict dynamics cause a deterioration of the security situation, threatening the safety of staff or beneficiaries, ACTED will potentially adapt the program if needed in coordination (whether delivering assistance through partners, proxies, or by shifting the area of activities to more secure locations). Risk 4.4: ACTED will ensure the provision of PPE materials throughout the entire implementation of project activities. ACTED staff will utilize and distribute PPE materials such as hand sanitizers and masks to all beneficiaries participating to in-person activities, such as, children, ACTED staff, community members, parents and caregivers. Furthermore, in other to mitigate the spread of the COVID-19 virus and abide to its preventive measures, ACTED will conduct small in- person group activities and ensure adequate space for social distancing between beneficiaries. Risk 4.6: Access to all sensitive specific data on beneficiaries will be strictly limited to those staff members directly involved in processing it and working copies will be deleted once no longer needed. Access to data will be limited using password-protected documents. ACTED will utilize red flag forms when dealing with more sensitive cases. ACTED will also ensure trainings to all staff team on confidentiality and data management.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

ACTED’s AME Unit will conduct Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with parents, caregivers, and community members in order to receive feedback and suggestions and improve the project activities accordingly to ensure that none of the activities are indirectly harming any of the beneficiaries. Consultations will be held in the start-up phase of the project to adapt the project design and the implementation methodology to the priorities of beneficiaries and communities. ACTED will also continuously monitor the quality and effectiveness of project deliveries including by collecting feedback from beneficiaries directly and through the complaints and response mechanism (CRM), ensuring that the proposed action remains relevant to the needs of beneficiaries throughout the project implementation. The CRM is managed by the AME staff to ensure independence and for which confidentiality is a fundamental principle. Complaints are received via a dedicated CRM phoneline and are then recorded and transferred to the CRM Focal point confidentially and registered in a password-protected database, categorized based on the level of sensitivity. Information is shared on a strictly need-to-know basis and restricted as much as possible. The CRM Focal Point responds to all individuals who have made a complaint to inform them of the outcome of their complaint. Within this project, ACTED will actively work to ensure that the CRM is able to be accessed by any parent or caregiver regardless of their level of literacy, to ensure that marginalized groups can access it. This served as an active line of communication for ACTED to receive any grievances from community members regarding the implementation of the project activities or any feedback and suggestions for changes. These calls therefore directly translate into recommendations to programs for improvement of programming, and the resolution of such complaints communicated directly back to the beneficiaries.
