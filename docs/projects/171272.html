Project Summary

The persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices. At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence has remained Serious (10–14.9%) for the past four consecutive seasons (11.8 in Gu 2020, 13.1% in 2019/20 Deyr, 13.8% in 2019 Gu and 12.6 % in 2018/19 Deyr). High levels of acute malnutrition continue to persist driven by several factors, including high morbidity, below sphere immunization and Vitamin-A supplementation, poor childcare practices, inadequate access to clean/safe drinking water and acute food insecurity. Consequently, the country is facing a Triple Threat of Desert Locust, flooding’s and the global pandemic COVID-19 contributing towards the deterioration of production outputs, social economics increasing vulnerabilities of households. These culminations result in impact on the states of acute malnutrition situation affecting children and women. As of September 30th, 2020, the nutrition cluster partners reached 244,727 boys and 287,318 girls aged 06-59 months covering 66% of HRP targets. Of which 190,366 are severely acute malnourished children, 341,679 moderately acute malnourished children and 104,017 PLW admitted. In order to improve the situation, integrated programming and linkages to (WASH, Health and FSL) are needed and through early action and preventive interventions to the high risk groups the situation can be addressed effectively. A Critical (4-5.6%) prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was observed in the Riverine livelihood of Beletweyne district and in urban Beletweyne. Morbidity among children was high (≥20%) in 15 out 33 population groups surveyed. However, Crude Death Rate and Under-Five Death Rate were relatively low in most areas. Integrated interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation. Hiraan, Bay and Bakool region were considered hotspot areas with a projected Critical GAM prevalence, indicated by a weight-for-height z-score of 15-29.9% or by a Mid-Upper Arm Circumference below 125 mm of ≥10%, IDP areas of concern are Baidoa and Beletweyne is an urban area of concern for acute malnutrition and need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions This project therefore target to reach a total 9,000 vulnerable boys, girls and pregnant and lactating women through an integrated response around Nutrition. It is intended to mitigate the physical and protection consequences of drought on populations and communities directly affected in Bakol, Bay and Hiran region, in particular, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Beletweyne town. The main objective of this project is to provide integrated lifesaving emergency Nutrition services to 9,000 drought affected direct beneficiaries and intends to achieve the following, capacity building of nutrition staff on IMAM. Referral of malnourished children with complicated cases will be done to the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) centers and directly linked with primary health care services managed by HIDIG and other actors in the program areas thus providing more holistic opportunity to the target beneficiaries HIDIG will closely coordinate with Local MOH as well as Federal level MOH and social mobilizers, elders all involved in activities on mobilizing communities. To support increase access to basic nutrition services in the area

Mitigation Measures

Child labor, sexual exploitation, discrimination and poor documentation on the specific needs of the people at risk, to mitigate this, HIDIG will however, put in place strict policy that will protect the right of children in emergency and conflict status, it will ensure all its staff are capacity build on child right and protection mainstreaming in its nutrition intervention/activities, it shall also advocate community awareness In its implementation of nutrition interventions through equal representation of community nutrition volunteers (CNV) responsible for selecting the most vulnerable household as well ensure no harm policy within its implementation of the program. HIDIG will strive to adopt most reasonable project implementation methodology such as registration of beneficiaries in the far village to enable take services to the most vulnerable with less mobility status, through this, HIDIG will achieved handsomely on wider coverage to its target population. HIDIG will ensure to protects its beneficiary information and as well adopt reasonable program location that will ensure access and safety of the beneficiary. it shall also ensure both men and women boys and girls have equal access to project information and services in the target areas, through this program HIDIG will ensure its staff and community members were capacity built on “DO NO HARM” principle of neutrality as well as disseminates project information to the community members through consultative forums and in particular religious’ institution such as mosque. This will facilitate more inclusive of both gender and various community members hence create an atmosphere of more responsive coverage among the target group. HIDIG will ensure the safety and dignity of its beneficiaries by providing regular monitoring and reporting of the new challenges and opportunity in consultation and coordination with other stakeholders and community key point The following are main risk factors that might impact negatively on the beneficiaries during implementation such as intimidation as well as sexual exploitation among poor women and girls beneficiaries, it may also risk of beneficiary by registering one main clan against other dwellers in settlement through collusion with project staff hence this might create community tension in this case conflict might a raise thus dangering the safety of these vulnerable population. Also leaking of vital beneficiary data might be risk associated with arm conflict and violence this is because in the instance militia groups get the beneficiary details they might intimidate and force to surrender the little foods provided thus risking the life of the vulnerable women, men, boys and girl’s population. In mitigating the above HIDIG intends to establish strict beneficiary data protection for safety purpose as well as facilitates equal representation among the target community in selection of community health workers that will directly work with HIDIG nutrition teams. Staff and community capacity shall be build and ensure staff signed code of conduct
