Project Summary

. PROPOSED ACTION
A) Context
Chad, the fifth largest country in Africa, currently ranks 186th of 188 countries in the Human Development Index (HDI) . Chad hosts more than 609,400 displaced people, of which 407,996 are refugees. Since 2015, the Lake Region of Western Chad has experienced attacks from the Jihadist group Boko Haram based out of Nigeria. According to Chads Demographic and health Survey 2014 (DHS) , One in four children aged 12-23 months (25%) was completely vaccinated. Immunization coverage of children has improved since 2004 (11%) but a lot still remain to be done to improve the coverage. In February 2017, IMC participated in an OCHA-led multisectoral needs assessment of 14 villages (population of around 40,000 people) in the Bol district where women and children who may have formerly been with or held by Boko Haram returned after surrendering to local authorities at the end of 2016. The assessment revealed that the diseases prevailing in the villages surveyed are first of all malaria (95%), diarrhea (50%) potentially related to the water quality problems and maternal health problems (20%). The results also reveal that only about a quarter (26%) of children who had symptoms of IRA, received treatment from a health facility. Most of the villages are located in the island areas of the region, where communities are remote from health centers and other social services, and vulnerable to insecurity. These communities feel abandoned by the Government and have not been reached by humanitarian interventions since the government-declared state of emergency was established in July 2015. This state of emergency expired at the end of 2016, and has not been renewed. During the assessment, 10 out of the 14 sites were characterized as having critical health needs (score of 4 out of 5; 5 being the highest level of need).
The major causes of morbidity and mortality remain preventable illnesses such as malaria, acute respiratory infections, worm infestations, and diarrhea. Sexually transmitted infections and HIV-AIDS are also diseases of public health significance in the area and the potential for disease outbreaks (i.e. Acute Watery Diarrhea, Meningitis, and Measles) in the project areas underlines the need for increased emergency preparedness. Currently, despite the recent increase in prenatal and immunization services, coverage rates remain below international approved targets. Hard to reach areas in the Lake region islands are not currently benefitting from these services due to difficult access.
Through this project, IMC will engage with the MoH authorities at the health area and health district level to re-establish this link through the CHW who will be responsible for providing basic health services, raising awareness and referring cases to the health centers. The CHW will be held accountable by the populations they serve. It is worth noting that International Medical Corps is the co-lead of the health cluster in Chad and has been discussing this approach in the health cluster coordination meetings and so far there has been no funding available for this important intervention to be rolled out and there is no partner implementing the iCCM approach in the Lake Region of Chad.
B) Proposed Intervention Strategy
The iCCM is an equity-focused strategy that complements and extends the reach of public health services by providing timely and effective treatment of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea to populations with limited access to facility-based health care providers, and especially to children under 5. In June 2012, WHO and UNICEF issued a joint statement to support iCCM to improve access to essential treatment services for children. The availability of high-quality rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs) for malaria has made testing for malaria at the community level possible. The iCCM approach incorporates WHO’s recommendation that all suspected malaria cases undergo diagnostic testing prior to treatment.
Deploying community health workers can increase coverage and access to treatment. Community health workers are an effective option for investments as part of a comprehensive primary health care system. However, community case management should not be viewed as an inexpensive or low-cost measure. Effective implementation requires policy support, training, supervision, performance maintenance and regular supplies. In addition, community health workers are increasingly responsible for many health and development tasks.
As part of International Medical Corps iCCM approach, 50 front-line CHW will be identified through elections, trained, supplied and supervised to diagnose and treat children under 5 years of age for malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea, using artemisinin-based combination therapies, oral antibiotics, oral rehydration salts and zinc. All patients will be screened for the 3 diseases and treatment will be administered based on the results of the examination and diagnostic testing that includes malaria RDTs, disease history and respiratory rate. Experience suggests that the iCCM strategy can be effective in achieving high treatment coverage and delivering high-quality care particularly for sick children in the community.
C) Proposed Activities
IMC proposes to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality in the conflict affected populations, especially among children in hard to reach areas in the Lake region including Islands that are difficult to reach, by provision of an integrated community case management of common illnesses. This will be achieved by strengthening the capacities of 50 CHWs in 2 health catchment areas (Kangalom and Fitine) in the Bol health district. This approach will be implemented through the following activities:
1) Startup workshop to engage local MoH authorities
iCCM should be considered as an outreach activity of the local level MoH authorities. To engage the MoH authorities to be more present at community level, a startup workshop will be organized bringing together health authorities and representatives of the community to present the project.
2) Training and capacity building of CHW on the iCCM Approach:
Through this activity, IMC will work together with the local MoH authorities to identify 50 CHWs (25 in the Kangalom health area and 25 in the Fitine health area).). The project will target a network of already existing CHW but in the case where CHW are not in the communities, they will be selected through a community vote. The project will also aim for a 50% men and 50% women. The CHWs will be trained by IMC and the MoH authorities on the symptoms of common diseases including Malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea. The CHW will be trained on the efficient management of drugs and drug stock. They will also receive trainings on the diagnosis of Malaria using RDTs. The CHWs will also be trained on the referral of complicated cases they identify in the communities to the nearest health center for appropriate services.
3) Supervision of CHWs by the health area head nurse:
To ensure that the CHWs operate within norms and follow the treatment standards they will be supervised by the health area head nurse of each health area who will go through their records and provide them with supportive supervision. In doing so they will ensure treatment guidelines are respected. IMC will train the health area head nurses on supportive supervision of the CHWs.
4) Provision of an uninterrupted supply of medicines:
IMC will ensure that there is an uninterrupted supply of drugs to the CHWs. The essential drugs for the management of these 3 diseases will be procured and positioned in the health center covering the health catchment area. The CWHs will be provided with kits to equip them with the necessary drugs and diagnostic materials enabling them to carry out their duties.
5) Production of IEC materials for community sensitization:
IMC will make copies of existing IEC materials including flip books, posters, T-Shirts, to be used by CHWs during household visits. Community members will be educated on the role of the CHWs, what services they provide, and on how to hold them accountable.
6) Incentives for CHW:
To motivate CHWs in their duties, the project will provide incentives for them, including communication credit, transportation, umbrellas, rain coats and rain booths and food during household visits.
7) Documentation and publication of best practices and lessons learned
International Medical Corps will document and share best practices and lessons learned from the intervention. This will be done through articles and blogs and will be published on websites and journals for public access

In it we describe a pilot on a strategy to improve the health of the population at community level, especially young children under five years of age, in communities undergoing the repercussions stemming from the humanitarian crisis in the Lake Chad area. We feel this Concept Note is worthy of your financial consideration. Although the level of conflict precipitated by Boko Haram in the Lake Chad Basin has decreased over the past year, the humanitarian crisis persists in the region. The health needs of internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees, refugees and host communities continue to surpass the capacity of the existing health system. While some coordinated response has been effective in responding to these needs, there remain neglected areas in the region including the hard to reach islands where populations feel abandoned by the Government and are not reached by humanitarian partners. International Medical Corps (IMC) through this intervention proposes to pilot an integrated community case management (iCCM) for common illnesses including malaria, acute respiratory infections and diarrhea that affect a greater proportion of children 0-59 Months. The intervention will be implemented in two health catchment areas in the Bol health districts (Kangalom and Fitine) where most of the isolated islands are located. The iCCM intervention will be implemented through the following activities: identification and training of 50 Community Health Workers (CHWs), provision of drugs and kits for CHWs to carry out their activities at community level and supportive supervisions of CHWs by head nurses of each health area. This pilot intervention will cost $500,000 and will be implemented in five months starting in May 2018 and directly reaching 4,500 children and about 25,000 larger population will receive targeted health messages from the same trained CHWs about prevention of these three diseases, and how to care for children who suffer from them (including refugees, IDP and returnee populations).
