Project Summary

The proposed project is designed to respond to the most essential need for child
protection responses children with and without disabilities. Based Protection cluster HNO and MSNA, and Atfaluna needs
assessment for 2022 it becomes clear that children with and without disabilities are of need child protection response. They need to enjoy a safe environment within their homes, schools, and the whole surroundings. The project tackles the problems faced by the children that
threatens their protection and mental-wellbeing. Children protection and psychosocial
support is of significance to mitigate the effects of child violations in family surrounding
and schools. The project will provide equal access to children with and without disabilities
through distributed service points across the Gaza Strip. Medical care will be provided to
children whose families cannot afford the cost of treatment. Children who were exposed
to severe harmful act will receive legal counselling and representation. Children will learn
to have a voice and express and report child protection issue that they encounter. Parents
will be targeted to help them cope with life burdens and turn improve their parenting skills
which will create a safe environment for children at their homes. Service providers,
teachers, and community members will be sensitized on child protection issues such as
child labor, schools’ dropout, and abuse to that they integrate the child protection
mechanisms to mitigate the risks that children face in the community.
The project will be implemented in partnership with four NGOs across the
Gaza Strip.
The 2022 August sclation has caused children with and without disabilities to suffer from severe post-traumatic disorders which makes it is important to have structured psycho-socials support program for children with and without disabilities.

1 - Needs

Exposure to violence in the short and long term negatively impacts on the mental, emotional, behavioral, social and physical well-being of children and unless they receive counselling these effects can become worse over time. During 2021, child protection needs in the oPt have been compounded by the continuing COVID-19 pandemic, as well as an increase in conflict-related violence in the West Bank including East Jerusalem and the May 2021 and August 2022 escalation in Gaza. The Child Protection HNOs states that there are considerable needs for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) interventions including specialized case management, and approaches targeting adolescents and caregivers, particularly males. The Protection Analysis Update (PAU) and MSNA estimates that there are 499,187 children (253,078 boys and 246,109 girls) among them 148,242 children with disabilities are in need to protection and humanitarian assistance. The MSNA identifies the following protection risks: % of households by most frequently reported main safety and security concerns for children, (Verbal harassment female: 14.2% males 8.5%), Sexual harassment/violence among 6.5% of females, bulling 4.7% amon females and 6.7 % among boys). While the percentage among children with disabilities is as following: % of households by most frequently reported main safety and security concerns for children with disabilities (including both girls and boys): Bullying 28.9% Verbal harassment 9.0% Wildlife, stray animals 6.1% Unsafe transportation infrastructure 4.9% Physical harassment/violence 4.5%. In August 2022, ASDC in conducted a needs assessment and identified the following among females and males: 98.9% of the mothers reported that their children had heavy feelings of fears due the sound of the explosion, out of then 87.1% of the children reported being anxious even after the end the aggression, also 82.8% of the adult males of the family reported that they were anxious due the escalation and the sound of explosions. And 59.1 of them still being anxious even after the end the assault. While 91.4 of the adult females in the family reported that they were anxious. And 77.4 of them still being anxious even after the end the aggression. The psychosocial trauma among children as their mothers expressed that their children experience nightmares, sleeping disorders, behavioral changes, lack of concentration, all in severe posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) symptoms among mostly children and women. 77.4 % reported changes in the children behavior due to the escalation out of them. 52.7% become feeling terrified when hearing loud sounds such as doors closures, 40.9% become aggressive, 17.2% bed wetting, 16.1% crying without any reason, 34.4% lack of concentration. 23.7 of the parents reported that they deal with their children negatively/aggressive way during the escalation, as they are themselves distressed. 55.9% of the respondents reported that the family violence increased against women and girls during the escalation. 40.9% of the assessment respondent reported that the family violence increases against the children with disabilities during the escalation. 44.1% of the respondent reported that the social media (Facebook, WhatsApp, and twitter) is the most popular for disseminating awareness and the health messages, while 45.1% of the respondent prefer to use direct phone call with the PSS specialists, 10.8% of the respondent use the radio spots and T.V cannels.Based on the findings of PAU, MSNA, and ASDC’s needs assessment the psychosocial support interventions are of high priority to be provided to children boys and girls with and without disabilities, the MHPSS interventions are recommended to be provided to the families in order to improve their coping and resilience mechanisms.

2 - Activities

Result1.) Children with and without disabilities at risk of violations and psychosocial distress resilience is enhanced Activities: • Establishment of an inclusive online psychosocial support activities and materials for children with and without disabilities the materials shall be developed in inclusive and accessible formats that meet the different needs of disabilities such as visual, hearing, and learning difficulties. The materials will introduce to the children and their families PSEA guidelines and complaints mechanisms in an inclusive manners. • Structured psychosocial support group and individual activities to children with and without disabilities 1000 (500 girls, 500 boys) who are at-risk and affected by violations. (A total of 250 children will be targeted per each center, pre and post evaluations will be conducted on children to determine their needs and impact of the intervention, the group PSS will evolve 12 session about, 8-12 children per each group). • Provision of child protection case management services to 320 children with and without disabilities (100 girls, 100 boys) who were exposed to different types of abuse • A total 30 interactive sensitization sessions for 2000 (1000 boys, 1000 girls) children with and without disabilities on how to be self-protective • A total of 30 life skills sessions for about 1000 children with and without disabilities (500 boys, 500 girls).All interventions will be provided in inclusive and accessible formats, as ASDC has adapted the MHPSS guidelines to respond to the different needs of children with and without disabilities. • Provision of emergency medical services to the most needed children • Use child protection referral pathways and Legal Task Force to ensure children with and without disabilities, boys and girls, have timely and confidential access to protection services • COVID19 precautionary measures awareness raising messages directed to children and their families in inclusive and accessible formats • A total of 30 parenting skills awareness workshops for about 1,000 mothers and fathers • Provision of PSS kits to children and their families Result2.) Targeted community will acquire increased understanding and sensitization of child protection and issues of abuse • Documentation and monitoring of child violations through quarterly fact sheets • A total of 20 awareness workshops to 400 service providers (100 females, 100 males) on inclusive approach in serving children with disabilities • A total of 10 auditory sessions with 50 social actors, Mokhtars, parents, media, CSOs, and DPOs with participation of Children’s Councils • A total of 30 community outreach workshops on child protection issues for 1,500 community members • Printing of awareness materials on child protection issues • Training of 100 females and males pss workers, case managers, social workers on integarting PSEA in their pss work • Sensitization sessions for 3,000 parents, females and males on PSEA and safeguarding of children with and without disabilities. • Introduce PSEA complaints pathway and the hotline to children and parents through delivery of the interventions and producing of materials that are accessible to persons with disabilities. ASDC facilities and partners facilities are adapted to receive persons with disabilities. The adaptation works for the partners were carried through ASDC funded programs. The staff are trained on applying inclusive strategies in service delivery. Any publications or social media materials will be published in inclusive formats that include sign language interpretation, braille, and easy to read texts.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Atfaluna will ensure that the affected children, families, and community members have access to timely information to on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected target group over information provision. Atfaluna will actively seek the views of affected target group to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. Participation: Atfaluna will enable affected target group to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence, this will be conducted through the following 1. Design, monitor and evaluate the goals and objectives of project with the involvement of affected target group, feeding learning back into the organization on an ongoing basis and reporting on the results of the process 2. Participation of the target group boys, girls, men, and women in the evaluation of the project activities through evaluation forms and focus group discussions.
