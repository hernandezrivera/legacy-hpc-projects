Project Summary

Through this project IYD planned to focus on the camp management and coordination in three camps with the following activities:
• Establishment of a reliable camp management system, set up a management office and provide supportive tools.
• Improve the management quality in and accountability of camp managers in IDP sites.
• Establishment of IDPs’ relocation and registration mechanism and information management system with disseminate operational information on movements of IDPs on a timely basis.
• Monitor the provision of streamlined humanitarian life-saving, multi-sectoral assistance in IDP sites through create coordination mechanism for providing inclusive services (Food, health & nutrition, protection and education) in cooperation with relevant clusters and NGOs.
• Ensure taking COVID19 prevention measures with regards to sterilization, provision of PPE, appropriate to promote handwashing and hygiene,social distancing in general and during distributions, health screening of IDPs and sensitizing them to follow precautionary procedures
• Enhance community participation and form committees, strengthen the capacity of staff and committees, and build accountability mechanism towards IDPs.
• Strengthen household and communal coping mechanisms in the IDPs sites and develop the exit strategies.

4 - Needs

Ten years into the Syria crisis, humanitarian needs are deepening. Ongoing insecurity and the compounded impact of displacement, combined with worsening socio-economic conditions characterized by sharp currency depreciation, record-level price increases and cuts in subsidized goods have prompted an almost 20 per cent increase in the number of people in need compared to early 2020 - with a disproportionate impact on women and children. As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, including 6 million in acute need, due to a convergence of factors arising from a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.IYD field team identified the needs in 3 camps which include new IDPs and those camps established by IYD through site preparation, roads graveling, tents installing, and insulation. The total of IDPs number of beneficiaries is 22,870 those IDPs located in three camps between Idelb and Aleppo governorates, those camps shows that lack of the following points: camp management, maintenance to basic infrastructure, fire mitigation and response mechanism. Through this project, IYD will establish an accountable governance system by setting up a management office and provide supportive tools for maintenance and smooth provision of services to cover all CCCM needs.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

the border between Turkey and Syria closes sometimes which might affect the delivery of some required items into the proposed locations especially the capital equipment, IYD has increased local procurement of project supplies inside Syria, recruitment of staff from inside Syria further access controls on sending supplies to the project area would make it extremely difficult to implement the project activities. Therefore, this proposal presumes some kind of continued access to the project area or the availability of supplies and equipment in the local market. Sudden clashes or air attacks which probably happen during the project period. To mitigate these risks, IYD staff have received mine awareness training which will be repeated on a regular basis during the project period. IYD cross-border security group provides a forum for IYD staff at different levels in the organization structure to analyze security risks and to update the security management plan. Security groups are used for tracking of staff movements within Syria and for the dissemination of information about security incidents to all staff. This proposal presumes that the local authorities in the targeted area will continue to allow NGOs to implement projects according to humanitarian principles without undue interference in the selection of beneficiaries and staff. IYD will not pay fees or taxes to armed parties to the conflict inside Syria or to ISIL or other sanctioned groups as per the Joint Operating Principles. Beneficiaries are made aware that goods and services under this program are coming from IYD rather than from the local authorities. IYD is managing risk and potential fraud by ensuring compliance with and adherence to IYD’s approved and documented
