Project Summary

GBV incidence continues to worsen in due to multiple displacements, forced evictions, loss of livelihoods, disruption of social and community protection systems due to floods, droughts, sacking of communities by Alshabab with significant impact on the lives and well-being of women and adolescent girls. GBV risks and exposure includes distance to water points, Schools, markets and GBV service sites. Lack of lockable latrines, poor lighting in IDPs camps and overcrowded shelters/lack of privacy. Media and programme reports of gang rapes, homicide, forced abortion of young pregnant adolescents, attempted suicide of rape survivors and increasing levels of FGM are common place. The recent displacement in Lasánod aggravated the situation for women and girls. More women and girls including elderly have been left without food and shelter. The displacement is coming at a time when Somali authorities are facing difficulties in containing the spread of COVID19 virus.
Women and girls living in IDP Camps are more prone to sexual violence and abuse than those living in host communities with a better housing infrastructure and service sites and markets. The JMCNA 2021 informs that 13 percent of households were concerned that girls in host communities and 17 per cent in IDP camps were exposed to sexual violence and harassment, while 16 per cent (host community) and 18 per cent (IDP) households were concerned that girls were to be forcibly married. In addition, it was indicated that 29 percent of respondents lacked proper bathing facilities, 26 per cent poor lighting, and 14 per cent privacy in shelter; 31 per cent lacked lockable latrines; 93 per cent use common latrines that are far or not in proximity. Similarly, the GBVIMS report from January – June 2021 indicates 62 per cent of the reported GBV incidents were physical assault, 11 per cent rape, 10 per cent sexual assault, 8 per cent denial of resources, while 6 per cent psychological/emotional abuse, and the remaining 4 per cent percent forced and early marriage.
Safety concerns of adolescent for adolescent boys and men continue to worsen with severe food insecurity and prevailing armed and communal conflicts. They are subject to kidnap, forceful recruitment by armed groups. Women and adolescent girls are relying on direct cash and voucher assistance to meet basic survival and material needs to improve their resilience to mitigate/escape GBV. Given the widespread food insecurity in Somalia, there may be a possibility of vulnerable women and adolescent girls exchanging sex for food. Reliance on cash support is providing a buffer for poor families but may also contribute to rising intimate partner violence with divergent priorities for families on where to invest the cash support. Increasing population in need for GBV services, limited services provision to cover the landscape of needs is a major concern for the GBV humanitarian response in Somalia.
UNFPA interventions will target the displaced and non-displaced communities including all age groups, women and girls living with disabilities, widowed and divorced women, married adolescents and women and adolescent girls from minority clans. Some of the key interventions will include: 1) Support access to comprehensive multi sectoral GBV services (medical, legal, psychosocial) for 6000 women and girls. 2) Strengthen the capacity/and mobilize 200 PSS counsellors; 150 health workers to provide timely, quality, and confidential PSS and rape treatment. 3) Support the establishment 6 GBV one-stop centers in South West State, Banadir and Lasanod. 4) Provide 2,000 dignity kits and 36 post rape kits dignity protection for women and girls and treatment of rape. 5) Support 600 women and girls’ survivors of GBV with cash voucher assistance and grants for livelihood.

1. - Projects activities

The major objective of this project is to broaden access for services for GBV survivors through direct cash and voucher assistance to meet immediate basic needs of vulnerable women and adolescent girls for GBV mitigation; strengthening the capacity of service providers to reach population in need of women and adolescent girls in remote locations; procurement and distribution of Rape kits for CMR; provision of dignity kits and menstrual hygiene kits for women and girls; and the establishment and support of service sites, Specific activities include - i. Support for the establishment and operations of 6 GBV one stop centers and 4women and girl’s safe spaces to provide rape services, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and counselling, referrals for legal aid/support,ii. Procure and distribute 36 post rape treatment kits for treating GBV survivors and conduct orientations/training for 80 CMR actors to treat and manage rape and other survivors of GBV. iii. Procure and distribute 2000 dignity kits and 2000 menstrual hygiene kits. iv. Undertake training workshops for 200 PSS counsellors to deliver cultural appropriate, age and gender responsive PSS service. v. Establish and support the operations of 6 GBV one stop centers and 4 women and girls safe spaces by the end of 2022.vi. Support the provision of livelihoods skills trainings and marketable livelihoods options for 300 vulnerable women and girls including GBV survivorsvii. Support the provision of direct cash and voucher assistance to 600 vulnerable women and girls including GBV survivors.viii. Mobilize communities to disseminate information on GBV prevention, mitigation and response through the initiatives focused on the dissemination of existing integrated referral pathways.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher assistance is important to restoring and sustaining the dignity, safety and security of women and girls affected by the humanitarian crises. Loss of livelihoods/possibility of creating friction (IPV); traveling long distances to seek funds to procure basic needs, increase of early and forced marriage and FGM because of water and food insecurity exposes women to sexual violence, harassment and abuse. Cash in hands of vulnerable women and girls will provide them capacity to assess basic goods and service and reduce the reliance on negative coping mechanism. It will enable women and girls control to a large extent the level to which they expose themselves to GBV, harassment and abuse.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Implementation of activities proposed by the project will work in synergy with initiatives that target the promotion of safety and security of women and adolescent girls of other protection actors and complement the efforts of key cluster actors such health, Protection, WASH and Food to minimize risks of GBV in their respective Cluster activities and operations. Cash and voucher assistance will complement efforts of food security cluster and cash working group to ensure that a greater number of women and adolescent girls can access items for meet basic material and survival needs.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

UNFPA has a PSEA policy in place for reporting and handling Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA)-related complaints and all its national and international staff have completed the mandatory SEA training. UNFPA has PSEA focal points at country office and sub-offices which regularly conducts training and awareness sessions with implementing partners. As part of project agreement, UNFPA implementing partners sign a commitment to ensure that no SEA related offences will be committed by any of their staff, and in the event that it occurs, appropriate actions will be taken. The project will endeavor to ensure maximum consultation with marginalized communities in all phases of the program with a particular focus on including vulnerable women and girls from minority clans and those living with disabilities. The project will ensure inclusion of marginalized individuals in community-based structures to ensure their priorities are tailored in to the program. UNFPA partners will closely work with community committees including GBV survivor advocates, youth organizations, organizations of persons living with disabilities to select beneficiaries for the project. Also UNFPA and its implementing partners will ensure consistent sex disaggregated programming to ensure men, women, girls, and boys are targeted adequately.
