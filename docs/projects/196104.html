Project Summary

Within the framework of the Education sector JRP for 2023, UNFPA will continue provision of Life Skills Education (LSE) programme, including Comprehensive Sexual Education (CSE), among Rohingya and host community adolescents and youth to ensure that they have knowledge and power to make informed choices about their bodies and lives in an environment where harmful social and gender norms such as gender-based violence (GBV) and early and forced child marriage, unwanted pregnancy, uncertainty about future still pose serious risks to their health and well-being. Guided by the International Technical Guidance on CSE, the project aims at delivering scientifically accurate, incremental, age and developmentally appropriate, curriculum-based CSE programmes as part of the Myanmar Curriculum (MC).
The project is built on the “Gender Equality Movement in Schools (GEMS)” curriculum, which has been one of the successfully implemented flagship programmes of the Ministry of Education and UNFPA in Bangladesh. The curriculum is contextualized into Burmese and covers all eight key concepts of the International Technical Guidance on Sexuality Education focusing first on strengthening and motivating learners to explore values, attitudes, and norms concerning sexuality, before going on to address specific facts, knowledge, and skills. Edutainment technology based approaches such as games, short videos and cartoons on SRHR and gender equality will complement classroom sessions. This project will reach 10,000 Rohigya students (50% male and 50% female) under 18 enrolled in the MC in 250 community learning centers, will distribute MHM kits among the female students, and will build the capacity of the host and rohingya community teachers to deliver the GEMS sessions in learning centers.

To reach adolescents and youth, and to reinforce internalization of knowledge and positive gender norms among adolescents, UNFPA will establish 3 more adolescent and youth centers in three different camps and will continue to provide its two flagship interventions that target adolescent boys and girls from Rohingya and host communities in community and multipurpose spaces, including 7 established adolescent and youth centers.
Girl targeted education programme - Girls shine- aims at empowering girls, 7,500 in the Rohingya camps and 2,500 in the host community, to acquire skills and knowledge to identify different types of GBV and seek support services if they experience or are at risk of SGBV. Moreover, the Girls Shine programme includes sessions on bodily autonomy, gender equality, MHPSS, SRHR, menstrual health, and promotes positive social and emotional skills, awareness of existing GBV response and case management services. Through this particular intervention as well MHM kits will be distributed among the female students.
The Champions of Change education programme- Boy specific intervention- aims at creating safe space for adolescent boys and young men, 7,500 in the Rohingya camps and 2,500 in the host community, to reflect on what it means to be a man and encourages them to redefine masculinity and to be more supportive, inclusive, compassionate members of the community.
Capacity building and awarenss are also important part of this project in which sessions for male and female caregivers, community leaders will be provided to challenge existing harmful gender norms and practices such as SGBV and early and forced child marriage. As such, besides Parents/Caregivers meetings, UNFPA will continue implementing structured, community-level sessions for caregivers, community leaders and community members and volunteers to complement awareness building activities both in the camps and immediate host communities.
Coordination and advocacy are important elements of this project by which UNFPA aims at building the capacity of partners of the Youth Working Group to advocate for youth sensitive data and programmes across the Rohingya response.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Adolescents, who represent approximately 24% [1][2] of the Rohingya population (Population data, continue to face numerous challenges due to disrupted family, community and social structures, limited access to social protection, education and health services. Due to the protracted nature of the crisis, adolescents and youth are often pressured to take major responsibilities and roles within households without any support, which makes them more vulnerable. Published reports reveals refugee adolescents and young people remain mostly on the margins of humanitarian programming in the camps and are largely excluded from current intervention which in turn contributes to increased rate of early and forced child marriage, participation in informal labor, and negative coping practices such as engagement in drug peddling and illegal activities [3]. Evidence suggests that forcibly displaced adolescent girls experience a disproportionate burden of poor sexual and reproductive health, including high rates of SGBV, child and forced marriage, maternal mortality, unintended pregnancy and unsafe abortion [4]. Moreover, substantial physical, emotional, cognitive and social growth and developmental changes that all adolescents experience has been particularly challenging for Rohingyas, especially those who are intersex and question their gender identity or expression [5]. As bodily changes caused by puberty are considered taboo topics in the Rohingya community, caregivers are hesitant to discuss these issues with their children, which results in young people not knowing how to cope with the changes they are going through [6]. Hence, many Rohingya adolescents approach adulthood with conflicting, negative, and confusing understanding about life skills, gender norms, sexuality, which cause more harm than help. In the case of adolescent girls, a long-standing practice of restricted mobility for girls and women outside of the home has further increased their vulnerability. Compared to married adolescents, unmarried adolescents face even greater barriers in accessing information and services including family planning services [6]. Moreover, the situation is worse for adolescents facing any kind of disabilities, although they comprise 4% of the total adolescent population [7][8][9]. A significant body of evidence shows that life skills education enables children and young people to develop accurate and age-appropriate knowledge, skills, positive values, including respect for human rights, gender equality, and diversity, and attitudes that contribute to safe, healthy, positive relationships [10]. Government permission that allows Rohingya girls and boys to enroll into formal schooling opens the door for humanitarian actors to educate adolescents and youth about CSE, STI, HIV transmission, and family planning needs. Comprehensive sexuality education proposed by UNFPA is a curriculum-based process of teaching and learning about the emotional, physical and social aspects of sexuality. The proposed project will equip adolescents and young people with knowledge, attitudes and values that will empower them to realize their well-being and dignity, develop respectful social and sexual relationships, and consider how their choices affect their own well-being and that of others, and lastly, ensure the protection of their rights throughout their lives. The curriculum also aims to dismantle taboos around GBV and will focus on building a gender equal society. 2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As the recognized partner of the Cox’s Bazar Rohingya response, and in line with rationalization exercise and its organizational mandate, UNFPA has been coordinating with all relevant appealing partners that are under protection (GBVSS, and CPSS), Education, Livelihood and Skills Development sectors, to avoid overlapping of services and ensure that there are no current actors providing similar service within the targeted area. Prior to initiating the project, UNFPA has conducted the need assessment at the community level, and engaged in a number of discussions and coordination mechanisms with appealing and non JRP partners, and working groups such as SRHR working group, ASHR task team, and Youth working group. UNFPA has been one of the active partners in the inter-sectoral coordination mechanism since the beginning of the influx and always maintained respectful partnership and networking with relevant stakeholders and government officials. Moreover, UNFPA has been prioritizing multi-year programming and funding when advocating for and fundraising resources to ensure continuity of interventions and bringing out the optimal positive impact of the programme. Simultaneously, UNFPA’s implementing partners are also equipped with the skillsets, and institutional capacity to operate on the ground. Given its mandate and policies on transparency and accountability to affected populations and donor communities, UNFPA has been active in publishing Sit Reps, Fact Sheets, and Appeal sheets which included operational elements of the programming such as information about implementing partners, funding needs, mobilized resources and programming targets. UNFPA ensures that information is accessible to all including ISCG. Finally, all UNFPA IPs are part of the current JRP process and meet the minimum PSEA related requirements while being active members in the Cox’s Bazar PSEA network, all of whom have appointed focal points and Child Safeguarding Focal Points.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Safety and dignity of beneficiaries are prioritized in all stages of implementation, and fully integrates the minimum standards for child protection. This includes safety of learning spaces and presence of referral links, where needed, to specialized protection services. The learning centers will protect children from the physical dangers around them-including abuse, exploitation and provide hygiene supplies and nutrition. The centers will offer psychosocial support giving children stability and structure to cope with the trauma they experience every day. A multidimensional vulnerability index will be conducted, including vulnerabilities that marginalized groups such as female headed families, orphan adolescents, and PWD face, and reflect findings in the project implementation. In addition, engagement of affected populations will be ensured in every stage of the programme implementation alongside the capacity development of the community.

3.2 - Gender

The featured strategies of this project promote programming that is gender-responsive in terms of participation, coordination and assessment and advances gender equality in access, teaching practice, curricula and learning. It addresses gender-based barriers, respects gender differences, promotes community structures, systems that are sensitive to gender. The project approach and curricula respects and acknowledges differences based on gender and intersecting variables such as age, disability, and sexuality status that are all part of a learner’s identity. The project supports and safeguards SRHR for learners. Moreover, it ensures integration of gender mainstreaming into comprehensive sexuality education in the following ways: a) Ensures that the specific needs of girls and boys are identified; b) Educates boys and girls of their rights, entitlements and engages them in programme implementation; and c) Monitors and evaluates impact of programmes and strategies.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project will collaborate with children and other stakeholders to design, implement and monitor joint child-friendly, accessible and confidential safeguarding feedback and reporting mechanisms. This will ensure that all actors have signed and been trained on safeguarding procedures and policies that prohibit corporal (physical) punishment and other degrading forms of punishment. The teachers training curricula will support more protective learning environments by including training on Psychological first aid, Social and emotional learning (SEL), Gender- and disability-sensitive approaches, Positive discipline, Participatory methods, and Child protection principles and concerns. The curricula will ensure that content is age and developmentally appropriate. The project monitoring and evaluation indicators will be disaggregated by sex, age, disability and other relevant diversity factors.

3.4 - Diversity

The project embodies non-discrimination and inclusion principles including respect for the inherent dignity, diversity and acceptance of all children. The project will hire community teachers who are refugees and prioritize the recruitment of staff with appropriate language, gender, age and cultural competencies, including staff with disabilities, to work with Rohingya populations. Where appropriate, the project will provide training to staff and recruit cultural mediators to build the skills needed for working with diverse populations. All indicators of the project will be disaggregated by sex, age, disability and other relevant diversity factors. The project will serve ethnic minorities and people with disabilities through ensuring their engagement, participation and ownership in every aspect of project design, implementation, and monitoring. Disability inclusion specialists will be consulted in all stages of the project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will ensure beneficiaries are meaningfully and continuously involved in the project to generate the best possible outcomes. In line with the AAP framework, UNFPA, during the intervention will ensure 100% involvement of the targeted population through meaningful engagement in decision making and feedback mechanism. UNFPA, in its existing intervention, continuously follows the transparent and accountable approach by doing need assessment before designing interventions and incorporating the feedback from the beneficiaries and stakeholders, which will also be followed for the proposed intervention. Continuous focus group discussion, pre and post programme survey will be conducted with enrolled beneficiaries to assess quality and impact of the programme. Capacity building of the staff and awareness rising for communities about feedback mechanisms will be conducted and complaint box mechanisms will also be a part of the programme which will ensure confidentiality.
