Project Summary

Nutrition situation remained at a grips of serious humanitarian interventions throughout 2020 attributed by combination of factors like poor harvest,conflicts,displacement and lack of basic needs. Exceptionally heavy flooding experienced from second half of 2019, throughout 2020 & impacts of global crisis on COVID-19, further compounded the public health burden & impacted the nutrition status with more children acutely malnourished than the previous 3 years due to water borne diseases & food insecurity attributed to distraction of crops and loss of livelihood, leading to families adopting to negative practices as the coping mechanisms are weakened by the cumulative shocks.
Malnutrition situation in Mayendit, Panyijar, Cueibet and Manyo counties targeted by UNIDOR in 2021 continue to be a major concern showing localized pockets of humanitarian needs attributed to sporadic armed fighting/cattle raiding, severe food insecurity, poor access to health nutrition services, extremely poor diets, poor sanitation, hygiene and COVID- 19 effects. November 2020 – March 2021 IPC AMN analysis cites Upper Nile, Unity & Lakes state with counties at AMN 4 at 31% ,17% &3% respectively attributed to high prevalence of disease (36%) poor quality &diversity of food, poor access to health, nutrition services & Covid-19 disruptions.
October 2020 – July 2021 IPC analysis projects that IPC 4 food insecurity is expected in Unity, lakes and upper Nile states. October – November 2020 IPC analysis cites panyijar, Mayendit Cueibet and Manyo among the 34 counties classified at Emergency IPC 4 acute food insecurity
Due to lack of 2020 surveillance data constrained by COVID -19 restrictions, the situation might have worsen than what is projected by the previous data in Greater Upper Nile &Lakes state where communal fighting and flooding limiting humanitarian access.
UNIDOR seeks continue providing timely, qualitative and quantitative lifesaving services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women & people with disabilities through provision of integrated nutrition services reaching at least 80% of SAM, 75% MAM and 60% PLW in line with the set cluster objectives in the counties of Mayendit and Panyijar, UNITY, Cuiebet Lakes and Manyo in Upper Nile respectively, targeting total of 39,350 beneficiaries. Through management and treatment of 5285 boys and girls with SAM (Mayendit 1170, Manyo 100,Cuibet 1615,Panyijar 1400) 9601 boys and girls with MAM (Mayendit 3017,Manyo 151,Cuibet 3097,Panyijar 3336), PLW (Mayendit 2133,Manyo 112,Cuibet 2887,Panyijar 4208) through OPT/TSFP static sites and outreaches per the cluster objective two. In line with the cluster objective one, 15104 will be reached with MIYCN counselling integrated with COVD-19 messaging, 17,886 and 16886 boys & girls will receive Vitamin A and Deworming respectively in prevention of malnutrition in the four counties with an estimated budget of $ 1,882,720
UNIDOR will adopt combination of strategies for this nutrition response such as establishment of static services and outreaches in Manyo and Cuebet counties and continued treatment through the static OTP/TSFP sites in Mayendit and Panyijar counties, capacity building, and integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening, EPI &GMP distribution of LLITN), WASH (provision of safe water, soap, education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL on kitchen gardening & cooking demonstration, Education though provision of age appropriate toys and child friendly spaces. Protection through referral pathways and GBV safety audits and feedback mechanism through establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complaint desks, and community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback i line cluster objective 4. There will be strong component of surveillance through continuous program monitoring that will include Mass MUAC screening, SMART surveys and supporting in FNSMS rounds in line with cluster objective 3

2 - Project activities

In order to ensure that the intended purpose of this project is effectively archived in providing lifesaving project intervention, the project intend to address the following needs; Conducting community nutritional assessments for acute malnutrition through community screening and referral to the respective programs Treatment of SAM U5 without medical complications in OTP. • Treatment of SAM with medical complications in SC • Treatment of MAM U5 children in the TSFP sites • Treatment of MAM pregnant and lactating women in TSFP sites • Maternal, Infant and Young Feeding practices to mothers and caregivers.•Malaria testing and treatment for all SAM children U5 at OTP and MAM U5 symptomatic at the TSFP sites. MIYCN/GMP/ECD and CMAM training to nutrition and health workers • Training of nutrition workers on GBV referral pathways • World breastfeeding week celebrations • Conducting cooking demonstrations.• Vitamin A supplementation to children 6-59 months boys and girls during NIDs • Deworming to children 12-59 months boys and girls during NIDs • Rehabilitation of 11 nutrition sites and construction of 4 nutrition sites destroyed by flooding •Provision of preventive packages ( Soaps, water purifiers & treated mosquito nets) • Integration of ECD in the OTP/TSFP sites for stimulation by ensuring availability of Child friendly spaces with age appropriate toys• Implementation of minmum package of intergration on Nutrition,WASH,FSL,Education, health and CP • Community awareness on Growth monitoring and promotion with the GMP charts. MIYCN counselling and behaviors change messaging to mothers/caregivers of children 0-59 months and pregnant women through mother support groups, individual and group counselling at the community and facility • Developing kitchen demonstration gardens at the nutrition sites • Community mobilization and sensitization with integrated health,hygiene and COVID -19 messaging • Provision of PPEs to nutrition front-line workers • Conduct Mass MUAC ,SMART surveys and screening and supporting in FSNMS

3 - Project indicators

UNIDOR is committed to regular monitoring and evaluation (M&E) using indicators and targets as priority one to effectively track the progress of project to ensure implementation is guided by a results orientation and continuously informed by lessons learnt and beneficiary feedback. UNIDOR will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan and indicator tracking table to measure progress towards the expected results. The Indicators on the tracking table will include those in the log frame corresponding to on-going and planned activities. The Monitoring tools (indicator tracking template) will include the Departmental Questionnaires, online reporting tool, nutrition cluster NIS tool and DHIS 2, Programme Tally sheets, and Pictorial evidence especially during health facilities visits and trainings , will also use FGDs with the Nutrition/Health Workers and the local Authority to collect views on how the project is impacting on their lives. Data will be collected by the relevant Programme Managers and weekly/monthly/quarterly reports will be produced for internal monitoring. UNIDOR will submit quantitative data, disaggregated by sex, age and IDP/host when relevant both progress and final programmatic and financial reports online in line with donor reporting templates and Nutrition Cluster donor requirements within the stipulated period as will be evidenced in the contractual documents . A joint quarterly supervision visits to all health and nutrition facilities will be conducted together with our CHD counterparts using QoC/QSC tools in order to see the HFs compliance as per South Sudan BPHNS.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNIDOR being a zero tolerance organization on SEA and with it staff well trained and signed SEA policies, the project commits to ensure that robust awareness is created to the beneficiaries and the community at large on PSEA through routine mobilization and sensitization session. Further, all the staff working with this project will sign code of conduct with the PSEA safeguarding principles

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through the mother support groups, father groups and the focused group discussions, all members including persons with disability will be inclusive. Mothers and fathers that are not literate can air there grievances through the lead mother/father of each group. UNIDOR will also ensure the project engages literate employees from the community that they can address there feedback with the local languages. UNIDOR will also use Community meetings with other stake holders as a means of getting feedback from the project target community

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

From the inception of the project through the implementation cycle the community will be consulted and involved in design including the duration of the projects and donors funding the project, role s and duties of the community and the organization. Decisions and opinions will be sought through avenues such community sensitization and mobilizations, during focused group discussions and Mother support groups through the focal persons. Project team will conduct monthly stakeholder meetings to discuss or review the project . Lastly, UNIDOR will also place suggestion boxes in the respective nutrition sites for access to all beneficiaries and members of the community

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Upon receipt of any feedback from the beneficiaries , the project team will always evaluate the feedback for consideration and adaptation where possible and give back feedback to the community. In a scenario where any feedback does not give any tangible changes on the project design , then UNIDOR will have internal deliberations and take a step further to to consult with the donor on the actions of implementation.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

UNIDOR staff will sign a code of conduct with commitment that they will never use their positions to take advantage of the beneficiaries served by the project. There will be continued awareness to the beneficiaries that services are free and ensure that the nutrition sites are accessible to all. UNIDOR will work closely with the local authorities to monitor the security situation and dangle services if the beneficiary and staff safety is not assured. The terms of reference for recruitment will be shared with the local authorities to avoid leaders pushing to employment of unqualified staff as quality as paramount. Mothers and caregivers will be asked to provide views directly to the organization or community leaders like chiefs and lead mothers. Community nutrition volunteers will be recruited from the respective villages of the project target to enable community members give feedback to people that can understand there language as well as trust since their live in the same villages. UNIDOR will also place suggestions boxes in all facilities for those that can read and write. This project will ensure that any factors that hinder beneficiaries from accessing services like distances or flooding are resolved by bringing services closer to people through outreaches and also ensure timely provision of services to enable beneficiaries get home on time thus minimizing the risk concerns along the home terrains
