Project Summary

This project is a continuation of an existing project run by MTI since 2017.
The objective of this project is to continue providing access to primary healthcare services to the population of Atareb, Sarmada, Termanin, and Armanaz in NW Syria.
With co-funding from WHO, MTI is currently supporting four PHC centers:
Atareb PHC Center
Sarmada PHC Center
Termanin PHC Center
Armanaz PHC Center
The support aims at implementing the essential components of the standard package of primary healthcare services, which is adopted by the health cluster in Gaziantep.
Under this project, MTI proposes to continue addressing the critical needs for primary healthcare in northern Syria, by supporting the same facilities throughout 2019. MTI will continue providing the health facilities with running costs, essential salaries, emergency maintenance costs and referrals. Building on the feedback received from the beneficiaries during the previous project, and due to the increasing needs for medication in Idleb and Aleppo, the project will increase the amount of free medication and medical supplies for the supported health facilities.
With this support MTI will ensure that the health facilities will continue to operate, staff will be retained, and health services will continue to be provided to people in need in northern Syria. This is necessary to provide essential life-sustaining and life-saving primary healthcare services to the increasing numbers of new IDPs in Idleb and Aleppo who came from southern Idleb, East Ghouta and rural Damascus, and from northern Homs since the beginning of 2018.

Needs

Based on HNO 2019, an estimated of 11.7 million people were still in need of humanitarian assistance as of the end of 2018 including 3 million people with disabilities. An estimated 6.2 million people remained internally displaced, with well over 1.6 million population movements recorded between January and December 2018. Outbreaks of measles, acute bloody diarrhea, typhoid fever and leishmaniosis were reported in various areas of the country throughout the year. Rates of displacement were particularly high in Idleb, with some 610,000 arrivals tracked from locations both inside and outside the Governorate from Rural Damascus, Hama and Aleppo among others. Due to the escalation of military operations in southern and south-eastern rural Idleb from early 2018, 273,000 arrivals were reported in central and northern Idleb, northern rural Hama and western rural Aleppo in January 2018 alone. Additional “local agreements” reached between March and August 2018 resulted in the displacement of some 130,000 people to north-west Syria from the formerly UN declared besieged areas of eastern Ghouta (Rural Damascus Governorate) and Yarmouk (Damascus Governorate), as well as contested areas in southern Damascus, eastern Qalamoun, northern rural Homs and southern Syria. The estimated people in need for health assistance is 2,614,498 in Aleppo and 1,738,432 in Idleb according to HNO 2019. Aldana and Atareb subdistricts host 134 IDP camps with 40,382 children under five. As of November 2018; Sarmada has a population of 54,053 (including 32,801 IDPs), Termanin has 31,948 (15,268 IDPs), Armanaz has 18,679 (6,549 IDPs), and Atareb has 46,819 (16,577 IDPs). The health status of this population was impacted by the lack of access to PHC and nutrition services. Health centers are overwhelmed by the mass influx of IDPs. Between February and June 2018, the MTI-supported health facilities witnessed an increase of 30-70% in the number of consultations and this was not matched by an increase in the capacity of facilities. Combined with a shortage in health personnel, especially of female workers this results in overworked practitioners and reduced quality of services. Since 2017, MTI addressed the critical needs for primary healthcare in the Atareb, Sarmada, Termanin and Armanaz communities by supporting four PHC centers with running costs, staff salaries, emergency, medicen and medical supplies, maintenance and referrals, ensuring that the facilities continue to operate, health staff are retained, and health services continue to be provided to people in need in northern Syria.On a monthly basis, the four centers provided an average of 17,557 PHC consultations to 3,095 men, 7,909 women, 3,269 boys, 3,284 girls. 41% of the services were provided to IDPs. Out of the total consultations, 26% were for respiratory tract infections, 22% for OBGYN conditions, 15% for NCDs, 7% for both trauma and skin conditions. The centers are currently employing 133 workers including 57 female workers. The proposed project will ensure that health services continue to be provided to people in need in the four communities in NW Syria. This is necessary to provide essential life-sustaining and life-saving primary healthcare services to the increasing numbers of IDPs and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo.

Implementation of activities

**1-Provide primary healthcare consultations to the beneﬁciaries in Atareb, Sarmada, Termanin and Armanaz.The staff and the essential infrastructure is already available in the PHC centers. MTI will pay for staff salaries and running costs for the four PHCs and provide technical support to the medical and non-medical staff to deliver essential primary healthcare services, manage the facility and ensure record-keeping and data collection for the supported PHCs. MTI’s staff in Turkey will supervise and coordinate with the facility management and other actors to ensure that the services are provided at the best available quality. **2-Procure the necessary medicine and medical supplies according to the needs of the health facilities through MTI Field Coordinators and as per MTI Procurement Policy.MTI through partners will procure the medicine and medical supplies according to the needs of the health facilities and based on the drug distribution rates and the pharmacy records of each facility. MTI’s Field Coordinators will obtain the drug consumption reports of each facility review and report to the team in Gaziantep. The Health Officer will review the records and prepare a list of the needed medicines. This list will be procured according to MTI’s procurement procedures and delivered to the facilities. MTI will also keep receiving donations from WHO and other partners and this amount will be combined with the procured medicine to meet the needs of the health facilities.**3-Monitor the services provided and the medical conditions received at the health facilities.MTI’s Syria Field Coordinators will conduct planned and spot-check ﬁeld visits for quality and technical control of the delivery of services The Field Coordinator is responsible for collecting ﬁnancial documentations and medical data on a monthly basis and will monitor the distribution of salaries to staff. He will do physical checks for the pharmacy and meet with beneﬁciaries to collect qualitative and quantitative feedback and receive any beneﬁciary complaints through complaint boxes and face to face interviews. Report activities to MTI and WHO The health-related data in addition to program reports and ﬁnancial reports will be reported to both MTI management and WHO on monthly basis. Additional analysis related to the health conditions will be carried out by the program team and provided to WHO, the Health Cluster and other stakeholders to inform the decisions related to improving the quality of services in northern Syria.

Protection Risk Analysis

**Risks:1. Risk of discrimination and inequality in providing services to particular groups or individuals. Probability: Low. Risk severity: High.2. Risk of harassment and sexual abuse during consultations. Probability: Low. Risk severity: High.3. Risk for limited access for vulnerable (pregnant women, singles women and PWD) to physically access the PHCs and beneﬁt from Services. Probability: Moderate. Risk severity: High.4. Risk of abduction for a ransom of personnel during travel to the health facilities. Probability: Moderate. Risk severity: High.5. Risk of a military attack on health facilities resulting in injuries and death of facility personnel and patients/escorts. Probability: Low. Risk severity: High. **Mitigation measures:- MTI Field coordinators and MEAL oﬃcer will conduct spot-check visits to the PHC.- Exit interview with beneﬁciaries to verify the equal and appropriate treatment by staff.- Enhance the conﬁdential complaint mechanism including a Whatsapp and phone hotline.- Continue announcing the independent reporting site, Ethics Point, for maintaining anonymous complaints. Provide awareness sessions for beneficiaries on using this site.- Conduct training for staff on PSEA and code of Conduct.- All personnel to sign and commit to MTI's PSEA, child protection and code of conduct policies and the adoption of a very strict termination and dismissal action towards any misconduct - Continue to improve the PHC structure to be disability-friendly and provide training for staff on dealing with PWDs. - Activate the ambulance system for vulnerable people or ensure transportations.- Continue MTI's CH project to support the referral system to the PHCs. **MTI will monitor this risk by: - Monitoring and Evaluation surveys will be conducted by MEAL department including the provided services and staff treatment including PSEA questions- Share services map for MHPSS and GBV support centers- Third party monitoring by community representative such as (HDs) to conduct random monitoring visits. - Cooperate with HI to identify accessibility barriers or PWD and pregnant women. **resources:MEAL Oﬃcers in each facility reported directly to MTI Turkey oﬃce for conducting the surveys and analyzing data and reports. With mitigation measures in place, the impact of the above mentioned risks will be reduced and the activities can be conducted.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

**Assumption:- Air strikes and military operations do not affect project progress in the operational area.- Access to project locations and community acceptance is maintained.- Sources for medical and non-medical supplies remain available.- Qualified staff continue to operate in the project location. **Risks and mitigation- Monitoring of activities is limited due to conflict-related access limitations. MTI will rely on local field-based staff to conduct monitoring activities. Where possible, MTI will cooperate with the local health directorates and other NGOs to conduct peer-monitoring of activities.- Airstrikes closely or directly affecting the health facilities causing damage to the facilities or impacts on facility staff and patients. MTI will develop suspension, relocation and hibernation plans for each facility to limit the impact of air-strikes. Local alerts about potential threats will be monitored and the facility staff and patients will be advised to avoid the facilities during high-risk periods.- Active conflict in Idleb restricting staff and patient movement to and from the facilities. MTI will closely monitor the conflict dynamics in the area of operation and adjust the project activities according to the analysis of these dynamics.- Border closure interrupting medical supply chain. Where possible, MTI will try to stock the facilities with a 2-month medical stock to limit the impact of supply chain interruptions.
