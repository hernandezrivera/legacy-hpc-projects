Project Summary

Entrenched gender inequality is the root cause of largely spread gender-based violence in Somalia. Accepted cultural practices such as FGM/C, forced and early marriage and intimate partner violence are prevalent throughout the country and are generally underreported. RI will focus on community based GBV prevention and access to safe reporting and referrals for GBV survivors, while at the same time will work on quality, age and gender appropriate multi-sectorial GBV response to survivors, through capacity building of service providers. Interventions at community levels will include: promoting safe reporting through trained community protection focal points (FP). 24 FP (18 female) will conduct community outreach to identify and link willing survivors to Women and Girls Safe Spaces (6) for receiving support. The community focal points will be trained on survivor-centered approaches, including how to promote safe reporting by upholding confidentiality, considering survivors’ privacy, dignity, safety/security and PSS needs while referring them for service provision. These community focal points will be sensitized on existing situations of vulnerability that require extra attention, in particular among marginalized groups of girls and women at heightened risk of GBV. These groups include: the elderly women, girls and women living with disability, IDP, returnees, refugees and stateless girls and women, girls and women heads of households, girls and women from minority clans among other marginalization factors. Proactively reaching marginalized groups at risk will contribute to address GBV under reporting through improving immediate access for GBV reporting from service delivery points to community level. To tackle GBV prevalence, engaging men is essential, given their influence in the lives of girls and women as fathers, husbands, brothers, uncles, community and religious leaders. To rally the men in the fight against GBV, RI will conduct advocacy/ mobilization workshops with various categories of men including religious leaders (150 males) with the aim of getting them to identify actions they could implement in their respective roles, to prevent and address GBV in the community. Following the workshops, supportive religious leaders will lead GBV awareness raising in their community, in particular among men and boys, to address harmful and discriminatory social and cultural attitudes and practices..
To encourage help seeking behavior and demystify health centres, RI will establish 6 Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSSs) attached to health facilities where girls and womenwill benefit from weekly dialogues on GBV prevention and response. Girls and women will be encouraged to visit the safe spaces through outreach activities led by community focal points The WGSSs will deliver a range of services.Counsellors will facilitate psychosocial support activities based on girls and women’s needs. WGSS staff will also share and make available information on reproductive health including menstrual hygiene, GBV, COVID and any other topics that will be of interest to girls and women. Guest speakers will be engaged by the WGSS staff depending on topics being handled to ensure accuracy and relevance of disseminated messages. GBV information will seek to address GBV stigma and myths. The WGSSs will also facilitate the establishment of peer support groups for girls and women (4800 girls and women will be reached). As the WGSSs are connected to a health facility, GBV survivors coming to the WGSSs will have immediate access to medical care including Clinical Management of Rape) within the same facility. Besides medical response, the WGSSs social workers will inform GBV survivors on existing other services including safe house, access to justice and livelihoods services and do referrals for these services, using a survivor centered approach. The project will provide material and cash assistance to ensure survivors timely access corresponding services.

Project Activities

RI will implement the following activities : - Identification, training and deployment of community protection focal points, RI will ensure the focal points come from diverse groups in the community including people living with disability where possible; they will be sensitized on marginalization, access barriers of people living with disability and facilitated to support this group through community outreach, the project will support community outreach for people living with disability to ensure messages are passed in various modes; Safety and GBV risk mapping in the new IDP camps where people affected by floods, drought and conflicts have relocated to and mitigation measures put in place, people with disabilities will be targeted as part of community participating in the safety audit and ensure risks they faced are captured and addressed ; - Religious leaders and men GBV advocacy/ mobilization workshops; - GBV prevention, mitigation and response awareness raising activities, including use of radio, IEC materials among other avenues that are sensitive to the needs of people living with disabilities, communities will be sensitized on heightened sexual violence risks faced by girls and women living with disability and support they need to be protected or seek help; - Door to door community outreach by the protection focal points following COVID prevention protocols, households with people with disabilities will be targeted among other households; - Construction /rehabilitation of Women and Girls Safe Spaces within health facilities including making them disability friendly and accessible; - Training of clinical and non-clinical staff on survivor-centered GBV response, CMR, PSS and Psychological First Aid gender and disability sensitive service provision; -Multi-sectorial response through referrals for essential services including special services that may be needed by survivors with disabilities; - MoUs development with GBV service providers to supporting referrals ; - Updating and disseminating of GBV service providers mapping and referral pathways among community protection focal points, social workers and protection stakeholders ; - age, gender and disability sensitive GBV case management;, including counselling, referral linkages and follow up to access multi-sectorial GBV response; - provision of medical services, Mental health and PSS services; - Provision of NFI (including dignity kits, solar lanterns, hygiene kits, emergency support kits) and cash assistance to GBV survivors including survivors living with disabilities; RI has elaborate safeguarding policies and procedures that includes child safeguarding and Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH)mechanisms. RI will ensure all engaged personnel are aware of safeguarding commitments and are engaged after through background checks to ensure they are fit to work with children and vulnerable adults. All personnel will commit to uphold safeguarding commitment through signing up of safeguarding behavior protocols. Children and communities will be alerted of expected code of behaviors from personnel and on available channels to providing feedback to RI. RI already has properly trained and supported safeguarding focal points whose contacts and names are shared with parents and children for any feedback. Through this project, RI will facilitate children and communities to conduct safety audit of 10 IDP settlements.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will provide little cash to support GBV survivors’ referrals and access to services.. The cash intends to cover transportation costs and other expenses related to access to services. This financial support will not be provided to all survivors, it will depend on identified needs, agreed upon referrals and distances to the service facilities.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The proposed amount range takes into consideration various transportation options available across project locations including use of public means but also with options of hiring vehicles for areas where public means of transport may not be reliable or available.

Mitigation Measures

- Mitigation measures for risks associated with distance to access services; Part of this project will look at safety audit and identify mitigation plans. RI will intentionally facilitate communities to review conditions for ensuring safety of girls , boys, women and men accessing the static and mobile WGSSs and other critical services including markets within the community ; and to identify ways of making access safe for all including for persons living with disability, elderly persons, boys and girls. Girls, boys, women, men, PLWDs will be consulted for identifying sites of construction of static WGSS and locations where mobile WGSS will be stationed. WGSSs facilitators and community protection focal points will raise awareness among girls, boys, women, men, PLWDs and elderly about safety planning such as moving in groups, avoiding walking while it is dark. - Mitigation measures for barriers faced by beneficiaries to access complaints and feedback mechanisms; RI will hold consultations with different members of community (boys, girls, women, men, IDP, minority clans, host community, PLWDs and elderly) to gather their preferred mode of providing feedback, identify issues of access, safety and community confidence in the feedback mechanism. These inputs will be factored in the design and operationalization of the feedback mechanisms. RI will ensure there are diverse age, gender, and diversity friendly ways of providing feedback. RI will ensure that, once the mechanisms is set-up, it is highly socialized among beneficiaries using diverse user friendly and appropriate communication modes. Communities will be made aware of the expected support and code of conduct for personnel involved in the project and encouraged to report any concerns and unacceptable behaviors. RI will uphold confidentiality, safety and ensure prompt response is provided to issues raised through the mechanism. - Mitigation measures under risk of unsafe/unethical data management; RI will train all personnel involved in the project including community protection focal points, clinical and non-clinical staff on data protection. Informed consents will be sought before any survivors information is shared with other service providers and before provision of any service and collecting of details of cases. Personnel will be required to sign up safeguarding protocols that have data protection protocol as a way of committing to protect the beneficiaries' data. RI has elaborate data management and protection policy which guides all our data management processes, this includes ensuring safety of hard copies case files by keeping files with personal data locked and only accessible to staff on 'need to know' basis. Soft copies are safely safeguarded with passwords that are renewed after staff leave or take up other responsibilities. Official information including survivors and patient information is only saved on few selected office laptops and sent/received through official emails only (with passwords). Case files are coded and coding information kept under lock and key in a separate cabinet.
