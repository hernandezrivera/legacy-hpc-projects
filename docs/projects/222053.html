Project Summary

South Sudan faces multiple crises, including conflict, climate disasters such as floods and drought, social and political instability, food insecurity and inflation. About 9.3 million people, or 75 per cent of the population, are projected to need humanitarian and protection assistance in 2025. Additionally, the Sudan conflict has seen South Sudan receive over 700,000 refugees and returnees, exacerbating needs and tensions in already struggling, highly vulnerable host communities and stretching the humanitarian response capacity in a context with 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs). Out of these 9.3 million affected population and people in need,4.2 million individuals will specifically require SNFI assistance.
Reference South Sudan Flood Preparedness and Response Plan
In October, the humanitarian situation continued to deteriorate in South Sudan, driven by high food prices, a severe economic crisis, conflict, disease outbreaks and cross-border movements from Sudan and flooding. As of 31 October, multi-agency assessments identified about 1.4 million flood-affected people across the country. Over 377,000 people have been displaced by floods, seeking shelter on higher ground. Reference South Sudan: Humanitarian Snapshot (October 2024)
UNHCR South Sudan will focus on targeting people across the 23 most severely affected counties in upper Nile, Jonglei, Unity state and will aim to support 167,650 in need of essential SNFIs ,shelter solutions. UNHCR will also focus on resolving the HLP related issues in some of the priority 1-2 counties specifically for the returnee’s population in close consultation with ministry of land, infrastructure and public utilities and county, Payam administration officials.
UNHCR’s Response
UNHCR’s project will address the humanitarian needs of IDPs and returnees through the provision of targeted, protection-focused core relief items (emergency shelter and NFIs), prioritizing the most vulnerable households and individuals with specific needs, particularly women and girls. UNHCR has established strategies for shelter and NFIs responses with SOPs for each step including beneficiaries’ identification, requesting, and releasing NFIs, distribution and post distribution. All these steps are aimed to ensure fair, accountability, transparency and gender sensitivity.
Project Objectives
The project will contribute to the following sectoral objectives:
1. Tier 1: Focuses on life-saving interventions, particularly the distribution of emergency shelter and NFIs (S/NFIs) for the most vulnerable population.
2. Tier 2: Supports the reintegration of returnees.
UNHCR will support the reintegration of returnees through HLP support with the close support of relevant government authorities
3. Tier 3: Maintains IDP and transit sites, with a specific focus on repairing and refurbishing shelters in critical condition.
4. UNHCR will maintain and upgrade Renk TC-1,TC-2 ,Bulukat TC ,and Abyei administrative area to provide basic essential SNFI needs to returnees during their transitional stay at these locations until relocate to their final native destinations.
Strategic Alignment
UNHCR will implement lifesaving Shelter and NFIs response with an aim to complement to resilience and recovery initiatives, promoting local and sustainable solutions wherever possible.
UNHCR will advocate for protection mainstreaming and joint vulnerability assessments which will help optimize limited resources and ensure a timely response to the most critical needs of vulnerable IDPs, refugee returnees, and other affected populations.
Protection Prioritization
UNHCR recognizes that protection risks remain particularly high for women, girls, boys, and individuals with specific needs. These risks may require emergency shelter response and other essential NFIs assistance, which will be central to the organization's response strategy.

1. Needs

As of 31 October, multi-agency assessments identified about 1.4 million flood-affected people across the country. Over 377,000 people have been displaced by floods, seeking shelter on higher ground. Reference South Sudan: Humanitarian Snapshot (October 2024)Similarly, UNHCR-IOM Joint Dashboard,168245 HH/678923 South Sudanese individuals have returned from Sudan across various border points and the majority of these individuals lacks basic essentials items and shelters. UNHCR South Sudan will focus on targeting people across the 23 most severely affected counties in upper Nile, Jonglei, Unity state and will aim to support 167,650 in need of essential SNFIs, shelter solutions. UNHCR will also focus on resolving the HLP related issues in some of the priority 1-2 counties specifically for the returnee’s population in close consultation with ministry of land, infrastructure and public utilities and county, Payam administration officials. The various IRNA reports recently conducted in the last six months have identified common needs that the population is sleeping under the open sky, exposed to rain and severe weather condition. The population requires urgent support with shelter materials and household materials, including mosquito nets, sleeping mats, cooking sets, water containers, etc. UNHCR over the recent years has scaled up the CBIs interventions and wherever possible will be engaging youth for labor intensive CFW modalities in shelter fabrications, land clearance and related works. UNHCR has been improving the essential social service infrastructure and flood resilient projects that will indirectly ensure sustainability of existing tukuls and related communal infrastructure.UNHCR with the support of partners including NRC,DRC,HCO,HDC,MASS,ADRA,WVI ALIGHT,ACTED,AIRD ,RRC has a strong presence on ground and will identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries which are desperately needing SNFI responses in the proposed 23 counties .UNHCR will preposition essential SNFIs to the field warehouses and will keep on replenishing the essential SNFIs for each field location based on the needs compiled from field locations. UNHCR follow stringent beneficiaries’ selection criteria for provision of SNFI responses. UNHCR through the settlement approach and master plan approach will be implementing various infrastructure interventions which will contribute to the enhanced protections and resilience and will be directly contributing to the durability of the shelter /tukuls specifically in flood prone areas through extensive community engagement.The beneficiaries selected by partners will be quickly verified by UNHCR Multi-functional team including protection, shelter, security and field colleagues together with RRC Officials (wherever required/feasible) before the distribution exercise. UNHCR and partners have set criteria for assistance distribution including crowd control and even post distribution grievance redressals.Wherever possible, UNHCR will collaborate with humanitarians on ground to complement the interventions to increase the beneficiaries base and aiming for carpet coverage rather than scattered limited responses.HLP is a complex issue in South Sudan and UNHCR will aim to address HLP issues for the returnees in these locations with close coordination of relevant government entities and community elders.
