Project Summary

This project proposal is the result of CUAMM collaboration with Ethiopian local authorities and Regional Health Cluster led by WHO in Somali Region and specifically in Liben Zone where CUAMM runs an office in Bokolmayo.
In 2020 CUAMM intervened to strengthen the health system in the Harawa Woreda with the aim to guarantee better quality and accessibility of maternal and child services. During the harshest months of the pandemic, CUAMM also provided support to the isolation and quarantine centers of Jigiiga and the Dharwanaaji Health Center, supplying PPE and training local health workers. In 2021 CUAMM was also involved in supporting the Somali region's health system during the cholera outbreak.
This project proposal will intervene in Filtu, Boklomayo and Dolo Ado Woreda counting a total population of 171.628 people. In Boklomayo Woreda there are more than 8.300 IDPs and 43.000 refugees, in Dolo Ado there are more than 84.000 refugees and in Filtu more than 8.000 IDP.
The project aim is to strengthen the quality and accessibility of preventive and curative health services, and the capacity of local health systems to respond to health emergencies and epidemic outbreaks.
The project will contribute to the Health cluster following objectives:
i) Provide accessible essential lifesaving health services to targeted populations including internally displaced persons and affected host communities, returnees, children, women of reproductive age and pregnant women through supply of emergency drugs and medical supplies (including PPE and cleaning materials) to 3 health facilities (1 HC per Woreda) and conduction of community outreach/mobile sessions for remote areas.
The Mobile service will provide basic health care to the population farthest from the health facilities providing at least 150 OPD per month especially for mothers and children, reducing maternal and under-five mortality and morbidity in the districts and identifying the most complicated cases to be referred to the Health centers.
During outreach sessions (1 per month per woreda), at least 20 children and 35 adults will be screened for common illness and communicable diseases and those identified with disorders will receive treatments including deworming and vitamin A supplementation. In addition, pregnant and lactating women and children under five will be screened for malnutrition and those identified as malnourished will be referred to the health center; pregnant women will benefit from ANC services, will receive folic acid, and will be counseled on the necessary preparation for childbirth. Finally, at least 240 children will be vaccinated for vaccine-preventable diseases.
ii) Prepare for, detect and respond to health emergencies and epidemic prone disease outbreaks, including COVID-19 pandemic.
The CUAMM assessment in 2021 registered the need to work at the community level on the prevention of infections, the adoption of healthy behaviors and the recognition of signs of illness, promoting the access to health facilities. The project will conduct awareness campaigns on hygiene and sanitation as well on the prevention of COVID 19, cholera and other communicable diseases involving community volunteers and Using IEC/BCC materials.
It was also noted that there was a need to provide health center staff with more knowledge on the prevention and management of Covid-19 and public health emergencies, also involving the category closest to the communities, the Community Health Workers that will be trained on integrated disease surveillance and response. To impress sustainability and efficiency, CUAMM will also train and offer logistic support for Zonal Woreda Surveillance and RRT.
The initiative will be in line with the international principles of Aid Effectiveness and with the Good Humanitarian Donorship Initiative 2012/2015, SPHERE, ICRC Code of Conduct for NGOs in Disaster Relief, Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction.

1. - Projects activities

Provide accessible essential lifesaving health services to targeted populations including internally displaced persons and affected host communities, returnees, children, women of reproductive age and pregnant women. A1.1 Support to local health facilities through supply of emergency drugs and medical supplies to the health facilities (including PPE and cleaning materials).In order to ensure the full functioning of one health centers for each Woreda (3 in total), essential medical equipment and drugs necessary for the provision of basic services that due to the emergency situation cannot be covered by the government authorities will be provided. In an effort to ensure continuity of health services and provide protection for workers, appropriate PPE and supplies that can be used for the prevention of COVID-19 and communicable diseases will be provided to health facilities to address deficiencies identified during the needs assessment. A1.2 Implementation of mobile services and community screening.To ensure coverage of key basic services among the most vulnerable communities that do not access health centers, project staff in collaboration with district authorities and health centers will organize 36 (1 per month per Woreda) community outreaches in the areas of intervention where the main communicable diseases (malaria, diarrhea and tuberculosis prevention and control activities) found in the community will be monitored. During each session, at least 35 adults and 20 children under 5 will be treated for common illness, screened for malnutrition for children under five and pregnant women will also be carried out, and those with malnutrition will be linked to health facilities. Children found to be non-compliant with routine vaccination will be identified and linked to nearby health posts for vaccination. Prepare for, detect and respond to health emergencies and epidemic prone disease outbreaks, including COVID-19 pandemic.A2.1 Support district Health office in strengthening disease surveillance activities and regular supportive supervision to Health centers and health postsIn order to support the activities of disease surveillance, the project intends to support local authorities through logistic facilities, trainings and supervisions.A2.1.1 Logistic support for zonal and woreda surveillance and RRT Team A2.1.2 Training of HCWs on Infection Prevention and ControlFrom each Woreda, 10 Health workers (30 in total) will be trained during 3 days on IPC.A2.1.3 Training for health care workers and HEWs on integrated disease surveillance and response (IDSR)10 HCW and HEW per Woreda will be trained during 3 days on IDSR followed by supervision.A2.2 Conduct awareness campaigns on hygiene and good sanitation practices and prevention and recognition of COVID 19, cholera and other communicable diseases Involving community volunteers and Using IEC/BCC materials, campaigns will adopt following methodologies:i) Awareness raising in public places where the community usually gathers such as markets, bus stations, school exits, etc. by spreading prevention messages with a megaphone and distributing information materials. ii) Specific awareness sessions that will be presented in the form of entertainment through games, quizzes, etc. so as to attract people's curiosity. Known people such as actors, singers and/or local personalities will be used for the sessions.(iii) House-to-house awareness with the collaboration of local actors such as HEW, Woreda Health Officers and DAs. A2.3 Training of community volunteers on hygiene and good sanitation practices, referral system and malnutrition identificationA total of 450 people (150 per Woreda) will be trained with basic knowledge to support communities in understanding and adopting good sanitation practices to prevent infections, to identify malnutrition and major health needs and to refer to appropriate health facilities for treatments.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The initiative will be conducted in close collaboration with relevant local authorities, Zone and District Health Offices, with whom a coordination platform will be constituted contributing to: (i) ensure the framing of the intervention in the framework of local strategic priorities, (ii) ensure compliance with Ethiopian guidelines and protocols, (iii) enhance local human and technical resources, avoiding duplication, (iv) promote sustainability and replicability of the action, through the strengthening of existing institutional capacities and services and sharing experiences with other stakeholders. A monitoring plan will be developed, detailing the methods of data collection, the frequency of data collection, and the responsible person assigned to the specific task.In coordination with relevant authorities and partner, an evaluation will also be carried out to highlight lessons learned and common visions for future interventions.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Feedback mechanisms will be strengthened (or, where needed, established) to ensure affected community members the possibility to provide feedback on the services provided by the Action and, in case, complaint about their quality or the conduct of frontline workers. Feedback mechanisms will be organized according to CUAMM Policies and Procedures and will include the following channels: hotlines, compliant boxes in supported health posts, regular consultations with women, girls, boys and men conducted by trained staff. Particular attention will be given to the inclusiveness of the channels to complaint, to guarantee the possibility to provide feedback also to people with disability. A database to register all the complaints will be created and will be monitored at Addis Ababa level. Field staff will share on a monthly basis the results of the consultations with target population and all feedbacks received (including specific complaints). Feedbacks will be classified according to the type (suggestions, complaints on the quality of services, complaints on the conduct of frontline workers) and will be treated according to internal Standard Operating Procedures to ensure the most appropriate response depending on the type of feedback.
