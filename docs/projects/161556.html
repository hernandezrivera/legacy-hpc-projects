Project Summary

Goal
The goal of this intervention is to support IDPs in camps and host communities with strategies to develop their ability to resist abuse through improved resilience.
How
• IDPs will be screened and the most at risk of abuse including SGBV will be identified through a mapping exercise in camps and host communities. These IDPs will receive training through local camp and or community structures. Training will develop their ability to identify all forms of abuse they are encountering. Training will also provide respite for abused IDPs within the target groups. Emotional and psychosocial counselling and support will be provide to all IDPs depending on their assessed specific needs. AUN will utilize camp and host community public structures that provide emotional and psychosocial counselling where possible.
• Once training is complete, we will start to group the IDPs into self selected groups based on the business they want to venture in. We will promote simple micro businesses that are marketable within the camps and target host communities and those that require no more than N40,000 of start up capital. Going for single owner small business as opposed to cooperative with many members is a better option as the survival and success of cooperatives with IDPs form different areas is very low. However, depending on identified need and market information, there might be some beneficiaries who will prefer to work as a cooperative.
• IDPs in their trade groups will receive livelihoods training based on the selected business area. Training will be conducted in local language by by local business vendors already practicing such trades. We will work with the trainers to have one common training approach that details the training steps and mentorship plan. Upon completion of training, AUN will provide all trained PoCs with a basic financial literacy and small business development training to equip them with the knowledge and skills to run small business and manage their funds. After this one day training, PoCs will then undergo 3-5 apprenticeship training with their trainers at their business premises. This apprenticeship is to hone their business management skills so that they are more likely to succeed when they start their own business.
• At the end of the apprenticeship, we will assess and evaluate the readiness of each PoC to start a business. Our assessment tool to be implemented through an interview will inquire about their assets and liabilities, their needs and current incomes as well as their reasons for starting small business. The result of this assessment will enable us to select the most qualified PoCs to support to start a business. Those PoCs who pass this assessment will be supported to identify location for their small business either a market shed, or a place near their home. We will support each PoC to set up the business is a safe and accessible location.
• PoCs will then receive startup kits during the graduation. Once PoCs have set up and started running their businesses, AUN will then provide a small financial support for them to use to re order goods for their business before the business starts making a profit. Once the business is up and running, beneficiaries will be supported through basic savings strategy to start saving a fixed on a weekly basis. At the start, AUN staff will be the collectors of the savings from each business and keep records. On a monthly basis, savings status will be shared with all PoCs in their business groups.
• On monthly basis, these small business holders will be supported to meet to discuss their businesses and share ideas and advice one another. This meeting will also be used by AUN to follow up on their protection. Issues of abuse will be brought up and coping mechanisms that the PoC can use will be discussed. Those cases of abuse that require legal support will be forwarded to legal authority including Police. AUN staff will support the PoCs to make follow ups.

1 - Needs

The increasing spread of nefarious activities of the Boko Haram sect in Nigeria, resulted in massive violation of human rights, loss of lives and destruction of infrastructure are some of serious issues that cannot be dismissed with a wave of a hand. According to the Human Rights Watch (2014), Boko Haram insurgency has led to the high rate of human casualties, as thousands of deaths are being recorded. These numbers have changed the status of women into widows and the children into orphans in all the North Eastern states; with Borno state having highest casualties. The most prevalent Human Rights violation is the Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV), which is a widespread phenomenon among female internally displaced persons (IDP’s) within various camps and in host communities. (International Displacement Monitoring Centre, May, 2014). Attacks on defenseless women and children and using them as shield and bait are part of the strategies adopted by the terrorists with the most celebrated being the abduction of girls from a secondary school and attendant sexual violation while in captivity, as well as, those of other women whose numbers are undetermined. Unfortunately, Nigerians and particularly government are mainly pre-occupied by the scale of the humanitarian crises with limited attention to the gross human rights abuses, particularly those related to Sexual and Gender Based Violations (SGBV) being suffered by women, girls and boys. The likely risk of abuse for women and children is high in both camps and host communities in particular when they struggle to get basic needs that are provided by international humanitarian agencies. Women face further risk when they carry out daily routines such as going to get firewood, accessing water and even when they go to their farms. Women and adolescent girls in particular, are more exposed to sexual and gender based violence. And if such women and adolescent girls have the right knowledge about what constitutes abuse and their rights and if such women and adolescent girls have improved incomes, they are less likely to fall victims of SGBV.

2 - Activities

• The intervention will focus on both IDPs who have already been assessed as victims of SGBV and those who have not reported being abused. • Mapping Exercise – where there available records from various entities to determine and address the prevalence of SGBV in IDP Camps we will use such data to map the beneficiaries. In areas with no data, we will conduct a mapping exercise• Emotional and Psychosocial Support – These will be provided to all IDPs depending on their assessed specific needs. Counselling points will be established and managed by experienced AUN staff and experts. AUN will work with relevant public and accredited private institutions to provide follow-on programs that could help to prevent abuses or erase abuses on victims. Mentoring programs shall be of immense support to the IDPs• Training – The identified IDPs will receive basic knowledge on Human Rights and channels of redress when abused. The training modules will be adapted, translated and delivered in the local languages (Hausa & Kanuri). Also as a follow-on, the IDPs will receive training in basic financial literacy which will help them to boost their trade and increase their knowledge on business management.• Small Business Development – The beneficiary IDPs will be encouraged and exposed to better business management and solutions. This will help them to have increased knowledge and skills. They will be delineated into business/trade groups based on selected business interest. Training will be provided using the local business practitioners already practicing the trades. The training will equally be done in local languages. At the end of the trainings, the project will provide simple micro business that are marketable within the camps and targeted host communities which require no more than N40,000 of startup capital. They will also be supported with micro banking opportunities, where they can save monies and receive credit support by the bank to boost their business. • Apprenticeship – Based on interest of the PoCs, they will be expose to 3 -5 apprenticeship opportunities from where they could make their choice. Upon enlistment, the PoCs will be enlisted to undergo apprenticeship program with their trainers at their business premises. They will be opportuned to hone their business management skills, so that, they are likely to succeed when they start their own business. At the completion of their apprenticeship, and assessment of their performances through structured evaluation processes, they will be supported with a startup kits and small finance at their graduation and help to identified a location for their small business in a safe and accessible location, either a market shed or a place near their homes.• Response Mechanism – There are public institutions and private organizations established to help and fight abuses, most especially the SGBV. The statutory institutions will be enlisted and supported to discharge their responsibilities through networking and collaboration to educate, ameliorate, apprehend abusers and even prosecute. The camps managers, community gatekeepers, policing institutions and other civil defenders will be synchronizing into the supportive programs to stem IDPs being abuse. AUN will provide refreshers capacity to these responders especially in better management of camps and IDPs settlements, to strengthen their response capacities. A well-structured reporting mechanism and checks on the response mechanism will be institutionalized that will provide a protective service to the IDPs being abused. AUN will also make available to the IDPs, the channels for redress for any IDP that is abused especially the services of the Police, Civil Defense Corps, Ministries of Justice, Health, Education and Women Affairs. AUN will follow up on reported cases until there is an adjudication of the reported matter. Engaging sustainability plans will be formulated before project end
