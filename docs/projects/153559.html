Project Summary

The intervention will focus on IDPs, Returnees and Host Community (conflict, malnutrition and disease affected). Emphasis on female headed households, PLWHIV/AIDS, elderly and disabled.
The needs of the population in terms of WASH is directly related to the number of IDPs in the community, which always puts a strain on existing services and water sources. The cluster has taken into account high GAM rates that make populations more prone to diseases, and areas with a history of cholera outbreaks. Overall, this relates to poor WASH infrastructure and bad hygiene practices.
In conflict-affected areas, water and sanitation infrastructures in IDP camps are often overstretched by the arrival of new IDPs or need to be upgraded/ maintained due to the protracted situation. Significant refugee influxes in areas that are already underserved in terms of access to water and sanitation facilities are creating additional health risks whilst emergency response struggles to meet sectoral standards in time. Self-reliance should be fostered and enhanced in all projects. The increasing demand for natural resources in densely populated areas, coupled with climate change, continues to challenge sustainability of water resources and their exploitation.
Oxfam 2019 WASH response will target 120,000 most vulnerable people of which 25,000 IDPs, 15,000 Returnees and 80,000 Host communities affected by crisis, food insecurity (high GAM rates) and disease outbreaks, with a strong focus on the most vulnerable members of the community.

Needs

"Over 5.3 million most vulnerable people in South Sudan are in urgent need of WASH services. Malnutrition and food insecurity make communities more prone to water-related diseases, especially children under 5. In 2017, a cholera epidemic spread to new areas that were previously not affected by outbreaks, placing even more pressure on already vulnerable communities. In the conflict-affected areas, most of the water infrastructure is destroyed, making the situation dire. Additionally, the situation in urban areas has been impacted by increasing fuel prices and unavailability of fuel at times putting more strain on existing water systems and substantial increase in cost of water forcing people to resort to unsafe drinking water.In conflict-affected areas, water and sanitation infrastructures in IDP camps are often overstretched by the arrival of new IDPs or need to be upgraded/ maintained due to the protracted situation. Significant refugee influxes in areas that are already underserved in terms of access to water and sanitation facilities are creating additional health risks whilst emergency response struggles to meet sectoral standards in time. Self-reliance should be fostered and enhanced in all projects. The increasing demand for natural resources in densely populated areas, coupled with climate change, continues to challenge sustainability of water resources and their exploitation.- IDPs: there are almost 2 million forcibly displaced people within South Sudan (of whom more than 50% are estimated to be children), out of which more than 218 000 have sought refuge in UNMISS bases called Protection of Civilians sites (PoCs), 62% being children, 26% women and 13% men . New forced displacement occurred between 2016 and 2017 in particular in Upper Nile State, Greater Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Jonglie and Eastern Equatoria. Large numbers of people remain stranded, hiding in hard to reach areas, in bushes or in swamps, where they lack food and basic commodities to sustain themselves. Regular access to adequately assist this population remains a major challenge. - Vulnerable host populations often face the same risks and vulnerabilities as forcibly displaced persons. Displacement into host communities will stretch the already limited resources further. - Local communities not affected by conflict may also experience severe levels of humanitarian needs including food insecurity, under-nutrition, and epidemics. - Returnees: voluntary returns of IDPs or refugees are limited, however if conditions change, the return movement might increase.

a) 10 new boreholes Construction and 90 borehole rehabilitation, Rehabilitation or construction of 150 emergency sanitation facilities; c) Procurement, and distribution of 8000 WASH NFI and hygiene kits; d) Conduct 100 regular hygiene promotion sessions, including 8000 household visits; e) Training 72 Community Hygiene Promoters; f) Establish 90 WASH Committees and train 48 pump mechanics to ensure the maintenance and operation of the rehabilitated water facilities g) Construction of Toilets and hygiene facilities especially in schools.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

As an organization striving to be accountable, Oxfam provides relevant and clear information to stakeholders including the community that we are serving. Information sharing is not a onetime activity and does not follow a one-way approach. Affected communities will be communicated with relevant and timely information they require to make informed decisions and choices. It is an ongoing dialogue between the organization and its stakeholders over project life time.Oxfam will also make sure that vulnerable community groups are included in information sharing and information will be shared in languages, formats and media that can be easily understood by the communities we work with. As per the accountability framework of the country programme the following are key information that needs to be shared with affected people.• Organizational background, vision and mission. (who we are, what we do, how we do it, who we work with)• Project specific information – objectives and timing of the projects, expected results, beneficiary selection criteria, entitlements, implementation modality, etc.• Contact details for each project• Dates and locations of key events • Basic reports on project progress• How to give feedback and make a complaint on quality of project implementation/service delivery, OXFAM/partner staff behaviour and fraud or attempted fraud

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Apart from the general information about Oxfam and the project, we share specific information tailored to specific groups including and gender. Information related to beneficiary selection criteria, project entitlements, dates and locations of key events etc. are some of the information that will shared with respective groups. To this end, consideration of local languages in information sharing is a key thing that will be emphasised in this project.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

As part of empowering the community in target, we consider strong community awareness on Sexual exploitation and abuse and jointly set culturally sensitive and socially acceptable reporting mechanisms in cases of happening. As we have safeguarding focal points in each project location and at Juba level we will encourage community members to report on staff behaviour to these focal persons using the most preferred and safe mechanism they think and Oxfam will make sure appropriate action is taken on time after investigation. For wider reach Oxfam will translate the poster against SHEA to local languages and disseminate the targeted implementation areas. Apart from these ways the routine post distribution monitoring (PDMs), exit interviews, continuous monitoring’s will be another mechanism through which we get feedback and complaints on staff behaviour and fraud related issues.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Oxfam shall enable beneficiaries and their representatives (i.e. Community leaders, Beneficiary Selection Committee Members, etc.) to participate from the programme identification stage in programme planning, implementation to monitoring and evaluation. Participation of female and male beneficiaries and the most disadvantaged and vulnerable community groups will be ensured through various mechanisms including regular gender assessments, monthly feedback sessions via sex specific FGDs, surveys and assessments, community consultative sessions, verification exercises and others. The views and requests of community members will be feedback in to the programme cycle to make sure that affected people are making decisions in the issues affecting their lives in relation to project deliveries. Feedback from beneficiaries that have influenced the way activities or outputs are delivered will be documented for evidence and learning.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Guided by the accountability framework of the country programme the project will have built-in feedback and/or complaint management system that is effective, accessible and safe for intended beneficiaries, affected communities. Feedback and compliant collection mechanisms will be defined after discussion and agreement with people affected by the project/programme. Multiple and complementary feedback and complaints collection mechanisms will be considered to avail safe and convenient options to the community. We keep records of complaints and feedback regularly using the recording data base we have and follow up on the referral and response progress. Conducting Post distribution monitoring, conducting exit interviews and setting up complaint desk in each distribution sites will be additional sources of information to get the views and opinions of targeted community groups.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

limited humanitarian access to affected populations, bureaucratic access challenges, and limited access to services has been the major protection risks facing youths, girls, women, people with disabilities and people or groups with concerns in South Sudan. The project will address this in four ways: 1) Through reporting and documenting barriers to access to the Access Group for documentation, advocacy and evidence based protection advocacy with different appropriate stakeholders including the the Protection Cluster, HCT, UNMISS etc2) Applying the risk reduction approach by mainstreaming protection in other sectors like Gender, Governance, FSL, WASH and Education. 3) Providing protection individual protection assistance to those people at risk, people with specific needs, - NFI for protection, and or cash for protection. 4) Inclusive engagement, participation and empowerment of groups/populations facing barriers to access.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project intends to address inequalities and protection threats through the following ways: 1) through proactive engagement with the affected populations in assessments, project implementation and monitoring, 2) Improving the protective environment of the affected populations by sharing evidenced based protection needs and analysis with the protection cluster and relevant stakeholders3) Supporting local peace and security initiatives, platforms and dialogues for women, youths and communities towards promoting peaceful coexistence and peacebuilding 4) through continuous protection analysis, situational analysis and contextual analysis, conflict sensitivity approaches will be employed to maximise positive impact of the interaction between the conflict context and the action; minimise negative impact between the interaction of conflict context and action. Finally, regular protection risk assessments for major activities in WASH, Education, FSL, Govenrance and Gender will be taken into consideration for safe programming and Do No Harm.
