Project Summary

While the intensity of conflicts have shown a relative decline and clashes have been majorly contained in many regions of the country, the humanitarian situation continues to be critical with impacts spilling through 2020 further elevating the need for protection assistance with 805,535 as the target population for Gender-Based Violence (GBV) assistance according to the 2020 HRP. Women and girls remain the priority groups targeted by GBV in emergency programming. Out of 805,535 people in need of GBV assistance, 386,657 (48%) are women and 418,878 (52%) are children. Of the total population identified for urgent humanitarian assistance, UNFPA estimates the number of women in the reproductive age group in the conflict-affected areas to be 231,384. Overall, UNFPA intends to reach 233,334, the majority (60%) of whom are adult women of reproductive age, 20% young women, 10% young males and 10% adult.
Gender-based violence (GBV) is one of the most pervasive human rights abuses. GBV encompasses a wide range of violations, including rape, sexual assault, physical violence, emotional violence, denial of resources, services and opportunities, forced marriages (including early marriage) and other harmful practices. Vulnerability of women and girls to GBV in South Sudan is exacerbated by—and particularly acute in—the conflict crisis, where the “peacetime” risks of violence are compounded by the realities of the humanitarian situation, including years of conflict, displacement, breakdowns in particular social norms and protection mechanisms, changing roles within the family, famine, and cultural beliefs. Following years of conflict and displacement, formal systems of protection and justice weakened and disrupted; separation of families and communities occurred, shock-absorbing capacities depleted putting women and girls at increased risk of multiple forms of GBV. GBV primarily affects women and girls, who represent 99 per cent of the reported incidents in the first half of 2019 as stated on the GBV IMS report.
UNFPA proposes to continue strengthening the provision of the on-going critical GBV in emergency programmatic interventions to crisis-affected populations by focusing on GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services. South Sudan remains in a severe humanitarian crisis due to the cumulative effects of years of conflict and violence against civilians. Nearly 4.5 million people have been forced out of their homes in search of safety and security. Within South Sudan, UNOCHA Situational report of October 2019 places the number of internally displaced people at 1.5 million.
Survivors of GBV need access to lifesaving medical, psychosocial, safety and legal services to meet their complex and multiple needs. The reduced access to essential and lifesaving reproductive health services coupled with the lack of basic items which respond to the hygiene and reproductive health needs of women and girls, make living very difficult for displaced women and girls to maintain acceptable hygiene standards which lead to discrimination, forcing girls in some instances to quit schools for fear of being stigmatized by their fellow boys and men. Displaced women and girls need basic items to interact comfortably in public and maintain their hygiene, particularly menstrual hygiene. Without access to culturally appropriate clothing and hygiene items, the mobility of women and girls is restricted, and their health might be compromised. Without certain items, women may be unable to seek essential social services, including humanitarian aid, which may increase their vulnerability to GBV.
Furthermore, with the ongoing political events, there is a growing recognition to seize further recovery and development opportunities, where they exist, and to strengthen synergies between these efforts and the ongoing humanitarian response. 2 - Activities

Through its programming, UNFPA will continue to provide specialised and integrated GBV services through the static and mobile approach including comprehensive case management, referrals, enhance access for women and girls in WGFS, awareness-raising and Dignity kit programing UNFPA will regularly update risk assessments and service mapping of the areas while advocating with the different Clusters like WASH, Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Nutrition, Education clusters among others at state and national levels, for a prompt and more comprehensive response to the identified needs. The services provided within the WGFSs will focus on initial healing and the ability to identify and strengthen their coping mechanism. Women and girls can access this service at particular risk of sexual violence, exploitation, abuse, denial of resources or forced and early marriage. UNFPA will challenge any deliberate attempt to exclude parts of the affected population and will provide assistance based on need and guard against any form of direct or indirect discrimination. The intervention will focus on the following activities among others, 1. Comprehensive GBV case management including individual psycho-social support,2. Establishment and strengthening of WGFSs and listening centres where women and girls can find a safe and supportive environment to gather for different kind of services including skills training, 3. Awareness-raising on GBV risk prevention, mitigation and available response services4. Engaging men and boys to enhance their capacity to be partners, advocates and champions in the fight against GBV5. Work with male leaders and gatekeepers, especially religious and community leaders to identify strategic allies for the prevention of and response to GBV.6. Training of front-line service providers from different agencies, community-based organisation (CBOs) and key actors and community leaders on basic GBV concept, case management, PFA/ PSS, GBV IMS, GBV Coordination among others7. Procurement and distribution of Dignity Kits8. Develop GBV SoPs /update and disseminate referral pathways,9. Train community mobilisers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning10. Support regular safety audits11. Support partner organisations and women-led organisations to procure/ produce and distribute fuel-efficient stoves in locations where there is established the risk to GBV while collecting firewood12. Conduct advocacy and support for the establishment of safe houses through an inter-agency partnership. UNFPA, through the financial contribution of different donors, has been operational and implementing various components of GBV in emergency programming in South Sudan.The activities have include procuring and distributing dignity kits and post-rape treatment kits; establishing and operationalizing women and girl-friendly spaces; ensuring the provision of case management, service mapping and establishing / strengthening GBV referral pathways; provision of awareness-raising sessions and psychosocial support for vulnerable women and girls in conflict-affected communities, and capacitating front line service providers on clinical management of rape survivors, psycho-social support and GBV Information Management system (GBV IMS), comprehensive case management among others. UNFPA has also a comparative advantage for swift and smooth customs clearance because of its longstanding partnership and experience of working with national authorities on its Reproductive Health Commodity Security system that often imports massive amount of supplies from abroad including dignity kits to be distributed to different partner organizations giving it an advantageous position for immediate dispatch of humanitarian supplies to the target beneficiaries. Also, UNFPA is the designated core pipeline organisation for dignity kits procurement and distribution for South Sudan humanitarian response.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

A majority of project beneficiaries are women and girls. Since gender-based violence disproportionally affects more women and girls than any other segment of the populations. Therefore not consulting women and girls would pause a significant risk to the project. Women and girls input into the project design would enhance ownership and improve service uptake. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence and exploitation during access to humanitarian assistance would have a devastating impact on the project. The women and girls would be gravely affected physically and psychologically, among others, while the image of the humanitarian system as a whole would be tainted. The consequences of sexual violence and exploitation are life long and can have a severe impact on the community as a whole. Unsafe and unethical management of data, especially in the provision of health services, can result in severe consequences. In some situations in the past, health workers have been attacked and injured due to it, while beneficiaries have shunned services due to such practices. Data management is, therefore, an important part of project management and all those involved in the implementation of the project have to commit to managing data safely and ethically
