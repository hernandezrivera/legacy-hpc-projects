Project Summary

According to the 2020 HNO, “the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 million people in need; of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity”. The 2020 HNO also stresses the importance to support medium term solutions via primary production capacity and incomes, especially for agriculture, and providing inputs to improve the agricultural services sector in order to improve local availability of food to complement short term measures. The project consequently aims to establish foundations for a transition from emergency to life-sustaining intervention. TGH will thus, support emergency livelihood opportunities to strengthen the resources of the communities in Rural Damascus (Meidaa & Modira) and Hama (Harbanifse). The response will address both urgent and medium- term needs which is required to provide people safe and dignified living conditions during their displacement as well as in the frame of a permanent return.
To address the economic hindrance rural population is facing in Eastern Ghouta (EG) and rural Hama, TGH will provide to beneficiaries the means to invest in the 2020 winter season through agricultural inputs, via vouchers redeemable in local specific shops. This cash-based modality offers greater choice to beneficiaries and help to foster dignity in the assistance. Eligible items will be, but not limited to, high quality seeds of different crops, adapted fertilizers and chemicals etc. This intervention will contribute to diverting input expenditures to other items, such as machinery renting or fuel expenditure and thus will allow maximizing cultivated areas, given that workforce is not the limiting productive factor at the moment. In addition, home garden starter kits will be distributed– as recommended by Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) - to support transition from self-consumption to small scale commercial agriculture. These kits are made of high-quality vegetable seeds and adapted doses of fertilizers and chemical. This intervention will specifically target women within rural households as they are the one in charge of managing home gardens. Direct distribution will also ensure that the assistance will reach women and avoid issues related to difficulties of movement necessary under a voucher modality, as well as men overtaking decision processes in women-led livelihood activities.
Noteworthy is that, although not fully developed under this sector specific project description, the targeted locations have also been selected to be part of a more comprehensive response. They are thus also targeted by WASH, Education, Protection and Shelter activities under other projects. The synergy ensured by TGH between its several sectors of intervention aims at increasing the impact of the Food security activities described below.
Protection mainstreaming will be ensured; guaranteeing a meaningful access and a sound accountability, further fostered by the proposed community-based approach and protection risk analysis and mitigation measures in every step of the project.

4 - Needs

According to 2020 HNO, in Syria, in total, 9.8 millions of individuals are in need when it comes to food security. Moreover, this year the estimated number of people in need has increased by 8 percent whereas the wheat production is still 3.4 million metric tons short to meet domestic requirements. HH are facing difficulties in relaunching their livelihood activities, especially in the agricultural sector. The lack of cash availability hinders their investment capacities. In EG and in Hama, farmers resort to renting out their land to better-off farmers outside their village. This situation is creating a poverty trap, as farmers do not manage to generate enough income that would allow them to expand their cultivated areas during the next season. Throughout 2019, TGH on-field national staff based in Hama, Daraa and Damascus continuously monitored the needs in their areas. At the end of 2019, specific assessments were conducted which enables TGH to present a clear picture of the needs and opportunities. Farmers interrogated by TGH estimated that between 5 to 10 donum of wheat (0.5 to 1 ha) per HH is the minimum area to crop each year to create benefit. Moreover, the voucher modality has been preferred as agricultural inputs are available in sufficient quantity in local markets. Assessment carried out in the field by TGH confirmed the access and large number of agricultural input shops close to the targeted villages (Duma, Adra, Harbanifse). Regarding vegetable farming, it has been observed by TGH and then confirmed by the communities that in targeted areas, most HH are cropping vegetables in a home garden close by to their home. This cultivation is the responsibility of the women which contributes to the food diversification as well as the reduction of food-related expenditures. There is a strong potential of scale up. This intervention specifically targets women who are particularly impacted by the after-effects of the crisis.

5 - Implementation of activities

Act 2.1 Distribute agricultural inputs and provide related trainingAct.1.1: Provision of agricultural inputs for the 2020 winter season through voucher modalityThe action will support the most 400 vulnerable farmers of 3 villages with the procurement of agricultural input, specifically winter seeds, fertilizers and adapted chemicals, via vouchers of an amount of 80,000 SYP for the 2020 season. Standard vulnerability criteria and agricultural related criteria will be used. Shops (around 10 in each governorate) will be selected with advice from the community. The amount of 80,000 SYP per HH has been discussed during the assessment with communities. The aim is to provide the extra cash needed to reach a critical point for the farmers to produce enough to invest for the following season.2.2 Support small-scale food production (horticulture, poultry-egg laying hens, market gardens)Act.2.1: Provision of vegetable farming starter kits and consolidation kits to vulnerable womenIn Meida, AVSI Foundation targeted 119 women with vegetable farming kit for the 2019 winter season and will continue for 2020 summer season. TGH proposes to support 40 of those women for the 2020 winter seasons, with a vegetable farming consolidation kit (based on the MoA recommendations) as well as specific technical advice to scale up their production (selection on scaling-up potentiality criteria). Another 40 women will be supported by TGH with the same kits (selected on vulnerability criteria). In Modira and in Harbanifse, where no support was given in 2019, 220 vulnerable women will be selected supported.M&E: Regarding the voucher modality implemented by TGH, a specific follow-up mechanism will be set up. At HH levelf or vouchers and vegetable farming kits, a PDM will be carried out with the KoboCollect tool a month after the distribution to assess the satisfaction of the beneficiaries. At shop level, a continuous follow-up of the voucher use will be ensured.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A) Discrimination (or perception of it) in the service delivered between members of same communities and/or between neighboring communities. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: high. B) Risks of theft of assets and earnings (Likelihood: low; Impact: Medium) Mitigation for A) and B) risks: TGH will design and implement the project with systematic consultations with potential beneficiaries. TGH complaint mechanism will allow to receive feedbacks and identifying response options.C) Risks that prices will increase or exchange rates will fluctuate, thus making it more difficult for both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to access the same quantity and quality of goods and services (Likelihood: high; Impact: High) D) Risks that markets will be distorted, other than price distortion (Likelihood: high; .Impact: High). Mitigation for C) and D) risks: Market prices monitoring is ensured by TGH on a monthly basis. It enables TGH to have up-to date information and thus adjust the voucher amount according to the economic forecasts. E) Backlash from family or community members when women or girls start earning moneyMitigation: TGH will ensure consultation with potential beneficiaries and targeted individuals for proper design and understanding from all stakeholders of the activities purpose and beneficiaries’ selection criteria. TGH will ensure the confidentiality of the whistle blowing and complaint mechanism. F) Exploitation or abuse by staff members, service providers. Likelihood medium; Impact: high.Mitigation: TGH will request at least 2 years of experience and recommendation letter from previous client. A child safety code of conduct and will be signed by all actors involved in the implementation of the project. TGH will make sure its anti-abuse policy is endorsed and well-known by all staff.

TGH authorization to operate in Syria remains granted. Syrian laws ruling humanitarian organizations remain favorable. Field access remains granted.
