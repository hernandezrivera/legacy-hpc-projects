Project Summary

"IOM will undertake wide variety of activities to create an enabling environment for the Rohingya refugees towards eventual sustainable voluntary return and reintegration in Myanmar in the future when conditions may permit. Such activities will help Rohingya beneficiaries increase self-reliance skills. The livelihoods activities will also focus on the host communities affected by the influx by integrating income-generating livelihoods through support to agricultue and other sectors.
IOM will implement skills development projects to promote the economic resilience of Rohingya refugees in the camps. This training will enhance their capacity and help them utilize their skills in the camps in Bangladesh as self-reliance opportunities and can be used as transferrable skills by them upon return to Myanmar. Rohingya beneficiaries will receive skills training related and applicable to the humanitarian sector (e.g. health, wash, sanitation, site development-construction, etc.) to enable them earning income in the camps. After the end of the training IOM will facilitate in-kind asset support materials to Rohingya beneficiaries working outside the agricultural sector who are willing to gain basic skills or competencies but lack the physical assets to do so.
IOM will provide host communities with transferable and life skills training (e.g., computer skills, communication, negotiation, psychosocial) and vocational skills training including skills related to green businesses and green financing such as plumbing, ac repair, secretary, patient administration, catering to disaster resilient off-farming tools and methods, waste recycling, electronic refurbishment, financial literacy related to resilient investments and planning, etc. The trades will be identified by thorough skills and needs assessment exercises. This training will give scope to beneficiaries in the host community for income generation. Productive asset support will be given to women beneficiaries who have technical skills but lack inputs/opportunities/assets but are willing to earn a modest income. IOM will provide small business grants through Entrepreneurship Program to the existing Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) to help beneficiaries expand and strengthen their businesses. Support will be given for the establishment of women's cooperatives (such as livestock, handicrafts, etc.,). Cash for work activities will also be included as an income-generation activity in the host community.
To enhance the private sector engagement and promote market linkages, wage subsidy opportunities for beneficiaries will be implemented. To ensure sustainable income generation, emphasis will be given to activities like product fairs for/by the beneficiaries in the host community to sell and promote their products to individuals and wholesalers. A labour market assessment will be conducted to identify the needs of the beneficiaries based on which suitable activities will be selected. All these activities in both host, as well as refugee community, will be carried out by either implementing partners or implemented directly by IOM. IOM will ensure integration of various cross-cutting issues in the project design by identifying and including the most vulnerable beneficiaries through gender and disability-sensitive beneficiary selection criteria. The selection criteria of vulnerable women will be based on eligibility criteria such as female-headed households, income below the poverty line, widow, etc. IOM will also focus on disability throughout the beneficiary selection criteria and assist livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities. Women empowerment, leadership, and decision-making will be promoted by incorporating income opportunities and entrepreneurship opportunities, business development training, financial literacy, etc., for women in the Host Community and self-reliance opportunities for women in the camps to ensure sustainable development and integration into the society.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The socioeconomic condition of Bangladesh was able to withstand the shock of the COVID-19 pandemic through its diversified agricultural and skills-based income generation activities (Tackling the Covid-19 Social and Economic Crisis in Bangladesh, UNICEF, 2021). However, the price hike of daily commodities created another pressure on the day-to-day life of lower- and middle-income families to meet their household needs. The current food inflation rate is down to 8.2% in July 2022 from the highest of 8.6 in June 2022 (WFP VAM report, July 2022). However, the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic is still observed in many communities due to the unstable market chain causing price hike of daily commodities. Access to foods, shelters, and income generation activities are three main priorities according to the Joint Multipurpose Needs Assessment in December 2021.Despite many humanitarian and development activities in Teknaf and Ukhiya, the resilience and capacity index of these two Upazila are the lowest compared to Cox’s Bazar Sadar and Ramu (FAO Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2022). The women are the most vulnerable observed in the assessment. In Bangladesh, the provision of the social safety net is very limited. Around 33% of the host community population had access to social safety net according to the findings. The support were mostly dedicated during the COVID-19 pandemic, however, the trend declined during the price hike of daily commodities when this safety net was also a requirement for the sustainability of the vulnerable population. Bangladesh has a long history of sheltering Rohingya refugees in Myanmar. In 2017, millions of Rohingya were forced to seek refuge in Bangladesh after the brutal ethnic violence initiated by Myanmar. These displaced Rohingyas are currently living in different makeshift camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, waiting to return to their homeland. The total number of Rohingya in Bangladesh has increased from 767,324 (154,264 households) after the August 2017 influx to 901,703 (UNHCR population data, November 2021) in December 2021. These additional populations have a social, natural, environmental, legal, and financial impact on Bangladesh. The skills development support for the Rohingyas is mostly dependent on the humanitarian assistance provided by national and international donor organizations. The purpose of the support is to provide tailored vocational skills so that they can utilize the skills once they return to Myanmar. The overall vulnerability of the Rohingya population has increased since 2017 (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment 2022). The prevailing dual crisis of the COVID-19 pandemic and the war between Russia and Ukraine leading to instability of global markets and reducing the purchasing power of both communities are some of the causes for increasing vulnerabilities. The study finds that food is still the highest number of expenditures incurred for both the host and Rohingya community. The food expenditure for the Rohingya community has grown at an alarming rate due to the increased population and lower resources. Besides, the income opportunity in the camp is very limited. Only 30 per cent of income came from work-based income sources in the Rohingya camps compared to 91 per cent for the host community.Nevertheless, the resilience of women is also low. According to the latest report from FAO (Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2022), women have the lowest resilience capacity index compared to men in both the Host and Rohingya community. To ensure equal opportunity for women and increase their empowerment, IOM will take wide ranges of activities focusing on women both in the camps and host communities. Persons with disabilities will also be included to enable them to contribute to society and family so that they can be considered as resources rather than burdens for their families.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

IOM will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. “Do no harm” is the guiding principle for implementing the activities for the vulnerable people. The privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be considered while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness-raising sessions to protect them from any unwanted incidents. IOM will deliver services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, that are culturally appropriate, and that avoid any unintended negative consequences. IOM will provide assistance and services according to needs and not based on age, sex, gender, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance, and so on. Services and assistance are provided on an adequate scale, within safe and easy reach, are known by the affected individuals, and are accessible by all groups including medical cases, disabled individuals, discriminated groups, and the like

3.2 - Gender

IOM activities will be targeted for all gender in the targeted camps irrespective to their race and ethnicity. The needs assessments will identify the specific needs of different gender and provide tailored support to them. IOM will institutionalize and integrate gender into the entire spectrum of activities -projects, programmes, and staff policies. For gender issues to be fully integrated into the Organization’s vision, all staff need to understand and implement the Strategy in their work. This mainstreaming process is not exclusively related to women and their specific needs and concerns. It also includes initiatives for men when needed, provided they promote overall gender equality. IOM will target 50% men and 50% women in the program activities. Gender sensitive training centers, equipment’s, trades will be provided to the beneficiaries. Since women are left behind in many societies, IOM will specially focus to increase women empowerment and leadership through out the program.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

IOM plans to include youth and adult community under the direct beneficiary of the self-reliance and livelihoods activities. However, the activities will indirectly benefit all the age groups of the benefited household. The interventions will be designed considering the safeguard of the children. The training centers will be equipped with breast feeding corner, child friendly space so that their mother can take the training courses while the children can safely stay with them.

3.4 - Diversity

All the activities planned for the year will consider people from different races and ethnicity. The activities will try to benefit all the beneficiaries in need. IOM will specially consider the minority people in the community to mainstream them into the society. The activities will be tailored to their specific needs and skills based on the outcome of the needs assessment.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"Accountability remains a vital and integral component of this proposed project. During the needs assessment, the affected populations in all the targeted locations will be consulted for their needs and priorities. They will also be given the opportunity to contribute to the design and planning of trade selection, opportunities, market analysis, with support from IOM. The affected people will take a lead in making decisions on their roles, local resources mobilization, and livelihoods/ self reliance skills considering safety, security, and gender dynamics. Affected women and girls will be consulted, in addition to and separately from men and boys, about trades and skills. IOM and the partners will establish a complaint feedback mechanism at the project sites to receive regular feedback from the beneficiaries regarding the activities. The privacy of the beneficiaries will be ensured and the response to their feedback will be delivered regularly.
