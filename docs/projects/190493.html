1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

In 2022, OCHA Mozambique will focus on the following priorities: Ensuring strategic and operational humanitarian coordination of the humanitarian response to the conflict in northern Mozambique. Through the existing coordination structures and mechanisms that OCHA leads and/or supports (e.g. the Area Humanitarian Country Team, Inter-Cluster Coordination Group, District Coordination meetings, etc. in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa), the country office will ensure timely and effective coordination of a principled humanitarian response in northern Mozambique, in the framework of the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Facilitating Civil-Military Coordination and supporting dialogue on safe and unimpeded humanitarian access. Following the intervention of multiple foreign forces in Mozambique from July 2021 onwards, Civil-Military Coordination (CMCoord) has become even more vital, and OCHA expects to play a leading role on CMCoord, in accordance with its global mandate. At the same time, with the conflict still highly fluid and complex, humanitarian access discussions and dialogue will be vital. To this end, OCHA convenes and leads regular Humanitarian Access Working Group meetings, and has established a Civil-Military Advisory Group which will continue its work in 2022. Ensuring effective and timely emergency preparedness activities are implemented in preparation for the rainy/cyclonic seasons in 2021/2022 and 2022/2023, including through the finalization of a risk-based HCT Contingency Plan (closely aligned to the Government’s National Contingency Plan, which OCHA also supports the development of); a harmonized multi-sectoral initial rapid needs assessment tool (MRA) jointly agreed with national authorities and humanitarian partners; and, the regular collection of key information regarding the availability of in-country emergency stocks, including critical gaps. Developing and disseminating regular high-quality information products in order to inform humanitarian decision-making and advocacy (including snapshots, dashboards, maps, 5Ws, etc.), including based on analysis of needs, response and gaps. Throughout the conflict in Mozambique, OCHA has played a critical role in heightening the profile of the crisis, as well as ensuring that key partners (including Government, UN Agencies, NGOs and donors) have all the necessary information available in a timely manner for decision making and high-level advocacy. Galvanizing resources for the implementation of the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and preparing the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). The 2022 HRP is expected to be launched in January 2022 and OCHA will support key resource mobilization activities and advocacy efforts through local and international donors, including the Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF), in line with the prioritization set out in the HRP. Mainstreaming critical cross-cutting issues—including PSEA, gender-, age-, HIV- and disability-sensitive response, community engagement and centrality of protection—into the humanitarian response, by leveraging each of OCHA’s core functions (operational coordination, information management, humanitarian financing and advocacy). OCHA plays an active role in the PSEA Network and recently brought on board additional PSEA Coordination capacity in Pemba for December 2021 – February 2022. OCHA also actively engages in the gender focal points network and works proactively with the Protection Cluster to ensure that protection is central to all discussions in the HCT and ICCG at both national and Cabo Delgado levels. In the development of humanitarian needs analysis and response plans, OCHA ROSEA ensures disaggregation of people in need by gender, age and unique needs (e.g. people with disabilities, people living with HIV). 5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

[10:13 am] Sergio DinoiOCHA is committed to implementing the United Nation’s zero-tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). OCHA follows the applicable UN Secretariat rules and regulations on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), including the Secretary General’s Bulletin on Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13); the Secretary General’s Bulletin on Protection against retaliation for reporting misconduct (ST/SGB/2017/1); and other relevant PSEA initiatives from the Secretary General. In addition, OCHA has developed an internal Standard Operating Procedure describing to OCHA staff the arrangements for receiving complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. The SoPs define roles and responsibilities for PSEA. In field offices, the OCHA Head of Office is the OCHA PSEA focal point. All OCHA staff are responsible for completing mandatory trainings on PSEA; adhering to procedures outlined in the SoP; and reporting on SEA that they have witnessed or have a good faith suspicion.
