Project Summary

To reduce morbidity and mortality among vulnerable girls, boys, men and women in emergency situations by providing access to quality essential primary healthcare, including reproductive health. To support communities and the MoH to prevent, detect and respond to disease outbreaks.

Needs

• 382,900 excess deaths have been attributed to the crisis in South Sudan, between December 2013 and April 2018 (LSHTM 2018).• At least one in ten health facilities have been directly affected by the conflict, only 22% of facilities are fully operational, and more than half of facilities are functioning at only 10% of their capacity (HNO 2018). At least 44% of the population do not have access to health services (HNO 2017).• As of July 2018, 1.96 million people were internally displaced (draft HRP 2019), living in formal and informal settlements, with host communities and in hard-to-reach areas.• In 2018, the leading causes of morbidity reported have been malaria, 62%, respiratory infections, 10.5%, and diarrhoea 13.7% (IDSR annex w41).• Only 26% of children in South Sudan are fully vaccinated (UNICEF/WHO 2017). Disease outbreaks in 2017-18 have included Cholera, Measles, Malaria, Hepatitis E, Kala azar, Guinea worm, Rift Valley Fever, Rubella, Cutaneous Anthrax, Meningitis and probable Rabies (HNO 2018, IDSR).• Estimated maternal mortality is 789/100,000 live births (WHO 2015), with only 14.7% of births with SBA (UNFPA 2018), reflecting a critical shortage of BEmONC and CEmONC facilities across the country. Country-wide, there continue to be recurrent, acute public health emergencies, requiring flexible provision of both integrated and health-specific rapid responses by dedicated emergency response teams. In Renk, sporadic insecurity and the economic crisis have led to the progressive collapse of the health system and a nutritional emergency. Medair’s SMART survey in 2018 demonstrated a GAM of 26.9% in the host community and 22.8% among people living in the displacement sites. The MoH PHCUs are either closed or barely functioning; there are no fully operational PHCCs, no fully functioning referral facilities for secondary care, and no primary health care facilities across the county with capacity for all required inpatient admissions. In Leer, the absence of effective public services leaves the population at significant risk of communicable diseases and preventable deaths. There are still no options for secondary health care referral and existing health resources are insufficient to meet the basic needs of highly vulnerable groups (e.g. severely malnourished children). In the Sept 2018 IPC, Leer was projected to have some populations in IPC phase 5 (catastrophe), until at least March 2019 (the beginning of the lean season) highlighting the particular vulnerability of this community. In Aweil Centre, the significant health needs, compounded by an underlying nutrition emergency, were highlighted by a measles outbreak in February 2018, to which Medair responded with an emergency vaccination campaign. There remains insufficient access to curative and preventive primary healthcare services, especially for highly vulnerable groups such as malnourished children, and inadequate provision of basic reproductive healthcare across this area.

Activities

Through this project, Medair will maintain an emergency response team that can provide integrated multi-sector and health-specific emergency responses across South Sudan. The emergency response health team will assess and respond to public health emergencies, including disease outbreaks, underlying health contributors to nutritional emergencies, and emergency health needs associated with acutely displaced and other vulnerable populations, including in hard-to-reach locations and camp settings, such as PoCs. Emergency interventions will be designed to last for a maximum of 3 months before handing over to other actors if required. Medair will maintain the flexibility to provide rapid responses using different modalities appropriate to the context, such as temporary or mobile clinics, emergency PHC services, iCCM (including SAM management where appropriate), and supporting longer-term health partners to restore PHC services at fixed sites in conflict-affected locations. In Renk, Medair will continue to address the most critical primary health care gaps through provision of at least two emergency primary health care facilities in displacement sites, such as Abayok and Wunthou, and iCCM to link with primary health care clinics for populations with more limited access to health facilities. Medair will continue providing 24-hour reproductive health care services as well as inpatient care for children with severe acute malnutrition and medical complications at the stabilisation centre in Abayok. In Leer, given ongoing insecurity and frequent population displacements, Medair will implement a flexible model of iCCM to treat the main diseases and provide other services, such as treatment of SGBV and mental health and psychosocial support. In Aweil, Medair—in close coordination with the HSS partner—will integrate an essential package of health services into its nutrition programming. Medair will train flexible health surge staff on IECHC, as well as ANC/PNC, provision of LLINs and vaccinations so that malnourished PLW and children under 5 presenting on nutrition clinic days have access to regularly supervised treatment for the main causes of morbidity and mortality. Medair will integrate ANC, PNC and clean delivery kit distributions into emergency nutrition programming in areas where these services are not available at the nearest health facility.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback similarly assimilated. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to update them regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Volunteer networks are used to disseminate outbreak-related messaging in emergency responses and health facility promotors provide information to those attending Medair’s facilities. Medair recruits staff locally wherever possible, equipping community members with the knowledge and skills to treat their own communities ongoing.When the emergency response team deploys for an assessment, they initially meet with community leaders to inform them about the objectives of the assessment as well as the fact that the assessment does not automatically result in an intervention. The same message is consistently shared during the assessment at HH level, as well as during FGDs and other meetings held. Based on the needs found in the assessment, Medair will decide in collaboration with the cluster whether an intervention is appropriate. Local authorities and community leaders will be informed about the outcome. In case an emergency intervention will be conducted, continuous messaging through a mixture of channels as mentioned above will ensure the communities are well informed.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Medair routinely undertakes ‘exit interviews’ as part of health programming, proactively eliciting feedback from individual male and female beneficiaries. In addition, regular meetings with communities (including FGD) are undertaken throughout interventions and feedback mechanisms including a beneficiary telephone hotline are implemented where appropriate.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback gathered through these Care Groups. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to elicit their opinions and advice regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Medair recruits staff locally wherever possible, creating a network of local contacts from which community feedback is sought from both men and women. Additionally, Medair conducts regular ‘knowledge, practice and coverage’ evaluations, using local staff to conduct these exercises which elicit information from communities regarding health needs and behaviours. Barrier analysis is utilised to engage community participation in overcoming obstacles to accessing services and practicing healthy behaviours.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Medair will monitor the age and gender breakdown of those accessing health care services, comparing these with expected breakdowns based on demographic statistics. If discrepancies are found, Medair will use focus group discussions and community meetings to understand, address and mitigate access barriers.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

As a health provider, particularly with regard to the most vulnerable groups, such as survivors of SGBV, Medair ensures that beneficiary confidentiality is maintained appropriately. Medair liaises with existing protection partners in all locations to identify and refer cases requiring specialised protection support. Medair monitors beneficiary perception of safety through use of a specialised protection mainstreaming questionnaire in all emergency interventions.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity is one of Medair’s values. As such, it is an organisational priority in all interventions and staff are trained to understand what this means in how to treat beneficiaries. Examples of this include providing private spaces in clinics for clinical examination, confidential counselling spaces in clinics for those presenting with SGBV, and using screens to provide privacy for patients within cholera treatment units. Medair monitors beneficiary perception of dignity through use of a specialised protection mainstreaming questionnaire in all emergency interventions.
