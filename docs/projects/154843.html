Project Summary

This project seeks to capitalize on the capacities and local presence of each consortium member: Leading this consortium is Mission East, an international NGO, working in Iraq in 2003 and again since 2014 until now. Mission East has a strong presence in Kirkuk and Ninewa. ME has implemented a livelihoods/food security project in Ninewa for 2 years,. ME has mobile case management teams (WPU) handling GBV and (FPU) handling CP cases. ME operate multipurpose community centres and strengthens capacity of community protection structures, such as local grassroots and volunteer groups of women and youth. ME has been providing comprehensive WASH services to more than 20 villages since 2016. ME has an environmental policy in which Water conservation and environmental awareness is central to WASH programming.
Mercy Hands is Iraq's biggest national NGO with a country wide presence. Established in 2003, Mercy Hands has projects in most of the governorates of Iraq. Relevant to this project MH have offices and activities in Tikrit, Mosul, Baghdad and Erbil. They have strong multi-sector capacities, with experience in: Food security, livelihoods, WASH, CP, GBV, Education, NFI, health centres, legal assistance and cash transfer.
The Critical Needs Support Foundation (CNSF) is a rapidly growing national NGO which specializes in Shelter, WASH and cash transfer responses. CNSF conducts multiple cash transfer projects across Ninewa, for various sectors. CNSF targets marginalized communities especially in Ninewa. CNSF have completed a successful WASH project in Mosul which included distributing hygiene kits, water trucking to 6500 households and installation of water tanks at 26 schools. CNSF have a strong presence in Ninewa and Anbar, they have a country office in Erbil and Baghdad.
This consortium will adopt a harmonized approach across two governorates, integrating six activities from two sectors in each location to deliver an integrated programme addressing some of the root causes of the vulnerability of families. This is designed to build on each organization's strengths and experience in Iraq with a specific focus on localization, following Mission East’s strategy for the coming 3 years to build the capacity of national NGO and local volunteer groups.
In each target location a fully integrated double-sector response will be implemented in targeted, rural and per-urban, under-served returnee locations. WASH and Child Protection (CP) will be holistically addressed. The program is designed to provide synergies between the sectorial responses to support returnee needs in a rounded and comprehensive way. This will be implemented through pre-existing community protection structures which will be trained to support child protection services including parenting skills training. In all locations mobile child protection case management teams will operate alongside outreach teams who will deliver multi-sector key messaging, covering topics on hygiene promotion, water conservation and child protection. Parenting skills packages will be delivered in each target location with the support of pre-existing community groups. This integrated package will be implemented in under-served returnee locations between Samara district, Sinjar and Telafar,. Targeting a total of 9,000 people.

Needs

2018 has seen the humanitarian crisis in Iraq has seen significant on-going needs across all sectors. It is estimated that 6.5 million people are currently in need of assistance, the HRP 2019 targets 1.75 million with some form of humanitarian assistance. Approximately 500,000 returnees are in locations with very high and high severity of conditions mostly in Anbar, Baghdad, Diyala, Erbil, Kirkuk, Ninewa and Salah al-Din governorates. Their priority needs are in lack of basic services as well as challenges to social cohesion and safety which are also preventing IDP’s from returning to their areas of origin. The WASH cluster priorities; restoration of basic WASH services and support good hygiene practices for highly vulnerable populations in areas of return and facilitate the takeover of water and sanitation facilities by local authorities (DoW). The WASH Cluster have identified WASH needs in Tikrit and surrounding areas , 85,769 target returnees and 7,612 target OOC IDPs; in Talafar, 45,683 target returnees; in Sinjar 8,437 target returnees. An estimated 2.3 million people across Iraq remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to safe and appropriate WASH services and lack of proper wastewater treatment facilities.The CP Sub-Cluster expects around 35,000 extremely vulnerable children in need of case management services in places of returns, Among them, children with perceived affiliation to extremist groups, as well as children subject to exploitation or abuse. Inadequate funding for case management services has been identified as a major bottleneck, coupled with the continuous need to train case management staff in the new operational areas. -Ninewa: Sinjar 2,000 individuals with multi-sector needs [REACH IRQ RASP NINEWA August 2018] [REACH Situation overview, ROAR NINEWA - comparative report September 2018] [UNHCR/ DRC POPULATION RETURN TRENDS, PROTECTION, AND SOCIAL DYNAMICS IN NORTHERN NINEWA, IRAQ ] -Ninewa: Tel Afar 1,000 individuals with multi-sector needs [REACH IRQ RASP NINEWA August 2018] [REACH Situation overview, joint Rapid Assessment of Markets (JRAM) Telafar September 2018] [REACH Situation overview, ROAR NINEWA - comparative report September 2018] [UNHCR/ DRC POPULATION RETURN TRENDS, PROTECTION, AND SOCIAL DYNAMICS IN NORTHERN NINEWA, IRAQ ] -Salah-Al-Din: Tikrit 1,000 individuals with multi-sector needs [REACH IRQ RASP SALA-AL-DIN August 2018]

Activities

In areas of return, municipality water networks will be rehabilitated to provide safe drinking water along with wastewater systems. Site assessments will be jointly conducted with engineers of the Directorate of Water and the final BoQ will receive approval of the Governorate’s office. The resulting rehabilitated water supply will meet SPHERE standards, receive validation upon handover and be tested over at least two monitoring periods, with the Directorate of Water leading on the testing of water quality. Technical monitoring on output (flow rate, pressure, etc.). The wastewater network will be engineered to recycle grey water and drainage water so that it can be utilized as an agricultural input for greenhouses (installed under other projects ). Where possible WASH and wastewater channels will utilize local labor in the form of CFW with pre-exiting community mechanisms such as youth groups to rehabilitated water/waste networks with supervised grassroots and volunteer groups. Due to the complexity of some of the works anticipated (as per prior experience), some components of the rehabilitation, which cannot be implemented through cash for work, will be contracted to a local engineering firm. In the same vicinity, outreach teams will be trained to deliver key messages from both sectors to households, on topics including hygiene promotion, water conservation and child protection principles. The child protection awareness raising includes child rights, risks including physical, emotional abuse, dangers and injuries, physical violence and other harmful practices; sexual violence including early marriage; psycho-social distress; children associated with armed actors; child labour; justice for children. This awareness raising will help communities identify, refer and advocate for vulnerable children. This project will strengthen and build the capacity of community protection mechanisms, by training grassroots and volunteer groups to deliver Parenting skills circular for caregivers of children and of adolescents, as a cross cutting measure, the parenting skills programme will incorporate hygiene promotion and water conservation messaging. Protection will be mainstreamed throughout all of these activities, everybody involved in both sectors in all 6 activities will be trained to identify and refer child protection cases. In all locations, there will a mobile child protection case management team. All of these activities (Water and waste network repair, Hygine promotion, CP Case Management, Parenting, and strengthening community protection structures) will be implemented in un-served peri-urban locations in Salahadin in Samara district (2 locations), in Ninewa (2 locations in TelAfar, and 2 in Sinjar). However in Sinjar the child protection component will be omitted from this project based on lower needs and as Mission East has a pre-existing child protection project in Sinjar and will be linked to this project.Mission East is the Technical Lead of this consortium for both WASH and Child protection, ME will help design the program based on their technical expertise, use of guidelines and standards and experience. The Implementation will be conducted by the organization who has the operational presence in that area. If they lack specific required skills the technical lead can support implementation by providing training or the skill set needed. For quality reasons, Mission East will be directly responsible for implementing case management and strengthening community protection structures in all locations.Mission East will directly implement hygiene promotion water system repair and wastewater management in Sinjar (2 locations) and TelAfar (1 location). Mercy Hands will implement Hygiene Promotion, water system repair and wastewater management in Samara (2 locations). CNSF will implement hygiene promotion water system repair and wastewater management in 1 location in TelAfar. Mission East will directly implement case management in all locations, Mercy Hands will implement parenting in all locations, strengthening community protection structures will be implemented by Mercy Hands in Slahadin and Mission East in Ninewa.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Mission East is a certified member of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) Alliance and has stated its commitment to upholding the CHS and maintaining its certification. This means being committed to communication with beneficiaries and promoting beneficiary feedback at all stages of the project cycle, from needs assessment to project evaluation, our Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team remains committed to ensuring beneficiary accountability throughout the project. Accountability to the affected population can be broken down into the following commitments as listed below along with how they are put into practice: 1. Enable improved participation: regular opportunity for the local community to inﬂuence relevant decisions including inﬂuence the Consortium's policies, priorities, and actions at local, national level. 2. Information Provision: -Regular, timely and accessible information sharing with beneﬁciaries as well as stakeholders including government and the local community -Communicating in Arabic, and Kurdish respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of Iraqi society, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups in Ninewa and Slahhadin. - Utilizing pre-existing communication structures relevant to the Iraqi society. 3. Feedback and complaint mechanism: -Accessible and appropriate ways to different affected groups to access the formal feedback and complaints mechanism including emails, phone, face to face, text etc. -Regular review of barriers to giving feedback from the community. 4. Leadership and governance: -Mission East Code of Conduct is signed by all staff, volunteers and partners working on this project. -Mission East Child Safeguarding policy is signed by all staff, volunteers and partners working on this project as well as training of them. -Regular CHS training for Mission East, Mercy Hands and CNSF Staff. 5. Transparency: -Sharing information about Consortium successes and failures more openly, to help promote a system-wide culture of openness and accountability. 6. Design, monitoring and Evaluation: -MoUs with local grassroots/ voluntary groups at the village level to ensure relevance of programme and accountability to the community. Mission East has feedback and complaints mechanisms integrated into its ongoing programming and each new activity. There is a designated phone line for complaints and a station for complaints or a member of the MEAL team present at each distribution site. The M&E Officer, with support from the MEAL Manager, will take responsibility for ensuring that complaints and suggestions from beneficiaries are received, processed and documented in a monthly report. These will impact program adjustments and future design wherever necessary. The MEAL team will employ a variety of monitoring methodologies and processes to ensure proper implementation and target achievements with feedback to management at all levels. Beneficiaries will be allowed to their comments and concerns to help Mission East improve modalities and approach to implementation wherever and whenever needed. This will also serve as an opportunity to check that no unintended harm was caused by these project activities. Information about Mission East and our contact details will be made available to all beneficiaries at all target locations.
