Project Summary

According 2019 HRP indicates that 1 in 2 people in Syria are potentially at risk from the threat of explosive hazards. Based on current population estimations, 10.2 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 1,980 communities reporting explosive contamination in the last two years . These communities are spread throughout the country and correlate to areas that have seen most explosive incidents over the past 12 months. Men, women, and children, especially young boys, are exposed to the threat of death or debilitating and life-shattering injuries from explosive hazards on a daily basis.Based on available data from where collection has been possible, on average 1.5 people are killed and 2 are injured in explosive hazards accidents, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation.
The social and economic impact of this explosive contamination will last for generations. Survivors of explosive hazard incidents often suffer physical, emotional and psychological injury, economic loss which impacts families and communities. Most importantly, explosive hazards in Syria will also jeopardize the safe return of refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs) given their lack of familiarity with resulting contamination.
Mitigating the impact of explosive hazards on civilians in Syria is therefore a major protection priority. It is with this in mind NAS is planning to implement this ten-month project to drastically reduce the problems that civilians regularly encounter with land mines and explosives.
This project consists of two main interventions:
A- Conduct community awareness on threats posed by unexploded remnants of war (UXOs)
NAS will conduct awareness raising activities on Explosive Remnants of War (ERW). Two mobile teams (5 each) will be deployed to reach beneficiaries with messages in schools, IDP camps, collective centers as well as IDPs on the move. Targeted people will be equipped with improved knowledge on the risk presented by unexploded remnants of war through mine risk education in schools, community centers and open spaces in IDP Communities School and community-based risk education will be delivered to targeted population to generate behaviour change that minimizes death and injuries from landmines and remnants of war.
B- Conduct Contamination Impact Survey:
Increased awareness of contaminated or potentially hazardous areas through survey, contamination reporting and mapping, as well as the marking of explosive hazards to reduce accidents, increase community resilience, and enhance sense of local security, CIS will be conducted through the following steps:
- Desk assessment of available information. Information will be collected from past records; seeking information from central institution and other sources of information such as the police, military, hospitals, local authorities, other local groups, etc.;
- Visit the targeted areas and meet with women, girls, boys, men, and other stakeholders in the local community. conduct interviews and inspect, from a safe distance, any areas that may be contaminated by mines/ explosive remnants of war;
- Analyse the information in order to distinguish between irrelevant information and important evidence.
- Mapping and Marking unsafe areas.

Needs

According 2019 HRP ,Assessments Indicates that 1 in 2 people in Syria are potentially at risk from the threat of explosive hazards. Based on current population estimations, 10.2 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 1,980 communities reporting explosive contamination in the last two years,Based on available data from where collection has been possible, on average 1.5 people are killed and 2 are injured in explosive hazards accidents, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation.The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating, with the loss of community members, an increased number of people with permanent injuries and loss of access to properties, basic services and livelihoods. It also prevents safe delivery of humanitarian aid, affects population movement and limits the scope for recovery and reconstruction efforts. A wide range of infrastructure, such as private property, schools, hospital.Idleb Governorate has seen intense shelling and fighting among armed actors, and resulting in high level of landmine, UXO and IED contaminations. The use of cluster bombs is also reported in the area. Even some areas that are currently not under attack its land tends to be contaminated from the past shelling and battles.Mine/ERW contamination impedes people’s right to move safely and to access livelihood, such as farming. Many people, both host communities and IDPs, are unaware of the risky behaviors that can lead to mine/ERW accidents, while some are forced to engage in dangerous activities in contaminated areas. Among the “unaware” people living in affected areas, children are most at risk of mine/ERW accidents due to their curiosity. UNICEF (2015) suggests children’s risky behavior of playing in contaminated areas. Through its RE project in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate, NAS has found that many children pride themselves on touching and collecting ERWs, and are unaware of risks. They also tend to pay more attention and listen to RE experts who come from outside than parents and teachers. Women and men, especially IDPs, also face significant risk. IDP families seeking shelter or travelling through unknown areas may behave in a risky way without knowing. In addition, upon their return to the communities of origin, the risk of accidents during reconstruction and rehabilitation is immense as mines and ERWs may be buried under rubbles, in their farmland, and on the way to school. As it is extremely difficult for the returnees to accept they cannot simply go back to their lives before the war, it is essential to raise awareness about the lingering effects of mines/ERW. Among people “forced” to take risky behaviors, poor families are reported to be scavenging in contaminated areas to find explosive devices for economic profit or scavenging garbage for any flammables for heating and cooking. Children primarily engage in such dangerous activities . Under such circumstances, well contextualised RE will be a lifesaving tool to girls, boys, women and men. Despite anecdotal evidence suggesting significant impact of mines and ERWs on civilians in north western Syria, its true extent is unknown due to lack of comprehensive data, hindering mine action actors and other stakeholders from implementing effective mine action activities. The local authorities and medical facilities have patchy records of casualties, and casualties of the conflict are documented as “war casualties”. The cause of injuries and deaths, whether it be aerial strike or mine accident, is not recorded. According to OCHA (2015), the lack of care for persons with disabilities including rehabilitation is a major health problem. Identification of the survivors would be the first step for delivering assistance.The proposed project with two main components, 1) Risk Education and 2) Survey on casualties of mine/ERW accidents, fits the Protection sector objective “Reduce the impact of explosive hazards.

Implementation of activities

NAS is an independent non-profit NGO which has been working for the last 4 years in Mine Action awareness campaigns. With our expertise in Mine Action and risk education, we are planning to implement a ten-month contamination impact survey with awareness raising project in Syria. This project plans to implement a contamination impact survey and a series of awareness campaigns throughout Northern Syria specifically in the north countryside of Aleppo and the western countryside of Idlib to combat the lack of knowledge that people have of the dangers of landmines and explosive remnants of war present in their communities. In addition, NAS will increase knowledge on child protection issues in the community. The project will use videos, presentations and trained teachers, to reach communities and children in schools with messages about the hazards of mines and explosives, how to recognize them, and what to do when they are encountered. Awareness messages on various protection concerns affecting children due to the conflict, will also be disseminated.1.1 Training of 10 Trainers (TOT) on RE : NAS trainers will be trained to equip them with sufficient knowledge and skills to conduct Training of Facilitators (TOF).1.2 Train 14 EWR protection network members and 10 of NAS teams Facilitators : Facilitators (community volunteers) in Idleb and Aleppo will be trained by NAS trainer to provide community based Risk Education sessions. In principle, facilitators will be hired from local community to promote community participation and root RE knowledge among community members.1.3 RE sessions: Facilitators (community volunteers) will provide RE sessions to women, men, girls and boys, including returnees and IDPs in target communities to raise awareness about the risks of explosives and promote positive behavioral changes. Efforts to target more children will be made to reduce risks of accidents caused by their curiosity. Session contents will be developed and customized considering local context, and it will emphasis on “stop, warn, and report” as a key message. RE team will coordinate with local authority including Local Council and Local Relief organization to obtain consent to RE activity from target community.2.1 Training of 8 officers on data collection : Train five teams (4 female teams, 4 male teams) of data collection officers on the survey methods and techniques2.2 Casualty Survey: Carry out casualty survey in the target location. Data collection team will gather information about mine/ ERW casualties from local councils, local reliefs, hospitals and clinics in the target areas, and visit each casualty or his/her family for in depth interview. Female team will be responsible for children and female casualties, and male team will be for other casualties. In principle, data collectors will be hired from local community to promote community participation. Before carrying out casualty survey, data collector team will visit target villages and coordinate with local authority to introduce objective of the survey by highlighting humanitarian priorities. 2.3 Data analysis: Analyze data and produce a report on the accident details, geographical concentration and needs of casualties, and share it with other mine action partners, health cluster and other relevant stakeholders.With regard to monitoring of casualty survey activity, NAS’s trainers and field teams of NNGO will have bi weekly meeting either in Syria or via skype to share project progress and to discuss any improvement necessary for ensuring the quality of the project. The trainees of NNGO will be tested in the form of demonstration of casualty survey interview.The project will be monitored on a regular basis in order to ensure satisfactory monthly progress and accountability to the beneficiaries including collection of monthly data from beneficiaries, monthly technical reports and regular technical supervision missions fielded as required to enhance success.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: Weakness of interaction of the beneficiaries of the activities. Mitigation: Preparing participatory activities. Risks: The survey team might face a mine injury during the survey. Mitigation: Follow the safety and security procedures. Risks: The targeted beneficiaries are not aware of the importance of the activities being provided. Mitigation: The importance of activities will be announced in cooperation between mobile teams and local councils within the targeted villages. Risks: Airstrikes and bombed cars might happen in Idleb area. Mitigation: security staff will do a daily, weekly and monthly assessment of the security situation to ensure the security of staff and beneficiaries. Activities may be delayed when situation is unsafe. Risks: Cultural background and norms could be sometimes as challenges against the achievement of the outputs. Mitigation: NAS team will conduct focus group discussion with all beneficiaries to explain that the activities of this project are not against the norms and culture of the target communities. Risks :Survey teams may be perceived as militants or affiliated with an armed group. Mitigation :To mitigate the risk, the following measures will be taken:Teams will use clear and visible identification to mark the teams as humanitarian workers; Teams will minimize non essential interaction and exposure with armed factions; Teams will avoid use of equipment that could be construed as dual military in nature.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one of the major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conflict in northern Syria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2- Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries. 3- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensified shelling 4- Daily Risk Assessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding the crowding of beneficiaries during activities. B-Low Quality of Programming/ This includes limitations on direct monitoring, statistical data, percentages, samples and tight data related to gender, age or protection issues etc.This is the setting for a decline in program quality unless remote management tools are accurately employed.
