Project Summary

The SERP project is an integrated project that will directly contribute to support the local community by linking education services (70%) to the protection sector (30%) to provide a safer environment for children. The project contributes to qualitative and integrated education services, also contributes towards maintaining and enhancing equitable access to services.
This project will be implemented in Northwest Syria, Idleb in Dana sub-district (Atmeh, Qah, Sarmada). Locations have been identified based on accessibility, however, this might change due to situation on the ground.
The education objective is to increase access and quality of education to IDP children and host communities. SERP intends to cover the current gap, as the schools are operating without any secured fund.(104 classrooms, 5,140 students (2,264 boys and 2,876 girls) will be targeted.
The main goal of this project is to scale up safe and equitable access to formal\ non-formal education for school-aged girls and boys between ages 5-17 within a protective environment by establishing child friendly space inside the targeted 6 schools as a continuation of the already completed OCHA funded project. As per the following activities:
1. Provide operational and running costs (such as fuel for heating, salaries) for the targeted schools, hygiene and classroom’s stationary which includes adapted tools for children with disabilities;
2. Minor rehabilitation and maintenance of classrooms which includes ensuring accessibility for children with disabilities;
3. Provide school furniture and safety equipment, including white boards, desks, equipment, etc.;
4. Conduct trainings for teachers and education personnel; Teachers in Crisis Context (TICC), PSS, Safety trainings and training on working with children with disabilities;
5. Provision of non-formal education: Accelerated learning programs (ALP), Basic Literacy and Numeracy (BLN), Catch-up classes, self-learning program (SLP), Remedial classes, support to ensure that these programs are accessible for children with learning and intellectual disabilities;
6. Provide education kit (school bag and stationary) for children which can include adapted devices to enable learning for children with disabilities and kits for teachers;
7. Provide recreational materials which are diverse to ensure that materials are accessible for all children;
8. Payment of incentives to teachers and education personnel;
9. Provision of psychosocial support for children in both formal and non-formal schools which is accessible for all children;
10. Referral of children to relevant services (Health, Nutrition, mental support, etc.).
Under the SERP project, we will extend the essential child protection services in the targeted locations to provide:
-Specialized and non-specialized protection services, including Psychological First Aid (PFA), Psychological Support Services (PSS), awareness raising activities, referrals, direct support and strengthening referral pathways.
Community-based protection initiatives, such as Women’s Committees and Child Protection Networks.
While we will maintain our existing essential services, our teams will also be able to absorb additional beneficiaries if there is an acute emergency and activities need to be diverted to large numbers of new IDPs. This proposal therefore presents a comprehensive protection CP and education program. SoP will implement this project directly, drawing on our existing operational presence, relationships with local authorities, and our capable technical staff.

4 - Needs

According to UN OCHA, Recent Developments in Northwestern Syria Situation Report No.7 - as of 29 January 2020, 2 million Internally displaced people in northwest Syria, Since 1 December 2019, nearly 390,000 people – around 80% of whom are women and children – were displaced in northwest Syria, 2.7 million People in need of humanitarian assistance in northwest Syria. On 27 January 2020, SOP conducted a rapid needs assessment in the targeted locations, the Data Collection Methods Used were observation checklists and questionnaires together with FGDs. In this assessment form, the focused discussions mainly were on the barriers to accessing and participating in education, and ways that they could be diminished, when asked about the educational needs in the proposed schools to be targeted, the results have indicated that the first need is to add the number of classrooms and to provide financial support to cover the salaries of teachers. The second requirement is to provide the schools with the required furniture from desks, chairs and others. As the school season just started and there is no coverage for the salaries of teachers and schools’ admin staff, there is a gap needs urgent consideration, in covering the running costs of the targeted school, there are need to do a maintenance of the majority of the classrooms, there is a shortage in supplying schools’ furniture and supplies and need for minor rehabilitation and maintenance of classrooms which includes ensuring accessibility for children with disabilities;. Teaching and learning materials are not fully available. The education representatives in the targeted locations confirmed the need to secure the winter needs of schools, provide students with educational bags, provide schools with safety requirements such as first aid kits. The project will ensure inclusiveness, targeting People With Special Needs for assistance, including children at risk, children, and we will also prioritize those with disabilities

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will contribute significantly to the gender marker as it specifically addresses services to vulnerable population. The project will specifically address the cross-cutting issues that will contribute to gender equality. One of the main protection risks or this project is that student undergoes bullying by other student or teachers. SOP will ensure that all learning facilities have an enforced code of conduct and policy on bullying and all teachers are trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. It will also provide awareness sessions for both students and their caregivers.Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. SOP will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.Another risk is the Privacy concerns along with confidentially and safety which could accessed by people who could pose a threat to beneficiaries. SOP ensures that confidentially principles are in line with global standards and will adhere to all protocols for risk mitigation, personal safety, movements, communications, and operations within Syria. We also have robust contingency planning and incident response protocols in place.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SoP has coordinated with other NGOs implementing and will be implementing projects in the same targeted locations. • Target schools are not threatened or occupied by armed groups, we will pass this risk by strength our staff within the communities we are targeting, always community people are the safe place to go when facing problem with armed groups.• The project area is not seized by armed groups that would require major changes to educational curriculum/teaching and learning; in case happened, we have the support of the local councils to mitigate and negotiate with parties to avoid educational facilities and locations.• Parents and caregivers are supportive of educational activities and committed to students' enrolment in, and attendance of, schools• The risk of having difficulties in purchasing the needed materials from the local markets, therefore we will make sure to get the needed materials ready for the required activities in advance.• Drop-out rate of students throughout the courses maybe high;• Availability of good literacy and language teachers, but we will ensure to get the service of the most trained and qualified teachers;• Bad security situation and as an assumption the security situation stays safe. We will adhere to all protocols for risk mitigation, personal safety, movements, communications, and operations within Syria. We also have robust contingency planning and incident response protocols in place. We will also stay in close coordination with other NGOs who are currently operating in northwest Syria in order to get real time updates and share information with them as the security situation on the ground shifts. We will also work to gather and exchange information about security dynamics in and around the proposed target locations in northwest Syria with other NGOs in the areas of operation.
