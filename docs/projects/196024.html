Project Summary

WVI intends to improve the food security status of vulnerable Rohingya Refugees and the host Communities through integrated food security interventions. The project will reach out to vulnerable Rohingya refugees with awareness, capacity-building initiatives on DRR, environment, homestead production, conditional cash grants, and input support aiming to address the food security dimensions. The project will establish learning centers inside the camps, which will serve HuB, and a one-stop service center for the refugees to get solutions on climate-smart farming. The benefits and progress will be tracked through regular monitoring, and data will be collected through program reports, studies, and evaluations. Considering the resilience, the HHs will be brought under orientation DRR basics and climate-smart agriculture. Social cohesion is one of the components, and youth & women groups will be targeted in the processes where they will be brought under groups and mobilize in social cohesion. The project will incorporate PSEA and gender and purposively work with persons with disability inclusion. The project will include women 60% of the total beneficiaries.
The project will cover 12 camps (8E, 9, 10, 11, 12, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, and 24) and in the Host communities of two sub-districts of Ukhiya and Teknaf - under Cox's Bazar districts. Moreover, the project aims to build social cohesion by establishing market linkage between FDMN and the host community. Proper sensitization and coordination meeting will be organized with relevant government and other stakeholders to incorporate the findings and learnings and to adapt those learnings accordingly. The project is focused on accountability mainstreaming to ensure that the needs of the affected population are at the center of every intervention. The Field team of WV for DRR inclusion and the Energy and Environment team for better integration of climate-smart agricultural practices and environmental protection. The interventions will be promoted to address climate-smart agriculture and green growth. Through the project, WV will be able to support the refugees and host communities with - Agricultural skills development in climate-smart agricultural technology followed by the inputs, food production, and preservation, including DRR/DRM tools for the protection of the agricultural products. - Productive assets, regular capacity building, and improved technologies provided to vulnerable host community households, including green skills training creation of green -small or micro-enterprises. To address the Complaint, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (CFRM), WV has a separate entity to capture feedback, complaints, and suggestions as part of the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP). The project will adapt learning from the community and improve the intervention.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Refugees in Cox’s Bazar generally have no regular income opportunities and lack the skills that would allow them to adapt quickly to change in their situation. The fifth round of the Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 5) published in March 2022, revealed that 95% of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Despite the current level of humanitarian assistance, 51 percent of Rohingya households cannot afford the MEB. So, food remains the most cited priority need for both communities (82 percent among Rohingya households versus 59 percent among host community households) driven by the need for greater access to fresh food or continuation of food assistance. The world’s largest congested Rohingya camps and surrounding host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar have become food insecure due to loss of economic opportunities in pandemic COVID-19 and global economic inflation. Approximately half of the assessed households in camps not having an acceptable food consumption score, and reporting challenges related to their food assistance; The most worried issues are that still 10% HH are poor in FCS (Ref: J-MSNA, 17 Oct’2021). The same study shows that in camps, 70% of households reported having adopted livelihoods-based coping strategies due to a lack of money to meet basic needs. Though, Host are in better position, yet 89% of households reported engaging in coping mechanisms due to a lack of money to meet basic needs. However, 20% of households, reporting this strategy not to be available to them or to already have exhausted it. While socio-economic impacts on Rohingya refugees are multi-dimensional, COVID-19 also threatens to exacerbate the already dire conditions for the communities. Limited self-reliance options lead the vulnerable groups to increased negative coping mechanisms (J-MSNA 2020). The study of Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) on chronic food insecurity (CFI) analysis of Bangladesh observed that in Cox’s Bazar, a significant portion (70%) of the people are food insecure either mild, moderate, or severe. Among them, 25% are moderately and at least 10% people are severely food insecure (CFI, 2022). The FAO/WFP conducted the Resilience Index Measurement Analysis (RIMA) study showed that about half of the Rohingya households were found with acceptable food consumption patterns. Among the remaining half, 44 percent have borderline consumption. The male-headed households are experiencing a better consumption pattern than the female-headed households in both the host and the refugee community (RIMA, 2022). The socioeconomic condition of Bangladesh was able to withstand the shock of the COVID-19 pandemic through its diversified agricultural and skills-based income generation activities (Tackling the Covid-19 Social and Economic Crisis in Bangladesh, UNICEF, 2021). Therefore, despite targeting women and girls, they still lag in food security and the nutritional deficiency (Gender & intersectionality report October 2020, UNHCR). In synchronization with sector findings (JMSNA 2020). The total number of Rohingya in Bangladesh has increased from 767,324 (154,264 households) after the August 2017 influx to 901,703 (UNHCR population data, November 2021) in December 2021. These additional populations have a social, natural, environmental, legal, and financial impact on Bangladesh. The skills development support for the Rohingyas is mostly dependent on the humanitarian assistance provided by national and international donor organizations. The purpose of the support is to provide tailored vocational skills so that they can utilize the skills once they return to Myanmar. The overall vulnerability of the Rohingya population has increased since 2017 (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment 2022).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

World Vision (WV) is an humanitarian organization dedicated to the well -being of Children, especially the world's most vulnerable children, their families and communities. WV are committed to serve and partner with people in need regardless of religion, ethnicity or gender. WV has been supporting communities in Bangladesh since 1971 and intervening in Cox’s Bazar District since 1996, where it is supporting both host and the Rohingya refugee communities through its high-quality interventions. WV thus has many years of contextual experience and acceptance built through strong engagement of host and Rohingya communities, other organisations, authorities - including at camp level, and other relevant stakeholders. WV has a strong technical expertise in Food Security and Livelihoods, including skills development, ensuring food and nutrition security as well as resilient and climate adaptive livelihoods. Throughout its long experience in the country, particularly in Cox’s Bazar, WV has promoted numerous innovative ideas in the field of Food Security and Livelihoods in order to strengthen the resilience of both host and refugee communities through actions similar as the one proposed. WV Was-chaired on Transfer Working group (TWG), Co-chaired on Livelihood Technical Working group (LTWG), lead the WaSH Cluster. WV is also FSS and WaSH Sector SAG member and member of different clusters, sectors and working groups, including the Food Security Sector, basing its humanitarian support on its guidelines. WV has long-term partnerships with UN agencies, including, UNICEF, UNHCR, UNESCO, IOM, WHO, and WFP. WV also ran several projects funded by UNICEF, UNHCR, UNESCO, IOM, WHO, and WFP and WB and is thus familiar with its policies, practices and programmes. WV has also established strong partnerships with INGOs like CBM, IRC, HI, and local NGOs such as RIC, SHED, ESDO and BGS through the lead of several consortiums.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

WV will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. “Do no harm” is the guiding principle for implementing the activities for the vulnerable people.WV incorporate protection principles in designing and developing programme interventions in Cox’s Bazar to ensure that all activities target the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation Privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be considered while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness raising session(GBV awareness messages, prevention messages, referral mechanism, menstrual hygiene management and safe access of women and adolescent girls) to protect them from any unwanted incidents .WV will conduct PSEA training for all staff and volunteers.

3.2 - Gender

WV programmes are prioritize in developing safer and gender-sensitive interventions. In acknowledging socio-cultural norms, the project will ensure gender segregated services enable effective participation in the planned programming of boys, girls, women and men.Women play a large, vital and growing role in Agriculture, Nutrition, Food security and a wide range of Income-Generating Activities. Production activities largely undertaken by women include post-harvest activities, processing and preservation of crops. The project will strive to provide equal opportunities for women and men to be part of project activities and the project will keep provision 60:40 female:male ratio of beneficiary selection. WV will conduct periodical community-based inclusive approaches (e.g. PRA) and gender analysis, using sex, age and disability disaggregated data to analyse and tailor to food security needs, understand capacities, barriers and the differential food and nutrition requirements.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project ensures age including child safeguarding by providing the safe space for women and child. The training centers will be equipped with breastfeeding corners, and child-friendly spaces so that their mother can take the training courses while the children can safely stay with them.The project target beneficiaries will be 18-45 years and no children will be engaged in project activities to eliminate the child labor.WV hearing to the voices and opinions of the children, adolescents and adults to identify the components of livelihoods and understand the outcomes of its actions. Particular attention is given to enable the participation of groups or individuals (girls, women and persons with disabilities) traditionally excluded from power and decision-making processes. WV is committed to ensuring that the rights and entitlements of the communities that it serves are protected and met. WV holds a Zero Tolerance Policy towards child safeguarding which applies to all WV employees.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will serve the rohingya refugees and host community will maintain gender ratio for a better inclusion in the project.WV conducted a understand the current challenges people at-risk group .WV believes diversity in the project or at workplace. A wide variety of activities will all age, gender, ethnicity, race, and religion-diversified children will be eligible to enrol in the learning centres though the most vulnerable will be prioritised, including disabilities. Regarding staff selection, everyone has the opportunity to be a staff regardless of gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, or disabilities irrespectively. A broader spectrum of diversity will be promoted through activities and policy.pregnant women, older persons, and persons with disabilities). Livelihood/self-reliance beneficiary selection and persons with disability disaggregated data collection were done by using Washington set group of questions.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

World Vision (WV) recognizes that the essence of accountability is to respect the needs, concerns, capacities and disposition of those with whom we work and to answer for our actions and decisions.WV’s priorities and approach to AAP is outlined in the Protection and Accountability Policy (2020), elaborated in the Community Engagement for Accountability Strategy (2021 – 2026) and mutually reinforces the Gender Policy (2022) and the Disability Inclusion Roadmap (2020). WV has established a Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) which includes a Hotline, help desk, and complaint boxes, for receiving feedback and complaints from the targeted populations. Protection assessments and the CFRM process protection issues in the community by collecting community feedback and responses in an organized manner. WV will do capacity development training for Staff to enhace their understanding on protection and AAP.
