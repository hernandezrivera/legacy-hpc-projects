Project Summary

The project aims to provide support to out-of-school children affected by conflict and displacement to access and complete basic education. The ongoing crisis in the two regions has had a significant impact on access to education for children, as schools are regularly targeted, leading to school closure and an estimated 667,000 school-aged children, of which 367,000 are IDPs out of school. The latest analysis reports that 68% per cent of schools in the Northwest and 39% per cent of schools in the Southwest are non-operational – translating in a total of 2,245 schools still not functioning in the two regions alone (NRC, 2023). The 2023 back-to-school period has been relatively timid, impacted by the security situation, the three-week lockdown imposed by NSAGs.
This project will provide out-of-school children (aged from 7 to 16), regardless of their statuses, with opportunities to catch up to classes missed, and reintegration to formal education when possible. Complementarily, to improve the learning environment, and mitigate risks of school in the formal education from dropping out, the project will work in schools in both urban and rural areas, particularly on key drivers impeding access to quality education in the 2 regions, especially the training of teachers, including community teachers to overcome the shortage of qualified teachers which disproportionally impact the most remote and insecure areas and training of teachers, facilitators and caregivers on psychosocial support to help children and youth having experienced traumatic events to ensure they are equipped to contribute to children rebuilding a sense of stability and safety in schools. To this end, NRC will scale up its Better Learning Progamme.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In the North-West and South-West regions, the entire formal education system is under attack, making it difficult for children and teachers to access formal and non-formal educational. Attacks against school staff, students, and educational premises (gun shooting, killing, arson, and abductions) continued in 2023. In the North-West, South-West, as well as in communities in the Littoral, West, which welcome IDPs from the North-West and South-West, an estimated 667 000 school aged students (of which 367,000 are displaced children) need support to access education. Over 55% of displaced children are reported to be out of school due to the security situation, limited and poorly maintained infrastructures, shortage of qualified teachers and unaffordable school fees. To respond to theses needs, NRC will implement the following activities: • Teachers training on Psychosocial Support (BLP) • Paiement of School fees/exam fees for vulnerable children• Provision of learning and recreational material • Support to Remedial/catch up classes for out-of-school children (class 6)
