Project Summary

Through this project, INSO Cameroon will continue to disseminate and aggregate detailed analysis of the contextual developments in its services to partners.
INSO Cameroon will provide the following services:
INSO Alerts - Prior to, or on the occurrence, of an incident carrying an imminent threat to NGOs and requiring an immediate operational response (such as movement restriction) INSO will provide an immediate Alert via the quickest technical medium available (e-mail, SMS text, voice call). The Alert will include the time, place and nature of the incident and a recommended response plan.
INSO Reports - Upon the anticipation or occurrence of a significant incident - including all incidents which directly involve the humanitarian community- but which does not require an immediate operational response from NGOs, INSO will provide a written Report of the incident via email noting the time, place and nature of the event along with a succinct analysis that includes an assessment of the probable impact and - where necessary - an advisory notice.
Monthly Reports - A written monthly analytical report with analysis plus key data and trends covering the Far North and the North-West South-West regions, as well as a macro level summary for the period. The report is intended to complement data with qualitative insights and observations on the security, political, demographic, social and political context for that period. The report is provided in electronic format only and is distributed regionally and nationally.
Quarterly Reports - A quarterly report, providing the highest level of analysis and statistics. The report has a focus on longer term trends and incorporates developments at the national, regional and international levels with summarized data on the humanitarian sector and the various conflict trends. The report is intended to orient NGO in the 'strategic' context, within which all local events are occurring, and so to try and look ahead at the implications of changes in the structure of the conflicts, politics and inter-regional dynamics.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The security context in Cameroon is marked by a significant intensification of violence in the North-West/South-West regions, which make it among the most violent (in terms of the number of incidents, not the impact) administrative regions in Africa, second only to North-Kivu in Democratic Republic of Congo. Although the number of civilian deaths in 2024 is relatively low compared to more intense crises on the continent (431 deaths from January 2024 to date), the number of abductions surged to 2,697 (up from 1,294 in 2023) and arrests reached 9,639 (up from 5,619 in 2023). Armed groups in the NWSW have notably enhanced their capabilities, adopting more sophisticated equipment, including radio-controlled IEDs. While these devices were initially sourced from neighboring countries, they are now locally manufactured, with operatives potentially benefiting from logistical support from affiliated OAG within the region. On the other side, government forces have stepped up targeted operations killing virtually all major OAG commanders in 2024. From a humanitarian perspective, the resulting fragmentation of armed groups continues to hinder access in the NWSW, with up to 50 autonomous OAGs operating in the region. These groups rely on extortion, kidnappings for ransom and illegal taxation to fund their operations. Since 2023, competition for territorial control has intensified, partly due to reduced funding from diaspora-based activists. Movement restrictions, irregular road checkpoints and behavioral regulations (e.g., yellow taxi bans, internal ‘border closure’, school boycotts) have increased as OAGs enhance their enforcement capabilities, escalating violence against civilians and posing greater risks to both civilians and humanitarian workers. Aid workers face increasing threats of ambush, extortion and temporary abductions, making access negotiations time and resource intensive. To mitigate these risks, INSO has been collaborating with OCHA and NGOs to review the 2022 NWSW access strategy. INSO will continue to provide the needful trainings and advisories services to humanitarians and support cross-NGO learning by identifying lessons learned to build a body of evidence on effective and ineffective negotiation practices. In the Far North, on the other hand, the security situation remained largely unchanged, as security incidents rose only from 1,012 in the first three quarters of 2023 to 1,169 in the same period of 2024. Unlike the NWSW, which is perceived as an internal “Cameroonian” problem, local authorities see insecurity in the Far North as a spillover from North-Eastern Nigeria. For this reason, counter insurgency operations are centered on cooperation within the regional joint forces like the MNJTF. This year, Operation Lake Sanity II led to a temporary decrease in OAG violence in the LCB. However, this reduction was short-lived, since after MNJTF troops left the area, Cameroonian forces' forward posts were not able to reclaim and effectively control rural areas. As a result, civilians were forced to comply with OAG rules, including illegal taxation. In addition, the Far North faces environmental challenges which compound the region's humanitarian crisis, making recovery and stability even more difficult. It is in this context that INSO Cameroon aims to continue providing credible, reliable, and high-quality security information, including analysis and advisory services, to registered humanitarian partners. These services will be delivered in a timely and appropriate manner to support partners in planning, operating, and delivering aid more safely to affected communities in areas covered by INSO in Cameroon. Finally, INSO Cameroon intends to organize a series of thematic training sessions focused on building the capacity of both international and national NGO staff.
