Project Summary

In accordance with WFP's National Strategic Plan for Cameroon (CSP 2022-2026), life-saving assistance will be provided to 40,000 people displaced from their villages along the border with Nigeria and settled in safer locations in the Far North region. Food aid to beneficiaries will be provided monthly through cash and in-kind transfers.
WFP will use cash transfers in areas where markets are functional and accessible to provide assistance to 15,000 IDPs; the ration is XAF 7,500 per beneficiary to help them access adequate and nutritious food; the ration provided to beneficiaries is equivalent to the food deficit defined in the Far North SEM. In addition, 25,000 IDPs will be supported through in-kind distributions that will provide 1,050 kcal of energy per day per person to beneficiaries who are entirely dependent on humanitarian food aid. Food rations include 185g of cereals, 60g of legumes, 20g of vegetable oil and 2.5g of salt.
For the conception of projects, beneficiaries’ opinions have been considered through focus group discussions, gender and protection evaluation field missions, monitoring exercises. These gave beneficiaries opportunities to state their preferences and transfer modalities and opinions on targeting criteria. The preparatory activities are inclusive, WFP ensures that women, men, girls and boys are involved in various stages such as targeting process, cash transfers and monitoring. WFP together with local authorities and partners, conduct assessments to identify IDPs to be considered for assistance based on WFP vulnerability criteria. These criteria include household size, presence of under-5 children, the elderly and handicapped, women-headed households, assets.
After distributions monitoring will be conducted through Cooperating Partners and attention will placed on the role played by men, women, girls and boys including decision-making on food consumption and food security in the household.
Through the established complaints and feedback mechanisms, beneficiary IDPs will freely express their views and concerns on the project implementation. Their views will be considered for project adjustment. Through sensitization, WFP will promote women and men equal participation in decision-making and social cohesion between host and displaced communities.
This assistance will be coupled to the nutrition prevention and treatment activities, WFP will provide support for children aged 6–59 months, this will include blanket supplementary feeding, counselling and support for moderate acute malnutrition treatment and improvement in adherence to treatment; mother and child health and nutrition programmes; and gender and nutrition- informed social and behaviour change communication as part of an integrated nutrition package. The daily ration for the specialized nutritious foods per assisted child will be 100 g.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Far North region of Cameroon is facing several complex crises, including the insurgency in the Lake Chad Basin, climatic extremes (drought and floods), and, more recently, several waves of inter-communal clashes over natural resources. These factors have plunged the region into a prolonged and growing humanitarian emergency and food insecurity. The number of people in need of food assistance has steadily increased over the years. According to the March 2022 Cadre Harmonisé (CH), the Far North region remains among the most affected region by food insecurity, representing 22 percent of the total acute food-insecure people in the country. The projections for June to August 2022 estimate that approximately 20 percent of the population within the region will be acutely food insecure, which represents nearly 1 million people out of the 3.é million people estimated nationwide. Moreover, in this very region, more than 70 percent of the population cannot afford a healthy diet, according to the latest “Fill the Nutrient Gap” Report published in 2021 by the Government of Cameroon and WFP. Since 2014, repeated incursions by armed groups into villages near the border with Nigeria have led to massive population displacement. As of June 2022, there were approximately 130,000 Nigerian refugees who fled the Boko Haram insurgency to Cameroon's Far North region, 60 percent of whom live in the Minawao camp, with the remaining living within host communities. In addition, there are an estimated 357,631 internally displaced persons (IDPs), and according to the results of the return intention survey conducted by IOM, 76 percent of them (IDPs and refugees) do not intend to go back to their place of origin because conditions are not conducive to return.This situation of insecurity is further exacerbated by climatic events. The Far North has experienced an increase in the frequency, intensity, and variability of weather shocks in recent years, including floods, droughts, and erratic rainfall. An observable aggravating factor is human-induced vegetation loss driven by the need for fuelwood and land for agricultural production. The loss of vegetation results in the inability to prevent the direct impact of raindrops on the soil, and the loss of roots decreases soil stability, destabilizing the soil structure and increasing vulnerability during floods.
