Project Summary

A sound needs analysis was conducted in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor through primary data collection from community members and relevant stakeholders (KII and FGDs), Market Studies, and secondary data review. Direct observation is ongoing through project implementation. The analysis highlighted the highly negative effects that the significant and concerning deterioration of the Syrian economy is having on the access to basic services and livelihood and job opportunities. Indeed, lack of access to basic services, high unemployment rates, and shortage of income-generating opportunities are leading many local communities under the poverty rate. The situation is exacerbated by the lack of access to quality educational services and to employment opportunities in the local market. Moreover, the increasing shortages of fuel and electricity is leading to negative effects on functionality and operations of critical services and hindering the availability and quality of essential services.
With a budget of 1,185,000 USD, the proposed intervention targets 22,000 individuals (50% Females, 50% Males, 20% PWDs) in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor cities, with the aim to enhance their access to basic services and improved livelihoods opportunities, and strengthen resilience of at-risk communities. The proposed action focuses on improving public services’ infrastructure, especially vocational training schools and facilities. This is possible through rehabilitation, and installation of solar power systems in vocational schools to ensure use of equipment. Soft activities include technical education, career counselling, internships, and skills training programs identified through labour market assessments. These activities facilitate not only the access to VT opportunities but especially the transition from vocational education to employment opportunities in target areas. Beneficiaries of vocational courses will receive start-up kits to encourage self-employment, and targeted communities will be targeted with social cohesion initiatives.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

With a budget of 1,185,000 USD, the proposed intervention targets 22,000 individuals (50% Females, 50% Males, 20% PWDs), 2,200 unique direct beneficiaries and 20,000 indirect beneficiaries, in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor cities. The project is built on WWGVC ongoing programme in both target areas, and is in line with the ERL HRP. Indeed, objective of the action is to enhance access to essential services and improve livelihoods opportunities, while fostering community resilience and social cohesion. The proposed intervention focuses on rehabilitating 5 Vocational Schools, to equip with identified equipment, material and furniture. Solar power systems will be moreover installed in 3 of them, to ensure that – despite the low availability of power – students and teachers can use the needed equipment. After conducting 3 Market Studies, Vocational Training and soft skills (i.e. marketing) courses will be offered in the target schools for 400 people (50% F, 50% M and 20 PWDs) with severe socioeconomic disadvantages. With the aim to improve the quality of Vocational Education, and of the transition from education to the Job Market, 300 MoE teachers in Vocational Schools will receive training on inclusion, class management, but also on preparing and following Individual Educational Plans (IEPs) – following career counselling WWGVC methodology. Selected VT teachers in the offered courses will be in charge of preparing IEPs for each of the selected trainee, by assessing capacities and setting and monitoring individualised goals. This approach will enable the students to receive a support tailored on their needs, and also to help them better transition to the job market. Indeed, within the initial Market Study, Local Businesses will be identified to place 50 trainees for internships upon successful completion of their training course. At the end of the Vocational Training, trainees will receive a start-up kit to set up an IGA and will be hence supported by WWGVC and SARC Livelihood in setting up new economic activities (in-kind support). Through such activities, WWGVC estimates to create 50 long-term job opportunities. The overall intervention, moreover, focuses on social cohesion and community engagement. Community members will be involved in participatory workshop to identify ERL opportunities, develop ERL plans, and promote awareness-raising campaigns in the target areas. Campaigns can also promote the importance of Vocational Education, whose importance is often underestimated, as highlighted by participants in current WWGVC ERL projects.
