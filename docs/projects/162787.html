1 - Needs

With an increase of more than half a million people classified as IPC phase 3 and above , the food insecurity situation in Sudan continues to worsen in 2019 due to continued economic decline and natural shocks, resulting in the increased vulnerability of people. In 2020, it is estimated that 6.2 million individuals, representing 14.2 percent of the total population, will need urgent and timely lifesaving food and livelihoods security support. The highest prevalence of food insecurity is in the 6 states that for the first time have been classified as IPC phase 3 and above, including South Kordofan (40 percent prevalence), Red Sea (28 percent), North Darfur (24 percent), Central Darfur (22 percent), South and West Darfur (21 percent).People received aid in Sudan for 2019 according to the GHO) may exceed 4.4 million. Access to WASH in Sudan suffers chronic underdevelopment throughout the country and is further exacerbated to the extent of survival for people in the areas affected by conflicts. In Darfur, intermittent conflict and inter communal tensions continue to impact civilians, notably women, children and Elders, and also prevent the return of displaced people to their areas of origin. In some areas access has improved as in BN, access to other areas, including where there is active conflict, remains restricted. According to the 2018 HNO, about 5.5 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in Darfur. About 36,500 South Sudanese refugees arrived in Darfur in 1st quarter of 2017, bringing their total number in the region since December 2013 to about 121,300 people. South Kordofan state has been at the centre of an armed conflict between GOS and the SPLM N since June 2011. The ongoing conflict drove thousands of civilians from their homes which put additional pressure on host communities to share limited resources and increase the vulnerability of the affected girls, boys, men and women. Gender and protection will be a priority for this project. All activities will be gauged against the 'Do No Harm' criteria to ensure they do not exacerbate vulnerabilities of communities and particularly of women, girls and boys. This project will follow the SHF 'Accountability to Affected Populations' guidelines for protection. The project targets 29,404 women, 23,012 men, 38,354 girls and 37,075 boys with safe water, sanitation and hygiene promotion services. Project targets beneficiaries who are clearly defined in terms of gender and age (women, men, boys and girls) ,the project will contribute to gender equality in the following ways: - Promoting and strengthening the role of women in the management of water and sanitation facilities. Previously low proportions of women were meaningfully involved in the water management committees, yet the water burden is mostly shouldered by women due to the fact that in Sudan, culturally, it is the role of women to fetch household water. - Taking deliberate step not only to ensure inclusion of at least 50% of women in the water committees, but also empower and encourage them to meaningfully participate in decision making processes on issues pertaining to WASH in their communities. - Lack of adequate sanitation facilities affected women and girls. Women and girls also face risks of GBV when defecating in the open or having to walk long distances to latrines. WV will consult with women and girls in the project area, and support construction of latrines at locations that are secure for women and girls.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is going to address the environmental issues by considering the local environmental context of the targeted area, and a conduct continuous assessment for potential environmental impacts will be carried throughout the project life. The project is also going to do mitigation measures by compensating for negative impacts (if any), and enhance environmental benefits in the project by practicing some measurements, the followings are part of it: • Replace the use of fired bricks with Soil-Stabilized-Blocks (SSB) or Cement-Stabilized-Blocks (CSB).• Locally available material is recommended provided that it is not impacting negatively on the environment.• Include environmental awareness sessions in projects, and support solid waste management schemes • Reduce packaging material and discourage use of plastic bags to reduce the amount of waste generated. • Promote alternatives such as Fuel-Efficient Stoves to reduce use of wood, solar panels for lighting and pumps. Include fuel efficient cooking techniques (e.g. pre-soaking beans, sheltering cooking fires, etc.) in trainings.• Raise awareness on environmental protection and conservation• Reduce wood consumption by supplying iron pipes and using environmentally friendly material for constructions and rehabilitation; and promoting alternatives such as Fuel Efficient Stoves, and fuel efficient cooking techniques. Consider also alternative energy sources such as solar panels for lighting and pumps.• Optimize ground water pumping in a way that it does not exceed the recharge ability of the aquifer. • Enhance tree planting

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed into sectoral activities. As mainstreaming issue, the project will ensure that each activity will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults.Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, department of welfare in the Ministry of social welfare to ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staffs or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children.To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staffs will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to beneficiaries will be two-fold; to direct beneficiary groups and accountability to host community leadership. Accountability to direct beneficiaries will similarly be based on MoU with group members and regular joint review of progress. Accountability to host community leadership will be through MoUs that WVI represented by ‘Village Focal Points’ - will sign with community representatives. The MoUs will spell out the roles of the organization as well as the role and contribution of the communities. In addition to being accountability tools; MoUs enable WVI to communicate decisions such as criteria for selection of beneficiaries and project locations. MoU are reference points for issues that arise during implementation. MoUs have enabled WVI hold communities accountable for commitments they make at the onset of projects. Participation: This programme will be designed in consultation with refugee and host communities leadership and government entities. In addition to regular consultation as part of ongoing interventions, refugee and host communities and government leaders were informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries and prioritizing interventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level will be facilitated project staff to further publicize the programme and communicate opportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institute feedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and ‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully in Darfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularly women – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamper participation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanisms and / or through or discovered through interactions. The project staff will work towards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements, ensuring bens have timely access to relevant and clear information and they are satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the response. The program staff will consult with beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and accept complaints, and communicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it can address. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages.
