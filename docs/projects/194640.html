Project Summary

The states of Western Bahr El Ghazel, Northern Bahr El Ghazel and Central Equatorial according to the WASH cluster severity mapping display significant gaps in water supply and quality, sanitation and hygiene. Jur River, Aweil North and Morobo counties that this project targets are under WASH severity level 4, 4 and 3 respectively indicative of the high WASH needs in these counties. Provision of WASH services is key in reducing mainly malnutrition in U5s and child mortality. Thus FADM that works in the states of in Western Bahr El Ghazel, Northern Bahr El Ghazel and Central Equatorial States proposes to implement a WASH project in Jur River, Aweil North and Morobo Counties to address short- to-midterm WASH concerns targeting conflict, emergency and disease prone affected populations by providing integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations. The project aims at increasing access to clean and safe drinking water at the community and nutrition facilities through rehabilitation of boreholes, increase access to sanitation services in nutrition facilities through rehabilitation and construction of latrines at nutrition centers and finally to increase access to good hygiene practice messages through hygiene promotion at nutrition centers and the community.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs overview in 2022 estimated that 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing were expected to be impacted as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Only 39 per cent of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34 per cent in rural areas. Difficulty in securing enough water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49 per cent of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source and that the surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods, when latrine pits seep into waterways. Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points, with 18 per cent of households, both rurally and in urban settings, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting. Sanitation is very poor in South Sudan with sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. Overall, 80 per cent of households did not access a latrine. Moreover, floods regularly damage existing sanitation infrastructure and given their annual occurrence and paired with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by communities to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities.Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Overall, only 16 per cent of the households reported owning soap. The counties of Jur River, Aweil North and Morobo, face a couple of humanitarian challenges as a result of multiple shocks affecting not only the internally displaced persons and returnees but also the host communities. Access to safe water is low in these counties, with the WASH cluster severity mapping indicating below acceptable thresholds with both Aweil North and Jur River classified at level 4 while Morobo is at level 3. Less than 30% of households in these communities have access to a safe water source, majority of these relying on unsafe surface water, which is shared with livestock. Open defecation is a common practice of disposal of human excreta and the community knowledge on good hygiene practice is very low. This situation has exposed the populations to high level risk of vulnerability to water related illnesses.Jur River, Aweil North and Morobo Counties are among the counties that are prone to flooding that causes displacement of people, destruction of WASH facilities and expose the affected population to water borne diseases. IRNA carried out in August 2022 Jur River County after reported flooding established that 11,082HH were displaced by flooding in the County resulting into increased WASH needs. FADM therefore proposes a WASH project targeting Jur River, Aweil North and Morobo Counties that will rehabilitate boreholes, construct and rehabilitate latrines in nutrition centers and carry out hygiene promotion in order to increase access to WASH services among the targeted communities.

2 - Project activities

1. Borehole rehabilitation. FADM will target to rehabilitate boreholes in both nutrition centers and the community to provide increased access to clean and safe drinking water. In flood prone locations the boreholes’ rehabilitation will ensure that the boreholes are made to be flood resistant by raising their platforms based on the WASH cluster standards. FADM will also carry out water quality tests for the rehabilitated boreholes before they are commissioned. FADM will also carry out safety audits at the targeted boreholes to ensure that they are safe for both women and girls. 2. Training of water management committees. To ensure that the rehabilitated boreholes are properly managed, FADM will train water management committees based on the WASH cluster’s set guidelines. The committees’ formation will ensure that women are adequately represented. 3. Hygiene promotion. FADM will carry out hygiene promotion both at the community and at the nutrition centers. At the community FADM will carry out door to door hygiene promotions and hygiene promotions in public places. FADM will also carry out hygiene promotion at the health facilities with the hygiene promoters visiting the nutrition facilities to teach caregivers on good hygiene practices. The project will also use mother to mother support groups to ensure that the good hygiene practices shared are practiced by the caregivers as the support groups will be used to support the members to ensure that the good hygiene practices are practiced by the mothers who are group members. 4. Rehabilitation of latrines. FADM will rehabilitate existing latrines that will require rehabilitation at the nutrition centers in the targeted locations to increase access to sanitation services. 5. Construction of latrines. FADM will construct temporary latrines in nutrition centers that lack latrines to increase access to sanitation services at the nutrition facilities. 6. Distribution of WASH NFIs. FADM will distribute standard WASH kits to both caregivers at the nutrition facilities and the vulnerable community members in the targeted locations. The WASH NFIs will be acquired from the WASH core pipeline supply.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most vulnerable member are key in project success and they will be involved at various levels right from the project design, planning, implementation, Monitoring and evaluation. All these will be done in a participatory way. Feedback will be obtained from these by having designated persons dedicated to them, while depending on context, culture and available means and infrastructure to design best mechanism of beneficiary feedback. These will range from setting up hotlines, suggestion boxes, interviews with key informants, focus group discussions as well as house to house visits while observing C -19 IPC protocols.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Beneficiary information will be provided through various means such as interviews with key informants and beneficiaries. Project will have selected place within the community where one staff member will sit once or twice a week to receive and give feedback to the community members. There will be a box for feedback as well as receiving direct feedback from the community members. Staff will also get feedback during activity implementation and provide feedback to the community. Feedback obtained will be fed into project designs and appraisals, to ensure needs are met and necessary mitigations carried out.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will conduct awareness creation on sexual exploitation and abuse to both women and men. PSEA policies present within the organization, FADM will also use project staffs and together with community members to identify SEA victims and report. We will establish referral pathways and feedback mechanism such that SEA cases are reported and managed while keeping conﬁdentiality. Staff training will be conducted on PSEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This project will ensure that the feedback received from the beneficiaries are integrated into the implementation processes of the project to ensure that that the targeted beneficiaries are satisfied with the services that will be delivered. At the design of the project, views of the targeted communities will be sort and these views incorporated into the project’s design to ensure that the proposed project meets the needs of the targeted population. When the project starts, the project implementation team will hold information sharing sessions during which the beneficiaries will be informed about how the project will be implemented and the deliverables of the project. During these sessions the targeted beneficiaries will be given opportunity to give their views on the project and these views are incorporated into the project implementation process at the start. In the course of the project implementation the project implementation team will collect feedback from the targeted beneficiaries and these feedback will be used in shaping the project implementation until the project ends. At the end of the project the project will carry out a project evaluation and a learning event from which the learnings from the project will be documented. These learnings will then be incorporated into the next design of a similar project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

FADM will provide public information to the beneﬁciaries about the programmes provided through local government, community outreach and health facility-based awareness and health promotion activities. FADM will consult with local authorities, community leaders, Department of Water and sanitation, CHD and health staff throughout the project implementation regarding decisions to commence, adapt or complete programmes. Emergency Rapid Team’s assessments include key informant interviews and focus group discussions amongst community groups. In static facilities, FADM will use annual household surveys to assess programme coverage and post exit interviews are used at the facility level for monitoring the quality of health service provision. Every staff member who joins FADM will get orientation on the Code of Conduct and has to sign upon it, together with a “Summary of Minimum Standards for the Protection of Women and Children Against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation” which form part of the Staff Guidelines. In addition, FADM will conduct consultative meetings with the community to identify their needs. The project will conduct awareness creation on sexual exploitation and abuse to both women and men.
