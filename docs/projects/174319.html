Project Summary

NRC livelihoods and food security (LFS) project is based on the NRC’s LFS strategic objective for 2021: to enhance adaptive capacity of LFS vulnerable households (HH) improving their economic and social well-being.
Specific objectives are:
1) Improved access to adequate and nutritious food.
2) Improved access to reliable livelihoods opportunities.
The operational area focus will be selected LGAs within both Borno and Adamawa states. The main contexts that NRC will respond to include:
o Displaced populations with limited access to lands but may be able to receive support for income generation/meeting basic needs. Each intervention with IDP will include the vulnerable Hosting community.
o Returnees in safe and accessible areas, back to their home location, that are facing early recovery phase of the emergency.
o In Adamawa State, with more stable contexts, LFS is looking at durable solutions. NRC will continue to look for opportunities to respond in new LGAS. The most likely LGAs on interest for promotion and recovery of livelihoods would be in Adamawa.
o Refugees form Niger and Cameroon in areas where HC could not absorb them.
The main activities that NRC will undertake include:
• emergency food assistance (cash/voucher/in kind distribution)
• support to community-owned irrigation schemes construction & rain-fed agriculture by means of:
a. Agricultural inputs support (seeds, organic fertilizers, biological fight, equipment)
b. Agriculture training, accompanying the whole agricultural cycle wit. Provided with collaboration with the Borno State Agricultural Development Programme (BOSADP) and technical support by RUWASA (Rural Water Supply and Sanitation Agency) and quality monitored in collaboration with NRC agronomists.
c. Construction of irrigation schemes: deep boreholes and/or tubewells
• promotion of livestock and aquaculture

2 - Activities

• Activities*• (Max. length is 4000 characters)• 3676 characters with spaces • Immediate food assistance to save lives and reduce suffering of most vulnerable households. Food assistance provision will range between 4 to 7 months but at least cover the 4 months’ lean season. Assistance will be designed to meet at minimum 70% of the food minimum expenditure basket (FMEB). The prioritization of the delivery of food assistance through cash-based initiatives (CBI) and using e-voucher system, collaborating with the with approved e-voucher delivery mechanism partners (Red Rose) but in kind when the NRC selection modality for the context advice it. • In case of emergency response, NRC will provide lifesaving food assistance through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The RRM response applies a holistic approach for newly displaced IDPs seeking to meet basic life-saving food security needs, emergency shelters, and basic sanitation and hygiene needs. The RRM mechanism is a key element in the project execution to move into new geographical areas and increase the number of beneficiaries in established areas. NRC will coordinate the response with other agencies implementing emergency response. Emergency response interventions will set a basis for other core competencies to start their early recovery operations and adopt a multi-sector approach with different sectors depending on the needs and results from the Multi-Sectoral Rapid Need Assessments.• Construction of livelihoods community assets: expanding irrigation schemes (e.g. irrigation canal rehabilitation, water sources- Bore holes and tube wells drilling and rehabilitation) for small farmers, reviving the all year-round food and cash crop production in rural areas. NRC will coordinate with the relevant technical government ministries and departments to ensure only those schemes located in safe locations are supported in order to minimize the risks of farmers being exposed to possible attacks• Re-establish agricultural crop production (e.g. through seeds & tools distribution, training on new cropping techniques, etc.). NRC will adopt community-based approaches to mobilize existing smallholder irrigation farming schemes and facilitate establishment of community structures to oversee management of community assets for the good of all. • IGAs: NRC will support both farm and non-farm-based livelihood activities and promote diversification of livelihoods activities by the target beneficiaries. The farm based activities will include supporting agricultural activities for food production for both use at household level and for market purpose to improve on the household income. Support to none farm based livelihoods activities will involve supporting establishment of small business, and vocational skills acquisition for employment and trade. • Innovations for 2021: 1) Market based programming, 2) Value chain analyse and improvement in the activities and 3) introduction of villages, services and loans. o Standard: Inclusion of cooking energy payments in 100% of the food basket as per FSC advised (2,000 Naira/month per HH) and more cook stoves in the design of projects. o Focus IGA on value chains addition by processing of farm products and/or by-products (strengthening the marketing). NRC through IGA support grant will facilitate acquisition of simple processing machineries/ tools to support cottage industries for the target beneficiaries, this will facilitate the value addition intention and increase the value of the production and/or by-products (transformation, processing and/or labelling) (farming, livestock, fishing, , forest natural resources, others…).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRC's protection approach stands by three key principles:1) Our programming will not further expose people and cause harm2) Our interventions will be accessible to all without discrimination3) Our approaches will effectively involve and be accountable to the communities we work with NRC ensures that safe programming is mainstreamed in all its core competencies. The basics of safe programming in NRC are as follows:1. Prioritise Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: Prevent and minimise any negative effects of your intervention.2. Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without barriers. Make sure that your programme is designed for the most vulnerable.3. Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms, through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.4. Participation and Empowerment: Support the development of communities’ and individual capacities and assist people to claim their rights. NRC Nigeria has also established clear reporting routes for PSEA and Child safeguarding incidents. Several channels are available for the complainants (affected communities and staff members). Complainants can directly report incidents through the complaint and feedback mechanism (complaint boxes, green telephone line, in-person), the PSEA and Child Safeguarding designated focal points, the CD, the Global PSEA and Safeguarding Unit, NRC Protect or through the whistleblowing mailbox.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC is committed to setting up systematic and effective mechanisms to collect beneficiary and non-beneficiary feedback, and to provide on-going feedback to NRC during its projects. NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standards definition of Accountability and understands Accountability to Affected Populations as a process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. As with other NRC projects, a Complaint and Feedback Response mechanism (CFRM) integrated in this project will ensure that beneficiaries and communities have the opportunity to register feedback and complaints. This mechanism includes the provision of a complaints/suggestion boxes at project sites, and dedicated complaint and feedback hotline, email, and face to face sessions with staff. NRC uses this feedback to resolve any issues that may arise, and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. This CFRM will be managed by a dedicated team, and report directly to senior management to provide additional accountability towards targeted communities and ensure that complaints are discussed on a senior level within the organization where appropriate. Furthermore, populations served by NRC staff through this project will be informed on NRC’s Code of Conduct and how to report any breaches of conduct. Post distribution monitoring will also gain feedback from the target population.
