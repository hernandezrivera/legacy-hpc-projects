Project Summary

IOM will enhance its GBV program, that was established in 2017, with a focus on refugees and host communities. The program centers around three key pillars: Support survivors, risk mitigation, and addressing the root causes, in line with IOM's GBV in Crises Institutional Framework targeting people of all genders and ages, including diverse sexual orientations, and identities.
IOM provides case management services through 9 Women and Girls Safe Spaces at 8 different sites, appropriate camp facilities for male case management, along with an emergency safe shelter for survivors of GBV. While increasing the capacity of the existing shelter, IOM has initiated a second safe shelter for accommodating the needs of men and GDPs. These initiatives aim to reduce risks, prevent GBV, and empower individuals at risk.
IOM’s WGSS offers various services, including information dissemination, age-appropriate psychosocial support, SRH services including comprehensive family planning services, clinical management of rape, menstrual regulation with medication, syndromic management of STIs and health education & promotion on basic SRHR, awareness and educational activities, life-skills training, and forums for information-sharing and learning.
IOM, with its partner PULSE Bangladesh Society, will continue to roll out MBR (Mother and Sisters Way) edition 1 & 2, EMAP, and Girl Shine Curriculum in its locations. IOM will introduce SASA! Together, a comprehensive community mobilization approach addresses the GBV root causes in three locations.
To promote localization and enhance sustainability, IOM will engage community by focusing on capacity-building initiatives, advocates, imams, majhis, GBV volunteers, and women's committees as part of process to enhance community ownership and accountability, to address GBV and promote gender equality. IOM will collaborate with the Protection Research Unit to develop peer-led, evidence-based curricula, involving men, women, and diverse gender identities, ensuring inclusivity and safe GBV services access.
The program will contribute GBV risk mitigation through increased participation of women, girls, GDPs, and persons with disabilities. IOM will identify GBV risks and vulnerable groups through safety mapping, participatory assessments and data collection, including regular safety audits. Menstrual health education, life skills, and male engagement programs will be key focal points. Empowerment and skills development activities for women and girls will be more structured and diversified, including through CFW. Graduates from skills-building programs will serve as peer trainers, receiving coaching and support. IOM will ensure the preposition and distribution of dignity items addressing women's and girls' needs. Capacity building for IOM, partner staff, and national authorities will cover protection, GBV core concepts, risk identification, PSEA, AAP, and Code of Conduct.
IOM will also support Rohingya women and girls in identifying opportunities for enhanced participation and engagement, create platforms for leadership and improve decision making. IOM’s program will complement fostering social cohesion as well as ensuring disability inclusion.
IOM’s 2024-2026 GBViC Action Plan for Bangladesh aims to mainstream GBV considerations across all sectors to mitigate risks, address gender and GBV concerns, and enhance capacities. This promotes the rights, safety, dignity, and well-being of crisis-affected populations, ensuring all IOM sectoral programming remains safe, dignified, and accessible.
IOM’s GBV program actively participates in various subsectors and advisory groups, such as the GBV Subsector, GBV Strategic Advisory Group, GBV Case Management Task Team, GBV Prevention Working Group, Dignity Kit Task Teams, and PSEA Network. IOM's GBV staff serve as interagency GBV Camp Level Focal Points in all IOM GBV AoR and are part of emergency and response structures like the MMTs and the PERUs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The protracted crisis situation of the 1,010,298 refugees after seven years displacement, concurrent with the camp security situation, has worsened existing GBV trends for both Rohingya refugees and host communities. The 2024 ISNA findings and various interagency assessments have confirmed this, underscoring the security challenges that have given rise to new protection concerns. This particularly affects individuals facing discrimination and additional barriers to accessing services, including single female-headed households, adolescent girls, persons with diverse gender identities and sexual orientations, and persons with disabilities. Reports from 2023 indicate a rise in intimate partner violence (79% of all GBV incidents in Q3), sexual violence (8.4% in Q3), domestic violence, and polygamy. According to GBVIMS quarterly reports, GBV incidents have increased throughout Q1-Q3. Most cases occur in survivors' homes, streets, near water points, public toilets/latrines, and bathing facilities, underscoring the need for GBV risk mitigation in WASH and Shelter/NFI programs. The reports also note the rise of a new GBV trend which is technology facilitated GBV. During ISNA 2024, 46% of respondents expressed increased safety concerns for women and girls, and 52% felt the same for men and boys. IOM is seeing more referrals for emergency safe shelter services, highlighting the urgent need for additional emergency shelters. Integrating GBV services into SRH and Health is essential to meet identified need. To improve community-level awareness, strategies such as community-based outreach and peace and security campaigns should be implemented. Ensuring accessibility for marginalized groups is essential. Implementing targeted awareness programs for gender-diverse individuals, persons with disabilities, or new arrivals can effectively bridge knowledge gaps regarding available services. The gender norms are evolving within Rohingya communities, impacting the mobility of women and girls and their ability to control resources, information, and access to services. These changes in gender norms are intersecting with new family structures and demographic shifts. Prioritizing safe and meaningful participation and empowerment of women, engaging with men, behavioral change interventions is crucial for broader GBV prevention efforts (evident and recommended in GBVIMS reports and ISNA) and ensuring safe access to services. Scaling up evidence based GBV prevention work is a top priority to challenge harmful norms and ensure access to services for vulnerable populations. IOM has also been testing a culturally appropriate Cash for Work initiative to further enhance access to safe and dignified services. Women and adolescent girls face increased risks due to factors such as poor living conditions in camps, limited access to basic resources, a lack of income-generating activities, insufficient lighting, gender-segregated facilities, the distance to water points, and insecurity. These risks can lead to coping strategies such as child marriage, survival sex, and human trafficking. Focus group discussions and reports indicate that barriers to reporting include pressure and fear from parents and community leaders, as well as the limited participation of women and girls in household decision-making. Common concerns include the risk of forced marriage with the perpetrator and a lack of protection against reprisals. Social stigma, perceptions about survivors, interference by community leaders in handling rape cases, a lack of protection for survivors, insufficient awareness of the consequences of rape, the benefits of services, and service accessibility may delay survivors' decision-making in reporting and accessing services promptly. Specialized GBV services, including multi-sectoral services, require further enhancement to consider the inclusion of male survivors and individuals with diverse gender identities and needs.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM places protection at the core of its humanitarian response. IOM is committed to mainstreaming protection across all of its programming, incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while making sure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people IOM seeks to assist. IOM ensures that service and assistance delivery preserves the physical integrity of individuals and communities, their dignity, and is culturally appropriate and minimizes any harmful and unintended negative consequences. Centrality to protection is mandated and operationalized across response including assistance and services are provided according to needs and not on the basis of age, sex, gender identity, nationality, race, or ethnic allegiance. Services and assistance are provided in good quantity, within safe and easy -to -reach locations, are known by the affected individuals and are accessible by all concerned groups including persons with disabilities, gender diverse popualtions. Affected individuals and communities play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions that affect them and put in place easily accessible mechanisms (complaints and feedback mechanisms) increasing accountability. Inclusive participation to decision-making processes is fostered to support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empower themselves. IOM provides specialized services through its protection specialists, covering areas like general protection, counter-trafficking, and child protection. The GBV team follows a survivor-centered approach, adhering to the GBViC framework and GBVIMS+/Primero standards. The Cash for Work initiative empowers vulnerable women, enabling them to contribute economically to their families and gain support from their husbands, while challenging negative social norms and promoting positive coping mechanisms.

2.3 - Age & Disability

IOM remains an advocate for Age and Disability inclusion. This commitment ensures that our efforts are inclusive and respectful of the unique needs and circumstances of all individuals, regardless of their background, age, gender, or abilities. Within IOM's WGSS, Child-friendly corners are featured, providing a dedicated space for children that is managed by trained staff while their mothers participate in activities. Furthermore, IOM implements the MBR (Mother’s and Sister’s Ways) Edition 1-2, an age-appropropriate two-way exchange of culturally acceptable and accessible information. IOM will also implement Girl Shine Curriculum to support, protect, and empower adolescent girls including the caregivers. IOM’s WGSS and community outreach also offers age-appropriate psychosocial support and services including for elderly from the community. Persons with disabilities are among the most marginalized people in crisis-affected communities and are disproportionately affected by conflict and emergency situations. IOM's work focuses on reducing or removing barriers and risks faced by persons with disabilities, identifying and improving their resilience, safety and protection. IOM is committed to the inclusion of persons with disabilities in its humanitarian response. The IASC Guidelines on the Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action (2019) and the UN Disability Inclusion Strategy inform and guide IOM's work on disability inclusion. IOM supports the improvement of data collection on disability, the reduction of barriers to ensure more meaningful access to humanitarian services for persons with disabilities, sustained and meaningful participation of persons with disabilities throughout project cycles and capacity development of IOM staff and partners on disability inclusion.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM's GBV program prioritized its community-based and participatory approaches, which aim to cater to a diversity of needs and ensure inclusive participation. Information-sharing on humanitarian aid and programs is ensured through the design, translation, testing, and dissemination of messages for awareness-raising and sensitization—empowering affected populations to make informed decisions. Multimedia resources and IEC materials are contextualized for understanding and accessibility. Two-way communication is maintained through regular, participatory community engagement and outreach initiatives and routine activities that protect the right to information and promote meaningful decision-making inclusive of gender, diversity, and vulnerable, marginalized, and at-risk groups. A standardized, multi-sectoral CFM is available across all IOM camps for refugees to ask questions and request assistance while ensuring data protection and privacy. It complements referral pathways for sensitive complaints by activating appropriate processes and procedures for internal and external referrals and replies in a timely manner. This aims to foster trust and provide communities the opportunity to share their satisfaction and resolution. CFM reports are disaggregated to capture relevant data and respond accordingly; they are published for transparency and to support interventions and course corrections. IOM contributes to various coordination forums through close collaboration with peers and its leadership position and active membership in the AAP TWG for technical support, principles, and commitments to collective inter-agency responsibility. Aid effectiveness is ensured through rights-based, people-centered, and community-led programming. Evidence generation tracks rumors and identifies urgent issues to amplify voices and support advocacy with stakeholders. A dedicated CwC team supports all IOM units for a harmonized AAP strategy across its AoR.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM will continue to implement the Zero Tolerance Policy toward SEA which binds staff members, implementing partners, third party contractors, vendors and service providers to standards of behavior at all times. SEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and is grounds for disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution. IOM has a designated and dedicated PSEA Officer to support the ongoing efforts related to combat PSEA. IOM will continue to roll out PSEA training packages and offer Training of Trainers sessions for PSEA Champions in the coming year to ensure that IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers adhere to the PSEA guiding principles and mandatory reporting systems. IOM will continue to coordinate with PSEA network for initiatives including training for govt. actors. At the start of the project, SEA risks will be assessed, and mitigation measures identified to ensure safer programming. IEC materials will be developed to inform affected populations about their rights to free assistance, reporting mechanisms, and available referral and assistance services. The PSEA officer, along with the program team, will regularly consult with beneficiaries through focus group discussions to boost PSEA awareness and address concerns. IOM has a robust online reporting mechanism which is WEAREALLIN.IOM.INT, allowing anyone to report SEA incidents. Recognizing the communities we work with might not be able to access and use the WAAI platform, IOM has established complaint and feedback mechanisms in place to receive complaints including concerns related to SEA from beneficiaries and community members. IOM ensures follow-up of SEA complaints through its specialized investigation team and ensures access to protection and assistance of potential survivors in coordination with its MPHSS-Protection team and the PSEA network, and in line with the existing GBV and Child Protection referral pathways.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM delivers its Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Climate Change Adaptation (CCA) activities in line with the UN Plan of Action on Disaster Risk Reduction for Resilience, reinforcing local and national initiatives to prevent and prepare for disasters and related population movements, providing emergency assistance and protection where displacement cannot be avoided, and fostering post-disaster recovery solutions and resilience-building. IOM works extensively in support of governments and communities to: i) Enhance disaster preparedness measures in vulnerable locations, looking at evacuation management, early warning systems, physical risk mitigation, community-based disaster risk reduction capacities and related activities and ii) Integrate disaster risk reduction into recovery and reconstruction programming, related to shelter and housing support following disasters, support in the construction of disaster-resilient infrastructure and livelihoods, and approaches to increase resilience and reduce the likelihood of future disasters. IOM’s WGSSs in six camps are reconstructed with treated bamboo and other environment friendly materials. IOM will replicate such environment friendly approach with other WGSS reconstruction where appropriate to ensure sustainability.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

IOM maintains a Rohingya Response Business Continuity Plan (BCP), and contingency stocks to support overall EPR efforts for host communities and refugees via relevant Sectors and ISCG. IOM, along with its implementing partners, is an integral part of the Rapid Protection Assessment team, Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU), and Mobile Medical Team (MMT). This ensures an immediate response to the needs of people affected by crises and disasters. They maintain close coordination with Site Management and the Camp-in-Charge (CiC) offices and provide support to Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers, offering training on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and safe referrals for GBV. Moreover, IOM and its implementing partner, PULSE Bangladesh Society, will continue to preposition clothing items and Dignity Kits as part of their emergency preparedness and response activities.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Engaging with youth is a priority for IOM. The Organization is committed to developing activities targeting youth and young people on the move, addressing their needs and vulnerabilities and promoting meaningful youth engagement. The project will apply IASC Guidelines on working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises. IOM engages with youth through various programming activities, including EMAP and the Girl Shine curriculum which have strong youth participation. IOM also encourages youth involved in women’s support groups, protection volunteer initiatives, and Community Protection Committees to actively participate in community-led activities and attend relevant capacity-building sessions. The IOM GBV team coordinates with Child Protection, General Protection, and Counter-Trafficking teams to ensure that all youth between the ages of 15 and 24 are supported, regardless of gender or ability.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

IOM’s GBV programming fosters refugee self-reliance by enhancing resilience, safety, and agency among individuals affected by or at risk of GBV. Core strategies include:Psychosocial Support: Survivor-centered counseling and support help survivors heal, build resilience, and regain independence.GBV Prevention and Protection: Preventative measures, awareness, and outreach reduce GBV risks, supporting self-reliant lives in safe environments.Community and Family Networks: IOM strengthens local support systems to foster protective environments where survivors feel valued.Access to Essential Services: Linkages to healthcare, legal aid, and shelter remove barriers to recovery and independence.Capacity Building: Training local partners and security sectors enhances sustainable, survivor-centered GBV response and protection.Economic empowerment- IOM engages Rohingya women in a CFW model, providing them with short term income-generating opportunities and fostering economic resilience.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

IOM Protection team provides specialized expertise in security sector activities within the host community, particularly through training programs for law enforcement actors -APBn and District Police. These trainings are crucial for equipping officers with the necessary skills for victim-centered investigations of cases, and supporting victims of crimes in a protection-sensitive manner. IOM’s GBV programming enhances life skills and provides capacity-building for at-risk individuals. Additionally, IOM’s host community women’s support groups foster peer-led support, share lifesaving information, and enable safe referrals for GBV cases. This approach reflects IOM’s commitment to strengthening the rule of law and fostering safer, more resilient communities—key components for achieving sustainable development in host communities, and in line with Cox's Bazar district development priorities.
