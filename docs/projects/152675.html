Project Summary

In response to the growing humanitarian crisis in Southern Unity, UNIDOR is proposing to enhance the provision of life saving services to 16,000HH conflict affected vulnerable populations in Southern Unity, covering Koch, Leer Mayendit and Panyijiar Counties. These areas are in dire need of humanitarian assistance and UNIDOR already has running projects in these areas providing different interventions. UNIDOR will also integrate food security and livelihood into the already existing UNIDOR's Nutrition, WASH,Protection, Peace and Health programs in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Koch counties.
However, based on the March-September 2018 IPC analysis, it is expected that 6.1 million people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4).
But with calmness experiencing in many parts of the Country and where agriculture activities took place, food security has improved slightly with the green harvest in September relative to July and August, and further improvements are expected in the post-harvest period between October and December 2018 when the number of people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse is most likely to reduce to 4.4 million (43% of the total population), with 26,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5).
In the early of the year 2019, an anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season will result in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). KEY IPC FINDINGS: SEPTEMBER 2018- MARCH 2019. Food insecurity continue to rise to yet higher unprecedented levels due to the protracted combination of violence and conflict. The foreseen deterioration can be attributed to a number of factors: these include continuing economic crisis compounded by spiral commodities price increases, continued inflation, poor harvests and early depletion of meager household stocks, continued population displacements resulting from localized violence and conflict, food insecurity, disruption of livelihood activities, collapse of healthcare and social services. The worst affected populations are the conflict affected host communities and Internally Displaced People (IDPs) who are characterized by minimal assets and low purchasing power to satisfy their food needs. In this context women and children, especially girls, represent a vulnerable group. The most vulnerable to hunger are pregnant/lactating mothers, children, elderly and disabled as they may not be able to walk long distances in search of food. The conflict results in gradual erosion of traditional family structures and support mechanisms, domestic violence and child abduction. Girls are at increased risk of early marriage, early pregnancy, contracting HIV/AIDS and dropping out of school. They are exposed to physical, sexual and psychological violence and harassment.
The proposed project will target conflict affected population in Koch, Mayendit, Leer and Panyijiar Counties being supported through provision of cash vouchers/transfer, distribution of crop seeds, vegetable kits and agricultural tools, distribution of fishing kits, Establish demonstration plots,, formation of farmers producers groups, training of farmers of land –harvest, storage and marketing skills, formation of fisher folk groups, training on fish preservation method, marketing skills and provision of cash vouchers/transfer.

Needs

Food Security and nutrition situation remains critical in south Sudan with an estimated 6.1 million people (59% of the total populations) faced crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at peak of the lean season July- August) of whom 47,000 were in catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in emergency(IPC Phase4). However between January and March 2019 the normal lean season is anticipated to start earlier than normal where food security situation will deteriorate further to an estimated 5.2 million(49% of the total population) people in crisis(IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with 36,000 in of catastrophe(IPC Phase 5) September 2018 – March 2019 IPC. This is as a result of the combination of protracted conflict and violence resulting in large scale displacement and the economic crisis characterized by rising prices of items. This situation has worsened the terms of trade for cereals in exchange for labour or livestock and has continued to drive food insecurity to unprecedented levels. The insecurity due to sporadic political conflict, intra and inter communal conflict and cattle raiding witnessed in Koch, Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar counties continue to uproot and displace people resulting to households abandoning their traditional sources of income and preventing the affected communities from cultivating their lands. This frequent displacement witnessed in these areas has undermined already compromised agricultural production destroying the livelihood of the communities and causing food shortages. The conflict affected population from the four counties who were displaced are returning back to their former homes are in dire need of humanitarian assistance as all their livelihood were destroyed.The affected populations are increasingly adopting negative coping strategies by reducing meals, going without food, surviving on wild fruits or selling their last livestocks. These results to food consumption gaps, reduced dietary diversity and increased household hunger scores. The deepening food insecurity is as result of protracted conflict and insecurity; limited market functionality; high food prices caused by fast-rising inflation and depreciation of the local currency; diminishing purchasing power; depletion of household stocks and high cost of living.A planned intervention will enable UNIDOR's food security department and other partners to address the current severe food insecurity situation in Koch, Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar Counties focusing on famine prevention and response through multi-sect oral integrated actions. This will be through distribution of crop seeds, vegetable kits and agricultural tools, distribution of fishing kits, distribution of cash vouchers/transfers, establishment of demonstration farms, formation of fisher folk groups, formation of seed producer groups, training of farmers on the best agricultural techniques practices, training of farmers on integrated pest management practices, post- harvest and good storage practices, training of fishermen on fish handling procedure, processing, other preservation methods and marketing and training of Community Animal Health workers on basic animal health. This project aims at enabling the community increase food production and to save lives of the affected population of Mayendit, Leer, Koch and Panyijiar Counties of Unity State.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultative mechanisms with the community to gather information on the needs and specific vulnerabilities of potential beneficiaries will be set-up. The consultative process will include respective members of the IDPs, returnees and host communities to ensure that decisions taken by the group will be accepted by the broader community; however, UNIDOR will also make sure that groups include men and women, boys and girls, as well as representatives from vulnerable and marginalized groups. This community-based approach will integrate the information employed for community targeting and contribute to the response design of the intervention.
