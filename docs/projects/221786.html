Project Summary

Medair plans to implement multi sectoral interventions in Leer, Pibor and multistate under the Emergency Response Team (ERT) in IPC 4 and 5 locations. According to the Intersectoral Prioritization map for 2025, both Leer and Pibor are Priority 1 counties. Medair will provide WASH services that are integrated with health, nutrition, MHPSS internally in all its static and emergency response project sites as well as collaborate with other nutrition, health and protection partners, to maximize the impact of the proposed WASH project. The project is designed based on Core Humanitarian and Sphere Minimum Standards to ensure that affected populations receive timely, coordinated, adequate, resilient, and impartial basic WASH services. Medair involves diverse groups of affected populations in the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of WASH programs, ensuring they are informed and included. It also adheres to the "Do no harm" principle to avoid exposing beneficiaries to risks. Medair will apply the AQA approach to deliver high-quality WASH responses, essential for saving lives, preventing harm, and promoting dignity. The proposed WASH project will adhere to the WASH Cluster technical guidelines and WHO drinking water quality standards. WASH activities will integrate with health efforts to prevent and respond to water-related diseases like diarrhoea, cholera, by breaking transmission cycles.
According to the IPC 2024 report, in 49 counties the acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%. The report also indicates that the nutrition situation has deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025. Contributing factors to this crisis is low access to safe sources of drinking water which has led to the consumption of unsafe water and increased incidences of water borne diseases such as diarrhoea that is exacerbated with compromised hygiene practices. Open defecation is also rampant with a national average of 74% which increases chances of environmental contamination, disease burden and acute malnutrition. Flooding remains a significant challenge, especially in Jonglei and Unity State, where our project sites are located.
Medair will address poor access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services by drilling new boreholes; rehabilitating non-functional handpumps; distribution of water containers and water treatment chemicals, monitoring drinking water quality and disinfecting contaminated water sources; promoting good hygiene and sanitation practices and training of community members (handpump mechanics and water user committees) on operation, maintenance and effective management of water points.
To support health and nutrition clinics, access to safe and sufficient water will be ensured through constructing or rehabilitating boreholes and installing water storage tanks. Adequate sanitation will be ensured through the construction of safe and gender-segregated latrines, rehabilitation of existing latrines and the provision of sufficient functional handwashing stations. WASH will also support the health team with aspects of Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) activities at facilities. WASH NFIs will be distributed to patients admitted to nutrition programs targeting malnourished children under 5 to reduce their risk of contracting water-related diseases.
For emergency responses, we will coordinate closely with the WASH Cluster as part of the Rapid Response Team (RRT). The interventions are expected to last a maximum of 3 months. As part of the exit strategy after the intervention, ERT aims to augment local capacity and fill critical gaps in response to acute emergencies.
Overall, the project is targeted to reach 23,500 people through improved access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services by the end of 2025.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has worsened due to economic challenges, severe flooding, and inter-communal violence. Declining funding has reduced WASH partners' ability to address growing needs, exacerbating poor access to WASH services and food insecurity. This has led to deteriorating health conditions, including high malnutrition rates, and increased vulnerability to waterborne diseases, particularly among women, children, and the elderly. According to HNRP 2024, approximately 5.6 million people will require WASH assistance in 2024. A significant portion of the population (41 per cent) lacks access to improved water sources and 37 per cent need to travel over 30 minutes to reach their primary water source, posing hardships for persons with disabilities and making women and girls more vulnerable to GBV. The 2025 HNRP is targeting 2.25 million people out of the 5.01 million people in need, including host communities, IDPs, and returnees, to address the pressing needs. According to the IPC 2024 report, in 49 counties the acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%. Additionally, the report also indicates that the nutrition situation has deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). Contributing factors to this crisis is poor WASH practices. Low access to safe sources of drinking water has led to the consumption of unsafe water and increased incidences of water borne diseases such as diarrhoea that is exacerbated with compromised hygiene practices. Open defecation is also rampant with a national average of 74% which increases chances of environmental contamination, disease burden and acute malnutrition. Flooding remains a significant challenge, especially in Jonglei and Unity State, where our project sites are located. According to Medair KPC surveys, the average per capita water usage in Pibor is 10.6 l/p/d, while in Leer it is 10.46 l/p/d, both of which are below the minimum Sphere standards. Reduced humanitarian access for WASH actors due to insecurity or flooding occurs frequently in Pibor, severely limiting the effective delivery of WASH services. The area has an insufficient and poorly distributed number of handpumps, especially outside Pibor Town. These pumps frequently break down due to inadequate management, are prone to flooding, and are at risk of being targeted during intercommunal violence. As a result, large parts of the community are forced to rely primarily on surface water for drinking and domestic purposes. According to the Pibor SMART survey 2024, 59.2% of the population still have no service as per the JMP ladder and rely on surface water as their main source of drinking water. This indicates significant gaps in terms of access and highlights the need for additional sources. Knowledge and practice of proper hygiene behaviours is limited in South Sudan. However, even when the knowledge exists for such a critical practice as handwashing soap is not widely available. This environment, characterized by insufficient water and lack of latrines and poor hygiene practices increases the risk of water related illnesses particularly for children under 5 suffering from malnutrition. The 2024 Leer and Pibor SMART surveys reveal a strong hygiene practice in Leer, where 78.2% of households wash their hands 3 to 5 critical times daily. In contrast, Pibor shows a significant gap, with only 48% of households aware of these critical handwashing time. The WASH programs will be integrated with both health and nutrition programs at facility and community level. Medair also has an emergency response team which responds to emergencies all over the country based on triggers, where partners on the ground have limited or no capacity to respond.

2. Project activities

The goal is to increase access to quality essential services and reduce morbidity and mortality through integrated health, nutrition, protection, and WASH services for emergency affected IDP, returnees and host populations in targeted areas in South Sudan. This contributes to sectoral objectives SO1 to SO4 of the WASH Cluster objectives. Needs assessments will be conducted to understand the context and environmental and climate factors that may influence the project during implementation. This will include an analysis of factors that contribute to gender inequality in WASH, including an understanding of the gendered norms, roles and barriers that impact on access to, use of and influence on WASH.Supporting nutrition programmes and improving access to basic WASH services are integral parts of WASH activities in Priority 1 Counties and IPC 4/5 locations across the country. Medair will address poor access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services through construction and rehabilitation of resilient water points such as boreholes, water yards and surface water treatment systems (SWATs). Medair will scale up raising handpump platforms in flood affected areas. In areas where there is no safe water source, Medair will consider distribution of household water treatment systems such as chlorine products and water filters with water collection and storage containers to targeted households. Medair will also monitor drinking water quality and disinfect contaminated water sources; promote good hygiene and sanitation practices and train community members (handpump mechanics and water user committees) on operation, maintenance, and effective management of water points. To support Medair-supported health and nutrition clinics, access to safe and sufficient water will be ensured through constructing new boreholes, repairing / rehabilitating non-functional handpumps and installing water storage tanks. Adequate sanitation will be ensured through the construction of safe and gender-segregated new latrines and rehabilitation of existing latrines and the provision of sufficient functional handwashing stations. Medair will only consider emergency latrine construction in the community in active displacement and disease outbreaks and if their absence has a significant public health risk. WASH will also support the health team with aspects of Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) activities at facilities. WASH NFIs will be distributed to patients admitted to nutrition programs targeting malnourished children under 5 to reduce their risk of contracting water-related diseases. Improving water quality and availability, along with better hygiene practices, will reduce the risk of waterborne disease transmission and indirectly lower the prevalence of acute malnutrition.In all project locations, Medair ensures the ‘Do No Harm’ principle is implemented by training the WASH staff on PSEA and creating community wide awareness about PSEA. Furthermore, Medair will continue to integrate protection mainstreaming by conducting safety audits and disability-inclusive focus group discussions (FGDs) with individuals from various vulnerable groups, particularly women, girls, and people with disabilities, during the design stage of interventions. The aim is to identify specific protection risks, such as GBV or concerns related to the proposed intervention, and to mitigate these risks wherever possible while ensuring that the Do No Harm principles are applied. Medair will conduct KPC surveys, exit interviews and PDMs to assess the project's overall impact on target beneficiaries, including changes in hygiene behaviour and to assess the relevance, appropriateness and timeliness of the support provided to target beneficiaries. It will also monitor the protection mainstreaming key output indicators to assess the safety, dignity, do no harm; meaningful access; participation and empowerment and accountability.
