Needs

Malnutrition is one of the most persistent humanitarian challenges in Somalia despite concerted efforts to address it. Acute malnutrition in children between 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, WASH and health situations among others. The signicant deterioration of malnutrition situation seen among the IDPs can be attributed to high morbidity (disease incidence e.g. AWD, measles …etc), low humanitarian support, poor child feeding and caring practices. Similarly, partners on the ground generally felt that food insecurity, limited health service availability, like poor EPI coverage, and increased morbidity, poor health seeking behaviour, and diculty of accessing clean water supply are driving factors for the current situation. Malnutrition is an outcome indicator highlighting needs a number of sectors, and the vulnerability of communities. DA has good coordination with federal MOH and as well JMOH in their common view as well as decision making when planning projects, and contributes its experience during its project implementation in Somalia and applied region,Gedo in particular, such as needs,gaps,risks,and accessibility of all administration in Belethawa district and will implement community leading projects having its role of decision making, Communities and providing accessibility and timely information to affected population by establishment nutrition committees, Food management committees, community help desk and community suggestion boxes in order to raise their concern and appreciation to the project. DIRECT AID (DA) plans to implement integrated nutrition program TSFP,BSFP,MCHN and IYCF in Belethawa districts Gedo region in order to respond above mentioned nutrition crisis according to latest nutrition survey ( Gu 2016),and during DA implementation in the coming 12 months of 2019,will attend 12 nutrition sub clusters and other technical meetings.

Activities

1. Conduct 180 IYCF promotion sessions for women of child bearing age, pregnant and lactating women one session per visit/month for all 15 fixed nutrition sites and 19 satellite villages in Belethawa.2. Establish 40 IYCF community support groups in all 15 food distribution points (FDPs) fixed sites and 19 satellite villages which are mobile sites.3. Conduct 3 IYCF training and 36 individual group counselling sessions for women and men with beneficiaries of 780 each village/nutrition site 52 women.4. Refer the nearest OTP 371 boys, 432 girls who are severely malnutrition (SAM) cases and will receive further treatment from the OTP sites.5. Screen 300 boys and girls 400 and treat them for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) in the OTP sites6. Screen 3000 boys, 4000 girls and treat them for moderate malnutrition in the TSFP sites in Belethawa district Gedo Region.7. Screen and treat 1800 pregnant and lactating women 600 in the 15 TSFP sites and 1200 at the Halanley MCHN) in Belethawa Halanley Village which is currently operational8. 60 community volunteers mobilize the community to send their children for treatment in OTP/TSFP in the 15 static nutrition sites and 19 mobile nutrition villages9. Train 180 health educators/ mobilizers to the community (90 men and 90 women) to support IMAM programmes, and enhance the quality of nutrition implementation.10. Provide systematic treatment (3000 boys, 4000 girls) with Vitamin A and deworming11. Provide 1800 pregnant and lactating women (600 in the TSFP sites and 1200 in the MCHN )with multiple micronutrients/ FeFo supplements12. Provide 700 boys and 700 girls under the age of two with multiple micronutrients at the MCHN and 34 nutrition sites.13. Conduct 15 hygiene, sanitation, health and nutrition promotion sessions in the community on a monthly basis, each static village one hygienic promotion sessions14. Provide soap to 3000 boys, 4000 girls, 1800 pregnant and lactating women on a monthly basis15. Provide water
