Project Summary

An estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and face significant consumption gaps through December 2019, an extended hunger season following the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall. An additional 4.2 million Somalis are in IPC 2 (Stressed) and engage in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. In total, 6.3 million Somalis face acute food insecurity through the end of 2019. The food security situation reflect the cumulative effects of below-average rains experienced across most parts of Somalia during the 2018 Deyr (October-December),followed by harsh weather conditions during the dry 2019 Jilaal (January–March) season and the delayed and poor performance of the 2019 Gu (April-June). These consecutive seasonal poor performances have been exacerbated by limited recovery from the extended impacts of the severe 2016/2017 drought. An estimated 2.6 million Somalis across Somalia remain displaced primarily due to conflict, lack of livelihood opportunities and drought.The project aims at addressing food insecurity and building resilience of the drought affected IDPs and host communities in qandala and Bossaso. The project targets an estimated 500 households, particularly women, children the elderly and the poorest of the poor families. Assistance will help affected households to meet their immediate and long term food and cash needs, as well as provide appropriate capacity building to the target beneficiaries. The priority of the strategy is to protect life and dignity (to reduce the risks and effects of disasters), empowering and supporting the marginalized and vulnerable communities to being income and food secure households. The project will not only address the immediate causes of food insecurity but also build on structures to address the underlying causes of food insecurity through capacity building of the locals and also integration with other sectors such as nutrition IEC materials

Mitigation Measures

Vulnerability analysis and needs assessment done together with the target beneficiaries have informed the project design and defined activities, numbers and the profiles of people to target for food security, which in turn will help to generate criteria and choice of approaches. The project consultation didn't only look at the community leaders but focused further on the different ages and genders across the selected locations for information and opinion which better informs the projects. The project intends to reach to women, men, girls, and boys throughout the project implementation period, through the designed project activities that beneficiaries directly are targeted and furthermore the information dissemination activities. This measures out access to beneficiaries with the project.The project intends to have community meeting in relation to the project activities with the community leaders to solicit feedback and further also intends to hold monthly meetings with women and girls who attend activities at the TSS to address their concerns on the activities enrolled and make changes on the activitiesThe project has put measures to and reporting channels regarding any protection issue be it from our staff or the beneficiaries themselves to prevent violations of rights from occurring or recurring stop ongoing violations, provide remedies, through reparation and rehabilitation, if violations have occurred; and foster an environment conducive to respect for the rights of women, men, girls, boys and persons with disability in accordance with the law. TSS has a safeguarding policy which will be used to guide the project staff, Reporting System, Disciplinary Action Policy Zero Tolerance for all forms of PSEA articulated in Safeguarding Policy All staff orientation on PSEA Routine staff refresher training on PSEA, Information Materials in place.As the project design is to benefit the host community, IDPs, returnees and other affected populations, the project intends to ensure that the services provided don't pose threats to the beneficiaries but rather address protection concerns across the population group targeted through carrying out joint activities and also further the project intends to engage the local authorities in the intended project locations.TSS will Incorporate diversity analysis into all aspects of our work is essential to understanding and addressing the different protection risks within a community by doing so TSS envisages to recognizing that, within a community, different members face particular inequalities, risks, and needs, depending on their age, gender and social or other backgrounds, identifying and analyzing the different protection risks faced by women and men of different ages and backgrounds. This includes protection risks arising from their particular situation and the protection implications of our own activities; and taking targeted action to address and prevent these risks and inequalities and to empower groups within the community to claim their rights.TSS will also support the establishment of complaint and conflict resolution methods/mechanisms, including representatives of both communities, to address and resolve any disputes that may arise. Such mechanisms will include both women and men of different ages and backgrounds, will be developed with modest material support, for instance in the form of meeting space, transport and/or refreshments during meetings. Mechanisms will be encouraged to pay particular attention to and address any disputes that may arise over the use of land and other resources at an early stage.
