Project Summary

Aligned with the Food Security sector's strategy, this action aims at alleviating the most urgent food and non-food needs of 3250 vulnerable Lebanese and Migrant households across the country.
1- Provision of unconditional and unrestricted cash assistance to 2650 (2250 Lebanese, 400 Migrant)
households (respectively 9600 Lebanese individuals, 1800 Migrant individuals) in the form of Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) over a period of six months.
This action is an expansion of AAH’s ongoing Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) portfolio. AAH will identify potential beneficiaries through the existing nationwide referral systems in addition to direct referrals from other NGOs, self-referrals received through the AAH hotline, and referrals from municipalities as well as recent MPCA vulnerability assessments conducted for ongoing projects. For the migrant community specifically, AAH has started to engage with IOM which agreed to collaborate on a referral mechanism and to set up key communication channels for the outreach to migrant communities in the project areas. Beneficiaries will be selected through AAH’s pre-defined vulnerability criteria based on core context-related indicators aligned to FS and BA sectors guidelines (eligibility survey including core indicators such as the Food Consumption Score, reduced Coping Strategy Index, Persons with special needs, etc.). To complement AAH's nutrition programming under the ERP, 40% of the targeted HHs will include pregnant women, lactating women, and/or caregivers of children under 5.
As recommended by the FS chapter under the ERP, monthly cash transfer of an approx value of 1,600,000 LBP per household according to the household size (300,000 per person per month to cover food needs and LBP 400,000 for non-food needs per household based on a family size of four) will be provided to the target population. Cash transfers will be redeemed through the LOUISE platform operating with Banque Libano-Francaise (BLF) which enables AAH to benefit from a preferential exchange rate (currently set at 17,000 LBP/USD) and allows to target beneficiaries without IDs, which is essential to reach migrant communities. On an exceptional and case-by-case basis, cash envelopes may be provided to extremely vulnerable HHs who cannot safely access the Financial Service Provider. AAH will closely monitor transfer amounts and coordinate with the Food Security and Basic Assistance Working Groups, of which it is an active member, to ensure assistance is of value and well-coordinated with other actors and stakeholders. Contingent upon reactivation of the Migrant Workers Coordination Group and Cash Task Force AAH will ensure close collaboration with those entities as well.
2- Provision of a 6-month assistance package including dry food parcels to 600 Lebanese households through the LHF.
In line with the LHF strategy and using OCHA's unified vulnerability targeting tool, AAH will provide nutritionally balanced food assistance through dry rations paired with WASH kits to mitigate hygiene risks for a duration of 6 months in Baalbek-Hermel, Bekaa, South, and Nabatiyeh.
3- Provision of one-off dry food parcels and hygiene kits to COVID-19 affected households through the LHF.
As part of its ongoing COVID-19 response, AAH will provide nutritionally balanced food assistance through dry rations to 100 households (regardless of nationality) as per the agreed geosplit between the partners in the South governorate (Sector 6 and 7). An additional caseload will be considered as a contingency plan based on the evolution of the COVID-19 pandemic and variants.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Prior to any cash-based and MPCA project, AAH conducts a full Protection Risk Analysis taking account the specific vulnerabilities and capacities of right-holders and identify the threats they are exposed to and the risks they may face because of the intervention. This will be a living document and will be updated to integrate new, unforeseen risks as they arise. In addition, AAH’s code of conduct, gender policy, child protection policy, and PSEA SoPs are systematically included and made binding in contracts with FSPs. AAH stakeholders are held accountable for any breach of the CoC that may lead to contract terminations when serious breaches occur. The safety and dignity of right-holders in accessing the FSP will be tracked and monitored throughout the program by MEAL and via the hotline. Tailored transfer modalities will be adjusted to ensure people with specific protection risks are also able to access assistance in a safe and dignified way, without being exposed to potential harm.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

AAH ensures the consultation and participation of beneficiaries through the need assessments, baseline, PDM, consultations, as well as a dedicated hotline throughout the intervention. AAH has extensive experience monitoring cash transfers specifically, the hotline will be used as the primary point of contact for HH for which a dedicated staff are trained. PDM and endline assessments will be carried out by MEAL team to make programmatic adjustment and to evaluate the impact of the cash distribution. Through the FCRM, right-holders will have access to AAH hotline & will be able to raise complaint/feedback around access to assistance, impact of receiving assistance, inclusion/exclusion errors, etc. AAH has a 2-track FCRM to address issues related to the project that are non-sensitive & can be solved by the MEAL and project teams and for sensitive issues against AAH staff, its stakeholders, and allegations of PSEA, with separate channels. All referrals to be tracked through RIMS as needed.
