Project Summary

It is estimated that there are 2.6 million internally displaced persons in Somalia (2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview). Successive years of drought exacerbated by a prolonged conflict has resulted in an enduring humanitarian crisis that demands urgent life saving interventions. Concomitantly, the protracted nature of this crisis requires a strategy that sees beyond the immediate needs and incorporates durable solutions throughout the response. With this project IOM will provide camp management and camp coordination services to men, women, boys and girls in displacement sites in the districts of Baidoa, Kismayu and Doolow to ensure that the most urgent needs of the IDPs are met, while also setting the foundation for the achievement of durable solutions.
Latest estimations of displacement conditions in Somalia indicate that around 2.3 million people have settled in around 2,400 IDP sites (CCCM Cluster HRP 2021 Guidance note), most of which are informal, small and scattered settlements in urban or peri-urban areas. Living in private land often means that IDPs are at constant risk of eviction, which further stresses their already vulnerable situation. Protracted conflict-driven displacement aggravated by natural disasters, limited livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services and limited access to durable solutions all contribute to the continued increase in the number and vulnerability of IDPs.
Baidoa hosts the largest number of IDPs after Mogadishu with more than 392,300 displaced persons living in 517 sites (CCCM site verification exercise). In 2019 government authorities allocated public land for IDPs which has enabled IOM and other humanitarian/development partners to relocate families at risk of eviction, linking traditional CCCM activities with an approach tailored towards the achievement of durable solutions. As more public land becomes available to IDPs, IOM plans to support more families to relocate. However, for the foreseeable future the majority of families will remain in makeshift sites under the constant threat of forced eviction.
In Kismayo, 64,000 IDPs are hosted in 144 sites (CCCM site verification exercise). These sites are privately owned and most IDPs have to pay to live there; however, these agreements do not prevent families from the risk of being evicted. Most of these IDP sites are overcrowded and lack basic infrastructure; living conditions are poor with overstretched services and shelters often made of locally available materials such as plastic and worn-out clothes.
Doolow is currently hosting around 62,600 IDPs in two makeshift sites located in public land: Kabasa and Qansaxley. The high population density in these camps and the subsequent concentration of service provision makes service monitoring and coordination key aspects of CCCM related needs. In addition, community mobilization and the enactment of representative governance structures are needed to facilitate dialogue between service providers and affected populations, and to enhance accountability towards them.

Project Activities

IOM will continue CCCM activities in Baidoa, Dollow and Kismayo. IOM will coordinate the delivery of multiisectoral services in sites by representing CCCM in relevant coordination forums; conducting site verifications on a quarterly basis; mapping the services available in sites; monitoring service delivery in sites. IOM will improve living conditions of displaced people by supporting community led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites; conducting safety audits to identify areas of high protection risk; implementing emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites, developing new land for the relocation of displaced people and facilitating decongestion activities to attain minimum living standards for IDPs. IOM will strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations by supporting diverse and inclusive governance structures; providing capacity building to existing governance structures on CCCM and related principles; providing community spaces for management committees; and operating timely and responsive feedback and complaint mechanisms - accessible at help desks in community centers, through outreach teams or via a toll-free hotline.

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1: Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation measures: IOM will ensure all staff operating in a camp and camp-like setting are trained in the Humanitarian Principles and Do No Harm (one of the Protection Principles) and sign and abide by the Code of Conduct including PSEA. The camp manger will ensure that all referrals for GBV is mapped and updated quarterly and this information is passed on to beneficiaries during outreach work or at the information desks. Risk 2: Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) Mitigation measures: IOM will conduct safety audits of sites in collaboration with the appropriate partners to identify safety risks and mitigate GBV. IOM will ensure the training all community workers in GBV referrals. IOM will coordinate with GBV partners to ensure that GBV referral pathways are operational and known to community members in IDP sites. Risk 3: Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures: IOM will ensure diverse and inclusive governance structures are present in sites that represent all subsets of populations. IOM will ensure 2 way feedback mechanisms for populations to voice their concerns on services and site governance. IOM will provide information to service providers on service availability and access in IDP sites to ensure non biased targeting of IDP sites by partners. IOM will encourage the participation of women through activities specifically geared towards reducing barriers to women’s participation. Where no governance structure is presence IOM will support communities to come together and agree on the type of representation they would require that allows the participation of all groups in decision making. Risk 4: Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area. Mitigation measures: IOM will work with HLP partners to ensure that all IOM managed sites have their eviction status mapped. IOM will work with local authorities to mitigate evictions of displaced populations IOM will ensure the land tenure of all public land designated for relocation of displaced populations is documented legally.
