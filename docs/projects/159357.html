Project Summary

The proposed action is aligned with the 2018-2020 HRP priorities in the Shelter/NFI sector for Gaza Strip (speciﬁcally Objectives 2 and 3): “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL” and “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.” It is also in line with the Shelter/NFI cluster third objective in oPt: “Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, eﬃcient and effective manner is increased”. The proposed project will increase preparedness and response capacity of Middle Area and Khan Yunis communities in case of future escalations of hostilities, speciﬁcally through the prepositioning of Emergency NFI kits for Internally Displaced People (IDPs) in Designated Emergency Shelters (DES) and Host Communities (hosting IDPs); establishment and capacity- building of a Community-based Emergency Response Network (CERN), based on existing community-based associations, providing of an Emergency assistance (including NFI distribution and protection mainstreaming) to IDPs and to Host Communities during escalations of hostilities. Based on assessments of the international community since 2014, and extensive consultation with key stakeholders, i.e. Shelter Cluster and Palestinian Authority in charge of 22 DES in the Gaza Strip, and currently ongoing project, the project will focus on Middle Area and Khan Yunis governorate. In case of hostilities’ escalation, it is expected that large-scale movement of individuals will occur from North East to Mid and South West of Middle Area and Khan Yunis, but also from Northern governorates (Gaza, North Gaza), based on past 2014 trends. In total, the project will beneﬁt 13,074, including 11,571 IDPs and 240 hosting households in Middle Area and Khan Yunis governorate: 2,270 IDPs and Host-community households are expected to be assisted by 2,270 Emergency NFI lots prepositioned during the project and distributed to affected households in case of crisis. Based on a standard calculation using as reference the average of 1 NFI Lot/HH, a minimum of 12,939 IDPs and Host individuals will beneﬁt from NFIs, based on Palestine’s standard average of 5,7 members/HH in GS. ACTED Project team expects to reach a higher target since some beneﬁciary HHs will not receive the full NFI Lot including 6 NFIs kits, but part of it, based on HHs’ proﬁle and vulnerability identiﬁed during rapid needs assessment following ACTED trigger for the deployment of Emergency Response; and 135 community members (including CBOs, CBPCs and community volunteers) will be engaged under the Community-based Emergency Response Network (CERN) and trained in Emergency Response. The project is supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach, with speciﬁc focus on women and vulnerable groups, and a strong Accountability to Affected Populations. Gender principles will be mainstreamed, CERN will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases with a particular focus to women and children issues i.e. GBV. ACTED Protection Oﬃcer will be focal point of the committees to receive and follow-up on cases. During and after the project, ACTED will remain the ﬁrst Emergency Responder for Middle Area and Khan Yunis governorate to trigger and supervise the Emergency Response plan to be deployed by CERN members. This project builds on a similar project that ACTED is currently implementing in Rafah governorate, funded by OCHA. The currently ongoing project which aims at strengthening the capacity of Rafah community to face escalations of conﬂicts, is proving to be very successful and generated large engagement among Rafah community members and volunteers.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip’s fragile humanitarian situation caused by over 10 years of Israeli blockade has been signiﬁcantly deteriorated with the succession of violent events since 2008-2009, including three wars (Operations “Cast Lead” in 2008/2009, “Pillar of Defense” in 2012 and “Protective Edge” in 2014) and Great March of Return in 2018. During the 2014 war, nearly 500,000 people representing 28% of the total Gaza population were internally displaced (UNOCHA, 2016). Most of Internally Displaced People (IDPs) sought emergency shelters within the Host community directly or in Designated Emergency Shelters (DES), managed either by UNRWA or the Palestinian Authority (PA). In total about 300,000 IDPs found shelter in UNRWA DES (“Internal Displacement in the Context of the 2014 Hostilities factsheet”, OCHA, 2014). In The Middle Area, about 18,085 individuals were considered as IDPs during and after the 2014 war; among them, 13,394 found shelter in the UNRWA DES (no PA DES at this time) and 4,691 were hosted in Households (HHs) of local families (Host community). About 14,600 individuals are still internally displaced from the 2014 hostilities (2019 Humanitarian Overview - HNO, OCHA). In case of future violent escalations, the Gaza population will face severe diﬃculties in accessing to essential basic resources and services and in ﬁnding solutions and coping mechanisms to sustain basic families’ needs. Former violent crisis has shown that the level of preparedness to respond effectively and in a timely manner to IDPs’ needs in case of conﬂict was very limited (HF Allocation Strategy Paper 2019). The action aims at increasing/strengthening the capacity of the Middle Area and Khan Yunicommunities to face escalations of violence through the prepositioning of Emergency NFI assistance and the creation of a Community-based Emergency Response Network led by ACTED. This project will assist IDPs who seek shelters in the Host community and in the PA-managed DES in Middle Area and Khan Yunis and as such, reduce the burden on the Host community during the onset of crisis. During and after escalations of violence, IDPs’ HHs remain vulnerable to a range of protection threats including GBV, particularly for women and children. A UNFPA survey (“Conditions and suffering of girls and women in shelters and with host families during the latest war on Gaza”, 2014) shows that, during the 2014 war, only 20% of Non-Food Items (NFIs) assistance related to personal and hygiene needs were safely available for girls (16% for women). Lack of safe and adequate access to such parcels, especially during the ﬁrst days of aggression, as well as after dispersion and displacement, negatively impacts vulnerable groups of IDPs, in particular women and children. Host-community HHs also suffer from additional economical and psychosocial burden with the hosting of IDPs, especially when IDPs and Host families do not share family relationship nor equivalent social background. The action will ensure that IDPs and Host families of the two governorates can access to basic families’ needs, essential to ensure security/safety/dignity of IDPs. As conﬁrmed by ACTED ongoing project in Rafah, despite existence of DES, there is a lack of Emergency stockpiling arrangements and therefore, lack of access to Emergency NFIs in several areas of the Strip. Furthermore, ACTED identiﬁed high needs in terms of capacity-building of Gaza communities to respond to conﬂict escalation. Since 2016, ACTED conducts annual Multi Sectorial Assessments in Gaza, thus ensuring up-to-date data about HHs proﬁle, concerns and needs. A rapid- needs’ assessment on IDPs’ HHs’ proﬁle will be conducted by ACTED and CERN to adjust the implementation of the Emergency Response Plan based on HHs’ speciﬁc needs to ensure adequate and inclusive Emergency NFIs’ distribution. This rapid needs ‘assessment will enable the CERN to identify vulnerabilities of IDPs’ HHs.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Establishment and capacity-building of Community-based Emergency Response Network (CERN). ACTED will be ﬁrst responder and will lead deployment of CERN, which will continue after the project. ACTED will partner with existing CBOs and established Community- Based Protection Committees. The established CERN will be composed of members of the CBOs(9), CBPCs (21) and Community Volunteers Network (105). During massive population displacement (due to conflicts or natural disasters), they will participate in all steps of Emergency Response, based on ACTED Contingency Plan and SoPs, including rapid needs assessment, organization/monitoring of NFI distribution, information sharing, implementation of CRM, protection monitoring of IDPs/Host. A detailed Contingency Plan will be developed describing all the cases and procedures to be followed during emergency. An electronic version of the needs assessment, developed during previous project, will facilitate data collection and analysis and accelerate the NFIs distribution. CERN will be responsible for implementation of ER plan, triggered and managed without limited ACTED ﬁeld presence. Activity 2: Establishment and empowerment of a volunteers’ network. ACTED will encourage commitment and ownership of the project by CERN members. The CBOs and CBPCs will be responsible of the establishment of a Community Volunteers’ Network (105), to increase the response capacity of the ER. The volunteers will inform the affected communities about emergency distribution points when the ER is triggered, and will support during emergency distribution. ToR will be prepared and distributed to the 105 volunteers, and they will receive a training protection and emergency preparedness. 240 HHs will be identiﬁed for their ability to receive IDPs during emergency based on eligibility criteria (proximity to the DES, previous experience in hosting IDPs etc.). They will be trained in Protection, GBV, crowd management and involved in developing the contingency plan. ACTED will refer the most vulnerable HHs in need of shelter repair, in order to adequately host the IDPs, to relevant stakeholders. Activity 3: Set-up of 4 warehouses in Middle Area and Khan Yunis. ACTED, together with the local CBOs, will identify warehouses to stockpile 2,270 emergency NFIs. The warehouses will be under full oversight and ownership of the CBOs. The warehouses will be selected based on the geographical situation and the basic stockpiling requirements. ACTED will equip the warehouses with security and safety materials. It will ensure their commitment during the project, and 24 months after its end (including management of warehouses and coordination with ACTED and CBPCs). ACTED, along with the identiﬁed CBOs, will design a stockpiling plan and a management tool, based on ACTED’s experience in emergency NFI’s prepositioning, and will provide a training on warehouse management and monitoring. Activity 4: Purchase and storage of 2,270 Emergency consolidated NFIs kits ACTED has identiﬁed 6 NFI sets to be packaged as one standardized NFI Set. Each Host Household (HHs) would be eligible to receive (based on needs): 1 Sealing-Off set, 1 Bedding set, 1 Hygiene kit, 1 Washing set, 1 Cleaning set and 1 Family dignity & ﬁrst aid kit. ACTED will launch a competitive tender to purchase the kits to support 2,270 HHs (IDPs and IDPs Hosts). Standardized by the Shelter cluster, the kits will include essential material to address basic needs of IDPs and Host Communities. At the time of hostilities, ACTED will closely coordinate with shelter cluster and OCHA to trigger the deployment of Emergency Response by CERN, based on the emergency level. ACTED and CERN will conduct rapid-needs’ assessment of the IDPs and Host Households in order to identify and record HHs’ vulnerabilities based on gender, age and other vulnerability criteria to ensure a gender integrated emergency response. NFIs should be accessible and/or and/or delivered in a safe way.

3 - Indicators

In addition to the cluster standard indicators, ACTED will measure the following indicators: # of households with improved capacity to host IDPs (target: 120); % of female members of Community-based Emergency Response Network (CERN) (target 40%); % of CERN members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the capacity-building training. (Target 85%). The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit, independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. If the Emergency Response Plan is triggered during the time of project, ACTED will be responsible to conduct Onsite Monitoring Checklist/NFI and Post Distribution Monitoring/NFI. AME activities will enable reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data and measuring project outcomes of different targeted groups. ACTED will be responsible for the overall monitoring of projects’ indicators (cluster and ACTED indicators) and workplan. The delivery of purchased items will be controlled by ACTED team and the four CBOs in Middle Area and Khan Yunis. Random checks on NFIs sets will be conducted at the time of delivery to ensure that types/quality/quantity of NFIs comply to ACTED standards and technical speciﬁcations mentioned in contracts signed with selected suppliers. CBOs will be responsible for monitoring the prepositioning of NFIs in the warehouses and will provide monthly inventory reports to ACTED including updates of warehouses’ plans, list of items, stock’s pictures, and observations on material conditions, warehouse security/safety, if any. ACTED will conduct random checks on warehouses throughout the project duration in order to ensure accuracy of inventory reports provided by CBOs. ACTED and CBOs are engaged to maintain NFIs prepositioning in the subsequent 24 months of the project as per MoU to be signed at the beginning of project implementation. ACTED AME unit will monitor CERN participation and commitment during capacity-building activity by applying Onsite Training Observation Sheet, as well as through the Pre-Training Assessment and Post Training Evaluation, allowing team to identify any gap/potential weaknesses. Impact of trainings will be evaluated during a practical “simulation” exercise which will enable ACTED team to identify potential weaknesses and gaps in the organization and delivery of Emergency NFIs’ distribution. After this evaluation, ACTED team will organize a ﬁnal capacity-building sessions to share feedback on simulation exercises, identiﬁed good/wrong practices, lessons learnt and recommendations for future ER deployment.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ﬁrstly ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including Palestine. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of the activities. Partner community-members (CBOs, CBPCs and volunteers) will be informed and trained on CRM in order to share information with Middle Area communities and encourage beneﬁciaries to report any issues or concerns if escalation of hostilities happens during the time of project, thus triggering the deployment of CERN in Middle Area. Several channels will be available to beneﬁciaries (including IDPs, Host families and other community members) to ﬁle a complaint or raises concerns: through a CERN member (who will then refer it to ACTED) and ACTED dedicated phone line open 24/7 (incl. via SMS/WhatsApp), dedicated email address, complaints desk (during distribution), complaints box (within ACTED and CBOs’ oﬃces) and AME monitoring visits. All beneﬁciaries of the action will be explained their right to provide complaints in conﬁdentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance, and will be provided with small pamphlets summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth stressing that the CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, ﬁeld paper forms, complaint tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanism, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries and non beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanism features several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBPC members, ACTED Protection Officer to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

During the project, ACTED will raise awareness of the CERN and community members about humanitarian and protection principles. ACTED will train them in conducting rapid needs’ assessment to be implemented 3 days following crisis’ trigger. The rapid needs’ assessment will be an essential tool to prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on affected populations. The NFIs kits are designed by Shelter cluster based on SPHERE and protection humanitarian standards. ACTED solid experience in implementing similar Emergency projects in Iraq, Syria, Nepal or Haiti, enable teams to beneﬁt from the capitalization of lessons learnt which contribute to building ACTED expertise in Emergency assistance and inconstantly improving projects’ design and implementation modalities. Finally, the establishment of community-based emergency network (CERN) will develop and strengthen links to local communities, ensuring that feedback is shared and communicated to ACTED project team whenever new needs/concerns or implementation’s gaps are identiﬁed throughout the project. The CERN members will be trained on GBV, child protection, Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), BLS and PFA. They will also be trained into planning psychological activities, with a strong focus on women and children. Engagement and acceptance of community members is key component in the design, implementation and monitoring or ACTED projects. ACTED has been implemented projects in Gaza since 2010 and beneﬁt thus from a high level of acceptance in local Gazan communities. The project will be implemented in compliance with ACTED internal security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented. The selected 240 households who will host the IDPs in case of massive population displacement will also received a 2-days training in GBV, protection principles of displacement, crowd management, children protection etc. Hosting households will be supported with the required assistance in order to be able to adeqautely host the IDPs in case of massive displacement. ACTED will identify the needs of the households in terms of shelter repair and maintainaince, especially regarding privacy and protection concerns, and refer the cases to relevant stakeholders and other organizations. This will ensure adequate living spaces are adequate and greater dignity and safety both to hosted and hosting families.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The kits allocation and distribution will be done on a needs-based approach. During crisis, a rapid needs assessment will be conducted in order to ensure that most vulnerable people and most urgent needs are being addressed. This will guarantee a safe, gender-integrated and impartial emergency response. To deliver an equal response, the 105-Volunteers Network will be in charge of displaying the ER procedures, including warning the Middle Area and Khan Yunis communities of the distribution’s location in case of conﬂict escalation. The goal is for vulnerable communities or people who would be the most affected by the crisis to be aware of this project and his objective. This will allow people to reach out in case of urgent need during crisis, and to be mainstreamed by the Emergency Response Mechanism. Moreover, the NFIs should be accessible and/or delivered in a safe way and beneﬁciaries should not travel in excess of 5km to obtain NFIs (in line with UNHCR Handbook/CCCM Toolkit practice). These measures will ensure that the project address the most urgent needs and most vulnerable people. It will also allow to provide access to aid to disabled people and elders, by shortening the distance to the distribution point.
