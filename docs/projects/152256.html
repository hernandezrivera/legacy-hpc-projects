Project Summary

The programme seeks to address gender inequalities in the delivery of humanitarian assistance and development programming, in order to reduce women and girls' vulnerability to gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. It will harness women’s leadership and contributions to address both short and longer-term assistance and recovery needs of affected communities. The programme will ensure that a gender perspective is adequately mainstreamed in humanitarian response and development programmes in North-East Nigeria, while promoting targeted interventions that will empower women and girls to effectively take leadership/participate in and be better served by humanitarian response and recovery efforts. It will respond to critical gender gaps in the current humanitarian response, coupling life-saving interventions to more long-term development needs and facilitate transformation of gender roles and relations in humanitarian, peacebuilding and development efforts that will empower women and reduce their vulnerability to GBV.

Needs

The Humanitarian situation in Nigeria is first and foremost a protection crisis, with dire impact on women and girls. The protracted conflict has exacerbated pre-existing gender inequalities due to the destruction/collapse of systems and structures that protect women and girls. The conflict has had differentiated impact on women, girls, boys and men; and this means that their vulnerabilities and needs are quite also distinct and different. They have unequal access to resources and services, they develop different survival mechanisms and they possess different capacities to support their families and communities both during the crisis and recovery processes. Addressing gender equality in the Northeast crisis means that in planning and implementing humanitarian-development nexus programmes, full consideration is given to the specific needs of different groups in a community. This is a joint humanitarian and development responsibility; and since response, recovery and development tend to happen in parallel, coordination and complementarity have become increasingly important for improved gender-responsive programming. Hence the need for mainstreaming gender across sector needs assessments and strategies that feed into the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). An estimated 54 per cent of the displaced population are women and girls and about 29 percent of IDP households are headed by single women and girls. According to the UNHCR Vulnerability Screening Report (December 2017), 63 percent of vulnerable households within the most conflict-affected areas in the North-East are female-headed. Unequal access to resources and opportunities have resulted to limited skills which have also affected women's employability. The skewed gender role of unpaid domestic work, caring for children and the elderly also continues to leave women and girls with little or no time for their engagement in empowerment programmes. The cultural and traditional practice of early marriage and early child bearing has been worsened by the conflict. Teenage pregnancies and the number of child brides have increased as marriage is considered a means of ‘protection’ for girls in order to ensure social security and reduce responsibilities for families. GBV is also widespread, with women and girls most vulnerable. About 99 per cent of reported GBV incidents between January-June 2018 were made by women and girls. However, cases are mostly not pursued or compromised due to lack of access to justice and limited resources. It is however important to note that men and boys also face GBV but they hardly report due to societal gendered expectations that men should be ‘powerful’ and not become victims. The culture and tradition do not also permit homosexuality, further limiting men and boys to report associated sexual assaults. Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is also high as many young women and girls are forced to engage in transactional sex in exchange for survival needs. The crisis has further resulted in changes in family structures and gender relations as single women are now solely responsible for household income. It is however not easy for women who are also married as men have lost their sources of livelihoods and they have great difficulty in dealing with the loss of their breadwinner role. This creates tension in the home, thereby exacerbating intimate partner violence. Furthermore, women report being marginalized from decision making and from partaking in opportunities that are culturally reserved for men. Nigeria has signed and ratified international and regional treaties which urge the promotion and protection of women's and girls' rights; and the elimination of discrimination against them, and there is a need to ensure implementations are aligned to these agreements. In line with the GBV sub-sector’s identified population in need, this project will target conflict-affected women and girls and twenty percent of men.

Activities

The project will focus on three major priorities:1.Gender Inclusive and responsive planning frameworks and programming. UN Women will provide coordination, technical and capacity building support to state and non-state actors, including civil society organizations to develop gender inclusive and responsive humanitarian response and development plans and programs as well as monitoring and reporting frameworks. This will include providing training and guidance on the use of gender tools, collection and use of sex and age disaggregated data, undertaking gender audits and analysis and advocating for the needs of especially women and girls. Key activities will include: •Undertake 2 gender-audits (baseline and end of programme) to determine how and to what extent gender issues have been considered in the humanitarian/recovery/development response in order to provide guidance on the design of required initiatives that will institutionalize gender equality outcomes in organizations•Conduct a gender analysis of the various sectors to provide data for the review of the HNO and HRP (2020-2021)•Provide technical support for mainstreaming gender into the 2020-2021 HNO and HRP, including Sector plans•In collaboration with OCHA, provide technical support and training to 300 humanitarian actors to facilitate effective gender mainstreaming into the various stages of the humanitarian programme cycle, including at organizational/institutional level. Partners will also be trained on the gender-age marker for monitoring and accountability on gender norms and standards.•Conduct a Women’s Security Awareness Training (WSAT) for female 200 humanitarian actors in collaboration with UNDSS. 2. Access to protection mechanisms, including those that prevent and address GBV. The program will support women and girls to access economic empowerment opportunities to enhance GBV prevention and access to justice services. It will also mainstream gender into Access to Justice programmes of partner agencies and promote awareness on the linkage between gender and GBV in order to open-up the space for preventing and reporting incidents of GBV, including among men. Activities will include:•Provide income-generation skills training and livelihoods support to women and girls at high risk of GBV, as well as survivors of GBV•Provide young mothers with context-specific alternative child care facilities •Support gender-sensitive access to justice programs in formal and informal institutions in collaboration with UNFPA, UNDP and UNHCR •Organize community conversations/awareness programmes through the “HeforShe’’ campaign (including use of IEC materials and audio-visuals) to create awareness among women and girls, men and boys in temporary shelters and host communities about gender norms, the prevention of GBV, and access to services . 3. Access to equal opportunities, leadership and decision-making structures. The programmes will facilitate the leadership and participation of displaced women in decision-making on humanitarian services and recovery/community development programmes. It will also mobilize community support and social acceptance of survivors of NSAG in order to curtail marginalization and negative coping mechanisms. Activities will include: •Train 500 IDP women on leadership and engagement in service delivery in humanitarian settings and non-conflict community settings to enhance their resettlement•Support community mobilization and awareness raising initiatives, including establishment of 10 networks of male champions (through the ‘HeforShe’ campaign) to advocate and support women’s engagement in decision-making •Organize 3 community dialogue/ conversation meetings with community leaders (including traditional and religious leaders), women and youth to promote community acceptance of survivors of NSAG, especially women and girls, as well as community actions to support their needs.
