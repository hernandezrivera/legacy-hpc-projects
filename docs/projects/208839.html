Project Summary

In line with the 2023 Sudan Country Refugee Response Plan and people in need (PIN) figures of 2024, UNFPA's refugee response plan aims to address gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response, as well as critical life-saving and essential sexual and reproductive health (SRH) needs of women and girls among refugees and asylum seekers. The current situation indicates that many internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities are relocating from state to state where the refugee community is settling, thereby exacerbating the burden and vulnerability especially on access to health services coupled with the ongoing food insecurity which will impact on the maternal mortality and increase risk for gender based violence.
This plan will encompass an integrated response for SRH-GBV prevention and service deliveries. Key interventions will include mobile and temporary clinics, women's centers including semi-permanent and temporary centers, capacity building for local authorities, institutions, and service providers, community awareness raising on SRH and GBV, community engagement, community-based protection, advocacy for SRH-GBV services, and scaling up referral mechanisms, including service coordination for at-risk women and girls and GBV survivors, irrespective of sex, age and background. Cash and voucher assistance (CVA) will be integrated into GBV case management rather than standalone intervention. The implementation of activities will be carried out through INGOs, NGOs and Government partners, with strong technical support from UNFPA which will maintain a field presence. UNFPA staff will directly facilitate capacity building elements and manage procurement and distribution of supplies. The project will provide individual and group skills building, strengthen community structures, and enhance institutional capacity in Sudan to respond to the needs of refugees.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNFPA will ensure protection is mainstreamed through various means:Conducting safety audits regularly with beneficiaries.Undertaking joint assessments of protection risks.Empowering women's social groups to address various protection challenges and risks in refugee sites. The project will also maintain community advocacy and consultations engaging community opinion leaders to deepen their understanding of risks at home and within the community. Community leaders will be engaged to mitigate protection needs.Providing orientation on PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) to staff and facilitating beneficiaries' awareness of reporting mechanisms. At the institutional level, the project will enhance the capacity of service providers on GBV guiding principles and the Do No Harm Approach.Establishing GBV and PSEA focal points at each service delivery point.Integrating a risk reduction strategy into every activity of the programs.Addressing host and refugee conflicts by following the principle of social cohesion, allowing the most vulnerable host communities access to services.Designing the program target based on gender analysis that reflects the distinct needs of women, girls, boys, and men. Simultaneously, the project will focus on addressing the vulnerabilities of specific groups, such as female-headed households, survivors of child marriage, pregnant women, and GBV survivors, given the heightened vulnerability of the refugee population due to the current crisis in Sudan.To mitigate protection risks, the project will conduct separate consultations with women and men, as well as young people, to understand various protection risks associated with accessing services. UNFPA will integrate guiding principles, ensuring meaningful access, safety, and dignity for the target community. The response will be conducted in a participatory manner to reach the most at-risk populations.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

UNFPA endeavors to systematically and meaningfully engage project beneficiaries, incorporating their inputs to define priorities and program activities from the planning phase. Additionally, UNFPA will collaborate with implementing partners (IPs) on the ground, ensuring ongoing consultation with beneficiaries throughout the project cycle through various modes of information sharing and feedback collection. Any feedback received will be considered for corrective measures, if needed, during the course of the project.Community sensitization and awareness campaigns, including media campaigns, will be employed to promote health-seeking behavior and encourage feedback.Following the outbreak of conflict in April, despite administrative and access challenges, UNFPA is adapting its activities across Sudan to focus on a life-saving humanitarian response, addressing acute needs with active participation and involvement of affected populations

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

UNFPA maintains a zero-tolerance policy for all forms of sexual wrongdoing, whether directed at a recipient of assistance or a coworker. The PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) policy is applicable to all phases of the program. UNFPA Sudan is a member of the PSEA Network, which includes UN agencies, donors, and international NGOs, with the possibility of expansion. The office collaborates closely with UN system partners and Sudan PSEA Networks, leading the PSEA Capacity Building and Survival Assistance task forces. All UNFPA partners, both governmental and non-governmental, including vendors, have been informed of PSEA compliance requirements, and agreements have been updated accordingly. UNFPA is an active member of the Refugee Protection Working Group, which activates complaint and feedback mechanisms. UNFPA will orient its implementing partners to support these inter-agency efforts.Additionally, UNFPA Sudan has established systems to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse, where:a) A focal point is designated for AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations) and PSEA.b) The UN protocol for Implementing Partners is applied.c) Capacity building on PSEA for both staff and implementing partners is prioritized.d) A PSEA community knowledge and educational package has been developed.In 2022 and 2023, UNFPA developed and implemented a PSEA Community Feedback and Complaint Mechanism tool in 10 areas in Central, North, and West Darfur, and South Kordofan. Community-based protection networks were trained on ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations, PSEA, and the implementation of the Community Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (developed in consultation with community protection networks), as well as GBV referral mechanisms.
