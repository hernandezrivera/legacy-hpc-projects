Project Summary

IRC is seeking to build partners’ capacity to ensure that timely clinical and mental health care and psychosocial support for GBV survivors is functional, available and acceptable to women, men, boys and girls in host and refugee populations in cox’s bazaar and also providers deliver quality clinical care, mental health and Psychosocial support services to GBV survivors. We will do this through: Training of GBVSS and Health partners on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Caring for Child Survivors (CCS) as data from the GBVSS service audit report reveals that 66% of CW are not trained on CSS; and Training of CW on handling survivors of IPV. Additionally 30% of CW have no training on IA CM guidelines. Additionally, IRC will seek to strengthen GBV multi sectoral pathways by supporting partners to initiate GBV case conferencing and coordination at camp and community level. IRC will also seek to build partners capacity to ensure proper and confidential documentation of GBV referrals, while also building capacity of non- GBV partners to support and conduct appropriate referrals as it is one of the gaps identified by the GBVSS. IRC will also seek the to ensure that women, girls , men and boys are protected from the consequences of violence by engaging communities in programming that seek to challenge power imbalance and inspire and enable communities to rethink and reshape social norms using the EMAP and SASA! Approach. In Addition work with adolescent Girls to support and navigate a safe and healthy, transition into adulthood protected from GBV, supported by their caregivers and peers and able to claim their full rights using the Girl Shine Methodology.
IRC will capacity build GBV actors across the Rohingya response in Bangladesh targeting both refugee and host community actors from LNGO’s and INGO’s and including government. This will be done through trainings, support supervision of partners, strengthen community of practice learning forums, technical review and standardizing of Case management tools. In addition in 6 camps IRC will work with communities to challenge power imbalance and inspire and enable communities to rethink and reshape social norms. IRC will strengthen community participation through capacity building of community resource persons, to lead GBV prevention interventions in the community. IRC has a policies on PSEA and child safe guarding, and has provided a reporting structure for staff and beneficiaries to report violations. To strengthen this policies and create an organizational culture that does not tolerate any form of sexual harassment, IRC has developed sensitization structures such as IRC WAY DAY, Women@ Work Forums, IRC way Ambassadors and Gender Equality Champions. Throughout the intervention IRC will collects feedback from communities through reactive and proactive feedback channels.

Objectives

1. Improve access to quality survivor-centered services by responding to individual needs, preventing and mitigating of GBV risks, and empowering women, girls and survivors of GBV in Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host community sites. 2. Promote a community-based approach to the response, support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services for persons at heightened protection risk, including girls, boys, women and men of all ages and with diverse needs and vulnerabilities, with the ultimate aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks, strengthen the resilience of affected communities, and place communities at the center of the response in line with AAP principles

Needs

"Response services in both host and refugee community continues to remain inadequate amidst high GBV prevalence rates. Often actors lack technical expertise in case management- a service audit revealed 66% of case workers had not received training on caring for child survivors of sexual abuse, while 45% had not been trained on responding to IPV . Clinical management of rape services by health providers remains weak. On the other hand ,GBV incidences also continue to be under reported due to fear of and actual abduction, harassment, and sexual violence keeping women and girls from moving around freely; further limiting access to service information, social support networks, and safety alternatives.Further still, there were low levels of referrals with very few partners mentioning a satisfactory collaboration with non GBV actors. Lack of knowledge about GBV referral pathways and coordination between field actors were cited as reasons for the afore mentioned problem. IRC will be seeking to address the above challenges that would hamper the recovery of GBV survivors as well as interfere with the development of children as a result of undergoing child sexual abuse through capacity building partners in responding to child survivors off sexual abuse, responding to intimate partner violence. It is worth noting that physical assault was the most reported form of GBV in the past 3 quoters as per the GBVIMS hence a critical need to enhance service provider capacities in response, while CMR is an immediate need for survivors of rape, timely access to the service is greatly hampered by inadequate service points and where the providers are present most of them are ill equipped to offer quality services, hence the need for capacity building on clinical care for sexual assault survivors.GBV referrals is an integral part of effective multisector approach to response of GBV services yet confidentiality is not guaranteed while some actors have not been able to keep the referral pathway vibrant and non GBV actors have been shy to use the referral system.
