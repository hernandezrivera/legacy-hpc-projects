Project Summary

Since 2011, Libya has been affected by political, security and economic volatility resulting to a governance vacuum, which has created significant security, rule of law, and social and economic consequences. An estimate of 900,000 people (including internally displaced persons, returnees, non-displaced conflict-affected people, refugees and migrants) are considered to be in need of humanitarian assistance and basic services. The majority of people in need are found in urban areas of Libya while many of the most severe needs are in the southern Mantikas such as Al Kufra. Key humanitarian needs reflect the deprivation of essential services and commodities. Thus, Libyan crisis is largely characterized by major protection issues.
Furthermore, 7 years into the crisis, people across Libya have been increasingly impacted by the deterioration of public-sector services. The dysfunctional public health system (gap in service provision filled by the private health facilities) has resulted in a lack of access to primary and secondary healthcare. In 2020, the rapidly evolving conflict, exacerbated by increasing numbers of migrants and internally displaced people, led to mounting health needs. In the public health system, severe shortages of health staff, medicines, supplies and equipment have been compounded by years of under-investment in the health system and a lack of transparency and accountability.
Thus, PUI will develop an integrated approach to contribute to alleviating the suffering of conflict-affected populations in Al Kufra. Specifically, the overall objective of the present Action is to improve access to essential services, including qualitative and inclusive primary health care and mental health services for conflict-affected communities in Al Kufra region.
The specific objectives of this Action will be to:
- Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health assistance, with an emphasis on the most vulnerable (including IDPs, migrants, refugees and returnees) and on improving the early detection of and response to disease outbreaks.
- Strengthen health system capacity to provide the essential package of health services and manage the health information system.
- Strengthen health and community (including IDP, migrants and refugees) resilience to absorb and respond to shocks with an emphasis on protection to ensure equitable access to quality health care services.
The Action will address Libyan and non-Libyan acute and chronic health needs and will entail a particular focus on persons with specific needs and vulnerabilities, internally displaced persons; refugees and migrants. PUI will deploy two mobile teams in the Mantika of Al Kufra and more specifically in the municipalities of Al Kufra and Tazerbou.
PUI health teams will provide direct service delivery in the areas presenting a high-concentrated needs and deprived of functional health public system. The package of primary health care services will be free of charge including a range of curative and preventive medical services. Provision of SRH services will be one of the focus Thus, one female SRH Medical doctor flying will join the mobile teams. It will include antenatal and post-natal consultations, family planning services, and gynecological examination. Mental Health and psychosocial support (PSS) intervention will be also offered to adult and children through individuals’ consultation and counselling.

1 - Needs

Libya remains one of the most vulnerable countries in the region due to the ever-present threat of large-scale hostilities, the presence of foreign armed groups, the trafficking of drugs and migrants, uncontrolled borders, organized crime and corruption. The dire humanitarian situation has been exacerbated by the continuing spread of COVID-19 in Libya. Since the first confirmed case of COVID-19 was reported in Libya in March 2020, the number of cases has risen steadily. Given the acute shortages of laboratory capacity and the lack of a comprehensive testing strategy, the real number of cases is likely to be much higher than reported. It will be difficult to suppress transmission of the disease for several reasons including the decrepit health system, the high number of internally displaced people (IDPs), and the presence of well over half a million migrants and refugees, many of whom suffer stigmatization and discrimination when seeking public or private health care. In stark contrast to other countries that have recognized the threat posed by COVID-19 and have put measures in place to ensure that emergency supplies are released within 48 hours, supplies for COVID-19 continue to be blocked in Libyan customs for several weeks. Health assessments conducted in 2020 showed the main challenges were fragmented health sector institutions, poor governance and a lack of accountability and transparency regarding the allocation of funds and distribution of medicines, equipment and supplies from national to municipal levels. The situation was compounded by acute shortages of health care facilities, staff, medicines and supplies across the country. More than half of the health care facilities that were functioning in 2019 have since closed, especially in rural areas, mainly because of security threats and lack of national and health sector funding. Those that remain open suffer frequent electricity cuts, exacerbated by shortages of fuel to run back-up generators. Libya’s health system is close to collapse. According to recent assessments, only 40% of communities have child health and emergency services, only 35% have general clinical services, and only 15-20% have services for reproductive health care and non-communicable and communicable diseases. COVID-19 lockdowns and curfews have further reduced people’s access to primary health care (PHC). Although families and communities face increased economic hardship and rising stress levels, mental health services are basically non-existent. The Al-Kufra’s district, already deprived because of its isolated remote in the desert on the Egyptian-South Chadian border, has been further affected by the COVID-19 epidemic. Indeed, in addition to the closure of certain health facilities in order to optimize resources during the epidemic period, the provision of essential health services has been impeded as nearly 90% of the physicians practicing in the zone have contracted COVID-19. The mortality rate for Kufra is around 10%, due to the lack of overall preparedness and response capacity observed in the area in 2020 and the underestimation of the number of positive cases related to the breakdown of PCR tests recorded at the national level since August 2020. Essential health services are difficult to maintain in an area already suffering from gaps in human resources and medical supplies, without external support from partners. Following the needs definition shared by the health sector for 2021, Al-Kufra as a severity score of 4 out of 5, making this district the one with the highest health needs for Eastern Libya for the coming year.
