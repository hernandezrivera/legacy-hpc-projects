Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (AAH) proposed project in 2023 will target the most vulnerable families in Area C (including Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Jericho, Ramallah, and Tubas governorates).
These groups suffer from no, or limited, access to water and sanitation services due to Israeli restrictions on construction and development in the area, as well as the massive demolition and confiscation of WASH structures that took place during 2022. This makes them particularly vulnerable to the threat of forced displacement. Further, the settler violence increased during 2022 as per the latest SV assessment made by the WASH cluster, have added additional challenges to these communities to meet the minimum required WASH standards. The 42 targeted communities, the majority of which are farming and herding families dependent on livestock and agricultural activities to maintain their livelihoods, heavily rely on water availability, quality, and affordability. The lack of water has forced families to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as reducing their daily water consumption as stated in the WASH Vulnerability Map 2022, depending on unsafe water resources, or reducing their expenditure on basic needs such as health and education to afford the high cost of water.
In line with Strategic Objectives 2 and 3, the project aims to:
1. Decrease target communities’ vulnerability and dependence on trucked water or unsafe sources of water by constructing and rehabilitating water networks in order to have a sustainable and reliable water source at these communities.
2. Improve communities’ access to water & sanitation facilities through the construction and provision of household WASH facilities. Families often disregard these facilities and prioritize other basic needs such as food and water. This negative coping mechanism has left many households in poor health, hygiene, privacy, and dignity conditions, which are contributing to household members’ vulnerability to face hygiene related diseases. This activity will be associated with hygiene awareness sessions on subjects ranging from hygiene promotion to water saving techniques.
3. Mitigate the risk of flooding during winter through the supply and installation of storm water systems for areas in serious needs. This will prevent target communities to experience further assets losses, displacement, and disrupted access to livelihoods services.
4. Improve the capacity of the service providers via the provision of water management tools & plans. This aims to support them in managing the water sector and helps AAH in achieving the localization approach on community level.
All these activities will mitigate the effects of the Israeli occupation on water access and thus improve the target communities’ livelihood prospects. The project will reduce the exposure to forced displacement of an estimated 152,651 people (36,981 men, 35,764 women, 40,122 boys, 39,784 girls, and 2,283 PWD) and provide them with the means to show resilience in the face of the aforementioned challenges.
The proposed project is guided by AAH Gender and Protection policy and AAH staff endeavour to consider safety and dignity in the design and implementation of all humanitarian interventions. Accountability and participation will be ensured through a detailed Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework specifically developed to ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner and through applying AAH’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism to guarantee the inclusion of communities’ suggestions into the project. AAH seeks to achieve equal and impartial access to vulnerable populations, aiming for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible including through community consultations and focus groups to further achieve beneficiaries’ empowerment and sustainability beyond the project’s lifetime

1 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO, more than 700,000 people across the West Bank reside in areas that are either not connected to piped water services or are very poorly supplied. Area C communities, particularly Southern Hebron, East Jerusalem periphery and the Jordan Valley, are particularly affected by this issue. All Palestinians living in Area C are directly affected by the Israeli restrictions and control over WASH-related infrastructure and water sources. As per the 2023 HNO, water scarcity in Area C is one of the main factors leading vulnerable families to poverty and food insecurity, as well as further deteriorating living conditions and hygiene practices. Moreover, the settler violence assessment prepared by the WASH cluster in 2022 has also reflected the impact on the WASH sector at these communities adding more challenges to the existing situation. Additionally, most Bedouin and herding communities in Jericho, Ramallah and the East Jerusalem periphery receive water through old water pipes laid over-ground. Irregular water supply, the small size of old water pipes, high water losses and the destruction of water pipes by settlers and the ICA (e.g., in the R317 communities of South Hebron) restrict access to safe, sufficient, and affordable water. This has negatively impacted the needs of different gender groups mainly women, children, and PWD. The lack of proper access to WASH services at the targeted forced the people to adopt coping mechanisms which have caused negative hygiene practices and led to several health issues including water born diseases with the vulnerable groups (Women & children) being the most affected. Data from 2023 HNO, 2022 WASH vulnerability Map, and other assessments made by AAH and other partners, validated by the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), highlighted that most of the targeted communities consume less than 50 l/c/d and use rain harvesting cisterns and trucked water as coping mechanisms for the water shortage. Thus, adequate water storage capacity is vital for vulnerable families to harvest rainwater in winter and store trucked water during dry months. In addition to the limited water resources and infrastructure, the service providers also lack the required technical and financial capabilities to manage these resources. The assessments show real need to increase these service providers capacities via the provision of required technical support; soft components including development of hydraulic models and required training, as well a hard component (maintenance tools and equipment).Access to water is not the only need in such areas- the 2023 HNO the figures indicate a real need at these communities in both sanitation services, and flooding risk mitigation measures on community level. The lack of such services is causing several health, environmental, and social issues which requires urgent intervention to avoid the negative impact caused by this. AAH, through the West Bank Protection Consortium, uses a community-based approach (CPA) to assess on a continuous basis than 70 communities in the West Bank. It established that many families in Area C lack basic household WASH facilities and do not have sufficient water storage capacity (<5m3). Limited income force families to prioritize other basic needs, leading to deteriorated WASH facilities conditions and negative hygiene practices. The CPA is addressing the needs of different groups, via conducting separate focus group discussion (Women & Men) in order to tailor different interventions based on the specific needs of each gender groups. AAH regularly conducts coordination meetings with educational and health directorates in order to prioritize the institutional interventions (Schools & HCF) all led by the Vulnerability maps shared by the cluster. These needs for WASH facilities in schools & clinics are also better identified during meetings, field visits and interviews with women, men, students, teachers, and health care providers.

2 - Activities

The project will achieve its objectives through:(1) The rehabilitation and construction of water transmission pipelines and water networks for vulnerable communities including Bedouins and herders in Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, the Jordan Valley, Tubas and East Jerusalem periphery. AAH will provide a sustainable and reliable access to clean water through the rehabilitation and/or construction of 24 water networks.(2) The construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in institutions (handwashing, latrines, sewer systems): a) For 4 schools in Area C, and 1 school in Area B serving one of the most vulnerable neighborhoods in the south of Hebron. This will improve student’s access to adequate, safe and healthy WASH facilities, and will also improve their general educational environment, thus upholding the children’s right to education. b) For 4 HCF in Hebron. This shall support these health care facilities in improving the standards of the WASH facilities, which will have a direct impact on the quality of services provided to vulnerable people.(3) The provision of access to sanitation and hygiene for communities in Area C: For this activity AAH will be considering a new approach to cover all the WASH needs on household level, including required latrine rehabilitation, kitchen sink, access to water, with associate hygiene awareness for two communities in Ramallah & Jericho governorates. 43 households will be targeted, and the implementation will be via mixed modalities (Cash & In-kind).(4) The construction/ rehabilitation of storm water network/ rainwater drainagesystems: This activity shall include the supply and install of culvert to drain water floodsfor three communities in Hebron, Jericho, and Tubas.(5) The development of WASH services operational and management plans and analysis through the implementation of a hydraulic map and conducting technical capacity building training that incudes provision of tolls and equipment necessary for the operation and maintenance of the water sector at Bani Na’em, Ramadin, Khallet Al Maiyya, Ar Rihiya in Hebron governorate. This integrated water approach will help in designing WASH interventions in the area, contributing to provide equal and impartial access to all. (6) Construction of a sewer water network to connect 37 households with a small-scale sewage treatment plant in At Tuwani community. This intervention will increase the sanitation and hygiene standards at this community as it will provide a complete solution for the sewer problem in the community, where the sewer water will be treated and used for irrigation and public purposes. AAH will coordinate with the PWA, Ministry of Education (MoE), Ministry of Health (MoH), and other organizations to avoid gaps and overlap. Stakeholders and partners in the targeted locations (municipalities and village councils), who have been involved in the design of the project, will support the collection of baseline and end line data to ensure gender needs and priorities are considered. The interventions will focus on the most vulnerable groups mainly women, children, and PWD being the most affected gender groups based on all the social assessment conducted over the year. All over the project stages, the social team will be working jointly with the technical team to ensure that all specific needs of different gender groups are covered and addressed. From design till handover, the teams will ensure that the activities are customized based on the needs of each gender groups to ensure privacy and dignity to all.

3 - Indicators

AAH aims to improve access to basic WASH services to sustain livelihoods and avoid/respond to displacement, support the WASH advocacy strategy, and improve coordination platforms. The proposed interventions will contribute to improving the safety and dignity of women, men, girls and boys, and the overall protection of the targeted communities. Expected results include: 1. Improved water quality for vulnerable families: construction and rehabilitation of water networks, which will decrease the dependency on trucked water and will deter families from sourcing unsafe water. This will encourage behaviour changes and contribute to a reduction in waterborne diseases. 2. Increased availability of water supply. Men and women will participate in the planning, design, and implementation of the activities. Families will reduce their expenditure on trucked water; consequently, they will have additional disposable income for other basic needs. The family member responsible for fetching water will have more time to fulfill other household activities. 3. Improved awareness, knowledge and hygiene practices: Associated with the rehabilitation of WASH facilities, the hygiene promotion activities and awareness raising will be provided at household level, with a special focus on women for topics regarding domestic consumption management, as per the previously conducted gender analysis of household roles and responsibilities. The core hygiene promotion messages will be hand washing, efficient use of water, domestic hygiene, cleaning and maintenance of WASH facilities.4. Strengthened capacity and resilience of vulnerable communities to respond to adverse environmental conditions resulting from natural disasters and climate change. The distribution of water and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities will improve and strengthen the capacity of vulnerable communities to withstand adverse environmental conditions. 5. Improved information management, coordination and dissemination of information among the cluster members and other stakeholders: the use of GIS and statistical software in data collection will facilitate data comparison and analysis; ad hoc databases will be set up to collect and compare information collected during the project implementation; complemented by AAH’s data management system. Elaborated data will be shared with relevant stakeholders and made available through regular reporting. KAP surveys and general project monitoring and evaluation will be carried out throughout the entire project in coordination with other agencies operating in the area; AAH will contribute to exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community and to be used for advocacy purposes. Expected indicators and targets for the project are: • Water expenditure decreased by 30%• Water consumption increased by 30%• Interruptions of water supply in the network decreased by 30% • Water loss in the network decreased by 30%• Number of small expensive water deliveries (with tractors) decreased by 60%• WASH facilities in 5 schools and 4 health care facilities improved through construction /rehabilitation of handwashing, latrines, sewer system• Living conditions for 53 women, 51 men, 45 girls and 47 boys are improved through the rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities • 24 communities improved their access to water through the construction/ rehabilitation ofwater pipelines and networks • 3 communities improved mitigation and prevention of floods and storm water drainage• 1 community improved access to sewer networks via construction of sewer collection system.• 4 councils/service providers increased financial & technical capacities (hydraulic model & tools)

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance, specifically for projects implemented at household and school levels. AAH has been working on its own sector specific gender and protection mainstreaming checklists. Up to date, AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheets. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. AAH in oPt prioritises this through context specific (risk) analyses as well as the protection coordinator position stirring questions and discussion. AAH (and community representatives, legal organizations and local authorities) will conduct a thorough risk analysis for proposed activities in each targeted location to identify potential risks and propose mitigation measures, including: 1- Community legal status: to assess if there are military orders or other legal concerns, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will not trigger the community legal case in a harmful way. 2- Community location related risks: to assess the community surrounding risks, like the existence of settlements, military base, firing zones and bypass roads, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will be applicable and operational in such context, not exposing beneficiaries to further risks. 3- Relations and sensitivity inside and outside the community: to assess the different cultural and social relations inside the community and in its surrounding, to ensure that the proposed interventions will not increase the social sensitivity or tension between the community, households, and with the surrounding communities. 4- Type of activity: to assess the community capacities and needs to follow up on activities such as the communities’ ability to operate and maintain the water networks which can contribute to the sustainability of the delivered assistance. In addition to doing no harm, the participatory approach contributes to the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. In order to further inform its assessments and implementation, AAH continuously works on understanding gender roles and household dynamics. By doing so, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH also has in place gender focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, in spring 2019, all staff received specific in person training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Even more, most of AAH staff have gone through protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV. The indicator “% of communities/ project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries. AAH will ensure that its activities, especially connecting communities with the pipe water service, will not cause overcrowding of the limited water sources and will not cause competition between the communities regarding water supplies, through the coordination with PWA and water service providers. Moreover, and in cooperation with community representatives, AAH will ensure that the project fully covers the water and sanitation needs of communities and households (each household has the right to have 1 latrine, water connection and 1 water tank), avoiding more tensions between the community families and other surrounding communities. Safety of the students of targeted schools will be improved, through increased adequate access to water and sanitation facilities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention has been paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected population. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible, including with households, community consultations and focus group discussions accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is starting to use increasingly ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age, though pending the reporting format AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs.AAH has a safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanism in place which allows beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. It is designed to increase their protection and complies with sensitive data management guidelines to uphold the principle of ‘Do No Harm’. Beneficiary knowledge of this system will be promoted through several field assessments and monitoring visits in which staff explains how the mechanism works. In addition, a paragraph will be included in each agreement with beneficiaries explaining the steps to report any complaint through the AAH feedback and complaint mechanism which is accessible by phone or email according to AAH protocols. AAH will process and treat all information collected with ad-hoc committee composed of project staff to understand the situation, identify appropriate solutions and include technical adjustment or modifications if needed. A monthly meeting will be conducted at community level to update beneficiaries and community leaders relating to project development and challenges. All formally received complaints and feedback are registered by the MEAL unit, considered for further investigation and adequately addressed. Additionally, the AAH anti-corruption and Anti-Fraud Policy and PSEA guidelines are implemented. With regards to access in this specific project, AAH tries to achieve equal and impartial access through for MEAL measures. AAH’s MEAL unit uses results-based monitoring tools in accordance with AAH’s M&E guidelines. A detailed MEAL framework will be developed to ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner and prioritizes vulnerable families and those who care for members with disabilities. For this project AAH will use its own criteria for WASH interventions to measure each household vulnerability regarding water and sanitation services. The response will therefore be designed cover each household vulnerability using the same standards, extracted from the WASH cluster minimum standards and HRP prioritization. M&E measures will be participatory, both developed and conducted collaboratively with key stakeholders, target groups and beneficiaries, and with emerging findings and results shared transparently throughout the project. The aim is to ensure equal participation of all, sustainability and follow-up beyond the project’s lifetime.
