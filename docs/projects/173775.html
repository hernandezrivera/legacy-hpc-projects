Project Summary

The project will target the most vulnerable IDPs in Jere LGA. Through multiple WASH interventions, SIF will address the lack of access to quality water and sanitation infrastructures and the deterioration of the hygiene conditions of targeted beneficiaries, damaged by the prolonged displacement. SIF will particularly focus on supporting the most marginalized and vulnerable social groups, including persons with disabilities and women and girls by ensuring a safe and equal access to all beneficiaries. One thousand displaced families will benefit from an improved access to water via the construction of two boreholes. Sanitation services will be provided through the construction or rehabilitation of latrines, showers and handwashing stations. The project will also provide hygiene kits with COVID-19 prevention items, according to the standards developed by the WASH Sector. In close coordination with local authorities and WASH Committees formed by the project to ensure the sustainability and qualitative maintenance of the WASH facilities, SIF will conduct hygiene promotion sessions in order to guarantee the adoption of good hygiene practices as well as preventing the risks of disease outbreaks, including COVID-19.

1 - Needs

According to the September 2020 DTM report from IOM, more than 2.1 million people are still internally displaced in North-East Nigeria and around 1.5 million of them only in the state of Borno. Displaced families are in need of basic assistance, especially an improved access to water, sanitation and hygiene. For example, in camps, there is on average only one latrine for over fifty people, and in some camps even only one latrine per one hundred. The risk of water-borne diseases during the flood seasons as well as the COVID-19 pandemic, further exacerbate the need for emergency WASH assistance.Over the last two years, SIF has been present in Farm Centre Camp in Jere LGA, cooperating with humanitarian partners in providing WASH services for the IDPs residing in the camp. In November 2020, SIF staff was able to conduct preliminary discussions with stakeholders to understand how to continue to capitalize on the expertise developed through these WASH interventions in camp and out-of-camp settings. These discussions indicated that most of the IDPs residing in the communities targeted (villages around Farm Cantre Camp) had to walk more than 9 kilometres to get access to water. The functional boreholes in some of these communities are overstretched and cannot accommodate the growing water needs of the IDPs. In addition, the water treatment of these water sources is insufficient. Water collection points are located far from IDP settlements and with an average of over 700 people per tap, while the recommended ratio is 250 people per tap. Consequently, the queuing time increases and community members end up spending 2 to 3 hours fetching water.Most of these IDPs do not have access to bathing and latrine facilities within recommended SPHERE standards and with privacy for women and children, which is critical as women are more exposed to risks of sexual violence when latrines are far from their shelter. The combination of poor access to water, insufficient sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices and dire living conditions increases the risk of waterborne and communicable diseases and worsens malnutrition. These conditions might result in disease outbreaks.According to the information collected by SIF, the availability of hygiene items is also limited. Most of the families interviewed indicated that they do not have access to basic hygiene items such as bathing soaps, laundry detergent and jerry cans, as they are unable to afford them.

2 - Activities

SIF will address the needs of vulnerable IDPs needs in terms of access to WASH services through the construction of boreholes, the rehabilitation and/or construction of latrines and showers as well hygiene promotion and the distribution of hygiene kits. The activities will particularly focus on supporting the most marginalized social groups, including persons with disabilities and women and girls, since they are more vulnerable and are bearing a higher burden of mobility restrictions, economic loss, and restricted access to services. SIF will support displaced families with safe access to water and sanitation services through the construction or rehabilitation of WASH infrastructures including 2 shallow boreholes, 10 latrines and 10 showers. The constructions and rehabilitations will strictly follow SPHERE quality standards in emergencies (1 borehole to cover 250 people per tap; min 1 latrine for 50 people). Following a specific assessment to identify needs and a thorough coordination with the WASH Cluster to determine the gaps in assistance, one borehole will be built to support in-camp IDPs while the other will be targeting out-of-camp IDPs living in informal settlements reaching a total of 1,000 families.Similarly, SIF will rehabilitate and/or install latrines and showers in each of the selected location. In addition, SIF will install handwashing stations (40 litres buckets with 1 adjustable tap and lid) at each block of latrines and place hygiene awareness messages. The WASH facilities will be designed to ensure a safe access for all beneficiaries, including women, elderly and people with disabilities. To this end, the project will implement gender-separated latrines and showers with doors that can be locked from the inside and enough light to prevent risks of GBV and allow women and girls to manage their menstrual hygiene with dignity.In coordination with SIF’s Livelihoods project, vulnerable beneficiaries will participate in the construction and rehabilitation of the boreholes and the sanitation facilities through cash-for-work. The contribution of the community will maximize local engagement resulting in a strong sense of ownership and better attitudes to maintenance. Moreover, in order to ensure the sustainability and quality of the boreholes while closely involving and empowering the community, WASH Committees will be formed. They will be trained in community-level maintenance and in charge of the management of the facility. SIF will ensure that WASH Committees will be inclusive and representative of all social groups, including marginalised ones such as persons with disabilities and women. The project will support the displaced families benefitting from the access to water component of the project, with the provision of COVID-19 kits and hygiene kits including feminine hygiene items following the standard kit composition developed by the WASH sector. The kits will be replenished every three months. In addition, SIF will distribute specific items for COVID-19 prevention including reusable masks. Priority for targeting will be given to COVID-19 affected families; with disabled or ill dependant(s) and female-headed households. The awareness raising activities will benefit both targeted displaced families as well as WASH Committee members. SIF will train community mobilisers and the WASH Committees to conduct the sensitization during the project and to continue it in their communities, ensuring more sustainable results. The hygiene promotion materials will be prepared based on Sector’s recommendations and they will be adapted according to the targeted groups (children and women). COVID-19 prevention measures will be observed, especially during the hygiene promotion activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Since SIF is committed to ensuring protection considerations are always present in its interventions, the needs assessment planned for January 2021 will prioritise the voices of women and people with disabilities. More specifically, SIF will take into consideration the needs of women and people with disabilities when designing the WASH facilities (ramps for access, good lighting, lock, private locations, gender segregation, easy access). Similarly, equal representation of all the marginalised groups will be ensured in all community structures involved in the design and the targeting phase of the project. The beneficiaries will be receiving information sessions related to SIF’s procedure for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The WASH Committees will be engage to support raising awareness, especially during distributions. Regular training on this topic will also be delivered to the project team to reduce the risk of SEA.The project plans on targeting only vulnerable IDPs, which might spark community tensions. For this reason, SIF field team will be sensitised to social stability issues, in order to identify possible community tensions at an early stage. If these circumstances materialise, SIF might consider the possibility to target vulnerable host community members in the project, especially for kit distributions.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The initial discussions SIF conducted with the communities where it is present were meant to have a first overview of the main needs of vulnerable communities facing prolonged displacement. However, SIF is planning to conduct a thorough needs assessment to identify specific areas of intervention. The needs assessment will be conducted in January 2021 and it will include community consultations. SIF will mobilise the community from the very beginning, to select locations of the WASH facilities and to determine the distribution modalities and hygiene promotion sessions.The communication with the communities will include information sessions on the complaint and feedback mechanism. A Community Mobiliser will be specifically trained on the complaint and feedback mechanism of the project, and they will be in charge of registering, processing and responding to the complaints. They will work closely with the MEAL team to ensure beneficiaries’ feedback is included in project learning. Complaints will be collected through a helpdesk during distributions and hygiene promotion sessions, through the activation of a hotline as well as through the implementation of regular focus group discussions to solicit the opinion of specific groups who might not have access to the other modalities.
