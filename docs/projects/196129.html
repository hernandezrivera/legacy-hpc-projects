Project Summary

ACF will strengthen livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable women and youth in the targeted households of host and refugee communities through skills development training based on formally recognized curriculum/ certification programmes with electrical work, Tailoring / Sewing, Fish processing, handicraft training etc. to reduce physical and protection-related risks and opportunities to become self-sufficient. Under this project ACF intends to target 4 FDMN camps i.e. 1 E, 1 W, 2E, 2W and 3 host community at Rajapalong, Ratnapalong and Subrang under Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila respectively. The project participants will be identified from most vulnerable HHs i.e. female headed HHs, HHs with person with disabilities, Orphan hosted HHs, HHs with elderly person from age 18 to above. ACF will target to capacitate 240 HHs from FDMN community, 75 HHs from Host Community in Ukhiya and 1480 HHs from Teknaf Host community with livelihood and skill development training i.e. electrical work, Tailoring, handicraft, driving, etc. In Ukhiya and FDMN community, 30% of targeted HHs would be female headed and in Teknaf 655 HHs out of 1480. ACF will conduct a training need assessment to find out in which skills the community prefers to be trained on and standardize the curriculum. During the training ACF will provide the input support required for relevant skill training. In the training financial literacy, life skill and DRR actions will be included. At the end of the training, every participant will receive required necessary equipment and tools along with inputs to start the business. ACF has the intention to establish a production cum training center near the targeted community, where participants will be able to sell their products and can earn cash. A job fair will be organized engaging employers and TVET participants to enable employment opportunities for the participants and establishing a linkage between the participants, financial service providers and employers. ACF’s MEAL team will develop M&E toots based on the project output and outcome. MEAL unit of ACF will conduct periodical survey, FGD and PDM of the targeted population. In the project implementation area effective Compliant and Feedback mechanism including help desk and box will deployed to ensure the transparency and accountability for both host and refugee community. A contingency plan will be included in the project to address any kind of disaster during the project period and all staffs will be equipped with necessary tools and protection gears to response in any emergency within 72 hours. The proposed project will be implemented by following LSD Sector policy and guidance.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Bangladesh is hosting more than 0.9 million FDMNs in 33 camps at Teknaf and Ukhiya region of Cox’s Bazar. The availability of livelihood opportunity of Refugee and host populations differ significantly, as the Government restricts work opportunities for refugees, officially preventing them from accessing formal employment and half of Rohingya households were not engaged in any income-generating activities. A number of factors including COVID-19 restrictions has limited self-reliance interventions, and delays in approvals from camp authorities, which sometimes led to planned activities not being implemented (REVA 5). Also, there are response-wide limitations on the number of days refugees can work (maximum 16 days in a month and 90 days in a year). Despite the restrictions, scaling vocational and skills training and socio-economic empowerment and self-reliance activities – with special attention to uneducated and unskilled persons, youth and women in the camps and host communities – is still critical to enhancing access to economic opportunities, as these play a crucial role for long-term resilience in food security. 95% Rohingya community primarily depends on humanitarian assistance as their primary income source (REVA 5). Due to limited access to access to regular income & livelihood opportunities, the FDMN population are highly reliant on humanitarian assistance. According to JMSNA, 72% HHs in refugee camps is living with food security and livelihood gaps. 59% HHs reported food and 30% reported access to self-reliance activities as priority needs. About 33 percent of Rohingya households and 4 percent of host community households had no work-based income sources other than assistance in the 30 days prior to the REVA-5. Eighty percent of Rohingya households identified as highly vulnerable had an average monthly income of BDT 3,401 (USD 39) or less. By comparison, 12 percent of highly vulnerable host community households reported an average monthly earning of BDT 8,447 (USD 95) or less. The limited livelihood opportunities forced refugees to adopt more negative coping mechanisms to fulfil their needs. About two-thirds of households in the camps (68 percent) and half of the households in the host community (52 percent) relied on less preferred or less expensive food at least one day, representing the coping strategy most frequently used for both populations (REVA-5). In Camps, 1 West, 2 East and 2 West 60-70 percent of households had unacceptable consumption. The Ukhiya host community showed poor food consumption among 44 percent of households versus 33 percent households in Teknaf, even higher compared to both the registered refugee camps in Ukhiya (36 percent) and Teknaf (37 percent). Income deprivation remains highest in the Rohingya community where 45 percent of households are deprived of income. However, the proportion reduced from 68 percent in 2020 to 45 percent in 2021, yet remains 5 percent higher than that of 2019 when 40 percent of households were multidimensional poor. The proportion of host community households facing income deprivation has increased slightly every year since 2019, growing from21 percent in 2019 to 29 percent in 2021. The highest deprivation was faced by female-headed households in the Rohingya camps followed by Rohingya male-headed households and male and female-headed households in the host community (REVA 5).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Action contre la Faim (ACF) is an INGO fighting against malnutrition. ACF has been supporting communities in Bangladesh since 2007 and intervening in Cox’s Bazar District since 2009, where it is supporting both host communities and the Rohingya refugee communities through its high-quality interventions. ACF thus has many years of contextual experience and acceptance built through strong engagement of host and Rohingya communities, other organisations, authorities - including at camp level, and other relevant stakeholders. ACF has a strong technical expertise in Food Security and Livelihoods, including skills development, ensuring food and nutrition security as well as resilient and climate adaptive livelihoods. Throughout its long experience in the country, particularly in Cox’s Bazar, ACF has promoted numerous innovative ideas in the field of Food Security and Livelihoods in order to strengthen the resilience of both host and refugee communities through actions similar as the one proposed. ACF has been hosting the NGO Platform from June 2020 to March 2022 and lead the WaSH Cluster up to mid-2021. ACF is also the Assessment and Information Management Technical Working Group Lead, Nutrition and WaSH Sector SAG member and member of different clusters, sectors and working groups, including the Food Security Sector, basing its humanitarian support on its guidelines. ACF has long-term partnerships with UN agencies, including, Unicef, IOM, WHO, UNOPS and WFP. ACF also ran several projects funded by UNHCR, and is thus familiar with its policies, practices and programmes. ACF has also established strong partnerships with INGOs like Cordaid, United Purpose, Plan International and local NGOs such as Sushilan, Friendship, SHED, and SARPV, notably through the lead of several consortium. ACF has its country office located in Dhaka, coordinating and providing support to the Cox’s Bazar office, thus allowing efficient and timely response in the district.

3.2 - Gender

ACF has own gender protection policy which incorporates key gender equality measures. Based on Gender analysis tailored activities for women, men, girls and boys including persons with disability are designed considering the different needs, roles, vulnerabilities, capacities, access and control over resources. Thus, the project will prioritize service to special group’s especially female-headed households, Pregnant and Lactating Women, under 5 children (vulnerable to malnutrition), Person with Disability and Elderly during implementation of the proposed projects interventions. Reasonable support based on the situation for different people provided as first priority. Such as separate queues at distribution point, breast-feeding corner at training center and input distribution point. Door step support to the PLW, elder and PWD beneficiary. Gender equity formula will be implemented in every step of the proposed project activities. Program will initiate to aware community people on GBV

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

ACF maintain Sex, Age and diversity Disaggregated (SADDD) data for targeted beneficiary in order to address the specific needs of the each beneficiary group. ACF abide by the local laws and by applicable international instruments, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and International Labor Organization conventions. The activities will be enabled for age group 18+ under the project. ACF discourages child labor and any violence against children. Inclusion of child in any project activities, distribution program is strictly prohibited. Additionally, ACF will ensure breast-feeding corners for lactating mothers in every distribution point and a nursing corner will be placed in project office for female participants with children. HHs with pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized during selection of persons of concern.

3.4 - Diversity

ACF will provide equal opportunities to everyone and meet the range of different needs that a diverse community needs. The participants and location selection, implementation modalities and in every cycle of project will made consideration for people of different gender identities, sexual orientation, race, ethnicities, class/caste, people with disabilities and other marginalized groups. In this project ACF will cover at least 30% women participation at Camp and Host Community under Ukhiya Upazila and 100% Female participation at Subrang under Teknaf Upazila. ACF will also cover 2% PWD participation in all project implementing areas.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project beneficiaries will be actively engaged in the assessments conducted by ACF like post distribution monitoring (PDM), food security surveillance survey (FSS). The beneficiary selection will be done by community consultations and the affected people will decide to select beneficiaries as per criteria of project interventions. HHs with women, PLW, person with disability, children under 5 and elderly are prioritized in the criteria of selection of beneficiaries. Communities also will provide their feedback on ACF led project activities formally through the complaint and feedback Mechanism (CFMC) and informally through meetings and individual interactions based on which actions will be taken to rectify the project activities if needed.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, project will contribute to the CRA (Community Risk Assessment) and RRAP (Risk Reduction Action Plan) of concern Union Parishad. Project will also assist UP for reviewing CRA & RRAP. Project will participate in the need assessment of CiC & site management dept. To address the environmental issues and climate change ACF will closely coordinate with other NGOs/stakeholders and will support in the CFW activities for tree plantation in road side, hilly slope and also in cleaning the cannel. Moreover, All the participants under the training activities will receive disaster risk reduction and disaster preparedness training. Also, for ensuring environment friendly approaches, ACF encourage and monitor beneficiaries to not to use any plastic materials for any purposes.
