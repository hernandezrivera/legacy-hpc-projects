Project Summary

In 2021, the Shelter and NFI sector will continue both with emergency response as well as more durable and tailored support depending on the specific needs of the population targeted.
In order to prevent further deterioration of the shelter and NFI availability situation of the most vulnerable populations of concern, the sector’s response will be delivered in accordance with the following objectives and UNCHR projects will be focusing on the same below objectives too:
• Objective 1: Provide humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support
• Objective 02: Contribute towards the resilience of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
The first key objective for this project will be to save and sustain lives through the provision of timely, targeted and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items.
In-kind assistance will be prioritized in the forms of family NFI packages during emergency responses and for long-term displaced populations in need of support. Seasonal items will also be distributed to sustain lives and reduce the impact of exposure to extreme conditions and will be one of the main focuses of the NFI response.
In response to COVID-19 needs, UNHCR will ensure the continuation of periodic assessments to all IDPs settlements in Libya to ensure the needed quantities of hygiene kits are provided as well as other items which contribute to increase the hygienic environment such as mattresses, blankets…etc.
UNHCR is estimating to assist 39,922 individuals with both shelter and NFIs projects with the above objectives and with a cost of 2,789,831 USD.

1 - Needs

The situation in Libya remains unstable with widespread humanitarian concerns. Waves of conflict have caused population displacement and severe damages to housing and infrastructures across Libya, essentially along the coastal areas, impacting the living conditions of the affected populations, Libyans and non-Libyans. The lack of adequate shelter options has led to housing shortages and an increase in rental prices, affecting the most vulnerable population. The need for housing continues to stretch, especially into urban areas and around the major cities, exacerbating the difficulties in securing affordable housing and putting an increased number of families at risk of eviction. IDPs in informal settings are among the most vulnerable in need of shelter and basic infrastructure support. The 2020 year has been marked by new waves of conflict and increased numbers of migrants, refugees and internally displaced people causing population displacements and damages to housing and infrastructure in Libya and severely impacting the living conditions of the affected populations, Libyans and non-Libyans. On top of this waves of conflict, COVID-19 pandemic hit hard the current economic and continued the negative impact on livelihood opportunities of Libyans and non-Libyans. Displaced populations encounter greater difficulties to afford rental costs and can’t afford the costs of repairs while constraints to return their damaged dwelling of origin. Adequate shelter options were already severe before 2020 and now rental prices have risen, and most vulnerable population have also headed to collective centres due to a lack of affordable solutions. The impact of the conflict and the pandemic continues to affect the living standards, and capacity for resilience and recovery of Libyans and Non-Libyans. Many people, especially the most vulnerable, are unable to independently meet their basic needs such as affording shelter and NFIs. The most vulnerable people in Libya and those in acute need of humanitarian assistance are those directly affected by the last waves of conflict, particularly those who were displaced and whose lives and livelihoods were uprooted and destroyed. For IDPs residing in informal settlements, essentially in urban areas, humanitarian assistance continues to be central to maintain a basic standard of living. While 65% of IDP families are staying in self-paid rented accommodation, an estimated 2% of IDPs are currently hosted in an information camp settings. Following the suspension of hostilities in South Tripoli, a slow return of some displaced households was observed in Western Libya, although the lack of basic services combined with the presence of explosive remnants of war (ERW) and unexploded ordnance (UXO) in previously contested areas have so far hindered most displaced families from returning to their areas of origin. For IDPs residing in informal settlements, essentially in urban areas, humanitarian assistance continues to be central to maintain a basic standard of living especially the increasing risk of eviction they are facing. An estimated 493,716 Returnees (DTM N.32, 2020) continue to form a complex caseload for humanitarians, development actors, relevant government agencies and donors. Many of the returnees are grappling with critical problems related to resilience and recovery. Of the total returnees in need, an estimated 63,300 Returnees are considered in acute resilience and recovery needs ranging from lack of proper shelter support to livelihoods and lack of social cohesion. According to the Shelter and NFI Sector analysis, there is presently in Libya an estimated 1.2M people of concern regarding their living conditions – and among this population 376,036 are considered as in need of Shelter and/or NFI assistance by the Sector. UNHCR is aiming through this project to assist 39,922 IDPs where is the sector is aiming to assist 103,380 IPDs.

2 - Activities

In order to prevent further deterioration of the shelter and NFI availability situation of the most vulnerable populations of concern, the sector’s response will be delivered in accordance with the following objectives and UNCHR projects will be focusing on the same below objectives too:• Objective 1: Provide humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support• Objective 02: Contribute towards the resilience of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.UNHCR will save and sustain lives through the provision of timely, targeted and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items. This will include the provision, distribution of core relief items and the rehabilitation, repair or upgrade of existing shelters that are below minimum standards in collective centres, unfinished buildings or any other type of emergency shelter space. This will ensure such shelters meet a minimum standard and provide a higher quality of living conditions and better protection for those who are expected to remain in these substandard shelters for an extended period of time.In-kind assistance will be prioritized in the forms of family NFI packages during emergency responses and for long-term displaced populations in need of support. Seasonal items (such as warm clothing, thermal blankets, winter boots…etc. in winter, summer clothing and fans in summer), will also be distributed to sustain lives and reduce the impact of exposure to extreme conditions and will be one of the main focuses of the NFI response. In response to COVID-19 needs, UNHCR will ensure the continuation of periodic assessments to all IDPs settlements in Libya ensuring the needed quantities of male and female hygiene kits are provided as well as other items which contribute to increase the hygienic environment such as mattresses, blankets. UNHCR is estimating to assist 39,922 individuals with with the above objectives and with a budget of 2,789,831 USD.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

Facing limitations on direct consultation due to COVID, UNHCR introduced Protection Needs Assessments mechanism approach, through which UNCHR determines needs and designs SNFI response among other responses and this approach helped for 2021 planning.
