Project Summary

DCA will support the continuous teacher professional development (TPD) for female Rohingya teachers, design and develop curricular materials and TPD modules, and will continue to implement non-formal education services for Rohingya female adolescents and youth through the delivery of its Education Sector-endorsed Non-formal Curriculum Framework for Rohingya Youth and Adolescents. DCA will design and develop materials to reach 2000 female learners who are outside the intended target group and domain of the Myanmar Curriculum Pilot through the implementation of literacy, numeracy, and life skills sessions in complementarity with DCA’s GBV program in co-shared cross sectoral spaces (WGSS) and through a home-based approach to reach those learners with disabilities and movement restrictions. Through the provision of education programming aligned with protection, DCA will ensure female learners are able to access a full arrange of services. DCA will also develop an upgraded level of the framework for those learners who have graduated in the previous cycle.
During the previous year, DCA developed and implemented remote TPD for female Rohingya teachers and developed a 10-module training course adapted from its Remote TiCC Training Pack. The modules were adapted and contextualized to meet their latent literacy and numeracy skills and were provided over the course of several months to include repetition, practicums, and scaffolded content. DCA will further refine this training course that can be implemented both in-presence and remotely using a combination of print-based materials, audio visual tutorials, and interactive audio instruction via MP4 players. This blended approach will reach teachers should another lockdown be implemented and will also allow for a gradual return to more face-to-face sessions while adhering to COVID-19 protocols. As part of the continuous TPD modules, DCA will focus on developing Myanmar language literacy and numeracy tools to support teachers and learners in the acquisition of Myanmar language skills. DCA has developed a strong working relationship with Burmese language experts at Translators Without Borders who have supported the development of DCA’s audio files, workbooks, and Self-Learning Kit. DCA will support the development of a training on language pedagogy and ways to support teaching Myanmar language, which will provide an opportunity to help teachers upgrade their skills and accelerate learning. DRR will be integrated into the Teacher Training Modules as well as considered an outstanding agenda item during the Alternative Learning Committee meetings.
The project will continue to gather evidence for DCA’s remote and blended interventions developed during COVID-19 to support lessons learned and to prepare for stronger resilience and response in the case of future crises. The project will continue to support community engagement and awareness raising on the importance of ensuring girls can return to learning opportunities through its robust network of Alternative Learning Committees. The project will continue to provide training on PSS, DRR, PSEA, and inclusive education and will expand its ECD programming as a lack of childcare means that female learners must often bring their children to the learning spaces. Ultimately, the project will support teachers, caregivers, adolescents and youth, contributing durable solutions that aim to generate resilience within the entire community.

1 - Identified Needs

The humanitarian response for the Rohingya influx is now into its fourth year and the protracted nature of the crisis, compounded by COVID-19 and the ensuring lockdown, confinement, and disruption of services, is having a considerable impact on the right to education, affecting the physical access to education facilities, learning opportunities, and learning outcomes. The impact of COVID-19 has accelerated challenges for adolescents and youth, particularly girls. According to the 2021 JSMNA 18% girls of age 15-18 years old were enrolled in learning facilities before learning facilities closed in March 2020 (pre-COVID), and only 15% regularly accessed home-based learning since the start of the 2021 school year until support for home-based learning stopped at the end of March 2021. The report also highlights that only 12% are reported to be sent back once learning facilities will re-open. The education coverage for female Rohingya youth during COVID-19 aged 19-24 and the intention of re-enrollment is even more dire. Among 19-24 year females, only 3% were enrolled, 2% received regular home based learning and 2% are reported to be sent back after learning facilities reopen. For girls, marriage and/or pregnancy was the second most commonly reported barrier towards home-based learning, while it was the most commonly reported reason for not sending them back. The most commonly reported challenges related to home-based learning being ineffective is lack of qualified teaching staff, as well as children having fallen behind as a result of learning facility closures. The existing barriers for women and girls to access learning opportunities has been exponentially exacerbated by COVID-19 and further expanded upon in a recent report by UN Women on the gendered impact of COVID-19. The need for programs that target the multi-dimensional needs of women and girls that integrate protection is urgent as is the need for wholescale community sensitization to ensure girls can continue to access education and protection services. Additionally, as mentioned above, one of the largest barriers for learning during COVID-19 was the lack of qualified teachers. According to the REACH assessment of the Education Sector Response to the Rohingya Crisis, over 80% of the Rohingya teaching corps are men. Due to cultural practices, community perceptions of education, and limited access to educational opportunities in Myanmar, Rohingya women and girls lack the required competencies to serve as a classroom teacher, and very few (521 women according to UNHCR Registration Data) have achieved sufficient education to be included in the teacher training cohort for the delivery of the Myanmar Curriculum. The absence of female teachers in the classroom and who are able to support home-based learning creates major challenges for reaching youth and adolescent female learners. Teacher quality is one of the highest predictors of educational achievement and therefore quality, sustained TPD that is more continuous and nurtures the iterative process all teachers go through, particularly new, female teachers, is imperative.
