Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its SNFI intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs (MSNA), need severity for SNFI assistance, and HNO2021 key messages, as well as to be in line with SOs of HRP and SNFI Cluster.
The project will address SNFI needs for totally 23000 INDV (11420M/11580F – 12%PwD) of the most vulnerable people of IDPs, HC and RTRNs in NSAG controlled areas in Aleppo and Idleb.
First component will contribute to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support targeting 18250 INDV (8750M/9680F). BO will respond to the emergency or high severity situations; especially in hard-to-reach areas, last resorts, and informal settlements. BO will conduct assessment survey in order to collect SNFI needs data (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e., market assessment or security assessment).
As for the response to NFI needs, BO will procure core and essential 1,000 NFI kits, and 1,000 seasonal and supplementary winter kits (including heating fuel) customized to meet different age and gender needs, then will distribute the kits, based on the vulnerability criteria, to ensure that the affected people have their core and essential NFI support, and they are protected from harsh seasonally conditions. Likewise, BO will respond to shelter needs targeting 1300 HHs through, Installation of tents with associated infrastructure, Installation/repair of tents, Installation of infrastructure, Rehabilitation of unfinished buildings, and Installation of transitions shelter.
Second component will contribute to reinforce an enabling protection environment and cohesion of communities by improving housing targeting 4750 INDV (2850M/1900F). To achieve this, BO will provide shelter rehabilitation/ repairing support to increase shelter adequacy with considering specific needs of all age and gender groups. Along with the rehabilitation, BO will provide essential trainings to members of LCs in the project area as well as to BO staff targeting 150 INDV (90M/60F) to equip them with proper capacities and skills in field of HLP due diligence, resilience and quality-oriented shelter, and basic legal contracting.
Throughout the project, BO will select the target communities based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with SNFI Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be

provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria (HLP verification will be included). Principles of Do No Harm and AAP as well as humanitarian standards are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation.

4 - Needs

SNFI needs are severed in Idleb and Aleppo across NSAG territory, and the plummeting of SYP has a negative effect on purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their SNFI needs, especially during harsh weather causing households to resort to harmful coping mechanisms. MSNA shows that FHHs, youth, elderly and PwD have reduced access to basic services including shelter, and livelihoods that supports access to SNFI. Each group has had a unique experience of the crisis and are often exposed to different risks and vulnerabilities. Women and girls as well as PwD face distinct protection risks, including gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse In NW Syria o Shelter adequacy is a critical issue in most communities, especially HHs living in informal camps face much higher rates of adequacy issues, most IDPs report facing three or more adequacy issues. Common issues are lack of lighting inside or around the shelter, lack of heating or insulation from cold, lack of privacy and inability to securely lock their homes. However, shelter and repair materials are too expensive; repairs require professionals but cannot afford the costs.NFI needs remain substantial, particularly the high numbers of newly displaced in need for core, supplementary and seasonal assistance. Also, communities require resilience-oriented support to reduce dependence on external assistance and enhance the ability to withstand future hocks and stresses. While market access appears to be gradually improving still items are unaffordable such as hygiene kits, cooking fuel & utensils, heating clothing lighting sources, mattresses, and blankets.Aleppo: Shelter PiN: 1,148,842 /NFI PiN: 538,565Idleb: Shelter PiN: 1,543,877 /NFI PiN: 1,246,637
