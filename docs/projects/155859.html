Project Summary

Establishing a new community center in Hama and Tartous City is considered to be one of the most vital steps to meet the complicated and various needs of their people, especially in light of the presence of Trust teams on the ground and having a close look on existing needs and delivered services from other humanitarian actors working in both city centers, where their services are either different from the nature of Trust services, or directed towards IDPs communities who've settled in specific areas.
The community centers aim to empower the most in need IDPs, hosting communities, people with disabilities, women headed household,and people with specific needs under their numerous yet diverse objectives including community mobilization, psycho social support, gender-based violence prevention and response, child protection, people with specific needs strengthening , education, and vocational training through comprehensive services like awareness sessions, campaigns, case management, vocational trainings, informal education, and community initiatives. As a result, benefiting all segments in the catchment area of both community centers (IDPs, returnees, female headed households, dropouts, elderly, people with disabilities and with specific needs,,,,,,etc). The project will be able to target a total of 19,160 beneficiaries from POC in Tartous & Hama city.

Needs

Since Syria Trust has a community center and other operating programs in Rural Tartous & Hama, Trust was in position to the needs and requirements in both locations. After analysis, conclusions of instability and insecurity in Tartous & Hama are realized. Eventhough Tartous has a severity scale of (2), and Hama have a severity scale of (3) which is due to crisis not physically affecting the city (civil damage), IDPs from surrounding governorates (Aleppo, Homs, Idleb, Raqqa, Dier Al-Zor and Abokamal) took both city centers as their shelter.According to our needs assessment, about /600.000/IDPs (the data illustrated is acquired from our networks established there including concerned authorities) have fled in the mid of 2018 from new governorates (Dier Al-Zor, Alraqqa and Albokamal) to Tartous and settled either in temporary shelters such as: Basira, Moaaskar Altaleaa, Mashroaa' Alton Aljerd, Al- Drakish, old real estate building...etc, or have rented int Tartous city center or in Banyas.Nevertheless, the governorate of Tartous have bared a lot with the burden of large influx, however, is working on fulfilling the needs of both host communities and the increasing rate of IDPs. These needs include with no limitation electricity, water, heat, and distribution of food and non-food items. Despite the safety returning to some governorates, but most IDPs who flee to Tartous won't go back to their governorates, they said "where do we live if we have no home to go back? and even if we have a home how we will meet our needs of amperes, water and other services when we lack the source of income?"In Hama, the city center has experienced at the beginning of the crisis an armed tension which led to destruction, deterioration, and poor economic conditions. With the beginning of 2014 the city witnessed a certain stability with the absence of any armed conflict which shifted to rural Hama, during which many humanitarian actors intervened for a short period of time. Currently there is only about 5 community centers operating in the community centers which will be not enough to fulfill the needs in the light of the armed escalation in both rural Hama and Idleb which will result an increase in the number of displaced families to Hama city, putting an extra pressure on the limited infrastructure once again.This lead to the over-population of the cities resulting in infrastructure deterioration, overburdened services,rise of harmful coping and adaption strategies,while facing increasing protection risks. It is worth mentioning the lack of significant efforts that should be undertaken in both locations to identify and reach the most in need. For instance:- Over-reached capacity in schools due to large numbers of students so the need for remedial classes and homework cafes offered in the CC is essential.- High levels of poverty and increasing needs is illustrated in the diminishing markets which farmers have depended on; Moreover, the sources of income have been divided on huge numbers of both IDPs and host communities. So vocational training is one of the approaches to diversify income opportunities.- High levels of injured, disabled people, and families who have lost their supporting members, There is need for psychosocial support in order to increase their resilience and social cohesion. Through this project, Syria Trust will benefit from its networks including local partners (AlBor, Environment Friends, Sanad..) concerned authorities, and stakeholders to implement Trust's community mobilization activities (FGDs, field visits, mobile visits) giving rise to a comprehensive intervention to meet the needs and benefit the maximum number of beneficiaries.

Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented in the community center through four categories: - Intellectual capacity development (non-formal educational activities) which include non-formal remedial classes and homework cafes conducted by teachers.- Psychosocial support services which include awareness sessions (PSS, CP, GBV), case management (PSS, CP, GBV), recreational activities, International day campaigns, and life-skills.- Community mobilization will includes households visits, Community based initiatives, and Community based organizations (capacity buildings).- Vocational training which will be identified based on the market needs. Moreover, will serve the most vulnerable people in need (women-headed households, large-memebered families with one source of income,....etc. This paves the way towards achieving resilience, financial stability, prevent and mitigate protection risks and negative coping strategies.Both community centers will have an ORV team (about 20-25 out reach volunteers) who is responsible for introducing the community center's services to the community and conducting FGDs, household visits, and helping in the implementation of activities. Moreover, both centers will include mobile teams responsible for delivering specialized services outside the CC who will be from the CC skilled personnel to be able to conduct the specialized services. In addition to the availability of implementing Legal Aid services through our existed legal first responders team in Tartous &Hama who are allocated to deliver: awareness sessions, and counselling; those lawyers can support this community centers through referral, conduct needed awareness sessions and counselling.The Syria Trust for Development will ensure monitoring of activities by deploying staff to the project and will conduct required assessment and data collection in order to evaluate the progress and impact of the project.Syria Trust will extend all possible support and facilitate monitoring and evaluation visits to the project location, FGDs, direct interviews, and segregated data collection before, during, and after the implementation.The CC staff will undergo specialized training that matches the training needs to ensure the quality of services.The activities will be designed and delivered to the beneficiaries according to their needs, taking to some extent gender considerations into account through involving them in planning and implementing the activities their-selves, However,the planned to be delivered service will be in coordination with Humanitarian actors and will be expanded to cover the near rural through mobile teams.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Access to services and assistance might be different to various vulnerable groups.M1: Ensure consultation with communities that may be at particular risk of exposure and risk of exclusion (Focus group discussions). In addition to selecting staff for all activities (especially outreach volunteers) with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile. Risk 2: Risk of discrimination of the individual - if interventions are not conducted in the proper environment and confidentiality is not ensured.M2: Ensure proper space/ location/environment for carrying out the activities to preserve comfort and confidentiality, as requested by the targeted individuals/ groups. For instance: PSS, GBV, and Case Management. These are considered to be a very sensitive services which require a compatible comfort zone (Manara - Community Center). Risk 3: The population targeted by the intervention is put at risk as a result of both infrastructure deterioration and demographic inflation as mentioned earlier.M3: Community-based protection services and other activities delivered through the establishment community centers/ static facilities and outreach activities (mobile units and outreach volunteers) as well as CBO/ CBI). Risk 4: Exclusion from access to services due to lack of documentation and other legal issues (marriage registration, child-birth certificate).M4: Providing legal aid and assistance through specialized lawyers inside/outside the Manara ( community center). Risk 5: Lack of awareness of women and girls on GBV subjects.M5: Raise the awareness of women and girls on the subject of SGBV through conducting a series of awareness sessions and distribution of dignity kits. Risk 6: Child labor increase - Children are working at a school age in order to participate in the income generating process. M6: Raise children and parents' awareness regarding child protection subjects through various campaigns (ex: International Child Day), awareness sessions, and implementing non-formal education activities. Risk 7: Increase of early marriage - girls are forced to get married at an early age as they don't have any opportunity to participate in income generating. M7: Based on results of market needs study as mentioned previously, the Manara (community center) will conduct vocational trainings which target mainly women and girls. Risk 8: lack of specialized and well-trained staff/service provider in these particular areas.M8: conduct specialized training for the CCs staff. Risk 9: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reappraisal and further trauma, GBV service providers safety and security because of their role and PTS for dealing with violence, Breach of confidentiality putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providers M9:"Case management with survivors will be conducted by trained personnel only, Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services, self-care strategies and peer support. Risk 10: Inadequate trained staff on GBV mainstreaming to sensitise non-specialised staff from within the organisation. M10: Training of some staff as key resource persons to act as trainers, and assess impact of the trainings introducing appropriate methodology and base lines.Risk 11: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.M11: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers on ST code of conduct and work core values.R12: Confidentiality of sensitive beneficiary data is breachedM12: keeping Data secured in the Trust CCs system (Manarat system).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Availability of needed approvals to conduct the planned activities and services.M1: maintain constant communication and coordination with rel event authorities to obtain needed approvals, R2: Unstable security situation, (especially in Hama in light of the unstable situation in neighboring goveronorate Idleb.M2: maintain constant coordination on security situation with relevant authorities. R3: Shortage of skilled staff in the governorate.M3: publishing the vacancy announcements on the ST official pages on social media & job specialized websites as well as within the community and clarifying the needed qualification to fit the vacancy criteria .
