Project Summary

In line with Lebanon Flash Appeal 2020 Strategic Objective 2 and based on needs identified, the project aims at restoring access to education in Beirut neighbourhoods severely affected by August 4th explosions, such as Remeil, Achrafieh, Medawar and Bourj Hammoud. In particular, the assessment and rehabilitation of 5 public schools will be conducted upon coordination with MEHE and Education sector, to ensure safe access to school for an estimated 1,500 students. In order to ensure a comprehensive intervention, children enrolled in the same affected schools will be provided with education supplies, considering also the possibility of delayed schools reopening due to COVID-19 pandemic. Moreover, in between rehabilitation works and full functionality of schools, remote learning support will be provided to children and teachers to ensure continuation of learning paths, reduce school drop-out and mitigate resort to negative coping mechanisms, such as child labour. Even though WeWorld-GVC does not have previous experience in the selected localities, the INGO is an Education partner in the Governorates of Akkar and Bekaa, with an ongoing project which include schools' rehabilitation, distribution of educational materials and recreational activities. In addition, WeWorld-GVC has been active on the ground in Beirut since the blast, conducting assessments and responding to needs for other Sectors, such as WASH and Shelter. Existing capacities include a technical team, composed of a WASH & Shelter Specialist, 3 civil engineers and 1 architect, an Education Specialist and several social workers who can be deployed for the activities' implementation.

Associated response plan

Lebanon Flash Appeal 2020

Plan fields

1 - Needs

As reported in Lebanon Flash Appeal 2020 and verified by WeWorld-GVC field assessments in severely affected neighbourhoods, the needs identified include: medium to severely damaged public schools, for which both minor repairs (such as substitution of windows and doors) and major rehabilitation (such as structural damages) are necessary to ensure safe access; families living in critical economic conditions, which have been worsened after the blast, cannot afford minimum expenses, including those for their children’s education. These needs to be addressed to prevent school drop-out and favour school retention.

2 - Activities

1 - Educational institutions assessment and rehabilitation: in-depth assessment and rehabilitation of 5 public schools affected by the explosion, upon coordination with MEHE/Sector (considering the possibility of addressing both minor and moderate repairs, 45,000USD have been allocated for each school as average). While the intervention will focus on the needs caused by Beirut explosion, the national situation related to COVID-19 pandemic will be taken into consideration for safe Back-To-School. This will be done engaging the whole school community using participatory methods (involving children, parents, school personnel, etc.), in line with National Standards. 2 - Education supplies for children enrolled in affected educational institutions: provision of education supplies to children attending the 5 schools foreseen to be rehabilitated. These could include school bags, text books, stationery, based on needs identified for both face-to-face and remote learning and upon coordination with MEHE and the Sector. Estimated 300 students per school. 3 - Remote learning support (e.g. equipment and connectivity) to educational institutions not ready to fully function due to explosion damages and rehabilitation works: provision of remote learning support, including laptops (at school level), tablets (for students and teachers), internet connection through recharge cards or local providers (for 3 months as per Flash Appeal timeframe, for students and teachers). This will be done for the 5 schools foreseen to be rehabilitated. Estimated 300 students per school.
