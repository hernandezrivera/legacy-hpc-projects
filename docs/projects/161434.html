Project Summary

South Sudan continues to present a complex humanitarian and food and nutritional security situation, characterized by national and localized conflicts, displacements, poor infrastructure and a deteriorating economic climate. As a indirect and direct result, acute malnutrition continues to be prevalent across the country. An estimated 1.8 million children under five years and pregnant & lactating women are acutely malnourished. The Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FNSMS, R24) indicated 16.2% global acute malnutrition (GAM), an increase from 13.3% in 2018 lean season. National rates of acute malnutrition in women of reproductive age are at high levelof 18.9% , with similar prevalence (18.8%) for pregnant women . Geographically, four out of the ten former states had prevalence of wasting ranging from 15% to 23.8%. The states with highest prevalence of wasting among women coincide with that of acute malnutrition in children (0-59 months), namely Unity, Jonglei and Warrap, showing how impactful the inter-generational of cycle of malnutrition is on child nutritional status. Similarly, 4.5 million people (39% of the population) are estimated to be facing crisis, emergency or catastrophe level (IPC Phases 3, 4 and 5) acute food insecurity (IPC, 2019). The persistent high malnutrition levels are attributed to high levels of food insecurity, displacements, conflict and poor infrastructure which hamper availability and access to basic health and nutrition services, low harvests, dysfunctional markets, poor infant young child feeding (IYCF) practices and high morbidity.
The project will ensure that populations affected by conflict and displacement have access to emergency nutrition services in order to prevent morbidity and mortality due to moderate acute malnutrition. WFP will continue to work with cooperating partners to support an emergency nutrition response through a preventive supplementary feeding programme and treatment services for moderate acute malnutrition targeting children aged 6-59 months, pregnant & lactating women, and people living with HIV/TB across South Sudan. Prevention and treatment services are provided both to IDPs and host populations. WFP also conducts direct delivery of life-saving nutrition services in hard-to-reach areas under the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (health, education, FSL, WASH).

1 - Needs

The food security and nutrition situation in South Sudan remains of public health concern with an estimated 1.4 million children aged 6-59 months, and pregnant & lactating women acutely malnourished while 4.5 million people are facing crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity according to the August 2019 IPC analysis. The same IPC analysis found that a total of 43 counties in the former states of Warrap, Eastern Equatoria, Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei have critical levels of acute malnutrition (GAM: 15.0 – 29.9%) while 14 counties in Lakes, Warrap, CES and WES reported serious levels (GAM: 10.0-14.9%) of acute malnutrition. Displacement and poor access to basic health and nutrition services further compound an already extremely difficult food security and nutrition situation. WFP currently supports acute malnutrition prevention and treatment services in all the 10 former states targeting residents, IDPs, refugees and host communities. This project will not only support the continuation of existing services, strengthening quality and provide for a scale up of coverage in life saving nutrition services particularly in high burden affected counties.

2 - Activities

Under this response supported, in collaboration with cooperating partners, WFP will: 1. Provide Children aged 6-59 months, PLHIV/TB and PLW screened in communities and identified as moderately malnourished an appropriate treatment protocol, inclusive of specialized nutritious food (SNF), through the Targeted Supplementary Feeding programme (TSFP) under the CMAM approach. The expanded criteria will be used as strategy in some areas when WFP/UNICEF /Nutrition Cluster jointly agree. 2. Provide preventive ration of specialized nutritious food to children U5 and PLW in counties with GAM rates above 15% through a preventive Supplementary Feeding Programme in the aim of preventing acute malnutrition. 3. Support an outreach network of community nutrition volunteers to provide nutrition and health message, outreach services, actively screen and identify those at risk or with acute malnutrition and ensure proper referral to health and nutrition services. 4. Ensure the project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (Health, FSL, Education, WASH, Protection, NFIs).5. Ensure revised delivery modalities will allow for prepositioning in locations with irregular access year-round, in strategic hubs with the aim of reducing pipeline shortfalls during the rainy season. River deliveries and airlifts are conducted to UNS, Unity and Jonglei States to the final distribution point where access is impossible by road to ensure the programmes are not disrupted. 6. Monitor and provide joint supervision through a network of field monitors, programme and nutrition staff- to ensure implementing partners activities and stocks are meeting the high quality and reaching the most nutritionally vulnerable individuals.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

WFP had strengthened its Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in South Suan, through enhancing two-way communication between WFP and the communities it serves and to take the appropriate measures on the issues raised. For nutrition programmes, WFP operates three modalities for CFM, 1) helpdesks at distribution and health facility sites 2) established a toll-free hotline and 3) established the use of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) within the community. For the RRM locations, WFP integrates the helpdesk and utilizes CNVs during the mission period. The variety of modalities used allows all forms of beneficiaries opportunities to provide feedback on its operations, service delivery and issues to WFP and cooperating partners. Due to its transient nature, the Rapid Response Mechanism modality establishes a help desk during registration and distribution, and taps into the nutrition partners’ CNVs to provide feedback to WFP during the mission. WFP has used this feedback to coordinate with relevant stakeholders to discuss and identify possible solutions as well as strengthening its existing programme. WFP also works closely with humanitarian actors on ground in all locations of assistance to disseminate information on beneficiary rights, entitlements and processes for sharing and receiving feedback, including on sexual exploitation and abuse.
