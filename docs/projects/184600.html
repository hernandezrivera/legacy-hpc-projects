Project Summary

According to the 2021 Humanitarian needs overview report, it is estimated that 3 million school aged children need sustained equitable access to quality education in a protective learning environment. Out of these children, 2,419,059 are from non-displaced families and 667,891 are from displaced families. Despite the low caseload of IDPs, children living in IDP camps are particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to limited provision of services and poor living conditions. REACH 2021 site assessment shows low education service provision in IDP camps. This is the result of a lack of government support, and inadequate and short-term funding to support affected IDP school-aged children.
It is estimated that 463,042 of the 3 million children in need of education are school aged children living with disabilities. Children with disabilities are significantly affected and face challenges to access an inclusive learning environment. Recent school screening identified prevalent negative attitudes towards children with disabilities by teachers and parents, and structural challenges that hinder these children to access an inclusive learning environment. According to rapid assessment conducted by Ministry of Education in Somaliland in September 2021 in drought affected areas shows 42 schools were closed due to drought. Findings from the assessment reveals 80% of the schools closed are in the rural areas (Sool, Sanag and Togdheer regions are among the regions where most of the schools closed are located). The findings from the assessment highlights that the main reasons for the school closure is due to the movement of the families as a result of the prevailing drought and lack of or inadequate number of school teachers. The HRP project will target areas with most marginalized and deprived communities (displaced and extremely poor host communities) in Southern states, Puntland and Somaliland.
The project will provide safe and protective learning environments and access to life-saving services for children through an integrated approach targeting 95,600 children in the worst emergency affected areas and drought displaced children in South Central Somalia (47,800 (50% girls) Puntland (18,032; 50% girls) and Somaliland (29,636; 50% girls) 1,000 schools per area will be targeted in South Central Somalia, Puntland and Somaliland respectively (40:25:35).
SC will ensure school going children are retained in their current schools while those dropped out are re-enrolled. Children will be provided with access to safe drinking water, emergency school feeding, hygiene promotion and provision of teaching and learning materials. This is expected to mitigate and prevent children from dropping out from school as a result of the drought. The response will provide a protective learning environment through the construction of temporary learning spaces, distribution of teaching and learning materials, and support to teachers with emergency teacher incentives.
The project will target 95,600 (50% girls) learners who are either dropped out or at risk of dropping out from school due to the impact of the drought. Psychosocial support services (PSS) will be provided for teachers, Community Education Committees (CECs), Child Welfare Committee (CWC), education officials in terms of case management, referral services for learners, especially in response to the drought impact and enhance their wellbeing. Community-led back-to-school campaigns will also be conducted to ensure that progress made on access to education is not lost. SC will work with MoE to promote alternative education pathways for children who lost time out of school and will provided catch up and remedial classes to cope with their classmate. Emphasis will be given to IDP children, poor host community children, including children with disabilities and those marginalized children to ensure they are also reached with education services.
