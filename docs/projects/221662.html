Project Summary

This project aims to reduce nutrition-related morbidities and mortalities in the Upper Nile State by preventing and improving the treatment of acute malnutrition. It targets vulnerable populations, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, refugees from Sudan and other third countries, and host communities, with a special focus on children under five years with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM), as well as Pregnant and Women women and girls (PBWG) with MAM.
Relief International (RI) will implement a comprehensive package of Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programs (BSFP) in Melut, Maban, Longechuk, and Maiwut counties. In Nasir, Uland, RI will continue with nutrition screening and counseling through Integrated Mobile Medical Units (MMUs), while referrals for treatment will be made to partner organizations, such as GOAL and Nile Hope.
The project aligns with the Nutrition Cluster Strategy for 2025 and includes four key objectives:
1. Enhance equitable access and utilization of quality, lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and foster resilience among children, adolescents, and women in Maiwut, Longechuk, Melut, and Maban through static and outreach programs, and in Nasir and Ulang through mobile responses.
2. Improve early detection and treatment of wasting in children under five and pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBGW) across six counties (Maiwut, Longechuk, Melut, Maban, Nasir, and Ulang), ensuring quality lifesaving nutrition services.
3. Promote nutrition and health-related knowledge and practices to prevent malnutrition among pregnant women, girls, mothers, caregivers, and community members through social and behavior-change communication strategies.
4. Strengthen program monitoring, data analysis, and utilization of nutrition information for improved decision-making and outcomes.
To localize responses and ensure sustainability, RI will build the capacity of County Health Departments (CHDs) to oversee nutrition interventions, coordinate with stakeholders, and implement national guidelines for managing malnutrition and maternal, infant, and young child nutrition.
RI will also actively participate in Nutrition Cluster working groups at the national and state levels, ensuring that responses are coordinated, integrated, and comprehensive. Protection mainstreaming will remain integral to all project activities, ensuring that services are delivered in safe and dignified spaces for women and children.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to grapple with an escalating humanitarian crisis despite decades of sustained efforts. In 2025, the Humanitarian Country Plan (HCP) projects that 11.3 million people, including returnees, will require humanitarian assistance. The worsening food insecurity and malnutrition crisis, driven by economic collapse, repeated climatic shocks—primarily widespread flooding—and conflict, has been exacerbated by the influx of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan. Between September and November 2024, 6.3 million people (47% of the population analyzed) faced IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse, including 1.71 million in Phase 4 (Emergency) and 41,000 in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). This marks a significant increase of 500,000 people in IPC Phase 3 or above compared to 2023. The Phase 5 population includes 10,000 individuals in Malakal County and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees from Sudan.Upper Nile State, home to 1.57 million people across 13 counties, bears a disproportionate share of the burden, contributing 15% of the national acute malnutrition cases. Of the state’s population, 306,436 individuals require acute malnutrition services. Key drivers of malnutrition include poor dietary diversity (MDD at 20.7%), inadequate meal frequency (MMF at 9.1%), and minimal acceptable diets (MAD at 3.9%). High disease prevalence, especially malaria or fever (exceeding 20% in eight counties), poor child-feeding practices, and inadequate WASH conditions exacerbate the crisis. Most counties have less than 50% access to improved drinking water, less than 10% access to improved sanitation, and rampant open defecation rates exceeding 90% in eight counties.Between July and September 2024, all counties in Upper Nile were classified as IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) or worse, with eight counties, including Ulang, Melut, and Renk, in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). High food insecurity affects 56.9% of the population, or approximately 895,000 people, who are in IPC AFI Phase 3 or worse. During the post-harvest period (October 2024 to March 2025), the nutrition situation is expected to remain critical, with 795,000 individuals (50.6% of the population) in IPC AFI Phase 3 or worse. The lean period from April to June 2025 will likely worsen the situation, with 1.02 million people (65% of the state population) projected to experience IPC AFI Phase 3 or worse, including 10,000 individuals in Malakal County falling into IPC AFI Phase 5 (Catastrophe).Women and girls face heightened protection risks, including sexual violence and exploitation, while traveling to flee conflict or access humanitarian aid. Families resort to negative coping strategies, such as child labor or early marriage, due to food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, further exposing women and girls to gender-based violence (GBV). Despite the severity of the crisis, lifesaving GBV response services remain insufficient.South Sudan’s humanitarian challenges are compounded by systemic economic, health, protection, and infrastructural gaps. Vulnerable populations, particularly women, children, refugees, and returnees, face urgent needs in health, nutrition, and protection services. The ongoing Sudan crisis and recurrent flooding have severely strained resources, underscoring the need for integrated, multisectoral interventions to address the interlinked crises of food insecurity, malnutrition, and protection risks.

2. Project activities

Relief International (RI) will sustain and enhance its delivery of high-quality nutrition services across the counties of Maban, Melut, Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasir, and Ulang in Upper Nile State. As the lead nutrition partner in these counties, RI will integrate its activities with the WFP/UNICEF-funded nutrition projects, ensuring a seamless and comprehensive response to nutrition needs. In hard-to-reach areas, particularly in Longechuk and Maiwut, RI will utilize Integrated Mobile Medical Units (IMMUs) to provide mobile nutrition services. The program aligns with the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster's strategic and sectoral objectives 1, 2, 3, and 4, focusing on both the prevention and treatment of malnutrition in adherence to national guidelines.Key Activities:1. Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and Behavior Change:RI will implement IYCF activities across both static and mobile sites, guided by the National MIYCN Guidelines. Messaging will be integrated at every beneficiary contact point within the CMAM program, and mother-to-mother/care groups will facilitate knowledge sharing and promote behavior change. In violence-affected areas, child-friendly spaces will offer additional support to mothers.2. Management of Acute Malnutrition:RI will screen and treat children under five years and pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWG) for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) through CMAM programs at static, mobile, and outreach sites across up to 32 locations.3. Community Mobilization:RI will establish networks of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and care groups in all six counties to raise awareness and promote participation in nutrition interventions.4. MIYCN Counseling:Preventative measures to reduce the risk of malnutrition will focus on behavior change communication targeting maternal, infant, and young child feeding practices.5. Immunization and Supplementation Campaigns:RI will participate in National Immunization Days (NIDs) and periodic campaigns for mass distribution of Vitamin A for children aged 6–59 months and deworming with albendazole for children aged 12–59 months.6. Growth Monitoring:Growth monitoring activities will be conducted in Melut, Maban, Longechuk, and Maiwut counties to track and address malnutrition trends.7. Cooking Demonstrations and Kitchen Gardens:RI will establish kitchen gardens and conduct cooking demonstrations at OTP/TSFP sites to improve dietary diversity and nutritional outcomes.8. Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program (BSFP):Vulnerable children and PLW will receive BSFP support to prevent malnutrition.9. Integrated Multisectoral Approach:Nutrition activities will be integrated with WASH, health services (e.g., RDT and measles vaccinations, deworming, and treatment of co-existing illnesses), and food security and livelihoods (FSL) initiatives, ensuring holistic care aligned with CMAM protocols and Nutrition Cluster directives.10. Coordination and Collaboration:RI will actively engage with the Ministry of Health, County Health Departments, and partners through national and sub-national nutrition cluster coordination meetings.11. Emergency Nutrition Response:RI will address the acute needs arising from the Sudan crisis in Renk, Melut, and Maban, and respond to other emergencies across Upper Nile State as required, guided by needs assessments and identified gaps.This integrated approach ensures that RI's nutrition interventions address both immediate needs and long-term resilience, fostering improved nutritional outcomes and better overall health for vulnerable populations in Upper Nile State.
