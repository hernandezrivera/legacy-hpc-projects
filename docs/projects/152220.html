Project Summary

Education is an important tool to provide children and communities with a sense of normalcy following a disaster, instilling hope and mitigating the psychosocial impact of violence and displacement. It is a fundamental right of all children, but children in emergencies are constantly denied this right.
An estimated one million children have been displaced and three million are in need of emergency education support as a result of the Boko Haram insurgency which began in 2009. Over 2,295 teachers have been killed and 19,000 others displaced in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states in the last nine years.
With almost 1,400 schools destroyed, damaged or looted, the crisis has further devastated an already bad education system characterized by a severe lack of infrastructure, learning and teaching materials and overcrowded or classrooms.
Schools and other learning spaces continue to be targeted; some are being used as shelter by IDPs and other have been used by parties to the conflict, thus negatively impacting the safety of students and their teachers. Students have also kidnapped and forcibly conscripted by Boko Haram in all three states
Since 2013, over 600,000 children have lost access to education as an estimated 943 of the 1,627 schools in Borno State remain closed for the fourth year running. Almost 750,000 children and teachers remain displaced, vulnerable and in need of psychosocial support. The absence of qualified teachers means that those still working are overburdened with the workload of overpopulated classrooms. They are also hampered by the lack of scholastic materials and equipment as such items were destroyed during attacks on the schools.
This project therefore is targeting 7,000 children, 100 teachers, 50 SBMC for continue education in Jere and Konduga LGA as an intervention project.
To ensure continuity of education in a humanitarian response the AHSF would work with communities, Communities Leaders, Other Education Actors, and all levels of governments to ensure children have access to formal education.
In humanitarian situations where children cannot access education we would works with a variety of partners to implement non-formal programmes that support re-entry into formal systems for IDPs, new arrivals/Returnee and Host communities.

Needs

From the joint education needs assessment Conducted in Northeast Nigeria in November 2017. The single biggest barrier for most out-of-school children in the northeast is poverty. Unless action is taken to either reduce the cost of education or provide financial support to families then many children will continue to miss out on education even if issues such as infrastructure and quality are addressed. School Safety and Security is also a need identified with the education sector as the target of many of the insurgent attacks during the first years of the crisis it would be expected that the safety of children in school would be central to any response.Support for Volunteer Teachers in several schools, especially in high security areas are reliant on volunteer teachers have also been identified as a need. Many of these volunteers received little or no support. Identifying and supporting these volunteer teachers through our organisation will help to ensure the continuity of education in these areas until such time as security allows the formal education system to restart in earnest. Provision of Teaching and Learning Materials including chalk,Boards etc is a a big need,with 45% of schools reporting little or no teaching and learning materials available to children then the continued push to providing materials should continue. Children having materials has a knock-on effect in other areas. They will be more motivated to attend class and will probably be more engaged, this will also support the teaching staff. Also outlined in the findings section teacher’s morale in the northeast is at rock bottom. Low salaries, poor conditions, a lack of respect and the impact of the crisis itself have all taken their toll. In one focus group discussion a teacher declared he would disown is children if they themselves decided to become teachers, such was his disillusionment with the profession. The major issue is the salary with expected promotions and increments not actioned, salary arriving late with unspecified deductions and teachers facing increasing costs for transport and accommodation. Alongside salary, the conditions facing teachers in the region are also extremely tough. Overcrowded classrooms, a lack of teaching materials, text books and classroom equipment, even chalk is in short supply. To move education forward in the northeast teachers, need to be better supported and reasonably compensated. Encouragingly 86% of schools reported functional school based management committees or parent teacher associations. As with all developmental interventions issues of the long-term sustainability of an intervention and maintenance of infrastructure and equipment provided need to be addressed by School Based Management

Activities

This project would have several activities;Four (4) communities would be selected from the two LGA for Planning, Advocacy, meetings with all stakeholders in the education sector, Camp Manager/ Coordinators, communities leaders and committee would be set up (SBMC) for ownership and sustainability, implementation of project, evaluations (Ongoing Monitoring and evaluation)for lesson learnt and feedback.In this LGAs there would be:- Advocacy visits with community heads, - Meetings, trainings for teachers and Volunteers. - Setting up of Monitoring and evaluation teams- Training of Head Masters/ lead teachers- Basic Literacy and Numeracy activities for Children in this category-Provision of Education Facilities in the LGAsOther Activities1 Provision of regular stipend to teachers and Volunteers, alternatively, food baskets or NFI provision along with continued training and support will help retain the volunteer teachers. Such that Children affected by this displacement have equitable access to quality basic education and psychosocial support in a protective learning environment.To move education forward in the northeast teachers, need to be better supported and reasonably compensated. In addition, helps such as food baskets or NFI could also provide a short-term boost through conditional Cash Based Tranfers. Support ,trainings and Capacity building of resources person(Teachers, Volunteers) and different stakeholders.Building of Makeshift/ temporary facilities in IDP Camps, and Host communitiesThe SBMC is one avenue to try and support communities to play a larger stake in the management and support of their local schools. Whilst the current economic situation makes it difficult for parents to make financial contributions, there is some availability of labour within the community.Communities strengthen their involvement, ownership and capacity to promote education and safeguard their children’s protection and well-being within learning spaces and the wider community.These outcomes are addressed through a series of non-formal education, resilience and life skills, literacy and child protection activities. They are delivered through camp-based child friendly learning spaces (CFLS) with a dimension of the programme carried out in host community, non-camp settings.
