Project Summary

Barely out of a wetter than normal Deyr rainy season, communities in south and central Somalia are grappling with a new wave of flooding during the Hagaa season in low lying areas along the Juba and Shabelle rivers. The impact of the ongoing flooding compounded by economic and social impacts of COVID, escalated conflict, and forecast of below-average Deyr rainfall from October to December 2020 may result in further deterioration of .humanitarian outcomes for affected communities through 2021.
Juba foundation through the proposed response will work in line with the cluster objective towards ensuring that we provide access to specialized, age, gender and disability sensitive. This will be coupled with psychosocial support and referrals on MHPSS, child protection, GBV and victim assistance services to our beneficiaries including boys, girls, adolescents, persons with disabilities and older persons, facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, injury and severe distress. This intervention will be offered within the selected districts of Bay, Bakol, Lower Juba, Gedo and Lower Shabelle Juba foundation will as well offer support on the provision of the primary healthcare services which includes consultations for common illnesses. JF will Improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis affected population as well as improve on the coverage, reproductive health including BEmONC as well as referrals for CEmONC. JF will ensure that immunization services are adequately offered to children under 5 years as well as pregnant mother for tetanus toxoid. also ensuring readiness to prevent and respond to outbreaks such as malaria, measles or Acute Watery Diarrhea AWD/Cholera and promote health update through health education and beneficiary sensitization as well as linking this with the nutrition program in order to enable provide a holistic services within the organization target site. Physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement coupled with effects of COVID 19 will be offered psychosocial supports as well as referral for the mostly affected beneficiaries. The project intends to ensure access to basic primary and life-saving health care services integrated with mental health and counselung to survivors of GBV to 43,200 Vulnerable IDPs and Host Communities including 12,500 Girls, 12,500 Boys, 14,200 Women and 4000 Men.
JF has the capacity to access the proposed district with our presence being represented in the targeted districts. Juba foundation has the previously offered support to the beneficiaries in the above district as well as participated in the analytical assessments which have previously been done as the multi-cluster carried out by multi- agencies. JF has been actively participating in both regional and national coordination meetings with MOH and other stakeholders within the targeted districts. Participation in the coordination meetings within the regions will still be carried out progressively.
