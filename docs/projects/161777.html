Project Summary

Under this project IOM will scale-up its provision of shelter assistance targeting internally displaced people, returnees and host communities. To this end, IOM will: (A) ensure access to different types of emergency shelter solutions for displaced populations through the distribution and prepositioning of emergency shelter kits, construction of emergency shelters, rehabilitation of damaged public buildings, etc.; (B) ensure that displaced populations have access to different transitional shelter solutions; (C) ensure that the care, maintenance and upgrading of existing emergency shelters and transitional shelters are conducted for continued safety and comfort of the affected population; (D) ensure that returnee households have access to materials and labour for repairing their damaged houses; and (E) support local construction market through business grants and training for construction traders, as well as through the use of cash and voucher mechanisms.
IOM’s shelter activities will be implemented in three States of North East Nigeria; Borno (12 LGAs), Adamawa (9 LGAs) and Yobe State (3 LGAs), targeting 229,000 individuals.
IOM ensures that all shelter activities taken are accountable to the affected population and that the gender-specific needs of project beneficiaries are integrated. Kits and other shelter solutions are harmonized at sector level and coordinated with partners.
The project will also include the continuation and scaling-up of Non-Food Items (NFI) support for people in camp and camp-like settings, in host communities, including for new arrivals. IOM plans to guarantee the participation of Women, Men, Girls and Boys as equals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and different age groups, and address women's, girls' and boys’ specific needs, especially in terms of protection and access concerns. To illustrate, (1) the content of NFI kits will be tailored in accordance to the needs of different age and gender groups (2) All activities related to this project will equally target the different age and gender groups and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. Regarding assessment, monitoring and evaluation of gender specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels with them where possible, and all activities will be conducted based on Do No Harm principles.

1 - Needs

Camps and camp-like settings in North-East Nigeria present a variety of shelter conditions, such as emergency shelters (37%) and self-made/makeshift shelters (37%). Other types are host family houses, government buildings, schools, individual houses and community shelters. The shelter situation produces a variety of needs of the conflict-affected population, including emergency shelter construction for newly-displaced populations, reinforcement and repair of existing emergency shelters, and improved emergency shelters in urban/ peri-urban environments. Non-Food Items (NFI) remain the second most reported need of affected people in North-East Nigeria, both in host communities and in camps or camp-like settings. Blankets/mats remain the most needed items in camps and camp-like settings, as well as host communities (DTM Round 28). According to the figures from the CCCM/SHELTER/NFI sector, a total of 2,603,964 individuals are in need of shelter assistance in the three states in North-East Nigeria, with Borno having the highest case load of 1,870,249 individuals, followed by 426,815 in Adamawa and 306,899 in Yobe. Out of these, a total of 309,887 IDPs are at risk of exposure to harmful elements due to lack of shelter solutions. In addition, 68,090 returnees are living in heavily damaged shelters. An additional 1,156,509 IDPs living in camps and host communities are without adequate shelter and NFI solutions; 277,530 returnees are living in partially destroyed houses.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and speciﬁc vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities are factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the complaint and feed-back mechanism team and tools . Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
