Project Summary

Since October 2019, the country is facing an unprecedented financial and socio-economic downturn, further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the port explosion, leading to increased levels of poverty and humanitarian needs. While past crises were primary affecting refugees, asylum seekers and migrants, the weakening of the already fragile Lebanese infrastructure has drastically affected Lebanese as well, resulting in more than 2 million people living below the poverty line and in need of assistance (Lebanon Emergency Response Plan 2021 – 2022, August 2021).
Despite the fact that, persons with disabilities have more healthcare needs comparted to persons without disabilities, yet access to healthcare remains challenging including for older people. But, due to the deteriorating situation in Lebanon, the vulnerable groups are increasingly deprioritizing the rehabilitation (functional and psychological) needs in order to cover their basic needs.
The project is designed in line with Objectives three objective of health Emergency Response Plan (ERP) (1,2&4), and aims to ensure that the most vulnerable people affected by the economic crises have access to inclusive, safe and quality health services to meet their critical needs. It envisions 4 expected results: 1) Vulnerable populations have access to dignity kits, and inclusive, safe and quality health services through multi-disciplinary outreach.The project will recruit 2 Multidisciplinary outreach teams to approach the most vulnerable communities in Beirut , Bekaa and Tripoli. HI technical team will train and coach the outreach team to provide safe and inclusive Psychological First Aid, awareness on COVID 19 and other topics to at least 5000 vulnerable community members. Also, the outreach will assess the comprehensive needs, refer and remover barrier of transportation to at least 900 to different services. People will be referred to; rehabilitation partners for home care, PHCs for health services, EMBRACE for emotional support and suicidal prevention beside other service providers. in addition, and based on the needs identified by the outreach teams, at least 1500 women and girls living in highly vulnerable conditions will be provided with dignity kits 2) The most vulnerable population (people with limited mobility) including persons with disabilities, older persons have access to the needed health care services., HI technical team will deploy and technically support Mousawat (Beirut and Bekaa) and CBRA (North) home care teams to provide home care (physical, and/ or MH sessions..) to at least 600 patients with limited mobility. Moreover, the home care teams will make sure that targeted people will be provided with the needed medical consumables, such as: Diabetes tests, strips, diapers, catheters, urostomy,bags and bases,ulcer .. 3) PHCs are supported to handle increased caseloads for Rehabilitation and MH services. HI, in close collaboration with MoPH and more specifically PHC department, will support 6 PHCs offering mental health and physical rehabilitation, to maintain operations during the ongoing emergency context. The selected PHCs are ; 2 in Beirut( Al Zarif Medical Center for Primary Health Care and Dar Al Fatwa Primary Health Care Center) , 2 in Tripoli (Ibn Sina Medical and Social Center for Primary Health Care and Al Karama Primary Health Care center) and 2 in Central Bekaa (Al Farour Primary Health Care Center , and Emam Al Husien Primacy Health Care center).HI will be covering centers’ running costs, some equipment and PPE materials, in addition to increasing the working hours of physiotherapists and psychologists in order to respond to the increasing needs.4) NGOs conducting outreach activities within the coordinated emergency health response are aware about inclusive outreach activities where HI will participate to the coordination meetings,and will train at least 10 interested NGOs on creating inclusive messages for outreach activities.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Given the imminent protection risks, links will be made with HI’s protection intervention to ensure individuals’ needs are addressed through a strong cross-sectorial referral mechanism; this ensures populations most at-risk of being excluded are identified and included in the response. Concerning GBV, the outreach team will be supported in understanding the concept, effectively refer when needed to specialized services. Institutional policies are in place to ensure PSEA and protection mainstreaming. Concrete actions are distribution of leaflets on HI’s complaint mechanism; dissemination of specific key messages on PSEA. As for conflict sensitivity, the outreach team will be accompanied to understand the concept and apply integrate ways of working to support social stability in their work. C-19 SOPs are in place for field staff and HI’ premises; staff care is within the framework of HI contracts. Teams have access to free support to ensure their well-being and address concerns.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The project eligibility for assistance will have a strong focus on persons with disabilities,elderly and vulnerable women.HI project will ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders will have access to information about the project activities via posters, leaflets, and through the existing coordination working groups. The project will ensure that all HI and partners staff receive training/refresher on PSEA and on FCM. Through the outreach activities, the beneficiaries will be receiving basic messages around protection and they will be informed about all FCM channels to make sure that all groups are aware of how to submit feedback or complaints in a safe and confidential manner.FCM includes complaint phone line, email, complaint boxes, etc .A detailed MEAL plan will be defined which includes indicator matrix, and systematic monitoring will occur as well to oversee activities implementation. HI will implement of Post Intervention Monitoring to assess and report beneficiaries’ satisfaction
