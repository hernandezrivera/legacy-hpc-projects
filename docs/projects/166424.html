Project Summary

The project is intended to create mass awareness on COVID-19 outbreak in order to protect people from this pandemic. The project also includes advocacy and campaign to support local communities, especially the vulnerable people, marginalized farmers to be resilient and avoid the economic consequences of COVID-19. Apart from health related messaging, the project will focus on food security and agriculture related information and knowledge services to empower local communities and farmers.

Needs

As Bangladesh faces gaps in health sector, far less attention has been paid to another pandemic-driven shortage lurking over the horizon: food. Food and Agriculture should be the next immediate and important priority as loss of livelihood will expose the vulnerable people to COVID-19 and good health require proper and nutritious food. Hon’ble Prime Minister (PM) Sheikh Hasina during her video conference with government officials on 31st March 2020 therefore emphasised, ‘the most important thing is food security. Our food production must go on. We have to ensure that not a single inch of agricultural land remains fallow so that we can fulfil our food security. The ongoing lockdown, which is important to control the outbreak, has impacts on the livelihoods and food security for the vulnerable local people, particularly who are working in the agriculture sectors. In alignment with Government priorities, that is why FAO has been creating community awareness across four sub-districts of Cox's Bazar to combat the outbreak of COVID-19 promoting awareness on hygiene, nutrition, and ways to produce food for household consumption. Food and nutrition security of women, people with disabilities, children, transgender, older people are specifically vulnerable due to their limited access to market and reduced purchasing capacity, further fuelled by COVID-19 situation. Advice on seasonal vegetable cultivation and promotion of best practices on home gardening/street gardening etc. can allow them to produce food and meet their household nutrition. This project will be an integral part of FAO's overall initiative, in collaboration with Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE), Department of Fisheries (DoF) and Department of Livestock Services (DLS), to scale up its effort to support the government to protect the food supply chain and boost agricultural production. Food and Agriculture Organisation of the United Nations (FAO) has been actively supporting the local community in Cox’s Bazar since October 2017. In partnership with the DAE, DoF and DLS FAO is empowering the local farmers through strengthening of farmers’ groups, enhancing technical capacity of the marginalized and smallholder farmers in four Sub-district of Cox’s Bazar. FAO with the Forest Department is also leading reforestation and environmental rehabilitation activities in Cox’s Bazar and supporting the Forest dependent local communities. FAO, with support from Ministry of Agriculture, will utilise its strong network with different Government departments, farmers groups and community leaders to conduct massive campaign on hygiene, food, nutrition and ways of boosting agricultural production maintaining social distancing other health measures. Based on rapid assessment by FAO, government database, the most marginalised communities will be targeted. Food and nutrition security of women, people with disabilities, children, transgender, older people are specifically vulnerable due to their limited access to market and reduced purchasing capacity, further fuelled by COVID-19 situation. Advice on seasonal vegetable cultivation and promotion of best practices on home gardening/street gardening etc. can allow them to produce food and meet their household nutrition. Targeting: The project primarily addresses the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak on the livelihoods and natural resource base of 141,854 vulnerable beneficiaries (of which, 45% women) comprised of 28,370 households of the Rohingya camp and host communities in the targeted areas (Four Sub-districts i.e. Cox’s Bazar Sadar, Ramu, Ukhiya and Teknaf). Accountability to Affected People: FAO will continue to utilize its existing internal systems of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) to ensure that all activities incorporate measures of information sharing and recognition of vulnerable groups, as well as grievance gathering and address.

Activities or outputs

(i) Development of Communication Resources: Develop Information, Education and Communication materials (posters/videos/audio message) on household food production to increase income and food security of affected population; awareness on nutrition security and dietary diversity to help them maintain healthy lifestyle and boost their immunity; and hygiene and social distancing to keep themselves protected from COVID-19. (ii) Capacity Development: Enhance COVID-19 community awareness and engagement capacity through, trainings for staff and volunteers; and training for Farmer Leaders and Community Leaders. (iii) Community Engagement: Mass campaign on food heroes to gain support for marginalized farmers and daily wage workers engaged with food value chains; mass campaign on ‘cultivate each inch of land’ to support local production and food supply for host community and refugees; dissemination of messages/ information through community mobilization (i.e. community leaders, local community representatives, religious leaders etc.); dissemination of messages/information through video show; dissemination of messages/information through door to door visits (IPC); and dissemination of messages/information through loudspeaker. (iv) Information and Feedback Service centres/Facilities: Information and feedback hotline on food/nutrition; and information service through cell phone messages, web portal, ICT tools, mobile apps for local farmers, public and private extension and health promoting advisory service providers.
