Project Summary

ACRA proposes a 12 months Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene Promotion services to the returnees, host and IDPs communities in in Jonglei (Fangak, Akobo and Uror Counties), Upper Nile (Baliet and Maiwut Counties). The Program goal is to reduce and/or mitigate potential further escalation of WASH related diseases and to provide integrated services on nutrition, protection and WASH activities aimed at reduce risks relating to GBV of vulnerable populations affected by triple shocks of flooding, conflict and COVID-19 through timely, sustainable and equitable access to clean and safe water, sanitation and hygiene services. This is in line with the first WASH cluster sectoral objective: Contribute to reducing prevalence of malnutrition and the second WASH cluster sectoral objective: Contribute to reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality. The project will also contribute to cluster sectoral objective 3; Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster and objective 4: Disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility and finally objective 5: Provide durable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster through resilience building in locations that were affected by flooding in Jonglei and Upper Nile. ACRA will however endeavor to mainstream protection and mitigate WASH Gender Based Violence to guarantee that beneficiaries of WASH services, in particular women and children can access facilities in a safe manner.
ACRA will integrate WASH activities with nutrition and health to maximise the impact and reduce excessive morbidity and mortality of children under five will also provide standard WASH NFI kits along with hygiene promotion to 8,000 SAM/MAM children and their family/caretakers at facilities and follow up visits will be conducted for households hosting the children. In addition, ACRA will provide access to safe water and sanitation facilities to 20 nutrition facilities. ACRA will also support communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rate through improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices. WASH activities will also be integrated into health activities in the context of disease prevention and response. This aims at breaking the transmission cycle of water related diseases including diarrhoeal, cholera, vector borne diseases and COVID-19. This is intended to be achieved through multiple integrated approaches (improving access to safe water and hygiene promotion) and ensuring minimum IPC in health facilities supported. ACRA will work with protection partners, community leaders and religious leaders to identify people with disabilities, elders, marginalised and PLWHIV. Focus group discussions will be conducted with these groups of people or their representatives to identify their needs, barriers, and obstacles that limit them from accessing WASH services. During project design, these groups of people or their representatives will engage with ACRA and forward their views and opinion and design preference for the construction of sanitation facilities. In all the project locations, ACRA will assess protection concerns related to women and girls accessing WASH services and take robust action to mitigate the risk of exposing women and girls. Depending on the contexts, ACRA will install 10 new water points where there is low coverage, upgrade two existing handpumps with solar power system, rehabilitate 40 existing water points and/or distribute water filters or household water treatment products. ACRA will conduct general community hygiene promotion in the target locations with emphasising disease prevention (such as COVID-19, cholera and AWD), safe excreta management, safe water storage and handwashing with safe water and soap. Water user committees shall be formed and trained to ensure project sustainability. ACRA will construct institutional and communal toilets in crowded settings.

1. Needs

According to South Sudan Country Programme 2023-2025, UNICEF report, ongoing conflict and economic challenges severely hinders people's access to basic services. Only an estimated 10 per cent access improved sanitation, and 70 per cent lack access to basic healthcare services. The national budget allocation for healthcare is 8 per cent, falling short of the recommended 15 per cent of the Abuja declaration. Safe water is available to just 35 per cent of the population; while over 60 per cent of people practice open defecation, risking contamination of water sources.WASH conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin States of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal, (HNO, 2022). Displacements associated with insecurity, severe flooding and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes in areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water to the people, with the situation worsening when additional people are displaced. According to IRNA report for Maiwut County August 2023, Maiwut County in Upper Nile State is experiencing increased health and WASH needs needs due influx of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia across different Payams worsening the already poor WASH and health services in the County. According to the protection cluster assessment report April 2023, Baliet County has hosted many IDPs from Akobo, Nyirol and Uror Counties during the internal conflict that erupted in December 2022 and peaked in April 2023. This is supported by a quick partner assessment report February 2023, where ACRA participated confirming mass influx of IDPs from Pibor Administrative area to Gelachor Payam. This continuous influx of IDPs has negatively impacted the host communities of Baliet County which is already struggling with poor primary health care services, inadequate WASH facilities and protection concerns. According to the rapid assessment conducted by REACH March 2023 in Akobo West, internal conflict that took place from December 2022 to March 2023 caused heavy displacements worsening the already vulnerable population leading to more health, WASH, protection and food security needs. This also led to increase in number of patients with Acute watery diarrhoea and other waterborne diseases.Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes will remain across many counties, Nationally, 28 counties are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4); Of greatest concern will be Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Akobo of Jonglei. With More than 18,000 returnees, with more expected to come, are currently in Akobo, with some 95 per cent of them having arrived not from war-torn Sudan but from Ethiopia further straining the available WASH facilities in Jongolei and Upper Nile. The two States are some the state worst hit by the triple climatic shocks including flooding that led to mass displacement of populations and has caused a lot of strain and stress on existing WASH facilities. Improvements in security Jongolei has resulted in mass influx of returnees placing considerable strain on resources and humanitarian partners’ ability to adequately meet the WASH needs of both returnees and hosting communities. The return of South Sudanese refugees from neighbouring Countries has further stretched the demand for WASH assistance. According to October, 2022 IRNA report for Akobo East County, there is mass influx of returnees of about 17,000 individuals from Ethiopia into the County which are affected by the Ethiopian crisis. Therefore, immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and avert total collapse is required.

2. Project activities

The following activities will be implemented;1.1. Installation of additional water points and rehabilitation of water point including hand pumps repairs targeting the Payams with lowest coverage; Safe and adequate drinking water will be provided at health facilities level for patients, caregivers, and staff. ACRA will also ensure all the nutrition facilities are implementing the basic IPC WASH services. ACRA will also supports communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rates through improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices. Depending on the contexts, ACRA will install new water points where there is low coverage and rehabilitate existing non-functional water points. During rehabilitation in flood prone areas, the aprons of the water points will be raised to avoid inflow of contaminated water from floods. 1.2. Establishment and training of Water management committee (WMC). The training will include specific modules on operation and maintenance of water sources and material support the WMC with pump maintenance tool kits. ACRA will ensure that at least 50% of committee members are women.1.3. Construction of communal latrines that are gender and disability sensitive, using iron sheets and plastic slabs clearly marked with separate gender, lockable from inside, well-lit for women & men, boys & girls fitted with handwashing point. ACRA will also install handwashing facilities in crowded public places including communal latrines and markets. Institutional sanitation facilities will also be provided (in schools, health facilities and in nutrition sites) 1.4. Establish Solid Waste Management Committees (SWMCs) in the target villages supported with cleaning tools and training with active participation of both men and women. 1.5. Treatment of water sources at HH level through chlorination and distribution of household water treatment and storage (HHWTS) kit (including 1 bucket with lid with tap, 1 Jerican, soap for 3 months, 1 filter cloth).1.6. Provision of safety audits before and after any new construction and at every facility rehabilitated facility aimed at addressing protection and specifically GBV ACRAs in WASH services. The safety audits will also include the establishment/update of referral pathways in every location. ACRA conducts protection analysis and focus group discussions with women and girls before constructing both water points and communal latrines1.7. Provision of MHM kits for menstrual age women, including solar lamps and prioritizing GBV survivors or particularly vulnerable women and girls at risk (widows, child headed households, etc.)1.8. Hygiene promotion at community level with emphasis in the following topics: diseases prevention (Water-borne diseases and COVID-19), handwashing, safe excreta management, GBV messages and promotion of referral pathways. Training of health/hygiene promoters who will be 60% female will conduct hygiene promotion at household and community level, early detect and manage AWD/cholera cases in the areas targeted by ACRA and also identify and refer GBV cases to ACRA protection team for subsequent actions and guidance.1.9. Provide WASH and nutrition integrated responses: ACRA will continue supporting SAM/MAM children and caretakers by providing a standard WASH NFIs kits along with hygiene promotion at the facilities and follow up visits to the households depending on the COVID-19 situation. The WASH NFIs include household water treatment products, a bucket with lid, a bucket with tap and lid, soap, and filter cloth. Hygiene promotion targets caregivers or mothers at the facility level with priority hygiene messages such as hand hygiene, safe handling of food and drinking water, and safe excreta disposal1.10. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM): ACRA will carry out both mini (at end of each distribution) and comprehensive (after 1-2months of distribution). PDM to document feedback from beneficiaries on the items preferences
