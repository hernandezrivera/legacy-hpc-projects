Project Summary

Humanitarian assistance involves the aid and action intended to save lives, lessen suffering, ensure protection of human dignity during and aftermath of man-made crises and natural disasters, as well as to prevent and strengthen preparedness for the manifestation of such situations. In the context of Rohingya Response - BRAC is intending to provide services under objective 2 (Support safe living conditions through rationalized and participatory site planning, promote inclusive representation through feedback mechanisms, and work towards environmentally conscious construction and site maintenance initiatives) and 3 (Ensure emergency preparedness and response activities to natural hazards while protecting and rehabilitating ecosystems) for 26,481 population (5,882 HHs) in camp 2E.
Considering existing camp operation structure and coordination system BRAC will align with other actors to support the gaps/shortfalls as the SMSD fully covered by UNHCR in order to avoid the duplication. This programme will be planned to strengthen the existing community participation and empowerment including Disaster Risk Management with more effective camp governance and participation of all age and gender groups and persons with disabilities from the targeted communities.
BRAC is planning to support the following activities:
Under community participation and empowerment activity groups – BRAC will carry out an assessment to identify existing gaps, gender roles in disaster risk reduction and environmental protection in consultation with the targeted refugee community including women, youths, person with disabilities, religious, ethnic and other diversity groups. Based on this assessment BRAC will raise awareness among the targeted communities to define gender roles in protecting environment and reducing disaster risks. Furthermore, BRAC will form community based youths and women groups to ensure their participation in ECO DRR establishment. BRAC will also pilot community led eco-friendly infrastructural solutions, like cross water body management and land stabilisation which will reduce environmental vulnerability by restoring/protecting environment.
Since risk is a combination of hazard and vulnerability, it is important to understand the level of vulnerability of the refugee households to reduce risk and potential loss. Under Disaster Risk Management activity groups - BRAC will pilot a “Resilience Framework” for Refugee communities in camp 2E in collaboration with the SMSD and other key humanitarian service actors. The “Resilience Framework” will summarize existing set of practices considering Early Warning System (EWS), sectoral services and ECO-system-based DRM mechanism considering socio-economical, physical, environmental, institutional interventions to support safety for vulnerable refugee population. It will boost environmental restoration as well as help to map the future interventions to increase risk knowledge, adaptive skills, and social protection which will contribute in the long-term local humanitarian and development planning process.
Along with the regular activity, the M&E team of BRAC will conduct regular monitoring to improve DRM programme quality. BRAC will collect information from program participants regarding the design, implementation, and completion stages through Community Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) and respond accordingly through established referral systems in both communities. BRAC’s staff will be oriented on PSEA/safeguarding and gender mainstreaming to ensure a safe environment for program participants and staff. Interventions will be tailored to the needs of vulnerable groups in disaster risk management.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Bangladesh is extremely vulnerable to the impacts of climate change and rated as the seventh most affected country from extreme weather events globally (Global Climate Risk Index 2021). Furthermore, managing one of the largest refugee camps in the world in Cox’s Bazar with around 1 million Rohingya refugees of Myanmar has put extreme pressure on the environmental resources in the region. Many of the camps in Cox’s Bazar are built on steep slopes and large forest areas have been cut down to build shelters and used as firewood. These activities have made the ground susceptible to landslides. The extreme climatic conditions have also threatened refugee livelihoods, ecosystem services, food security, health, human security, and socio-economic development of the targeted communities. In addition, in the densely populated camps, flammable construction materials (tarpaulin, bamboo) of the shelters have resulted in increased frequency of fire incidents, especially during the dry season. In Camp 2E 48% of the total population are youth (12,711), 52% are women (13,857) population and 270 person with disabilities. The topography is mixed with high terrain and flood land where population density relatively high with less amount of vegetation coverage. SMSD has identified 38 landslide prone sites where a total of 270 HHs are at risk. These Landslide prone area ranges from a minimum height of 2 feet to 12 feet. In addition, in camp 2E there are 13 flood prone sites where a total of 123 HHs are affected by heavy rainfall. In these low lying areas water logging lasts from a minimum of 1 hour to 4 hours; in two specific sites where the water is logged for 8 hours and affects a total of 23 HH at C block. Consequently, natural drainage system remains the major concern for camp 2E where the natural watershed catchment needs to be rehabilitated considering population settlement pattern. In addition, Cyclonic wind affects two specific site of camp 2E; one is in block A and one in B block as per nat-hazard by UNDP and REACHThe above dynamics has transformed the disaster management response system into a complex scenario with respect to disaster response, recovery, and preparedness.To counter the multiple hazards in refugees’ settlements, there is a need for an integrated, holistic and gender equitable approach in disaster management response systems and mitigation interventions (both infrastructural and non-infrastructural) to create resilient lead-instruments for vulnerable refugee populations. Critically important is the environmental restoration of resources to also ensure to facilitate peaceful co-existence with adjacent host communities to establish better environmental and social protection.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Through rationalization, BRAC will ensure that common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools are used across the targeted camp. This will lead towards a standardized and cost-effective approach to providing equitable services to refugee communities which also ensure a minimum standard of service deliveries by involving all sectoral service actors working in the camp. Where the integration with service sectors will be consulted and appropriation will be addressed accordingly considering continuation and replication in other camps. BRAC has prerequisite relationships with other camp actors, host communities, refugees, community groups/committees and the government departments, RRRC, CICs, UN, INGOs and local NGOs/CBOs to advocate to integrate into long term planning process and implementation. Moreover, BRAC will support eligible local NGOs/CBOs to increase their capacity on DRR and Humanitarian response mechanism in regards of sectoral integration to strengthening humanitarian localization in Cox's Bazar context.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

BRAC focuses on non-discriminatory access to support and services, identifying the most vulnerable and their specific needs and strengthening positive community protection capacity. As a PSEA network member, BRAC has compliant policies prioritising protection and safeguarding, emphasising on 'Do No Harm' to prevent unintended negative effects of any programme. BRAC’s SMSD programme will ensure meaningful access, participation and empowerment of its participants through programme design, planning and implementation with embed accountability at all levels. BRAC's protection and harassment policies will be strictly followed, and staff and volunteers will be trained on safeguarding, prevention and reporting on GBV/SGBV before deployment. Any protection issues including CP, GBV, SEA, protection of persons with disabilities as well as specific protection issues that arise in disasters and emergencies will be addressed through information-sharing, advocacy or referral to specialised actors.

3.2 - Gender

Gender issues are important to consider for emergency preparedness and increasing resilience among the refugees communities. To better understand the different needs of affected persons based on gender, age and other diversity, the project will conduct a needs assessment with specific questions related to resilience and disaster risk management. Considering how gender dynamics can facilitate or obstruct, BRAC will identify areas where understanding gender dynamics and the potential impact of project activities will be evaluated and discussed to determine how the gender analysis findings will be integrated into programme planning. BRAC will ensure that the special needs of all gender groups are identified, taken into consideration and guided by the Gender Policy.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Safety of the programme participants is a primary consideration of BRAC. The prevention of sexual harassment, child and adolescent abuse, child labour, intimidation, violence, bullying, humiliation and discrimination, neglect and exploitation is ensured at all levels of programmes from desgining to implementation. Moreover, BRAC will ensure the engagement of different age groups especially children (Age 0 to below 18 years) and elderly people (greater than 60 years). Through Focus group discussions (FGD) and baseline surveys, specific needs of different age groups will be identified and will be reflected in programme design, development, and implementation. DRR interventions will aim to achieve community resilience for different age groups and gender groups.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity refers to the differences based on gender, age, disability, cultural background, sexual orientation, social and economic background. BRAC promotes equity and equality at every stage of project design and provide services to all according to the needs and insists on upholding the rights and dignity of the affected people. BRAC's SMSD programme will be designed to address and respond to the needs of more vulnerable communities irrespective of gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, and disabilities. BRAC has a Gender Response Team (GRT) which will monitor and ensure the inclusion of gender-diversified people in the SMSD programme.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

While designing humanitarian projects, BRAC consider will UNHCR’s AAP framework- i) participation and inclusion; ii) communication and transparency; iii) feedback and response; and iv) organisational learning and adaptation. In the designing phase, BRAC’ SMSD team will ensure community participation through consultations. BRAC has a specific Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) to address specific needs of the Rohingya refugees. The SMSD sector will collect and analyze gender, age, and disability-disaggregated data, and conduct participatory gender analysis to understand capacities, barriers and aspirations. SMSD sector will regularly analyse the feedback/trends/unresolved issues and link with respective organisations/sectors for feedback referral to ensure AAP. Based on the community needs, interventions, planning and priority of the programme will be modified.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC will conduct an assessment to identify the gender based risk knowledge gaps including the resilience indicators in context of refugee settings. Moreover, community led mitigation interventions contributing to environmental restoration and recovery will be identified through the assessment. The Nature-based solution will be considered to help protect, manage, and sustain natural ecosystems. The project will identify the most vulnerable small-watershed area and conduct a comprehensive vulnerability mapping and community risk assessment by analysing data on water bodies, populations, resources and capacities within targeted geological settings. The risk assessment will be conducted through a community-based participatory approach by engaging women and youths. Based on the assessment, the project will develop an action plan to reduce the risks.
