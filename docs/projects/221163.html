Project Summary

This project aims to promote optimal nutrition practices and prevent and treat acute malnutrition in the most vulnerable population groups like children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers. These aims will be fulfilled through the implementation of the revised community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) interventions in Rumbek Centre, Wulu, and Cueibet counties of Lakes State. The project locations are among the ones where the new WHO prevention and treatment of wasting and nutritional oedema guidelines are going to be piloted. The project implementation period will be from January 1, 2025- December 31, 2025. PI will ensure that most marginalised groups like those with disability, minority ethnic groups, and other groups due to economic, political, social and cultural factors are reached and participated throughout the project cycle. Furthermore, preventive activities like MIYCN counselling, gardening, vitamin A and deworming, health, nutrition and education will be integrated into the program.
Furthermore, PI will participate in FSNMS, SMART and other joint assessments that may happen in the counties of operation. PI will implement OTP and TSFP programmes together in Cueibet (14) counties and shall implement OTP in Rumbek Centre (21 nutrition sites) and Wulu (14) counties. Besides, Plan International will provide inpatient treatment services in 2 stabilization centres: one in Rumbek Centre and one in Cueibet County. In addition to the aforementioned interventions, PI will implement BSFP for under two-year children and PLWs in all three counties for the lean season from April-September 2025. In total, this project will benefit 105022 populations (boys, girls, men, and women, WASH education /promotion, setting up demonstration gardens, complementary food cooking demonstrations and distribution of WASH and other preventive NFIs (like soap, aqua tab, mosquito net etc.) will be done at nutrition sites and at the community level. As part of the treatment protocol, all children with MAM and SAM will be screened and treated for malaria according to the national protocol / or referred to nearby health facilities. Furthermore, preventive kits (jerrycans, Mosquito nets, and soaps) shall be provided to mothers /caregivers of children who are enrolled for treatment in OTP TSFP or SC sites as these children are at higher risk of being infected and to promote proper personal hygiene and sanitation practice. All children who will be discharged cured from OTPs will be enrolled on TSFP programs for full nutritional recovery, and those who deteriorate while in OTP will be referred to SC. Besides, if a child in TSFP is nutritionally deteriorated, he/she will be enrolled/referred to the OTP/SC program. Those who recovered shall be given a protection ratio for two weeks and referred to other (FSL, BSFP) services after providing counselling. 1. Needs

The humanitarian emergency in South Sudan is complex and severe, driven by inter-communal conflicts, climate change, a fragile economy, an influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees from neighbouring countries, and limited access to infrastructure and essential services. As a result, an estimated 9 million South Sudanese are in critical need of humanitarian support in 2024. Food insecurity is among the most critical issues, with 7.7 million people requiring food assistance during the lean season from April to July 2025, according to the latest IPC report, released in November 18, 2025, an estimated. Besides, children remain at critical risk of malnutrition, and the IPC projection for the period between July 2024 and June 2025 is that 2.1 million children aged 6 to 59 months are at risk of acute malnutrition, of which 650,000 are at risk of severe acute malnutrition. Besides, 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity, and Warrap. Furthermore, the impact of the food security and nutrition situation is further aggravated by the Sudan crisis that caused the influx of vulnerable populations that require aid, and increased market costs of food and non-food items in the market, further pressuring people’s coping capacities. The situation of malnutrition is not far better in Lakes State, where seven out of all counties are in IPC AMN 2 and 3 (Alert and serious) according to the same IPC report.Above all, the infant and young child nutrition indicators are also not satisfactory. According to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring (FSNM) 26 report, July 2021, exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) was practised by 58% of the population; minimum dietary diversity is 24%, minimum meal frequency is 31%, and minimum acceptable diet is attained by only 6% in Lake's state. Likewise, a SMART nutrition and mortality survey by IRC, in April 2023 in Yirol West County, found a prevalence of GAM of 15.1% (11.7-19.4% C.I.) and the prevalence of SAM was 4.1% (2.5-6.55% C.I.), 31.4% children were sick in the two weeks prior to the survey date, and Vitamin A supplementations rate of 29.8%, Furthermore, IYCF was found to be sub-optimal with only 16.2% (n = 6 from37) of infants started solid, semi-solid or soft food in a timely manner. Moreover, only 15.9% and 15.3% of children had acceptable MDD and MMF, respectively. Bottle feeding was practised by 15% of children before 24 hours of the survey. The food security situation was also found to be worse in that only 12%(n=45) of respondents had acceptable or borderline food consumption, while the rest counterpart, 88% (n=388) of the households, had poor consumption scores in the past seven days prior to the survey. The situation in other counties of Lakes could not be far from these findings. Furthermore, the need assessment concluded by Plan International (PI) in September 2024 found that FGD participants identified key problems being severe food shortage leading to hunger, disease outbreaks, insecurity and flooding, and a lack of boreholes or water points. These factors are major underlying factors for maternal and child malnutrition. Given the high rate of acute food security coupled with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macroeconomic crisis, conflict, insecurity, outbreaks of diseases and low agricultural production, it will be mandatory to continue providing preventive and curative nutrition services within the three counties. Furthermore, providing these services will significantly contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality of children, PLWs, and other high-risk population groups and improve the health, development and productivity of the general population.

2. Project activities

The key activities in this project are • Nutrition, health and WASH education in side nutrition sites and at outreach sites• Providing Maternal, Infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to mothers/caregivers • Screening for acute malnutrition at community outreach, campaigns, etc.• Admission and treatment of children 6-59 with severe and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM) and Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with acute malnutrition • Regular collection (from WFP and UNICEF) and delivery of nutrition supplies to nutrition sites • Doing Exhaustive MUAC assessment• Community and local leaders mobilizaiton on prevention of nutrition supply diverison• Participating in FSNMS and other assessments like IRNA• Establish/promote chicken rearing, establishment of kitchen gardens and promotion of seed multiplication for vegetable gardens and support fish preservation in the community in coordination with FSL cluster and government counterparts • Distribution of preventive items like soap, aqua tab, and mosquito net• Training of staff, volunteers and government staff on Community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM updated guideline for piloting), MIYCN, child protection, Gender, GBV, WASH • Supporting Vitamin supplementation and deworming campaigns • Train the mother support groups on Maternal infant and young child nutrition, nutrition screening, identification of malnourished children and PLW and referral• Start-up and close-up workshop • Printing and distribution of visibility materials • Establish a community complaint and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing suggestion boxes, suggestion books and regular meetings with beneficiaries in the SC and OTP/TSFP sites• Training of CNWs CNVs and other nutrition cadres on the identification of children under five with Acute watery diarrhoea (AWD), treated using ORS and Zinc as a conjugate regiment • Construction/rehabilitation of latrines, water points, nutrition facilities (screening areas, waiting area, storage facilities) handwashing facilities and garbage pits • Implementation of Parenting under Pressure (a parenting program specifically designed for families who are facing multiple hardships, including mental health problems, family conflict, severe financial stress, etc. The program is adopted for use in South Sudan)• Integrate child stimulation and ECD in nutrition sites • Train CNVs on agronomic practices including fish preservation techniques attending nutrition sites • Distribution of WASH NFI to beneficiaries with malnourished children • Doing GBV safety audits in nutrition sites and mainstream child protection and GBV measures in nutrition centers • Protect, support, and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices including celebrating the Breastfeeding Week campaign and others • Establish and ensure the presence of referral linkage with other services/clusters, such as protection, FSL, Health, and others.
