Project Summary

Yei is one of the counties in Central Equatoria State that has been heavily affected by the 2016 conflict between government and opposition forces, resulting in the displacement of people from their places of origin. Yei is currently home to some 61199 IDPs, 22848 returnees and 4056 refugees, who have settled in the main town of Yei. According to the RRC, the county continues to receive a daily influx of returnees from Uganda and the DRC.The conflict in Yei has disrupted the health system in the county, with most of the infrastructure destroyed and in need of repair. As a result of the conflict, the provision of health services has become a major challenge, particularly in rural areas. The most affected payams in Yei that require health interventions are Lasu, Tore, Mugwo, Yei Town and Otogo. This project will therefore aim to improve health services through the provision of drugs, rehabilitation of existing health infrastructure, child immunisation, health education and treatment of the most communicable diseases. The intervention will target 82513 people including men, women and children from the 5 payams of Tore, Mugwo, Yei town, Lasu and Otogo in Yei County.

1. Needs

South Sudan is facing a dire health crisis due to a lack of adequate health services and infrastructure. The country relies on international humanitarian assistance to provide basic health care, but this is not enough to address the multiple and complex health emergencies facing the country. Floods, food insecurity, conflict and disease outbreaks have worsened the health situation and increased the vulnerability of the population, especially women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities.The aim of this project is to provide low-cost, high-impact primary health care services in Yei River County, which covers the 5 payams of Yei town, Lasu, Tore, Mugwo and Otogo. Yei River County has been severely affected by the 2016 conflict in the region, with many people internally displaced and others in refugee camps in Uganda and the Democratic Republic of Congo. Most health facilities have been destroyed and community health structures have been weakened. With the current peace agreement in the country, the security situation in Yei is relatively calm and many people are returning to their original homes from the refugee camps, making the provision of life-saving health services to the region even more important. Yei County is home to approximately 4028 refugees from DRC and Sudan (UNHCR 2023), 61199 IDPs and 22848 returnees (IOM 2022). This huge number of returnees and IDPs puts a lot of pressure on the few existing health facilities in the counties. An assessment of health facilities in Yei River County conducted by the County Health Department in January 2023 indicates that out of the 40 health facilities in Yei River County, only 18 health facilities are functional and despite being functional they still lack some basic services such as drugs, lack of skilled personnel and ANC services. This is in line with and assessment conducted by humanitarian agencies in Tore Payams in Yei in February 2022 where cases of malaria, diarrhoea and respiratory infections are on the rise. The DHIS report for Yei River County also confirms that there is high morbidity due to malaria, diarrhoea and RTI among children. Therefore, this project will address to reduce the morbidity and mortality of the common diseases among children, women through the provision of basic health care services.

2. Project activities

Therefore, this response will provide quality health services to the affected population of Yei, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, general and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and gender-based violence (GBV). The response will also improve sexual and reproductive health services in EmONC (Emergency Newborn and Obstetric Care), family planning, PMTCT (Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission), clinical management of rape and supplies for vulnerable women and girls. The response will use referral mechanisms to improve efficiency, integration and quality of care. The response will follow the principles of universal health coverage and prioritise prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation for vulnerable groups. The response will also continue Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) prevention, preparedness and response activities as Yei River County faces the threat of EVD and other viral hemorrhagic fevers. The response will support health facilities and community-based systems to care for people in need. The response will also strengthen technical, operational and field coordination with partners and authorities. The response will also support the disease surveillance and reporting system and pre-position medical supplies and equipment in Yei River County.
