Project Summary

Through this intervention ZOA will effectively and efficiently respond to urgent and identified livelihood needs in Aleppo and Rural Damascus. With local partners, ZOA will provide vocational training for 300 members of vulnerable households in Aleppo and Rural Damascus, set up a sewing factory in Douma, Rural Damascus, for women with sewing skills, and rehabilitate 36 small shops in Arbin, Rural Damascus, to provide income and support local economic infrastructure. The approach is based on the specific context in each location.
ZOA prioritises communities with high severity of needs, and selects vulnerability and selection criteria in line with sector standards. ZOA vulnerability criteria also incorporates analysis of protection concerns. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project and implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach and impactful support to households. Beneficiaries are selected after consultation with local authorities. Project committees are set up in Arbin and Douma to oversee the project implementation and take ownership of the project activities after project end.
For vocational training, vulnerable households will be selected, and one person from each household will receive a vocational skill training. The topic will be decided based on the wishes of the attendees as well as the economic situation in the community, with the goal of providing an income after the training. Participants receive cash for attendance, to support their families during the training. The vocational training is supplemented by lifeskills training, and each participant will receive a relevant toolkit. Families with members with disabilities will be targeted, and training will be explored that is relevant for people living with disabilities to gain an income independently.
In Douma, Rural Damascus, there are many skilled seamstresses. The area once hosted multiple sewing factories, which are no longer operating. This project will establish a sewing factory to employ 50 skilled women. A further 25 women will be trained in sewing.
In Arbin, Rural Damascus, shops have closed during the last years. By rehabilitating 36 small shops, the local economy will be supported. Each shop owner is required to employ at least one person from a vulnerable household.
Projects will be monitored and adjusted accordingly by ZOA and local communities. Combining vocational training in topics deemed relevant in the local communities with rehabilitation of shops and establishing a factory will give ZOA important insight into the effectiveness of each modality in a Syrian context towards the objective of improved livelihoods for vulnerable families.

4 - Needs

Assessments continue to highlight significant needs for families in Early Recovery and Livelihoods in Syria. In the project areas, the HNO list Aleppo as the governorate with the most people in need in ERL in absolute numbers, although the severity scale has reduced slightly in the past year. Rural Damascus has a severity ranking of 3.17 in the HNO. Specifically the communities targeted in Aleppo and Rural Damscus have a high severity in income (3.2,4.7) and VT (3.8 ,5.5). Community assesments from local community centres supported by ZOA in Aleppo have shown that PiN request support with income and livelihoods to support their families in the longer term. With a deteriorating economic situation, even people with employment struggle to support their families, and families with no bread-winners or female headed households are even more vulnerable.In Arbin the destruction and deterioration of its infrastructure including its shops, have resulted in most of the community workers to lose their jobs or flee the area. Approximately, around 1000 shops have been destroyed causing the most evident challenge to the Arbinian community, and hindering local economic development, based on assesments by ZOAs partner. After years of intensive instability and tension, Arbin is now a safe accessible area, which has witnessed a large returnee’s movement leading to more needs arising especially the essential need for livelihood assistance. ZOAs partner assesses the 18.000 people are in need of assistance in Arbin.In Duma, ZOA’s partner assess that 35,000 people are in acute need for livelihood opportunities to retain their resilience. Moreover, the crisis has negatively affected Douma transforming it to a poor city after it was considered to be the richest amongst Rural Damascus’s districts containing approximately 1500 sewing workshops of which 90% of them are destroyed. Respondents are requesting the activity to strengthen livelihood opportunities for women.

5 - Implementation of activities

ZOA will implement this project with two local partners. With GOPA, 300 vulnerable families will be identified in Aleppo and Rural Damascus. One member from each family will receive inputs in the form of vocational skill training, life skill training and a relevant toolkit. Families will receive cash for attendance to support them during the training. The topics for the skill trainings will be decided based on a local assessment of employment skillgaps, and dialogue with the attendees. Beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerability, especially targeting women headed households and HH with PLWDs, in consultation with sector actors and local community. Care will be taken to find suitable income activities for PLWDs. IDPs and returnees are especially vulnerable and will be selected along with host community members. With the Syria Trust for Development, two activities will be undertaken. In Afrin, 36 small shops will be rehabilitated. Shops are selected with a local community committee. Each business owner commits to providing employment for at least one person with high vulnerability for at least six months. In Duma, a sewing factory will be established, to provide employment for 50 women plus management staff. An additional 25 women will be trained in sewing skills through the factory. Market analysis will be undertaken and links made to other businesses to provide products relevant to the market. At project end the factory will be given to the community committee.The Syria Trust has good understanding of the situation in both areas, having implemented projects there before, and having established community committees that input into the project design and implementation. ZOA will oversee the project implementation and ZOA’s livelihood technical advisors will provide technical input to the local partners. Projects will be monitored by ZOA through monthly project updates and site visits.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Market and exchange rate stability: While some products are still difficult to access in Syria, markets across the target area are largely functional. However, there is risk of volatility with the Syrian pound exchange rate which could not only affect prices of goods but also overall implementation as ZOA works through multiple currencies. As this project is heavily reliant on local supplies and links to the local market within Syria, economic and currency markets will need to continue to be monitored.- Access and security: The proposed activities rely on access to the project area. ZOA will adhere to GoS protocols and approval processes while observing core humanitarian principles and maintaining ongoing security and context mapping, security risk analysis and rigid security procedures. ZOA will ensure that all staff receive training on ZOA’s approach to safety and security in the field. ZOA will also maintain positive relationships with communities, local leaders and authorities as part of it’s acceptance strategy and access planning. - Availability of and ability to train staff: ZOA will hire Syrian staff and ensure gender equality wherever possible (in particular, aiming to ensure inclusion of female staff for working with women).- Community members and local stakeholders accept and are allowed to participate in project activities: The success of ZOA’s project depends on active participation of community members and local stakeholders, facilitated the local partners. ZOA will work within the parameters of GoS protocols in relation to beneficiary engagement through approved bodies such as Syria Trust. ZOA will define beneficiary engagement protocols based on its existing relationships with relevant authorities and its experiences.
