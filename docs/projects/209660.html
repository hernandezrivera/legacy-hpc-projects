Project Summary

In 2024, 4.5 million people reside in an area outside the control of the Syrian regime, with the population in NWs estimated to be 5.1 million according to another OCHA report. These individuals face severe deprivation, worsened by the recent earthquake. Recent assessments reveal the top five priority needs: Medicines and other commodities (60%), Paediatric consultations (48%), Treatment for chronic diseases (45%), First aid/emergency care (41%), and General/specialized surgical services (29%). The main barriers to accessing health services include the high cost of transportation (88%), lack of medicines/medical equipment (65%), inability to afford health services (64%), lack of transportation (61%), difficulty accessing facilities for those with mobility impairments (46%), and overcrowded health facilities (44%).
The project aims to reduce mortality and morbidity rates among IDPs and hosts in conflict-affected communities in NWS by improving access to lifesaving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those in need, especially the vulnerable people, including women, children, and the elderly as well as the people with disabilities
To accomplish these objectives, UOSSM seeks to support 17 HFs, including 5 hospitals,5 specialized centers, and 7 PHCs in Idleb, Aleppo and Ar-Raqqa. The mentioned HFs are well-equipped but have a gap in salaries, medical supplies, and operational costs. The HFs are located in areas with high priority, according to the health cluster and the TWGs, and distributed in densely populated and accessible locations, most of the people are IDPs with a low level of medical care and a dire economic situation. Notably, 4 of these HFs are recognized by the Syrian Board of Medical Specialties as training centers for specialized physicians.
Supporting and upgrading the capacity of these HFs is very important in light of their strategic locations in addition to the size and variety of health services provided that include:
- PHC services: IMCI, management of NCDs and CDs, providing RH and dental services, applying IPC measures, lab tests, vaccination and bandage services.
- Hospital services: 3 general hospitals (Al-Andalus, Jisr-Ash-Shugur and Quenya) provide delivery services, maternal and child health care as well as general surgeries and related health services; in addition, 2 specialized hospitals offer ophthalmic health care, audiogram services, pronunciation rehabilitation, orthopedic and reconstructive surgeries, and associated services.
- Specialized centers services: diagnostic, treatment, and blood transfusion services for thalassemia patients, specialized radiography services, dialysis services, and providing patients with blood products for them as well as providing the surrounding hospitals with blood units through the blood bank
To ensure the integration and continuity of health services, UOSSM also seeks to sustain the functionality of the referral system through support ambulance vehicles within the central referral system in Idlib, which coordinate referrals (3500 patients per month) through 60 ambulances between HFs in Idleb and Aleppo western rural. Also, as a CRS, it is interested in raising staff capacity in other referral and ambulance systems so that technical training will be conducted for 100 paramedics on ACLS. Those paramedics are staff from the CRS and other functioning ambulance systems in the field
It's important to note that all UOSSM staff will undergo training on PSEA, sign a TOR, and be familiar with the organization's internal PSEA policy. Monitoring of this policy is carried out by both the HR and MEAL teams. Additionally, all UOSSM staff will sign a code of conduct that includes a commitment to PSEA. The team is equipped to identify any cases of concern regarding PSEA and refer them to UOSSM's designated PSEA focal point for management and follow-up. Furthermore, beneficiaries will be provided with the PSEA hotline for lodging complaints.
