Project Summary

The project aims to provide formal and non-formal education to 5434 IDP out of school children (OOSC) :3260 girls, 2172 boys aged 6-18 in 11 camp schools in tents and in buildings built with bricks and metal roofs in the camps themselves focusing on PWDs. The proposed project contributes to the HRP Objectives through:
-Increase safe and equitable access to formal education for 5434 crisis affected students: 3260 girls, 2172 boys (aged 3 15 years)
-Improve the quality of formal education for 5434 crisis affected students: 3260 girls, 2172 boys (aged 3 15 years)
The camps are located in rural Idleb, Harim district. The action will focus on access and quality .Each camp school will have around 494 children from both genders, in addition to 155 teachers(35 Pupil Teacher Ratio), 11 head teachers, 11 cleaners and 11 guards who will be recruited based on a fair hiring criteria. Teachers will be paid monthly incentives according to days worked as tracked by timesheets. As a plan A, Syria Relief will deliver physical education based on the EDs decisions taking into consideration the protective measures for COVID-19.However, home-based education will be as a plan B if an emergency situation occurred, where teachers will deliver lessons to children in groups in safe camps if ,for instance, if one of the camps was shelled or evacuated or flooded The plan C can be applied through the distance learning in which the children can attend online sessions over the smart devices. SR will provide contextualized and adaptable capacity building through trainings and improved learning circles to school staff as relevant and needed and further coaching and mentoring to improve professional development of teachers within each school, with the collaboration of teachers, school management and relevant education authorities.
For formal education, the project will use the Syrian Interim Government (SIG) curriculum. For non-formal education, a BLN curriculum developed by SCI and IRC will be used. In the school year, provide inclusive formal education in the targeted locations: in case schools remain shut, the formal curriculum education
will be provided through online learning for children with access to smart devices, or through SLP work papers for children with no access to smart devices. SR will ensure equal access of siblings to smart phones, through mobilizing parents and communities and close monitoring and follow up. SR will provide remedial classes in the summer season in order to help children who are falling behind their peers at the class (due to school suspension) in addition to SEL and PSS Recreational activities/sessions. In case school suspension continues, SR will provide 5 remedial classes (2 Arabic, 2 Math and 1 English) in collaboration with the ED, in addition to at least 1 recreational PSS session and 1 SEL skill session that will be done either online using
online platforms for children with access to electronic devices, or through SLP for children with no access to electronic devices.
BTL campaigns will target the 11 camps for key messages on the importance of learning. There will be classroom Observation for overseeing the teachers and support them.Teachers will form Teachers Learning circles in each school for exchanging the experiences and teaching
CWD will have an access to education through including them in the schools among their peers.there will be ramps and reels for them in addition to special WASH gender-sensitive facilities

4 - Needs

As per Syria 2021 HNO Advance Copy, an estimated 6.9 million people (97 per cent children) need humanitarian education assistance. 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. The escalation of violence led to increasing PSS trauma, their families, and personnel. Syria Relief conducted a needs assessment in Idleb governorate prioritizing 11 camps in Idleb which have IDP out of school children who fled recently from southern Idleb, Hama, rural Aleppo or when the conflict erupted from all over Syria. The 11 camps have 5434 children (3260 girls, 2172 boys )aged 6-15 (including 19 children with disabilities).Children are out of school for 9 months at minimum. The assessment revealed that there is an urgent need to support the IDP camps aged 6-15 by providing formal and non-formal education as well PSS activities as well as emergency food kits in which SR will cover as much of those emergency needs from other donors.The camps are in big need of education items, incentives for 98 male and 90 female education personnel who are currently working as volunteers, there is also needs for teacher training on nonformal education and education methods. Existing classrooms and WASH facilities are exhausted and have been used by the IDP communities and they need light repairs for the WASH gender-sensitive facilities and for the furniture inside the tents.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project focuses on the following activities related to access and quality:*Light repairs for the existing classrooms and WASH facilities, *supplying the camps with school furniture : heater, desks and board, *provide safety items as 1st aid kits and extinguishers,* provide disabilities with rails and ramps, *deliver non-formal sessions for girls and boys whose ASER score are 0-1 using BLN, *Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns and convey key awareness-raising sessions and messages, *Hold meeting with stakeholders and local actors, *enroll children without discrimination, *conducting ASER test to measure the learning outcomes, *provide children with remedial classes during Summer School, *provide students with learning materials ,*provide teachers with teaching kits, *provide referral pathway services to affected girls and boys, *conduct child-centered and CP related trainings to teachers,* pay monthly stipends to education personnel, *provide girls and boys with recreational materials, *distribute fuel and heaters in cold months, *pay running cost for schools, *establish parent teachers associations in each school,* establish teacher learning circles in each schools, *printing formal SIG textbooks , BLN for children,* conduct PSS semi-structured activities for the children in the camps,*conduct MEAL activities related to the project,such as monitoring the distributions,trainings,complaints(accountability),assessments.The Head teachers will be responsible for general management of the school, e.g. all documenting, recording, registering related to the teachers and the students, meeting with the parents/local council. The Head teacher coordinating with the project field officers for reporting the project activities

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Whilst impossible to mitigate loss of access due to changes in the conflict lines in its entirety SR is working to minimize risks, e.g. preparing evacuations for existing programs; focus on security assessments during location selection process for new projects; updating risk assessments and contingency plans. Further, SR has identified several trigger points and resulting actions. There are 4 key escalation points in this regard - 1) temporary suspension of activities until deemed possible to resume, 2) relocation of programms, 3) suspension of programs, 4) termination of projects. Where viable, SR would work to resume activities through the adaptable modalities SR developed such as home-based education and mobile education responses. SR will ensure that all incidents or disruption to activities are reported immediately and analysed by the programme in coordination with the SR Safety and Security team. SR will be updating risk assessments of site selection and contingency plans, and a lessons-learnt exercise will be carried out after an incident or near miss. In addition, should airstrikes occur when children are already in classes, SR will support schools to ensure that children are aware of how to act in the event of an airstrike through school safety plans. Home learning is also conducted when schools are closed. SR was one of the last education actors in besieged Aleppo and Damascus and has collected lessons leant an experience to deal with such conditions. and If cross-border access is restricted in the longer term (eg. non-renewal of UNSCR 2332) this will significantly impact cross-border access from Baba-Al-Hawa for all partners and agencies.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

SR has PSEA Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure all effort is made to protect allProgramme participants. SR’s PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising, Prevention, Reporting, andResponse. A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and report CSG cases of childabuse, exploitation or violence. In addition, all forms of sexual harassment and exploitation by staff who work in and for SR areunacceptable, both during and outside of work. In addition to PSEA, SR reporting system in place to prevent and take actions against suchbehaviors, which clearly stating zero tolerance for sexual harassment. All staff, contractors, cleaners, etc. must adhere to these, and SRoffices have been already trained on the latter policy.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures 1. During the selection of the staff ,some issues may be faced:camps management may order to hire from the camps only,undergarduates,campers refuse to hire from outside their camp Mitigating Measure: Selection criteria will be clear with no chance for personal judgment taking into consideration the participation of the local communities as references, exit interviews 2. Difficulty in securing textbooks or education materials Mitigating Measure: Contracting with printers in Syria, communicating with interested donors 3.Delay in the disbursement Mitigating Measure: Teachers can work as volunteers since they were told that a delay may occur, SR can pay from its own fund until the disbursement is transferred 4.Attacks on schools Mitigating Measure: Safety procedures,plans,awareness 5.Duplication when selecting the locations Mitigating Measure:coordinating with the Education Cluster and organizations 6.Dropou of children Mitigating Measure:Awareness to parents, back to school campaigns and mobilize the community
