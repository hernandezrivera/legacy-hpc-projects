Project Summary

The project will provide GBV response and prevention and services to women and girls including other vulnerable populations in drought affected areas of BeitBridge, Chimaninani, Chiredzi, Chipinge, and Nyanga. In order to assess the risks and barriers to accessing services, a safety audit will be conducted and recommendations implemented to mitigate the risk of GBV against women and girls.

1 - Activities

Following the unprecedented global spread of the Covid-19 pandemic and it’s transmission in Zimbabwe, the government enacated restrictions that limited movements and large gatherings as preventative measures to mitigate transmission of the virus. These measures contributed to economic hardhsips at household level, limited access to information and services, increased incidences of Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) and Gender Based Violence (GBV), teenage pregnancies especially as adolescent girls were not engaged in school or in other socially-friendly activities that could keep them busy. These and the continued effects of the cyclone Idai have contributed to increased vulnerabilities among women and girls in Beit Bridge, Chimanimani, Chiredzi, Chipinge and Nyanga districts where the the International Rescue Committee (IRC) is implementing the Women’s protection and empowerment, Food and Security, and WASH projects. The proposed project will target 5,000 adolescent girls (15-19 years) who are in and out of school and 10,000 vulnerable women. The IRC will support women and girls through providing awareness sessions on sexual reproductive health, GBV prevention and risk mitigation and psycho-social support sessions to enable healing and recovery from distress and other traumatic experiences. While it has been noted that there is an increase in GBV cases after cash transfers in drought affected communities, the IRC team will use the GBV awareness prevention information sessions, campaigns and dialogues to engage communities and the leaders to contribute towards reduced scenarios of violence and abuse . The IRC will provide survivor-centered comprehensive, quality and timely case management services to GBV survivors. through the women and girl spaces, individual and groups psychosocial support sessions will be conducted for women and girls as social-support systems for healing and recovery. An updated GBV referral pathway will be printed and disseminated in communities to facilitate appropriate and informed consent referrals. GBV prevention awareness and information sessions will be conducted across Chimanimani, Chipinge, Chiredzi, Beit Bridge and Nyanga district.The IRC will support the establishment of 10 Girl Empowerment Movement (GEM) clubs (each clubs with 12 members) in 10 schools in all the targeted districts as an avenue of building confidence and self-esteem as well as creating a space to conduct sessions on adolescent sexual and reproductive health, menstrual hygiene management and GBV.20 peer educators (Gender champions of Change) will be trained in all targeted districts while 50 duty bearers and other relevant stakeholders community members on GBV concepts and guiding principles and referral pathway (Chimanimani, Chiredzi, Nyanga, Beit Bridge). 1000 women and girls will be empowered with vocational training skills in all targeted Districts. One Safety Audit will be conducted to assess the risks, barriers and security concerns that expose women and girls to GBV in all targeted Districts and 2 500 Dignity kits will be distributed to women and girls of reproductive age in all targeted districts

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Every stage of the project will ensure that the women and girls, the community and relevant stakeholders are consultated and involved to make sure their needs are included and addressed in our programming. Focus group discussions and listening sessions will be held with the communities so that feedback gathered will inform our programming. The community and relevant stakeholders will be encouraged to use the organisation’s hotline and suggestion boxes available to give their feedback on our programming.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will not cause any damage to the environment there wont be any construction of any infrastructure. The IRC will work with local traditional leadership and the Environmental Management Agency (EMA) at district level to identify any project activities that may promote behaviours that may be detrimental to the environment.
