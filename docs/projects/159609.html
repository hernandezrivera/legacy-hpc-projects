Project Summary

To address the high rate of malnutrition, ARC will support curative and preventive nutrition interventions to treat children under five (U5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). Services include nutrition screening, targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP) and outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP). All interventions will be aligned with MoH, WHO guidelines/protocols and Somalia integrated management of acute malnutrition (IMAM) protocols.
Nutrition services will be co-located at the MCHs noted in sub section Management of acute malnutrition Co-location of nutrition and health services will help ensure that children U5 a cross both genders and PLW presenting for health services are screened for nutrition and likewise those presenting for nutrition services can be referred and easily access primary, maternal, and child health services, as needed. Considering all genders, all children U5 presenting at the MCH will be screened and if necessary, referred for admission to the TSFP or OTP per the IMAM admission criteria. SAM cases presenting with medical complications will be referred to the nearest stabilization center (SC). ARC is also planning to combine the nutrition and health nursing staff to foster more integration across health and nutrition activities at the facilities.
The linkage to WASH and health components will ensure a comprehensive approach to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with the compound effect of diseases resulting from poor sanitation conditions, lack of access to safe water, and malnutrition. To increase the impact, the nutrition project will be coordinated also with ARC Protection and Livelihood projects.
ARC will closely coordinate with MoH and respond in locations where gaps in nutrition services have been identified. ARC will ensure the participation of the local communities, with special attention to participation of women, in decision making throughout the project cycle to strengthen community acceptance and ownership. Throughout the lifetime of the project, ARC will work with the MoH to pave the way for a proper phasing out at the end of the project.

Project Activities

• Manage MAM and SAM cases for children U5 and PLW according to Somalia IMAM guidelines at nine sites, including screening, treatment and referral for the appropriate service.• Conduct medical checks and routine treatment at nutritional screening and follow up.• Distribute take-home TSFP ration on a bi-weekly basis.• Refer and facilitate referral of SAM cases with medical complication to SC.• Provide refresher training on IMAM to staff. • Conduct routine community-based MUAC screening for children under 2 years / PLW through ttC.• Rehabilitate waiting shelters at nutrition sites for the MCHs.• Coordinate with UNICEF and WFP to receive supplementary supplies, including RUTF and RUSF. • Collect and analyze nutritional data of those presenting at the facilities.• Provide IYCF counselling and disseminate IEC materials to mothers/families though ANC/PNC and routine education sessions at the facility; and through ttC at community level. • Facilitate mother support groups; including providing training on how to take and interpret MUAC and weight measurements for children U5.• Promote early initiation of breastfeeding and exclusive breastfeeding through first 6 months.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement. ARC will prevent risks of exclusion based on displacement by ensuring that internally displaced persons benefit from the interventions just like the host communities. The project will target women, men, girls and boys in both IDP camps and host communities so that equity and equality is maintained at all levels. In actualizing this, there are risks that ARC will need to mitigate so that service provision to the most deserving communities is not hindered. All distributions will be done in safe, convenient places considering all aspects of protection and security of the beneficiaries as per the principle of "Do No Harm" ARC will also put in place community consultation and feedback mechanism Kuja Kuja to collect real time community satisfaction feedback (www.kujakuja.com). Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age. The risks that will be anticipated has to do with social exclusion of women and girls in accessing nutrition services because women and children especially girls are treated as socially inferior to their male counterparts thus will have an effect on health seeking behaviour. Inequities arising from gender and age are often associated with poor health and reduced well being. Health and nutrition problems often faced by women and children and vulnerabilities are poorly recognized by the community. ARC will ensure that women and children being the most marginalized and vulnerable have access to quality health and nutrition services in the target locations and basically integrating with other sectors such as WASH and protection. This will minimize the risks associated with exclusion based on gender and age because of the provision of an equitable service to all. The data generated from the field by ARC will have gender disaggregation so that numbers reached can be determined and analyzed regularly to inform decision making. The project will also collect feedback from beneficiaries on a quarterly basis including women and children to get their perceptions/views and recommendations about ARC program which will form a basis for improvement in future programming. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence. Through the adoption of "Do No Harm" approaches, efforts are made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs are implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of Somalia conflict situation. ARC believes that understanding the situation and the context in which we operate is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and to avoid negative consequences in the operation area. As a part of the Protection project, ARC will carry out community capacity building to promote peaceful co-existence and conflict resolution. Insecurity arising from extremist groups (Al Shabaab) and inter-clan clashes through contextual understanding and working with the community to acquire information about threats, ARC will maintain a high level of acceptance at community level and with key stakeholders and decision makers. ARC will continue monitoring the security situation and trends in the region, analyse risks (their likelihood and impact), review mitigation measures, and integrate the findings in to ARC’s Safety and Security Management Plan, and ensure that all staff are trained on and adhere strictly to our security guidelines Feedback and complaints: ARC International will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from).

Please provide further explanation

The proposed Nutrition interventions aim to support existing government health centers and Primary health units to promote sustainability, strengthening of healthcare and nutrition system and the continuation of care, focusing on where the needs are highest in proposed locations. As a result of quality programming and proven results, ARC enjoy strong relationships with target communities, local and national authorities. ARC has consults routinely with the RMO, DMOH and MOH on the program design and benefits from its support. In addition, ARC hire staff from local areas to ensure they have a good knowledge of local needs and possess the contacts and community knowledge necessary for success. The interventions will be implemented in close coordination with MOH, UNICEF, UNFPA, WFP and WHO as the health cluster lead and other health actors in the area. District Medical office of health will be the counterpart which ARC will interact with most frequently as they will facilitate any coordination required at facility level as well as make sure the staff in facilities are well-managed. RMO will work with ARC on staffing, facilities selection and technical monitoring at the regional level. Quarterly to semi-annual joint-monitoring visit will be coordinated at regional level to ensure the project keep abreast of the progress. At the central level, the major role for the MoH is to guide the health intervention at a strategic level for all region making sure the overall direction of the intervention is in alignment of the health/nutrition strategy for Somalia and the approach adapted are technically sounds and contextually appropriate.
