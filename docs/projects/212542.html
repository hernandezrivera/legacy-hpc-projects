Project Summary

Since its initiation by the Syria Strategic Steering Group in 2017, the Whole of Syria (WoS) Prevention of Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH) Programme has supported the interagency humanitarian response in the joint implementation of international PSEAH commitments. The WoS PSEAH programme is a multi-donor, comprehensive response, designed to address the risks of SEAH posed by the complex and protracted humanitarian emergency in Syria. The PSEAH programme through an established, robust interagency PSEAH Network consisting of more than 410 focal points from 225 UN agencies, INGOs and NGOs, and cluster/working groups strives to decrease the risk of crisis‐affected communities experiencing incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by humanitarian workers and provide technical and operational support to humanitarian actors to prevent and respond to SEAH cases. Since 2019, the WoS PSEAH Network operates an inter-agency hotline in NWS, which offers humanitarian staff, beneficiaries and communities a way to make complaints related to SEAH. In 2023, the PSEAH inter-agency feedback and complaint mechanism was formally expanded to include not only SEAH but also AAP and corruption, fraud and aid diversion and has been rebranded “The Safeline”. This inter-agency feedback and complaint mechanism is the only inter-agency complaint channel for affected populations in NWS. Through years of dissemination, it has gained the trust of communities. It is currently receiving thousands of calls per year, most of which are about how to access assistance, indicating a breakdown in communication with affected populations. This has led to the endangerment of vulnerable persons who are susceptible increased risk of SEAH. It was therefore essential to expand the hotline to include AAP concerns. Failure to do so would also result in failure to mitigate increased risks of SEAH. Although most humanitarian partners have their own compliant and feedback mechanisms in place, the expanded hotline, serves as a second line of defense and as many of the allegations involve NGO misconduct, it provides a safe and neutral channel for the affected population. Working in close collaboration with Clusters, the Safeline also provides general information about the availability and location of humanitarian services to all callers.
In NES, the current coordination structure overcomes existing communication barriers between NES key stakeholders including local authorities and cross line actors through active collaboration and coordination between the Inter-Agency (IA) NES PSEAH Network and the In-Country Network (ICN). As a result, PSEAH Focal Points from both Networks can ensure the timely referral of SEAH victims to service providers and the referral of complaints to the relevant organization to ensure timely investigation and that accountability measures are met. The IA PSEAH Network shared its Inter-Agency Community Based Complaints Mechanism SOPs (CBCM SOPs) with the ICN and engages in discussions with the ICN around high-risk SEAH cases, in areas of joint presence, namely in camps. This includes agreeing to victim safety plans or sharing recommendations, facilitating access to protection services, and engaging in joint PSEAH advocacy initiatives. It also ensures that SEAH intake channels are part of an efficient and comprehensive CBCM for all NES operating entities and that the referral process is standardized for all SEAH referrals across NES. Finally, the IA PSEAH network and ICN attend coordination meetings with participation of the GBV, Child Protections and Protection working group coordinators to discuss complex cases and joint initiatives, strategies, and gaps in response.
Leadership and Coordination: The PSEAH programme, through its team of dedicated staff, will maintain coordination capacity and provide leadership and strategic guidance of the PSEAH Network hubs in NES and NWS, and at the Whole of Syria (WoS) level.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Capacity Building: The PSEAH programme will build the capacity of humanitarian workers through interactive trainings based on IASC standards. The training plan includes mandatory focal point training and training of trainers (ToT) to ensure the presence of qualified trainers across the response, as well as sector-specific trainings for clusters and other stakeholders as needed. Prevention, Community Mobilization and Response : The programme will work with the Network to conduct direct consultations and awareness raising sessions with affected populations and humanitarian workers in NWS and NES. Mainstreaming into the broader humanitarian infrastructure: To enhance local leadership and sustainability of the PSEAH network, the programme will mainstream PSEAH into the humanitarian infrastructure, through integration of PSEAH messaging into key multisectoral tools, assessments, and guidance documents and will work closely with clusters/working groups to mainstream PSEAH across all sectors. Further, the programme will support PSEAH risks assessments to support the overall response and mitigate SEAH risks. SEAH by aid workers and actors involved in the provision of aid directly contradicts the principles upon which humanitarian action is based and represents a protection failure on the part of the aid community. SEAH inflicts harm on those whom the humanitarian community is obligated to protect, as well as jeopardizes the credibility of all humanitarian actors. Despite the significant progress made in instituting international commitments on PSEAH in the Syrian Response thus far, needs remain high, as evidenced by the 2022 household survey carried out jointly by HNAP/PSEAH. Key findings from the survey continue to show that affected populations remain highly reliant upon humanitarian assistance for survival. At the national level, 8 percent of households (HH) that received humanitarian assistance stated that they or members of their HH were asked for favours in exchange for humanitarian assistance or services (including requests for cash, labour and personal relationships, or SEA). Out of these, 38% of female-headed HH were asked for personal relationships in exchange of assistance or service compared to 1% of male-headed HH. SEAH in Syria continues to be underreported due to its sensitive nature and the number of actual violations is expected to be higher. Unfortunately, the majority (92%) of Syrian HHs who receive requests for favours in exchange for assistance did not report these incidents, largely due to concerns over confidentiality and fear of losing access to assistance. The programme is coordinated by IOM on behalf of the humanitarian community, under the responsibility of the UN Syria leadership (Regional Humanitarian Coordinator and Deputy Regional Humanitarian Coordinator). It will make available funding for the Senior PSEAH coordinator’s position and/or implementation of activities inside Syria.
