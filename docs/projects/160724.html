Project Summary


The proposed emergency food security and livelihoods support project is a dual sector project targeting Aweil North County of NBeG state and Cueibet County in Lakes state and will run for 12 months from January –December 2020.The main goal of the project will be to provide access to emergency food security and livelihoods support to vulnerable agro-pastoralists households by providing lifesaving emergency livelihood support kits. The proposed project will provide training to the vulnerable households to enhance fishing, crop and vegetable production mainly for consumption at the household level and sale of surplus at local markets in selected payams of Malual North, Jaac, Malual centre in Aweil North County and Abiriu, Mayath, Cuibet, Malou Pec payam in Cueibet County in Lakes state which will create extra income for these vulnerable households. These payams are chosen due to their suitability for fishing and vegetable production. SAADO will target 20,250 individuals in this proposed response with emergency fishing, crop and vegetable kits. The 2 locations have been chosen because of the needs prevalent in these counties according to the IPC August 2019 and the existence of active programming by SAADO in the areas. The emergency livelihood kits will be obtained from the FSL Cluster-recommended core pipeline to communities affected by the ongoing food crisis in NBeG and Lakesstates.
SAADO will put in place a mechanism for effective and participatory community vulnerability mapping in the selected payams and bomas and will use a comprehensive beneficiary selection criteria developed, discussed and agreed upon with the communities to include Men, Women, Boys and Girls. During community consultations at various levels, men, women boys and girls will be consulted to fully understand and appreciate the impact of the current crisis on men, women and children that would further inform response. “Do no harm” and AAP principles will be applied throughout the duration of the project, so that any possible negative implications of the project at community level are addressed before they occur through integrating a strong Communicating with Communities and Engagement (CCE) feedback mechanisms. The project will build upon and complement SAADO's long experience in implementing General food distribution(GFD) since 2014 and the 2.5 year DFID funded BRACE II projects in Aweil North County as well as the current 2nd standard allocation food security and livelihoods project supported by SSHF in Cueibet County in Lakes state. It will also complement the Lives Saving and livelihood protection and nutrition activities carried out by other FSL partners’ interventions in the counties especially WVI and PUI in Aweil North county and VSF-Germany in Cueibet County of Lakes state. The proposed activities are necessary and sufficient to achieve the stated cluster objectives for these interventions based on the relevance of each activity in increasing access to emergency food security and livelihoods support for the most vulnerable households in the targeted communities. The indicators selected are SMART and SAADO has in place a strong monitoring and evaluation system and mechanisms to measure these indicators and to ensure the intended results are achieved. The means of verification, such as monthly reports, distribution lists and regular field visits are strongly credible and are currently used by the organization in its MEAL processes across its field offices in the country.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan seasonal flooding snapshot 21 Oct 2019,unusually heavy seasonal flooding is affecting large areas of South Sudan, with estimates that between 600,000 and 800,000 people have been affected. As of late October, Aweil North county was among the counties most heavily affected by the floods which has impacted 80% of the 3,114 feddans of the DFID supported BRACE II project being implemented by SAADO in Aweil North County and 60% of the entire county.In addition,Pictorial Evaluation assessments(PET) already conducted in affected areas and in line with experience from previous floods in South Sudan, the degradation or loss of crops and other sources of livelihoods will cause longer-term humanitarian needs and reduce food security in the year 2020.This will further negatively impact the slight improvement in the food security situation in 2019 lean season according to the IPC August 2019 South Sudan report attributed to reduced insecurity after the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018, which has led to better access to livelihoods and markets. In the selected counties of Aweil North county the projected total population in crisis, emergency and humanitarian catastrophe is 40,000(stressed);56,000(crisis) and 32,000(emergency) which is 61.3% in Sept-Dec 2019 and will further increase to 62.4% in Jan-Mar 2020 in the hunger gap period if humanitarian response does not reach them in time according to the IPC August 2019.These results arise from a lack of major overall improvement in the insecurity situation,climatic shocks such as floods, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion and the continuing extreme staple food prices interacting with significant market dependence by rural populations according to FSNMS round 23,2019 report. In Cueibet County,acute food insecurity persists, driven by localized conflicts, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality. According to the FSL cluster mission report to Rumbek: 28th May – 3rd June 2019, 80% of the population of Lakes State practice subsistence crop farming with 69% of the community keeping livestock. The report states that the household food insecurity in Cueibet county reached new records mid 2019 with the coming lean period likely to see food insecurity levels rise even further according to the IPC Aug 2019 and indicates Cueibet County having 69,000 (Phase 3) & 35,000 (Phase 4) individuals at emergency level food insecurity with a cereal deficit of about >12% above the deficit estimated for 2019. This information is further corroborated by the CFSAM 2018 which revealed that the average yield per HH was at 270 kg/HH which is far below the national threshold of 800kg/HH.The cyclical conflict between Gok, Agar Pakaam, Duony,Tiap Tiap and Pager communities escalated in 2018 in Cueibet County resulting in major displacement of over 1,105 HH from the warring clans. It also resulted into loss of over 2,600 heads of livestock and disruption of the main cropping season activities in the county according to the FSL cluster mission report to Rumbek: 28th May – 3rd June 2019.Also,communities in Abaarkou, Abinajok and parts of Achol Malek, Ludo Cok bomas in Cueibet missed the cropping season due to displacement negatively impacting on food security, according to; https//fews.net/east-africa/south-sudan/Lakes.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

SAADO 's proposed emergency intervention will ensure that beneficiaries rights are legally recognized in all field sites.This will be done through having a robust Beneficiary Communication and Accountability Plan.This will include delivering initial orientation and ongoing information to communities on project activities, rationale behind beneficiary selection, and supporting the management and resolution of complaints throughout project planning and implementation. Established Project Management Committees will also be involved in supporting the implementation and management of project activities to ensure that these resources do not create a source of competition and dispute, the project will strengthen the capacity of community structures to develop and enforce context-appropriate systems for ensuring the equitable access and utilization of communal assets and resources. The project will also ensure communities are actively involved in beneficiary selection, will conduct face-to-face information,question and answer sessions, and establish a complaints system to receive and pre-emptively respond to issues with the potential to negatively impact community relationships.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

SAADO's proposed intervention will include perspectives of displacement affected people to ensure that they also benefit from the project.This will be done by ensuring that the 30% quota in selecting beneficiaries is adhered to and implemented in all field sites of Aweil North and Cueibet Counties.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

SAADO's proposed emergency intervention will link to the DFID funded Building Resilience through Asset Creation and Enhancement II(BRACE II)project which is a 5 year project in Aweil North county and will continue to support the communities in building their resilience to shocks such as floods and conflict/displacement.In Cuiebet county,SAADO's proposed intervention is expected to link to the community systems of food security and livelihoods sustenance.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

SAADO's proposed project is aligned with the national objectives as enshrined in the revitalized agreement on the resolution of conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS)chapter 4 for reconstruction and recovery of communities as well as the partnership for recovery and resilience(PFRR)in South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

SAADO’s proposed emergency intervention will ensure that the protection environment is safe and stable by mainstreaming protection concerns are put into consideration and by further ensuring that females have equal and equitable access to services and that targeted action to advance protection is based on a gender and age analysis. All activities and results (output and outcomes) will enhance protection considerations.The project embraces ADAPT & ACTC framework for protection and gender equality programming. For example our project (1) analyses the impact of the crises (violent conflict, shocks and economic) on girls, boys,women and men and what this means in terms of division of labour/tasks,workload and access to humanitarian assistance and protection (2) ensures that girls and boys,women and men participate equally and equitably in the design,implementation, monitoring and evaluation; and that girls, boys, women and men are involved in decision-making positions.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure the sustainability of the project SAADO will work with Project Management Committees (PMC’s) members and other key stakeholders and the host communities and will develop detailed plans relating to key tasks and persons responsible. The PMCs will own these plans, and be responsible for their implementation and adherence following project closure. Technical and soft skills training will be delivered through the project to ensure PMCs have the capacity. The training approaches employed by the project will also encourage sustainability by training community members as trainers. This will ensure that technical knowledge and skills remain in communities following project closure.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Mitigation 8:2 This proposed project addresses the issue of women and girls not being included in decision making as planned activities/outcomes are based directly on the differentiated needs of the various gender categories.Gender sensitivity including protection is factored in all activities from needs assessment, community sensitization, beneficiary selection, training, reporting and result monitoring. All project reports generated will be disaggregated by gender. The project design will also ensure more than 40% of beneficiaries are females. Targeting criteria for activities will be determined by analysis on gender roles and protection issues to ensure the Do No Harm principle are applied appropriately. Moreover,SAADO has a dedicated Gender and Protection Specialist who will be key at the project inception to mainstream protection. Mitigation 8:10.The project will ensure that beneficiary sites and training centres are safe spaces for both genders.During meetings, Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) and trainingsessions or any gatherings on this project, SAADO gender and protection staff will address the people we serve/beneficiaries on issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Whenever such issues are raised SAADO will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part ofstrengthening the referral pathways. Mitigation 8:15.Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings,verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons withspecial needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.
