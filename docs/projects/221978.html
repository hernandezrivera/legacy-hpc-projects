Project Summary

The proposed HRP project will cover 12 months from January 1, to December 31, 2025 with focus on addressing malnutrition among children under the age of 5 years, reduce WASH-related morbidity and mortality and GBV risks among vulnerable communities in Fangak and Pigi Canal of Jonglei and Leer, Koch and Rubkona of Unity State. The approach is through WASH integration with nutrition, health and gender based violence (GBV). The project will reach 21,500 vulnerable people (3785men, 7355 women, 5160boys and 5200girls) among IDPs, returnees, host community and people with special needs with access to safe water supply and improved sanitation and hygiene practices.
Strategy 1: Integration of WASH with nutrition and health to combat malnutrition and reduce water borne diseases respectively. The proposed intervention will integrate WASH with nutrition and health through construction/rehabilitation of water and sanitation facilities at community level and nutrition facilities. In all the project locations, water interventions will be complemented by hygiene education. Water Supply: SPEDP will drill and construct 5boreholes and rehabilitate 3mini water yard systems and 35 dysfunctional hand pumps and conduct water quality testing and surveillance of targeted water points. More so, SPEDP will conduct trainings/refresher trainings to water management committees and community based pump mechanics.
Sanitation: Construction of 10 compartment of improved toilet blocks in 10 nutrition/health facilities. Water and sanitation interventions in the targeted communities will be complemented with hygiene promotion on key hygiene messages targeting vulnerable groups at risk of water borne diseases and undernutrition; oversee production of messaging materials printed in local languages on posters, banners, posters and thus will contribute towards working against malnutrition and water borne diseases among the affected population. In case of emergencies, SPEDP will distribute standard kits to SAM children/caretakers and pregnant lactating women (PLW) paired with hygiene promotion (hand, food and domestic hygiene) and water household treatment and safety.
Strategy 2: Integration of WASH with GBV and Inclusion SPEDP takes into account women’s dignity and security during WASH service provision to reduce possible risks of GBV and fear of not using the facilities. Also protection of females against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse during access to WASH service will be closely monitored. During the project cycle, the project team will conduct focus group discussions to consider views and special needs of females, children and persons with disabilities are incorporated in project activities, which will improve services effectiveness, timeliness, usage of facilities and beneficiary participation. SPEDP will conduct separate discussions with women and girls for allocation of sites for of WASH facilities preferably done GBV female staff. We shall also set out a box for women and girls to give feedback and complaints. Conduct community meetings with target beneficiaries to ensure that designs meet their specific requirements, including women, adolescents, men, persons with disabilities, lactating women, pregnant women and the elderly; including safety audits and risk analysis for selecting locations. The proposed intervention will contribute to the improved well-being of the people affected by disasters or chronically exposed to lack of basic water and sanitation services in the proposed project locations hence reducing water and sanitation related morbidity and mortality and also mitigate WASH related GBV and disability inclusion. SPEDP has static presence in the proposed locations with ongoing health, nutrition, WASH and FSL projects.

1. Needs

South Sudan, since its independence in 2011, faces extreme challenges in meeting basic needs due to violent conflicts, political instability, and socioeconomic underdevelopment. Over 80% of the population lives in poverty, with a fragility index just below Somalia and Sudan. While the country has experienced relative calm at national level since the signing of the revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) and formation of the Transitional Government of National Unity in February 2020, there has been a spike in conflicts at sub-national level which have resulted in displacement of thousands of communities in many areas. An estimated 9 million people, including refugees in South Sudan, will experience critical needs in 2025.Despite a 2018 peace deal, conflicts over resources and inter-community clashes persist. More than half a million Sudanese refugees and returnees from South Sudan have registered in South Sudan since the start of the Sudan crisis last April 2023. Food security situation has deteriorated due to multiple shocks, including flooding, ongoing conflict, displacement, and a high cost of living. The livelihoods situation in South Sudan is extremely challenging, shaped by ongoing conflict, economic instability, and environmental factors. The persistent violence and political turmoil have displaced millions, disrupting agricultural activities and livestock management, which are the primary sources of livelihood for most of the population. As per the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) of September 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience Phase 3 or above. Of this total, 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4, and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees will likely experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. Humanitarian and development needs in Jonglei (Fangak and Pigi canal) and Unity (Leer, Koch and Rubkona) are driven by multiple hazards i.e. flooding, conflict, displacement and disease outbreaks including cholera outbreak in Renk of Upper Nile, is feared to spread across the Country and may further exacerbate vulnerabilities. The lack of access to safe water supply and improved sanitation and hygiene practices further affects child’s nutritional status in those areas. WASH also has a clear gender dimension as women and girls often tasked to fetch, which may further put them at risk of attacks and SGBV, which is also used as a weapon of war on women and girls.
