Project Summary

UNHCR’s strategic vision is to provide services following the public health approach through the integration of health nutrition services to foster access and a continuum of care for beneficiaries as per UNHCR’s public health strategy and nutrition sector strategy. The project will ensure implementation comprehensive nutrition programmes in 19 integrated nutrition centers across 14 camps and two stabilization centers in partnership with health and nutrition partners. UNHCR will implement comprehensive nutrition programmes following the CMAM approach for community outreach programmes and treatment of acute malnutrition in the Outpatient therapeutic feeding programme and targeted supplementary feeding programme, blanket supplementary feeding programmes, infant and young child feeding programmes and community engagement. UNHCR will lead the implementation of the annual nutrition surveys following the Standard Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) methodology and support other nutrition sector assessments.
Programmes to improve the nutrition situation among children under five years will be strengthened by the partners and more collaboration with other sectors health, WASH, food security and livelihoods and education to address the high levels of malnutrition. In terms of health addressing child hood diseases, sexual reproductive health services (ANC/PNC) immunization and early detection and treatment of anaemia will be strengthened. Collaboration with WASH will focus improving the hygiene and sanitation through messaging and community-based hygiene promotion with focus on households with malnourished children. New collaboration with education to promote community awareness on the causes and effects of malnutrition, general nutritional and dietary requirements, including balanced diets at the household level by engaging school-going children as agents for positive change.
Since 2022 UNHCR started the health and nutrition integration in UNHCR managed camps to foster more support and access to health care and nutrition services with to partners providing both health and nutrition and where there are nutrition partners referrals and screening of children and PLWs was initiated and is being monitored. In 2023 UNHCR will strengthen this programmes and where feasible expand to other camps.
The nutritional needs of older adults and other vulnerable categories, such as children above five years, medical cases and other specific needs, will be considered and supported per the sector guidelines. Continued capacity building of the nutrition staff will be strengthened to ensure implementation of the nutrition services as per the protocols to ensure good quality of care and overall outcomes and the overall nutrition well-being of the beneficiaries. In 2023 UNHCR and its partners will strengthen programme monitoring and make relevant adjustments to ensure the implementation of good quality programmes and accountability to the affected population. More focus on the implementation of nutrition-sensitive programmes, disability inclusion, and AGDM approach in all the nutrition centers m, community engagement and scaling up of the mother lead MUAC concept to ensure its sustainability will be done in collaboration with other relevant sectors.
UNHCR continues to support the GOB in strengthening its localization strategy through the engagement of two national NGOs and is working towards building its capacity to implement comprehensive nutrition programmes and increase their coverage in the future. The camps in Cox’s Bazar are regularly affected by the monsoon rains and cyclone threats and to ensure uninterrupted provision of life saving nutrition services, UNHCR will coordinate with the nutrition sector, and partner on emergency response plans, ensure adequate supplies and training of nutrition mobile teams and funding for partners to carry out emergency nutrition activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In terms of the nutrition needs of Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh, UNHCR and its partners conducted in 2021 the Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) in the Kutupalong and Nayapara registered camps to determine the health and nutrition situation and assessed the water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), food security and health indicators. The nutrition situation among the Rohingya refugee camps remains unstable due to the COVID-19 pandemic. As per the last nutrition survey carried out in 2021, the overall prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) was 13.7 per cent, representing a slight increase from 11.3 per cent in 2020. This depicts a serious state of malnutrition in the camps as per the WHO classifications of acute malnutrition (GAM 11-14%). The trends since the emergency period in 2017 had declined significantly and then increased in 2021; this trend is primarily attributed to changes in humanitarian assistance, especially with regard to food security and nutrition service delivery modalities in the refugee camps that were in place up to mid-2022. Chronic malnutrition rates remain above the critical WHO /UNICEF thresholds of >30 per cent, while anaemia prevalence remained high, comprising 50.5 per cent among children aged 6-59 months and >70 per cent among children aged 6–23 months standard (<40 per cent). Adoption of infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices is still a concern, with 69.8 per cent of children being initiated to breastfeeding at birth and 65.2 per cent exclusive breastfeeding rates in the newly established camps in Kutupalong and Teknaf. The causes of malnutrition remain the same as per the 2019 casual study due to poor maternal and childcare practices and vulnerability to diseases and infections, as well as inadequate food as 45 per cent of households are reported to have poor and borderline food consumption scores as per 2021 food security assessments. Programmes to improve the nutrition situation among children under five years will be strengthened by partners and through increased collaboration with other sectors to address the high levels of malnutrition. The nutritional needs of older adults and other vulnerable categories, such as children above five years, medical cases and persons with other specific needs, will be considered and supported per the sector guidelines. Continued capacity building of nutrition staff will be strengthened to ensure implementation of the nutrition services as per the protocols to ensure high quality of care and overall outcomes and the overall nutritional well-being of beneficiaries. In 2023, UNHCR and its partners will strengthen programme monitoring and make adjustments as necessary to ensure that the implementation of high-quality programmes and accountability to the affected population is maintained. Increased focus on the implementation of nutrition-sensitive programmes, disability inclusion and age, gender and diversity mainstreaming (AGDM) approach in all the nutrition centres through community engagement and scaling up of the mother-lead mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) concept to ensure its sustainability, completed in collaboration with other relevant sectors. UNHCR continues to support the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) in strengthening its localization strategy through the engagement of two national NGOs and is working towards building its capacity to implement comprehensive nutrition programmes. A total estimated number of 103,922 beneficiaries (58,686 children aged under five years, 30,340 adolescent girls and 14,896 pregnant and lactating women) will be supported in the UNHCR-funded nutrition programmes as per the sectors persons in needs analysis."An additional 2000 refugees will be supported in the transit centre in 2023.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The nutrition programmes planned will be implemented as per the outcome of the 2020 nutrition sector rationalization of nutrition programmes wherby one nutrition partner will implementing comprehensive programmes per camp. 3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection partners implementing UNHCR-funded programmes will ensure protection mainstreaming in all components to ensure the "Do No Harm" principle is achieved in service delivery to all the beneficiaries receiving support from the integrated nutrition centres. Assessments of the partner’s understanding of key protection principles and mainstreaming will be jointly done by the UNHCR protection and nutrition units. Training on PSEA, GBV, child protection and referral pathways between nutrition and protection in the camps will be carried out to ensure staff capacity to support the refugees to access services in a dignified manner. Additionally, other components identified as potential protection risks will be monitored and mitigation strategies identified. Regular monitoring of protection-related issues will be ensured throughout the project implementation, and necessary actions to prevent GBV experienced by refugees receiving assistance through nutrition programmes.

3.2 - Gender

In nutrition programming, gender is an important consideration in the project design phase based on the nutritional requirements of women, boys and girls as per the needs assessments completed through nutrition surveys. In terms of implementation, partners will ensure that the age-sensitive programmes are implemented according to the nutrition sector strategy into consideration of age-gender mainstreaming principles. To foster adequate gender balance, partners will ensure that they recruit and train gender-balanced nutrition teams at the nutrition centres and community nutrition volunteers to support all beneficiaries. As the adoption of appropriate feeding practices is largely affected by community members, nutrition education will be implemented targeting key influencers of all ages.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

UNHCR is committed to preventing child abuse and exploitation of the refugees in the nutrition programmes; all partner staff will be trained on child safeguarding principles in order to understand the standards and conduct expected from all staff, volunteers and contractors working in the nutrition centres. Messages to improve the prevention of any form of abuse among the beneficiaries of all ages, boys and girls aged under five years, adolescent girls and women of reproductive age will be included in health education sessions to increase awareness. Reporting any form of child abuse or behaviours violating thesafeguarding principles will be monitored by UNHCR and partners, and immediate actions to resolve the issues identified will be followed up.

3.4 - Diversity

Nutrition Partners implementing UNHCR-funded programmes will ensure respect for diversity among the staff and beneficiaries receiving services in the centres. This will be ensured through equal service provisions regardless of ethnic or religious groups and cultural beliefs, and special needs. In terms of community engagement, all the key influencers, religious leaders, community leaders, and women groups and adolescent girls will have an equal chance of participation.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AAP will be ensured throughout the project cycle, and participation of the refugees in the different stages of programming through consultations will be implemented. UNHCR will ensure that partners establish feedback and complaints mechanisms in all the centres with a clear plan to address any issues that arise in an integrated manner. This will also include the provision of different methods of communication with refugees engaged in awareness raising efforts with regard to the different programs, new programmes or any changes that may have an impact on their access to services and the overall nutrition status of the beneficiaries supported in the nutrition programmes. UNHCR will monitor the feedback and complaints mechanism put in place by the partner to ensure that they adhere to the acceptable UNHCR standards, including capacity enhancement of all INF focal points to keep the AAP mainstreaming as a continuous monitoring point.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

YES: UNHCR is committed to committed to reducing the negative impact of some elements in nutrition programmes on the environment through sensitization and training of nutrition staff and volunteers on climate change principles. UNHCR will emphasize the need for proper disposal of the nutrition packaging to reduce pollution and improper disposal in the camps, monitoring of the no use of polythene bags as per the RRRC directives will be ensured and all partners will be supported with adequate funds to procure jute bags or alternatives to carry or store nutrition supplies.
