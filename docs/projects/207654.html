Project Summary

The situation in the North West and South West Regions remains a call for concern, characterized by violence, leading to civilians being killed, kidnapping for ransom, arbitrary arrest, illegal detention,forceful disappearance and destruction of properties. An estimated 1,033,000 school aged children have been affected by the crisis. They were denied the opportunities to learn and develop themselves, failing in achieving sustainable development goal 4: ensure inclusive and equitable, quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all. The current situation has left more than 955,700 children out of school. 375,000 persons are internally displaced (children and adult: boys, girls, women and men) within these two regions. According to Education Cluster Strategy, as of September 2023, 54% of schools in the NW/SW are not operational most of which are in the remote areas. This has caused forceful displacement of children and high rate of influx into urban areas within the south west region or out to other regions like Litoral and West Region. These IDPs find it difficult to access formal schools due to poverty making it difficult to purchase learning materials for their children Coupled with social exclusion.
The separatist fighters have used education as tool to fight the state of Cameroon. The education sector has faced great challenges in their operations in the crisis affected areas. The estimated school aged children have been affected by the crisis. Data from the Education Cluster Strategy reveals that 54% of schools in the NW/SW are still not operational most of which are in the rural/remote areas in these region. There has been a significant increase in the number of operational schools within these regions. Nonetheless, the remote population still suffers greatly to access formal education as the NSAGs have denied reopening of school forcing families to relocate to urban towns where their children can have access to education. Report from OCHA also reveals that 225000 children are still displaced in the NWSW, Litoral and West.
These IDPs find it difficult to access formal schools in their towns of refuge due to poverty making it difficult to purchase learning materials for their children Coupled with social exclusion. More to this, the Multi Sectorial Need Assessment carried out in early 2023, reveals that access to education, drinking water, food and health remain the priority needs of these IDPs. As a result of the ongoing crises, these children have limited access to quality education. Thus this project aims to combat the educational barriers faced by internally displaced children aged 5-16 in Buea, Limbe 1 and Tiko through the provision of quality learning materials.
By securing funding for this project, we will empower 1000 internally displaced children in Fako division with the necessary learning materials. This project will meet the educational needs of 750 Primary School Children and 250 Secondary Students with equal educational opportunities, fostering their overall development and enabling them to overcome the challenges posed by crises.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The 2023 education need assessment conducted by the Islamic Private Education Secretariat for the South West Region showed that more than 5000 IDPs of school going age were unable to attend formal education within Buea, Limbe1 and Tiko due to lack of finances. This is further highlighted in OCHA's situation report covering January to June 2023 wherein, 375000 persons are displaced within North West and South West Region of Cameroon. 805,000 was targeted and just 198,000 has been reached. More to this, the Education Cluster Strategy indicates that 225026 school age children are internally displaced. It is for this reason that the Islamic Private Education Secretariat for South West Region deems it necessary to provide learning materials to 1000 displaced children in order to; 1. Ensuring Access to Education to 1000 IDPs in Buea, Tiko and Limbe: Internally displaced children face immense challenges in accessing education due to the disruptions caused by crises. By providing essential learning materials, we aim to enable these children to continue their education despite their displacement. Access to quality education is crucial for their cognitive, emotional, and social development, giving them a sense of normalcy and hope for the future.2. Mitigating Learning Loss: By equipping IDP children in Fako with essential learning materials such as textbooks, notebooks, and pens and hygiene will help to mitigate learning loss. These materials will enable them to engage in self-study, practice academic skills, and reinforce their learning.3. Promoting Psychological Well-being: The provision of learning materials not only facilitates academic growth but also supports the overall well-being of internally displaced children. Education offers a sense of routine, structure, and purpose, which are particularly vital in times of crisis. Having their own materials provides a sense of ownership and dignity, boosting their self-confidence and motivation to learn.4. Fostering Long-term Resilience: Education plays a critical role in breaking the cycle of poverty and displacement. By empowering IDP children with the necessary learning materials will contribute to their long-term resilience and increase their opportunities for a brighter future. Education equips them with essential skills, knowledge, and resources to rebuild their lives and positively contribute to their communities.Overall, by providing essential learning materials to internally displaced children, we aim to uphold their right to education, support their well-being, and foster their resilience in the face of adversity. This project has the potential to make a profound and lasting impact on the lives of these vulnerable children.
