Project Summary

The project is designed to provide formal and non-formal education to 9388 IDP out of school(OOSC) girls and boys aged 6-18 in 19 camp schools in classrooms in tents and in buildings built with bricks and metal roofs in the camps themselves.The camps are located in rural Idleb, Harim district. The action will focus on access and quality .Each camp school will have: 317 from both genders selected by a fair criteria:teachers, 19 head teachers, 19 cleaners and 19 guards who will be recruited based on hiring criteria. Teachers will be paid monthly incentives according to days worked as tracked by timesheets. Home-based education will be as a plan B if the situation got worse,where teachers will deliver lessons to children in groups in safe camps if ,for instance,if one of the camps was shelled or evacuated due to security issues.As a mitigation measure, home-based education can contribute to resuming the education process until the situation gets better. A plan C such as relocations will be applied in case there was any security risk. To better ensure effective teaching, teachers will receive training in child-centered topics. A pre- and post-training test will be done. ASER exercise will be conducted at the beginning of the project as a baseline followed by an end line for measuring the progress and improvement of students; those who score 0-1 in ASER will have extra remedial sessions (3 literacy and 1 numeracy sessions) in addition to the formal classes and these sessions (BLN) will be included in the school timetable. Teachers will receive training in child-centered topics for delivering quality lessons and CP related topics. The project will develop safety plans, scheduling drills for students and teachers, which require safety equipment. Hygienic materials will be distributed in addition to heating fuel and stoves in cold months. For formal education, the project will use the Syrian Interim Government (SIG) curriculum. For non-formal education, a BLN curriculum developed by SCI and IRC will be used. In the summer, the schools focus on core subjects (mathematics, Arabic and English), and on recreational activities. Parents will be involved in the parent-teacher associations to have monthly meetings for discussing school-related topics. The Back to Learning campaigns will target the 19 camps for key messages on the importance of learning and the necessity of cooperation between the community and school for encouraging parents to enrol their children. There will be classroom Observation for overseeing the teachers and provide them with a feedback after each visit by supervisors. Teachers will form Teachers Learning circles in each school for exchanging the experiences and teaching techniques. To improve the education inputs, girls and boys will receive education materials, and teachers will have education kits too. In cold months, the target camps will be provided with winterization. For improving the education environment for FE and NFE , there will be repairs for 257 classrooms and 38 WASH facilities in the 19 target camps; the repairs will be fixing the benches,boards,faucets for toilets..etc

4 - Needs

As per HNO, 2019 ‘’more than one-third of Syria’s children – out of school.A further 1.3 m children are at risk of dropout’’. ''5.8 m school-aged children and 120,000 teachers need education assistance in Syria. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks as child marriage and the worst forms of child labor.The escalation of violence led to increasing PSS trauma, their families and personnel. Syria Relief conducted a needs assessment in Idleb governorate prioritizing 19 camps in Idleb which have IDP out of school children who fled recently from southern Idleb, Hama, rural Aleppo or when the conflict erupted from all over Syria.The 19 camps have 5069 girls, 4319 boys aged 6-15 (including 37 children with disabilities).Children are out of school for 3 months at minimum.The assessment revealed that there is an urgent need to support the IDP camps aged 6-15 by providing formal and non-formal education as well PSS activities as well as emergency food kits in which SR will cover as much of those emergency needs from other donors.The camps are in big need of education items, incentives for 180 male and 175 female education personnel who are currently working as volunteers, there is also needs for teacher training on non-formal education and education methods.Existing classrooms and WASH facilities are exhausted and are been used by the IDP communities and they need light repairs as some of the classrooms are physical buildings and a few are tents located in the camps. The assessment also revealed that there is a need for raising awareness to reconnect school age children who are out of school to get back to school. Such essential efforts will encourage children to be back to learn. Via this response, SR will minimize risk of dropout o, link kids to education,enhance sustainability and reduce early marriage and child labor.Children are traumatized as many faced displacements and saw bad scenes.The19 assessed camps received no education support so far.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project focuses on the following activities related to access and quality:*Light repairs for the existing classrooms and WASH facilities, *supplying the camps with school furniture : heater, desks and board, *provide safety items as 1st aid kits and extinguishers,* provide disabilities with rails and ramps, *deliver non-formal sessions for girls and boys whose ASER score are 0-1 using BLN, *Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns and convey key awareness-raising sessions and messages, *Hold meeting with stakeholders and local actors, *enrol children without discrimination, *conducting ASER test to measure the learning outcomes, *provide children with remedial classes during Summer School, *provide students with learning materials ,*provide teachers with teaching kits, *provide referral pathway services to affected girls and boys, *conduct child-centered and CP related trainings to teachers,* pay monthly stipends to education personnel, *provide girls an boys with recreational materials, *distribute fuel and heaters in cold months, *pay running cost for schools, *establish parent teachers associations in each school,* establish teacher learning circles in each schools, *printing formal SIG textbooks , BLN for children,* conduct PSS semi-structured activities for the children in the camps,*conduct MEAL activities related to the project,such as monitoring the distributions,trainings,complaints(accountability),assessmnets

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated armed parties; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access, and lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure during and directly after service provision.Mitigation 1:Training for teachers on evacuation, drills, emergency preparedness, and preparation of a contingency plan. ,Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed-site service provision. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, and psychological issues). Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the communityRisk 2: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Mitigation 2: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for, such as training to staff and service providers on Code of Conduct and PSEARisk 3: Child abuse:verbal, emotional, and physical Mitigation: All staff sign on CoC and CSG policy and receive awareness sessionsRisk 4:disabled children face a difficulty to access the schoolMitigation:ramps and railsRisk5:beneficiaries not satisfied with the servicesMitigation:complaints received,accountability mecahanism in place
