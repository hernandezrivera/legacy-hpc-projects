Project Summary

This project will emergency food assistance to the most vulnerable individuals through monthly food e-vouchers and help develop self-reliance for those who are ready to transition out of food assistance by restoring their livelihood activities.//
As with previous phases, CRS will conduct baseline surveys at start-up, monthly on-site monitoring, and monthly post-distribution monitoring during implementation, as well as a final evaluation. All participating vendors and staff will receive training on CRS' Protection Policy and must sign a Code of Conduct regarding protection and humanitarian principles. Protection concerns are addressed in the project design such as through integration of biometric verification, providing gender-sensitivity, protection mainstreaming and SGBV training to all project and partner staff, referrals for identified protection issues to the protection WG. CRS' protection mainstreaming approach considers beneficiary safety, dignity and meaningful access to assistance supported by staff behavior, feedback mechanisms, communication and community involvement. To ensure transparency in project activities, CRS maintains rigorous feedback and complaint mechanisms including a toll-free hotline, suggestion boxes or in-person feedback during activity implementation and are channeled to the appropriate departments and addressed. Complaints are categorized as sensitive or non-sensitive in nature. Non-sensitive complaints are resolved usually within 72 hours but the timeframe varies depending on the nature of complaint. Sensitive complaints are given immediate attention and are followed up until they are resolved. In addition, regular PDM and OSM provide additional opportunities for participants to voice concerns, complaints and address the adequacy of the assistance. CRS also conducts spot checks with vendors and through "mystery shoppers" throughout implementation to ensure compliance. //
CRS is an active member of the Food Security Sector in Borno and Yobe and coordinates closely with key actors, such as the World Food Program (WFP) and Action Against Hunger (AAH). For its 2018 emergency food assistance in Gujba, CRS took over all of WFP’s caseload of 9,795 individuals in Buniyadi ward and passed on its caseload of 1,673 individuals in Gujba ward to WFP, such that each agency became the only actor in the respective wards to avoid duplication. Similarly, in Gulani, CRS incorporated all of WFP’s caseload of 10,294 individuals in Bularafa ward, transitioned its caseload of 1,430 individuals in Gulani and Bara wards to WFP, and became the sole actor in nine wards. In Borno, CRS remains the only actor delivering food assistance to settlements along the road between Magumeri and Maiduguri (Magumeri LGA), Gubio 1 & 2 wards (Gubio LGA), Benisheikh ward (Kaga LGA), and Muna and Simari (Jere LGA). CRS will continue hosting the Cash Working Group (CWG) Technical Co-chair to support strategic level coordination and the operationalization of cash transfer programming in the northeast. At the LGA-level, CRS will help reinitiate Local Coordination Group (LCG) meetings in Gulani and Gubio and continue to participate in LCGs active in Kaga, Jere, Magumeri, and Gujba, as part of the Grand Bargain’s commitment to improve coordination among humanitarian actors. LCGs will help avoid food assistance duplication and ensure that SAM cases are referred to nutrition actors providing treatment.

Needs

Northeast Nigeria has been severely affected by insurgency led by non-state armed groups since 2009. As clashes between Nigerian security forces and armed opposition groups (AOGs) persist into its tenth year in 2019, over 1.9 million individuals remain displaced across six states. General elections scheduled for February 2019 and events leading up to them add a high degree of uncertainty to the overall situation and may trigger new displacement. // Between December 2017 and August 2018, the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the northeast increased by 13%, after a year of gradual decrease in 2017, an indication of a highly volatile security context. Borno, which currently hosts over 1.4 million IDPs, 75% of the total IDP number in the northeast, witnessed an increase of almost 10%; while Yobe experienced a 31% increase, bringing IDP numbers in Yobe to 137,588. An average of 4,500 individuals arrive on a weekly basis, many fleeing to Local Government Area (LGA) headquarters from newly liberated areas. Nonetheless, the fluid nature of the crisis means that population movement is not uniform across different LGAs. For example, Jere and Maiduguri witnessed the largest decrease in IDP numbers in Borno in July 2018. At the same time, pockets of relative stability have encouraged households (HHs) to return to their places of origin. In August 2018, Borno registered 655,728 returnees and Yobe registered 165,771, an increase of 20% and 72% from December 2017 respectively. This trend coincides with CRS’ observations in Gujba and Gulani in Yobe. // Among those displaced, 73% indicated that food was their principal unmet need. UNOCHA estimates that 3.7 million people are still in need of in-kind or cash-based food assistance as of August 2018. The latest FEWSNET Nigeria Food Security Outlook projects the following outcomes until January 2019 for areas where CRS proposes or is currently providing assistance: IPC 3 for Gujba and Gulani, IPC 4 among the IDP population for Jere, Gubio, Magumeri, and Kaga, and IPC 4 for Monguno. The March 2018 Cadre Harmonisé projected that 658,859 individuals in the above mentioned LGAs were in IPC 3 & 4 in August 2018. Compared to previous rounds of the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Quarterly Survey, the 5th round (April 2018) indicates an increased prevalence of malnutrition in Central Borno at a Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) rate of 12.5% and a Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate of 2.4%, the highest in the state. In Southern Yobe, MAM rate is 9% and SAM rate is 1.9%. Despite improved market supply, declining inflation, and growing stability of the Nigerian Naira, affected HHs remain vulnerable as they face depleted food stocks and reduced purchasing power. // In Borno, CRS remains the only actor delivering food assistance to settlements along the road between Magumeri and Maiduguri (Magumeri LGA), Gubio 1 & 2 wards (Gubio LGA), Benisheikh ward (Kaga LGA), and Muna and Simari (Jere LGA). In Yobe, CRS is the only actor providing food assistance in Buniyadi ward of Gujba and in nine wards within Gulani LGA.

Activities

CRS is currently providing emergency food assistance to a total of 124,936 individuals (21,076 HHs) in Borno (Jere, Kaga, Gubio, and Magumeri) and Yobe (Gujba and Gulani). As the conflict evolves, CRS recognizes that the needs have changed across the northeast depending on factors such as the level of security, population movement, access to land and markets, livelihood opportunities, and assistance gaps in each LGA. Certain LGAs may now require more recovery-type assistance, while others still face acute levels of food insecurity. However, targeting decisions need to be made through a systematic process that enables CRS and its partners to ensure that emergency food assistance is delivered to the most vulnerable. As such, CRS will conduct a re-targeting assessment in 2018 Q4 of each LGA where CRS is currently providing assistance based on criteria defined in its newly developed Northeast Nigeria Emergency Response and Recovery Strategy. Communities no longer presenting acute needs will be transitioned out of emergency food assistance prior to the end of Phase 4. // Within “Emergency Phase” LGAs, the most vulnerable individuals (from the current CRS caseload will continue receiving monthly full food rations. Current assessment data indicate that these individuals will likely be IDPs as opposed to returnees and hosts, but CRS will conduct HH-level verification using tools developed based on lessons learned from the previous phases. // CRS will continue providing emergency food assistance through cash-based e-vouchers valued at 3,677.45 NGN ($11.14 ) per individual, equivalent to the Phase 4 voucher value and defined using the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) value set by the Abuja Cash Working Group (CWG) in April 2018. The vouchers cover 70% of the WFP minimum food basket, consistent with the November 2017 Food Security Sector Harmonization Guide’s recommendation. To date, cash-based e-vouchers have been the most rapid and appropriate means of providing food assistance while supporting local markets at all locations. The most vulnerable individuals will be registered to receive 11 monthly full rations, those receiving livelihood support will receive 5 half rations during the leans season.// To improve the food and nutrition outcomes of the program, CRS community mobilizers will conduct nutrition screening through MUAC measurements of all children under five to identify cases of malnutrition in all targeted LGAs during house-to-house visits. CRS will cover transport fees for the referral of children identified with SAM. Building on the successes of CRS’ pilot in Benisheikh, where 65% of MAM cases recovered with four weeks of Tom Brown supplementary feeding, CRS will scale up the program in LGAs targeted under Phase 5. Children identified with MAM will receive a maximum of 12 weeks of supplementary feeding. CRS will work with community mobilizers and “lead mothers” to prepare the Tom Brown feeding supplement by providing them with locally procured millet, guinea corn, yellow corn, soybeans, and ground nut, as well as a small cash payment to cover purchase of cooking fuel, milling, and transportation to and from the local mill on a weekly basis. Community mobilizers will carry out follow up visits on all SAM and MAM cases, and will provide training in Infant and Young Children Feeding (IYCF) during weekly group sessions. // Based on CRS’ experience in income-generation support through its Feed the Future program, CRS will pilot a livelihood restoration program to help 500 urban and peri-urban HHs from its current caseload resume productive livelihood activities and transition away from emergency food assistance. HHs selected based on their interests, experience, and vulnerability levels will receive financial literacy, business, and vocational trainings. CRS will conduct an in-depth market study at the beginning of Phase 5 to help beneficiary HHs develop market-appropriate small HH-based enterprises.
