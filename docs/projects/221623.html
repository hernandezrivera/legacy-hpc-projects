Project Summary

The project aims to enhance the health, dignity, and overall well-being of 5.01 million people in South Sudan through the provision of sustainable water and sanitation infrastructure and the promotion of hygiene practices. Its approach consists of three main components: improving access to safe water for vulnerable communities, reducing WASH-related diseases through hygiene education, and integrating nutrition and health initiatives to create synergies. By implementing these actions, the project seeks to improve health outcomes while fostering sustainable development in South Sudan.
ForAfrika’s contribution to the WASH cluster, aligned with the humanitarian response plan, will support key WASH strategic objectives and approaches ranging from CO1 to CO4 namely;
1. Contribution to the reduction of morbidity and mortality focusing on malnutrition through the WASH and Nutrition-integrated programming in targeted counties.
2. Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties
3. WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation.
4. Community member’s exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructures.
The project targets 25,000 people in Northern Bahr El Ghazal (9154 in Aweil East and 1,493 in Aweil West ), 9,777 in Greater Pibor Administrative Area (Pibor) and 4,577 in Warrap State (Gorgial West). Activities will include: the construction of boreholes, deep wells, hand pumps, solar water systems, and water quality monitoring. The project will also focus on building gender-segregated, disability-friendly sanitation facilities and providing hygiene kits. Community behavior change will be encouraged through Hygiene promotion and behavior communication education programs on key hygiene messaging covering handwashing, safe drinking water, waste management, safe excreta management and vector control for children and adults. Innovative water and sanitation technologies will be introduced to improve access to clean water, reduce waterborne diseases, and promote healthier practices.
To ensure sustainability and community ownership, training will be provided to local residents on the operation and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities. Community leadership will be encouraged, particularly by involving women in decision-making processes and establishing women-led water user committees. ForAfrika will also implement a grievance and feedback system, including phone lines and suggestion books, to ensure accountability to the affected populations. Additionally, the project will integrate WASH with health, nutrition, education, food security, and livelihoods to create a comprehensive response for community resilience and well-being.

1. Needs

Access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services remains severely constrained in hospitals, schools, internally displaced persons (IDP) camps, transit camps, and communities across South Sudan. The project aims to support 25,000 people, including 9200 in Aweil East, 1493 in Aweil West (Northern Bahr El Ghazal), 9,777 in Pibor (Greater Pibor Administrative Area), and 4,577 in Gorgial West (Warrap State). High levels of open defecation, unimproved sanitation, poor hygiene practices, and inadequate access to safe water pose significant risks to public health. A staggering 59% of the population lacks access to safe drinking water, while 14% of households lack essential WASH non-food items like buckets and soap (HNO 2024). This dire situation exacerbates morbidity and mortality rates, particularly among children under five, and contributes to high levels of malnutrition and waterborne diseases. In education, over 45% of schools lack latrines, and those that exist often lack safe drinking water and handwashing facilities. This has profound implications for children’s physical and cognitive development and contributes to school dropouts. Gender-segregated and disability-friendly WASH facilities are rare, with over 60% of latrines being inaccessible to students with disabilities (Education Needs Assessment, 2023). During the dry season, beneficiaries often walk over 15 kilometers to access water, further exacerbating vulnerabilities. Recent refugee arrivals and ongoing displacement intensify these challenges. As of November 16, 2024, South Sudan hosts 868,688 refugees, including 185,123 from Sudan. Assessments in refugee settlements highlight critical gaps in healthcare, food, water, and sanitation. Host communities are strained by the daily arrival of new individuals, underscoring the need for targeted interventions (UNHCR 2024). Flooding in areas such as Aweil East and South has destroyed critical WASH infrastructure, submerging 63 boreholes and 56 latrines, leaving thousands, including persons with disabilities, without safe water or sanitation. Stagnant floodwaters have increased the prevalence of malaria, respiratory infections, and other vector-borne diseases. These risks are compounded by the national open defecation rate, which ranges from 80–90%. Acute malnutrition is another major concern. Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, Jonglei, and Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) contribute significantly to the national burden of acute malnutrition, driven by poor dietary diversity, high morbidity, and sub-optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices. For example, Jonglei and GPAA have some of the highest malnutrition rates, with 10 out of 11 counties classified as IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) Phase 4 (Critical). Poor WASH practices, including low access to improved sanitation (<20%) and high rates of open defecation (>60%), compound these issues. Food insecurity is projected to affect over 7.69 million people (57.2% of the population), further increasing malnutrition rates. Health services remain inaccessible due to inflation and low economic power, while disease outbreaks, such as diarrhea, malaria, and acute respiratory infections, are expected to persist in flood-prone areas. ForAfrika will address these challenges, through its multi-sectoral interventions in WASH, health, nutrition, food security and livelihood which are urgently needed to mitigate vulnerabilities, reduce disease prevalence, and improve overall community resilience.

2. Project activities

The project employs an integrated approach to address critical WASH needs, with innovative solutions, sectoral collaboration, and community engagement to achieve sustainable results. To improved access to water, the project will drill 11 solar-powered boreholes and install 3 hand pumps across targeted locations, benefiting 15 new fetching points with flood-resilient designs. The aim is to provide safe drinking water for vulnerable populations while ensuring sustainability through solar-powered systems using gravity flow technology/technique. In reducing the effect of drought, water ponds and wells for irrigation purposes will be established, integrated with smart solar systems to pump water to farmlands. This innovation promotes agricultural productivity and food security while reducing dependency on fossil fuels.To ensure water quality and safety, the project will carry out monitoring of free residual chlorine (FRC) and faecal coliform bacteria, maintaining FRC levels within 0.2–0.5 mg/l and eliminating faecal coliform contamination. Training and equipping Water Management Committees (WMCs) and community pump mechanics (CPMs) with the tools and knowledge needed for operation and maintenance will enhance sustainability. The community members will be employed during drilling infrastructure activities, empowering families with income opportunities.Poor Sanitation will be addressed through Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) implementation in target areas while adopting safe hygiene practices. Further, the project will construct 29 gender-segregated and disability-inclusive latrines, and 25 bathing shelters in transit and reception centers. These sanitation facilities will feature flood-resilient designs, ensuring durability in disaster-prone areas.A strong focus on hygiene promotion will entail distribution of WASH kits (including soap, menstrual hygiene kits for refuges, returnees, SAM children and caregivers) and capacity building. Governance structures, such as hygiene clubs in schools and health centers, will strengthen hygiene practices in these facilities. Hygiene-related NFIs and training will integrate with other sectors, including food security, health, nutrition, and education, to mainstream GBV protection, social cohesion, and peacebuilding.The project prioritizes prepositioning for cholera outbreaks in high-risk areas. This includes the installation of handwashing facilities in public spaces, blanket disinfection of communal areas, chlorination and residual chlorination of water sources, and the distribution of water treatment products to vulnerable households. Training for Rapid Response Teams (RRTs) on infection prevention and control (IPC) in WASH will further enhance the response capacity. New refugee arrivals will be supported through targeted WASH interventions in transit camps and host communities through emergency water supply, sanitation facilities, and hygiene kit distribution to ensure immediate relief and protection.Innovative approaches include leveraging solar-powered water systems for both drinking water and irrigation, enabling efficient water delivery for agricultural activities, thereby supporting food security. Flood-resilient designs for latrines, handwashing stations, and other WASH infrastructure ensure durability and functionality during disasters. Community accountability mechanisms, such as suggestion boxes, help desks, and mobile feedback systems, will ensure affected populations are informed, engaged, and able to voice their concerns during project implementation. These sector integrations will increase access to safe water, improved sanitation and hygiene, and strengthened resilience of communities and infrastructure to climate-related and humanitarian crises.
