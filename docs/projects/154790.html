Project Summary

The project aims to support IDPs, returnees and vulnerable individuals to submit compensation claims to their respective compensation committee in relation to their damaged or destroyed properties during the war caused by terrorist attacks, military errors and other actions when areas were liberated from Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL). The project will also support IDPs with legal assistance during the process of submission of their compensation claims. IDPs whose properties were illegally occupied by other individuals without the owner’s permission, i.e. secondary occupations, will also be supported to regain their original property rights. Additionally, UN-Habitat will work closely with IDPs and returnees on advocacy for Housing, Land and Property (HLP) related issues, ensuring that women’s access to their rights is also prioritized.

Needs

Since 2014, widespread destruction of civilian properties occurred during military operations to liberate the country from ISIL occupation, thus preventing many IDPs from returning to their areas of origin. According to a recent UN-Habitat and Ministry of Planning assessment, 60,000 houses were destroyed during this period* and according to the World Bank there are 138,051 houses damaged or destroyed**. Anbar Governorate accounts for the second largest share of recorded number of damaged houses, compared to districts in other affected governorates. There is an important need to support returnees and IDPs with legal assistance related to their HLP rights in Anbar Governorate in connection with the submission of compensation claims. According to the Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA VI) conducted by REACH in August 2018, over 36 percent of IDPs do not intend to return to their home due to HLP-related factors, including damaged or destroyed houses, lack of HLP documents, and or the fact that their homes may be currently occupied by other families without the owner’s consent. Recent MCNA findings indicate that 8 percent of out-of-camp IDP households, 10 per cent of camp-based IDP households, 8 percent of returnee households and 6 percent remainee households reported missing documentation. Among IDPs surveyed across Iraq, 46 percent of out-of-camp IDP households and 33 percent of in-camp IDP households cited HLP issues as a reason for not returning to their areas of origin. Based on the Law on Compensation (Law 20 of 2009 and Law 57 of 2015, First Amendment ), displaced claimant households are required to complete many procedures in order to successfully submit their compensation claims to the Compensation Sub-Committee. Compensation claim procedures tend to be highly complex and bureaucratic, complicated by a lack of administrative instructions and unclear legal procedures. As a consequence, many IDPs may be unable or unwilling to pursue their compensation claims through established procedures. Additionally, based on qualitative assessments conducted by HLP Sub-Cluster partners in July 2018+, 57 percent of people who were assessed reported that they were unaware of the procedures and guidelines related to compensation. In Anbar Governorate, there are insufficient organizations offering HLP services compared to the volume of HLP needs shown by the sheer number of claimants. There is an urgent need to provide HLP legal assistance to all citizens whose houses have been damaged or destroyed. Secondary occupations are a major concern for IDPs and returnees. Many IDPs are unable to return home due to illegal occupation of their properties by other IDPs or individuals. This is a serious concern for them as it may trigger secondary displacement and forced evictions for those families currently occupying these properties. UN-Habitat will provide IDPs legal counseling through the Alternative Dispute Resolution method, specifically with mediation services to resolve secondary occupation cases. This mediation process is a new, innovative approach aimed at resolving such disputes. UN-Habitat will address such vulnerable peoples’ needs by providing them legal assistance, restitution support and mediation services. UN-Habitat will address complex cases which in principle still threaten the security of IDPs, vulnerable people, female-headed households, young adults and people with disabilities, as well as all of these groups’ safety and dignified return. It is imperative that UN-Habitat promotes and advocates for HLP rights across Iraq. Many IDPs and returnee families are not in possession of their substantive HLP documents, nor do they possess the knowledge of how to obtain them. Additionally, many IDPs or returnees never transferred or registered property titles in their own name after inheriting properties from late relatives. Related challenges are compounded for women in Iraqi society. Many female-headed households or widowed women do not enjoy their HLP rights or are often forced to relinquish their HLP rights against their will. * According to an assessment of January 2018, conducted by UN-Habitat and Ministry of Planning, based on satellite imagery, approximately 60,000 houses were destroyed during the armed conflict.** According to the Government of Iraq’s Damage and Needs Assessment of Affected Governorates, out of 138,051 houses that have been damaged or destroyed. Reconstruction and Investment, Damage and Needs Assessment of Affected Governorates, Part 2, January 2018.+ HLP Qualitative assessments conducted by HLP partners in 2018 and HLP Monthly Dashboard shared by HLP Sub-cluster on July 2018.

Activities

First Component: Legal Assistance1) Provide HLP legal assistance to IDPs and returnees to obtain missing, damaged/destroyed ownership documentation/title deeds; (2) Provide legal assistance to beneficiaries to pursue claim submissions to the Compensation Committee related to the process of claims submission; A total of 1,250 beneficiaries, of which 400 will be women, will be served by these activities. Second Component: Mediation Services(1) Conduct community consultations on reducing the risk of violence and preventing secondary occupations related to property dispute processes;(2) Facilitate Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR), negotiation and mediation activities to resolve secondary occupations; A total of 350 beneficiaries will be targeted through these activities. Female-headed households, vulnerable individuals and marginalized groups will be prioritized. A minimum of 25 per cent of the total number of beneficiaries will be women. Third Component: Advocacy(1) Disseminate information materials such as leaflets and brochures and conduct awareness-raising sessions targeting marginalized populations including women, youth, victims of violence as well as ethnic and religious minorities to inform them of their HLP rights;(2) Identify influential community members to be trained and serve as advocates for HLP rights in community meetings and workshops, including separate sessions for women; 1,650 beneficiaries will be targeted for this activity, of which 750 individuals will be women.

Protection Risk Analysis

The national compensation scheme is a legal remedy through which beneficiaries are entitled to compensation for their houses which were damaged or destroyed during the most recent conflict in Iraq. Failure to exercise this right would directly affect their basic rights and livelihoods, since without such compensation, IDPs could encounter extreme difficulty re-establishing their lives. Additionally, should affected households not pursue their right to compensation they may also be denied a dignified and safe return to their area of origin. A lack of knowledge about the compensation scheme, and its mechanisms, is a major concern as many beneficiaries may be unable to submit the relevant documentation in accordance with the required procedures and within the appropriate timeframe. As has been mentioned previously, complex, bureaucratic and lengthy procedures also challenge beneficiaries’ capacity and confidence to submit their claims to compensation committees. The appropriate type and package of legal advice from UN-Habitat and its implementing partners can effectively address this issue. Missing or destroyed HLP documentation is also an issue that challenges owners’ rights to submit their claims for compensation. Many IDPs and returnees will be unable to collect and prepare the required documentation to submit their claims. Additionally, time and physical distances between IDPs’ current residence and the location of compensation committees often dissuade them to pursue their claims. Additionally, prohibitive legal assistance expenses as well as the related high costs of preparing HLP documentation challenges affected households’ ability to launch and pursue their claims through their local compensation committee. A failure to plan and apply the appropriate professional mediation approach may also expose IDPs to potential conflict with the community. Secondary occupations constitute an additional threat that has the potential to prevent or delay households’ ability to re-assume practical possession and obtain full legal title to their original property. A failure to do so could lead to potential conflict with the illegal occupants of the property in question and or within the wider community of origin. At a community level where large numbers of secondary occupations are common, the critical mass of these occupations could severely delay, or directly prevent, IDPs from rightfully returning to their houses. Women and female-headed households in Iraq are regularly forced to relinquish or abandon efforts to pursue their HLP rights. Widowed women are often pressured to transfer the ownership title of their dwelling to another relative or community member against their will. They face discriminatory practices, and in some cases, are unaware of their basic HLP rights. A failure to consciously and proactively include women throughout the HLP process, would be a violation their basic human rights and a denial of their HLP rights. This could also expose them to a risk of discrimination, abuse and or secondary displacement.

Mitigation Measures

UN-Habitat conducted a pilot intervention on HLP in 2017-2018 in Nineveh Governorate to support highly vulnerable families in restoring the occupancy of their property or their ownership documents. Legal assistance for IDPs was provided up to the last step of the final decision by the court. Based on the above-mentioned successful pilot project, the proposed project has been designed to provide specific support to vulnerable people, female-headed households, not only through conducting awareness-raising sessions with women and men, but also by building capacity of local representatives (mukhtars) so that they will be able to provide appropriate support to vulnerable people within their areas of responsibility. Considering the timeframe required for the legal assistance and property restitution suite of activities, the proposed project has been designed to include the monitoring of these compensation claims through the last stage of the process - the transfer of the case by the court to the Compensation Committee. In order to address the main protection issues and risks related to a lack of knowledge of the compensation scheme, UN-Habitat will conduct an awareness raising campaign in the selected area and disseminate information and guidance materials to IDPs about the compensation scheme and its operating mechanisms. Regarding the challenge of complicated, bureaucratic and complex procedures, in coordination with private local lawyers, IDPs will be provided with legal assistance to explain the procedures and assist them in filing their compensation or restitution claims. Lawyers will also be able to represent IDP families at the courts as well as Compensation Committee sessions. Regarding the lack of HLP documentation, UN-Habitat will coordinate its activities with local authorities and advocate to restore damaged or destroyed HLP documents. Private lawyers will also work to restore or obtain missing ownership documents. Alternatively, UN-Habitat will work closely with mukhtars and local authorities to issue statements for beneficiaries related to missing or damaged HLP documents. Through this project, UN-Habitat in partnership with local NGOs and private lawyers, will provide free legal counselling and services to IDPs and returnees to complete all necessary HLP documentation in order not to burden IDPs and returnees with high financial costs. Regarding secondary occupations, in the initial phase of the project UN-Habitat will map and collect data on secondary occupations in Anbar Governorate. Thereafter, the project will conduct awareness raising activities in the affected areas and coordinate with mukhtars and other relevant actors to present and promote the new mediation approach. Additionally, UN-Habitat is equipped with experienced mediators and will develop a mediation session and training for mukhtars and other relevant actors on how to address secondary occupations and how to avoid potential conflict between IDPs and illegal occupants. Use of Alternative Dispute Resolution methods, will help beneficiaries involved in the dispute to maintain amicable relations and to build peace, dialogue, communication and robust relations between communities. This mediation approach will potentially result in a “win-win” situation for both parties as well as avoid any future obstacles between communities or parties involved in the dispute. UN-Habitat will conduct an advocacy campaign on HLP rights in the target areas to ensure that all people in need of HLP assistance are informed of their rights and the assistance available to them through the project. UN-Habitat will use international good practice approaches for advocacy as well as IEC materials on HLP rights. The most vulnerable individuals, including female-headed households, widowed women, disabled people and those with special needs will be prioritized.

How is your project accountable to affected population

An impact survey on compensation and mediation related to secondary occupations will be offered to each claimant family receiving HLP legal assistance under the project. This will capture their needs prior to the project. A subsequent survey at the end of the project will be conducted in order to assess whether the legal assistance and mediation services addressed their needs, and whether they are in a better position as a result of the intervention. Additionally, for the purpose of transparency and quality of the work, a complaints mechanism will be created for the purposes of this project, under which beneficiaries will be able to provide feedback on the quality of assistance, whether their rights had been respected, or to raise any other issues they wish to relay to UN-Habitat. The feedback mechanism will be anonymous in order to provide complete confidentiality for beneficiaries. UN-Habitat staff working on this project will undergo training on Gender-Based Violence (GBV) sensitivity and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) issues. They will also comply with the UN Code of Conduct. UN-Habitat will establish a complaints line related to protection, PSEA and GBV issues to ensure the highest standards of professional service delivery.
