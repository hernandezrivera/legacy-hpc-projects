Project Summary

The situation in NW of Syria remains grim, over 70% are considered people in acute need of life-saving assistance. With the COVID-19 pandemic, economic crisis and ongoing hostilities, the number of people relying on humanitarian aid to address survival and basic needs is increasing.
Long-term damage to civilian infrastructure has led to a direct negative impact on communities’ access to services. With the economic crisis and devaluation of the SYP, over 90% of the Syrian population are living under the poverty. Access to livelihood opportunities and income generating activities are limited, especially for vulnerable people living in rural areas and IDP sites.
Additionally, the water levels of the Euphrates River remain critically low, limiting the access to water for people living in northern Aleppo and Idleb. Low rainfall and damages to water stations and networks have further aggravated the reliable access to water for households and communal use. If left unaddressed, the water crisis may have critical food security and public health impacts.
In response to longer-term solutions to the provision of water supply, and other critical sanitation and hygiene services of the affected populations in North-West Syria to reinforce the most urgent life-saving interventions, Amal Organization aims to enhance the long-term WASH infrastructure. This intervention targets both host communities and IDPs in NW of Syria which is considered a highly prioritized and underserved area that is facing multiple challenges in light of unseasonably low levels of rainfall as well as sanitation services. thus, the planned intervention will respond to the needs of 109,232 individuals (44,480 IDPs, 64,752 residents) through the following activities:
Rehabilitation and maintenance of existing Water Station and linkage with water network and connecting the water station to the public electrical grid as well as installing solar power system which will contribute to reducing the monthly operational cost by approximately 45 percent. This will benefit a population in NW of Syria 109,232 individuals (44,480 IDPs, 64,752 residents).
- Establishment of wastewater treatment plants (Anaerobic Baffled Reactor ABR). This component will benefit the whole community by reducing water-borne diseases which will be improving the public health situation as well as use the treated wastewater for irrigation purposes for the local farmers. In consequence, the whole community will benefit from this component.
- Expansion and rehabilitation of sewerage networks in NW of Syria, to ensure safe access to sanitation infrastructure for the whole community members both IDPs and host communities.
- Supporting the regular solid waste management through operation, and maintenance (O&M) for current vehicles, distribution of garbage bins, and cleaning equipment. This component will benefit the target communities.
- Rehabilitation and maintenance of existing latrines, showers as well as heating of bath spaces using the solar system for the target collective centers which will be used for lighting too, in addition to providing water tanks for the IDP's families residing in the collective centers.
- Control of the spread of COVID-19 through RCCE and provision of soap for handwashing for the most vulnerable and subjected to infection focusing on PWD, elderly, PLW, women-headed households, and those who have chronical diseases and children in the schools who mainly transmit the infection to their families.
Consequently, the aforementioned components will help the affected population in target communities to avoid adaption of negative coping strategies in terms of having access to safe water and other critical sanitation services, and they will directly feed the WASH cluster objectives through support to water, sanitation/sewage, and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people.
In general, 0% of the budget is secured.

3 - Needs

Amal organization has made a rapid need assessment in the target community which is considered one of the high priority areas, to show the urgent needs of WASH sector according to the people in the targeted area. The assessment took place and a technical team of 2 field engineers supported by 3 M&E staff had conducted the survey, where the core target of this assessment was to highlight the severity scale indicators which can reflect the gaps and the possible mitigation interventions. and the summary of the key findings found as following: 1. Safe Water:72.13% of the respondents reported that there is no treatment of drinking water72.13% of the respondents reported that the water was not suitable for drinking, according to their belief71.31% of the respondents reported that tankers are the source that they rely on in providing drinking water. 2. Sufficient quantity of water on a daily basis7.38% of the respondents reported that their consumption of less than 15 liters per day.45.90% of the respondents reported that their consumption is between 15 to 20 liters per day.46.72% of the respondents reported that their consumption of more than 20 liters per day. 3. Sanitation system 64.75% of the respondents stated that the sewage networks was ready at 50%. 4.solid waste disposal77.5% of respondents reported that they are disposing of waste by throwing it outside the city 62 % of respondents reported that it was necessary to secure garbage containers.

Risk4: Escalation in Military Operation will lead to suspension of the activitiesMitigation4: Close coordination with the S&S department to develop a contingency plan.Risk5: Theft of equipment like the generator and solar systems:Mitigation5: there will be a fence and guards.
