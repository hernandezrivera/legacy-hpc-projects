Project Summary

The project will focus on the recovery of the crisis-affected populations through the provision of inputs for improved agricultural production as well as income generation activities for the development of sustainable livelihood opportunities. Action Against Hunger will contribute towards improvement in food security, nutrition status and improve household income for the most vulnerable people in the targeted locations through pro-poor economic empowerment initiatives and improved all year round agricultural production of diversified nutritious foods. The proposed project aims at improving the capacity of the vulnerable populations to be less dependent on aid and transit to a more sustainable livelihoods. The project will foster the resilience of Affected Communities by developing social cohesion through developing social networks and resilience of the displaced and integration with the host communities by developing social protection programs. The rebuilding and creation of community assets, as well as basic service infrastructure, which will build the capacity of communities to cope with crisis; infrastructure development includes, health facilities and WASH infrastructure as well as retraining and strengthening existing systems of government and capacity for relevant Ministries, local authorities and State actors to respond to the needs of conflict-affected populations. The project will prioritize the needs for different age-groups and sex will be prioritized and the protection of beneficiaries will be ensured during the implementation of activities.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria has endured for over ten years has eroded and destroyed public infrastructure and access to basic services remains inadequate, especially due to the continued influx of internally displaced across Borno and Yobe has placed additional pressure on existing services. The impact of several contributing factors such as hazards and vulnerabilities, food availability, food access, COVID19pandemic, food utilization including water and stability was assessed on these outcomes variables. According the Cadre Harmonize in October 2020, the results indicate that about 10 million (9.8 %) people of the analyzed population require urgent assistance in the current period (October to December 2020). During the projected period (June to August 2021), these figures are expected to increase to 13.8 (12.9%) million people unless resilience driven interventions and humanitarian assistance in conflict affected LGAs is sustained. Farmer-herder conflict across the Middle-Belt region and the arm conflict in northeast Nigeria have severely affected food production systems coupled with the COVID19 pandemic. Most livelihood activities across the states showed deterioration, with several zones/LGAs in emergency arising basically from the global pandemic (COVID-19), insecurity and rise in cost of production inputs. In the projected situation, several households in the some states (Katsina, Sokoto, Kaduna, Yobe, Adamawa and Borno) are likely to resort to emergency coping strategies that may result to further collapse in livelihoods; in the absence of conscious livelihood intervention stimulus packages. Nutrition situation in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States remains a challenge. This has been majorly attributed to gaps in caregiver’s nutritional dietary knowledge and hygiene practices. Stressed to emergency malnutrition thresholds was witnessed in several LGAs of these states (Adamawa, Borno and Yobe) during the current period. Stressed threshold GAM rate of 7.5% was reported in LGAs of Adamawa (North) State. In Borno (North) and Yobe (Central) crisis threshold GAM rate of 14.5% and 14.7% was witnessed; while in Yobe (central) GAM rate is under the emergency threshold of 15.8%. Cereals and cash crop production during the 2020/21 cropping year decreased marginally when compared with the 5year average. This may be owed to the hazard (floods) which submerged several thousands of rice and maize field across the cereal production belts of Kebbi, Jigawa, Niger, Bauchi, Kaduna, Kano States, and the FCT. Furthermore, the COVID-19 lockdown and restriction measures led to spikes in the cost of agricultural inputs and reduced agricultural operators’ investment capacity during the 2020/21 farming season. During the lean season (June-August, 2020) food availability in most states is expected to decline below normal levels, although the decline may be slightly cushioned by harvest from 2020/21 dry season farming. In areas with security challenges (Borno, Adamawa, Yobe, Katsina, Kaduna, Sokoto and Niger States); food availability has continued to be negatively impacted owing to disrupted agricultural livelihood activities and limited access to production assets (land, labour, and farm inputs)

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Fostering Resilience of Affected Communities through Restoration and Strengthening of Productive Assets, and Supporting Income Generating Activities. OUTPUT 1.1 : Community assets recovered, restored, and protected livelihoods through resilience programming OUTPUT 1.2: Beneficiaries trained to improve the productivity of household, including extension, entrepreneurship, capacity building, best agricultural practices, value addition and processing, and technology transfer. The output 1.2 will be given priority most vulnerable affected households with female-headed households and families have SAM beneficiaries’ undernutrition program. The activities include rehabilitation of irrigation facilities, environmental/soil & water conservation at the farm level and Adaptation to climate change and environment-friendly agriculture. The output 1.3 will be focused on Capacity building and knowledge transfer, Formation of Village saving and loan associations (VSLAs) and strengthening existing societies and theLinkage of beneficiaries to access micro-credit. Activity 02: Improvement of sustainable WASH services to affected male, female, boys and girls Output 2.1: Water facilities rehabilitated or constructed within affected communities for man, women, boys and girls.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure accountability to affected populations, Action Against hunger set up mechanisms to create awareness to beneficiaries. When the communities/camps were identified for support, the accountability team held community discussions to ensure communities/beneficiaries understand the intended purpose of the project, rights beneficiaries as well as complaints/feedback mechanism put in place by the project. The team places banners showing access to the feedback mechanism in strategic locations in those camps or communities. The Accountability team also holds bimonthly feedbacks days in camps/communities where the projects been implemented. Such feedback days are used as an avenue to continuously sensitize the targeted project beneficiaries on their right and availability of the feedback mechanism. Toll-free lines are clearly written on beneficiaries’ smart cards used for disbursement so that they can easily have access to the line when they have complaints or feedback regarding the projects. At all intervention sites, banners of complaints/feedback mechanisms are compulsorily displayed as well as prices of supported food items. Complaints/Feedback Channels is mechanisms or channels for receiving complaints/feedbacks in the projects include the toll-free line, field visit and FGD
