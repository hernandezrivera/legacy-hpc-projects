Project Summary

The project focuses on improving the knowledge and skills of schoolchildren on safety issues, namely land mines safety, first aids assistance and psychosocial stability. The project will also deal with the raising of awareness among teachers and schoolchildren on Human Rights/HRBA and UN 2030 priorities – issues especially relevant within areas isolated from other parts of Ukraine.
It is also vital to underline that one of the most important goals of the project will be to reassure the communities of Antratsit and Krasnyi Lutch that Ukraine and the international partners, continue to think of them, take care of them and consider them a part of the Ukrainian society; and will support them by all means possible. Despite the fact that they are located in not government-controlled areas. It is for such reason that, one more fundamental goal of this project is to work towards a future integration of the people living in these territories, into a single united Ukrainian community.
Social research results have shown that, although in 2014 an overwhelming majority of people (70 %) living in the Donetsk and Lugansk regions, scared by disinformation campaigns wanted to separate from Ukraine; in 2017 only 5% of people in the same areas, now under the control of the Ukrainian Government, followed the same opinion. It is not possible to conduct an independent social research in territories not controlled by the Ukrainian Government; however, it is a fact that due to the lack of objective information and disinformation campaigns, a great number of people living in the Antratsit and Krasnyi Lutch areas would like to separate from Ukraine. Indeed, the value of this project is to help enhance the opinion of these communities towards Ukraine and the democratic wester values.

Needs

An armed conflict in Eastern Ukraine is significantly and adversely affecting children. The humanitarian caseload of internally displaced has risen above 1,600,000 people with an estimated one- third of them being children. Families living in affected areas face problems of access to safe drinking water, as well as proper personal hygiene. Heightened safety issues exist for these children and their families such as mine safety and risks of increased violence. Dangers, risks and pressures in the affected areas have led to heightened levels of stress or distress experienced by many children and families. Enhancing protective factors for these children and their caregivers can be critical to nurture coping mechanisms and resilience. Furthermore, many children still have limited access to education due to safety reasons, fear of stigma, or inability to reach schools. The consequences of these events have shown the lack of preparedness of girls and boys in the non-government controlled zone for the current realities of life. There is an urgent need to provide psychosocial protection and psychological assistance to both individuals and whole social groups. In the non-government controlled zone in eastern Ukraine, there is an especially urgent need to disseminate updated information and skills on mine safety and first aid as well as improvement of the general psychological climate, promotion of the strategies of overcoming emotional distress and preventing discord among girls and boys.
