Project Summary

Through the proposed project, Tdh aims to increase access to essential and quality health and nutrition services for Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMN) in camps 26 and 27, and host communities in Teknaf upazila. This will be achieved through integrated, community-based mechanisms, with a particular focus on women and children under 5. The project aims to ensure continued access to adequate primary health services through operating Tdh’s two Primary Health care Centers (PHC), and through capacity building of healthcare service providers. Tdh has operated its PHC in camp 26, supported by ECHO, since 2018. In 2021, support will be taken over by PRM, with funding secured until 31st August 2021, and include the operation of an additional PHC. The projects goal will also be achieved via the dissemination of basic information on health, through the creation of community outreach teams and capacity building of communities/Community-based Groups (CbGs) to identify and respond to their own priority risks. Tdh will provide the full Essential Package of Health Services at the two PHC, including services for Mental Health, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) and nutrition. This will ensure quality continuum of care by improving patient diagnosis and treatment, reducing redundancy in diagnostic testing, and supporting more efficient and effective decision-making and care. This will also help to better assess disease patterns and medical stock management. The project will also increase the capacity of maternity services to ensure deliveries under a high-quality standards framework, ensuring antenatal and postnatal care processes and results are in place. Support to health services, especially for women and children will focus on nutrition though the strengthening of counselling activities in the Breast-feeding corner from an Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) Counsellor, and though nutrition screening triaging, services and referrals, specifically for SAM and MAM children. Where appropriate, indicators will be sex and age disaggregated. Tdh will endeavour to secure funding for these activities for the period 1st September - 31st December.

1 - Identified Needs

While most individuals may be able to access some health services, critical needs remain, particularly in maternal and child health provision. In the camps, facility-based births continue to be low at 18%. Only 44% of newborns were reported to be breastfed within the first hour after birth. In the host communities, 70% of the women continue to give birth at their homes. At the PHC (Primary Health Center) operated by Tdh, 172 pregnant and/or women of reproductive age were found to be low Hb positive, higher than standard averages. With respect to nutrition, 30% of children in the camps aged 6 to 59 months were reported as not being enrolled in any nutrition-feeding program at the time of data collection. The most common causes for seeking health services in both communities were febrile illnesses (particularly among children), physical pain, acute respiratory illnesses, diarrhea, diabetes and hypertension, revealing a high need for Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD)-related services. For mental health, the 2019 JRP continues to highlight Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) needs as “tremendous” and flags gaps in services as a top priority. About half the households from camps 26 and 27 reported at least one challenge in accessing NGO-run health facilities. Unavailability of supplies was cited as the topmost reason followed by unavailability of treatment and overcrowding. Resultantly, a quarter of households ended up resorting to paid services, with 66% of the respondents going into debt to pay for health expenditures. In host communities, 90% of the respondents reported to have no access to adequate pharmaceutical drugs when in need. Lack of referral pathways from community health facilities to higher levels of care were cited as a major impediment to adequate treatment. Finally, gender inequalities in access to health outreach also have been noted as only 36% of women reported being reached by a Community Health Worker (CHW) in comparison to 52% of male respondents. It is important to note that there is no PHC in Camp 27 and, considering the needs for refugees in terms of health conditions, the Health Sector and CiC are looking for a new health Actor in this area. Tdh has secured funding to respond to these needs until the end of August 2021, and will endeavor to secure further funding for the period 1st September - 31st December 2021.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provision of consultations at Tdh’s two PHCs Activity 2: Provision of capacity building of health service providers in the two PHCs, to enable them to adequately identify and refer vulnerable children and caregivers to CP services. Activity 3: Increase the capacity of maternity services to ensure deliveries under a high-quality standards framework and strengthen support of the maternity services so they may reach a higher number of women and children in the target areas. Activity 4: Provision of the full Essential Package of Health Services at the two PHC, including services for Mental Health, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) and nutrition. Activity 5: Implementation of community outreach activities disseminating multi-sectorial information on health, CP and hygiene awareness. Activity 6: Provision of an Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) Councillor to strengthen counselling activities in the Breastfeeding Corner (nutrition activity). Activity 7: Implementation of nutrition screening triaging, services, and referrals, specifically for SAM and MAM children.
