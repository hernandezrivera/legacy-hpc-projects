Project Summary

In 2022 Shafak organization will respond to the increased GBV concerns of the crises affected women and girls by providing prevention, response, and mitigation GBV activities at 11 static facilities as follows: WGSS at Marret Tamsrin, WGSS at RH facility, WGSS at Azaz, in addition to three WGSS in three camps (in Idleb and Azaz) and two WGSS in Jarablus and Ariha, one WGSS at Qurqenea, and two WGSS in Idleb city. Moreover, 15 outreach teams will conduct GBV activities in four main Districts Idleb, Harim, Jebel Saman, and Azaz. GBV prevention, response, and mitigation activities will be delivered to 35686 individuals from IDPs and host communities focusing on the most vulnerable people including adolescent girls, elderly women, and PWDs.
The proposed project will offer gender and age-appropriate prevention and response GBV services including provision of one-off unconditional Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) (cash or voucher modality), empowerment activities through the provision of small grants for women to be able to start new small projects/businesses with the goal of achieving economic independence, GBV case management, Young Mothers Club program, AMAL initiative, life skills, safety audits in the project locations, recreational and structured PSS activities, and GBV awareness-raising sessions and campaign targeting women, men, boys, and girls in both IPDs and host community sites, as well as capacity building training for humanitarian actors (workers in other sectors like WASH, Shelter/NFI, Education) on GBV topics which will be conducted to reduce risks of GBV and refer to specialized services.
Shafak will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on GBV topics such as; GBV core concepts, safe identification and referral, and communication skills to enable them to identify and refer people in need to the case management team. Moreover, the community committees will play a significant role in raising GBV issues that are faced by the community and introducing the project activities.
Shafak will also coordinate with women-led organizations in the targeted locations that are implementing GBV projects and will respond closely to their needs, specialty technical GBV capacity building training.
Furthermore, as part of response activities, the outreach teams will conduct Psychological First Aid (PFA) within camps, reception centers, and other IDPs collective places. Shafak will engages men and boys in GBV prevention activities through mobile GBV activities, which aimed to mitigate the GBV risks, besides, make the community more supportive of GBV issues.
Shafak will work on enhancing the quality of the GBV services through capacity-building activities for the GBV and non-GBV staff of Shafak as well as of other organizations.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world's most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people, triggering one of the worst displacement crises of our time. Today, according to HNO 2021, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance - a 21 percent increase compared to 2020 - with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. Out of the 13.4 million people in need, approximately ten million (75 percent) are women and children; including approximately 2.3 million females and males with disabilities who are 12 years and over. The aforementioned needs were confirmed in the Voices 2022 report which indicates that according to a group of women and girls who took part in the community focus groups in 2020, a range of different forms of violence affected them similarly to previous years. Sexual, emotional, psychological, and physical violence were all mentioned as commonly occurring within the community, as well as early marriage, economic violence, and denial of opportunity and resources. The same report, Voices 2021, stated that divorced and widowed women and girls continue to face various forms of GBV, made more pronounced in the COVID-19 crisis. Not only are they frequently sexually harassed such as when using public transportation, working, or accessing distributions, but they also face systemic discrimination when it comes to obtaining their rights in divorce and inheritance.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be delivered through 11 WGSS, Shafak RH facilities as integration with health/SRH, in addition to 15 mobile outreach teams. The project will include prevention, mitigation, and response GBV activities for 35686 individuals as follows:1-Safety audits in four IDPs camps.2-Non structures PSS /recreational, PFA for 15761, 3-Structured and non-structured awareness sessions for 11987 women and girls, GBV and COVID 19 topics will be covered in addition to the GBV tool kit.4-specialized GBV services to 1698 GBV survivor (includes GBV Case management, Case fund, and PFA).5-income-generating activities (IGA), vocational training 640.6-Life skills sessions for 800 W and G.7-psychosocial support activities for women and girls at risk 3300.6Young mothers club and AMAL initiative 250. 7.IPA and cash and voucher assistance for 1000. Besides, Shafak will conduct GBV mainstreaming training for 200 humanitarian workers and build the capacity of 50 of the newly recruited staff on GBV topics. Collaboration between the WGSSs and Shafak health facility will be maintained using the referral pathway to refer GBV cases between the WGSSs and RH services. Moreover, the project activities will ensure the inclusion of PWDs in the project activities through centers friendly principle for PWDs, building the project staff’s capacity, and providing transportation to enable them to reach WGSS centers. The MEAL activities will be conducted by using the GBV M&E tool kit.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Rosk1:Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services. Likelihood: Hight. Impact: High. Mitigation1: 1.GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs. 2.ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Risk2: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging and lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation2: 1.Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. 2.sure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities. 3.Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture. Risk3: Livelihood opportunities may increase risks of GBV and expectations of income may be unrealistic and might create dependency. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation3: consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the programme. 2. Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs 3. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report.Risk4: Lack of inclusive criteria for GBV survivors, especially in distributions. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation4: inclusion of GBV survivors in distribution criteria


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk Identified1: the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by the project personnel on project BNFs and women staff. Mitigation: 1- provide awareness raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA 2- train the new staff on PSEA, CSG and to have refresher trainings to the old staff. 3- Distribute banners, PSEA reporting mechanism including the suggestion box, WhatsApp number...etc. Risk 2: No reporting from SEA survivors: Mitigation: 1- Raise awareness between project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms. 2- Apply various reporting channels that are accessible to all BNFs. 3- Emphasize on the principle of confidentiality in dealing with SEA cases. 4- Assign gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage women and girls to report


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security-related SOPs in place. 1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to preempt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover, or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly. Risk 2: Disputes between host communities and IDPs; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 2.1:Targeting host community, 2.2: ensure Complaint mechanism is accessible by all people. 2.3 integrity and clear verification process conducted according to announced criteria. 2.4 Prior coordination with local actors and stakeholders in the community to ensure the process.Risk: Outbreak of COVID 19; Likelihood: High; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 3.1:Daily check of staff to ensure their safety is a major symptom of the Coronavirus.3.2:Emphasis during work on the need to leave a distance of at least 1 between workers.3.3:The necessity not to exchange equipment and tools before sterilization. 3.4:Ensure the presence of awareness brochures within the work area and in a clear place.3.4:In case of imposed curfew. Suspend the activities and change the modality to be through service providers and distribute the dues of the staff
