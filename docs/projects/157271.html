Project Summary

RCDO conducted an field survey through its staff in Jebel Marra- Rokro and Nerette, which found that there was an urgent need to intervene because of GVB in situations of armed conflict,also RCDO participated in Inter-Agency Assessment Mission took place from 27-29 June 2018 to Jabel Mara,from other side sudan humanitarian response plan 2019 mentioned that women and girls at risk,who represent respectively 25 % and 30 % of the displaced population, this percentage is higher in situations of new displacement and the newly accessible areas, such as Jebel Marra, social services are not available or have very limited capacity to take care of people affected by violence and displacement. one of the affected by displacement and areas newly accessible,It is the highest indicator of gender-based violence, are mainly caused by the armed conflict in Jebel Marra , Disruption has resulted in acute and multiple humanitarian needs. Violence in Jebel Marra has forced millions of people to abandon their homes and livelihoods to settle in IDP Camps, inter agency assessment from 8 13 Dec 2016 indicates that there is high need of protection assistance to be provided. During conflict situations women and girls experience (GBV), including rape, attempted rape, gang rapes and other sexual assault, intimate partner violence, sexual harassment, trafficking, and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) of girls in promise of employment. women affected by violence ,most women have lost their livelihoods which increases their vulnerability to GBV. Gender based violence is difficult to document especially in times of conflict because of breakdown of established protection and complaints mechanisms such as family, police, and clan elders. Reporting incidences is also difficult due to distance, fear of threats, ostracization and stigma, and low awareness of human right In light of this, there is a dire need for information to empower communities to protect themselves from further GBV, stigma, and associated repercussions such as HIV/AIDS. There is also a pressing need to inform, educate and empower people (women, men, girls and boys) about the medical and legal services available, and give women livelihood opportunities. Gender issues in Central Darfur include gap in education, inadequate maternal health and nutrition services, early marriage, gender-based violence, women's lack of access to economic opportunities and productive assets. Also due to the traditional concepts of Darfurian communities, gender roles show high burden on women however, women and girls are economically and socially more vulnerable and have a higher incidence of poverty. RCDO for quality coordination and enhanced protection efforts, reports from the capacity network and technical support committees, including government bodies, indigenous leaders, CBOs and other relevant bodies, will be shared to support and monitor the implementation of activities with the Sectors GVB and general protection.
