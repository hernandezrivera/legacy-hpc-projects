Project Summary

The project aims providing life-saving and life-sustaining WASH services and further strengthening resilience of the most vulnerable 15000 persons (6240 women, 5760 men, 1500 boys and 1500 girls) in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudug Region, Somalia by supporting their urgent as well as long term WASH needs for women, men, boys and girls from villages that are yet to recover from impacts of past successive seasons of below normal rains exacerbated by past devastating drought season that nearly culminated into famine in Somalia. The plight of the affected and targeted beneficiaries is further compounded by intermittent conflict and related tension between clans of Galkayo South/Galmudug and Galkayo North/Puntland that limit their access to water sources and sanitation facilities located at near the boundaries of the mentioned states in Hobyo and South Galkayo Districts. The urgent and immediate water needs will be mitigated through water distribution by vouchers to the 15 most vulnerable villages in the south of Mudug Region while increase of sustainable access to safe water will be ensured by drilling of one borehole in Xaaro village as well as rehabilitation of 4 boreholes in Gowlale,Jilable Hayre, Hilmo and Taaroge and upgrade of 5 shallow wells in Xero- dhagaxley village, coupled by training of water committee members (men and women) to manage and maintain the newly constructed and rehabilitated water systems. The project further aims at enhancing sanitation conditions by construction of 50 latrines in IDP camps in South Galkayo and conducting CLTS in targeted locations and further conduct comprehensive hygiene promotion campaign and distribution of hygiene kits to the most vulnerable conflict and drought displaced as well as vulnerable host communities/men, women, boys and girls including the elderly and those with disabilities in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts.
Quality in Disaster Risk Reduction will be ensured by effective implementation of the project through regular monitoring in line with the technical specifications as per WASH cluster standards throughout the project time till completion. Accountability to the affected persons will be guaranteed by ensuring comprehensive social mobilization and sensitization campaign where the targeted beneficiaries will be fully aware of the project prior to it commencement to ensure their expectations are met upon project completion hence accountability to them. Furthermore, there will be complains, reporting and response mechanisms for the project beneficiaries that will ensure their satisfaction in terms of project benefit to them hence accountability.

Needs

Despite good Gu 2017 rains (average and above average) that replenished water sources and improved livelihoods in parts of the country, the targeted project locations in Hobyo and South Galkayo Districts in Mudug Region, Somalia are yet to recover from impacts of devastating drought of 2017 that nearly culminated into famine (FSNAU post Gu technical report of 2nd September, 2018). A combination of past devastating drought and intermittent conflicts between clans of South and North Galkayo is attributed to huge displacements of women, men, boys and girls to IDP camps in South Galkayo as well as those that settled in remote villages of South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts with community owned permanent water sources/boreholes thus crowding them and worsening the already precarious sanitation and hygiene conditions besides subjecting the boreholes to over use. Their plight is compounded by limited sanitation facilities that have conditioned them to practice open defecation, coupled by poor hygiene conditions and practices worsened by careless dumping of garbage that is attributed to the common cases of AWD/cholera outbreak and other water borne diseases during onset of rainy seasons that further aggravates malnutrition cases among boys and girls aged under 5 and Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs). The affected IDP families and vulnerable host communities cannot afford to buy water from private boreholes as their purchasing powers are completely eroded having lost all their livelihood assets to the severe drought. Furthermore some of the boreholes and shallow wells in the project targeted locations are completely dilapidated owing to neglect and require urgent rehabilitation and upgrade to increase sustainable access to safe water. The project targets the most vulnerable 15000 persons (6240 women, 5760 men, 1500 boys and 1500 girls)
