Project Summary

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) report, the humanitarian crisis is expected to increase across South Sudan, with 8.3 million people in need of aid assistance. The main drivers of this are floods, the Covid-19 pandemic and the event of sustainable peace returning to local communities and triggering influx of returnees. Pockets of sub-national conflicts are expected to continue in some parts of the country, forcing people out of their homes and destabilizing livelihoods. Through the emergency shelter and NFIs, WVI intends to will use US$1,014,750 to support 90,000 IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable host communities (18,500 men, 20,500 women, 25,500 boys, 25,500 boys), including people with disabilities, through single sector approach of interventions. This project will be implemented in Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, Jonglei and Western Equatoria States. The materials to distribute will be in-kind emergency shelter and NFIs s as well as covid-19 personal protective equipment (PPEs). However, in locations that have functional markets, 18% of the overall targeted project beneficiaries will receive the assistance using unconditional but restricted modality of cash-based intervention. The project will contribute to two cluster sectorial objectives, namely, (1). Improve access to safe; appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities. (2) Support most vulnerable returnees and host communities/affected with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions. The WVI project team will be based in WVI’s National Office in Juba so that they can be deployed in project sites and in hard-to-access areas, using the mobile emergency response mechanism. During the project lifespan, WVI will collaborate with the National Cluster, IOM/Pipeline, UNHAS and partner agencies. Active community participation in project activities will be ensured from the beginning through Accountability to most Affected Population (AAP). HLP, SEA (Sexual Exploitation & abuse) and Covid-19 awareness sessions will also be mainstreamed in the project. In addition, in order to capture community feedback, a Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will be setup for use throughout the project cycle.

1 - Needs

In 2020, hopes of relative peace, stability and improved human welfare were high among South Sudanese; however, no significant outcomes were achieved from the peace agreement that was signed two years ago due to slow implementation. This uncovered severe humanitarian crisis. Lack of durable peace and limited investment are holding people from stability and sustainable development’’- Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021. The economy has increasingly been unbearable with hyperinflation increase market prices, and relegating more people below the poverty line. Security across the country remained fluid in 2020, with protracted pockets of violent conflicts, especially in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Central Equatoria States. Floods have also devastated over 30 counties, adding to the catastrophic effects of COVID-19 pandemic disease. These challenges have undermined coping mechanisms and resilience across communities; the 2021 Humanitarian overview records that, ‘’people’s mental and physical wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms are expected to deteriorate in 2021; Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be of humanitarian assistance’’. The shelter and non-food items sector remain essential in solving this crisis, as in the advent of sustainable peace in local communities there could be influx of returnees from the PoCs and across the national borders. Continued local conflicts, and floods related displacements of communities are predicated to happen. Based on this, World Vision International South Sudan will provide in-kind emergency shelter and life-saving non-food assistance to 90,000 (18,500 men, 20,500 women, 25,500 boys, 25,500 boys) IDPs, returnees and host communities through single sector approach in Upper Nile, Unity State, Warrap, Jongolei and Western Equatoria States. However, in case of existing functional markets in certain areas, 18% of the targeted beneficiaries will be assisted using the unconditional but restricted modality of cash-based interventions. With this, project World Vision International would contribute to two cluster sectorial objectives namely; (1) Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities. (2) Support most vulnerable returnees and host communities/affected with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions. World Vision International staff members will use the mobile response modality of intervention to respond to the project sites and hard-to-access areas that are recommended by the Shelter and life-saving non-food items Cluster. Interventions will be carried in participatory manner with the Shelter and Life-saving Non-food Items Cluster providing technical support at National and State levels. The other parties that would be closely consulted during implementation of the project include IOM/Pipe, UNOCHA, UNHAS, DTM & WFP and cluster peer-agencies. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to own the project by actively participating in activities for durable solutions to be realized. WVI will achieve this by mainstreaming AAP (accountability to affected populations) into the activities from onset of the project to closure. Existing local community structures will be explored and strengthened and where they are missing, WVI will facilitate the formation, for example, local committees. More so, Men, women, boy, girls and local leaders will be trained and sensitized on the project and roles of every party that is involved, including people with disabilities. CFM (Complaint Feedback Mechanism) will be introduced in each project area and community members would be sensitized on how to use it. Awareness creation sessions will also include topics like conflict mitigation, community long-term solutions for floods, prevention of COVID-19, because these are predicted to be the main causes of needs for Emergency Shelter and life-saving Non-Food Items in 2021.

2 - Project activities

Various activities will be carried to achieve the project goals. WVI will follow the Shelter and life-saving emergency shelter cluster standard of operations as below;1. Conduct needs assessment using both the direct and indirect inter agency rapid needs assessment (IRNA) missions to ascertain community need for In-kind, cash interventions, and prioritize severely affected people. This activity will start with desk reviews exercises, led by the WVI staff. Communities will be engaged from onset of the project in meetings, awareness workshops to discuss local projects. Methodologies like Focused Group discussions, Key Informants, Observation of affected populations will be used to gather information at the time of conducting needs assessment. SEA, HLP and Covid-19 issues would also be mainstreamed into the activities and parties will ensure that prevention measures for spread of the virus are been observed. CFM (Complaint Feedback Mechanism) will be introduced at this phase of project cycle.2. Conduct beneficiary verification and registration. WVI will identify and register beneficiaries with extreme vulnerability in line with the shelter and life-saving non-food items selection criteria. Volunteer, local leaders and beneficiary representatives will take part in the verification and registration to enhance community participation in the project. 3. Conduct ES/NFI distribution. During the NFI distribution, WVI will ensure that all aid distribution is based on the needs of the affected population and will ensure that structures like CFM (complaint feedback mechanism) where trained local personnel are assigned to manage. The covid-19 is still posing challenges; strict adherences shall be ensured for beneficiaries to mitigate spread of the virus. Educational messaging would be carried through distribution sessions to highlight the dangers and prevention measures for the pandemic virus. The distribution will include shelter, non-food items and covid-19 materials. 4. Deploy WV Mobile Teams to project locations. The mobile team will also engage and respond to the emergency needs of affected populations in hard-to-access areas where the cluster has recommended. Deployment will be both bi-sectoral and multi-sectoral. 5. Conduct Rapid and Post Distribution Monitoring. This activity will inform the project and WVI in particular on the level of dissatisfaction or satisfaction of the affected population who received in kind NFIs and cash transfers. However, WVI will ensure continuous monitoring for the cash interventions to understand market dynamics throughout the project lifespan. 6. Production of Covid-19 messaging and prevention materials. WVI will design and procure covid-19 materials that are used for community awareness. Facemasks, sensitizers and gloves will be supplied under the project for local volunteers, leaders, local committee members and other parties that are involved in the implementation of the project. Rapid community awareness sessions using WHO key messages on the virus, will be conducted throughout the project to enlighten beneficiaries on the dangers of the disease and the prevention measures.

3 - Project indicators

The project success will be measured against the project indicators which include: # of people reached with in-kind emergency shelter, target:18,000, # of people reached with in-kind life-saving NFIs, target:360000, # of people reached with in-kind NFI assistance, target:12,000; # of people reached with in-kind shelter assistance, target:30,000; % of complaints registered through CFM desks addressed, target:80% ; % of affected populations served with S/NFI assistance reporting assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible and participatory manner, target: 80% and % of distributions followed with rapid monitoring, target: 80% At the end of the project all beneficiaries must have improved access to shelter and non-food and improved dignity. Materials like plastic sheets would be used to constructed new huts for the case of returnees, Displaced persons can also rebuild their homes with it. Kitchen sets are used to prepare meals and improve hygiene of household members. Blankets and mosquitoes’ nets are to be used at night for beneficiaries to cover from cold and protect people from mosquito bites respectively. Community members of the project would have also been empowered in various ways by conducting meetings and awareness on coping mechanisms and community resilience. This is to be done from the start of the project by exploring and strengthening local community structures and using them to disseminate information.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be considered as a key pillar of empowering communities during implementation of the project. World Vision will ensure the project is owned by beneficiaries from onset by sharing information regarding the project for instance, project goals, objectives, project roles between World Vision and beneficiaries will be shared. PSEA, will also be enshrined in the project through creation of awareness where men, women, boys, girls and people with disabilities are sensitized on the causes and dangers of PSEA and reporting channels. World Vision will do this by facilitating selection of community members who are trained on PSEA. The members will be part of the local structures that are responsible of other communities. Through the knowledge given to them, they will support on passing of PSEA messages during the project life-span and beyond. Other mechanisms of passing information to community members include conducting of local campaigns where various members are encouraged to participate in. At the time of registration of beneficiaries for assistance which is usually done from doo-to-door of households, World Vision staff and volunteers will use the opportunity to discuss about PSEA. Distribution moments will be designed with key messaging materials that are displayed in distribution venues with trained local volunteers assigned to routinely pass information throughout. This project will directly target disabled people and when implementing activities, WVSS will use a community participatory approach where all members of the community including men, women, boys, girls, elderly and disabled persons are involved to take part needs assessment meetings through focus group discussions and key informants. Existing local structures will be identified and strengthened to enhance community communication and engagement, where they don’t exist, WV will facilitate the formation. Additionally, traditional methods like use of cooking pots made of mud, making of local mats and using grass the construct homes will be encouraged to enhance resilience and coping mechanism during crisis.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will conduct needs analysis of identified prior to implementation of project activities to identify needs of vulnerable groups. From this phase of project, World Vision will establish a system (complaint feedback mechanism) that makes smooth communication of every beneficiary feedback to World Vision. Individuals with disabilities and those who are illiterate will be given special attention by mobilizing them to participate in activities and provide feedback. Local translators will be trained on the project and asked to disseminate information to the illiterate beneficiaries. On the other hand, they would return feedback that were gathered to World Vision for analysis and use in the project. Local project committees that are formed in each area will be representatives from vulnerable groups including disabled, illiterate, women and girls. Specific vulnerable groups especially disabled persons will be actively during implementation of the project by gathering their views during community engagements through Focus group discussions and also use of community feedback mechanisms. They will also be represented in local project committees that are instituted to support implementation of activities

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During implementation of the shelter emergency and life-saving non-food items project, World Vision will use the CCE (community communication and engagement) approach to engage IDPs and returnees to provide durable solutions. Trained staff will carry rigorous sensitization meetings, focusing on HLP access right. Local authorities will also receive trainings on the HLP concept and some of the statutory and customary laws for HLP. World Vision will closely work with partners that provide legal assistance to respond to communities and raise awareness on how to report issues. Imperatively, the needs assessments conducted in communities to determine shelter and non-food items needs will include questions that are used to sought feedback on HLP especially during focus group discussions and key informants’ interviews, this includes land rights and clarity on tenure arrangements, authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership. The information gathered in the needs assessment meetings will be analyzed to understand the depth of problems that arise from HLP in the community. The project will also help mitigate disputes that result from construction of houses in new areas, by ensuring that land ownership and tenure arrangements are agreed upon from onset. Community leaders would play a key role in this in most South Sudanese villages, land ownership follows ancestral linages and some residents, especially IDPs are hosted by relatives. Evictions of beneficiaries and loss of assets will be minimized by sensitizing them on HLP rights and the processes of securing documentation for assets to ensure ownership is legal. The Shelter/non-item response will be anchored on the principle of do-no-harm; during response IDPs, returnees, local authorities and local partner NGOs would be engaged in various ways like meetings and trainings, so that outstanding community issues are discussed and amicably resolved by the end of project. WV will support with information, for instance where there is need to seek legal counsel etc.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback will be gathered from vulnerable members of the community by deliberately interacting with them and including in them in sample population when conducting monitoring visits and post distribution monitoring. They will also be encouraged to voice their views through the complaint feedback mechanism that would be set in the community. This done by holding meetings and establishing complaint feedback mechanism where trained local community members and World Vision staff are tasked to tend. This feedback will be used by mainstreaming them into the project for instance if beneficiaries have suggested significant ways of implementation activities or the type of materials they would want to receive. Some of the feedbacks may be immediately applied in the project for example during registration of beneficiaries and distribution of materials. And feedback that is received at the end of project would be used in design of future shelter and life-saving non food items projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

World Vision’s team will ensure safety of beneficiaries during implementation of project activities in communities. Interventions will take into consideration the approach of do-no-harm, where activities are done with adherence to zero effects on community lives for instance, distribution venues must be in safe location where beneficiaries have access to a proper shade or protection from sun heat, and drinking water is accessible. Need assessment meetings with community members and the time of beneficiary registration will involve awareness sessions on various issues like child protection, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), and Domestic violence. As communities may be exposed to the risks of explosive hazards, they will also be sensitized on how to protect themselves and how to pass information of suspected hazardous objects to agencies to support. This will be done by ensuring that the local project committees that are formed in each area to champion project activities are empowered with knowledge on protection of men, women, boys, girls and disabled beneficiaries. They will be assigned to continuously disseminate key messages on protection to keep their community members aware. Additionally, World Vision will work closely with UNMISS and UNDSS to respond to emergencies or cases that are related to explosive hazards. WVSS will implement this project in close collaboration with SNFI cluster at national and state levels to minimize overlaps of activities, and most importantly, to reach the most in need people. WVSS will achieve this by participating in cluster meetings like the OWG (operations working groups) where cluster interventions are discussed. Rigorous needs assessments will be done prior to distribution missions. Post Distribution monitoring will help to gather feedback from beneficiaries to inform WVSS on the effectiveness of the project and where there will be need by changes in the design of future project.
