Project Summary

The population in Ayod State is predominantly Nuer with very few groups of individuals from other locations/tribes. Others groups include Dinka, Shiluk, Anyuak and Darfurian. They are neighbouring to Eastern Jonglei i.e. In general, the population of Jiech, Mogok and Karmoun has an estimated population of about 15 ,000-25,000 inhabitants in each county. The children make up at least half of this population with the prevalence of young mothers as many of them are given compelled to marriage by force or lack of an alternative.
INTERSOS has been implementing Child Protection in Ayod state since 2017 through SSHF funding until August 2019. This was in response to the protracted and dire protection needs in regards to protection. During this project INTERSOS identified and registered 240 UASCSs (107 of them female) in the CPIMS. The project also revealed that lack of exclusive CFS and PSS for children and adolescents has also contributed to levels of distress among the two sets as they are forced to use the airstrips or very limited excluded spaces that expose them to safety hazards. Inaccessibility to the remote locations such as Karmoun and Mogok makes it all the more difficult to reach these cases except by foot which is about 3 hrs and 8hrs journey respectively.
INTERSOS intends to continue addressing the Child Protection needs in Jiech, Mogok, Karmoun Longor, Kandak, Luadiew and Gorwai. The locations were selected in view of the similar context of needs yet there are no Child Protection actors and their proximity to Jiech which serves as the most convenient hub, as all of them fall within 3-8hrs walk from Jiech.
INTERSOS aims to provide child protection to vulnerable, children affected by conflict through a holistic approach consisting of various activities. The primary activity will include lifesaving needs of children through; i) structured and comprehensive case management; and ii) PSS provision including Psychological First Aid and FTR services for girls and boys with protection needs such UASC, CAAFAG, GBV survivors, orphans and children with disability. As an additional channel for the identification of protection needs INTERSOS will also provide activities and space to provide PSS activities, conduct PFA and work with other actors such as nutrition and education partners who observe a high number of children in their activities. The intervention will also be characterized by a focus on community engagement activities such as awareness-raising and risk mitigation achieved through a strong system of Trainer of Trainers whereby INTERSOS will provide training and engage community members in specific techniques for contextualized approaches to strengthen an enabling and protective environment for children. Such trainings for the children will include positive parenting, decision making, foster care, conflict mitigation and peace building, family separation dynamics, and gender equality. INTERSOS endeavours to ensure activities are tailored to the specific gender and age of the target population as detailed in the needs analysis section
The objective of this intervention is to provide at least 800 (400 female) children with comprehensive case management support for CP and GBV and at least 11,000 (5500 female) in PSS and awareness. Past successful approaches have shown that CFS is an important part of the integrated approach of this project. Therefore, INTERSOS child protection interventions aim to reach at least 6,000 (3000 female) children through CBPSS activities, through access to 3 already existing CFS that will be refurbished for usage and establishment of 4 more in the locations not established. Other direct beneficiaries include all the community members targeted in relation to extending child protection by training adult caregivers and other community members on child protection, child rights, parenting skills, and positive discipline methods.

1 - Needs

Ayod County also referred to as Pow State by the IO is a frontline between the Sudan People’s Liberation Army (SPLA) forces and the SPLA-In Opposition (SPLA-IO) elements. SPLA and SPLA(IO) have ceased fire and this has calmed the displacement issue but has not solved the separation of children from their biological parents. There also exists a heightened need of protection actors who will assist in setting up community based protection structures to empower and create awareness in the community of the six grave violations against children.Ayod is also acutely prone to destabilizing feuds between clans that often are in pursuit of each other for revenge killings. According to INTERSOS August 2019 monthly focused group discussion, in the last 6 months of our CP project tenure, about 20 people lost their lives between March and August alone in Karmoun, Gorwai, Jiech,Longor and Kandak, This shows there is a need to have peace and conflict mitigation sessions integrated into CP to assist in highlighting the adverse effects that experienced by children who are the most vulnerable. These conflicts have also created a need to have parents and caregivers be coached on peaceful co-existence, impacts of family separation, conflict resolution, GBV and foster care. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019, Ayod falls under high priority in terms of protection particularly child protection. In South Sudan, over 19,000 children are estimated to have been recruited to armed forces and armed groups; 955 children (691 boys and 264 girls) have been released so far in 2018. Since 2013, more than 1 million children have been affected by psychosocial distress, whereas 17,125 children, of which 9,076 girls and 8,049 boys,73 are still in need of family tracing and reunification. Since 2018-2019, INTERSOS has trained 354 (19f) children associated with conflict either through recruitment or in the white army in conflict mitigation and peacebuilding, child rights and benefits of education through workshops with adolescents. INTERSOS has been operational in this location and seeks to ensure we identify and report any recruited or children participating in conflict to CP –Subcluster and the CAAF/AG WG. Another protection concern that emanates from this conflict is that, while men often take part in the armed conflict or are away with the cattle of the families, women are mostly the sole breadwinners of the households and are forced to leave their children alone all day while walking long distances to fetch water, wild fruits, and collect firewood. This leads not only to physical and emotional neglect of the children but also increases their exposure to significant protection threats such as exploitation, abuse, and recruitment within armed forces or armed groups. The heavy movements of IDPs during the last years and the lack of resources have increased the number of vulnerable children and, raising the importance of UASC making FTR services. Among the most urgent child protection risks identified are the high number of unaccompanied, separated children, orphans, and children-headed households. Also prevalent are high levels of psychosocial distress of the community in general, among both children and adults, given by the high level of insecurity and the spread of various forms of violence. This is exacerbated by the lack of community-based and government social and child welfare structures able to respond to the basic needs of the population which often leads to negative coping mechanisms. All the above issues are due to insecurity within the area, lack of economic opportunities and resources that force movements of the population and lead to abandonment of children, lack of awareness on fundamental principles and sustainable positive coping mechanisms.

2 - Activities

- Identification, registration, referrals and documentation, family tracing and reunification services for 400 (200female) UASC or families of missing children conducted by CP Case Workers. - Identification of other vulnerable children (including orphans) and children with disability, elaboration of individual case plans, referrals and regular monitoring and follow-up visits by case workers. - Provision of safe interim care arrangements for UASC and orphans and training of foster families on principles of interim care provision and positive parenting skills. - Provision of individual material assistance to the most vulnerable UASC and vulnerable children as per their needs e.g. Clothes, sleeping mat, blanket, soap, and food items.- Provision of structured and unstructured PSS activities in the CFS through the use of recreational items (local games and materials) with a focus on improving children’s wellbeing for 6000 (3000 female) beneficiaries. These activities will be age and gender appropriate with girls and boys of different ages engaging in separate but appropriate activities that allow them to participate, learn and share such as sports, PFA, life skills and vocational training.- Personal hygiene and health promotion activities in the CFS. - Provision of focus on specialized CBPSS and MHPSS activities for adolescents. Activities related to community engagement activities, awareness-raising, and risk mitigation - Establishment and strengthening of community protection mechanisms through community meetings, awareness sessions, FGDs and house-to-house visits including advocacy on the importance of child protection, the rights of the children and signs and symptoms of elevated psychological distress. Establishment and strengthening of CBCPNs. - Workshops with CBCPNs (parents, caregivers, PTA, authorities, and community leaders ) on children’s wellbeing, needs in different development stages, risks and threats in the community, positive parenting skills through active listening and constructive conflict resolution and strategies to prevent family separation. - Awareness events, campaigns and public drama sessions on child protection issues facilitated by children and adolescents (girls and boys) attending the CFS to advocate for children’s right, inclusion of the most vulnerable children and community protection targeting 8000 people. - Initiatives designed and implemented by children and adolescents to improve safety and security in the community and safety mapping exercises. These exercises are e.g. youth vocational training or sports groups that encourage the young boys and girls to engage in positive behaviour and grow their skills in various trades.-Training of staff of on minimum case management SOPS as recently re-launched.-Training the data base officers on CPIMS+ and information management for the UASCS and OVCs including GBV survivors-Training of 24(12 female) PSS facilitators on PFA, Counselling, CFS management, data recording, identification of cases, referral pathway and Information management

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

INTERSOS will re-instate already existing CBCPN, PTA, Local Authority and Non-CP actors to ensure sustainability. INTERSOS will strengthen the referral pathway in two dimensions. One will be community based mechanisms where CBCPN/PTA have the roles of identifying and referring cases to local authority or caseworkers for further action. This ensures the community is well equipped to identify and decide a course of action for the cases that touch on the grave CP grave violations. The second path way is inter-sectoral where INTERSOS will ensure all partners have a documented pathway that can be used to relay information from nutrition, education, wash, non-food items and health actors who are currently on the ground. INTERSOS will ensure the CFS constructed on free hold and public areas and that children can continue to use the spaces during and after the tenure of the project. This will be made possible through acquiring a clearly lease in writing from the local authority and commitment to maintain it for the purpose it is designed for. INTERSOS will ensure the local government MoGCSW social workers are trained and are ready to provide basic services together and in the absence of any NGO case workers

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Yes, INTERSOS ensures that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place during the implementation period. This is achieved through the installation of a mobile complaint box in each location where INTERSOS deploys as well as regular FGDs with beneficiaries about INTERSOS service delivery to understand how they are perceived and what can be improved. Furthermore, there will be internal meetings to understand the community feedback on existing services and additional needs identified by those who closely work with members of the community. INTERSOS will use its internal monitoring and evaluation and control project appraisal tool (PAT) as well as field visits to ensure accountability and guarantee corrective measures are taken timely whenever necessary. The PSS intervention will follow a flexible work-plan, which is constantly revised upon collection of beneficiaries’ inputs. During case management, all case management principles will be respected, particularly the confidentiality of survivors will be respected at all stages in field locations. All persons of interest under case management will be informed about confidentiality and can agree to what extent they wish to give consent about the release of information in case of referrals to third parties. All case files will be kept in a lockable cabinet and no identifiable information will be recorded.
