Project Summary

FAO Somalia will support the humanitarian efforts in Somalia through Enabling Programs to address the impact of flood and drought on vulnerable communities through the use of technology and communication. This project will contribute to the Enabling Programs Cluster’s strategic objectives 2 and 3 by producing humanitarian products including reports and updated data available for public use to inform near-real time, effective programming.
The proposed project objective is to reduce drought and flood impacts through the use of FAO Early Warning information. Currently, Somalia is facing the third consecutive poor rainfall season after two poor rainy seasons in Deyr 2020 and Gu 2021. This is followed by two significant flood events earlier in 2020. With the climate change projections calling for an increase in wet and drier than normal seasons in the country, this pattern of alternating droughts and floods is highly likely to continue taking place in Somalia. Such sequential events have had hazardous impacts, eroding resilience, economic reserves, livestock herd size, and health.
With this in mind, this project proposes to improve the technical capacity for forecasting climate hazards in Somalia. Also, it aims to scale up the use of modernized early warning systems and climate information to save lives and enhance livelihoods in vulnerable communities through expansion of hydro-meteorological monitoring network that will generate data which will be used in turn in the production of early warning information for early action. To sustain this, FAO will develop standard operating procedures (SOPs) together with the disaster management agencies (DMAs) and improve contingency planning based on improved forecasts and warnings. Further SWALIM will develop feedback mechanisms for improving products/services, processes and SOPs.
In Somalia climate change related hazards impacts disproportionately affect poor and vulnerable communities and are compounded by contributing factors such as population increase, unmanaged migration, land-use change, reduced soil health, accelerated groundwater extraction, widespread ecological degradation and biodiversity loss. In 2019, SWALIM established two Information Management Centers (IMCs) in Puntland and Somaliland to support the authorities and communities to adapt to climate change and variability impacts through sustainable watershed management approaches. This project is seeking to support the established IMCs in Puntland and Somaliland in collecting, analyzing and dissemination of land and water resources information. This support will also be extended to the Somali National Bureau of Statistics (SNBS) institution which has been mandated to take over FAO SWALIM activities in the near future.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

FAO has a toll free call center that is open for beneficiaries to anonymously report sexual harassment and any other exploitation cases they may encounter during project implementation. All reported cases are thoroughly investigated and action taken accordingly. The hotline number is disseminated through posters, radio messages and bulk SMS for all beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. Field monitors are also sent out regularly to the target communities to consult with beneficiaries, track progress and evaluate the impact of interventions. FAO also uses the call center to verify that the target beneficiaries identified in the selection process meet the selection criteria. All partners and FAO staff are trained on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and adhere to a strict code of conduct on sexual harassment in all its forms.
