Project Summary

The project aims to address the WASH needs of a targeted total of 69,500 conflict affected populations across Borno and Adamawa States. The overall project objective is to ensure that conflict affected have reduced exposure to WASH related illnesses through improved access to potable drinking water, appropriate/dignified sanitation facilities and strengthened hygiene practices.
Prioritization of interventions will be based on the needs identified and tailored to fit the needs for the disabled, elderly, different age groups and displacement status. The project will address the needs for the internally displaced persons (newly displaced and protracted crisis), returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities. The targeted communities will contribute to the implementation of the project through further needs assessments, contributing to the design, and participation during project monitoring and giving feedback during the project surveys and post distribution monitoring. The project will also be tailored to address the emerging and re-emerging epidemics, related to provision of adequate water, sanitation and hygiene services. This will include responding to such outbreaks such as cholera, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and other related epidemics.
More specifically, the project activities will include the following;
Rehabilitation/upgrading and construction water points. This includes optimization, solarisation, replacing pumps to optimize the capacity of the boreholes, improvement of the water collection points, replacement of taps and old pipes.
Water treatment and water quality monitoring. This includes introduction of inline water chlorination, monitoring of the free residual chlorine levels both at the point of collection and at the household level. Samples will be collected for bacteriological analysis from households that will have zero free residual chlorine levels.
Emergency water distribution/treatment via water trucking (where all other water supply options are not available). This activity shall be limited to a period of not more than 3 months.
Establishment and capacity building of water management committees. Water committees will be equipped with tool boxes in order to undertake minor works
Construction of new latrines and bathing shelters. Priority shall be given to communities who practice open defecation and do not have latrines
Operation and maintenance of latrines, including minor repairs and maintenance, desludging and minor rehabilitations such as replacement of broken doors, handles and some missing part of the super structure
Hygiene promotion, which will be tailored to include Covid 19 specific messages, NRC shall pilot the use of WASH’Em tools for hand washing. Hygiene Promotion messages will be delivered through community based trained hygiene promoters and the information, education and communication materials will be adopted from the sector approved materials.
Distribution of Hygiene Non-Food Items (NFIs), including the installation of hand washing stations. The distribution will be targeted, and will be done through both in kind and the use of vouchers (where markets are functioning). The NFIs will also be adopted from the sector approved kits, which includes family hygiene kits, Covid 19 kits, latrine cleaning kits, cholera kits etc.
Trainings and capacity building, this will be done for community based hygiene promoters, WASH committees, adolescent girls and other targeted trainings for water, sanitation and hygiene staff implementing the project.
The project will target Monguno, Dikwa, Jere, MMC, Gwoza and Damasak Local Government Areas (LGA) of Borno State and Yola North, Yola South and Mubi North LGAs.
The implementation of this project shall be closely coordinated with the relevant government ministry (ministry of water resources), Rural Water and Sanitation agency (RUWASA), UN agencies (UNICEF, IOM), other WASH implementing partners and local Non-Governmental Organizations.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Response Plan for Nigeria 2020-2021, approximately 7.9 million (4.5 million being under the age of 18 years below) are now in need of life-saving aid in 2020; 800,000 more people than in 2019. (Nigeria HRP for Covid -19, 2020). In addition to this humanitarian needs in the accessible areas, most recently the humanitarian community has identified approximately 1,000,000 individuals in hard-to-reach areas with little hope to be reached by humanitarian assistance.In December 2019, NRC conducted a MSNA of living conditions and gaps within formal and informal camp settings in Maiduguri and Jere of Borno State. During the MSNA, NRC identified that only 43% of the respondents reported that all HHs have safe water for drinking, while 50% reported that all HHs have water both for washing and bathing. This underscores the lack of water sources and water not fit for drinking. Regarding safe and dignified latrines, only 36% of respondents report that all HHs have access to facilities, while 34% report that many or all the existing latrines are broken/not functional.NRC conducted a MSNA in at least 12 camps/sites Dikwa in March 2020. The results from the household interviews conducted established that 6% did not have access to latrine, while 40.7% of those who had access to a latrine were communal latrines, At least 18.9% of the household interviewed had evidence of feaces around the living area. On water supply, 73.2% reported not having sufficient containers for water collection while 22.2% did not use any method of water treatment.The MSNA was conducted by REACH in 2019 in both Adamawa and Borno state. The relevant indicators for COVID 19 were highlighted in the two reports reproduced in April 2020. The results from the assessments in Borno indicates that 54% of respondents wash their hands but with water only, 10% not having washed their hands a day prior to data collection, 38% of HHs reported reducing water consumption for cleaning, bathing and washing, 60% of the households with no access to bar soap and 25% having to wait for more than 30 minutes to collect water from the water sources. The results for Adamawa State indicates that that 67% of respondents wash their hands but with water only, 8% not having washed their hands a day prior to data collection, 48% of HHs reported reducing water consumption for cleaning, bathing and washing, 58% of the households with no access to bar soap and 19% having to wait for more than 30 minutes to collect water from the water sources.It is also reported through the UNICEF, 2020 dash board that only 26.5% of the Nigerian population use improved drinking water sources and sanitation facilities with 23.5% defecating in the open, mostly in the rural areas. This report is not only limited to the North East where the project is targeting, but it reflects the situation in Nigeria. Due to the displacements in the North East the situation could be worse that the global reported figure.While we have the ongoing conflict occasioned by activities from the Organized Armed Groups the rainy season resulted to massive flooding affecting a number of areas across the north eastern states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe), with Local Government Areas (LGAs) around Yola, Adamawa State affected the most. In a report described by the UN as ‘’one worst Flooding in 7 Years in Borno and Adamawa’’, more than 100,000 persons and at least 19,000 persons were displaced across 7 LGAs in Adamawa State. This year, there have been destructions of infrastructure as a result of wind storms.In addition to the natural factors, cholera has been endemic in the north eastern Nigeria, with case reported every year for the last 10 years. The total cases however reduced in the year 2019. Adamawa state reported a number of cases in 2019 in three LGAs (Song, Yola North and Yola South) reporting a total cumulative number of 839 positive cases and 4 associated deaths as of 6th December 2019.

2 - Activities

Emergency response: This response focuses on any unforeseen natural disasters, displacements or epidemics and will be implemented jointly with the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The aim is to provide immediate assistance to the affected populations or basic survival before the conventional WASH response. The response will be closely coordinated through the RRM working group. These specific emergency response incudes emergency water trucking, which will only be limited to a period of no more than three months, while looking for other more sustainable water supply options. Response to outbreaks including disinfections, water point treatment, distribution of kits, awareness raising and coordination, provision of communal latrines and rehabilitation/dislodgement of existing ones, distribution of essential NFI hygiene kits that will includes soaps, detergents, water collection containers, menstrual hygiene items amongst other items.WASH response to protracted crisis: The specific WASH response will focus on improving service coverage, building community structures and setting up more sustainable systems beyond the emergency phase. The specific response includes the following; Water Supply: This activity will be conducted and guided by the needs identified through rehabilitation of existing water both functional and non-functional water sources, water point treatment and drilling of new boreholes. NRC will work closely with the state, local government and federal ministries of water resources. For sustainability of the infrastructure provided, NRC will take into consideration the issue of operation and maintenance on all the water facilities provided. To address this, NRC will establish a water user committee in close consultation with the target communities within camps, urban displacements and out of camps. Before handing over the constructed water points, NRC will ensure that water quality tests are conducted, and the results meet the recommended standards for drinking water. Batch chlorination will be conducted at the water distribution points/boreholes. Water quality monitoring will be done at the point of distribution and at point of use to ensure that it is in line with the SPHERE standards.Sanitation: NRC will provide user-friendly and culturally acceptable latrines and bathrooms (shared household latrines, communal latrines or emergency latrines) that are gender-segregated to prevent and mitigate the risk of potential GBV-related incidents, including harassment against women and girls. NRC will work closely with ICLA-HLP colleagues to negotiate for land for latrine construction. All facilities constructed will be equipped with a cleaning kit and community mobilization will be done to ensure proper use and maintenance.Hygiene promotion: Implementation will target behaviour change and prevention and mitigation of epidemics. WASH will lead in the harmonization of messages to be shared, should there be need to jointly disseminate some messages along with the implementation of activities from other core competencies. To address outbreaks, and with the understanding that behaviour change is a process that might take time, the hygiene promotion teams will work directly with the target beneficiaries in defining the health risks and economic burdens associated with these practices rather than disseminating direct messages. Contextualized Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials addressing the key risks will be developed and adopted to reinforce the messages.Hygiene Non Food Items (NFI) Distribution: Supplies to support the adoption of good hygiene practices will also be provided either through in-kind supplies or through the use of cash & vouchers, subject to proper markets assessments. The use of vouchers will be preferred over in-kind as it gives the beneficiaries a variety of choices to purchase the actual items needed and in the desired brands.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming NRC will put in place some key protection mainstreaming elements in this project. NRC will ensure that the proposed intervention provides safety and dignity, and prevents harm to the target beneficiaries. NRC recognises that the individual needs of women, men, girls and boys differ based on the person’s health, age, physical and displacement status, and living conditions. Therefore consultations shall form a key component on project implementation. WASH needs are also different for women and girls compared to men and boys, and also the general protection risks are highly dependent on gender, age, displacement status, ethnicity, geographical area and poverty level. NRC has developed detailed protection guidelines and will deliver trainings to all project staff, to increase staff capacity to identify protection issues and refer accordingly. NRC did map out the protection risks affecting the displaced populations in some locations in Borno will continuously monitor the information throughout the project life. In WASH, some of the common mitigation measures will arise as a result of siting and appropriateness of the WASH facilities. NRC will ensure that r women and girls (mainly charged with the responsibility of fetching water for household chores) are consulted on safety and security concerns, and site the water points to ensure they are close enough to the community to mitigate GBV risks for women and girls. For sanitation, NRC will mark the latrines for use, ensuring clear gender segregation, more applicable in camp-like settings. To further improve on protection, in regards to utilisation of WASH facilities, NRC will install lockable doors in latrines and bathing shelters and equip the beneficiaries with solar lamps for use at night. The latrines will be located near the shelters to mitigate possible cases of sexual harassment. NRC will ensure that an effective complaint and feedback mechanism is in place to receive opinions from beneficiaries and local authorities. A stronger NRC presence on-the-ground will ensure the ability to identify quickly and refer (to other partners) protection concerns through NRC’s field staff. Finally, NRC has established a specific Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) developed for crowd control during distributions, which will reduce the risk of attacks towards beneficiaries. The SOP consists in limiting the number of people during distributions (focusing on the most vulnerable first) and coordination with local authorities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback from the targeted beneficiaries will be implemented throughout the project, addressing the needs for the vulnerable communities. In order to collect feedback, beneficiaries shall be involved at different stages of the project, in which feedback will be collected. It includes the following; - Involving the target communities in the initial needs assessments. The beneficiaries will give feedback to the project design, this will ensure that the program delivery is based on the actual needs assessed and contributions from the community. - Establishment of a community feedback mechanism. This will be done by conducting scheduled meetings with the beneficiaries to receive their feedback on the project. Information provided by the beneficiaries will be used to improve the programme quality, and the complaints and issues will be addressed.- Organizing for Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), the project addresses the needs of different groups and through focus group discussions (FGDs) with men, women, elderly, and people living with disabilities, to collect their feedback on the appropriateness of the project design. - Identification of focal point persons from within the affected communities, such as through the WASH committees who will be the contact persons for the target beneficiaries. Any complaints or concerns about the project can be channelled through them. They will also ensure that the target beneficiaries get their entitlements as per the project description. For example, for the water trucking, community representatives will be required to verify the quantities of water deliveries, along with NRC staff and the water vendors.
