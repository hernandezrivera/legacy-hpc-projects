Project Summary

This project aims to provide humanitarian assistance to refugees and hosting communities under the Strategic Objectives of the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan. IOM will provide multi-sectoral interventions, including Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), Emergency Shelters and Non-Food Items (ES/NFIs), Health, Nutrition, and Livelihoods based activities to respond to the urgent needs of refugees and hosting communities in Sudan. The military conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that commenced on 15 April 2023 has caused increased vulnerabilities of over 900,000 refugees and asylum seekers across Sudan and triggered urban to rural, intra/interstate and cross border population movements. The conflict and subsequent displacement in Sudan are causing refugee crises in the neighbouring countries, putting pressure on already limited resources in often remote areas already facing their own crisis. IOM will focus on providing multi-sectoral assistance in South and North Darfur, Blue Nile, White Nile, Kassala, and Gedaref, where numerous instances of internal displacement and a sharp increase in the number of refugee and migrant movements have been reported in 2024 due to the crisis. The activities will target an estimated 213,350 refugees and host community members in vulnerable communities. The proposed WASH activities will increase access to safe drinking water, adequate quality sanitation facilities, and improve hygiene practices (particularly through rehabilitation of water yards, installation of communal latrines, distribution of hygiene material and sanitary materials to women, among others). The proposed ES/NFI interventions including distribution of shelter and NFI kits as well as cash support for NFIs not included in the standard kits will be used to improve living conditions for vulnerable refugees and host communities that recieve a large influx of displaced populations and offer protection to vulnerable people from the harsh elements. The proposed health activities will focus on delivering a basic package of primary health services (BPPHS) including mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and nutrition services integrated to BPPHS, to improve access to services, reduce risks associated with morbidity and mortality, and support more favorable protection outcomes. The livelihood activities will seek to provide income generating support to the most vulnerable, notably through vocational and skills training and conducting adapted activities for the youth. This project will focus on targeting refugees and hosting communities to mitigate the risks of tensions over scarce resources and increase the capacity of hosting communities to absorb population influxes. However, lifesaving assistance will be prioritized for new arrivals and vulnerable groups including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities or other vulnerabilities to ensure that the most urgent humanitarian and protection needs are addressed.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project activities will be designed in a way to support women, children, and people with disabilities. Special attention will be paid to women, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), and children most vulnerable to illness and severely lacking in access to basic services and support needed. Consultative participatory approaches will be employed to allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities such as in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability, especially for the distribution of the Menstrual Hygiene Management kits. IOM will undertake participatory assessments to identify gender and age specific needs and context specific risks of different groups, especially women and girls, related to access and safety around service provision. This inclusive participatory approach assures the inclusion of ideas from vulnerable women and girls, and people with disabilities, who are often excluded from community decisions or underrepresented during decision making processes, especially with regard to selecting the location for the rehabilitation of WASH facilities and shelter sites. The facilities themselves will be built with gender sensitivity, accessibility, privacy, and safety in mind considering protection risks are addressed and mitigated and chosen locations in nearby, well-lit areas with ramps and railings, and locks on the doors of the latrines and bathing facilities and for water points to decrease walking distances to reduce associated risks of gender-based violence (GBV) especially for women and children. Locations of shelter structures will also be chosen through community consultations and built in areas where there is access to other basic services. Based on need assessments, IOM will ensure that the most vulnerable households with specific needs including women, children, persons with disabilities, will be prioritized to receive emergency shelters. During the construction of the shelters the most vulnerable will receive additional support to assemble emergency shelters. Privacy and safety standards will be a top priority for the shelter construction activities especially emphasizing construction techniques and training of community on disaster risk reduction measures that address fire and flood related risk to the shelters. Health services will provide support to the most vulnerable beneficiaries but also wider host community which will reduce community tensions over resources. Pregnant women will be prioritized for assistance including antenatal care, skilled deliveries, and postnatal care. Livelihood assistance will prioritize support for women, female headed households, and youth for material and technical inputs.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

In the current context, IOM will be prioritizing the use of Communicating with Communities (CwC) to ensure that there is a continuous process of listening, responding, and adapting programs based on the needs of the community and factor in conflict sensitivity. The response will prioritize delivery of critical protection and multisector life-saving assistance to refugees living in camps, in urban and peri-urban areas and vulnerable members of the host community. Using a participatory approach to promote the role of women, men, girls, and boys of all ages inclusive of underrepresented groups such as persons with disabilities and special needs to plan and monitor the delivery of assistance. Using community-based mechanisms such as empowering community health workers and hygiene promoters, establishing community committees and peer groups to support the participation in decision making processes. The committees and other community-based mechanisms will also be an integral part of reporting on assistance provided highlighting the ways in which assistance has been provided in an integrated way especially where multiple services are being provided to identify further opportunities for synergies or gaps in services that still need to be covered.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Under this project, consultative participatory approaches employed will allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender, age, disability, and vulnerability. The participatory approach assures the inclusion of beneficiaries and the available avenues of feedback and complaint mechanisms, which IOM and its partners can use to address activity design flaws or other issues. For instance, individualized interviews with vulnerable beneficiaries especially women and girls during certain activities like PDM missions opens communication channels for beneficiaries to discuss topics they may not want to discuss in a group setting. IOM is part of the inter-agency coordination groups and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms as agreed by the inter-agency groups. The project will ensure safe and accessible communication channels are available to the project participants, stakeholders, and partners to facilitate feedback and complaints, including on SEA. Where there are no active protection partners at the site level, the project will facilitate joint advocacy through the Protection Cluster to establish linkages with protection services and referral pathways. Beneficiary feedback will be collected periodically at three critical stages of the provision of Multisector assistance: (1) During assessments information will be collected about the selection process, beneficiary criteria, and additional beneficiary needs. (2) During activity implementation and awareness raising sessions, feedback and complaints will be collected from beneficiaries through questionnaires about the selection process and report additional beneficiary needs. (3) During M&E missions information and complaints will be collected for beneficiaries through questionnaires about support provided. There will be follow-ups at each of these stages to ascertain if beneficiary complaints were addressed either by IOM or by partners. This will improve the feedback mechanisms and allow engagement with the local community on their feedback and addressing their issues in coordination with other agencies who may have provided additional direct support or referrals to other relevant service providers. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA, as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to handle a GBV disclosure and make safe referrals through existing referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance.
