Project Summary

The Syrian crisis has weakened intensely the capacity of the education system to address basic education needs. According to the Ministry of Education (MoE) over 120,000 teachers required capacity development, an estimated 180,000 educational personnel have left the education system and around 5,722 schools are either damaged or destroyed which has caused an acute need for additional safe learning spaces across the country affecting 2.45 million out of school. Education needs and gaps are also compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic which resulted to children drop out and a setback in learning.
The project aims to enhance equitably and quality access to education to crisis affected IDPs, returnees and vulnerable children aged between 5-17 years. Several barriers prohibit children from being in school. Schools are destroyed or unsafe, some children feel unsafe on the way to school or while in school due to lack of electricity especially for the second shift students, families have had to displace to areas where schools are no longer available; as a result schools in safe areas are overcrowded and tensions between host community and those who were displaced have increased, lack of documentation prevent children from accessing primary and/or secondary education in which case, children may be exposed to higher risks of child labour, early marriage and social protection. Beyond that, the socio-economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic combined with the currency depreciation has placed additional challenges for children in accessing education imposing children to work to support their families and families’ ability to afford school expenses such as school uniform, stationery, and transportation continue to be a key reason for children are not attending school.
UNHCR education intervention includes the provision of non-formal education in community centres. The non-formal education activities will enhance the school enrolment of out of school children and the retention of children with low school performance. School rehabilitation will be very limited as a last resort and part of a comprehensive intervention in priority return areas. UNHCR pays specific attention to the future adolescents and youth populations that can contribute to resilience and social cohesion in their communities through facilitating their access to education. In line to age gender and diversity, accountability to affected population and rights-based approaches, UNHCR will reach out to children and their parents during project monitoring activities and through focus group discussion seeking feedback including receipt of complaints regarding the project. Resources permit, UNHCR plans to provide non-formal education activities to over 185,000 children and rehabilitation of around 150 classrooms.

3 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria has hindered children from accessing and enjoying their full rights to education. According to the 2022 HNO 6.6 M children/youth are in need of education. Economic factors, including children working to support households, are key factors for children not attending school. 18% of children are out of school and half of the children are not in school have never enrolled. The formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school age children. Many students continue to learn in unconducive and/or unsafe learning environment. Existing services are unable to physically accommodate or meet the different learning needs of IDPs, returnees children including those with specific needs. Limited availability non-formal education services continues to reduce the opportunities for children to return to learning at age appropriate levels.UNHCR believes that children with access to education are less exposed to harmful negative coping mechanisms such as early marriage, child labour and other forms of exploitation including recruitment by armed groups. The routine and milestones of going through the education process offers children stability and a sense of normalcy. Socialization effects provided by the interaction with other children is essential component for reintergration process. Access to education give children and their parents’ perspective for the future as it has direct impact in the capacity of families to generate income and plan for the future.

4 - Implementation of activities

Education Iinterventions will be line with the “No Lost Generation ”initiative launched in 2013. With over 2.45 million children and youth out of school. Concentrated and joint efforts are essential to mitigate additional dropouts and ensure out-of-school children are back to school. UNHCR will participate in the EWG to coordinate education interventions and will respond to the identified needs with due consideration of security and resource limitations. The following education interventions builds on the priorities and the identified needs of the Education Sector. Education interventions will include non-formal education programmes such as remedial classes, catch up classes and education activities during summer for all children including those with disabilities. Activities will take place in UNHCR community centers and education facilities. Interventions will adhere to the MoE and Education Sector guidelines. School rehabilitation will be very limited as a last resort and as part of comprehensive intervention in a priority return area. Access to persons with disability will be taken into consideration in school rehabilation and UNHCR will coordinate with the WASH and Shelter Sectors and ensure that the rehabilated schools have WASH facilities in place for both gender and are disability friendly to safeguard the protection of boys and girls. Monitoring of the activities will be conducted through field visits, partners' monthly reports, and Partners Reporting Monitoring tool

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Inaccessibility to education due to lack of safety and insecurity including risks of explosives and mines. Mitigating measures: Ensure flexible and context informed mechanisms to adjust to a changing context. Ensure that relevant authorities are aware of and are engaged with the projects and are committed to ensure basic safety/ access for project implementation, coordinate with the Mine Action Working Group to ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security measures including in mine risk education. Risk 2: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools. Mitigating measures: Close coordination with local education authorities, community leaders and school management resulting in clear agreements on every actors' responsibility to create and maintain a safe learning environment. Regular monitoring to verify adherence to these agreements.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Inter-Country Network (ICN) in Syria. UNHCR will be co-funding the PSEA Coordinators position in 2023 to strengthen PSEA coordination and implementation of the ICN work plan. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV and Child Protection Sub-Working Groups and PSEA network, to ensure adequate and multifaceted prevention and response mechanisms are in place regarding the “do no harm” principle and that SEA victims received the requisite support through the victim centered approach. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR and UNHCR will work with all partners to ensure mechanisms are in place to address SEA concerns. In addition to delivering PSEA awareness-raising activities in all UNHCR community centres, PSEA mandatory training and annual refresher sessions are compulsory to UNHCR own and partners’ staff. Complaint and feedback mechanisms, including hotline numbers, email addresses, and complaint boxes, are functional -at the agency and partner’s levels- to ensure and support a confidential and effective reporting system. Since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partner capacity improvement plans have been developed for the partners scored medium and high risk, and UNHCR will be supporting and monitoring the improvement of partners in addressing SEA. Partners who fail to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 2: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply. Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price and reduced target. Risk 3: Threat of military offensives and disturbance in safety and security of some project areas or changes of security situation restricting implementation. Mitigating measures: For the priority limited school rehabilitation activities prioritize activities in safe and accessible locations. Ensure that projects are in insecure locations, if any, have security plans for staff and the project beneficiaries have sufficient resources to consent to those plans.
