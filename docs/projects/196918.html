Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to support the livestock sector and livelihoods (the productive value chain), through two interrelated activities (interrelated) and thus will work to achieve the productive value chain that has an integrated and continuous positive impact on the individual and society, which supports the resilience of breeders in the first degree.
Where the organization will work through this project to support livestock breeders(who are at risk of stopping work or selling assets) by providing them with concentrated feed and veterinary care, it will target 180 breeders of cows and 190 sheep breeders with compound fodder for a continuous period of 3 rounds,
The organization will target one cow for each breeder, provided that the breeder has no more than two cows
It will also target 10 female sheep for each female sheep breeder, provided that the herd does not exceed 15 female sheep
The daily average of fodder to be distributed to each of the selected cows will be 10 kg., 300 kg. in each month for 3 successive months.
As for the sheep, the daily average of fodder to be distributed to each of the selected sheep will 0.7 kg, 210 kg each month for 3 successive months.
This will lead to the production of high-quality fresh milk that will be healthy and thus the production of high-quality dairy products. In addition, the milk production rate will be doubled from 15 kg to 25 kg per day
The organization will also seek to achieve the value chain by establishing two centres equipped with the best equipment for dairy production and its derivatives in each of the Killi and Hazano regions, where it will do the following:
32 women (families led by a woman) will be supported as direct beneficiaries by setting up two dairy and milk derivatives centres that will secure a good financial income for their family, which will increase their resilience in the target community,

3 - Needs

Syria faces a multitude of challenges that make it one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies. According to (HNO, 2023) 12.1M people are estimated to be food insecure. Over 2M people live in informal settlements and planned camps, a 5% increase since 2021. Moreover, 85% of households are unable to meet basic food needs in 2023. The decline in agricultural production output was not only limited to rainfed food production systems but also to irrigated systems as cereals, legumes, barley, and vegetables, indications are that wheat production for 2022 was generally lower than LTA, especially due to the failure of most rainfed planted areas. As many as 77 per cent of households reported buying less expensive and less preferred foods resulting in poorer diets (63 per cent with low and medium diets). Lack of employment and income opportunities continues, with the burden driven by lack of purchase power which creates an increase in household food insecurity and difficulties to secure minimum food needs.Livestock hasn’t also been spared from the impact of prevailing water shortage & scarcity, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed, and the generally unfavorable weather conditions. The unfavorable 2021 – 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development, increased demand for fodder, limited supply, high prices, The worsening economic crisis affecting Syria has also resulted in less efficient animal health services.

4 - Implementation of activities

Sewar will buy the milk from some of the beneficiaries at the market prices and will be distributed to dairy processing centres that will be established through this project in which skilled women will be selected to conduct the dairy processing activities so they can have decent and sustainable income-generating activities. This will ultimately improve the community resilience and local economy and will enhance the food security situation by increasing the availability and affordability of the dairy food. Sewar will select 180 cows’ breeders and 190 sheep’ breeders to be supported with fodder and veterinary care for 3 months, Sewar’s veterinarians will be responsible to conduct regular visits to the beneficiaries to follow up with the overall health situation of their cattle and provided the needed vaccines, those veterinarians have extensive experience in implementing similar projects.The compound fodder that will be distributed to the beneficiaries’ cattle will be determined by the veterinarians to make sure that the needed protein, vitamins, and minerals are provided to secure the needed amount of milk. The healthcare follow-up to the beneficiaries’ cattle will be conducted twice a week to improve their health situation.Sewar seeks to establish a Governance Committee which will lead the centres jointly with Sewar during the project, this committee will be responsible to run the centres after the project ends.
