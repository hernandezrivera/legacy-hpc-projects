Project Summary

The project aims to address the vulnerability of the many out of school children in the city of Bamenda, some of who are war orphans and unaccompanied child IDPs. The five years long conflict in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon has rendered more than 700,000 children out of school and school boycott continues to be enforced by armed groups. There is an acute shortage of organized vocational training facilities in the region especially as the sector has remained unorganized. Existing trainers are profit oriented business individual who have very limited ability to manage the psychosocial and mental health needs of children and young people who have experienced violence and other forms of abuses. This limitation in credible stabilization facilities has kept of children in the region exposed to recruitment by armed gangs, trafficking and exploitation and engagement in other criminal activities including drugs
By establishing a vocational training centre in Bamenda the vulnerability of out of school young people including single adolescent mothers will be addressed in through three job creating industries with a high potential for growth, namely: Sewing and Design, Hairdressing and make-up and Lather works. The centre will provide tailored training to trainees in combination with other opportunities for soft skills development. Trainees shall be empowered and engaged as frontline respondent on child abuses and other GBV incidents.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The establishment of a customized vocational training center to meet the hard skills and soft skills needs of out of school children impacted by the conflict in the North West and South West of Cameroon is an initiative of the Global Welfare Association (GLOWA) which aims to transform the way children with limited parental care are treated in Cameroon in general and the Anglophone regions in particular. The center shall be a hub for entrepreneurship and development which will use modules customized for the needs of vulnerable children to promote the transition from isolated rehabilitation delivered by external agents to a team-based holistic and participatory system of rehabilitation. It will help participants to overcome gaps in their development through formal, informal and vocational education and work to involve communities in the rehabilitation process within a family-based participatory personal recovery system. The project shall provide sustainable solutions to the multiple problems faced by victims of armed conflict through the creation of a one-stop facility for recovery, rehabilitation and family support programs. The facility will reduce the likelihood for out-of-school youths of joining criminal gangs or taking part in prostitution or similar behaviours, through an active engagement in vocational training, community service activities and the sustainable reintegration of participants in community life. The proposed one stop facility shall be a transformation of the present vocational training by placement to a one-stop facility having customized rehabilitation modules for educating girls 20 years old and younger. The customized modules will create a fresh start for program participants through learning practical skills such as sewing, encouraging creativity and entrepreneurship, and earning an income. Program participants will be empowered and supported to engage in community counter activities to fight child abuse thereby contributing to break the silence that generally characterizes victims and survivors of gender based violence. They shall graduate as skilled professionals able to break the cycle of poverty and prevent the undesirable recurring patterns of trafficking and abuse.
