Project Summary

Through this project, the Community Communications and Engagement (CCE) working group will be strengthen to consistently advise the ICWG and HCT on CCE and AAP will be embedded in each national level clusters.
The main goal for this project is to mainstream and implement the adoption of a coordinated CCE best practice plan for the entire cluster system in South Sudan through information sharing and the use of standardized community engagement activities. The objectives are based on the need to mainstream CwC and AAP as developed in the previous 2018 HRP plan, these needs have been further developed by the Community Communications and Engagement Working Group. This highlights the need and desire amongst participating parties for deeper cooperation between implementing partners and clusters to share best practice and pool experience in both a nationwide and local context, especially considering the rise in the use of protests as a form of engagement by host and IDP populations. By drawing on a shared CCE expertise to ease and more effectively implement activities and forewarn and forestall issues it will be possible to increase the ability of humanitarian organizations to respond to the actual needs of the population and it will change their attitude towards humanitarian agencies. CCE will also support a humanitarian response based on respect for the dignity of communities affected by civil conflict in South Sudan.

Needs

Internews is drawing from the experience of the past five years working on Community Engagement to and from IDP’s and refugees in South Sudan using both AAP and CwC techniques. The IAHE report of the response to the crisis in South Sudan highlighted how there is limited awareness of AAP as an IASC policy priority or in terms of IASC documents guiding practice in this area. Despite strong interest in CwC and AAP by the HCT and ICWG, presentations to cluster groups by the CCE WG found that many partners still felt that they needed a lot more support in how to include a CCE element to their response and that it was not a priority during an emergency, but rather a ‘nice’ activity to carry out at a later stage of the response. The recent events in Maban PoC as well as protests in the Bentiu, Juba and Malakal PoC where, as a result, activities have been suspended or withdrawn completely, demonstrates how proper engagement with respect for all can help mitigate against or minimise such events occurring. Our experience of working in PoCs across South Sudan has shown that traditional engagement with established camp leadership needs to be supplemented with a wider CwC based approach encompassing more of the community. Traditional leaderships within the POCs are largely male dominated groups which may not represent the views of the wider community and which often do not effectively relay information back to their communities. Ongoing leadership struggles add to the rumour, conjecture and often unrest in all of the sites, with the various groups then becoming increasingly political. While this need to develop better tools and practices has been recognized, several staff from different agencies said they lacked any practical guidance on how to go about this, including training or direct support and advice from their organizations. There are few CCE champions within the South Sudan response who have time or resources to embed this critical activity; such champions might help to establish a culture of engagement and accountability with the community. Within the response, CwC/AAP is still seen as largely about passing on information, often one way, from agencies to communities, although there are also some strong examples of two-way communication and face to face interactions these are rare occurrences, and often only conducted by Internews on individual projects. What is lacking is a team to maintain CCE in a formal, cross-cutting and consistent way at the inter agency or cluster level locally and nationally, which would affect the way clusters are fulfilling their own duties to Community Engagement and Accountability to Affected Populations. Greater and more consistent attention to CCE can strongly improve the quality of the response in the future. Involvement of affected people in all stages of the response project cycle is still minimal and their expressed priorities (e.g. for education) often overlooked. It is critical for the humanitarian community in South Sudan to promote good practice in CCE though a formal network of CCE focal points or champions in clusters and in key hubs, who are able to document, share and promote good practices. The 2018 and 2019 HRP notes the need to scale up and embed good practice in CCE, which is exemplified by the work of Internews. Agencies and aid workers at all levels need assistance to move beyond information collection and sporadic cooperation in CCE activities towards embracing the full necessity and benefits of a commitment to community engagement. This is possible by focusing on the participation of affected people during the program cycle, with the creation of dedicated support to build capacity and integrate AAP into all clusters through training and mentoring.

Activities

While each agency and organization may have its own system for measuring and implementing community engagement, a proper strategy at the cluster level has not been drafted yet for South Sudan. This project will work towards ensuring that multi-sectoral partners develop more strategic, well-coordinated CwC they work with – giving them the technical support, and practical experience to deliver this effectively, ensuring a quality, needs based and accountable response to the affected population, with a particular focus on ensuring the needs of marginalized groups are met. Activity 1: Supporting the embedding of AAP into clustersThis project allow AAP to be embedded in all cluster strategy and activities. In addition, Internews will relay the feedbacks collected in humanitarian and CCE activities locations – Juba, Bentiu (PoC and BBR), Malakal and Wau including planed mobile response locations around the current locations. Internews currently gathers hundreds of feedbacks on humanitarian concerns per day in Juba, Bentiu, Malakal and Wau. Those feedbacks are relayed to the state level clusters but not yet to the national level. The new four Cluster Focal Point (FP) working on this project will cover all the national level clusters. There will be an AAP Cluster FP will contribute to cluster meetings and work with the cluster leads to support the embedding of AAP into cluster related activities and strategies moving forward. Activity 2: Strengthening the CCE WG The project will strengthen the CCW working group (WG) and increase its contribution to the ICWG by ensuring a dedicated staff to the WG that will focus on mobilizing the WG members, advising the ICWG, producing CCE assessment tools and analysis and mainstreaming CCE in new ICWG initiatives. The role of the CCE WG Coordinator will be to act as the lead and primary support for the working group to fulfil its mandate to development CWC and AAP methodology. The coordinator will also play a pivotal role in linking the advancement of CCE techniques with mainstreaming practices to the clusters in collaboration with the AAP focal points. E.g. assisting the development of CCE tools to inform all phases of the project cycle across all sectors. Activity 3: Mentoring and advising on CCE The FP and the CCE WG Coordinator will mentor and advise cluster members on specific organizations’ strategies for the implementation of CCE. This activity will be used for the design of a nationwide CCE Common Service Model project that will be implemented through existing networks and coalitions of humanitarian partners at sub national, national and international levels. The use of the model for CCE will ensure that we are responding to the needs of the community, providing them the information they need to make better informed decisions, informing programming with communities’ feedback, and building the trust needed to efficiently implement humanitarian operations on the ground. Activity 4: Monitoring and reportingThe M&E department of Internews will offer data analysis to all partners willing to contribute to regular feedback collection and rumour tracking in community engagement projects. Monthly targets will be set to ensure broad representation of the whole community including vulnerable groups. CCE focal points will also embark on a further measure to conduct a rapid information needs assessment on a biannual basis to measure the beneficiaries’ perceptions of the impact of humanitarian communication across all clusters and a rapid impact assessment with partners regarding the improvement of CCE activities following focal point and working group support.The CCE cluster focal points report on CCE to the ICWG on a monthly basis and to the HCT on a quarterly basis. Every month Internews will create one CCE summary for the ICWG that will be presented to all cluster leads in order to report on the status of CCE implementation. Recommendations will also be sent to the HCT on a quarterly basis

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Information can be lifesaving and life enhancing – critically so in an emergency. Without information and good communications, communities affected by an emergency (particularly the poorest and most socially excluded) are likely to be unable to access services and claim their basic rights, or worse they may be missed by service providers, leading to negative consequences. The Communities Communications and Engagement (CCE) approach that Internews uses is essential for humanitarian intervention to be effective; organizations have a wealth of information to share in an emergency - they spend millions of dollars in activities designed to change knowledge, attitudes and practice, and these CCE programs are essential to build community engagement, mobilization and ensure they take place with the entire community, rather than just with leadership groups. Over the course of the past five years Internews has delivered more than 400 hours of humanitarian content informing communities about the humanitarian response provided. Equally, agencies need information from local communities in order to effectively target their work, improve the impact of their interventions, deliver transparency and accountability to communities, and respond to and meet the real needs of people. Strengthened communications with communities means better accountability to communities. Strengthening humanitarian agencies’ CCE strategy and tools ensures actionable and useful information is delivered in a timely, reliable and useful way. Following the implementation of Internews’ Humanitarian Information Service in South Sudan, the organisation developed it’s own approach for CCE and AAP.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In POC sites, such as Malakal, Internews has been working with other agencies that focus on youth and women’s programming to help alleviate the barriers to access to information experienced by these groups. Internews will rely on its established relationships with existing organizations working with adolescents and women in the POC and harness its existing youth and women listening groups to discuss who and how people should be engaged in the project. Internews’ CCE project will be leveraging on the already established Humanitarian Information Service (HIS) projects across South Sudan, that work to deliver information to those who are excluded from traditional mechanisms e.g., women, youth, children - due to cultural, political and social barriers. The project has evolved to have a stronger community centred communication focus that puts people’s actual need for information at the heart of conversations. The project will make a conscious effort to recruit women and young people.

All Internews staff will be trained in awareness towards sexual exploitation, its prevention and will be required to undergo compulsory anti-harassment training on an annual basis. Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Involving those who are excluded from traditional mechanisms is a strength of Internews’ Humanitarian Information Service (HIS) projects in South Sudan. This project will strengthen humanitarian information flows – providing a platform between the clusters and communities to directly empower affected communities, particularly reaching the most vulnerable populations. Internews projects in South Sudan, via its radio stations and BBTT project talks to more than 7000 people a year, face to face, of which less than 20% come from community leaders and usual constituencies.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Internews has led in the area of AAP through the implementation of its Humanitarian Information Services set up specifically following this conflict in South Sudan. The HIS has set up an independent communications feedback loop using more than 200 listening groups across 4 POCs, thus providing a voice for communities and a platform for discussion between all actors. On a yearly basis Internews collects more than 4000 complaints and feedback, using almost 100% of them to inform agencies about services adjustments, or to provide more information to IDPs about specific issues. On average around 60% of the feedback reported to humanitarian organizations by Internews is resolved within a month. The services actively support agencies to deliver with broader impact; reaching those without access to information and allowing them to know what services are available, who is providing them, and, in the absence of these services or a shortfall in standards, who to talk to. This mechanism has significantly increased trust between agencies and affected communities. It is the intention of Internews that the project being proposed would use this approach to support a better response at the macro-level.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The Internews approach to accountability is local and engaging. It uses multiple continuous channels of communication, and puts the community’s need for information at the centre of our conversations. It ensures a two-way dialogue and wider representation for people affected by the crisis. Not only is there an exchange of information, but agencies are assisted to tailor their interventions based on the feedback they receive from all communities in an affected area. The conversation shifts from an NGO-led need to share information and a camp leadership claim to represent a majority, to an information exchange driven by all peoples’ need for information. Our 5 Star CWC Rating tool, shared with the cluster and the CCE WG of NGO’s in South Sudan, helps to move CCE/AAP/CWC strategies from an ‘NGO conversation’ to a ‘Conversation with Communities’ using multiple channels of communication that reinforce each other and adapt their programs to the reality on the ground, strengthening the impact of their interventions. To support skills training, Internews has AAP/CWC technical advisors to train in the implantation of this tool and to build the capacity of partner NGOs operating in the various humanitarian contexts. Internews has led advocacy and coordination amongst NGOs and UN agencies on CWC and accountability to communities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project is based on continuous assessments of the protection risk posed to the most vulnerable groups in society due to a lack of access to information to make informed decisions and a lack of access to decision makers to inform their provision of humanitarian assistance. On a weekly basis Internews collects an average of 20 feedback related to protection topics which accounts for 12% of overall feedback.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project aims at supporting humanitarian partners to better communicate and engage the communities they are serving. In doing so, barriers to access information is a primary concern of Internews. As it has been demonstrated in this project proposal, which sets out to continuously assess access to information and access to humanitarian decision makers beyond the voices of existing camp leadership structures.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Internews's projects in the last years have demonstrated how when people have relevant, accurate and timely information, tensions are reduced. CCCM and camp managers in Bentiu, Malakal and Juba asked the programs to be played during times when tensions were likely to be high such as during food distribution and registration. This was evidenced numerous times including for registration processes when the projects worked closely with agencies to deliver information in simple colloquial language that demonstrated peoples 'rights' and built trust - helping to avoid a 'them and us' mentality.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project is aimed at strengthening the cluster system which is indirectly benefiting affected community members. This will be done using tried and tested Internews methodology which continues to address inequalities in the field. In addition the collection of feedback data by Internews is highly valued by protection partners as it provides a source of continuous information about protection needs on the ground that may not be available any other way. Community Correspondents are given conflict sensitivity reporting training and understand the importance of sharing all sides of a story to come to a fair report of the situation on the ground. The project operates through a do no harm lens and correspondents are given training to help them see themselves as mediators in the site.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

It is the intention of Internews to conduct rapid information needs assessments on a minimum of a biannual basis to assess access to beneficiaries within PoC in which the organisation already works and to enable partners to do the same across all sectors. By actively canvassing the opinion of people outside of the current structures of camp leadership, Internews seeks to understand the barriers to access to information of the most vulnerable and often unseen sections of the community. This will be done by setting monthly targets of communicating with at least 40% women, 5% people over the age of 60 and by conducting at least 2 focus groups with special interest groups across all locations. In addition the CCE coordinator and focal points will seek to embed evaluation mechanisms in all CCE initiatives implemented by partners through the clusters. By implementing the Internews 5 Star rating system in the design project design phase of cluster projects ensuring access to information can be monitored on a continuous basis.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The nature of this project is to indirectly benefit beneficiaries through capacity building within the clusters. Underpinning the work of both the working group and the CCE focal points is Internews 5 Star Rating system. Internews identifies the most dangerous risk when conducting CCE activities, against which to mitigate, to be negative behaviour or poorly informed beneficiary decision making as a consequence of poor communication, misinformation and unequal provision of communication channels. By mainstreaming tried and tested methodology from both Internews and through the work of the CCE working group, this project will support information provision across the sectors and put in place impact monitoring controls to allow for course correction.This will be done by CCE focal points having continuous access to Internews listening groups, which monitor the impact of humanitarian information provision on beneficiaries, including the comprehension of the information by the listeners on a weekly basis. By continuing to survey beyond current structures of camp leadership Internews will mitigate against the risk of over-representing the views of some groups while more vulnerable sections of the population go unheard. This will be done by setting monthly targets of communicating with at least 40% women, 5% people over the age of 60 and by conducting at least 2 focus group with special interest groups across all locations. CCE focal points will also embark on a further measure to conduct a rapid information needs assessment on a biannual basis to measure the beneficiaries’ perceptions of the impact of humanitarian communication across all clusters. In addition to this, Internews continues to contribute to an ongoing beneficiary satisfaction survey through the CCCM cluster which measures the safety concerns of beneficiaries living within the PoC.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The underpinning motivation for AAP activities is respect for the dignity of beneficiaries. Through this proposal Internews endeavours to mainstream AAP and CCE methodologies across all clusters thereby improving humanitarian accountability to their beneficiaries at all stages of the project cycle. By continuing to contribute to an ongoing beneficiary satisfaction surveys through the CCCM cluster Internews can monitor beneficiaries’ perceptions of how the level of information provision and representations is meeting their needs in the PoC. CCE focal points will also enable partners working in both camp based and non-camp settings to monitor the effectiveness of their communications and engagement with affected communities.
