Project Summary


This project will support 500 farmers in Al Rouge plain with wheat and vegetables cultivation. It has two phases, the first phase will target 100 beneficiaries with autumn vegetable kits and each beneficiary will receive inputs such as seed, fertilizer, pesticide, sprayer, fuel cost and tools to cultivate 2 donums of Autumn vegetables. Beans and cucumbers are selected as the first batch due to its production cycle suitable in Autumn, and its high marketability.
The second phase will target 400 beneficiaries with wheat kits, by distributing wheat seed, cultivation cost, fertilizer, irrigation cost, and pesticide for each beneficiary.
Both Vegetables and wheat beneficiaries will be trained on Good agricultural practices to increase the maximize the benefits from the resources and reserve these resources.
This project aims to generate income to the BNs and increase the diversity of the foods in the market and secure sustainability for the BNs’ livelihoods.

Needs

The crisis in Syria has entered its eighth year with the living conditions deteriorating over these years of war. Prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity led to limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion of the most vulnerable populations. According to 2019 HNO, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. unfortunately, lack of rain during 2018 made the situation of farmers even worse and affected the production of wheat considerably, "The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production during the 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with a shortfall of 1.2 million metric tonnes of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes". HNO 2019. HIHFAD carried out a needs assessment in Al Rouge plain where it had already rehabilitated a water canal of 9K,. the result of survey shows that farmers are unable to cultivate their lands due to the expensive prices of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizers, fuel for irrigation). However, 64% of the interviewed farmers are willing to plant wheat this year if necessary assistance provided. Once vegetable/wheat production starts and harvest succeeds, the participating farmers are able to save vegetable/wheat seeds for the next production cycle, and they are able to pay some running costs of farming such as water tariff, equipment rent by themselves.

Implementation of activities

The project will support those experiences farmers with starter kits and operation costs, until they get the first harvest, and able to regain their self-sustainability.The project will adapt phased approaches, considering the seasonal factors, crop production cycle and farmers self-reliance.Phase I: August – December 2019 (Autumn vegetable production)Phase II: October 2019 – July 2020 (Wheat production)The assistance will target the local communities according to eligibility criteria to receive humanitarian aid (informed before registration) including the host community families. The selection criteria will include experienced people with agriculture, especially wheat crops farmers who have background in the agricultural activities, also the farmers who are not able to afford basic agriculture services on their own, and families who didn’t receive previous or current Agriculture assistance from other NGOs.The vulnerable households (women-headed households, families which includes pregnant women, persons with disabilities, new born infants and families with large number of members) will be prioritized during beneficiary selection. Activity 1 Supporting 100 farmers with autumn vegetable kitsHIHFAD will target 100 beneficiaries with autumn vegetable kits and each beneficiary will receive items and quantities to cultivate 2 donums of Autumn vegetables as described in annex 1. Beans and cucumbers are selected as the first batch due to its production cycle suitable in Autumn, and its high marketability.In mid of August 2019 HIHFAD team will start the selection of beneficiaries according to the agreed criteria, then will conduct training regarding the beans and cucumbers agriculture guidance including the expected diseases with the preferred way to avoid it targeting all of the beneficiaries from this phase.Once farming starts, the field team will visit farmers and give them guidance and advises to apply good agricultural practice. during this stage of the crop’s life cycle HIHFAD field team will visit the farmers’ field and check them from the insects and fungus diseases, then if there is guideline on how to fight disease and the recommended dosages of the pesticide will be provided. After 50 days of the planting the frames are expected to start collecting the production and sell it in the marketActivity 2. Support 400 farmers with wheat kits400 farmers will be supported to cultivate 4000 hectares of wheat (each farmer 1 hectare). Wheat cultivation will start in mid of October 2019 by conducting needs assessment and sign MoU with the local Councils and beneficiaries, then move up to the beneficiary’s selection process according to the criteria selection the MEAL team will filter the beneficiaries based on the scoring system program aligns to the criteria.HIHFAD will distribute Wheat crop inputs to the beneficiaries and will visit them to make sure that the crop was planted, guidances and tips on Good Agricultural Practices will be given. The harvest is expected to start on 10th of June 2020 and continue to the end of June, Wheat kits include provision of petty cash in order to rent a machine for harvesting and to buy fuel for water irrigation. Once they have the first harvest, they will save a part of their incomes of those running costs for the next cycle.Activity 3. Train 500 farmers on good agriculture practice and support Water User Association (WUA) on the project related activities Members of local councils and beneficiaries will be trained. There is an agriculture focal point within the local council, Certain activities need to be facilitated by the WUA, such as release of irrigation water into the channels. Coordination and supports from the HIHFAD staff will be given to WUA in order to facilitate timely actions from the WUA.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures•Risk: Low rainfall during winter for Wheat production. Mitigation: Provide a complementary irrigation. •Aggression from other Farmers. Explain the selection criteria to both the local council andthe WUA in addition to all beneficiaries •Risk: Closing of Turkish Borders. Mitigaton: Procurement from theLocal Market • Increase in the prices of Fuel. contracting a supplier and fixing the cost forlong duration • Risk: Self consumption or sell of seeds particularly among IDPs. Mitigation: Include thispoint during the training, and emphasizing seeds is only one-off distribution and nofurther distribution for the next season • Risk: Intervention from non-cooperative military groupsand worsen security situation. Mitigation:In the worst case, the project will provide remote assistanceto the farmers. As they have solid experiences in farming, once inputs were distributed,they can continue the project with minimum supports
