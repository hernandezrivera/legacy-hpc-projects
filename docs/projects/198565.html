Project Summary

The major aim of the Shelter/CCCM/NFI Cluster is to improve living conditions of displaced persons in humanitarian crises. The sector facilitates assistance and strengthens protection and dignity of the displaced and works with beneficiaries to attain durable solutions. Exacerbated vulnerabilities in 2022 as a result of floods, caused by heavy down pour etc, forced displacement and movement, restricted movements and access, congestion in the camp and harsh weather conditions especially in the rainy season have led to increasing needs for Shelter/CCCM/NFI Sector services both in the camps and host communities. Furthermore, the rainy season shows increase in floods across camps and host communities in general. Shelters and NFI damaged or destroyed have worsened living conditions. SHO aims to provide Shelter/NFI services in Ngala, Konduga, MMC, jere, Kala Balge and Yobe and Adamawa to mitigate impact of the rainy season and identified encumbrances, reduce vulnerability and address the accumulated and projected needs for shelter and NFI solutions.
Specific sector objectives for shelter/NFI will include:
SO 3: Specific Objective 3.1: Achieving alternative and durable solutions as opportunities allow. Sectoral Objective: Deliver flexible coordinated, adequate, and harmonized NFI assistance (including assessments, distribution, reporting and post-distribution monitoring) for the affected population.
SO 1: Specific Objective 1.1: All necessary emergency actions for newly-arrived IDPs. Sectoral Objective: Ensure sufficient, coordinated, and adequate delivery of emergency shelter solutions to respond to the immediate shelter needs of the affected people.
SO 1: Specific Objective 1.1: All necessary emergency actions for newly-arrived IDPs. Sectoral Objective: Deliver reinforced/transitional shelters and repairs systems to respond to the specific shelter needs of the newly arrived IDPs
Specific activities for shelter/NFIs will include.
SHO will address lifesaving needs of IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable members of host community, new arrivals, IDPs in BAY states by providing emergency assistance to the most vulnerable people affected in the BAY states to save lives and alleviate suffering, particularly amidst current needs. Through this project SHO will address the sector’s overall objective by providing critical and life-saving shelter and critical NFI support to affected populations, through Shelter construction and NFIs intervention towards improving the living conditions inside IDP camps and host and facilitating the immediate and targeted delivery of services to the displaced population. Furthermore, the project will address the needs of women, girls, men and boys throughout the planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Through the DTM and regular site monitoring, needs and gaps will be reported and shared with partners and all stakeholders. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to contend with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings. Shelter maintenance and strengthening during the season and small-scale community-driven mitigation measures.

1 - Needs

1.4 million people have been displaced. More than 300,000 houses have been damaged.With massive damage to croplands, flooding will likely increase food insecurity and malnutrition, where an estimated 19 million people face food insecurity. Priority needs include food items, nutritional food, non-food items, emergency shelter, water, sanitation and hygiene, medical supplies, and logistical support. The Government of Nigeria through the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) and humanitarian partners are responding with food, non-food items and clean drinking water but additional resources are required to scale-up the response to the unprecedented flood impact.Since July 2022, more than 3.2 million people in 36 states in Nigeria have been affected by floods triggered by heavy rains, with Adamawa hardest hit in the BAY states. The flooding has wreaked havoc among vulnerable communities, leading to the displacement of 1.4 million people, the loss of over 600 lives and damage to 569,000 houses. It has contaminated water sources, increasing the risk of waterborne disease in a country that is facing a severe cholera outbreak.The flooding has aggravated an already existing severe food and nutrition crisis. More than 440,000 hectares of farmland have been damaged, leading to a catastrophic loss of food access and livelihoods. According to the Cadre Harmonisé, more than 19 million people across Nigeria are facing severe food insecurity. Due to the flooding, high agriculture production costs, and insecurity, cereal production in 2022 is likely to decline by 3.4 per cent compared to last year, according to FAO.The just concluded rainy season which was predicted to cease in the north by mid-November according to the Nigeria Meteorological Agency. The heavy rains have caused the worst floods throughout Nigeria in a decade. According to the National Emergency Response Agency (NEMA), as at Nov 1, 2022, some 3.2 million people were affected, with more than 600 dying and 1.4 million displaced. 300,000 houses have been completely damaged, and some 569,000 hectares of farmland have been destroyed. Given the scale of the emergency they have requested all available actors, including United Nations, for immediate assistance. The scale of the natural disaster is considerable and requires timely interventions to save lives.Based on the recent HNO OF 2023 Shelter/NFI MSNA2 , DTM3 , and Site Tracker assessment4 conducted in July–September, approximately 672,050 households representing 3,360,254 individuals still require shelter and NFI support, which represents an increase of 12% from 2022. Among them, approximately three hundred thousand individuals are in acute need, composed of those living in the open spaces, living in public facilities, and sharing shelters in camps and host communities. Another one million individuals were identified as sheltering with other households (sharing) or in partially damaged shelters in return areas, thereby lacking basic privacy and raising protection concerns. In 2023, the priority focus will be on households living in the open spaces; households living in makeshift shelters made of nets or blankets, as well as new arrivals from inaccessible areas, will continue to be the most critical caseloads in need of external support to enable them to live out of harm and danger from exposure to harmful elements, while receiving support with housing and shelter repairs, thus supporting the recovery effort in durable solutions through the provision of transitional shelters and repairing damaged shelter in the return areas. According to the assessments, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in camps would still require critical life-saving assistance. Consequently, the Sector will continue to closely monitor environmental-driven events like fires and floods to continue to support the replacement of shelter materials, including repairs, the upgrade of emergency shelters to transitional shelters.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities; 2. Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services and 3. Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities. The project team will continue to assess the situation and respond to immediate needs among vulnerable groups, especially PLW, in the areas of intervention. SALIENT has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, SALIENT protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles:A. Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: SALIENT will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. SALIENT’s SHELTER/NFI team will ensure that community meetings and sensitization campaigns are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. B. Ensure meaningful and equal access: the SHELTER/NFI Team will strengthen participatory camp governance structures, facilitating direct inclusion of beneficiary groups into decision-making processes, facilitating greater equitable and meaningful access to assistance programs. SALIENT will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information onservices and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacyC. Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: Beneficiaries will be members of camp committees, ensuring strong participation in camp governance, channeling ideas and influencing the wider humanitarian coordination and response planning, and play a vital role in the implementation of site maintenance activities, providing foundation for community-led camp management and interventions. Daily laborers, hired to support the construction of the drainage network, will be recruited from the IDP community.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability towards beneficiaries will be guaranteed throughout and by the application of internal and external tools already available, such as the PSEA Policy, Child Protection Policy, Code of Conduct and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs); as for the latter, SOPs on Data Protection and SOPs on Complaint Mechanism will be particularly relevant for the current proposal and presence of trained PSEAH focal point in each of our operational locations, will contribute to ensuring meaningful reporting mechanisms to affected persons. These tools and the approach will also ensure vulnerable groups with equal and impartial access to the services provided. Beneficiaries will be provided with the contact details of the focal points and informed about the complaint mechanisms available. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including face to face with field staff, WhatsApp number shared, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. This information is sent to the SALIENT office in Maiduguri, where it will added to the Complaints Response Mechanism Register and referred to the most relevant person.Through participatory implementation and ensuring feedback are collected on a regular basis.
