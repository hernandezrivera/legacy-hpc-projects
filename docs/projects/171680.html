Project Summary

According to HNO 2021, Despite 2020 Gu season performance rated as favorable in terms nutrition outcomes, an estimated 2.3 million people including children <5 years, pregnant and lactating women across Somalia will require lifesaving curative and preventive services. It is projected that 1.6M require preventive services while 1.4 need preventive services. Worst affected are IDP populations who account for 511,706 of the total population in need, 15% of who are persons living with disability. This is attributable to their deep seated vulnerability to arbitrary eviction and displacements due to perennial conflicts and cyclic floods and attendant food insecurity. The nutritional vulnerability of PLWs, girls and women in IDPs and urban poor communities are further exacerbated by lack of access to basic livelihood and other social sector facilities such as primary health care services, access to safe drinking water and sanitation services.
A post Deyr Assessment conducted by FSNAU revealed that GAM levels in 24 assessed IDP sites assessed had 18 sites rated as either critical or serious, an indication of how IDP children are disproportionately affected and their need for immediate lifesaving interventions.
Somalia 2020 Micronutrient Survey shows increased susceptibility of mothers and children to malnutrition. The survey discloses that approximately 40.2% of non-pregnant women and 47% of pregnant women are anemic and similarly around 43% children below five years are anemic, revealing a serious public health challenge. The report further reveals that uptake of IYCF practices is below acceptable levels of less than 20% and the same is true for minimum dietary diversity, hence recurring infections among malnourished children thereby resulting in poor or non-response to treatment and mortality if left untreated.
In light of the above, Aid Vision proposes a 12-month quality and inclusively lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition care services aimed at supporting communities in Garasbaley and Shangani districts of Banadir Region and Balcad District of Middle Shabelle Region. The project intends to conduct facility and community-based screening for severe acute malnutrition for children aged 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and provide treatment of acute malnourished children 6-59 months (6400 children between 6-59 months and 2888 pregnant and lactating women) at the outpatient therapeutic centers linked to the health facilities to curb Malnutrition in the targeted districts. Further, micronutrient supplementation to PLW mothers and children 6-59 months for treatment and prevention of malnutrition, Routine individual IYCF counselling services will be conducted for PLW mother attending the facility; children aged 6-59 months will be supplemented with vitamin A, capacity building of health facility and community workers(Boys, girls, men and women ) on appropriate IYCF counselling services to mothers attending the facility, conduct training for the facility workers as well as community nutrition workers(10 male and 10 female ) on integrated Management of Acute malnutrition(IMAM) Guidelines to ensure basic nutrition package are provided. The centers will also develop referral pathway to link patients in need of specialized care to other centers. To enhance community capacities and nutrition service uptake, community based approaches like Mother-Led MUAC screening will be adopted. Aid Vision will closely work with other partner agencies, government line ministries and local authorities in the region to better coordinate humanitarian interventions and prevent duplication.

Mitigation Measures

Aid Vision will ensure all minority and majority as well as well as disable groups are involved in the implementation of the project and be able to transparent in terms of employment. Community nutrition workers selection and training will also consider gender sensitivity including men and women. All this will ensure gender and equity issues are addressed among the community members. Aid Vision will collect gender-disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to Nutrition activities to ensure they have equal access to quality nutrition care services.Aid Vision will ensure that all segments of the affected population including pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months have equal access to Nutrition assistance. The minority and marginalized communities who are at risk of being neglected/ excluded are also given quality and equal nutrition care services to prevent malnutrition. Aid Vision will also advocate community participation in the implementation of the program. This will help in getting complaints and feedback responses that enhances effective decision making and promote accountability to the affected populationAid Vision proposed project is designed in way that prioritizes the rights, safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. The target communities are fully involved and aware of the roles of each stakeholder where each sees the other as partners and equals. The proposed project will not discriminate against any beneficiary and will enable equal and impartial assistance and services for all. Targeting of vulnerable children and adults will be done on basis of their specific needs. The staff will be inducted on their duties and responsibilities and particularly the rights of the beneficiaries to ensure there are no any form of patient exploitation at the facilities and Aid Vision offices. Watertight mechanisms will be put in place to ensure served populations voices are heard and can file their complaints without being victimized.

The project will involve both communities and Somalia Health Authorities (both at Federal Government and State Authority levels) to ensure sustainability of the project interventions. Nutrition Facility staff and Community Based Nutrition workers drawn from the served populations will be trained on their roles and responsibility to ensure they provide quality nutrition care service to the affected population. Community Development Committees as well as community nutrition committees will be formed to ensure communities are at the forefront in all development initiatives, nutrition interventions included. Mothers will be trained on mother-led MUAC screening .The strategy is aimed at increasing coverage and early identification of malnourished children for referral to requisite service site for further management
