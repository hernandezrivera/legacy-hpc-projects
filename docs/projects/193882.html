Project Summary

DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid (DCA/NCA) and its partners, MAAN Development Centre (MAAN) and Women Affairs Centre (WAC), propose a multisectoral WASH-GBV project in the North Governorate of Gaza strip - Al Siafa area (Al Amrekyia) in Beit Lahia and Al Masreyeen area (Al Farata) in Beit Hanoun
The WASH component (through MAAN) will address the HNO needs indicators related to the limited access of people to 1) improved functional and safe sanitation facilities at HH level, 2) sanitation services at community level and 3) enough water for drinking and domestic purposes.
The GBV component (through WAC) will address the HNO needs indicators related to1) Improving GBV response, 2) Improving GBV case management, 3) Increasing focus on GBV prevention and risk mitigation
WASH objective: The project aims at improving the access of underserved people to safe and equitable WASH services.
SO1: Women, girls, men, and boys are enabled to improve hygiene practices and have access to hygiene items to protect against WASH related health risks
SO2: Women, girls, men and boys’ access safe, appropriate, and hygienic sanitation services and facilities.
SO3: Women, girls, men, and boys’ access safe, equitable and sustainable quantity of water for drinking, cooking, and personal hygiene
The WASH component contributes to HRP Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL, and the WASH Cluster Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians.
GBV Objective:
SO1 SGBV survivors safely access quality, survivor-centered mental health and psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment, and recovery.
SO2 GBV survivors access appropriate, quality case management services including coordinated care and support to navigate available services.
SO3 SGBV programming addresses harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality and informs communities about available services.
The GBV component (through WAC) is in line with the HRP Protection Cluster Strategic OBJECTIVE 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence.
The WASH partner (MAAN) will identify the most vulnerable families with deteriorated WASH conditions (based on socioeconomic and technical assessments) to improve access to WASH services. This will be followed by the GBV response (by WAC).
WASH component will include the rehabilitation of in-house WASH facilities, installation of sewer lines, distribution of water storage tanks, rehabilitation of desalination plant, hygiene promotion (HP) and hygiene material support via e-voucher system. GBV component will include referral and case management, psychosocial support (PSS), protection and GBV related awareness, legal counselling, and cash for case management services.
Complementarity and harmony will be ensured between the two components, where proper coordination will take place for this purpose. DCA/NCA and its partners are active members in the UN clusters and will be engaged effectively in all the processes and activities including joining meetings, coordinating the response and provision of information needed for the 4 W’s systems.
WASH stakeholders include the WASH cluster focal point in Gaza North (Save the Children), CMWU and the Municipalities in the tarted locations. DCA/NCA and MAAN have already coordinated the planned intervention with CMWU, PWA and WASH cluster focal point in the Gaza north.
GBV stakeholder include Protection cluster in Gaza and GBV SWG Coordinator and members, and GBV specialized actors of case management services, DCA/NCA and WAC have already coordinated the planned intervention with Protection cluster focal point in the Gaza.

1 - Needs

In January 2022, DCA/NCA and its partners, MAAN and WAC, have started to work in Al Siafa are (Al Amrekyia) in Beit Lahia and Al Masreyeen area (Al Farata) in Beit Hanoun in the North Governorate addressing the gaps related to people’s poor access to WASH and GBV services. Building on the current 2022 achievements and considering the updated WASH and GBV assessments in August 2022, it has been revealed that significant gaps are still in place and there is a need to continue targeting the same communities to comprehensively address the WASH and GBV needs in 2023.Upon MoH records, the Gaza north (including the project target locations) is among the most vulnerable areas in Gaza strip in terms of water borne diseases. People in the targeted communities suffer from limited access to improved functional and safe sanitation facilities and services at both household and community level, but also suffer from limited access to a sufficient quantity of water for drinking and domestic purposes due to unimproved water sources and weak storage capacity. The WASH assessment has targeted 78 families including 480 individuals (111 women, 119 men, 250 children, 16 females headed HHs and 19 PwD’s/with chronic diseases) and involved CMWU, municipalities and community structures in the targeted locations. Upon the assessment, 98.7% of the families have toilets that are below the national standards and result in protections concerns for children and women. 89.7% of the families have cooking areas that are below the national standards. 48.7% of the families are not connected to sewage network, while 52.6% suffer from flooding of sewage due to the current situation of the sewage connections/cesspits. 33.3% of the families suffer from weak storage capacity for both domestic and drinking water. 85.9% of the families were found abject poor and 14.1% are poor where access to hygiene items is a challenge for majority of families due to deteriorated socioeconomic situations. Poor hygiene conditions of kitchens, utensils, bathrooms, combined with shortage of hygienic material inside homes were recognized.The GBV assessment has involved 289 women and girls (16.6% under the age of 18, and the age groups for women varied, with a greater focus on age groups below the age of 44 and 7.3% of the 289 are elderlies). The assessment showed that 84. 7% of women are subjected to psychological/verbal violence. The percentage of women who experience violence continuously or sometimes is close to 90%. Psychological or verbal violence remains the most prevalent among different forms of violence. Physical violence remains high, as cases of violence in this sample showed that 29.8% of women and girls who experience violence are physically abused while 27% of them are subjected to social violence, and 7.4% are subjected to sexual violence. The results clearly indicate that women and girls are in very high need of psychosocial support in its various forms, with relative weights ranging from 81.4% – 87.1%. It was also clear that legal support services in its various forms were present in the need of women and girls with a relative weight of 86.9% for legal awareness, 82.8% for legal consultations, 81.2% for representation in the courts. assessment revealed that there is a need to provide legal assistance and initiate judicial proceedings against domestic violence, through (continuous legal consultations, and representation in the courts to enable women and girls to access their rights). The assessment revealed that the legal support is related to wife alimony, Child alimony, custody and child seeing. The assessment also showed indicators that the inadequate access to the WASH services plays a role in increasing GBV against women as the majority of women participated in the FGD reported that the absence of water negatively affects the behavior of the head of household. 27% of women participated in the FGD reported that lack of domestic water exposes them to violence including physical violence. The women and girls participated in in the FGDs, especially those of reproductive age, reported that the poor access to hygiene items, adequate WASH facilities and domestic water affect their personal hygiene and menstrual management which sometimes subject them to psychosocial and physical violence.

2 - Activities

WASH (MAAN): - The rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities in Gaza north (60 households, 355 individuals: 85 men, 83 women, 95 boys, 92 girls – including 8 elderlies)- The installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connections in Gaza north (650 M.L, reaching 208 individuals: 50 men, 49 women, 56 boys, 53 girls – including 5 elderlies)- The provision of standardized household hygiene kit in Gaza north (278 kits via e-voucher system reaching 1646 individuals: 395 men, 386 women, 442 boys, 422 girls – including 39 elderlies)- Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns in Gaza north (2 campaigns targeting 5,920 individuals: 1,422 men, 1,390 women, 1,591 boys, 1,517 girls – including 138 elderlies)- The provision of PE water tanks in Gaza north (45 tanks for 266 individuals: 64 men, 62 women, 71 boys, 69 girls – including 6 elderlies)- The rehabilitation of public water desalination units in Gaza north (1 unit reaching 16,576 individuals: 3,979 men, 3,890 women, 4,452 boys, 4,245 girls – including 388 elderlies) GBV (WAC): - Remote PSS via WAC’s emergency help line to 960 women (48 WWDs) and 192 girls (10 GWDs) through WAC’s emergency help line. GBV survivors will receive PSS remotely as the psychologist will receive the cases and follow them up. The hotline number will be announced to the targeted communities through CBO’s.- PSS First Aid to 600 women (32 WWDs) and 128 girls (6 GWDs) and refer cases needing further support to individual PSS.- Individual psychological counselling to 120 women (6 WWDs) and 25 girls (2 GWDs) and refer those with common problems to group PSS.- In- person group PSS sessions targeting 120 women (6 WWDs) and 30 girls (2 GWDs)- Specialized MHPSS to 25 traumatized survivors (20 women “2 WWDs” and 5 girls “1 GWD”). - 20-hour training for 25 front-line GBV staff on identifying GBV cases, case management and counselling services. WAC will hire external trainers to implement the case management training for WAC’s and CBO’s staff in the targeted location). - Case management to 26 women (2 WWDs) and 4 early married girls, including referral.- Individual legal counselling for 110 women (6 WWDs) and 20 early married girls (1 GWD)- Legal representation in courts to 25 women (1 WWDs) and 5 early married girls (1 GWD)- 2 structured recreational open days targeting 120 women (6 WWDs) and 30 girls (2 GWDs).- Cash for protection (102$/women) to 12 women (1 WWD) and 3 early married girls (1 GWD). - 15-hour training for 25 professionals and non-professionals including GBV programme staff, volunteers, and community workers on GBV guiding principles and Core Package. Two separate sessions will be done, one for the professionals and the other for non-professional. - Advocacy campaign to maximize outreach for women, girls, men, boys and addressing harmful social norms and inform communities about available services. The project will invest in WASH infrastructure at the community level (installation of sewer networks and HH connection that support families to replace the cesspits system that result in wastewater pools spilling onto roads and flow through the soil to the underground water. The intervention complements the strategic plans of municipalities, CMWU and PWA. The project includes hygiene promotion campaigns that mobilize the community positively and create permanent behavioral changes towards maintained hygienic and healthy life practices. The project also includes GBV awareness raising campaigns to address the social norms and behaviors. Rehabilitation of HH’s WASH facilities will address women security and privacy, child protection, supporting the wellbeing of elderlies and people with disabilities. The communication material for hygiene awareness will be tailored to consider the different roles and responsibilities for men, women, and elderlies inside the HH. Specific support will be provided for women in identifying and applying best practices relevant to their roles such as safe water handling, safe children excreta disposal and handwashing. The hygiene material will consider the specific hygiene needs for women and girls particularly teenagers, pregnant, elderly and women with disabilities. The hygiene awareness is planned to reach the whole communities in the project targeted locations through leaflets, social media posts, Radio spots, community sessions, etc. with particular focus on the families who will be targeted by the other project components – to be targeted through home visits for hygiene awareness. The hygiene promotion team will qualify the families to receive hygiene kits/vouchers through direct observations, assessing the hygiene situation inside homes and the socioeconomic situation. This will be done in consultation with community groups inside the targeted locations. DCA\NCA with its partners will facilitate access to WASH and GBV services for persons with reduced mobility (e.g., persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) via making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access, providing information about services in both verbal, written and pictorial forms. DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA session to its and partners’ staff and will disseminate awareness raising materials including messages and orientation sessions about PSEA and the available complaints mechanisms to the target communities. The contracts of project staff, contractors, vendors, and service providers will include a code of conduct that address SEA.The intervention has been coordinated with CMWU, PWA, WASH and protection clusters. DCA/NCA will ensure proper coordination among its partners to enhance complementarity.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

- E-vouchers: DCA/NCA’s has an operational electronic voucher system that will be used for hygiene items support. Th list of hygiene items and top up amount for each hygiene voucher/family will be coordinated and agreed upon with the WASH cluster considering the standardized hygiene kit content. The e-voucher system operates autonomous without regular power supply or internet. Vendors are provided a terminal which is an Android smart phone with a special e-voucher app pre-installed. Beneficiaries are registered with an electronic data collection app feeding data directly into the e-voucher registration database and are instantly issued with a SMART card which contains a chip where all the data is stored. Voucher-based assistance is a widely used modality in Gaza. Affected people prefer using voucher systems to meet their basic needs instead of direct in-kind distribution. The market is accessible by rights holders, the needed items are available at reasonable prices and the suppliers have the needed capacity. Rights holders will have the opportunity to select their preferences from a diversified lists of hygiene items based on their needs- Cash for protection/for case management: This provides flexibility towards addressing GBV survivors’ needs while receiving case management services.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA will support partners to increase the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. The project will organize regular focus group discussions with the community members to collect feedback on the provided services. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

How have you prioritized the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and considered the principles of Do No Harm, including an analysis of the protection context, identification and mitigation of risks to people’s safety and wellbeing that the project might inadvertently exacerbate? The projects focus is to enhance protection of GBV survivors and other vulnerable groups by providing a multisectoral WASH – GBV response. The project design recognizes the rights and needs of beneficiaries in terms of safety, security, and dignity and proposes activities that foster the realization of these needs and rights. It adopts gender-sensitive and culturally responsive approach to implement the project activities which will highly contribute to address the social and cultural risks that might undermine the participation of the beneficiaries, especially women in the project activities. DCA/NCA and partners use culturally responsive approach which involves respect for the cultural backgrounds of all right holders, particularly women and people with disability and fully recognize and understand differences, norms, taboos, and stigmas surrounding social and cultural back draughts of the right holders. During the inception period, the project elaborates an environment and social impact assessment to ascertain the environmental and social impacts of the infrastructural subcomponents under WASH response; and to manage, mitigate, and monitor any possible negative impacts. All safety and security measures will be considered when implementing the project activities to prevent incidences and reduce safety and security risks. DCA/NCA and partners will keep close monitoring for the security situation and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency(www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population. PSEA mainstreaming: DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA. • DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaint handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality (either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism: me.complaints@dca.dkand/ or using the partners complaints mechanism channels - WAC helpline1800333666 or complain@wac.org.ps. MAAN Website: www.maancomplaints.com Hotline: 1800900901) • DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA session to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities that will include awareness raising messages sent via SMS messages among other tools. •DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism. • DCA/NCA’s PSEA local focal point is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. Accordingly, DCA/NCA will continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information especially on available referral pathways for sensitive complaints and will continuously seek support from the available network services when needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

How does the project enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services and the targeting of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs? The response will be directly implemented by DCA/NCA and its local partners in Gaza. DCA/NCA will manage this response through its office and staff in Gaza and its Country Office in Jerusalem. The selected local partners and DCA/NCA staff in Gaza have experience working under conditions that lead to restricted humanitarian access and adjust timing and approach as required. DCA/NCA has developed an Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan for assuring access. Networking with CBOs and NGOs in the targeted areas, as well as empowering women leaders within safe spaces, will guarantee access of vulnerable groups to project services. The DCA/NCA will conduct regular visits to the response areas, following up on the indicators and response plan, and assessing response implementation via discussions with the response team and rights holders and by visiting the response sites. DCA\NCA with its partners will ensure equal and impartial access to WASH and GBV services as follow:- DCA\NCA access approach is consistent with the humanitarian core principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence, and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women and girls; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favor any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women and girls based on need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic, or other objectives.- DCA\NCA with its partners monitor access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g., persons with disabilities, elderly).- DCA\NCA with its partners use their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls to GBV services towards implementing UN 2030 agenda of “LEAVING NO ONE BEHIND” and to “endeavor to reach the furthest behind first”.-DCA\NCA with its partners facilitate access to WASH and GBV services for persons with reduced mobility (e.g., persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) via making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. Partners facilitate access to GBV services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities via providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations. Furthermore, to manage security risks and access, DCA/NCA employs a security focal point who monitors the security situation daily, exchanges information with other international organizations and the UN and updates staff. Standard security procedures are in place, which all staff are aware of and follow. Furthermore, DCA/NCA has an extensive network of both humanitarian and development partners, as well as actively participates in the humanitarian cluster system, which facilitates information sharing regarding recent security incidents and forecasting of events. DCA/NCA is connected and follows UNDSS security updates, which is shared with its local partners, who also follow their local networks for security updates. Additionally, daily assessment for the security situation on the ground in coordination with relevant organizations, partner CBO’s and local committees in the area will take place to ensure the safety and security of staff. DCA/NCA will involve its community groups/Community engagement committees in the project implementation and monitoring. This aims to build community confidence and networks and ensure proper access to affected people.
