Project Summary

Project Objective: To enhance the income of vulnerable female and male farmers in the Gaza Strip.
Project Summary:
• The primary purpose of this project is to assist vulnerable and poor female and male farmers through the provision of one or more of the following services:
• Rehabilitation of open lands: 500 dunums for 250 farmers (at least 12 female farmers and 238 male farmers age for both female and male is between 18-70 years).The cost of rehabilitating one dunum is expected to be around $600.
• Installation of solar systems for agricultural 10 water wells. The cost of installing solar system for one water well is expected to be around $24,000
• Rehabilitation of greenhouses: 150 greenhouse for 150 farmers (at least 5 female farmers and 145 male farmers age for both female and male is between 18-70 years).The cost of rehabilitating one greenhouse is expected to be around $1000.
The project activities will be implemented throughout the Gaza strip. NDC will partner with three locally registered cluster NGOs who have extensive agricultural experience which positions them to be the most capable to implement the project. In addition, the project activities will be conducted in cooperation with the Ministry of Agriculture and the Food Security Sector.
The estimated number of direct beneficiaries is 400 farmers, (Of which at least 17 beneficiaries will be female farmers and 383 males who are the head of the household) while the total number of the farmers and their families as indirect beneficiaries is 2200. The estimated total budget for the project is $ 991,279.

The project's objectives match the HRP 2022 oPt, Strategic Objective country #(3): The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The project will improve the resilience of households and communities to cope with and mitigate shocks and environmental hazards. The three project’s activities are expected to increase support to communities’ livelihoods which in turns will improve the general food security situation and will have a positive effect on their overall nutrition and health status.
Furthermore, the project is specifically in line with the Food Security Sector (FSS)
objective#(1) Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced.

1 - Needs

There are two million Gazans who are living under an Israeli blockade, and who also face an electricity crisis as well as an unstable political and security environment. These challenges continue to prevent Gazan’s access to a decent livelihood and limits the free movement of people, goods, and services. The situation became even more difficult with the spread of COVID-19 in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip and the deterioration following the declaration of a state of emergency and the frequent lockdowns. As a result of these factors, roughly 73% of households are severely or moderately food insecure in Gaza Strip (HNO2022). Furthermore, the May 2021 conflict had a direct and severe impact on the agricultural sector, which contributes to nearly 7% of the GDP and informally employs 20 percent of the Palestinian workforce in Gaza (WFP, June 2021). According to OCHA reports, large agricultural areas and several agricultural facilities such as animal farms, greenhouses and water wells have been damaged or exposed to disruptions, which has consequently decreased agricultural production (OCHA, Escalation in the Gaza Strip, the West Bank and Israel, May 2021). The Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) in Gaza assessed that the damage caused by the May escalation cost US$ 56 million, which was in line with the World Bank’s estimate which stated that damages and losses in the agricultural sector totaled $55-60 million. The high cost is as a result of Israeli airstrikes that targeted several agricultural facilities, hundreds of dunums of arable land and warehouses for grains and agricultural inputs. Further damage to irrigation canals, water pumping systems and greenhouses will also likely set back food production, while unexploded ordnances pose a direct risk and prevents use of the land. According to OCHA Situation Report No. 9 (August 2021) titled “Response to the escalation in the oPt”; power shortages, coupled with damage to solar power systems, are impacting farming livelihoods specifically interrupting irrigation schedules, placing around 35,000 farming and breeding households at risk. So, one of the top priority needs according to the report is the rehabilitation of damaged agricultural facilities, including water wells, irrigation systems, greenhouses, and livestock farms. After the end of the conflict in May 2021, NDC and its partners started a rapid needs assessment process. During the assessment, 17 focus groups & 21 individual interviews were conducted with farmers in the Gaza Strip. The objective of these focus groups and individual interviews was to discuss the main agricultural damage & losses as well as to determine the most urgent needs that address the sustainability of their livelihood. One focus group was conducted in the north of Gaza by Beit Lahia Ass. and attended by 7 famers. Four focus groups were conducted in Khan Yunis, the Middle area & Rafah by Al Nakheel Ass. and attended by 37 farmers (27 males and 10 females). Three individual interviews were conducted in Khan Younis by Al Nakheel Ass. with 3 farmers. Two interviews were conducted in the Middle area & Rafah by Al Nakheel Ass. with 2 farmers (one male & other female). Four interviews were conducted in Gaza city by NDC with 4 farmers. Twelve interviews were conducted in the north of Gaza by NDC with 12 farmers. Al-Fokhary Ass. conducted 12 focus groups in the Middle area, Khan Younis & Rafah. 125 farmers 80 males & 45 females). In total, the 17 focus groups and twenty-one interviews were attended by 183 persons (127 males and 56 females). The focus group participants’ ages were from 22 to over 65 with different marital statuses. During the needs assessment, both male & female farmers indicated that thousands of dunums of open land, greenhouses, water wells, solar systems, and irrigation networks were either partially or completely damaged by the recent Israeli aggression. They indicated that the rehabilitation of; open land, greenhouse, water well, and existing water ponds are the top priorities for them. All of the farmers that were consulted (100%), both male and female, & from different age group including PWD, indicated that utilizing solar systems to irrigate their lands is the highest priority due to the severe lack of electricity and the high costs of fuel. Solar systems will provide them with continuous access to water at very low cost. In particular, 80% of the female farmers indicated that the second priority for them is open land rehabilitation since many women own open fields and they usually work in their fields. In addition, the farmers, both male and female confirmed that they suffered severe losses not only due to the recent Israeli aggression, but also because of the spread of Covid-19, low purchase power, difficulties in marketing their products, etc. This led them into significant debt. In fact, due to the nature of these activities, there was no significant difference in the needs of male and female farmers as well as between age groups and ability. All the farmers that were consulted (male & female & from different age groups including PWD) indicated that they prefer to receive cash assistance for rehabilitating their land, greenhouses, and water ponds. For the rehabilitation of the water wells & installing solar systems, all of the women preferred that NDC do the rehabilitation/installation for them while around 40% of the male farmers preferred to do the rehabilitation/installation themselves (cash assistance and not service delivery). Both male and female farmers also indicated that they prefer to receive the cash from the banks or at the CBOs premises. The farmers, both male & female, indicated that they are willing to attend a PSEA workshop. In fact, 100% of the female farmers were enthusiastic about attending the workshop. They indicated that they would like to increase their knowledge about these concepts & they would like to know more about their rights.

2 - Activities

This project is designed in a participatory manner taking into consideration the interests of the project's stakeholders. Beneficiaries' dignity and independence are of utmost importance while preserving gender equity and sensitivity without any bias. In addition, the project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach to empower female farmers in the Gaza Strip. All targeted households will be selected according to protection-related, and socio-economic vulnerability criteria. Furthermore, the project will be implemented in coordination with the MoA and the FSS. COVID-19 health precautions have been taken into consideration in the design phase and will be also taken into consideration during implementation phase. Gender & Protection against PSEA were embedded in the needs assessment and design of the project, and will continue to be emphasized during implementation. For example, all project beneficiaries will be required to attend one of the 15 PSEA and GBV awareness workshops that will be conducted overall the Gaza Strip. This will be a condition for any beneficiary in order to receive assistance from NDC. The protection section includes additional details about these workshops. NDC will make sure that 100% of the beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. The project has three main activities:1. Rehabilitation of open lands: 500 dunums distributed equally all over Gaza Strip (for 50 farmers, at least 12 female farmers and 238 male farmers age for both female and male is between 18-70 years). The cost of rehabilitating one dunum is expected to be around $600).2. Installation of solar systems for agricultural water wells: (10 water wells distributed equally all over Gaza Strip (The cost of installing solar system for one water well is expected to be around $24,000).4. Rehabilitation of greenhouses: 50 in North Gaza, 20 in Gaza, 30 in Middle, and 50 in Khanyounis (for 150 farmers - at least 5 female farmers and 145 male farmers age for both female and male is between 18-70 years). The cost of rehabilitating one greenhouse is expected to be around $1000.).To implement these activities, NDC and its partners will use conditional restricted cash assistance for open lands, and greenhouse rehabilitation. NDC will provide each farmer with 3 installments: 30%, 40% and 30%. The installment modality is used to ensure that farmers use the cash assistance for its intended purpose. Furthermore, the farmers are required to provide NDC and its partner NGOs with copies of all the invoices of the agriculture inputs that they bought. For the solar system installation, NDC will use a direct service modality (section 5.2 has more details). To benefit from the project activities, NDC will ask beneficiaries to attend the PSEA/GBV workshops. Furthermore, NDC will conduct 2 days training for the staff working on the project (NDC staff as well as the 3 partner NGOs staff). To ensure the dignity and the security of the beneficiaries, especially female farmers, NDC will use local banks to provide their cash assistance payments. The project targets three main categories. The first is the affected farmers from the May 2021 aggression on Gaza, affected farmers in border areas (ARA), and farmers who are suffering from severe electricity cuts and who are facing considerable difficulties in running their water wells. It is expected that 400 (At least 17 female 383 male farmers) will benefit directly from the project's activities. Their immediate family members (Roughly 2750 persons) will also benefit indirectly. At the start of the project, a local advertisement will be published in each of the five governorates to ask farmers who meet the predetermined criteria to submit applications for participation in the project. The advertisement will be published in mosques, in local NGOs, local committee’s offices, etc. Five selection committees from NDC and its NGOs partners, local committee members, and other local figures will be formed to select the best applications that meet the criteria. NDC will make additional efforts to encourage women to apply. NDC will announce that women and people with disabilities will get additional marks when selecting beneficiaries. Also, NDC and their partners will coordinate with local committees and the MoA to ensure that the 17 female farmers that will be selected have all the required supporting documents to prove ownership of the targeted agricultural facilities. Finalized lists of applicants will be passed to the MoA for feedback on the selected beneficiaries. Based on the comments of the MoA, a final list of beneficiaries will be developed. NDC will be responsible for the overall management of the project activities, procurement, financial disbursement, monitoring and evaluation, and reporting. Three partners NGOs will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground, including the formation of the five local committees, finalizing the beneficiary list in coordination with MoA and the rehabilitation activities. In implementing the project activities, NDC will coordinate with the MoA, FAO, FSS, & INGOs. Coordination with NGOs, local committees, municipalities, and other governmental bodies will be the role of the three partners. NDC has extensive experience in working on Cash Assistance. NDC has recently implemented the World Bank Gaza Emergency Project which contains a Cash for Work component with a total budget of US$12.5 million. Furthermore, the 3 partners NGOs have good experience in implementing Cash Assistance project. They got funds from different donors such as oPt HF, Welfare, Arab Fund, and Global Communities.

In addition, NDC will implement its well-developed system of Community Score Cards (the system was developed with the support of the World Bank) to ensure quality service is provided to beneficiaries. The first session will be implemented at the start of the project while the second session will be implemented by the end of the project. If the quality of the service is not as expected, the Project Manager will immediately take corrective actions. For example, in the protection awareness workshops, NDC will only allow a limited number of participants required to maintain proper social distancing. Moreover, each participant will wear a mask. NDC and its partners will also provide hand sanitizer and masks, etc. Additionally, a compliant response mechanism will be put in place to ensure that feedback and complaints from beneficiaries are reported confidentially. NDC management will ensure that these complaints are immediately investigated and addressed in the most appropriate manner. Finally, NDC will provide OCHA with midterm and final completion reports with details on progress, achievements of outputs and indicators, difficulties encountered, and lessons learned. 5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

NDC and its partners will use a combined modality for implementing the project. NDC will use direct assistance (conditional and restricted assistance) to the selected beneficiaries for the following activities: open land and greenhouse rehabilitation. The main reasons for selecting this modality are: 1. Fast and efficient. 2. Flexibility 3. Give power of choice to the beneficiary 4. Dignity and limit sexual exploitation. For the installation of solar systems to power agriculture wells, NDC and its partners will use a direct service delivery modality. The main reason for this is that this activity requires technical specifications and design requirements to ensure efficiency and effectiveness. It would be extremely difficult for small-scale farmers to come up with high quality designs and technical specifications. NDC will procure the service of specialized consulting companies or qualified individual consultants/engineers to develop the technical specifications and the design requirements. Moreover, NDC will advertise for those activities in the newspapers and the relevant websites in order to benefit from the economy of scale.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

NDC will train local partner staff on the kinds of feedback mechanisms available at NDC, how to collect and forward feedback and concerns received, and how to follow up on the feedback. Any recommendations, suggestions, and concerns of the beneficiaries, along with the mitigation measures will be discussed during the bi-weekly senior management meetings. The senior management team consists of the project manager, NDC director, NGOs/CBOs managers and NDC M&E and Complaints officer. The team will make sure that all the issues and concerns are followed up with and appropriately resolved. If NDC or its partners receive any negative feedback or complaints related to the quality of the services, how the project staff deal with the beneficiaries, or any PSEA matters, a task team that consists of NDC Gaza Program Manager, the partner NGO Manager, and one member of the local committee will be immediately formed. The team will study the complaint, suggest solution, discuss the suggested solution with all concerned parties, implement the suggestion, monitor and evaluate the implementation of the suggested solution, and finally close the case upon satisfactory results. The whole process will be fully documented.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NDC will ensure the dignity and safety of the targeted farmers by conducting the following four steps: 1. Before implementing the project activities: NDC and its three partner NGOs will conduct 20 awareness raising workshops (four in each governate) about the project objectives, outputs, and activities. This to ensure that all the farmers are fully aware of the project and if any of one of them thinks or believes that they will be negatively affected, they will be given the opportunity to inform NDC and partner NGOs of their concerns. Consequently, NDC and partner NGOs will immediately take into account the farmer’s concerns and will solve all issues that might negatively affect them. 2. Beneficiary selection: Beneficiaries will be selected through the use of standardized social and technical vulnerability selection criteria consistent with humanitarian principles and practices and through gender-appropriate activities 3. Throughout the implementation of the project: NDC will conduct 3 community scorecard sessions in each governorate at the beginning, middle and end of the project. In total 15 scorecard sessions will be implemented. These community scorecard sessions will ensure that no harm is done to any member of the targeted community and will also work on improving the quality of services provided by the project. 4. Complaint mechanism: NDC already has an active complaints system that will also be available to the local communities should any project-related issues arise. NDC will take immediate corrective actions. Furthermore, to avoid any sexual exploitation and/or abuse, NDC will make sure that all necessary measures are taken throughout the project design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation such as staff training, enforce PSEA through NDC's established procedures, conduct awareness workshops for beneficiaries, etc. In addition, at the start of the implementation of the activities, a one-day workshop about protection be implemented in the 5 governorates of the Gaza Strip for the selected beneficiaries. The workshop will be repeated 3 times in each governorate and 5 times in Gaza (in total 17 workshops will be implemented all over the Gaza Strip). The aim of the workshop is to educate beneficiaries about protection guidelines and concepts, and how to deal with any abuse or harm that they might face. Furthermore, the workshops will inform the selected beneficiaries of the grievance/complaints system that is available through NDC and how to use it if they were subject to any abuse or harm. NDC will distribute the PSEA booklet to the participants (please refer to section 91.d for more information).
