Project Summary

This project will support the most vulnerable individuals impacted by the conflict including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees to meet their urgent basic needs through the provision of one-off multipurpose emergency assistance. For vulnerable individuals, cash-based interventions (CBI) will be devised to ensure that the most vulnerable will be able to meet their basic needs without relying on negative coping strategies. Acknowledging cash liquidity challenges in Libya and the well-known difficulties to withdraw cash out of banks and ATMs, such assistance will be delivered through value e-vouchers in areas where markets are functioning and accessible. CBI can be deployed rapidly to respond to the basic needs of socioeconomically vulnerable individuals in a manner that allows choice and dignity.

1 - Needs

Despite the prospects for a durable peace following the signing of the ceasefire in November 2020, the situation in Libya remains volatile and complex, as a result of years of protracted conflict and political and economic instability. Moreover, the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and the preventive measures and curfews have further impacted the precarious socio-economic situation and further stretching people’s coping capacities, especially for the most vulnerable. Reduced livelihoods and higher prices due to the temporary disruption of trade and closure of some shops, have affected the availability and affordability of basic services, goods and shelter. For weeks, people have protested in the streets against poor and deteriorating living conditions linked to difficulties faced in accessing basic goods and services and weakened in infrastructure. As of August 2020, IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) tracked 392,241 IDPs, 493,716 returnees and 584,509 migrants in Libya. Furthermore, according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview Libya 2021, there are more than 1.3 million people in the country in need of multi-sector humanitarian assistance, with shelter, food, health, non-food items and WASH being the most consistently identified needs among the majority of above-mentioned vulnerable groups in Libya. Needs are complex and diverse, with different vulnerable populations citing different needs depending on their situation and context. For displaced households, shelter is the most commonly identified need (30% of respondents), followed by food (27%). On the other hand, returnees identified food (21%), followed by non-food items (18%) as their most pressing needs. Displaced families and returnees both identified health services as their third priority need, something that has likely amplified against a backdrop of the COVID-19 pandemic. For IDPs and returnees, challenges in accessing health services were related to the lack of medicines at facilities, shortages/absence of health workers and overcrowded facilities. According the 2020 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), more than half of all Libyan respondents reported employing one or more coping mechanism to address a lack of resources in 2020. The most used strategies included selling non-productive assets, spending savings, borrowing money, resorting to begging, or by reducing spending, particularly on health. Of the Libyan population groups, returnees were more likely to use emergency or crisis coping strategies than other groups.In line with efforts to ensure accountability to affected populations (AAP), cash-based interventions are by far the most preferred type of assistance by those who receive it as it provides them the power to choose and control how to utilize the assistance based on their family needs and priorities. Since July 2020, such assistance has been the type of support most frequently requested by individuals calling the Inter-Agency Common Feedback Mechanism, of whom women accounted for nearly one quarter of the total callers.

2 - Activities

With the spread of COVID-19 pandemic, multipurpose cash-based interventions are considered most appropriate and relevant response option as it allows more remote delivery, less clustering at distribution sites and helps reduce transmission risk. Additionally, given the long-lasting cash liquidity issues that restrict people’s ability to withdraw money, IOM will prefer cash delivery modalities such as value e-vouchers that don’t require real money transactions, but still allow target groups to meet their multisectorial needs including medicines, food, S/NFIs etc.. IOM therefore proposes to complement its humanitarian lifesaving assistance in the sectors that are priority for the target groups through the provision of multi-purpose one-off assistance through value e-voucher to IDPs and returnees in and around Tripoli. Beneficiaries’ selection criteria will be based on vulnerability assessments that will prioritize IDPs and returnees displaced by the conflict, and those who have become unemployed due the spread or fear of COVID-19 and don’t have the means to meet their basic needs. Female, children, and elderly head of households as well as individuals with disabilities will also be prioritized. IOM will as much as possible align with the Cash Transfer Value (CTV) calculated by the Cash and Market Working Group (CMWG) and will keep into consideration the ongoing CBI carried out by humanitarian actors in Libya. This project will support 3,000 individuals, including 2,000 IDPs and 1,000 returnees for a total budget of 600,000 USD.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.1: Safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would be put in place to report incidents with selected services providers. The persons handling the complaints will be trained on data protection, confidentiality, ethical approaches, and importantly know how to refer cases. 4.2: By supporting IDPs and returnees, which are recognized by the population to be the most vulnerable groups in need of assistance, IOM should avoid creating tensions and conflict. Moreover, IOM coordinates beneficiary lists with the ministry of internal displacement to ensure those we assist are registered and in real need of support. Finally, being cash assistance not a very visible activity such as in kind distributions, IOM will not put beneficiaries at risk of individuals that might deviate from the common thinking explained above. 4.13: IOM will involve women and girls in assessing their safety around accessing selected service providers. Additionally, IOM will ensure that women and men can equally access this assistance and that gender/power dynamics related to access to and control of cash will not penalize women. 4.14: Training of staff on PSEA as well as on how to safely and ethically support and refer survivors to the services they might need/wish to access.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

IOM will engage beneficiaries during needs identification, planning, implementation and monitoring of this project to promote beneficiaries’ participation, ownership and decision making. The implementation of this project will be guided by vulnerability assessments to identify highly vulnerable households and individuals who need support to meet their urgent basic needs. The vulnerability of the household will be determined based on the vulnerability score card harmonized by the CMWG. IOM will also conduct post-distribution monitoring to assess beneficiary satisfaction with assistance. In coordination with CMWG as may be required, IOM will periodically revise the cash transfer value based on periodic joint expenditure and income monitoring.
