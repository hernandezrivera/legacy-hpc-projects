Project Summary

The September 2019 Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) report estimates that 2.1 million people are in Crisis and facing acute food security while one million children under the age of five will remain acutely malnourished through end of 2019. Drought conditions since late 2018 to May 2019 and erratic 2019 Gu rains have contributed to the high levels of food insecurity, as many households have not yet recovered from 2017 drought. In most regions, crops failed leading to hunger for poor riverine and agro-pastoral households. Low production has led to below average food stocks and income from agricultural employment. In pastoral areas, livestock productivity has declined reducing access to milk and increasing debt among many poor households, which struggle to meet their food needs. Poor pasture conditions are reported in South-Central regions leading to livestock migration, while in Northern and Central Pastoral, livestock asset holding has drastically reduced due to previous droughts, thus reducing households’ ability to earn incomes from livestock sales to meet household food needs and purchase water for livestock. Delayed and below average 2019 Gu rains across Somalia have led to significant reduction of cereal production with harvests reported to be the worst since Gu’ 1995, at 68 percent lower than the long-term average (1995-2018). Food prices have shown mixed trends across Somalia with up to 28 percent increase of local cereal prices, while imported food items have remained stable. The food component of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) has increased over the past one year except in Northeast and Central regions (WFP,September 2019)
About 750,000 of internally displaced persons (IDPs) out of the 2.6 million estimated IDPs are in Crisis or Emergency. Approximately 258,000 people in urban areas are in Crisis or above and face food consumption gaps due to high cost of living and limited income earning opportunities. A WFP assessment conducted in July in seven IDP settlements in Galkayo town reported that IDPs face food insecurity, lack access to basic services such as water, education and health. Majority of the households do not have access to credit and have limited access to income. Main expenditure costs are food, water, lighting and transportation to access basic services to the town. The households surveyed indicated willingness to engage in alternative livelihood skills to build longer term resilience.
Nutrition surveys indicate persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in the country attributed to food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and others with a national median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) remaining serious (10-14.9%) over the past three seasons. A prevalence of Critical (15-29.9%) GAM was observed in 10 out of 33 population groups surveyed.
WFP’s intervention aims to improve immediate access to food for vulnerable households and support resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks through unconditional and conditional food and cash-based transfers with the following outcomes
-Food-insecure people in areas affected by natural or human-caused disasters have access to adequate and nutritious food that meet their basic food and nutrition needs during and in the aftermath of shocks.
-Food-insecure people in targeted areas are better able to withstand shocks and stresses throughout the year.
Based on 19/20 Deyr performance it is expected that the food security situation will improve in 2020. Considering food security trend analysis and needs covered by other food security cluster members, WFP proposes to assist 700,000 beneficiaries with relief assistance for the first 6 months a reduction of 30-35 percent compared to the target in the last quarter of 2019.
A total of 980,000 people will be targeted to receive assistance to meet immediate food gaps, improve household food security, build household and community assets that strengthen livelihoods.

Project Activities

WFP implements activities under an integrated and multi-sectoral approach, leveraging resources with other partners to enable households meet immediate access to food, while protecting and restoring livelihoods and supporting resilience against shocks. Vulnerable women, men, boys and girls will be assisted through unconditional and conditional transfers in targeted IPC 3 and above areas to reach 980,000 people through the following:1. Provision of targeted relief rations providing up to 80 percent of energy requirements for households through in kind or cash unconditional transfers. For cash-based transfers, 100% of the food component of the MEB is provided (target – 700,000 beneficiaries); Jan-June (700,000 beneficiaries), July-Dec (490,000 beneficiaries)2. Rehabilitation of productive assets and skills training that will enhance resilience through conditional food/cash transfers based on seasonality (target 280,000 beneficiaries for 12 months)3. Beneficiaries will be targeted geographically through gender analysis-informed food security and nutrition needs assessments and targeted at the household level using vulnerability criteria that reflect the needs of men, women, boys and girls.4. Biometric registration of beneficiaries through SCOPE, socio-demographic profiling and assistance will be monitored through the SCOPE platform.5. WFP will aim to monitor 30 percent of all activities in a given month. In addition to physical monitoring, WFP will use alternate monitoring through its call center to cross-check information collected through physical monitoring and equip beneficiaries with an opportunity to contact directly WFP in case they have any questions, feedback and/or complaints and to provide them feedback on the action taken. Beneficiaries receive messages on programmes and entitlements as well as information on the hotlines and Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs).6. WFP will continue to co-chair and report to the food security cluster on a monthly basis and participate in or lead emergency food security assessments when necessary.7. WFP will continue to work in close coordination with the Cash Working Group (CWG), and participate in market assessments. WFP is currently co-chairing the Cash Working Group.

Mitigation Measures

• WFP has a large presence in Somalia and works with cooperating partners (CPs) to provide assistance in all regions of the country with humanitarian access. Beneficiaries do not have to leave their places of residence to access WFP assistance. The use of cash-based transfers and SCOPE cards allow beneficiaries to access their entitlement at convenient locations. • At the onset of any project WFP carries out consultations with the target communities, local authorities and partners to discuss overall objectives, duration and mode of implementation of the project. The eligibility criteria for the project, vulnerability and the rationale behind the targeting is discussed to avoid any backlash that could lead to violence against the recipients of assistance.• To mitigate against exclusion and marginalisation WFP employs community-based targeting principles and processes through which the most vulnerable and under-represented members of the communities including IDPs participate. Communities are consulted on the validation of vulnerability criteria, formation of all-inclusive selection committee for the identification of most vulnerable households at the village level with specific attention is paid to age, gender, minorities, disability and clan representation from across the community. WFP supports participation of both men, women and vulnerable groups by providing safe spaces for these groups to provide feedback e.g. women-only focus discussion groups• • To complement field monitoring visits WFP has a robust complaint feedback mechanism (CFM) that handles complaints and feedback from all beneficiaries and stakeholders. The CFM strengthens accountability to communities, identifies a range of protection, fraud and diversion issues and other WFP delivery assistance concerns. Complaints can be made through a Somalia-based toll-free hotline, directly (face to face) to WFP and CP staff at implementation sites, telephone calls, email or short messaging to WFP offices, through local authorities and leaders. Information on WFP’s hotline numbers are available through posters at food and cash distributions points and is printed on SCOPE cards. • WFP sensitizes beneficiaries, communities, gatekeepers and local authorities on the rationale for providing targeted populations with assistance and importance of receiving the full transfer. The use of biometric identification using SCOPE cards ensures that only intended beneficiaries receive their assistance thus mitigating risk of transfers going to non-intended people.• WFP works closely with its CPs to ensure the safety of the men and women accessing cash-based transfers and in-kind assistance. Travelling distances are reduced to ensure that beneficiaries do not travel more than 4 kms in high risk areas and 10 kms in safer locations to collect entitlements. Beneficiaries can redeem their transfers at over 1,000 retailers/cash points to reduce queuing time and overcrowding. Cash assistance is provided in batches on different days over extended periods of time to enable financial institutions plan and ensure available resources. WFP continues to work closely with local authorities and service providers to enhance security measures at distribution points and ensure provision of culturally-appropriate WASH facilities.• WFP has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse which focuses on acts committed by WFP employees or others associated with its work against the people it serves. WFP’s CPs are required to abide by WFP’s regulations and to adhere to its commitments against harassment and abuse. WFP in collaboration with the Gender-Based Violence-GBV sub-cluster in Somalia, has developed a GBV referral and programming protocol that outlines WFP's corporate strategy on GBV, particularly on reporting and referring cases for medical, legal or psychosocial assistance, as well as working with GBV survivors in terms of programming.

Please provide further explanation

Many vulnerable Somalis, particularly IDPs, struggle to meet their food and nutrition needs even after crises have abated. Others face chronic food insecurity as a result of poverty and exposure to shocks over many years. In response to this, WFP will implement broader safety net and resilience-strengthening activities, working across the humanitarian-development-peace nexus which are also aligned with the humanitarian response plan, chapters 7 and 9 of the NDP; and the livelihoods, employment, education, displacement, and safety nets recovery needs identified in the drought impact needs assessment which focus on resilience-building. This will include the following: 1. Food-insecure IDP and urban households receive safety-net assistance in the form of conditional or unconditional cash-based transfers and gender-informed social behaviour change communication that improve their food security and nutrition status and enhance self-reliance. 2. Food-insecure people receive safety-net assistance in the form of conditional in-kind or cash-based transfers for vocational training and the rehabilitation of community assets and gender-informed SBCC that reduce vulnerability to climate-related shocks and protect access to food.3. School-aged children in targeted areas receive safety-net assistance in the form of a nutritious meal every day they attend school. They also benefit from gender-informed SBCC and from the improved capacity of related ministries to meet their basic food and nutrition needs and increase school attendance and retention. WFP continues to be actively engaged with other stakeholders in the Durable Solutions discussions and meetings. We have also worked closely with other agencies such as UNHCR under the UN funded Peace-building Returnees programme in Baidoa.
