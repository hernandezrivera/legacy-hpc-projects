Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to the reduction of avoidable mortality and morbidity through the provision of life-saving primary and reproductive health services to the vulnerable IDPs and poor host communities through rapid and responsive healthcare packages which will focus in helping vulnerable women, girls, boys and men to achieve their potentials. IRW aims to use static health facilities and mobile clinics to quickly respond to emerging health needs of the target IDPs and host communities.
The facts observed in humanitarian situation is that population displacement exacerbates poor health outcomes in emergency settings both the displaced people and their host communities due to a lack of access to preventives, curatives and referral services, destruction of public health infrastructure and disruption of continuity of care. Health risks such as malnutrition among children under five, limited access to adequate supplies of clean and safe water, exposure to gender based violence, preference of women to give birth at home and lack of awareness on key health education messages have contributed to make individuals, specially boys and girls under the age of five and women, in the targeted sites more vulnerable to life threatening health risks. In Daynile the health care services are required to scale up by the partners to meet the needs of the growing IDPs and to have the capacity to respond to the key diseases such as cholera and polio. The current health status of the Daynile is classified as ‘fragile’. This is exacerbated by the presence of huge number of IDPs in the camps with aggravating factors such as probability of epidemic occurrences like measles, cholera, malaria or other communicable diseases. Besides, there is a low level of measles vaccination coverage and vitamin A supplementation, inadequate safe water supplies and sanitation.
The project will focus on the crisis affected populations, including both IDPs and vulnerable host communities. Within the target populations, IRW will focus on ensuring access to services for the women, men, boys and girls as well as the most vulnerable among these groups such as women between the ages of (15 to 49), children, elderly persons, and persons with specific needs (disability), pregnant and lactating mothers and also GBV survivors.
The overall aim of the project is to reduce the vulnerability to health related emergencies of both host and IDPs and host communities in Daynile and Balcad districts, by combining health emergency response/control (including safety nets) and institutional capacity building for preparedness. The project purpose is perfectly integrated within the Health Cluster strategy and is in line with all the Clusters priorities: • Maintain emergency primary health care services in targeted areas through provision basic equipment, drugs, medical supplies, basic lab equipment and supplies • Provision and prepositioning of essential medicines, vaccines and supplies • Communicable disease control and outbreak response including supplies • Support immunizations via fixed and mobile health clinics targeting displaced people, and other vulnerable groups including emergency mass vaccination campaigns • Capacity building of health authorities and staff, mainly on: a. Emergency preparedness and communicable disease control and outbreak response, awareness raising, hygiene promotion, education and participation in health-related issues and non-communicable disease treatment (even when complicated).
The project also aims to contribute to the prevention and response of waterborne diseases in the target area using a two-fold approach focusing on direct services and health education/promotion. Closely linked with environmental management, waterborne diseases are best prevented through community-wide mechanisms of good hygiene practices, access to adequate sanitation facilities and clean water.

Project Activities

1. Provision of primary health services through static and mobile clinics focused on Mother and Child Health, BEmONC services to a total of 74,000 people disaggregated to 33,056 women, 25,910 children and 15,034 men.2. Provide maternal healthcare services to 1,000 women of child bearing age of IDPs, Returnees and Host community including ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming, LLIN and TT vaccine.3. Conduct at least 240 safe and clean deliveries in health facilities during emergencies by trained health workers. The facilities will be provided with clean delivery kits.4. Provision of referral services to 4,000 severely ills such as women with complicated obstetrics cases, severely malnourished individuals, chronic illnesses, etc.5. Increase community awareness of GBV services through trained staff.6. Conduct rapid health assessments of suspected disease outbreaks and other reported emergencies.7. Provide case management responses to disease outbreaks.8. Provision and support to routine and mass campaigns immunizations, particularly for boys and girls under five.9. Provide training to primary health care workers in GBV, HIV, IMCI and safe delivery.10. Participate in the disease surveillance/early warning system to identify potential outbreaks for quick and adequate responses. 11. Implement and supervise social mobilization to increase awareness of preventive behaviors and early health seeking in affected communities. 12. Regular reporting to donor, MOH and cluster as required
