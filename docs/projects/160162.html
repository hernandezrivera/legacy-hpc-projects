Project Summary

This is a comprehensive Gender Based Violence (GBV) programme that attacks the root causes of GBV in Emergencies through evidence based social norm change progammes; mitigates the risk of GBV occurring in camp and IDP concentration points; and provides multi-sectoral services to survivors of GBV. The three core components of the programme will be implemented in key accessible areas of Somalia in all Federal Member states and engages UNICEF's network of civil society partners who will continue to benefit from capacity building support delivered through UNICEF's local, regional, international team of GBV experts. The purpose of the project is to ensure continued expansion of life-saving services whilst at the same time undermining the structural causes of GBV. We will aim to reach 3000 survivors of GBV; improve the safety of IDP camps and concentration points decreasing the likelihood of SGBV for more than 1 million displaced people; and roll out the Communities Care programme reaching 50,000 people through targetted norm change activities.

Project Activities

OUTPUT 1: Women, men, girls and boys affected by conflict and humanitarian emergencies have equal access to a timely, age, sex and culturally sensitive GBV package of services/ protection responses Activity 1.1: Provision of age, sex and culturally sensitive protection services. Activity 1.2: Training of key service providers in provision of survivor friendly GBV services (include training on the Clinical Management of Rape protocols, provision of psychological first aid, training in case management and improved policing through training of police officers) Activity 1.3: Strengthen coordinated protection responses through GBV Working Group and information sharing mechanisms among humanitarian, child protection and GBV actors Activity 1.4: Broad based dissemination of protection messages via multi media channels OUTPUT 2: Enhanced protection of vulnerable communities in IDP camps and hosting sites Activity 2.1: Implement recommendations of the 2018 safety audit in the WASH, Shelter, Camp Layout, Education, Nutrition, Health sectors. Activity 2.2: Conduct a repeat safety audit in key IDP hosting sites to assess improvements made against the 2019 safety audit. Activity 2.3: Integrating GBV in Emergency guidelines training provided to humanitarian workers from WASH, Shelter, Camp Layout, Education, Nutrition, Health clusters. OUTPUT 3: Enhanced community understanding of sexual violence and child abuse to undermine harmful social norms and increase access to services through community referrals and self-reporting. Activity 3.1: Scaling up of community-based protection system through Communities Cares which increases community-based referrals of survivors of abuse, decreases stigmatization and reduces the vulnerability of women and children. Activity 3.2: Community mobilization and sensitization campaigns reach and engage community members through the Communities Cares Community Discussions and Action Plans community dialogues, house to house visits, interactive radio and SMS campaigns, public events and trainings are undertaken by community leaders to change harmful norms.

Mitigation Measures

As in all GBV programmes, the risk to survivors of further stigmatisation, abuse or isolation is high if they access professional support services. In order to mitigate against this risk we ensure partners apply fundamental principles in the delivery of services ensuring absolute confidentiality (in both consultation as well as record keeping); ensure survivors are given full power and control through the case management and care process through the application of informed consent procedures. This is monitored by UNICEF and capacity support is provided on a rolling basis to all partners.

Please provide further explanation

The entire GBV programme supported by UNICEF aims for the development of formal systems aimed at the long term identification, care and protection of peoples vulnerable to SGBV. The programme also acts on the informal systems that either enable or disable normative systems at the community level which are the fundamental cause of GBV. This programme aims to change these norms and, thereby, de-stigmatise the provision of services to survivors, as well as, create communities that are fundamentally safer and more equitable for women and girls.
