Project Summary

The proposed project aims to support the ability of the population in Al-Qusair and Tal-Kalakh to withstand and live under the prevailing conditions through improve the economic situation of the population, and help them obtain income-generating sources through providing grants of animal production and cultivation of vegetables in Al-Qusair.
Al-Birr response is based on supporting the food security and agriculture in Al-Qusair and Tal kalakh through provide the livestock and agricultural grants that suit the nature of the region and the population, by two kinds of grants
one of them depends on providing 400 grants related to livestock to produce meat and milk by providing two sheep with its fodder for 6 months, utensils for animal feed, and periodic visits by veterinarians to supervise sheep health and treat them if needed.
The other grant will be agricultural grants to 600 beneficiaries to support the agricultural production which includes tilling the farmlands of beneficiaries, providing seeds of vegetable for the cultivation, and trickle irrigation network to cultivation 1 donum, while the grants will be monitored by periodic visits by agricultural engineers to ensure that the grants are going well.
Al-Birr will monitor the project activities by two M&E staff to ensure that the project is on the right track and it is achieving its goals according to the specified indicators.
Al-Birr will conduct tenders to providing the inputs and items of the grants, to deliver them to the beneficiaries, so there are no cash transfers or e-vouchers to them.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has entered into its tenth year, with 2020 having seen the emergence of new and complex socioeconomic difficulties that are further exacerbating the food security and livelihood situation of families across the country. The situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated, with an estimated 14.2m people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6%of the total population. Based on a countrywide assessment of 36,484 households and technical reviews and consultations, at least 12.4m people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3m people are considered severely food insecure. Furthermore, all 1.7m people living in camps are deemed to be 100% food insecure, and 1.8m people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity. The results show a sharp increase from last year, with an increase of 4.5m people facing acute food insecurity in 2021 compared to 2020. HNO2021_ Unprecedented livestock asset losses situation affecting livelihoods has led to irreversible coping strategies. In addition, livestock production is also further constrained due to a reduction of livestock number, in comparison to the last comprehensive livestock census conducted in 2010. According to the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform, sheep numbers fell by 45%, goat numbers by 30%, cattle numbers by 40%.in the end, the agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 25 to 30 percent of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. For these reasons, saving, restoring, and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase self-reliance and strengthen the resilience of Syrians.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented through:1- Animal production grants: This will be done by opening the registration to the needy and affected population within the targeted areas, and then a socio-economy study will be conducted to determine beneficiaries who deserve to receive the grant and who are able to work and develop it.- Veterinarians will be contracted to conduct periodic visits twice a month to each grant, to ensure that livestock is well cared for and to provide them with medical care when needed.Accepted persons will receive two sheep with its fodder for 6 months, and utensils for animal feed.- Supply contracts will also be signed with suppliers to provide the required numbers of livestock at the beginning of the project.2- Agricultural grants: this activity will be conducted by opening the registration for people who have farmland but can not cultivate it because they do not have the necessary resources, and then a socio-economy study will be conducted to determine beneficiaries who deserve to receive the grant and who are able to work and develop it.- Accepted persons will receive a drip irrigation network and 4 types of seasonal seeds, which are sufficient to cultivate 1 dunum, and agricultural tractors will be contracted to cultivate the lands of the beneficiaries. - Moreover, the 2 monitoring and evaluation officers will be recruited and responsible for monitoring and evaluation of the project implementation under the supervision of the M&E Unit of the Project Management Office, as a periodic visit will be conducted according to the M&E plan.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Increase in the prices of agriculture input and livestock due to fluctuations of the exchange rate. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation measure: Al-Birr through its wide-range network of suppliers and its good relationship with them, will work to contracting them to provide the inputs of Al-Birr project since the signature of the agreement. Risk: Delays in providing agricultural inputs to farmers, in time for cultivation, Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation measure:Al-Birr will contract with the suppliers once the signature of the agreement to start supplying the agricultural inputs and transferring the value of the contracts to them will be after the arrival of the first payment Risk: Beneficiaries are not able to deal with emergency accidents of their projects (exposure of livestock to disease, or exposure of agricultural inputs to pests), Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium.Mitigation measure: Al-Birr will contract with veterinarians and agricultural engineers to supervisor agricultural livestock grants provided to beneficiaries through periodic visits to them for 6 months.
