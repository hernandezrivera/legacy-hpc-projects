Project Summary

With Libya in a state of protracted conflict, WFP’s Interim Country Strategic Plan (ICSP) aims to introduce programmes that would support the resilience of vulnerable communities across the country. The reduction in available migrant labour, both skilled and unskilled, combined with the instability of Government salaries, has left many Libyan families reliant on coping mechanisms in order to meet their basic food needs. In this context, WFP Libya is scaling-up the Food Assistance for Training pilot, which started in 2019, matching people in need with skills trainings that prepares them for available work in the local job markets. Conditional food assistance is provided as long as minimum attendance requirements are met, giving an immediate boost to food security. After the training, if successful, food security levels should be improved, with less reliance on coping strategies. Household resilience to future shocks should also be improved due to an improved asset base through greater skills, tools, and savings.

1 - Needs

With Libya in a state of protracted conflict, WFP’s Interim Country Strategic Plan (ICSP) aimed to introduce programmes that would support the resilience of vulnerable communities across the country. The reduction in available migrant labour, both skilled and unskilled, combined with the instability of Government salaries, has left many Libyan families reliant on coping mechanisms in order to meet their basic food needs. The World Bank notes that the Libyan economy “has recently been hit by four overlapping shocks: an intensifying conflict that suffocates economic activity, the closure of oil fields that puts the country’s major income-generating activity largely on hold, decreasing oil prices that reduce income from oil production in surviving fields, and the COVID-19 pandemic.” As a result, Libya is expected to suffer from a deep recession in 2020, while the Central Bank of Libya continues to manage the situation by limiting hard currency for essential imports only. Meanwhile, the Libyan Dinar lost 54 percent of its value in the first half of 2020. According to the ILO, approximately 1.8 million out of a labour force of 2.42 million works in the public sector, which is very high for a country with a population of 6.7 million. This means that the majority of Libyan households are supported through public sector salaries, which continue to be paid, even if somewhat irregularly. As such the impact of COVID-19 restrictions has been limited for many Libyan households. A UNDP assessment under the UN framework for the socio-economic response to COVID-19 noted that informal sector workers reliant on daily wages would be hardest hit due to the lack of social protection and movement restrictions across the country. This includes migrants in particular. UN Women estimated that 52 percent of working women were negatively impacted in their work, while a USAID assessment estimates that 110,000 jobs will be lost in the private sector in 2020.

2 - Activities

WFP's Food Assistance for Training always begins with a rapid labour market assessment in the specific target location, learning what skills vulnerable people tend to have in the area and what sort of jobs they are interested in. Businesses and relevant local authorities are also consulted at this stage to explore what jobs are currently available or needed in the local job market. WFP and its partners then asses whether appropriate facilities and trainers are available for supporting trainings on the jobs that are needed. Conflict sensitivity assessments are carried out at all stages of the programme cycle, and WFP is also exploring possible contributions to peace through the Food Assistance for Training programme. Once training courses are selected and planned, participants are chose from existing lists of people in need from various local sources, with a secondary screening for likely vulnerability to food insecurity. Courses are then implemented, lasting anywhere between four and twelve weeks, and usually including a soft skills component so that participants can learn the basics of business and marketing. Throughout the training, WFP and its partners work to connect participants to available jobs. Also during the training, food assistance is provided to participants on a monthly basis, as long as they attend at least 80 percent of classes. This boosts food security in the short-term, while the income earned through jobs after the programme helps reduce coping strategies in the medium-term. The aim is that long-term resilience to shocks is strengthened. WFP is also increasingly integrating savings groups and distance learning of life skills, including the International Computer Driving License, into the project.
