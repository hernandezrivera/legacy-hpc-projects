Project Summary

According to the Health cluster targets in 2020 there are almost 230,000 people in needs in Sool and Sanag regions. These people are in need of emergency health interventions and access to basic health services. The ratio of primary healthcare in Sool and Sanag regions are 0.89 and 0.41 facility/10,000 respectively which much lower than the standard (1); making the health service availability very scattered and fragmented. Access to health care in Sool and Sanag is therefore very poor compared to the national average to many factors including poor partner coverage, poor capacity of existing facilities and lack of basic medications and supply making the service output generally unsatisfactory. CARE conducted rapid need assessment in March 2019 in Sool and Sanag across 5 districts and found that the key barriers to health access included: lack of money and poorly functioning health facility in terms of staffing and availability of supply and equipment as the main reasons why they don’t access health care services. Almost 70% of the respondents reported that they pay for health services and this will further hinder access of the poorest in the community. The drought has also brought outbreaks of diseases including AWD/cholera and measles. Most health facilities do not have cold chains and EPI services are limited to once a week. CARE wants to support health strengthening in these regions by provision of medical supplies and capacity building health workers to address these issues. Somalia has high burden of communicable diseases. Communicable disease remains the leading cause of death; including both environmentally driven and vaccine-preventable. Detection and control remain a major challenge due to weak surveillance systems and low accessibility to health care. Therefore CARE is proposing to support the control of communicable diseases at the grassroots level through dissemination of health education messages, provision of health mobile clinics for hard to reach areas and the equipping and improving the capacity of the healthcare staff in the management and control of diseases. The maternal mortality ratio is Somalia in one of the highest at 732 per 100,000 and this is due to complications that arise during late pregnancy and childbirth (haemorrhage, prolonged and obstructed labour, eclampsia and infection). Access to maternal health services remains low, with approximately only one-third of births attended by skilled birth attendants. In Sool and Sanag only 32% and 46% of the health facilities offer antenatal Care (ANC) services making difficult for mothers with pregnancy complications to be identified and referred for treatment. Access to basic obstetric emergency care is also limited with almost no referral pathways and systems in place to refer mothers with complications for support. Health seeking behaviour in Somalia is very poor with the number of people seeking outpatient consultation less than the global targets of 5 visits/per person/year. Currently in Puntland facilities get 0.32 visits per person per year and 0.22 visits/person/year in Somaliland. The poor health seeking behaviour is mainly due to low literacy rate, preference for traditional medicine, perceived low quality of health services, cost of accessing health service and poor availability of essential medicines. With 70% of Somalis with no access to safe water supply or sanitation and in rural areas 83% of the population practicing open defecations it is contributing to high incidence of diarrheal diseases among children, along with respiratory infections. In light of the growing humanitarian needs in country CARE is proposing to provide an integrated essential package of health and nutrition services to communities living in drought affected areas of Sool and Sanaag. This program will also be linked with other CARE water, sanitation and health (WASH); Food security and livelihood (FSL) programs in order to address the underlying causes of diseases.

Project Activities

CARE, in partnership with the MoH, will address the health challenges in drought affected areas of Sool and Sanag regions through emergency health program in 21 fixed MHCs and 32 outreach sites across 8 districts in Sool and Sanaag regions (Badhan, Dhahar, Taleh, Lasqoray, Caynaba, Lascanood, Erigavo and Ceelafweyn). The project will target pregnant and lactating mothers, newborns and children under 5 who are most vulnerable to malnutrition and disease in drought affected pastoralist communities. Below are brief summary of the different health activities that will be implemented. Health systems and clinical support: This program will ensure smooth running of these facilities and outreach services, including proper patient consultations and facility surveillance. Each static MCHs will have adequate and trained staff and will provide curative and preventive medical services, focusing most on services for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5. Activities• Provision of equitable access to basic and lifesaving health services at all supported sites • Support capacity building of health staff through an initial MCH training and On-Job-Training (OJT) for follow-up on identified gaps Communicable diseases: The focus of this intervention will be to treat and prevent common causes of morbidity and mortality. This intervention will seek to prioritize the provision of care and immunization for children <5 years of age, pregnant and lactating women. Pregnant and lactating women will receive tetanus vaccinations to support their health, as well as that of their unborn children. All health facilities will maintain surveillance and reporting networks for disease early warning alerts and outbreak detection. CARE in conjunction with the MOH, will support immediate and appropriate responses to disease outbreaks. Activities• Provision of treatment for epidemic-prone and communicable diseases at all supported sites• Provision of support for national vaccination campaigns in addition to routine immunization services for children under 5 and pregnant and lactating mothers at all supported sites Reproductive Health: Antenatal and postnatal care will be provided through the static sites. ANC visits will be sufficient to provide tetanus toxoid immunization, provide supplementation and administer prophylaxis for malaria. During antenatal clinics and health education sessions, pregnant mothers will be targeted with information, education and communication (IEC) messages to initiate family planning counselling; promote healthy diet and lifestyle during pregnancy including exclusive breastfeeding; help the family make appropriate decision about where the mother should deliver; and make necessary preparations for delivering at the health facility. This intervention will also encourage at least two postpartum visits for new mothers within the first six weeks after delivery; immediately after delivery and after the end of the first week. The identification and referral of complicated pregnancies to the nearest hospital for timely and quality emergency obstetric care will be done. Key staff at the MCH will trained in the identification of SGBV cases and enable referral of cases.Activities• Provision of equitable access to prenatal and postnatal health services at supported sites• Provision of access to facility-based safe deliveries at static sites• Training of health staff on early detection, treatment and referral of pregnancy-related complications • Identification and referral of SGBV cases. Community Health Workers will be trained on prevalent conditions such as symptoms of fever, diarrhea and respiratory illnesses, and malaria. CHWs will learn how to identify, classify and refer common childhood illnesses. They will provide individual and group counseling on positive heath practices at the sites and support health, nutrition and wash education at the community level.

Mitigation Measures

CARE is committed in protecting its beneficiaries before, during and after service delivery by providing a safe working space and privacy for consultations. CARE will provide equitable access to health care based on health needs of the vulnerable groups such as children under 5, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly and people with disabilities. CARE recognises that marginalised communities may feel excluded from the services provided and to tackle this CARE will 1) ensure they are identified during the design stage and mapped out; 2) consult with them during the design of the project to ensure they feel safe to access the services provided by CARE: 3) CARE will ensure a representative of the marginalised group is always present during screening and identification process in ongoing as a community volunteer; 4) follow up on any case of concern through the FCRM mechanism in place in case there is any marginalisation and address it; 5) Inform communities and beneficiaries about CAREs policies regarding discrimination and impartiality of assistance to improve awareness raising and show zero tolerance to discrimination based on creed and race. Care recognises there is risk of extortion or theft of the medical supplies and to mitigate against this we will do the following. 1) Provide a safe storage for all medical supplies in the health centre with a lock and key system to avoid theft by the community or staff; 2) Conduct physical stock count each month to determine use against the consultations given is appropriate; 3) to investigate any mis-use and encourage caregivers to come forward about any case of extortion or harassment during the consultation. Although CARE has complaint mechanisms there may be barriers preventing beneficiaries to report concerns and CARE will mitigate against this risks by1 1) always ensuring there are multiple and confidential ways of reporting; 2) make sure that beneficiaries feel protected and that their concerns will not reach a third party without their approval; 3) ensure there are always a female and a male staff present at the centres if the persons feels safer to report concerns face to face; 4) to respond to concerns in a timely and efficient manner so as to encourage beneficiaries to come forward; 5) to always be impartial and make clear to the beneficiaries that CARE will not tolerate anyone harming their beneficiaries for seeking services and appropriate referral GBV pathways are identified and used. CARE will ensure there is great visibility around the program to improve service uptake by all members of the society and will reach out to minority groups and people living in hard to reach areas through mobile health teams who will conduct daily outreach activities. At all supported health facilities, services will be provided during hours that appropriate for all persons, including women and children. The facilities have adequate female staff with requisite skills and experience. This goes a long way in ensuring that female beneficiaries feel comfortable accessing health services at these facilities. All data will be disaggregated by age and sex to better inform programing and policies Security and safety conditions at nutrition sites will be regularly monitored to ensure that they are safe and accessible for all groups. Key front health line workers will be trained on Clinical management of Rape (CMR) and sensitization on SGBV issues in order to provide referral support for SGBV cases.
