Project Summary

Since August to 2017 to date most of the shelter and site improvement interventions are made by bamboo which is a low-durability construction material, these interventions are easily damaged over time and due to any kind of disaster, especially natural disaster. So the shelters and site improvement interventions need regular repair/ maintenance or sometimes construction of new shelter with durable materials like treated bamboo, reinforce cement concrete (RCC) pad footing, nuts and bolts etc.. These services are changing and being refined over time to improve the quality of living and accessibility considering the needs of the beneficiaries. Based on these, in 2022 BRAC shelter sector will use improved construction materials and also follow the sector and RRRC office provided design for shelters. In 2022, BRAC’s shelter sector will use the improved construction material for construction of all kind of shelter like improve shelter, transitional shelter, transitional shelter-stilt to ensure the durability, safety and security of beneficiary households, except for emergency shelters and regular shelters.
The project will involve beneficiary household members, of especially vulnerable HHs in construction of shelters. Emphasis will be given for especially women headed households, person with disability, elderly and extremely vulnerable individuals (EVI) families/HHs. y with differently able household. This is to avoid the exclusion of certain groups from humanitarian services in Shelter and NFI. In case of emergency shelters and regular shelters, the support and assistance through materials distributions to household and for camp level site improvement interventions will use materials as used previously - untreated borak bamboo, muli bamboo, tarpaulin, rope, GI ware etc. Also 15,190 solar lamps for ten camps and 200 in four unions of host community will be given.
Beneficiary selection will be through house visits considering people in extreme vulnerability, prioritizing women headed HHs, elderly, person with disability etc. Split of the assistance per camp will be coordinated with other actors especially UNHCR, implementing similar activities to avoid duplication. Specific activities under this project include: Construction of 1,781 Mid Term Shelters in 7 camps (3, 4 Ext., 7, 21, 27, KTP-RC and NYP-RC); Repair and maintenance of 120 community building in 10 camps (1E, 1W, 3, 4 Ext., 7, 21, 26, 27, KTP-RC and NYP-RC); Construction including maintenance and improvement for drain, stairs, small bridges, retaining wall, ramps, handrails etc. in 10 camps; Provide 15,190 Rohingya HHs with solar lamps in 10 camps. Provide 200 solar lamps to host community HHs in 4 unions; Involve 576 persons (women, elderly persons, persons with disability) in small scale shelter related works in 10 camps. Engage 38,255 women in participatory planning of shelter interventions.
BRAC’s M&E team will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring of shelter/NFI materials distribution and shelter quality. BRAC’s community feedback process collects information from beneficiary household in design, implementation and completion stages based on which the shelter sector responds. The project’s M&E plan will track sector achievement as per target, on-going assessment, data collection and analysis. As per BRAC’s strict safe-guarding policy, all staff will be oriented on PSEA/safeguarding and gender mainstreaming, to ensure safe environment for both beneficiaries and staff, and interventions tailored as per needs of the vulnerable groups.

1 - Identified Needs

Currently over 900,000 Rohingya refugees live in overcrowded shelters across 34 camps with limited access to essential services like food, shelter, water, sanitation, protection, and health care etc. Since the refugee influx in 2017, BRAC’s Shelter Sector has conducted periodical and annual needs assessments and in collaboration and close coordination with Government of Bangladesh, Donors and different stakeholders have strived to provide the shelter and site improvement services.BRAC’s shelter sector conducted a needs assessment for 2022 on shelter and site improvement through focus group discussion (FGD) and door to door household survey in September 2021 to identify the needs of the community. The need assessment determined the type of needs and assistance required to address existing gaps and ensure and durable support to the community. Need assessment has found that still there is a considerable area to be improved and strengthen as many of the shelters are on steep and deforested slopes of sand and clay, camps face additional risks of landslides, waterlogging etc. 72% of Rohingya households reported shelter issues at the time of data collection. 36% of Rohingya households reported not having made improvements to their shelter despite having reported issues (JSMNA Oct 2021). In the host community 71% HHs reported shelter issues and 30% HHs reported that no improvement had been made to their shelters despite reporting problems. In the host 63% reported that they had no money to buy materials and 24% had no money to pay for the labour. Also need assessment revealed that quality of the current shelters and poor terrain as well as the lack of privacy within these shelters has a serious impact on the physical and psychological well-being of the refugees, especially for women, children, older persons and person with specific needs (PwSN).Despite the efforts of the humanitarian response to increase women’s participation in community based discussions, so that their specific needs are heard and addressed, there is a community resistance to this, especially in the camps. Women and girls still lack access to information about provisions and services, complaints and feedback mechanisms, making them less confident to voice their needs, complain and seek redressal. Rohingya households and host community require essential needs and replenishment of items, such as solar lamps which have been distributed in the previous year and is amongst the commonly requested NFI items. Currently Rohingya HHs in camp as well as in the host community report not having enough light.
