Project Summary

The project will provide life-saving and life-sustaining assistance to ensure access to livelihoods for the IDPs and host communities in Meconta district, particularly vulnerable women and girls. Interventions will restore the normalcy of the life of the IDPs providing them livelihoods opportunities, addressing the food insecurity, women economic empowerment and wellbeing.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Nampula Province has been recorded recently an increasingly number of IDPs who are seeking refuge. Majority are children, adolescent girls and boys and women. There is an increasing food insecurity among the IDP’ families and this is exacerbated by COVID-19 pandemic. The situation is putting lot of strains on the families which are likely to exhaust the little savings they had and are turning to negative coping mechanisms, including child marriage and transactional sex. There is a particularly need for attention to women economic resilience and addressing the food insecurity, supporting with livelihoods restoration and income generating activities, especially for vulnerable women (young mothers, single/widow women), that are founding themselves in high vulnerability situation.The project will provide life-saving and life-sustaining assistance to IDPs, particularly to vulnerable women, to ensure access to livelihoods, by promoting food production and livelihood diversification. This will be done through provision of agricultural inputs (distribution of tools and seeds for small scale agriculture production) and community-driven and gender-focused emergency employment (Cash for Work and income generating activities). The project will be implemented in Meconta District, targeting 10.000 IDPs and host families (1.500 people).

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The following activities will be carried out:• Conduct mapping and assessment of small-scale farming business opportunities for internally displaced women, particular vulnerable women (i.e. young mothers, single women, widow);• Faciltate the set up of the beneficiary registration committees with members of good reputation elected in public gatherings and with equal representation of men and women;• Identify in consultation with key stakeholders the potential community rehabilitation schemes in the centers themselves or host communities;• Facilitate the selection and registration of beneficaries for CFW schemes;• Support the beneficiary registration community committees in collaboration to identify and select vulnerable families (particularly families lead by young mothers/single/widow women) for provision of agriculture inputs to improve their livelhoods; • Organize training sessions on life skills for target vulnerable groups; • Provision of small business start-up kits to establish new income generating activities/ and seeds and tools for small scale farming for women groups;• Provision of training on small bussines development plans for the vulnerable groups;• Facilitate Cash for Work short schemes for construction/ rehabilitation for small community initiatives;• Carry out community awareness through use of community radio on women's rights and gendered roles and promoting a positive environment for women empowerment and issues around early marriage, GBV, abuse in the home and community.The project will be implemented in Meconta District (Namialo and Corrane), targeting 10.000 IDPs and host families (1.500 people).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will identify and understand the IDPs capacities and coping mechanisms through a community-based approach, which will allow us to be more aware of the broader political context, to recognize and understand changes in roles and power relations between women and men, young and old, and majority and minority groups, and to work with leaders and all members of the community to ensure respect for rights and equality. This will help us to ensure that our interventions do not undermine community structures which can provide protection and support.By working closely with the community, the project will be better informed about the protection risks faced by different groups, depending on their age, gender and background Collaboration with government, national and international non-governmental partners is also essential to provide protection and assist persons of concern in claiming their rights to humanitarian assistance

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The consultation process has been carried out through meetings with stakeholders and support for public hearings to set up the beneficiary registration committers and selection of the beneficiaries. We are already working with the local authorities and leaders to mitigate risks that arise for women experiencing backlash from community for participation in the project, through appropriate communication and reporting mechanisms, ensuring transparency on the beneficiary targeting.The feedback and complaints mechanism that Plan International uses include suggestion boxes, community engagement meetings, participation in camp meetings, surveys and evaluations, help desk, and the national emergency green line “1458”. In all these methods, we ensure the mechanisms are child friendly. These child friendly mechanisms promote gender and age-appropriate, inclusive, safe and confidential ways for children and young people to receive information, provide feedback and meaningfully participate in influencing humanitarian programming. In Namialo resettlement centers, Plan International already supported the District Directorate of Health to establish a health referral group with participation of IDPs. We have also trained the IDPs Protection Group in Child Protection and GBV referral and complaint mechanisms, including case management.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will follow the guiding principles of working IDPs with disabilities. These include Rights-based approach, with actions targeting persons with disabilities informed by the human rights principles and standards codified in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD). These include principles of nondiscrimination, respect for the inherent dignity, autonomy and independence of the individual, gender equality, respect for difference and diversity, and respect for the rights of women and children. The identification of people with special needs will be done through data regularly collected and shared by INGC. The project will validate the existing data through direct engagement with IDPs management committees.To remove barriers to their access to assistance, the project will include people with disabilities, their families and caregivers in all community-based participatory assessments, provision of training and awareness-raising programs on their rights, to empower them to participate in community affairs and decision-making processes.Particular attention will be given to promotion of the inclusion of people with all types of disabilities in camp management structures (e.g., camp committees) and in community leadership positions (e.g., through quotas/reserved seats/awareness raising).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Food secure communities, especially those reliant on the environment for their livelihoods, require healthy and productive ecosystems. As such we will ensure that the interventions will take into consideration the environment and will be sensitive to climatic conditions as well to ensure current and future food security and access to water and energy. The livelihoods proposed interventions will be delivered in a way that preserve and restore the natural environment from further degradation and do not contribute to deforestation or soil erosion. For agriculture/small-scale farming opportunities, attention will be given on the water consumption for irrigation, water pollution with pesticides, loss of biodiversity (crop selection), soil degradation, risk of fire, use of inappropriate farming machinery. And sustainable farming/agricultural production solutions will be promoted whenever possible. In coordination with relevant stakeholders we will ensure that the access to natural resources and sue of the land for farming is governed. Similarly, we will not promote small-scale livelihoods intervention that could have environmental impact. Community rehabilitation schemes that are environmental friendly will be promoted.
