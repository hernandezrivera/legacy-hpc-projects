Project Summary

INTERSOS Action tackles identified problems and unmet needs, through an integrated protection response (GP, CP, GBV) targeting the most vulnerable population in areas with high severity conditions (3 and 4 as per Protection Sector Severity & PIN_HRP 2021) in Hama and Rural Damascus. In line with Sector 2021 Syria HRP, the Action aims to improve access to quality and specialized protection services for the most vulnerable women, men, boys and girls, including persons at risk and survivors of gender-based violence to contribute reducing life-threatened risks, through mobile and static approach. In 2021, through the proposed intervention INTERSOS will reach a total of 19688 beneficiaries (B4509; G4029; M5095; W6055). INTERSOS partners with Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) delivering the action through a two-tiered approach: a) work directly with most vulnerable boys and girls to respond to CP issues and concerns at individual level, through case management; work directly with vulnerable adults through group Psychosocial Support and referral to specialized services; b) work at community level with children’s families and community in order to foster a protective environment, through community based and capacity building initiatives. To maximize the impact of the Action and enhance local sustainable response capacity, INTERSOS has developed a response strategy based on a strong community-based approach coupled with a robust capacity building programme to enhance the quality of services delivered to the affected individuals, including those with specific and complex needs. Community-based outreach is designed to incite awareness and behavioural change in the targeted communities and to identify needs and support appropriate referral of vulnerable persons to relevant services. It incorporates the active involvement and mobilisation of local community members through awareness raising, capacity building/training on protection and GBV sensitive behaviour and methodologies, and through the support of community-based activities to initiate appropriate changes in attitudes and contributing to the prevention of GBV with a focus on changing harmful social norms. The cooperation with SARC volunteers and INTERSOS community mobilizers ensures the most vulnerable individuals can be reached and identified. The Action builds local capacities and resilience through training of SARC staff and development of peer support systems. INTERSOS will therefore contribute to GP: strengthen communities’ self-protection mechanisms to ensure sustainability of initiatives; improve protection needs assessments, and monitoring to identify needs, risks, and challenges; strengthen referral mechanisms to increase access to specialized care and support; expand PSS and case-management; GBV ensure that survivors of GBV can access specialized/response services and that the risks of GBV are prevented and mitigated; increase the provision of quality, specialized GBV services; expand PSS and case management; CP: Provide structured and sustained CP programmes including PSS for boys and girls; Provide CP awareness raising; Strengthen specialised CP services for boys and girls through case management; Capacity building of CP front line responders including systematizing efforts to build a sustainable child protection workforce through ensuring minimum standards.

4 - Needs

2021 HNO estimates 13.4M PiN of humanitarian assistance, where 95.1% is under severe, extreme and catastrophic conditions. Protection remains a priority with 13.13M PiN and 5.913M are children under 17 yo. GBV is a major concern, particularly sexual violence and harassment, domestic violence, and early/forced marriage (reported by 62% of communities). Deep-rooted social norms are normalizing this violence, severely affecting the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing (Voices from Syria, UNFPA, GBV AoR 2021). Women who have experienced divorce and widowhood and women/girls with disabilities continue to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination. Adolescent girls face specific challenges in accessing assistance as well as freedom of movement limitations imposed by family members. Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, detained, recruited by fighting parties or involved in child labour. Separation from caregivers is a persistent issue across Syria. Death of a caregiver (15% B & G), economic need (25%B; 13%G), child marriage (11%B; 44%G) and child recruitment (10%B) are among the top cited reasons for family separations. The absence of alternative care options is a concern. Boys are also understood to be at risk of sexual violence. Men are affected by restrictions of freedom of movement and forms of violence directly associated to hostilities (e.g. detention, recruitment), impacting their mental health and contributing to protection risks. Older people, in particular women, also have specific needs, including challenges and barriers in accessing services, securing personal documentation, and reuniting with family members. 27% of the Syrian population above 12 yo is reported to have disabilities (GPC).

5 - Implementation of activities

Provision of protection activities is conducted through mobile and static approach. INTERSOS provides capacity building on relevant protection subjects (protection principles, GBV, CP, Safe Identification&Referral) to SARC volunteers and community members who then sensitise the targeted communities. Community based committees are established capitalising where possible on existing community structures. Safe and confidential CM is provided for individuals at risk and survivors of violence, exploitation, abuse, and other forms of protection violations, including GBV. To mitigate and reduce the protection risk of the case, in kind assistance is provided in line with INTERSOS SoPs. Legal awareness is facilitated by INTERSOS and specific assistance on legal docs provided by SARC. PFA support/structured PSS for adults are carried out, focusing on emotional debriefing and resilience building. INTERSOS supports SARC in providing CP CM and structured PSS. The PSS activities are conducted within safe spaces. INTERSOS will coordinate with protection actors to strengthen the Referral Pathways to specialised services for women, men, boys and girls with specific needs. INTERSOS will lead GBV CM response at community level or through remote modalities. An effective Data Protection Management is promoted by INTERSOS through the implementation of Country-Level SOPs. A M&E plan is developed by INTERSOS. Quantitative&Qualitative indicators are included in the plan to measure not only the achievement rate, but also the quality. Regular field visits are conducted using specific tools: spot check, structured observation, H Assessment, PDM, pre/post data PSS. FGDs strengthening active participation of PoC are conducted to collect feedback.
