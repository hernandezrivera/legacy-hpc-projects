Project Summary

The project will provide life-saving and integrated multi-sector assistance through multi-purpose cash assistance and livelihoods support (Cluster Objectives 1 and 2), aiming to increase access to food through cash and increased food production. It will also promote access to basic services through market support and referral pathways as well as reduce aid dependence through introduction of sustainable approaches to income generation and food production (Cluster Objective 3). Protection and inclusion principles are mainstreamed throughout all activities.

1 - Needs

Despite relative stabilisation of national level conflict in South Sudan, recurring inter-communal clashes continue throughout the targeted counties of Upper Nile, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria States. In addition, economic and climatic crises have prevented an improvement in food security and de-stabilised markets. COVID-19 restrictions have been negatively impacting the population, while the official number of cases is low. Limited humanitarian access and movement has effectively prevented the population from accessing essential services. Food security outcomes have deteriorated, with six out of nine targeted counties projected to remain in emergency (IPC 4) or deteriorate even further. Fashoda and Magwi Counties are projected to remain in crisis (IPC 3). Both inter-community conflict and flooding have caused displacement in Jonglei with 800,000 people affected. In Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria, flooding has caused displacement and destruction of harvests, shelters and livelihoods. Neighbouring countries have started to reduce services to South Sudanese refugees to encourage voluntary returns. Such projected influx of returnees will deteriorate humanitarian indicators. Land contaminated with explosives limits the use for productive purposes, especially in Magwi County, exacerbating food insecurity and leading to fatalities and severe injuries. Protection, especially GBV concerns remain at the forefront in South Sudan, where over 65% of women and girls have experienced physical or sexual violence.

2 - Project activities

DCA will target the 15,500 most vulnerable households (93,000 individuals) with multipurpose, conditional, and unconditional cash assistance to allow people to buy food and key commodities/services according to their household needs and preferences. Vulnerable households who are labour constrained will be targeted for unconditional and unrestricted cash whilst vulnerable households endorsed with labour will be targeted with cash for work activities. Through cash for work, the project will repair key communityassets through cash for work modality where able-bodied young men and women will be selected to repair community assets which will be identified by the community throughconsultation and participants of cash for work will be paid per task. Assets have in the past included vital infrastructures such as bridges, feeder roads, and dikes.Through the provision of production inputs, such as crop and vegetable seeds and tools, fishing kits, and training, at least 18,320 households (109,920 individuals) will be targeted. The cereal crop seeds, tools, fishing kit components, and canoes will be procured locally while vegetable seeds, as well as fishing hooks, are often not available in the target areas willbe procured in Juba or Sudan / Ethiopia depending on the trading routes. Each of the above-mentioned beneficiaries will receive basic training on post-harvest handling, storage, preservation, utilization, and nutrition, as well as equipment repair, marketing, and value addition. Farmers and fisherfolk will be linked to local traders to open opportunities to sell surplus produce in the market, further contributing to the availability of locally produced food.The project will provide critical support to 9 markets sothat markets recover and grow and will be more resilient to shocks in the future. Interventions will be based on ongoing assessments of markets and activities will include training and provision of equipment to store, transport and preserve food products; cash grants for transport to markets; small initiatives for income generation; improved storage facilities for traders; small cash grants for traders; provision of canoes/boats to business collectives; support to local markets and traders to ensure availability of and access of goods in community markets. DCA will establish and strengthen cash-based emergency response mechanism to support crisis/shock-affected populations with life-saving support. The ERM will allow for immediate assistance to 9,000 individuals and will only be triggered when agreed indicators have been met and markets are functional and accessible. DCA intends to respond with cash within 2 weeks of triggering the ERM.Covid-19 measures will be part of all activities. These include avoiding large gatherings, providing handwashing facilities, measuring temperature before activities, and social distancing.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Bi-weekly market price assessments are conducted as an integral part of the project. The data also contributes to the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (REACH/VAM) for improved coordination at national level. Market assessments together with representatives of the local trade union are conducted in targeted locations to determine gaps and develop an action plan on what market strengthening activities are prioritized. In the past, the following have been implemented to strengthen market functionality: safe storage to allow for prepositioning before the rainy season, drainage improvement, solar lights, market stalls, especially for use by petty traders, transport allowances for mobile traders to deliver goods to displaced populations.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

DCA chooses to integrate a cash-based intervention with the broader food security and livelihoods approach because responding with cash, where markets are functioning is faster and more efficient. At the same time, it gives people choice and dignity and supports the local markets. Voucher-based activities also support the local market but allow for less choice. For this reason, voucher approaches are secondary to direct cash.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DCA provides PSEA training to implementing partner staff on an annual basis. Targeted communities are informed about DCAs code of conduct for staff and partner staff, including PSEA. This ensures that beneficiaries know what to expect and can recognise misconduct. They also receive information about the feedback and complaints mechanism and related confidentiality regulations in place, especially for sensitive complaints. The feedback and complaint mechanisms are multi-layered, allowing to bypass any party if they are directly involved in the issue or are not fully trusted for any reason.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DCA and its partners aim to address inequalities through inclusion and participation through careful consideration of protection risks and existing inequalities and in consultation with targeted populations, specifically with vulnerable and marginalized groups. As an integral part of the project planning and design phase, potential barriers will be identified and mitigation measures, such as additional support to andprioritisation of certain groups (i.e people with disability, elderly) will be considered and guided by the beneficiary selection criteria. In addition, the targeted communities will be sensitized regarding the importance of inclusiveness and the targeting rational.Through the establishment and close collaboration with community-led project committees, DCA and its partners will monitor access and inclusiveness on an ongoing basis. Early identification of changes in the ability of beneficiaries to access assistance is addressed and mitigated. An example of a mitigation measure is the provision of transport to physically challenged beneficiaries to allow them to accessdistribution sites and markets. The choice of distribution site also takes into consideration the distance beneficiaries have to travel.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.1. Timing and location of distributions/trainings are key to mitigate this risk. Timing is chosen to ensure that beneficiaries are able to reach the location and return home during daylight hours. Beneficiaries are advised to travel in groups. Locations are chosen to be as close as possible to the biggest number of vulnerable beneficiaries. Transport is arranged for the most vulnerable, including people with disabilities and older people. In addition, the FSP registers an alternate person of trust, chosen by the primary beneficiary themselves, to attend the distribution on their behalf in case of sickness or difficulty accessing the location e.g. during rainy season.8.2. A code of conduct, including commitment to PSEA and safeguarding policies is signed by partner staff, DCA staff and FSP staff. All major distributions are monitored by DCA and in many cases the donor. All distributions have a feedback and complaints table set up for beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The code of conduct is discussed during community consultations to ensure the community members know what behavior is deemed unacceptable. 8.4. Lack of awareness and access to services has been identified as a major gap. DCA and partners develop a referral network together with other humanitarian actors, RRC/ROSS, community, community leadership and local authorities and share the information with targeted communities. Gaps are raised with the relevant clusters at state/national level.8.10. In some of the targeted areas, such as Magwi, Akobo, contamination with explosive hazards is still high. While DCA has a Mine Action department, activities focus largely on Magwi and Pageri in Eastern Equatoria. In Magwi, DCA is training one of the local partners to localize risk education knowledge. Beneficiaries can call DCA or reach out through UNMAS hotlines if any explosives are found. Either DCA or another MA partner will then be tasked with destruction of the explosive and clearance of the concerned area. In Akobo and other locations, DCA shares the contact details of the UNMAS hotline to link communities to services.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Protection is mainstreamed throughout all activities. Protection is also included in all monitoring exercises to identify any additional gaps. These may also be identified during FGDs and KIIs, as well as through feedback. The abovementioned referral pathways are developed to ensure that locally available services are known and utilized. Identified gaps will be raised within the cluster system.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Emergency food production is coupled with market linkages and training on seed multiplication, allowing for continuation of increased food production and income generation after the project ends. A graduation approach will be used in locations where the project has already secured follow up funding for a second year. Extremely vulnerable HHs receive unconditional cash first, combined with training, and later, based on capacity, graduate to income generation as the main source of income. This approach also aims at a reduction of dependence on aid.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

While cross-sectoral basic services are mostly difficult to access in the targeted remote locations, DCA and partners always work with RRC/ROSS, humanitarian partners and local authorities to map out available services and develop a referral network, which is shared with the communities. This ensures they are aware of what is available and where. In addition, DCA and partners will flag gaps with the relevant clusters at state and national levels.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project increases emergency food production as well as availability of locally grown food in the market. Households with children under 5 with signs of acute malnutrition will be prioritized as most vulnerable and referred to treatment centers. Kitchen gardening activities include training on nutrition and food diversity.
