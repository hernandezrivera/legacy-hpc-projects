Project Summary

Considering the current situation (volatile security situation, limited access to the islands, need for security, increasing number of IDPs, raining season approaching, ..), Istituto Oikos is proposing the following activities:
- District IBO: n. 1500 shelter, n. 100 trainings (7600 IDPs)
- District QUISSANGA: n.1200 shelter, n.100 Trainings (6100 IDPs)
- District METUGE: n. 400 permanent housing, n.100 Trainings (500 IDPs)

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project has the following objective: 1) Provide adequate and contextualized shelter and non food items life saving assistance responding to the immediate needs of most vulnerable crisis affected population; 2) Provide sustainable shelter and essential non-food items support promoting selfrecovery and access to livelihoods for most vulnerable crisis affected population; 3) Strengthen the Shelter and NFIs assessment, analysis and implementation capacity through community participatory approaches, to support quality emergency and early recovery response, and reinforce the Accountability to Affected PopulationBeneficiaries: Specific criteria will be adopted for the selection of beneficiaries for each disitric, this includes also age and sex. Totally the project will target 14200 inhabitants (Female: 7526 and Male: 6674)

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Selection of Beneficiaries The criteria IO will adopted for the selection of beneficiaries on each island are: age, number of people in the family, number of children under 5 years old, existence of family members with physical or mental disability, number of elderly people and single mother as head of household with children. Based on the above, with KOBO, Istituto Oikos field team carried out an assessment of all the IDPs and their level of vulnerability and produced priority lists from the collected data. Selection of Youth for training in Resilient Construction Techniques:The first step is the selection of the youth to be trained, through a written test that assesses the candidates' basic level of knowledge knowledge in the construction field.Training in Resilient Construction Techniques:Aqualified Oikos technician gives one week theoretical and practical class where he explains and demonstrates the basic Resilient Construction Techniques. At the end of the course a new test is taken to check the level of learning. Construction of shelte and housing:The first fase of the construction activity is the procurement of material. Must be drawn up the procurement plan, following the internal purchasing procedures the process of suppliers identification and quotation request started. When the procurement process is on going and all material are available, a voucher distribution will be organize in order to guaranteed the construction of a Shelter to each beneficiaries selected in the first activity. Then IO distribute a construction tool kit for each HH teaching them how to use each tool in the shelter construction process, using only four types of material (bamboo, tire rope, wood poles and tarpaulins). The IDPs families will be involved in the shelter construction process as helpers learning with the masters how to build and improve their shelters. Instead , recovery shelter will be contracted out to local companies. Oikos will supervise. PDM: At the end of the construction phase, Oikos conducts a PDM process to assess the satisfaction with both the construction process and the built product

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The provincial and districtal infrastructures services DPOPHRH and SDPI in the selected districts for implementation of the project activities will work with assistance from the shelter cluster and active partners in Cabo Delgado and utilize existing feedback mechanisms such as Linha Verde and Linha Fala Criança for which specific pre-formulated questions and replies already do exist. Therefore, the DPOPHRH, SDPI and partners will be aware and can follow up about any comment on the distribution or availability and quality of the services and products provided, and of specific requests for information.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

This action is designed according to the expressed need of target groups assessed during periodical focus groups, surveys and other participatory methodologies during years of presence of Oikos in the area. Furthermore, participation of target groups is mainstreamed during the whole project implementation. The action focuses on working in close collaboration with the community utilizing their internal resources and valorizing traditional and cultural knowledge. This will facilitate the acceptance of the intervention by the community and will guarantee its participation. The project will create spaces for interaction, such as public consultations, focus gender groups and community data collection, to identify constraints and ways to better production techniques. It will make the results more reliable, fostering ownership and capacities. 6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Local Institutional stakeholders will participate in the beneficiaries’ selection to ensure to include the most vulnerable households, including people with disabilities. The technical support offered to the vulnerable families will take into account the special needs of people with disabilities, conceiving the shelter structures accordingly to the evaluation of the specific situation.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will take into account some environmental considerations that can come up during the NFI distribution (such as the production of waste) or linked to the construction material provenience. The project identified the following solutions: ● During distributions activities, the population and other beneficiaries will be taught the correct way to dispose the waste produced (such as plastic bags and packaging) in a way that would not be prejudicial to the environment together with technics of how use this waste in the resilient house construction taking into advance the material for construction works; ● All the construction material will be provided assuring its sustainable provenience and avoiding to encourage the illegal traffic of wood, in order to prevent the exploitation of the resources of the QNP (Quirimbas National Park) ● The project will avoid to promote material coming from non-renewable resources and it will ensure a minimal use of non-re-used material to reduce mass waste production in IDPs accommodation sites and host communities.
