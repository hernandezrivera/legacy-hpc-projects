Project Summary

Terre des hommes with the support of partner organisation AICM have assessed potential of interventions for support of the children who are affected by the conflict through activities based on Tdh methodology Movement, Games and Sport (MGS).
The current project aims to relief the stress of children from NGCA, Donetsk and Luhansk, by providing psycho-social activities to over 5000 children.
The first objective of the project is to support the rehabilitation of the sports facilities (sport halls, town stadiums) in order to provide a more appropriate environment for sportive activities with children.
The second objective is to train 40 sports teachers (or other caregivers who run the activities with the children) in the methodology
The teachers targeted are using the sports facilities that we aim to renovate together with groups of children who attend schools that have no or insufficient sport facilities,
The third objective is to provide MGS activities to over 5000 children through the teachers trained. This project component includes support/coaching for the newly trained teachers as well as provisions of MGS kits (diverse sport equipment and toys for the implementation of the MGS methodology and sport activities) and, if appropriate, establishment of child-friendly rooms in the schools where the teachers develop their activity.
The MGS activities are designed to compensate lack of social interaction between children and develop social and collaborative skills. The activities are organised with mixed groups of boys and girls and have a cooperative approach.
The activities encourage girls to participate in the sports activities which leads to empowerment, responding to strategic gender needs that can influence in the future the response to behaviors affected by gender inequality. An example of such activity is Girl Inclusive Football, where the teams are mixed and only the goals scored with the contribution of girls (ex: pass or shoot for goal) are scored.
The MGS methodology encourages to reflect on, express feelings and deal with emotions could be beneficial for boys in a rather patriarchal society, especially in a context where traditional gendered roles might be reinforced because of the conflict.
Overall, the friendliness of the learning spaces in schools will be increased by the implementation of the new methodology in the interactions with the children and the provision of educational materials (related to sportive activities and to MGS games).
Acces to NGCA will be ensured by the implementing partner AICM who is registered and has a track record of health projects in NGCA. AICM will facilitate contact with the relevant local actors (an initial contact was made and needs were identified by discussion with local stakeholders) and will assist in setting up the operations. Together with AICM and local stakeholders involved we will decide the steps to be taken for the implementation of the project. One staff member (at least) will be localised in NGCA. The renovation activities will be organised through local contractors. The ToT will be provided by the Tdh trainers in the NGCA. The Tdh trainers will request acces also with the support of AICM. The monitoring and coaching will be made by the NGCA staff with support from the Tdh team in GCA (Mariupol/Severodonetsk office).

Needs

Since 2015, the sport activities outside schools for children decline and became very rare. In the same time, sport declines also in school for problems of infrastructure (heating, water…) and the risk of shelling especially near the contact line. The quasi absence of mental health suppoAffected Populationrt creates lot of psychological problems and each child needs more attention from the teachers and relatives. Sport can improve their situation but there is no more an offer of sport activities after school. Children in this region are generally freed from school at around 2/3PM Since the start of the conflict several sport facilities were damaged and in the same time the lack of fund obliged to stop lot of sportive activities in NGCA. We have identified 5 sport centres that need some light repairs and new equipment to restart their activity. Some others are in difficult situation or destroyed. We propose repairs for one stadium and 4 indoor gymnasiums which need some help at different level. In total, 4.500 children are immediately concerned by the repair of these sports centers and 3.000 more after the opening (total 7.500 children).In January 2017, Tdh conducted a Child Protection Needs Assessment in 42 locations of Luhansk (GCA). Regading to the psychological effects of the conflict on th children, The study shows that girls demonstrate asociality, high level of anxiety, pessimism and suffer from panic attacks, boys show aggression, insecurity and sleeping troubles. Main reasons of stress increase among boys and girls are fear of attacks, shelling and/or shooting (63.5%), fear of being separated from their families and friends (57.6%), as well as tensions within the families (with a noticeable difference among gender: 45.8% for boys and 59.3% for girls), nightmares and bad memories (34% for boys, 42% for girls) and fear of not being able to return home (28%). We do not have a similar assesment in NGCA but we think it is safe to estimate that the situation presents similarities in NGCA, possible with higher levels of stress effects.

Activities & outcomes

1. Activity: Rehabilitation of sport facilities that facilitate schools to do sports. During the assessment, the partner AICM supported Tdh in identifying sports facilities that need repairs. We have identified 5 centres that need immediate support and where the children come together with their teachers to perform sports activities. In the project we plan to support partially the rehabilitation of the spaces, in order to provide a friendlier and safer space to the children. These facilities are used by the schools in the area who do not have sports facilities to accommodate all the children or whose facilities are damaged because of the conflict. Outcome: over to 7.500 children have the opportunity to practice sports in safer and friendlier environment and to release stress and develop social skills 2. Activity: Train 40 teachers in Movement, Games and Sport methodology that stimulates cooperation and social skills development for the children. Initially, a ToT (Training of Trainers) will be organised for education professionals from the targeted area. Subsequently, they will deliver training to 32 more specialists and caregivers. Movement, Games and Sport (MGS). MGS was developed by Tdh and used internationally in different environments, including for supporting the relief of stress and development of social skills of children affected by humanitarian crisis. The methodology was successfully used in GCA area of Donetsk and Luhansk between 2015-2018. MGS encourages collaboration and communication between the children and supports the development of physical and motor skills Outcome: Increased capacity of 40 caregivers (school teachers, sport trainers to support social development and team spirit of the children). 3. Activity: Provide activities in the schools and the sport facilities, through the teachers trained, in order to relieve the stress and support the development of the children. The activities will be supported by the project with specific materials (such as sportive equipment and games, as well as consumable materials). The teachers will be required to provide a minimum number of activities and the implementation will be monitored with the support of the teachers, recording number of children, gender and age. Tdh, through own staff and/or the new trainers will provide support and coaching. Outcome: 5000 children will be involved in MGS activities that will contribute to the reduction of stress and improvement of individual and social skills.Accountability to affected populationTdh has an institutionalised approach to Quality and Accountability. Internal standards require that all projects allocate budget for M&E. Tdhhas a strong commitment to child safeguarding and follows strict requirements of the child protection policy and code of conduct. Tdh standard requires to implement 70 % of key actions recommended by at least 5 child protection minimum standards in humanitarian crisis. Child Participation is one of Tdh's mainstream approaches in all projects.In the current project the main targeted population are teachers and children. The teachers who become MGS animators are required to adhere to the Tdh code of conduct under the Child Safeguarding Policies. Reporting of abuse is encouraged through information available in implementation places. The implementation will include consultations with representatives of the teachers in the planning phase and require feedback at minimum two stages (during/after training and during/after implementation). The MGS methodology allows the animators to design activities according to the groups of children they are working with and requires child participation and the activities include a quick feedback from children and integration of the feedback in the activities. Tdh Ukraine uses a Child Friendly Feedback Tool that allows children to evaluate the activities. The reports provided by the tool are discussed with children and teachers for continous improvement.
