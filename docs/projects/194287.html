Project Summary

The proposed project aims at supporting education in emergencies for flood-affected populations in Northern and Central Fangak County. FCA will support 93 teachers (F-30, M-63) in 12 schools targeting 5450 children (girls-2453, boys-2997). Establish 5 new learning spaces based on need and equipped with gender-segregated latrines and hand washing facilities. Rehabilitate 7 existing learning spaces damaged during the flooding. Support 93 teachers (F-30, M-63) in 12 schools with training activities (under the Teacher in Crisis Context module) which favors short-term interventions. Training 132 PTA members (F-40, M-92) members in school management and governance and resource mobilization. Provide teacher incentives as motivation and dearness allowance to mitigate low teacher retention and absenteeism. Provide teaching and learning materials through the UNICEF pipeline based on the standard kits, including the new curriculum textbooks. Implement termly back to learning campaigns to mitigate dropout. Awareness raising activities will include education and protection messaging such as benefits of education, hygiene management, and GBV risks. FCA will implement the project in Northern and Central Fangak County where FCA has other programs running. To ensure learning and exchange of best practices, FCA will coordinate closely with education partners in the area and the County Education Department.

1 - Needs

According to the latest HNO 2022 report, an estimated 3.6 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict and crisis-affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2022, including more than 125,000 teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance. The prolonged closure of schools due to COVID-19, effects of severe flooding and the broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. Fangak County in Jonglei state continues to suffer from the effect of two years of consecutive severe flooding. The extent and severity of the floods observed in 2020 and 2021 in Fangak was much higher than in previous years, resulting to large-scale displacement. Unusually, the flooding in January 2021 occurred outside of the rainy season, indicating that rainfall is not the only trigger of flooding in the County. An initial rapid needs assessment (IRNA) conducted by aid partners (including FCA) in Northern Fangak in August 2021 indicated that the severe floods affected over 75% of the population in Manajang and Phom payam, resulting to damage of school infrastructure and widespread livelihood collapse, compounded by inability of households to depend on traditional coping strategies and income-generating activities. According to the findings of the assessment, the few functional schools in some of the villages visited like Thalier, Honketch, Dor, Kuerkan, Honketch and Kuertheni struggled to operate due to lack of basic school requirements. The county education officials and PTA members decried the lack of qualified teachers as a major challenge because the prolonged school closures pushed many teachers to pursue other jobs. In other villages like Bilchulkuon and Keew, the severe floods submerged and damaged most of the existing learning spaces and latrines, forcing children to attend classes under trees. Many of the schools assessed lacked learning materials, and the volunteer teachers available lacked basic training and no incentives or allowance. Other schools lacked adequate sanitation and water facilities and little to no support services for Children with Disability (CwD).

2 - Project activities

1. The project will enroll 5,187 children (girls-2532, boys-2655) in 12 schools targeted. This will be achieved through termly back to learning campaigns to mitigate dropout. Enrolment campaigns will target school communities, with greater focus in areas with a concentration of parents of children with disability. 2. Establish Establish five (5) new learning spaces based on need. The project will establish new learning spaces to complement existing learning infrastructures, especially in schools where the number of children has increased because of displacements caused by floods. FCA will ensure that the learning spaces meet minimum safety standards (addressing internal, external, environmental and community level threats). 3. Rehabilitate six (6) learning spaces damaged by floods. FCA will coordinate with county education authorities, PTA members of affected schools and other implementing partners in Fangak to assess the extent of damage on learning spaces in targeted schools. To promote community participation and ownership, FCA will mobilize PTA members and School Management Committee (SMC) members to participate actively in the rehabilitation of learning spaces, through provision casual labor, on cash for work basis4. Establish 12 blocks of two stance gender-segregated latrines and install hand-washing stands. The project will establish latrines in schools where floods submerged school compounds leading to collapse of latrines.5. Train 71 teachers (F-14, M-57) in 12 schools using the Teacher in Crisis Context module which favours short-term interventions, including training on psychosocial support and the protection mechanisms of Covid-19. The project will pay monthly incentives for the 71 teachers as motivation and dearness allowance to mitigate low teacher retention and absenteeism 6. Train 132 PTA members (F-40, M-92) and 25 county education officials/inspectors in psychosocial support (PSS), school management and growth and fundraising towards the welfare of teachers and children, and in promoting closer relations between parents and teachers.7. Distribute teaching and learning materials to the 12 targeted schools through the UNICEF pipeline based on the standard kits, including the new curriculum textbooks.8. Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for children and teachers in all the targeted 12 schools9. Implement back to learning campaigns to reduce the risk of dropout after prolonged closure of schools due to floods and the effects of Covid-19. During these campaigns, FCA will sensitize and encourage parents and the wider community to send children back to school, especially girls and including children with disabilities (CWD). FCA will implement these activities in close coordination with WASH actors and health partners especially during Covid-19 awareness raising activities. 10. Quarterly awareness raising and messaging to school communities, PTAs, and teachers about the referral pathway system to help in referring cases of child protection, especially CwDs.11. Quarterly psychosocial support (PSS) for families of CwDs to mitigate social stigma and cultural beliefs that reduce parents’ desire to send CwDs to school.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

FCA has complaint focal points at field offices who will have regular contact with beneficiaries during implementation, especially people with disabilities. Every complaint will be addressed in an impartial, equitable, objective and timely manner. Complaints will be processed by recording it in the complaint database (logbook) for tracking and follow-up. Only the Complaints Focal Point will have an access to the database. Receipt of a complaint will be acknowledged within the defined period. Operational complaints will be resolved through processes appropriate considering the type and urgency of the complaint.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

FCA will involve teachers, PTA/SMCs, children, CwDs and county department of education in collecting baseline data at the beginning of the project through focus group discussions, interviews, and site visits. Termly inspection and monitoring visits are conducted jointly with key stakeholders involving beneficiaries at all phases. This creates transparency, improves community ownership, acceptance and mitigates tensions and mistrust that may arise during implementation. FCA has a functional complaint response mechanism, with complaints focal points at field level. There is a dedicated complaints email address to support beneficiaries who prefer to use telephone as their channel for complaints. In addition, FCA will install complaints boxes in accessible places at schools, community centres and church compounds that act as a channel for beneficiaries to raise concerns, provide feedback and share ideas. FCA will also roll out the use of community-based focus group discussions with beneficiaries and stakeholders as an additional option for gathering feedback at project locations. In addition, the project will sensitize and encourage families with CwDs and illiterate parents to use the existing traditional complaint mechanisms, for example channeling complaints through the local chiefs who in turn can refer the complaints to FCA. The project staff, based on the Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) plan will do regular results-based monitoring in a participatory manner to ensure accountability and adjust the action, if necessary.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

At the inception of the project, FCA organize kick-off workshop that will target all stakeholders. In this workshop, FCA will provide orientation sessions about the key principles and practices of FCA’s CRM and PSEA policy. During the implementation of activities, for example training of teachers and PTAs/SMCs, the project will dedicate sessions the elaborate the principles and practices of FCA's PSEA policy and the channels through which beneficiaries can report cases of SEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

FCA will adapt its complaint system to needs and capacities of the beneficiaries to pass feedback and complaints. Beneficiaries will be informed on their opportunities to provide feedback to the project implementers. The outcomes of complaints, including the learnings will be used to adjust project implementation approach.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

FCA has in place a Code of Conduct to Prevent Sexual Exploitation in humanitarian response and will take seriously the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, including the demands for sex in exchange for goods or services. The project staff of FCA are trained on PSEA and have signed on the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. FCA's GBV and Protection focal staff will collaborate with other GBV and protection partners to make use of the existing referral pathways that can support women and girls who are survivors of rape and other incidents of sexual or gender-based violence, as well as to be able to identify it and make an appropriate referral to a neighboring health facility. In addition, FCA relies on acceptance by the community at the grassroots level to mitigate risks related to the safety of staff, office assets and project supplies. The project will continue to strengthen the strategy of promoting community ownership such that communities recognize the value of humanitarian assistance and embrace their moral duty of protecting humanitarian property or supplies intended for improving or saving lives of affected communities. A greater sense of community ownership can act as a mechanism for protecting NGO assets, staff and project supplies and infrastructure from abuse or physical violence. FCA’s strategic approach of recruiting staff strengthens the protection of beneficiaries. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that the program teams consist of combination of locally hired national staff at the project location, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. FCA will continue to leverage its relationships at the grassroots level to advocate for the safety of beneficiaries regardless of their tribe or political affiliation FCA has established a robust functional and active complaints and feedback mechanism, with field based complaints focal points, supported by the Juba complaints focal point. In order to ensure that there is prompt feedback to beneficiaries and project stakeholders, FCA’s field-based complaints focal teams receive operational complaints and provide instant feedback. However, complaints that are sensitive in nature must be forwarded to the Juba Complaints Committee that meet to determine whether such complaint should be forwarded to the HO.
