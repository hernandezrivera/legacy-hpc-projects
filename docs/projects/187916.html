Project Summary

Until now there are no funding for this project and this project is unsecured, Mercy-USA seek to implement this project from the privet fund during 2022.
This action has been designed to address the need for a more integrated comprehensive response across northern Syria while taking into account the unique needs across the country and population per HNO, HRP, and cluster objectives So2. The proposed action aims to build community capacity to contribute to achieving a greater level of resilience, mitigation protection risks, and negative coping mechanism and participate in economic life for 600 HHs including (3000 individuals), through two proposed value chains:
Agriculture vegetable value chain:
Mercy-USA in this project will provide the most vulnerable host communities and IDPs in Afrin district Aleppo governorate with garden seed kits for agriculture, tool kit, and training, in order to support their livelihood source for them. Targeted beneficiaries 300 HH can be broken down as follows (Men 210, Women 255, Boys 555, and Girls 480)
Livestock value chain:
To strengthen productivity and profitability for livestock holders and improve access to quality assets and inputs project will work through distributing E-voucher to the livestock holders to be redeemed against animal feed from the participant local suppliers. Total of 300 livestock breeders, 1500 individuals (735 female and 765male) Animal fodder will be supported with approximately 4500 Awasi sheep only with an average of 15 animals per HH and support food for three times. The E-voucher animal feed will be distributed within the project lifecycle. The number of rounds is three and each round will cover the needs for one month per round starting from January 2022 to December 2023. The animal feed will be pellet feed and quality will be based on the livestock technical working group (LTWG) under the FSL Cluster support.
Locations and beneficiaries:
Aleppo governorate - Afrin district- Jandairis sub-district, 300 HH (Men 210, Women 255,
Boys 555, and Girls 480)
Idlib (Idlib district – Idlib and Maaret Tamsrin sub-district) 300 HH (Men 210, Women 255, Boys 555, and Girls 480).
Targeted beneficiaries will be both IDPs and host households with 50% each. Also, the beneficiaries will receive training about the best agriculture and livestock practical’s. the training will be for local authority and focal point and as well as through agriculture and livestock farmers eld school, to make sure the farmers and livestock holders receive not only the necessary knowledge but also in practice for that IEC (information education and communication) will be distributed to the targeted beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

The agriculture and livestock sector in NW Syria is considered one of the potential and good opportunities for livelihood recovery from a protracted crisis that has lasted for more than 10 years. The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has been severely impacted, with half (i.e. 50 percent) of national grain requirements met through imports. HNO 2021600,000 farming HHs are suffering from the lack of agricultural inputs (cereals, vegetables, fertilizers, irrigation kits, etc). to support their own production (the targeted area mainly). The households of 20,000 breeders are suffering from the loss of productive assets (livestock) and damaged infrastructure (land, irrigation infrastructure, facilities, etc.), which is affecting both farmers and breeders. The number of livestock decreased significantly, part of the IDPs and even host households tend to sell part if not all their livestock which is considered as productive assets to cover the households need and as well as they cannot cover the feed and healthcare for their livestock which is considered as a negative coping strategy which is high in NW Syria. As well as the limited land available for farming in general low interest in forage crops, high cost of animal feeds, most of the farmers rely on rainfed farming with the low rainfall in winter 2021 and lack of water for irrigation, all these challenges force those livestock holders to cope negatively.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Also, Mercy USA carries out activities and uses an approach aimed at facilitating individuals and communities to achieve respect for rights in safety and dignity through community-based protection approach. Mercy-USA uses GAM “gender age marker” to tackle whether or not a humanitarian project is designed to effectively respond to the different needs of men, women, girls, and boys within the affected population. Also, Mercy USA In its project separates males and females and people with special needs have special services such as chairs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Overcrowded during E-voucher redemption to support livestock and agriculture inputs which may cause SEA incidentsMixed redemption lines for males and females •All distribution staff are males Redemption site is not accessible to all gender groups No shared clear information about timing and amount of redemption No clear procedure regarding loss of card/voucherTaxation on the assistance •No complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) in place Risk mitigation:Ensure extended redemption days to avoid overcrowding •Separate lines for males and females in all redemption processes •Ensure gender balance in the hiring process, ideally to be 50%of the team is female.Sensitization about the scope of the project to all communities with highlighting “This assistance is Free” and this statement will be printed on the beneficiary cards.Different CFM channels will be in place such as WhatsApp, hotline, email, phone as well as physical by MEAL team. • PSEA awareness materials (e.g. posters and pamphlets) and reporting hotline will be visible to beneficiaries at the redemption site.Also, Mercy-USA publishing PSEA material and inform BNFs and non-BNFs about their rights and have complaint mechanism in place as well as building the local capacity in terms of protection issues through conducting appropriate training such as PSEA As for Mercy-USA staff, all of its staff once hired Mercy-USA to conduct a PSEA and safe gardening as well as code of conduct and humanitarian principles training.
