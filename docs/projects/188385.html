Project Summary

PUI’s FOCOS strategy programming continues to be undertaken in a multi-year strategic way, with 2022 and 2023 representing the sixth year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area based approach (IABA) in Syria. Through this approach, PUI make sures to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need in order to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living, to enhance self-sufficiency and resilience in the long run and to promote social cohesion.
In line with PUI’s I4SC approach (initial standards, self-sufficiency and social cohesion) and the Early Recovery and Livelihoods sector’s objectives, PUI aims to support vulnerable conflict affected populations toward achieving self sufficiency. The project will be focused on developing and enhancing income generating capacities through:
1- Distribution of 605 Agricultural Tool Kits (ATK) in the following areas (Admin 4): Busra Esh-Sham, Al Mayadin, Abu Kamal, Sbeikhan, Talbiseh, Suran, Al-Haffa, Salanfa and Maadan.
2- One Bakery Rehabilitation in Dayr Hafir area in Aleppo serving around 20,000 beneficiaries.
3- Distribution of Fertilizer + Pesticide to 100 famers in Khan Shaykun area in Idleb.
4- Rehabilitation of three irrigation networks in Al Mayadin, Ashara and Maadan areas serving around 15000 beneficiaries.
5- Distribution of livestock for 75 farmers in Um Tinet Simaan and Arjel areas in Aleppo
6- Distribution of multi-purpose cash assistance for 200 households in Busra Esh-Sham in Dar’a and Talbiseh in Homs. The Cash distribution will be done on three installment on three months. The installment value will be 243,000 SYP based on the Cash Working Group recommendation.
7- Distribution of 520 Professional Tool Kits (PTK) in the following areas (Admin 4): Dar'a, Jizeh, Mzeireb, Busra Esh-Sham, Eastern Ghariyeh, Al Mayadin, Abu Kamal, Sbeikhan and Maadan.
8- Distribution of seeds and fertilizers to 740 farmers in the following areas (Admin 4): Jizeh, Mzeireb, Busra Esh-Sham, Tafas, Eastern Ghariyeh, Masud and Oqeirbat.
9- Rehabilitating 100 shops in Deir-ez-Zor city and Aleppo city (both are Admin 4)
10- Support 560 small business projects (SBP) in the following areas (Admin 4): Dar'a, Jizeh, Mzeireb, Busra Esh-Sham, Eastern Ghariyeh, Al Mayadin, Abu Kamal, Sbeikhan, Suran, Al-Haffa, Salanfa, Darayya, Duma, Ein Terma, Khan Shaykun, Sabka and Maadan.
Overall, the project will benefit 37900 beneficiaries and with a total cost of 4,227,250$ on the two years. The cost per beneficiary is around 111.5$

4 - Needs

According to the humanitarian Needs Overview for 2022, more than 13.8 million Syrians are in need for livelihood assistance. More than ten years of crisis have inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population. Throughout 2021, the macro-economic context continued to deteriorate sharply and is accelerating into 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue and increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss as well as unilateral coercive measures have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need, most notably in areas historically less affected by hostilities and displacement. People’s ability to meet basic needs has further decreased compared to 2020, with a disproportionate impact on female-headed households, older persons without family support, persons with disabilities and children. An increasing number of women and adolescent girls are forced to work in order to provide for their families, either as sole breadwinners or contributing to the family income. Across Syria, households are now reverting to negative coping mechanisms more frequently than before. This includes child labor and child marriage and the sale of productive assets. On average, less than two third of those surveyed indicated they had a job in the last three months. Nonetheless, the top need for 71 per cent of households remain food baskets/bread, immediately followed by livelihoods (56 per cent).

5 - Implementation of activities

Regarding the distribution of tools and materials activities, PUI has a standard Sops for implementing those activities as follows:1- Assessment Phase: PUI will conduct community consultations with the local communities and representative from local authorities to discuss needs and how to best respond to each need.2- Announcement and Registration phase: PUI will announce its intention to implement the agreed-upon activity, launch the registration of beneficiaries and conduct verification visits. 3- Selection of beneficiaries based on pre-established selection criteria.4- Training the selected beneficiaries on relevant topics based on the activity (agriculture best practices for ATK, business skills for PTK, etc.)5- Provision of needed material, goods or equipment, PUI will launch a procurement process to supply the beneficiaries with the needed materials.6- Distribution of the materials on the selected beneficiaries7- Conducting monitoring visits to provide needed guidance and assistance for the beneficiaries8- Conduct post distribution evaluation for the implemented activities to measure beneficiaries’ satisfaction and impact of the activities.While for the irrigation networks and bakery rehabilitation, PUI will consult with the local communities and contract a contractor to implement the agreed-upon works.For the MPCA, PUI will registers the names of beneficiaries based on selection criteria and will provide the selected beneficiaries with multiple installments.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

PUI identified the key protection risks prior to the launch of the intervention to ensure mitigation measures are in place to reduce the likelihood and impact of these risks on the project. Below are the risks identified along with related mitigation measures: - Risk: Security situation deteriorates and humanitarian support (whether directly PUI or contracted vendors) cannot reach the target area. (Likelihood: High – Impact: High)Mitigation measures: 1. Assess the situation in targeted areas before the intervention and constantly during the implementation; 2.PUI and the donor retain certain levels of flexibility linked to the selected areas of intervention in case it requires shifting due to the lack of access. -Risk: Selection of beneficiaries and lack of community ownership can create community tensions. (Likelihood: Medium- Impact: High)Mitigation measures: 1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the intervention; 2. Ensure as much as possible, taking into account the accessibility constraint, to provide equal services in areas where tensions exist; 3. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Première Urgence Internationale has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). As such, PUI is committed to implementing the six principles adopted in 2012 by the Inter Agency Standing Committee Task Force on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse. Risk: Vulnerable beneficiaries are exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse by PUI staff or other related entities; (Likelihood: low. Impact: High)Mitigation measures: Raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent beneficiaries’ exposure to these risks. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train Staff and other involved personnel on positive discipline; Train staff on code of conduct; address any SEA complaints.
