Project Summary

This project will be implemented by NSDO to provide life-saving WASH services aimed at meeting the critical emergency needs of returnees, host communities, and IDPs in 8 selected payams of Lofon/lopa County, Eastern Equatoria State, who are affected by climate change, conflict, and GBV threats during access to WASH facilities. The project aims at providing safe and equitable access to sufficient water and sanitation services for the affected population to mitigate WASH-related diseases and gender-based violence among 11,111 returnees, IDPs, host community members, and 1% of persons with disabilities in Lofon/Lopa County. Targeted sites are communal water collection points, nutrition services, and public places agreed upon after a GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls and people with disabilities. The budget to achieve this project is estimated at $500,000.
.
Activities will be implemented in Lafo/Lopa for 11,111 people, 15,36 men, 23,17 women, 3505 boys, 37,53 girls, and 1% people with disabilities, within a period of 12 months, except for hygiene promotion activity for 6 months, in close coordination with government authorities, beneficiaries, and humanitarian partners within Lafon/Lopa County and the WASH Cluster and Protection Cluster at the national level. The activities prioritized in this intervention are based on facts recorded from the targeted project areas during the recent information from the government authority in Loafon County. The project will ensure that WASH services are provided in safe and secure locations and that facilities are gender-sensitive and accessible to people with special needs. The project will begin by jointly reviewing the needs of beneficiary members of each activity site and the technical assessment of target WASH facilities suggested by beneficiary members for repairs. Five water points will be rehabilitated by flashing, pump testing, water quality analysis, platform construction, and the replacement of worn-out spare parts in all selected locations. 1 water points will be drilled and constructed in nutrition sites and with flood resilience design, 6 blocks of sanitation facilities will be rehabilitated in nutrition sites considering stances for persons with disability, construction of 2 blocks of latrine in nutrition sites including persons with disability, handwashing facilities will be constructed/ installed for the all constructed latrines, safety audit will be conducted for WASH facilities, distribution of WASH NFIs to children and caretaker in the nutrition site and IDPs, returnees and host community, provision of hygiene promotion activities, training of 6 community water user committees for the rehabilitated and drilled water points in Lafon county, 54 members where 60% of the members will be female, and training of community pump mechanics is a sustainability and exit strategy of the project, there will be 7 members per water point, 42 pump mechanics, and 30% trainees will be female.
NSDO has built the capacity of female WASH staff deployed on the ground to lead in observing and reporting on AAP indicators, coordinate with protection partners on the ground, and support beneficiary members with feedback and complaints. NSDO will coordinate the project activities with the Department of Rural Water and Sanitation in the county, and the RRC Secretariat already has strong experience working with the communities and authorities in South Sudan County and will prioritize reporting as communication gaps will be improved by the field staff from the project location.

1. Needs

The population of Lafon/Lopa County is considered to be 106,161 people, according to the 2008 national bureau of statistics, of which about 45% are still living as IDPs and refugees in the neighboring counties and countries. The population of Lafon/Lopa County is considered to be 106,161 people, according to the 2008 national bureau of statistics, of which about 45% are still living as IDPs and refugees in the neighbouring counties and countries.In Lafon/Lopa County, there has been no IRNA conducted in recent years. The last IRNA assessment conducted in Lafon was in 2014, and until date, no assessment has been conducted in all the payams, such as Arihilo, Burgilo, Imehejek, Kurumi, Lohutok, Longiro, Marguna, and Pachidi. According to the commissioner during our phone call conversation, the WASH situation in the county is alarming since the Mentor Initiative project with UNICEF ended early in 2022, but while they were in the county dealing with water supply, they rehabilitated five boreholes. As of now, there is no WASH partner, and no IRNA was conducted in the county; hence, the WASH need is at its peak.The current population this project is targeting is 11,111 in Lafon: 15,36 men, 23,17 women, 3505 boys, and 37,53 girls. People with disabilities are estimated at 1% of the total population, and with more returnees arriving from IDP and refugee camps, the majority of the population continues to share existing houses. They are faced with the huge need for WASH services since latrines have collapsed due to flooded areas and poor soil texture, and most boreholes have broken down. Kits for setting up sanitation facilities are lacking.Most broken and functional boreholes in areas affected by floods were submerged in water, which resulted in the contamination of boreholes in Lafon and Lopa in the targeted 8 Payams. Achieving water quality in Lafon/Lopa is a key priority for the affected population due to the turbidity of the water from the boreholes caused by solid particles, worms, and rusted pipes inside the water.According to commissioner Lafo/Lopa County, diarrheal high records, as witnessed on facility records, show that mostly children and the elderly are affected rather than other age groups; women and girls have no access to appropriate sanitary protection materials. Soap is not easily available in the local markets, and it’s very expensive at some locations. High public health risk realized and observed as there is direct open defecation practiced near water sources and swampy areas whereby all land space floated in most areas, and it's very worse in Lafon. According to County Commissioners, there were no sanitation and hygiene promotion services provided all these years in the county, which made the community practice open defecation due to a lack of awareness on how to construct a latrine in flood-prone areas, therefore increasing the prevalence of WASH-related diseases.There are existing protection concerns for women and girls. With armed groups from neighboring Pibor communities where cattle were raided and children were abducted anywhere, where women and children of both sexes fear, there are reported experiences of sexual gender-based violence (GBV), especially when collecting firewood and fetching water. Women and girls are also lacking dignity kits, and the coping mechanism for MHM among women is risky. With existing persons with special needs (elderly, disabled, and chronically ill), there is a need to strengthen protection action through WASH intervention by rehabilitating and drilling a borehole within the proximity of the human settlement.

2. Project activities

The key project deliverable activities to realize the achievement of the project are: 1) community engagement meetings involving women, girls, boys, men, and people with disabilities for the project kick-off meeting and in decision-making. 2) Distribution of WASH NFIs such as soap, buckets with and without taps, menstrual hygiene kits, aqua-tabs, and water purifiers to children and caretakers at the nutrition site, IDPS, returnees, and host communities affected by crises in Lofon/Lopa County, 3) Training of 64 hygiene promoters and 8 supervisors; 8 hygiene promoters and 1 supervisor per payam 4) Rehabilitation of 6 blocks of latrine in Lafon/Lop at nutrition sites; 5) Construction of 2 blocks of latrine in Lafone County at nutrition sites, each with 3 stances, including for people with disabilities. 6) Rehabilitation of boreholes in nutrition sites 5 in Lafon following the WASH Cluster guidelines 7) drilling and construction of 1 borehole at nutrition sites in Lafone County. 8) Conducting hygiene promotion activities targeting nutrition sites, diversity, and the host community. 9) Construction of a semi-permanent latrine with a flood-resilient design; 6 blocks of latrine of 8 stances each and 2 stances each will be designed for people with disabilities. 10) Conducting WASH barrier analysis at all the project sites 11) Conducting safety audits at all the project sites, 12) training of 6 community water user committees, 9 members per water point total of 54; 60% of the members will be female; 13) training of 42 community pump technicians for the rehabilitated and drilled water points in Lafon, 7 members per water point, as a sustainability and exit strategy of the project; and 14) construction and installation of handwashing facilities at all the rehabilitated and constructed latrines in the project locations.
