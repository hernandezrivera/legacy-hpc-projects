Project Summary

WFP aims to contribute to a reduction in acute and chronic malnutrition rates, and an improvement in nutritional status among refugees and local Bangladeshi communities in Cox’s Bazar through the provision of curative and preventive nutrition services for children below 5 years and pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW). WFP will implement its project interventions across 45 integrated nutrition facilities (INFs) in 33 camps, and 55 nutrition sites in the local Bangladeshi community in partnership with UNICEF, UNHCR and cooperating partners under the coordination of the Cox's Bazar nutrition sector. WFP will provide targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for moderate acute malnourished children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (PLW) to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition in both the refugee camps and in the host community. A 200g daily ration of Super Cereal Plus (Wheat Soya Blend Plus -WSB++) & 100g daily ration of Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) will be provided for children 6-59 months in the host community and camps respectively; and 250g of Super Cereal (Wheat Soya Blend -WSB+) and 16.66g of oil will be provided to PBW under TSFP.
An enhanced prevention of malnutrition package will be put in place to mitigate the increase in prevalence of acute and chronic malnutrition and its associated risks for mortality and morbidity and promote child development. In the camps, this includes provision of the blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP) for younger children 6-23 months and PBWs, and a nutrition sensitive e-voucher programme that allows older children (24-59 months) access to fresh fruits and vegetables aimed at enhancing their dietary diversity and promote growth monitoring and uptake of other complementary services at the INF. These children will receive a US$ 3 redeemable voucher. Social Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) activities and utilization of tools that positively influence behaviour, knowledge, attitudes, and practices around nutrition will be conducted in both the refugee and host communities.
Active case finding through community screening, referral and follow up of malnourished children under five and pregnant and breastfeeding women will be done. Additionally, growth monitoring and promotion for children 6-59 months will be part of the project interventions.
The underlying causes of malnutrition will be addressed through integration of nutrition sensitive approaches in ongoing general food assistance, school feeding, self-reliance, and livelihood interventions.
WFP will promote the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies particularly anaemia through the promotion of dietary diversification.
WFP will conduct capacity strengthening for partners and stakeholders to improve capacity for implementation of nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive programming. WFP will actively engage at nutrition sector and support programme intervention as member of SAG, Assessment, IYCF & CMAM technical working group.
WFP will support quantitative and qualitative nutrition assessments to ensure that nutrition programmes are tailormade according to the needs of the targeted populations. In addition, WFP will also conduct an operational study in camps using LNS for malnutrition treatment and prevention among PBW in collaboration with icddr,b .
Project interventions will be adapted to ensure an inclusive approach to promote participation of women, men, older people, adolescent, people who have chronic health conditions and persons with disabilities. Additionally, protection mechanisms will be put in place including complaints and feedback mechanisms and help desks to ensure accountability to affected populations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The November 2023 Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) shows that global acute malnutrition (GAM) is 15.1 percent compared to 12 percent at the same time last year. This is above the 15 percent emergency threshold as per the WHO emergency classification, and the worst since the 2017 influx. Chronic malnutrition (stunting) remained the same and critical (41.2 percent) compared to 2022, while anemia prevalence reduced slightly compared to 2021 – but it is still a medium-level public health concern among children under five (38.2 percent) and women of reproductive age (24.1 percent).Based on the 2023 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey result for the host community, prevalence of Global Acute malnutrition (GAM) rate among children under five years old based on weight-for-height ratio and/or oedema is within 10.6 to 12.8 per cent range in five Upazilas out of eight, which is high malnutrition threshold. Similarly, chronic malnutrition (stunting) is also in the serious category for Bangladesh households across all sub-districts with the lowest prevalence in Teknaf (22.3 per cent) and the highest in Pekua at 35.3 percent. Acute malnutrition is also concerning among pregnant and breastfeeding women in several of these sub-districts. According to the Infant and Young Child Feeding Survey of 2022, only 31% of children aged 6 to 23 months consume diversified diets. On the other hand, close to seven in ten households in Bangladeshi community are in moderate to highly vulnerable conditions, a deterioration from the previous year (REVA 7).According to the findings, children between 6-23 months were more acutely malnourished, while the older age group 24-59 months had higher levels of stunting. On the other hands, prevalence of acute malnutrition, chronic malnutrition as well as Underweight are higher among girls compare to boys. The findings highlight the importance of “the first 1,000 days”, from conception until the child reaches two years of age, a critical window in which the health and well-being of a pregnant and breastfeeding woman directly impacts on the growth and health of her child.In these areas – camps and local communities, prevalence for anaemia is considered a public health concern, coupled with sub-optimal IYCF practices, inadequate dietary diversity, poor care practices, high disease morbidity, low birth spacing, and inadequate WASH practices. These have been cited as key underlying factors contributing to the high prevalence rates of malnutrition in the Cox’s Bazar operation.The situation has become increasingly dire in the refugees camps due to funding shortfalls, forcing cuts to food assistance in 2023 and leading to a sharp rise in food insecurity and malnutrition. This disproportionately affects women and girls, as they are often the last to eat and receive less nutritious food than men and boys, mirroring global trends observed by WFP.Adding to these challenges, Bangladesh is facing persistent high inflation, driven by the global food crisis and the conflict in Ukraine. This economic strain has further limited access to food and essential resources for both refugees and host communities, exacerbating vulnerabilities and disproportionately affecting women and girls who are often responsible for providing for their families.These findings collectively underscore the urgent need for targeted interventions to prevent and treat malnutrition among children under five and pregnant/breastfeeding women in both the refugee and host communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

WFP ensures dignity, safety, security, and protection of all beneficiaries including nutrition programme from any kinds of harm by addressing protection risks and incorporating appropriate mitigation measures into WFP’s programme. The Protection and AAP policy describes strategies to ensure protection principle into WFP’s interventions. WFP ensures the collection of beneficiary data contributes to accountability, inclusion, good programming, assurance and improved coordination with partners, especially when introducing new technologies and services. WFP adopts a people-centred approach that emphasizes the identification of data protection risks and mitigation measures for beneficiaries as part of its context analysis, operational programme design and programme delivery, including the promotion of inclusion and digital literacy. The use of technology when supporting the collection of personal data must be informed by protection considerations to prevent potential discrimination or exclusion. When updating and sharing information on different services, WFP works with Interagency groups, cluster and field level protection focal points for case referrals to ensure that the beneficiaries’ protection needs are met. WFP’s strong collaboration with the Protection Working Group for the integration of protection concerns in all areas of humanitarian operation and wider advocacy support ensures the rights of crisis affected population.

2.3 - Age & Disability

WFP nutrition programme focuses on young and older children aged 0-59 months and pregnant and breastfeeding women affected by acute malnutrition or at risk of it. WFP works internally with different sectors like General Assistance, Livelihoods, Social Safety Net, and School feeding to ensure that other age groups in need of humanitarian assistance are met.WFP's Disability Inclusion Road Map provides strategic guidance for the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all programmatic activities, with a strong focus on upholding their human rights. Through our resilience programs, person with disabilities receive sign language and skill training, empowering them with valuable life skills and enhancing their livelihood opportunities. Nutrition intervention will ensure disability inclusion through disability friendly nutrition site along with prioritization on service delivery. Referral of identified beneficiaries with disability will be referred to appropriate service. In summary, WFP remains steadfast in its commitment to advancing gender equality, promoting inclusivity, and empowering individuals in the communities we serve, leaving no one behind in our pursuit of a world free from hunger and inequality.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In line with its Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) policy and guidelines, WFP has invested in direct and sustained engagement with communities to help secure strong protection outcomes and ensure accountability towards beneficiaries. WFP has developed a Community Engagement Strategy for AAP (2022-2026), using a human rights approach to humanitarian and development action that considers AAP as the central focus of its operations. Through its implementation, WFP emphasizes community participation and engagement, reflection of beneficiary voices. WFP recognizes the need for transparent and continuous two-way communication with the people it serves to capture and record risks, needs, and aspirations expressed by them. WFP has well-established community feedback and response mechanisms (CFM), such as a 24-hour hotline and help desks, for the Rohingya and Bangladeshi community to provide feedback to WFP or its CPs. These enable communities to participate in key decisions throughout the programme cycle from assessment and analysis, and planning and design, to resource mobilization, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. In addition, beneficiaries are well-informed about their rights and entitlements, and have access to CFM on WFP programmes across Cox’s Bazar and Bangladesh more widely. WFP’s Communication with Community (CWC) approach engages community volunteers in programme delivery. WFP also actively engaged with affected populations, such as the Rohingya refugees, to gather feedback, understand their needs, and address concerns, promoting community ownership and accountability.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

WFP mainstream Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through its programme, policy implementation and cooprating partner. WFP gave high priority to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) by adopting the Secretary General Bulletin, incorporating PSEA core principles into agreements with retailers, cooperating partners, and vendors. The same approach will also be followed for this project to mainstream the PSEA. This involves establishing safe and confidential reporting mechanisms, conducting awareness campaigns, and ensuring staff and partners adhered to these standards. For this project, WFP will establish a helpdesk with an AAP/CFM assistant skilled HR through cooperating partners in every nutrition center to ensure the accountability to the affected population and any PSEA related support. The PSEA network and/or protection sector shall be notified right away for swift handling of any PSEA-related concerns that are discovered in the complaints boxes or received by the nutrition facility’s help desks corner Through mandated reporting and frequent field monitoring visits, WFP will make sure that partners are adhering to this in a methodical and timely manner. In addition,WFP will implement a detailed annual PSEA action plan and with budget allocation for PSEA capacity development, assess cooperating partners' capacity using assessment tools, provid capacity development for staff and cooperating partners thorugh training and orientation, emphasizing ethical behaviour, incorporate protection principles into standard behavioral practices, and capture best practices for wider use in program operations to prevent abuse and exploitation. To ensure the voices of affected individuals were heard, WFP established confidential and accessible Community-based Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) for reporting concerns or incidents of abuse, reinforcing their commitment to taking appropriate actions in response.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, WFP is committed and will ensure measures to reduce impact of nutrition intervention on environmental degradation. WFP will ensure environmental awareness among nutrition programme staffs and volunteers. Nutrition partner will collect empty sachet of Specialized nutritious food (SNF’s) products such as Super Cereal, Ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF), Ready-to-Use Supplementary Food (RUSF). Collected packaging wastage will be then upcycled into items to be used for agricultural practice through sewing and crafting under WFPs resilience activities. In addition, though this project WFP will engage the community volunteer network to raise awareness and sensitize the community about the long term impact of climate change on nutritional status of the community. The community mobilization and sensitization session designed for the nutrition project will also include elements related to early warning and actions so that the community can be prepared themselves well to respond any unwanted impact of climate change. WFP will also support the cooperating partners by building the capacity of their staffs and volunteers on climate change principle to ensure proper delivery of awareness messages along the nutrition education. WFP will also collaborate with their other relevant units or relevant stakeholders from the program area so that the nutrition beneficiaries are prioritized for the climate resilient homestead gardening and LPG fuel entitlement. On top of that the nutrition beneficiaries will also be sensitized during their regular nutrition awareness session about the impact of climate change (e.g. lack of climate resilient homestead gardening practices, efficient use of fuel) on their nutritional status.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, WFP maintained the emergency preparedness and response plan for nutrition activities to ensure business continuity and service delivery based on need during any kind of emergency. In addition, WFP will ensure and support that all the implementing partners have developed their own ERP plan aligning with Nutrition sector and ISCG EPR guideline. WFP will follow up in regular basis and suggest for any revision if required. Being an active member of Nutrition sector, WFP in collaboration with Nutrition Sector, UN agencies and nutrition partners will ensure that a comprehensive assessment of every INF structure are completed and the preparedness and response plan has been prepared and incorporated in their annual EPR plan. WFP will also be flexible to provide their engineering support in need for any emergency preparedness and response. Along with this WFP nutrition unit will be in close coordination with other relevant units of WFP especially GFA that nutrition beneficiaries are prioritizing for any emergency food support during any emergency. As part of preparedness, WFP will make available contingency stock as buffer for specialized nutrition food commodity (e.g. RUSF, WSB++, WSB+ etc.) to use if there is any emergency arise. Mobile nutrition team will be embedded with partner and activated in case of any emergency. Coordination with Government and development partners through Local Consultative Group-Disaster Emergency Response will be ensured. WFP also ensure availability of updated key maps and lists related to nutrition intervention. The existing SBC networks and awareness raising sessions under this project will incorporate information on early warning advisories targeting families having children under five years (specially with MAM / SAM), Pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable groups for better preparation and respond to any emergency both in camps and host community.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this age group of 15-24 will be included or engaged in this project. They will have the opportunity to work as project volunteers after reaching 18 years old as well as will be benefitted by receiving different capacity building trainings or orientations. They will also be involved in assessing the malnutrition status at the community level through door-to-door screening and strength the knowledge of the community on nutrition and health outcomes by facilitating different nutrition awareness sessions and activities. In addition, the adolescent girls and pregnant and breastfeeding mothers within this age group will be included as project beneficiary to be benefitted by receiving designated malnutrition treatment and prevention support from the INFs and participating in different from awareness raising activities on nutrition.
