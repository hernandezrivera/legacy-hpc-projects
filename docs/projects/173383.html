Project Summary

"Caritas Bangladesh has Planned for the Shelter/NFI Program in 2021 targeting 23,016 families (109,364 individuals) in the Camp-4 (3,530 HHs),Camp-4 Extension (733HHs),Camp-5(2,980 HHs),Camp-13 (8,793 HHs),Camp-17 (1,150 HHs),Camp-19 (4,880 HHs),Camp-20 Ext.(300 HHs) and Host Community of Rajapalong union (200 HHs),Holdiapalong union (150 HHs),Ratnapalong union (150 HHs), & Jaliapalong union (150 HHs). CB aims to provide a wide range of interventions using the community led approach in the aforementioned working areas for both the Rohingya refugees and host community with protection mainstreaming across the activities through community engagement promoting community based decision making,program design,implementation and monitoring for sustained results including women, men,youth, adolescent, elderly peoples & peoples with disability (PWD).Caritas will engage daily laborers prioritizing women with light work under CFW for shelter & site improvement activities. Caritas will continue communication & close Coordination with Shelter/NFI sector throughout the project period in 2021 Including attending TWIG,Coordination meeting,SAG meeting,submission of 4W report,other support. Moreover, The project will ensure Coordination with Government authorities, RRRC, Camp-in-Charge(CIC),UNO, shelter/NFI Sector & HLP coordinators, Shelter partners, Protection partners, SMSD agencies, communities etc.
This program includes TSA-2,New Mid Term/Transitional Shelter (one or two storied),Shelter upgradation/repair/maintenance including existing MTS, Emergency Shelter Assistance, HH Level Site Improvement, NFI Distribution, Strengthening field coordination through Catchment focal including referral related to gender issue, Community training on DRR/shelter reinforcement/repair-maintenance and Supporting EVI families including labour support for materials delivery and implementation. Moreover, CB has targeted re-construction/repair/new shelter for host community vulnerable families. Caritas will also explore pilot of alternative shelter materials (i.e. mudwall/CRAterre, Jutin),NFI voucher system and community-led approach. Accountability to affected people (AAP) principles with effective complain/feedback mechanism,Covid-19 safety measures, PSEA and Child protection guidelines will be followed throughout the program. CRS will provide all kinds of Technical support to Caritas throughout the project. Caritas shelter strategy for 2021,focused on Shelter/NFI interventions with an aim to achieve more durable, safe, secure and dignified access to the shelter through participatory community led approaches to improve social cohesion and strengthening household resilience."

1 - Identified Needs

Small-scale weather events cause recurrent damage to shelter activities, resulting in an almost constant need for repairs due to the restricted use of more durable shelter materials in the camps. The main materials used untreated bamboo which are not long-lasting, tarpaulin requires regular material replacement and lack of structural shelter reinforcement such as lack of bracing, ties and connections etc. Targeted camps area is densely populated with limited compliance to plot standards or good site planning and service provision in the past. The humanitarian situation in the camps is further exacerbated by heavy rainfall during monsoon and COVID19 which complicates safe access to the shelter due to lack of sustainable shelter activities. Households located in a landslide-prone area are at additional risk of losing their shelters due to heavy rainfall, strong wind and lack of household level sustainable site improvement activities. According to the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) of July-August,2020 for the Rohingya refugees stated that Issues with shelter remained a common concern for the majority of households, with almost one third of households reporting having bought shelter materials to make repairs.65% of the female households & 63% of the male households reported shelter materials as priority needs over food and income generating activities whereas 74% HHs requested to provide shelter materials as in-kind assistance,31% Requested as Cash assistance and 13% for Labor support.Reduction of shelter activities due to COVID-19 containment measures in 2020 resulted some shelters not being upgraded for almost one year and, without assistance, some shelters condition deteriorated. According to Shelter/NFI sector need analysis, Rapid Damage Verification recorded over 100% increase in damages compared to the damages reported in 2019.More specifically, a total of 29,434 verified damaged shelters were recorded between May and September 2020, compared to 12,299 units during the same timeframe last year.38% of households reported facing mobility challenges inside and/or outside their shelters. The response shall be addressed with shelter-level site improvement with focus on the accessibility of PwD. 58% reported to not have enough light.27% of Rohingya refugees mentioned clothing and household cooking items as their priority needs. During COVID-19 restriction, the NFI support for the households have been reduced except elderly families. Concern of protection and Privacy within these shelters has a serious impact on the physical and psychological well-being of the refugees, and especially for children, Adolescent, women, Young Adults, middle age adults, older Adults and refugees with disabilities. There are gaps regarding protection mainstreaming across activities, including collaboration to promote community-led decision making, where equity issues need to be addressed. Some cash-for-work activities are available to men, but women and vulnerable groups have fewer options due to cultural restrictions of working outside their home. There are lack of Community building repair/maintenance and we have received few request from different CIC & site management agencies.According to J-MSNA 2020, In the neighbor host community Unions of the Rohingya camps, 12% of host community households stay in Jhupri shelters. As a general observation, the economic situation of a household correlates with their house’s condition.59% of the HHs reported issues with their houses, mainly with the roof. 43% households reported facing mobility challenges inside and/or outside their house. There are also lack of effective feedback & complaint mechanism in the camps and host community and lack of community consultation about needs, preferences and the delivery of humanitarian assistance.
