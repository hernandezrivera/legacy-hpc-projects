Project Summary

The project aims to increase safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 5 -17 years) and improve the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 5 - 17 years) within a protective environment. The project will be implemented in close coordination with MoE and related DoEs ensuring the protection of all beneficiaries through a ‘do not harm’ approach. ADRA Syria through this project will improve access to formal and non-formal education, learning opportunities, and Early Childhood Education (ECE) for girls and boys by creating safe and protective environments for 19,580 female and male children and teachers who have been affected by the crisis. This will be done through education interventions within the governorates of Rural Damascus, Quneitra, Homs, Aleppo, Dara’a, Lattakia, Hama, Raqqa and Deir Alzour. ADRA Syria through the project will enhance the formal and non – formal education level for the children, both boys and girls. This will include a holistic program with psycho-social, familial and educational support, encouraging the engagement of mothers and fathers in educational activities. ADRA will coordinate with key stakeholders to ensure the most vulnerable boys and girls are identified in the target locations, and ADRA will conduct comprehensive needs assessments in the areas identified to locate OOSC and students with priority educational needs.

4 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Syria has had a devastating impact on education nationwide. Currently, there are over 7 million children (3-17 years) in need of education support (HNO Education sector findings 2022), an estimated 2.45 million children out of school (OOSC), including an estimated 1.5 million boys who are engaged in child labour and other negative coping mechanisms. This is a 16% increase from the previous year. In addition, there are approximately 1.6 million children at risk of dropping out, an increase 23% from 2020. The deteriorating situation has been caused by multiple factors including the long-term impact of the crisis, continued and multiple displacements, as well as more recent developments including the impact of COVID-19 on the education process and the economic situation (HRP 2021). The infrastructural damage in conflict-affected areas also results in inadequate and overcrowded education facilities with the average number of students per classroom reaching up to 54 (HNO 2022), and children sitting on the floor in classrooms without furniture, doors and windows. In other areas where there are no functional schools, or where there is a shortage of schools, students walk 2-4 kms per day along dangerous roads to attend alternative facilities if they attend at all. Overcrowding is a great concern as it compounds the challenge of providing a conducive learning environment to mixed-aged, mixed-ability students many of whom may have socioemotional needs. It

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The selection of teachers and children to participate in activities or training and learning may also create tensions from those who were not selected, placing staff or beneficiaries at risk. ADRA will work closely with the MoE to select teachers from a variety of schools in areas of high need and according to certain criteria. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). Distribution of learning material and school kits might create tension between host community members and IDPs, especially in that most of the targeted communities include a large number of IDPs which could place beneficiaries or staff at risk. Thus, ADRA Syria will create certain criteria for distribution, depending on the vulnerability level and need of the beneficiaries which will include both IDPs and host community beneficiaries. (Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Acceptable level: Yes). Another risk could happen, beneficiaries placed at risk due to project interventions to mitigate these potential interventions will be discussed with key stakeholders and beneficiaries and follow education sector guidelines to ensure a standardized response across all humanitarian responders and that no harm will inadvertently come to project beneficiaries (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: The field staff or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, likelihood low, impact high. As a mitigation measure, ADRA Syria will stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct, and will regularly refresh and train its staff and partner staff on the importance of maintaining an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Project staff at all different levels, partner staff, contractors, and suppliers will be encouraged to read and understand the ADRA code of conduct and safeguarding, the protection manual and commit to abide by this principle. ADRA protection officer will ensure to have systems in place for activating the system, gathering information, and conducting an investigation where necessary to make decisions together with ADCOM for taking any necessary actions towards any misconduct reported.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The changing security situation. Mitigation: ADRA will monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments. ADRA will work closely with the MoE to choose locations which have been stable for some time and unlikely be directly impacted by renewed conflict. A slight risk that by reopening schools, children will be exposed to protection concerns either on the way to and from schools. ADRA will select schools in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the children when attending school. (Likelihood: low). Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities. To mitigate this ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals and discuss any delays with the MoE (Likelihood: Medium). The lack and difficulty in securing supplies and materials, ADRA's experience in Syria in a similar field has resulted in the identification of potential contractors and staff and thus is unlikely to impact project activities. Lack of staff capacity or skills needed to implement the project. ADRA Staff will continue to receive relevant training, including in the area of humanitarian principals to ensure that they are qualified to undertake the tasks assigned to them (Likelihood: low).
