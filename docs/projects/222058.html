Project Summary

The proposed NRC’s WASH (Water, Sanitation and Hygiene) interventions for 2025 aimed to continue the emergency response to support the people affected by conflict, natural disaster and displacement in emergency context and static as long-term support towards durable solutions for the IDPs, host community and returnees who voluntarily return, resettle and reintegrate with host community. The interventions are designed to address the emergency WASH needs through rapid response mechanism (RRT) and emergency response and contribute to improve health, nutrition, education, Livelihood and food security, shelter and protection outcomes and durable solutions through NRC static WASH response. NRC’s WASH interventions will be integrated and implemented in close coordination with NRC’s other core competencies of Shelter, Education, Livelihood and Food security, ICLA and protection programming and teams, applying an integrated approach to uplift people’s lives and contributing to achieve South Sudan HNRP-2025 and WASH cluster objectives. The proposed interventions are to reduce prevalence of malnutrition, contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality, provide access to basic WASH services in underserved and hard to reach locations affected by disaster, disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility and durable access to WASH amongst 228,200 IDPs, returnees, refuges and vulnerable host communities in in South Sudan.
Recognizing WASH services to be key drivers of health and malnutrition outcomes and women and girls to be the primary users of WASH services, the project aims to reduce GBV risk by ensuring adequate and needs based provision of WASH services that address key protection concerns. NRC’s integrated approach will further address high levels of malnutrition in targeted locations. NRC will consult and engage communities in preliminary assessments and design process to ensure all NRC’s interventions are tailored to local context and to establish appropriated and disaster- resistant water and sanitation facilities at community, institutions and household level.
To enhance and ensure sustainability and proper usage of the facilities comprehensive training will be provided to female and male members of Water User Committees (WUCs) , Schools and other institutional staff. Solid waste management training will also be provided to solid waste/hygiene committees to implement community-driven solid waste collection and disposal campaigns.
To ensure durable solution and continues access to safe water; NRC will establish / rehabilitate small scale solar powered water supply schemes, new boreholes through cost effective manual and machine borehole drilling . The proposed water supply interventions will increase the safe water access to schools, health, nutrition and protection centers, disability inclusion , mitigating GBV at WAH facilities and safe route to WASH facilities. NRC will construct owner driven household / family shared latrines to ensure that immediate and long-term sanitation needs are adequately met, and facilities are gender segregated and culturally accepted.
Hygiene practices will be improved through intensive hygiene interventions. Hygiene ToT (training of trainers) and awareness will be provided to community hygiene promoters who then will be conducting hygiene promotion at their own communities, conduct community hygiene campaigns and hygiene promotion sessions at house to house , intuitional and community levels. NRC will also provide hygiene / MHM kits, WASH-NFIs and soaps to the vulnerable communities specially in hard-to- reach areas to improve their hygiene practices.

1. Needs

Humanitarian situation in South Sudan is kept being deteriorated. The situation further attributed and worsened due to multiple shocks and unaddressed root causes of need of endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges, and economic and climatic shocks. All these challenges impacted affected and vulnerable people’s physical and mental well-being, living standards, and coping mechanisms, exacerbated by marginalization of women, youth, and persons with disabilities. The WASH cluster 2025, HNRP briefing note revealed that 5.01 million people are in need WASH services where cluster targeted 2.25 million people in priority 1, 2 and part of priority 3 counties. South Sudan, Floods Snapshot (UNOCHA, 1 November 2024) reported that about 1.4 million people are impacted by floods in 43 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area. Most affected states are Jonglie, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Warrap, Lakes and Upper Nile states with Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states comprising about half of the affected population. About 375,000 individuals are displaced in 21 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area. Heavy rainfall and flooding have rendered 15 major supply routes impassable, significantly restricting access to affected areas. Moreover, since the conflict erupted in Khartoum, Sudan on April 15, 2023, South Sudan has seen the arrival of 871,388 people as of November 19, 2024. Most of these individuals have crossed through various entry points along the border, with over 698,704 entering via the border crossing in Renk County, Upper Nile state. (dtm.iom.int/reports/Sudan). Unfortunately, conditions for returnees are deteriorating, and it is expected that a total of 228,877 individual (148,877 returnees and 80,000 refugees) are expected to transit through Renk, with an average of 627 arrivals per day in 2025. On 20th October 2024, SS Health Ministry declared cholera outbreak in Renk that has since spread to other regions. Compounding these issues, the capacity of Government authorities and local communities; there is urgent need for humanitarian assistance to protect the lives of those affected by this crisis in Renk and other regions. It’s also anticipated that; Transition Center (TC1) and TC2 will be overcrowded , congested and required additional WASH services to support the new arrivals in 2025 in Renk. Thus, timely provision of WASH support to the crisis affected population will be able to live a dignified life. Thus, NRC will deliver WASH services through static response and mobile emergency response in all Cluster prioritized counties in seven states in South Sudan. Access to water and sanitation is projected to remain a major issue in 2025, further aggravating the risk of malnutrition. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera, AWD, etc. Hence to significantly to contribute to achieve WASH Cluster objective 1: - Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition; NRC will implement integrated WASH , Health and Nutrition, Shelter, Education and LFS response in priority 1 & 2 counties in targeted seven states. Rampant open defecation is further creating public health risks especially in areas where people rely and using surface water, Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon access to improved sanitation. Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Through proposed WASH interventions in water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion. NRC further aims to improve and strengthen ongoing WASH initiatives and expand into cluster prioritized priority 1 & 2 counties.

2. Project activities

Conduct Need assessments and baselines surveys • In-depth situation analysis, baseline and KAP survey to assess WASH needs and key trends in target communities. • Feasibility, Geophysical survey, technical and sanitary surveys of targeted new water points drilling / sources for upgradation / construction / rehabilitations. • Conduct Safety audits for WASH facility establishments and rehabilitation.• Conduct NEAT+ environmental screening assessment to identify the potential environmental impacts through NRC WASH projects and implement the appropriated mitigation measures.• Post Distribution monitoring of WASH-NFIs and Hygiene kits distribution Water Provision: • Establishment and operation of water treatment plants for first line emergency response.• Provision of safe water through water tracking for first line emergency response.• Rehabilitation of water haffirs /water collection pond / dam.• Construction of new boreholes through manual / machine drilling – 10 BHs.• Establishment and rehabilitation of solar motorized small-scale water supply distribution systems - 8 WSSs.• Rehabilitation and repair damaged and abandoned boreholes – 80 BHs.• Establishment of safe Water Storage and distribution facility at Health Centres –5 Facilities.• Drill / Construct and rehabilitate water sources at schools.• Form and train Water Users Committees with more than 50% being women (at least one woman in position of higher responsibility)- • Introduce households water treatment methods.• Conduct water quality testing and surveillance for all NRC’s water sources, water points, distribution networks and random household’s level. • Conduct basic and refresher training for pump mechanics and caretakers with key attention placed on the engagement of women. Sanitation: • Construction of emergency communal latrines for Sudan crisis effected population including returnees and refugees and displacement affected IDPs and host communities.• Construction of family shared latrines through owner driven approach.• Construction / rehabilitation institutional latrines – (schools and Protection centers) Environmental Health: • • Conduct safe Solid Waste Management disposal, drainage and debris clean up campaigns-• Form and train solid waste management communities at village / Boma level. • Construction / rehabilitation of drainage system. Hygiene Promotion: • ToT training of Community hygiene promoters (40% male and 60% female) in targeted payams/bomas. • Conduct hygiene promotion sessions / awareness creation at house to house,. Community and institutional levels.• Distribute hygiene kits.• Distribute WASH – NFIs kits• Conduct special “Women and Girl’s menstrualhygiene education and promotion” sessions on good menstrual hygiene practices.• Form and train School Hygiene Clubs.• Establish handwashing facilities in institutions and common places. • Conduct and celebrate WASH global events in schools / Community (GHD , World water day, etc).• Sanitation and Hygiene promotion campaigns per project site.• Prepare and implement cholera prevention measures through hygiene promotion campaigns.• Broadcast Covid-19 and cholera awareness, prevention and control messages using Jingles in local languages using Mega phones.
