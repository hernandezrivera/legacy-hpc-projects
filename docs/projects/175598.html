Project Summary

The displacement trend in Jonglei and Unity state remain fluid.The country continues to experience conflict and natural disaster-driven displacing communities in informal site,camp like-setting and hard-to-reach area's. The project will ensure equal access and needs based assistance to improve the quality of integrated services for populations affected by displacement, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian intervention to 40 000 ( 10 000 Male and 30 000 Female) IDPs across seven (7) Counties ( Akobo, Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, Uror, Mayendit and Panyijiar of Jonglei and Unity state to improve their well-being, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. It will respond to emergency needs by empowering community and strengthening the capacities of local actors to have access to both essentials camp management service. Using CCCM cluster strategy , HRSS will coordinate and ensure that displaced persons have equitable access to basic services, assistance, and protection including support towards self-reliance, enabling them mitigate dependence on humanitarian assistance. HRSS will expand it activities to camp-like settings and hard to reach area's to strengthen accountable, community-led governance structures and site management mechanisms and community base led initiative, In conjunction with humanitarian and development actors, HRSS will effectively support the right to solutions for the displaced population,advocating for minimum standards relating to service provision in hard to reach area's.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is likely to continue to worsen in 2021, driven by cumulative effects of years of recurring conflict and violence, further aggravated by subsequent shocks. Continued macroeconomic crisis, the low preparedness capacity of national and state authorities, COVID-19 pandemic, relatively lower oil prices, and consecutive years of flooding are expected to further exacerbate existing vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs of people, while pushing more people downward spiral into crisis and reliance on humanitarian assistance. According to humanitarian overview 2021,there is 1.2 million people in need of CCCM service includes an estimated 150,000 people affected by floods in 2020 living with host communities in need of humanitarian assistance. Approximately 872,000 displaced people live in spontaneous sites or informal settlements or with host communities, they share limited resources which are grossly inadequate compounded with the limited basic services within the communities making them heavily dependent on humanitarian aid. Flood related displacement has the potential to exacerbate existing tensions in sites. It can also fuel new tensions as host and displaced communities compete for scarce resources and limited. These stressors present a burden for displaced individuals and families and negatively affects their mental health, psycho social well-being and daily functionality. All collective settings need some form of management to create safe and secure living environment in order to meet the minimum human rights standards for the displaced population. Increasing diversity in modalities of settlement requires the HRSS to preposition herself with robust approaches and skills to meet the diverse needs of the displaced persons. Unless these gaps are addressed, the protective environment of camp and camp-like settings will continue to deteriorate resulting in aggravated risks and abuse. Displaced persons in camps and camp-like settings including out of camp are constrained with challenges over options for durable solutions. People in informal and collective sites lack access to information about their rights, freedom of movement and available services. The existence of settlements without any coordination and management structures have led to sporadic or a complete lack of aid provision to vulnerable people living in these informal settlements. According to Humanitarian needs overview (2021 ), Based on site level key informants and CCCM analysis, 60 per cent of displaced people reported being exposed to increased protection, public health and gender based violence risks. Lack of livelihood opportunities creates a weak sense of coping mechanisms amongst some of the displaced families creating difficulty and triggers elements of frustration and hopelessness.Major efforts will be required to ensure potential for integration is fully explored, and peaceful co-existence initiatives; enhancing thereby community participation. To this effect, further engagement in integrated needs assessment by ensuring a protective environment in camps and camp like settings for the displaced population and host community, Providing a predictable and coordinated delivery of service, provision of information aimed at improving the quality of integrated services for the displaced populations in camps and camp like settings. Establishing inclusive governance structures to promote the camp population’s participation in decision making and issues which affect their lives by strengthening community coping mechanisms geared towards lasting solutions,local ownership, self-governance and self-reliance.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HRSS will conduct consultation with stakeholder at each location on how they can response when needs arise on PSEA and will continue to include PSEA topic in the training of project beneficiaries and community leaders at all the site. HRSS will also make sure that all the project staffs will be trained in PSEA and PSEA message are incorporated in community engagement meeting and provide PSEA information at complaint Desk at the site.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HRSS together with Community protection committee will promote the representation and participation of vulnerable members including persons with disabilities, women and girls, older persons and persons with chronic illnesses in community engagement forums. Message context will be tailored for the targeted audience respecting of age, gender, and diversity group. Through the satisfaction surveys HRSS will be able to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on services and tailoring the feedback on project improvement.There will be participatory with all stakeholder at the site.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HRSS will use Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) approach which is a system blending both formal and informal community structures, where individuals will be able and encouraged to safely reporting. Local communities will be involved in developing and approving the CBCM so that the structure is both culturally and gender-sensitive. The mechanism should have multiple entry points, allowing both beneficiaries and staff the opportunity to report at the organizational level – internally through the network’s and field agency focal points at the community level. HRSS will ensuring that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries at all stages of this project cycle. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner and frequent consultations of key beneficiary groups in decision making particularly women and girls. HRSS will spend at least two weeks in the field to engage and work closely with beneficiaries to set up a project implementation committee that will last throughout the project, nominated by the target beneficiaries and are composed of the beneficiaries. This committee will share with all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, the elderly, those who are illiterate, women and girls issues agreed upon during their meetings such as types of assistance needed, safety audits, meeting and distribution locations and sites.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

HRSS will conduct a town hall meeting monthly with community structure committee to incorporate their feedback . The town hall meeting will be inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries at all stages of this project cycle. This will improve participation and accountability for all affected populations. the feedback will be incorporating in the next project phase for improvement and share with other partners in order to improved service at the site. Through the satisfaction surveys HRSS will be able to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on services and tailoring the feedback on project improvement
