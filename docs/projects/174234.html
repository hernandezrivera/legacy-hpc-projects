Project Summary

The IRC seeks to improve the general health and well-being of approximately 95,000 conflict affected persons across Borno, Yobe and Adamawa (BAY) States. The main objective of this project is to ensure that people are protected from water, sanitation and hygiene related diseases through provision of access to safe drinking water, provision of safe sanitation and as well as ensuring people have the knowledge to protect themselves from WASH diseases. Focus will be placed on Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 of the Humanitarian Response Plan (2019-2021) which includes: Saving lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector WASH assistance and Mainstreaming protection interventions to the most vulnerable population. Activities will again focus on WASH Sector Strategic Objectives which includes: Strategic Objective 1: affected people have safe and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water for domestic needs, as per sector’s standards, Strategic Objective 2: affected people have safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities, as per sector’s standard, Strategic Objective 3: affected people benefit from community tailored gender- and age-sensitive hygiene messages and finally Strategic Objective 4 of affected people benefit from basic gender- and age-sensitive hygiene items including top-ups as per sector’s standards. Proposed interventions will target IDP populations in IDP camps and informal settlements, while also targeting host and return communities in which priority will be on empowering and capacity building community structures so as to promote sustainability of WASH facilities across BAY states. In addition COVID-19 preventive measures will be incorporated in all proposed activities to ensure mitigation of spread of COVID-19.
The IRC will achieve this through improving safe access to water, sanitation and hygiene awareness, through the construction, rehabilitation and upgrading of water sources, construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in IDP camps, supply of WASH NFI's to vulnerable households and through hygiene messaging at IDP camps. The IRC will also work in increasing the capacity of community structures including WASH committees, Infection prevention control (IPC) committees at health centers so as to promote sustainability of the WASH intervention. The IRC will also target health and nutrition centers so as to help improve the health service delivery as well as provide a platform for sharing targeted key hygiene messages to the patients and their care givers. Also households with severely undernourished children will be targeted for hygiene and water storage kits to promote health growth and recovery of the affected children.
The IRC through the WASH sector, will also work closely with other partners to continue promoting menstrual hygiene management (MHM) in IDP camps as well as continue supporting in cholera response when needed in BAY States.
Also the IRC will continue to enhance COVID-19 preventive measures across BAY states intervention areas so as to ensure that vulnerable communities are protected from the COVID-19 pandemic.
In Adamawa and Yobe state, the IRC will focus most WASH activities in health, schools and nutrition centers and also adopt long term recovery and development strategy in host communities. The IRC will build on current experience of together with RUWASSA of establishing, training and working closely with community structures which include WASH Committees and Community health clubs to promote community ownership of installed water projects.
The IRC will continue to coordinate its activities with the WASH sector and work with the local and state level Ministry of water, Ministry of health and Ministry of education during the assessments, implementation and monitoring of its WASH projects.

1 - Needs

From 2009, the Boko Haram (herein, Armed Opposition Groups [AOGs]) insurgency continues to affect Northeastern (NE) Nigeria. Borno State (Borno) where of the 2.2 million people forced from their homes across the region, over half (1.5 million) are in Borno State. Of the 2.06 million people forced from their homes across the NE region due to insurgency, over half (1.5 million) are in Borno. Over 80% of the IDP population is in Borno state, while women and children make up 79% of the displaced vulnerable population (HNO, 2020). These populations continue to face numerous challenges that compromise their dignity, health and wellbeing and living in congested camp settings. According to the WASH Sector Gap Analysis and MSNA 2020 data, an estimated 2.8 million people across NE Nigeria remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to clean water and dignified hygiene and sanitation services. Of these people, 1.02 million people receive less than 15 litres per person per day while about 1.06m people have intermittent water access with over 500meters walking distance and over 30 minutes queuing time at water points. 1.8 million People are internally displaced across the BAY states and are living in either a formal IDP camps or in an informal settlements with host communities which has occasionally led to complaints from host community members about the increased competition for key services such as water sources. In addition, overcrowding in the IDP camps has resulted to latrine access ratios of more than 20 persons per latrine cubicle in most IDP camps. The feacal sludge management is poor, with low infrastructure to support liquid waste from desluging activities, which is considered as one of the reasons for yearly outbreak of cholera. Additionally flooding is a major concern as most of the feacal sludge worsens with households facing exposure of untreated waste. Also, there continues to be a rise in reported acute diarrhea cases, with average bi-weekly data of 1,500-2,000 individuals amongst populations. The lack of adequate WASH infrastructure, combined with the congestion in the various IDP camps and host communities has led to overstraining of the already limited water and sanitation sources 35% of the health facilities with associated WASH services across BAY states were greatly affected by the insurgency which makes them ill prepared to respond to COVID-19. NE Nigeria has also seen a rapid spread of the virus and as at 5th December 2020, cases were as follows: Borno (745 cases with 36 deaths), Adamawa (261 cases, 19 deaths) and Yobe (100 cases with 8 death. According to a recent Multi-sector Needs Assessment for COVID-19 in BAY States, despite the improved water access in Borno state, 38% of population still have limited access to water leading to reduced personal and other general hygiene activities including handwashing within their homes. 25% of this population will spend between 15 to 30 minutes when collecting water, while another 25% spend more than 30 minutes during water collection times. This indeed means prolonged exposure of the already vulnerable populations to crowds in a time when social distancing is encouraged to prevent community transmission of COVID-19. Lack of soap continues to endanger the health of IDP populations with 60% mentioning soap as a key item needed in their households to enhance hand washing behavior. Although most LGAs in Adamawa and Yobe states have experiences some stability in relation to security, the populations still suffer from displacements as a result of flooding as well as from cholera outbreaks. Cholera outbreaks across the BAY states have continued to expose key WASH gaps in most health facilities that, though operational, are not adequately prepared to respond to the COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities

The IRC will build on existing experience and strong coordination with the state Ministry of water resources, ministry of health, NE WASH Sector, UN and other WASH and health Partners, community leadership and community structures to help provide sustainable WASH for vulnerable populations in IDP camps and return communities across BAY states. In IDP camps, the IRC will increase water supply from the existing boreholes through upgrading of existing motorized boreholes and hand pumps. The IRC will aim at increasing pumping hours of the generator and solar powered systems but ensure that ground water monitoring is in place to ensure water abstraction is monitored. The IRC will preposition chlorine, generator fuel, quick moving spare parts for hand pumps, and generators as well as spare pumps and piping that will ensure minimal interruption to water supply for vulnerable populations in case of breakdowns as well as ensuring water is treated before use. In return communities, the IRC will prioritize the upgrading of productive boreholes and through the community and WASH committee leadership establish sustainable ways for ensuring the projects are maintained by communities themselves. The IRC will preposition materials needed for quick rehabilitation of latrine and bathing facilities in the IDP camps. The IRC will identify and train experienced team of carpenters and masons from the IDP population on basic facility repair who will be responsible for repairs of the sanitation facilities. Also the IRC will preposition sanitation cleaning kits that will include latrine cleaning detergents, disinfectant, brooms and buckets for the already identified WASH committee members to ensure continued care and maintenance of the sanitation facilities. The IRC will in coordination with the CCCM and other WASH actors rehabilitate water and sanitation and clinical waste management facilities in health facilities located within the targeted IDP camps. The rehabilitation of these facilities will be followed up with training of the health partners responsible for maintaining these health facilities to ensure appropriate infection prevention and control (IPC) protocols are followed accompanied with weekly IPC monitoring. Increasing access to soap and communal hand washing stations for populations in IDP camps will help improve hand hygiene which is key in preventing COVID-19 and WASH related diseases. The IRC will install and maintain communal hand washing stations at all main entrance gates to the IDP camps, meeting places, distribution locations, markets, and latrine and bathing facilities. Also the IRC will encourage the construction of household level hand washing facilities in the form of tippy taps using materials such as used water bottles that can be installed at individual households to promote hand washing at home. Hygiene promotion will continue to be shared across target communities so as to increase people’s knowledge in protecting themselves from diarrheal diseases.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC will train all the WASH staff, WASH Committee, and community hygiene volunteers (CHVs) to strengthen and promote a shared understanding of protection mainstreaming, including child safeguarding principles and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC will uphold the do no harm principle by ensuring that all their services and structures are meaningfully accessible, safe, promote beneficiary dignity and promote ownership of the project by the beneficiaries through constant community engagement/participation and functional feedback processes throughout the project period. Together with the Protection team, the WASH team will conduct discussions with community members while paying close attention to the different groups with unique needs to establish where to set up WASH facilities and water points. Latrines and bathing facilities will be fitted with ramps and handrails to improve access for elderly and persons with disability. For privacy and security purposes, all latrines and bathing facilities will be segregated, fitted with locks on the inside and privacy walls provided for all female latrines and bathing facilities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include separate sessions with women, girls, persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups, these sessions will ensure social distancing of at least 2m between participants who will also be provided with hand sanitizer, face masks and temperature taken upon coming into the meeting area. Existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums will also be used to collect beneficiary feedback on key quality markers. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. Client satisfaction surveys that incorporates feedback from the vulnerable groups on water sanitation and WASH NFIs provided will be carried out on a monthly basis to ensure equitable access to WASH services among all population groups. Client exit interviews will be conducted via telephone to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allows the IRC to make rapid and informed adjustments to the project. Safety audits will also be conducted each month, where possible, with women and girls in the IDP camps to understand the protection related risks and concerns. Advocacy strategies will be developed together with other implementing partners, community structures, and female beneficiaries, to respond to identified needs. Phone interviews, text messaging and other virtual communication channels will be heavily relied on as a form of remote monitoring and client feedback mechanisms. If possible, feedback will be collected from community leaders/representatives, health facility in charges when direct service users are not reachable or accessible.
