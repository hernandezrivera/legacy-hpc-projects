Project Summary

UNICEF will support the humanitarian actors to strengthen the capacity of the health system, with a focus at the primary health care levels including at the community level to avail quality, life-saving and high impact interventions to 2,668,000 boys and girls (children under the age of 15 years) and pregnant and lactating women in humanitarian situations. UNICEF will support diversified service delivery modalities including through fixed health facilities, outreach sessions, mobile medical clinics and community-based health service providers to ensure access to quality lifesaving health services, which will include preventive, promotive and curative care for the most vulnerable.
Through this project, UNICEF will support the humanitarian actors to avail integrated primary health care packages of services including immunization, integrated management of childhood illnesses, maternal and newborn health (Antenatal Care, skilled delivery, postnatal care). In addition, UNICEF will support the humanitarian actors in timely responding to any shocks including outbreaks of communicable diseases, flood, displacements and others as they arise.
To achieve this, UNICEF will build the capacity of the state and locality level health sector managers, implementing partners, health service providers in a wide range of programmatic areas to ensure service delivery and its support systems are in place. UNICEF will provide the essential medicines, supplies and commodities which includes vaccines, medicines, medical kits, cold chain equipment, bed nets, registers, reporting formats and other M&E tools. At the community level, UNICEF will enhance community engagement to strengthen community’s resilience, enhance utilization of health services and promote healthy practices. In addition, to deliver these services and ensure synergetic impacts, UNICEF will promote and support the implementation of integrated programs in collaboration with the other sectors including nutrition, WASH and others.

1 - Needs

Sudan continue to face a range of significant natural and man-made disasters. Almost all these disasters have direct impact on the survival and the health of children and mothers. The socio-economic situations in the country has deteriorated, especially recently affecting the survival of the most vulnerable. This has continued in 2019, widening the existing health gaps and opening significant issues in new areas including in the urban. Sudan is currently in the middle of a historic transition from a military rule to a civilian one, and this has a significant impact on all sectors including health. The Health system in Sudan continues to be suboptimal, with the health indicators consistently low and the disparity among the different states and between urban and rural and between rich and poor is wide. Children continue to die mainly from preventable and/or easily treatable conditions such as neonatal conditions, acute respiratory infections, diarrhea, malnutrition and malaria. The coverage of services for mothers and children is one of the lowest in the region. Access to health care for the neediest continued to be limited. According to the 2019 S3M data, 35% of Sudanese need to travel at least half an hour to access a health facility, and about 19% need to do so at least for one hour to reach a health facility. Only 24% of children with each of diarrhea and ARI were brought to a health care provider for treatment, and of those who visited for diarrhea, only 27% were provided with ORS and of those who visited for ARI, 64% were provided treatment for ARI. the same data source indicated that the coverage of penta 3 among children under the age of 12 months is 78% with a defaulter rate between penta 3 and penta 1 of 14%, and those who are vaccinated for measles is 73%. About 15% of pregnant women never visited ANC sites. Only half of the pregnant women conducted four or more ANC visits, and only 34% of delivered women and their newborns received post-natal care. In addition, Sudan is witnessing outbreaks of multiple communicable diseases including cholera, malaria, dengue and other vector-borne diseases. The population in need for health humanitarian assistance has increased to 8.6 million, lacking basic lifesaving and life sustaining health services across Sudan. of which, about 175,000 people in need are facing a catastrophic health situation (severity 5), approximately 2.5 million people in extreme need for health assistance (severity 4), and 5 million people in areas of severe need of health lifesaving activities (severity 3). Around 3.5 million children under 5 years of age need Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) programs for early diagnosis and treatment of life-threatening health conditions. In addition, 2,000,000 pregnant and women of reproductive age in need of sexual and reproductive health services. Around 15% of which is expected to experience pregnancy related complications and need access to emergency obstetric care and referral services.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The following are the key activities to be implemented through this project:1. Procure and distribute the essential heath supplies (vaccines, ORS, Zinc, IMCI kits, MW kits, LLITNS and AWD kits) and commodities including cold chain and IMCI equipment 2. Build the capacity of the health managers (women and men) at the state and locality levels to strengthen the health system and improve health outcomes3. Build the capacity of the front-line health care providers (women and men) -at both facility and community levels- to deliver quality life-saving preventive, promotive and curative health services (EPI, case management of childhood illnesses, PMTCT, maternal and newborn care, case management of communicable diseases), 4. Support referral of severe/complicated cases to the secondary level of care, 5. Provide operational cost for health facilities, mobile clinics, outreach and mobile immunization teams to ensure that services points are open/functional 6. Support preparedness and timely response to health emergencies- including communicable diseases’ outbreaks- among the vulnerable population, 7. Support provision of health promotion and communication for behavior change/impact activities to the vulnerable population through media, campaigns and house to house visits, 8. Strengthen monitoring and evaluation of the health activities9. Documentation of best practices UNICEF will support the implementation of the above activities through diverse ways. This includes UNICEF's direct procurement of the needed supplies and commodities, supporting the FMoH, SMoHs and partnership with local and international NGOs to deliver services.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Climate change is one of the causes of the increased vulnerability of the population of Sudan to natural disasters including floods, droughts and their consequences such as eruption of conflicts related to competition for resources causing displacements, increase in vector-borne diseases outbreak and others. This project aims at supporting to strengthen the capacity of the health system in Sudan to make it more resilient to shocks as well as to be able to detect early signs of shocks before they happen to minimize their risks. Those localities with vulnerable communities related to the impact of climate change are targeted through this project. The project’s support at the community level will particularly contribute to community’s resilience to and ownership of their own health as part of the mitigation measures.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Beneficiary's safety and protection is one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. It has zero tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment. For the last couple of years, UNICEF has been training and sensitizing (both online and class room trainings) its staffs and implementing partners to raise awareness on Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating and reporting any such violence not only those may be happening within its staffs bit also with its beneficiaries. Because of its focus on this, UNICEF’s highest level (representative) is held accountable at organizational level to put in place this mechanism. In addition, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners for their sensitivity to the matter including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy: -The children, women, men and communities that UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse; -UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance and support to survivors; -All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment; -All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity and respect for diversity. They have the obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone or tolerate behaviour that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing SEA and SH- An Organisational Culture of zero tolerance: Create and nurture an organisational culture based on Accountability where there is a zero tolerance for SEA and SH, where rights are recognized, promoted and protected and where violations are actively prevented. - Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted: Empower and support individuals, communities, UNICEF personnel and partners to feel safe to report violations and to feel safe that reports will be handled in a manner that respects due process and other human rights. - Swift and Credible Investigation and Sanctions: Ensure a fair process for swift and credible investigations and sanctions for violations by UNICEF personnel, and actively promote swift and fair investigations and sanctions by (as appropriate) Government partners, implementing partners, commercial vendors, and other UN System organizations.- Survivor-Centred Response: Provide survivor-centred assistance and support that is timely, predictable, sustainable and adequately resourced. - Engaging Partners in the fight against SEA and SH: Engage and equip
