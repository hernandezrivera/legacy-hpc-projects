Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face extremely high prevalence of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), driven by a rigidly patriarchal system and historic marginalization of women. It is imperative that humanitarian interventions are sensitive to this reality, given that they may both cause and exacerbate the incidence of GBV cases. Action Against Hunger proposes to: a) continue its efforts to mainstream GBV and gender considerations in its Nutrition, Health FSL and WaSH programs, and b) support the humanitarian community in South Sudan to remain accountable for GBV and gender-sensitivity. In 2021, the organization will conduct a Gender Analysis in all of our areas of intervention to evaluate programmatic changes initiated by their previous iterations (e.g. Male Group Discussions) and continue to monitor gender dynamics and protection risks on the ground. Other actors will also benefit from this analysis. In collaboration with relevant clusters, we will continue to conduct semi-annual Safety Audits for all nutrition / health facilities, while developing and rolling out similar tools for the assessment and improvement of WaSH facilities. Through sensitization of men and boys, and engagement of men caregivers on a dialogue on protection issues in Men Group Discussions, the project will tackle gender stereotypes and some of the root causes of gender inequality. These discussions will also facilitate an enabling environment for women’s access to our services. In 2021, Action Against Hunger will continue to train all staff at community, site, and base levels on recognizing forms of GBV and strengthening awareness on existing referral mechanisms, in addition to Psychological First Aid. This is especially important, as our staff are actively solicited for counselling on GBV cases that are reported during program implementation. The project will directly benefit 753 individuals. Working with other partners through Nutrition, Health, FSL and WaSH Clusters, and GBV sub-cluster will amplify this impact. Due to the project’s focus on mitigating risks for the beneficiaries of Action Against Hunger’s programs in South Sudan, 223,616 individuals will benefit indirectly from project results, by receiving more appropriate and accountable humanitarian services.

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) threatens the overall well-being of South Sudan’s population, particularly women and children. Though there are no reliable GBV national prevalence statistics for South Sudan, one recent study estimated that in some conflict-affected locations 65 percent of women and girls in South Sudan have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime (USAID, South Sudan GBV prevention and response roadmap, September 2019). Action Against Hunger 2018 Gender Analysis in Aweil East and Gogrial West indicate that most prevalent forms of GBV include forced early marriage (25.2%), domestic violence (21%), and rape/attempted rape. Most affected age groups of women were those who were middle aged (28%), followed by young adults (27%). GBV can be detrimental to the nutritional and health status of women as well as her children if present, given that she is denied decision-making and purchasing power: this causes deterioration of physical health and mental wellbeing. Having little control over one’s sexuality and pressure on women to bear additional children increases the stress on household food and nutrition security and limits the ability to breastfeed. Our study revealed that those who experience SGBV are often ostracized, excluded from valuable income-generating opportunities. Moreover, men’s lack of knowledge and engagement in nutrition and health programs often lead to their restricting women’s movement to services in favor of attending to household needs, a reality that inhibits the access of women to critical basic services. Further, the stigma around GBV, especially sexual exploitation and abuse, has precluded many from seeking treatment, exacerbating the physical health and nutrition effects of the attack. Humanitarian agencies in South Sudan largely act as providers of health, nutrition, FSL, WaSH and other services, in support of state duty bearers. However, not all partners are equally equipped to ensure a gender-responsive and do-no-harm approach: hence the need for mutual support and standardization. The feedback from the Safety Audits conducted in 2019 was overwhelmingly positive and corroborates the need of a sustained effort in the coming years.

2 - Project activities

Gender Analysis: We will conduct Gender Analyses in each of Action Against Hunger’s areas of interventions to assess gender and GBV dynamics that affect or undermine current humanitarian intervention strategies. Results will be analyzed against our 2018 exercise to reveal contextual changes and the effectiveness of our gender-targeted activities that took place in 2019 and 2020. This will inform any recommendations and changes in approach for our organization as well as for cluster partners. Nutrition Safety Audit: In 2019, Action Against Hunger, in partnership with the Nutrition Cluster, validated and rolled out the Nutrition Safety Audit, meant to assess protection risks in and around nutrition facilities and suggest measures for risk prevention and mitigation. In 2021, we will continue to use the revised Safety Audit tools in each of our facilities. The findings will enable us to mitigate the potential risks posed through different sectors for example – ensuring availability of gender segregated WASH facilities at nutrition sites; reducing waiting time for beneficiaries coming to sites to avail services; ensuring FSL cash distribution centres are close to beneficiary homes to reduce long distance movement; provision of PFA and directing to relevant referral pathway to any GBV survivor approaching our team on ground to name a few. WaSH Safety Audit: In 2021, in partnership with the WaSH Cluster’s AAP Technical Working Group, WaSH Safety Audit tools will be shared with all national WaSH partners of South Sudan for promotion of protection-sensitive WaSH programming. WaSH infrastructure (sanitation facilities. Boreholes etc) constructed/rehabilitated will ensure safe and secure access for women, girls and persons with disabilities, based on the findings of the audit.Promoting Awareness on GBV Referral Pathways: Action Against Hunger will train all site and community level staff in our areas on identifying available referral mechanisms for cases of GBV, as well as raising awareness among program participants on such pathways. As part of this effort, we will work with the GBV sub-cluster to gather information on available services and latest referral pathways. Men Group Discussions: The organization is implementing Men Group Discussions alongside Mother-to-Mother Support Groups in all nutrition site catchment areas. We have refined the context-appropriate curriculum along with training guidance and monitoring tools to ensure an effective behavior change intervention. We aim to conduct a learning exercise in the end of 2021 to analyze the impact of this activity, as well as generate ideas on effective GBV-related messages and GBV-mainstreaming strategies for sharing with the greater humanitarian community in South Sudan. Staff Capacity Building and Gender Focal Points:In line with its internal Gender Minimum Standard certification in 2019 for the South Sudan mission, Action Against Hunger will continue to train its entire staff on gender and GBV concepts to establish a universal commitment to the values of gender equality and GBV awareness raising. Knowledge on gender and GBV dynamics will ensure that all the programs are implemented in a gender-sensitive manner. Psychological First Aid (PFA):We will ensure that all site and community level staff are capable of delivering high-quality Psychological First Aid. This is especially important as many cases of GBV are reported at the site level, and staff are often unequipped to appropriately interact with survivors. PFA will ensure that at each project site, staff are available to support survivors and safely facilitate referral to available services.Feedback and Complaints Mechanism: Supporting the implementation of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policies within the organisation and within community, and promoting awareness of complaint mechanisms.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Action Against Hunger trains all of its staff and volunteers on the organization’s PSEA Policy. During our mobilization meetings, Action Against Hunger shares information about PSEA from the organization’s PSEA Policy. During project implementation, the organization regularly shares PSEA information with beneficiaries as reminder/refresher to emphasize the premium the organizations places Zero Tolerance for PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our nutrition and WaSH programs. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1. Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate and nutrition facilities, 5. Anonymous Hotline Number. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites and on all project structures (e.g. equipment used by beneficiaries). IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the Head of Base and Program Managers through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Action Against Hunger implements a comprehensive, gender-sensitive feedback mechanism. This feedback mechanism uses a range of modalities to solicit feedback from frontline program implementers (including volunteers and field staff), local communities in which programs are operating, and direct program beneficiaries. While the feedback mechanism is firmly institutionalized, the organization continues to examine how to best track and respond to the feedback received, and how to integrate this process into its M&E strategy.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, Action Against Hunger will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All our project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Men Group Discussion facilitation, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on our mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Country Director, Head of Logistics and Head of Operations. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Action Against Hunger provides integrated services to beneficiaries at community level. Key protection services are incorporated within our programs which include GBV focal persons identified at all facilities; training of staffs on GBV risk mitigation and available referral pathways and services; provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) to GBV survivors approaching the project teams; hiring more female staffs in the project team and committees formed at community/village level; conducting safety audits at our project sites etc.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The production of more food, promotion of resilient activities will go a long way in supporting populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. Enhancing the productivity of people in need of humanitarian aid will reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 as they become self-reliant and continue to fend for themselves. This will also include sensitization of the community on potential GBV threats and available referral pathways and services.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Action Against Hunger has a policy on fair provision of cross-sectoral/integrated services to all sections of the affected population including women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities and special needs. The program will follow this policy and all sections of the affected population will be served according to their needs. The provision of services will be based on the organization’s needs/vulnerability analysis.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The overall food security program is aimed at providing sustainable sources of foods which will positively impact a reduction in the level of malnutrition in our intervention areas. Our nutrition sensitive agriculture activities are specifically intended to reduce the GAM rate among the U5 children. This is complemented by our intervention in the nutrition programs being implemented by Action Against Hunger at static nutrition and outreach sites. This will also lead to reduction in exposure to potential GBV risks for the beneficiaries and their caretakers.
