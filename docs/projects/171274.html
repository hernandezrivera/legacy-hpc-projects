Project Summary


The protection rights and needs of children is estimated at 1.6 million people, of whom 998 are IDPs and 602 are non IDPs while 1.2 Million people are in need of gender based violence services of which 786 are in IDPs and 414 are none IDPs of these 64 percent are children, and 48 percent are women and girls, affected by armed conflict and violence and destitution triggered by climatic shocks, including prolonged drought conditions and floods. People in need of protection include households with significant proportion of persons with heightened vulnerability households with vulnerable members such as people with disability, medical conditions, children, elderly or pregnant/lactating women. The vulnerability is further increased by the Corona virus global pandemic in which these with disability have been adversely affected. The total figure of people in need also comprises of households forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as hazardous child labor, early marriage or family separation, families facing safety concern, at risk of eviction or with unresolved land disputes, without awareness of or access to protection services, and excluded from participation. Children (Boys and girls) including disable continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights including family separation, GBV, physical abuse, psychosocial distress, mental health, child labor, wide spread recruitment and use of children by armed groups, forced displacement, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices and exploitation. This situation is exacerbated by the limited availability of basic services, weak governance, negative social norms, and persistent marginalization. Persistent violations of children’s rights continue to be a critical concern in Somalia as children and adolescents are often targets of killing and maiming, abduction, recruitment into armed forces or armed groups, detention, and sexual and physical violence. Armed groups attack schools or hospitals and deny children access to humanitarian assistance. According to the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) report May,2020 verified and documented a total of 1,283 cases (699 boys and 314 girls) of violence against children, with abduction and recruitment/use of children accounting for 41% and 55% of the overall number of children affected. Among the people in need 48 percent are women and girls, while children constitute 64 percent of the people in need. Thus, girls and boys remain a group particularly exposed to protection risks, such as violence (including GBV), recruitment, exploitation, and neglect). An estimate 480,000 people in need live with disability stemming from physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments, and as a result, face serious risks of exploitation, ill treatment, neglect and stigmatization. A large portion of the displaced communities in need are members of marginalized groups and disable people, who lack support network in the areas of displacement or residence. In addition to this finding from multi-sectoral safety audits in 2020 indicate specific GBV risks for disable group, women and girls such as sexual harassment and rape. The main causes are inadequate fencing of IDP camps, poor shelters constructed with inappropriate and light housing materials (e.g. dump cloths, plastic sheets and sticks), poor lighting, lack of private spaces for sleeping, over-crowding, and faulty solar lanterns and torches
The proposed primary objectives of the project will be to improve protection of conflict affected children boys, girls and women including disable group(10%) by preventing violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect through preventative and responsive community based child protection interventions as well as Strengthen prevention and response interventions against sexual and gender based violence against most vulnerable women and girls including disable people

Project Activities

1.1 Provide age and gender specific including PLWD with mental health and psychosocial support services to 6,000 most vulnerable boys and girls affected populations in Beletweyne, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Baidoa district 1.2 Conduct 12 session individual case management psychosocial services benefiting a total of 1,100 (10% PLWD) vulnerable children boys and girls in Beletweyne, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Baidoa district 1.3 Provide counseling and material support (dignity kits; solar lanterns, cooking fuel, fuel efficient stoves) to 200 vulnerable children (100 boys and 100 girls) released from armed forces and reintegrate/reunified with their families in the target location 1.4 Trained 40 caregivers/parents on positive parenting and mental health and psychosocial support services 1.5 Provide access to community based prevention and response on mental health and psychosocial services and children protection support to vulnerable 100 children with disability (60 boys, 40 girls) in Beletweyne, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Baidoa district 1.6 Trained 4o social workers, community volunteers, local authorities and child protection focal point on demonstrated increased knowledge and skills on child protection services 1.7 Provide access to enhanced safe, confidential specialized clinical management of rape services to 200 vulnerable girls and women in Beletweyne, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Baidoa district 1.8 Provide specialized age appropriate mental health psychosocial support and case management to 200 women and adolescent girls including PLWD with trauma related experience in Beletweyne, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Baidoa district 1.9 Provide protection material assistance/dignity to 100 vulnerable adolescent girls and women including people living with disability Beletweyne, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Baidoa district 1.10 Provide legal aid support to 100 GBV survivors women and girls including people with disability in Beletweyne, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Baidoa district

Mitigation Measures

IDPs in the targeted locations are discriminated from accessing public services and are sometime denied access to security due to the displacement. Clan-based discrimination is a common incident in all the targeted camps specially IDPs household from minority groups, people with disability as well as female headed households are particularly affected by the discrimination. Gatekeeping from the landowners and their proxies exploited IDP due to their vulnerability and sometimes subject them to absolute deprivation from aid service or even public services. Sexual exploitation is relation to access and document for humanitarian aid has been identified as common. Sexual violence against minority women and girl’s groups is extremely common and most often unreported due to lack of clan protection. Preventions/Mitigation Measures: HIDIG will have conducted awareness on SGBV and discrimination issues through structured engagement with relevant stakeholders including religious leaders, women leaders, person with disability and business people. We will make concreted efforts to bring local authorities and traditional communities leaders to raise their voices on the clan discrimination and will call for the protection of IDP minority population. IDPs will be empowered to raise their voices on matters that affect them and will be asked to prevention clan based discrimination and calls for minority protection will be raised through out the project period. The Community based Child Protection structure will play a key role in ensuring that all protection issues are identified and reported through appropriate channels. Due to existing community norms; girls and women and people with disability are often subjected to exclusion due to cultural belief dominancy. girls, women and people with disability needs are often overlooked and are not included ion community decision making processes. Children and elder people aren't also consulted in decision making within their communities and their actual needs are also not considered. girls and women are sometimes faced the risk of sexual exploitation due their vulnerability and lack of protection. Mitigation measures: all ages , genders and people with disability will be included in the project activities; awareness will be made on ending gender , age and disability based discrimination. We will ensure to have a balance male and female representation as well as age groups in all relevant activities. Displaced population in the targeted locations face the threat of forced eviction or live in IDP sites where there is exploitation is too common due to lack of secured land tenure for the IDPs.Land owners or their proxies often taken some of the humanitarian support from the IDPs as part of land rental cost. Armed men from both state and non- state actors have multiple of times sexually assaults women and girls within the IDP sites or outside of the camps thereby create a sense of insecurity and fear for IDP populations. Sexual violence against minority women and girl’s and people with disability is extremely common and most often unreported due to lack of clan protection

Please provide further explanation

This is short-term humanitarian project with the primary objective of strengthening protection of conﬂict-induced IDPs including disable people in three regions of Bay, Bakol and Hiran of Somalia. This project has community on community based child protection system which can contribute in the long run system building for the targeted communities. Child welfare committees will be formed and trained; these communities based structures will be trained and mentored during the project period. it is anticipated that these community structures will be able to continue the running of community based activities such as community awareness on protection issues; the project will ensure to provide adequate gender based violence prevention and response mechanism as well as strengthen clinical case management of rape and enhanced mental health and psychosocial support services by engaging with relevant state and non-state actors in terms of protection of civilian in their communities as well as ensuring that all community incidents are reported and managed through the appropriate channels and principle referrals with services providers is undertaken
