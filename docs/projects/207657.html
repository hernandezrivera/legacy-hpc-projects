Project Summary

UNICEF planned nutrition response aims at scaling up critical lifesaving interventions for treatment of severe forms of wasting and prevention of under nutrition in North West and South West Regions. The planned response is in tandem with the nutrition cluster HRP 2024 strategy and response framework.
UNICEF will support the screening of children under-5 for acute malnutrition and appropriate treatment. UNICEF will advocate and explore adoption of simplified approaches to management of acute malnutrition to improve coverage to care for children suffering from wasting. Family MUAC/Mother MUAC approach will be scaled up to enhance early identification of SAM . Preventative actions that will include promotion of infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E) and Vitamin A supplementation (VAS).
To reinforce partners and the community’s capacity to deliver basic nutrition services to affected communities, UNICEF will support training of additional Trainers of Trainers (ToT) at regional level who will later cascade the training at the health district levels. Furthermore, alternative approaches of capacity development to complement the conventional classroom training will be explored and tested e.g use of short videos, On Job Training. To inform evidence-based programming, the nutrition information system will be strengthened. UNICEF will strengthen the supply chain management with focus on prepositioning of nutrition inputs (RUTF, F75/F100), IEC Materials, drugs and anthropometric equipment at health districts level to ensure continuity of services in the event of movement restrictions. Furthermore, UNICEF will continue strengthening nutrition cluster coordination and enhance multisectoral collaboration with other nutrition sensitive clusters (Health, Food Security, WASH and protection) to maximize optimal nutritional outcomes.
UNICEF nutrition response will apply the principle of “do no harm” and mainstream aspects related to gender, protection centrality, age, gender, disability and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP). Nutrition trainings will incorporate sessions these cross-cutting issues. Interventions will be integrated with capacity strengthening for effective social and behaviour change communication (SBC).
UNICEF will partner with organizations that have a better level of operational capacity, access to and acceptance by populations in need. Intervention will use of static health structures, mobile clinics, rapid response mechanism (RRM) and comprehensive child response (CCR) approaches to access the affected population both in accessible and those in hard to reach remote areas.
The target groups will include boys and girls under-five for screening, referral, children with SAM, Vitamin A supplementation and deworming prophylaxis. Adolescent, pregnant and breastfeeding will be targeted for IYCF-E support. Health workers will be targeted for trainings on SAM management (mobilize, screen and refer acutely malnourished), information Management (DHIS II) and IYCF-E.
8,959 SAM children 6-59 months (4,390 boys and 4,569 girls) admitted for treatment
75,000 (39,000 boys and 36,000 girls) children screened for severe wasting
75,000 people (50% men) sensitized on Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In the North-West, South-West, West and Littoral regions more than 240,400 people including children under five and pregnant and lactating women will need nutrition services. Up to 60,700 boys and girls under 5 years are likely to develop acute malnutrition in these regions including 30,500 with severe acute malnutrition. While the 2023 National Survey on Food Security and Nutrition indicates GAM rates of 2.8 per cent and 3.7 per cent in the North-West and South-West regions respectively, data from routine Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screenings suggests the existence of localized areas with heightened malnutrition risks at lower administrative levels. Populations living in remote areas/bushes, IDPs in urban/peri-urban areas, and host households/communities are at risk of malnutrition due to food insecurity, water scarcity, poor sanitation, and overstretched basic social services. Access to health care remains a major concern particularly for IDPs due to long distances and financial constraints. Furthermore, the weak disease surveillance system has led to delayed detection and weak responses to epidemics, giving rise to repeated cholera and measles outbreaks. According to the Cadre Harmonisé analysis of October 2023, 273,568 people in the North-West (10 per cent) and 234,912 people in the South-West (19 per cent) are facing acute food insecurity (phases 3 to 5). Given the anticipated persistence of key malnutrition drivers—food insecurity, morbidity, poor sanitation, and recurrent internal displacement—the nutrition situation is likely to stagnate or worsen in 2024.The specific response plan for the North-West South-West Nutrition Cluster will focus on increasing access, coverage and use of lifesaving nutrition services and enhance protection of the nutrition status. A comprehensive package of treatment and preventive actions will be delivered through mobile clinics, community platforms and health facilities when feasible. In selecting priority areas, several parameters were considered including expected severe acute malnutrition (SAM) caseloads, severity of food insecurity (based on Cadre Harmonisé analysis) and access to health care and global access (security). A key objective will be to enhance the detection of high-malnutrition hotspots in both regions through comprehensive mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) screenings and targeted rapid evaluations. These efforts will further inform the prioritization of the response, ensuring that interventions effectively reach the most vulnerable and hart-to-reach populations. The Cluster will continue to advocate for the uninterrupted availability and movement of critical nutrition supplies prepositioned at regional and health districts to mitigate stock shortages and pipeline breaks as a result of movement restrictions like roadblocks, lockdowns, etc.
