Project Summary

Based on the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (2023), an estimated 9.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. Child protection needs have significantly increased since 2022, with the situation greatly exacerbated by protracted displacement and limited access to basic services which continues to affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children, adolescents and caregivers. Further, the target locations have limited availability of mental health and psychosocial support services as well as low level of core child protection services including family tracing and reunification, case management, and reintegration services for children affected by armed forces and groups, leaving many children untreated and unable to access the needed services.
This project aims at ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas of Pibor, Uror and Akobo of Jonglei Counties and Kapoeta East of Eastern Equatoria state. GREDO is seeking to provide critical child protection services and assistance to separated, unaccompanied and children associated with armed groups through strengthening existing child protection services such as the existing referral pathway ensuring safe and secure identification, documentation, referral to service providers and follow up of all cases of child protection.
The implementation of this project will involve identification and training of the workforce (social workers, case workers and community mobilizers), establishment of youth and child friendly centers and implementation of structured activities targeting at risk children. This will include community awareness, identification, registration, family tracing and reunification processes and provision of mental health and psychosocial support services both the children, their caregivers and parents (pre and post reunification) among others. Young girls of reproductive age will be provided with Menstrual Hygiene Management training and hygiene kits.
GREDO will establish a Children Based Protection network in each county and train them on CPiE, protection awareness and messaging, preparedness and Child Protection Risk Protection mitigation. On integration, GREDO will work with nutrition partners on Child protection messaging targeting women. Children with protection and malnutrition concerns and malnourished will be referred to nutrition service providers for appropriate care. Education partners will be engaged in enrolment of at risk and out of school boys and girls. Injured children or those suffering from extreme trauma will be referred to health providers for specialized care. A complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will be activated on contact with target communities where GREDO will provide sufficient information to affected persons on how to submit complaints and receive feedback.

1. Needs

The deterioration in the humanitarian situation has stripped people of their physical and mental well-being, dignified living standards and coping mechanisms. According to HNO 2023, it is anticipated that protection needs are likely to increase, exacerbating protection risks and vulnerabilities of especially women and children. This coupled up with repeated shocks have weakened community-based structures and services. Inadequate access to services will continue to push families to adopt negative coping mechanisms, exposing the most vulnerable, particularly children and women, to protection risks, such as violence, exploitation, abuse, forced recruitment, trafficking, child labour, and early forced marriage. Protection concerns remain high as civilians continue to be displaced and killed, their houses and properties destroyed, and access to livelihoods and essential services disrupted due to violence. Gender Analysis and Safety Audit conducted by ACF in March 2022 shows that persistent gender inequality for women and girls in South Sudan hampers their ability to access social services. GBV remains a critical threat, particularly for women and girls who are exposed to risks when carrying out routine activities as simple as collecting water and firewood. South Sudan children and adolescents continue to face a multitude of risks, including abduction, GBV and other forms of violence, recruitment into armed groups and separation from families. An estimated 2.9 million children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services in 2024. Kapoeta East, Pibor, Uror and Akobo Counties experience frequent cattle raiding that is usually coupled with child and women abduction. For instance, on 22 September, unknown criminals entered Morwale village in Kapoeta East, stabbed a mother, and grabbed her infant son. In Pibor, UNICEF recently reported abduction of 65 people (16 women and 49 children). On 4th of October, three women were killed, and seven children abducted in Akobo and finally in Uror, in January 2023, 1 person was reported killed and 7 children abducted. These are just a few examples of the continued outbreaks of violence and how children have been used as an art of war, weakening the protective environment for children and significantly impairing the capacity of families to appropriately care for children, putting them at risk of violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation. The 2022 FSNMS+ qualitative findings indicate the presence of mental health and psychosocial concerns within communities and identify an urgent need for MHPSS and life-skills education. Other factors driving protection concerns among children include: lack of alternative pathways particularly for adolescent girls due to limited education, education related expenses making it difficult for IDPs and returnees to retain children in school, patriarchal gender norms and cultural practices that restrict girls options, the practice of bride price, restriction to girls mobility and participation in activities outside the home, weak community protection structures and poverty and limited options for conflict affected familie. GREDO therefore aims to aims to prevent and respond to immediate life saving child protection services to vulnerable children and their families in Pibor, Uror, Akobo and Kapoeta East counties.

2. Project activities

GREDO will work towards ensuring that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, advancing centrality of protection among humanitarian actors to reduce protection risks, and addressing coordination gaps/issues through close dialogue with partners and effective protection monitoring to inform programming, advocacy, and response. GREDO will conduct 717 Key Informant Interviews (KII), at payam level, covered by the Protection Monitoring System. Information derived from these KIIs will help guide and inform programming, advocacy and response in the target counties. GREDO will also ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. To achieve this, GREDO will 1. Undertake family tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children, including case management, referrals, interim and foster care services. This will target 314 UASC. 2. Provide community-based mental health and psychosocial support targeting 8,732 children, adolescents and caregivers. GREDO will interweave these activities with the ongoing CPiE and youth and Adolescents programs to ensure maximum and all-round support to the target groups.3. Conduct awareness sessions targeting 267 children and caregivers. This will also include sessions on positive parenting. 4. Awareness raising among men, women, girls and boys with protection concerns or specific needs. this will target 14,510 men, women, boys and girls. In order to promote a nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities, GREDO will undertake the following activities:1. Updating of four referral pathways (1 in each location)2. Training of 384 women and men, including community leaders or humanitarian and government staff on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE)3. Establishment or enhancement of 7 community-based protection structures. 4. Implementation of community-based protection activities including resilience, PSS activities, PSEA, peace-building activities and trainings through AGD approaches.
