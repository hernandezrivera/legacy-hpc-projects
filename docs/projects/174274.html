Project Summary

NRC’s shelter project objective is to adopt a participatory-based approach to improve the living conditions, dignity, and physical protection of the most vulnerable people affected by conflict in targeted areas of Northeast of Nigeria. In response to the protracted nature of the crisis, NRC will increasingly work in an integrated manner, bringing in Shelter and any other relevant competencies to collaborate on providing more durable and holistic solutions to displacement and return. Considering the complexities of displacement and return in both urban and rural areas, NRC will adopt a flexible approach to target specific needs with adaptable shelter options and incorporate safe programming principles in order to mitigate and respond to any potential adverse effects, which might result from the proposed shelter solution. The following include the overall area for the focus of the project:
1. Emergency Response: Responding to the urgent shelter/NFI needs of IDP and returnee population occurring due to unforeseen reasons such as security incidents, communal clashes, natural disasters (flooding, fire, etc) or premature return or resettlement;
2. Protracted Displacement Response: Improving the shelter conditions in camps and camp-like sites by decongestion of camps and improving the emergency/transitional shelters habitability conditions;
3. Return Context Response: Contribution and facilitating safe and dignified return of IDPs by provision of adequate shelter conditions and exploration of diverse shelter modalities in host communities and areas of return.
Based on its mandate, NRC will target the people directly impacted by the conflict, especially the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host-communities living in inadequate dwellings. Based on humanitarian analysis and internal assessment results, NRC in collaboration with the shelter sector will select areas of intervention using defined specific selection criteria, which will consider physical access and security in addition to the presence of IDPs in the area, and the areas offering opportunity for safe and voluntary return. NRC will target and select beneficiaries using a community-based approach, to promote the commitment of leaders and involvement of the host community and target groups. The target group will be defined and identified as follows:
 Most vulnerable IDPs: NRC seeks to target 6,000 IDPs households (HHs) divided into three following LGAs that NRC has currently a presence in: Maiduguri/Jere, Dikwa, Damasak, Monguno, Gwoza/Pulka, Mubi & Yola.
1. Newly displaced needing emergency protection against the elements and those living in makeshift shelters;
2. IDPs who have been protractedly displaced and have no prospect for a clear durable solution, safe and voluntary return to areas of origin or local integration.
3. IDPs choosing integration in displacement areas and identified in coordination with local authorities or relevant sectorial mechanisms. Amongst this group will be those who have consented for integration or delocalization.
 Most vulnerable returnees: 3,500 HHs will be targeted amongst those already returned in their area of origin but living either with a host family, in destroyed houses, or in other alternative solutions. NRC will also target those who are newly returning facing secondary displacement and in need of emergency shelter/NFI assistance. LGAs included for shelter assistance to returnees are Maiduguri/Jere, Dikwa, Damasak, Monguno, Gwoza/Pulka, Mubi & Yola.
 Host communities: 500 vulnerable HHs that regularly host IDP families and that provide preliminary assistance by sharing their living space, food, and access to their lands. Maiduguri/Jere, Dikwa, Damasak, Monguno, Gwoza/Pulka, Mubi & Yola LGAs with high urban concentration of IDPs and returnees are selected for shelter assistance to the vulnerable host community.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in North-East Nigeria has left 7.9 million people in need of life-saving aid (800,000 more people than in 2020 than 2019). More than 2.1 million people are displaced in 2,388 locations across the six most affected states in the region with 78% of the IDP population being women and children. The states with highest number of IDPs are Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. 43% of IDPs live in 300 camp and camp-like settlements, with 234 out of 300 sites being in the worse affected state of Borno. The remaining 57% of IDP population (1,211,116 individuals) are residing with host communities, making it harder to access them with assistance and putting additional pressure on the already stretched resources of these communities. There are also an estimated 1,714,682 returnees in three states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (with the majority or 819,148 in Adamawa). The number of returnees is estimated to continue to increase specially with the announcement of the Borno State Government having the intention to fast track the return process and its imminent plans to return more IDPs to their places of origins. However, a number returnees (20,000 in 2019) still continue to move back to camps or camp-like sites due to insecurity and lack of services in some of these areas of return. In addition, emergency movements and new displacements continue to happen due to security related incidents, communal clashes or natural disasters. Up to 642,320 IDPs, returnees and host communities are without adequate shelters and have resorted to negative coping mechanism such as using makeshift shelters, sharing accommodations with relatives and friends, living in partially damaged shelters, in public buildings and collective centres. The living conditions in the IDPs sites are very poor, with one in two camps in Borno state being overcrowded. Land allocation for new IDP camps or the expansion of existing ones remain extremely low. The informal and spontaneously settled camps are also severely congested and underserved due to lack of space and resources as well as complications relating to private land ownership. Overcrowding poses several serious threats, the main one being the increase of risk of infections and outbreaks such as of the COVID-19. It also continues to exacerbate risks and impact of fire outbreaks. In April 2020, fire outbreaks affected over 1,200 household in the International School camp on Ngala LGA, causing the loss of lives and tension in the IDP community in the camp. Overcrowding is also a particular protection concern for women and girls, and negatively affects the elderly and people with disabilities. In addition, natural disasters such as floods and strong winds make the replacement or repair of emergency or makeshift shelters an ongoing need. In terms of shelter typologies, there are a variety of shelter conditions in camps and camp-like settings, with the most common type of shelter being makeshift shelter at 37%, followed by emergency shelter at 35%. In the host communities, 60% of IDPs live in a host family’s house, 25% live in rented houses and 10% in individual houses. For returnees, 77% resided in household with walls, 18% resided in traditional shelters and 5% in emergency/makeshift shelters. However, even for those having a shelter, conditions are not always adequate. A household level survey was done by NRC from March to June with 2575 heads of household (IDPS, returnees, host community and refugees) in 12 LGAs (not including MDG and Jere). The data collected indicated that only 19% thought their shelter provided adequate space and privacy for their families; only 18% agreed that their shelters provided adequate personal security and security of their belonging; and only 17% concluded that their shelter provided good protection against the elements (cold, heat, wind, flooding, etc.) and other hazards (fire, etc).

2 - Activities

Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs): NRC will distribute Emergency Shelter Kits to support beneficiaries in building basic emergency shelters using traditional construction techniques. The ESKs will be provided as necessary as identified by the RRM assessment. It is a flexible approach, fast, simple to implement, and cost effective. It is used to construct temporary shelters or to repair or upgrade existing shelters in order to address the needs of newly arrived IDPs or affected people living in dilapidated tents or partially covered spaces and makeshift shelters either in transit centers or in IDP sites. Temporary Shelter Construction (BAMA Shelters): NRC shelter construction preference for 2021 is transitional shelter (Dikwa shelters). However NRC will still build a number of Bama shelters in 2021 as contribution to sector’s decongestion plans and prototyping of sectors’ staged shelter concept and in emergency context where construction of shelters are necessary. Transitional Shelter Construction (Dikwa Shleters): In new displacement areas where land is accessible and tenure secured enough and also where IDPs live in makeshift shelters or their existing shelters have been destroyed (more than 70% damaged) NRC will provide complete transitional shelter solutions. The design and composition of Dikwa Transitional Shelter model is in line with sphere standard and approved by Shelter sector and provides a higher level of privacy and protection against elements and other hazards. Temporary/Transitional Shelter Repairs: For those protracted displacement areas, where communities have lived in temporary or transitional shelters for several years, without an opportunity or permission to build more permanent structures, NRC will provide repair kits.For repair, in-kind or voucher modalities might be used to allow more flexibility to shelter owners to prioritize some of the upgrading components. Otherwise, NRC will provide in-kind building materials and pay for labour fees to support the construction of the repair process. Permanent Shelter Rehabilitation: For returnees living either in damaged/abandoned building, their own property or a rented house, NRC will provide shelter rehabilitation assistance. In the cases where returnees are paying rent, Legal agreement between Landlord, NRC and the HH will be developed by ICLA prior to the intervention. Free occupation or rent freeze is negotiated for a period of 6 months or 1 year. The rehabilitation includes doors and windows installation, roof and wall repairs. The activity will be coordinated with ICLA/HLP team to maximize programming effectiveness. Permanent Shelter Construction: NRC shelter team will design and develop a complete permanent shelter solution (mud shelters) in 2021 in response to the needs of return context and also government shift in use of more durable construction materials even in displacement context. The design and composition of the permanent shelter solution will be done in consultation with community’s representative and in line with Sphere standards with specific attention to local standards, in order to maintain cultural harmony and easy maintenance. Shelter design will look at maximizing local knowledge and construction techniques for producing and using mud blocks. Design and prototype of the permanent shelter solution will be presented to the community, local authority representatives and shelter sector partners for technical feedback and approval. Improved NFI kit Distribution: NRC will ensure that all targeted shelter beneficiaries in all three above categories, would also receive adequate household items during the shelter handover process. When applied, NFI kits will be distributed to all targeted shelter beneficiaries through direct distribution and/or through an electronic-voucher system according to market capacity. NRC will ensure that NFI kits follow Shelter/NFI/CCCM guidelines and are adapted to the cultural context.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In all the shelter activities, NRC will look into mainstreaming gender and protection principles. This would be achieved through different mechanisms and approaches. NRC will ensure the participation of all beneficiaries in the decision-making process by establishing a committee at the outside of any shelter activity in every community. The committee is comprised of various members of community, representative of both genders and different age groups. In addition, it will be ensured that minority groups and populations with disabilities are also represented. Specific sensitization conducted by NRC staff and community mobilizers will facilitate social mobilization and strengthen the capacities of communities to participate and take ownership of activities. The committee will also assist NRC team in identifying vulnerable households and assess the capacity level of the community. NRC Shelter’s ultimate goal is that beneficiaries become self-sufficient and equipped to repair community infrastructure, their homes, or pursue livelihood activities. On-the-job and workshop trainings provided will allow for improvement of capacity and skills development within a short period-of-time. NRC construction activities, along with IGAs developed in the form of cash-for-work, will be provided to youth as a way to reduce their exposure of being recruited into armed conflict for militant activities due to lack of economic opportunities. NRC will also attempt to include whenever possible vulnerable female headed household in need of livelihood opportunities in its construction-related capacity building training and cash-for-work opportunities. In addition, NRC shelter team will ensure to seek the feedback of various community members in form of focus group discussions in the process of design and improvement of shelter modalities. Specific attention needs to be made to ensure the needs of such highly vulnerable population are responded to by following gender and protection mainstreaming principles. This could include provision of internal partitions with lockable doors and windows in shelters whenever possible. In cases where emergency shelters kits are distributed, technical and labor assistance should be provided to highly vulnerable population who might not have the physical capacity or social network to construct their shelter themselves. Paying special attention to providing transportation, labor and technical assistance to highly vulnerable households such as female-headed households, child-headed households, elderly and people with disability. Inclusion of female beneficiaries in construction-related capacity building activities. Focus group discussion with different age and gender groups in order to mainstream gender and protection principles in emergency and transitional shelters design and improvements. Providing internal partition in emergency and transitional shelters and locks for all doors and windows. Alongside all shelter and settlement activities, awareness-raising and trainings will be provided to maximize the project output and strengthen this intervention. Specific training and awareness sensitization will focus on habitability measures and environment to promote healthier living surroundings. Following the training, communities will easily integrate proper designated places for animals and people, respectively and will ensure safe healthy kitchen spaces is available.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC is committed to setting up systematic and effective mechanisms to collect beneficiary and non-beneficiary feedback, and to provide on-going feedback to NRC during its projects. NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standards definition of Accountability and understands Accountability to Affected Populations as a process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. As with other NRC projects, a Complaint and Feedback Response mechanism (CFRM) integrated in this project will ensure that beneficiaries and communities have the opportunity to register feedback and complaints. This mechanism includes the provision of a complaints/suggestion boxes at project sites, and dedicated complaint and feedback hotline, email, and face to face sessions with staff. NRC uses this feedback to resolve any issues that may arise, and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. This CFRM will be managed by a dedicated team, and report directly to senior management to provide additional accountability towards targeted communities and ensure that complaints are discussed on a senior level within the organization where appropriate. Furthermore, populations served by NRC staff through this project will be informed on NRC’s Code of Conduct and how to report any breaches of conduct. Post distribution monitoring will also gain feedback from the target population.
