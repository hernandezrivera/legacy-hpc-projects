Project Summary

Multi-sectorial emergency response in Eastern Sudan (Kassala/Gedaref/Red Sea) and North Darfur with regard to WASH, FSL, Protection, NFI-Shelter assistance. This project also includes Covid-19 reponse activities in all locations and protection activities focus on sensitization and awareness raising of key protection issues such as GBV. This project mainly targets IDPs and vulnerable host communities members. In North Darfur, the project interlinks with a planned BMZ project to operationalize the Triple NEXUS.

1 - Needs

With this project, WHH will respond to the needs of IDPs and vulnerable host communities in different sectors across 4 states of Sudan. North Darfur: Localities Al Lait and Kebkabiya In comparison to other localities within North Darfur, both Al Lait and Kebkabiya localities are remotely located. Even though the two localities host high numbers of displaced population, a lot of unmet humanitarian needs still exist in the two localities due to limited funding opportunities. Despite the efforts already put by various partners to support the local population, there is still a huge gap in access to drinking water, shelter and NFI as well as agricultural inputs that can contribute to saving lives of the IDPs and vulnerable host community members. Existence of both the South Sudanese refugees and the IDPs within their host communities is exerting pressure on the existing limited resources such as water points due to competition between the displaced population and the host community members over access to drinking water. The land for crop cultivation is fertile both in Al Lait and Kebkabiya localities therefore there is need to support the target group with agricultural inputs for short-term production. According to the IPC classification, the target localities are projected to be in level 2 (stressed) from February to May 2021. Kassala: Localities Wad El Helew and Gedaref: Localities El Fashaga, Galabat Ash-Shargai (only for protection) The living conditions of the host communities are equally dire as the majority of refugee camps are located in areas of Kassala and Gedaref with IPC Level 2 (stressed) or 3 (crisis) acute food insecurity (FEWSNET Sudan Food Security Outlook Feb-Oct 2020). The aforementioned conditions make the target group particularly vulnerable to diseases, especially in view of the current COVID-19 pandemic in relation to which on 16th March 2020 the Sudanese government declared a national health emergency. Across both states, vulnerable host communities suffer as well from insufficient access to safe drinking water and inadequate hygiene practices. Further, host community members are equally affected by natural disasters such as the recurring floods experienced in the states, which make them in need of emergency Shelter and NFI support. In addition, there is a need for support for short-term food production and food use and processing (preservation) to help meet frequent supply shortages. There is little knowledge within the target population on about protection needs and topics such as GBV and Child Protection. Red Sea State, localities: Tokar, Agig, SinkatWHH is one of the few humanitarian agencies present in Red Sea State. The state is regularly hit by natural disasters such as the devastating floods in 2020 which affected most of Tokar locality. There is a need to improve preparedness and disaster response to mitigate the negative effects, especially for the most vulnerable target group in the rural and remote localities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will address the needs of IDPs and vulnerable host communities through a multi-sector response. In the area of WASH the project aims to improve the access to safe and sufficient water supply. Hygiene and COVID-19 awareness-raising is part of this component. In the area of FSL, the project aims to provide timely food support and enhance the self-reliance of the target group by providing training in agriculture and livelihood activities. In the area of SNFI, the project aims to provide vulnerable host communities and IDPs with essential SNFI support. The response also includes prepositioning of key SNFI items, market assessments as well as sensitization and training for the target group on mitigating the impact of natural disaster to improve preparedness. For this component, all localities where WHH is operational are targeted. IDPs and host communities alike are targeted for protection sensitization which is very relevant to the context since their localities host refugee populations who feature increased protection vulnerability. The activities focus on sensitization sessions (involving a limited number of people i.e. less than 50) in a set-up such as FGDs and/or short workshops and will focus on information/sensitization about the concept of GBV as well as related services and referral pathways, GBV and health, domestic violence and child marriage. Under the area of energy, the target population´s access to clean sustainable energy sources (fuel-efficient stoves) is improved. The project will be carried out in line with WHH MEAL standards and respective activities such as base/end-line, continuous monitoring and a final evaluation. Specific selection criteria will make sure that most vulnerable groups are addressed taking vulnerability criteria such as age, gender and disability into account.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The intervention area is characterized by resource pressure and degradation of natural resources. Water is a key natural resource which is scarce in all the targeted areas. The project aims to enlarge access to save drinking water through rehabilitation/construction of water sources and reduce the potential for deforestation through promotion of fuel efficient stoves among the target groups.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project mainstreams protection through protection sensitization of all target groups, including awareness of GBV and Child Protection issues. WHH coordinates with other actors on the ground to provide assistance in areas where WHH cannot support and participates regularly in protection meetings and working groups. WHH protection mainstreaming follows the 4 main principles: 1) Do no harm, 2) ensure impartial access to assistance, 3) Pprotect people from violence and 4) assist with rights claims, access to remedies and recovery from abuse.
