Project Summary

In line with the Protection cluster strategy for 2024, Acted will implement a protection response for crisis-affected populations in border regions and areas of concerns across South Sudan. Through this project, Acted will contribute to create a safer and protective environment by providing accessible and quality protection services to host communities, vulnerable residents, internally displaced individuals (IDPs), and returnees in the states of Upper Nile, Central and Eastern Equatoria as well as places with prevalent urgent protection needs. Upon consultation of with the Protection Cluster, Acted will dispatch its Emergency Response Unit to places with emergency protection needs such as Akobo, Fangak ans other locations affected by crises. Acted and its national partner HDC will target up to 160,000 beneficiaries, contributing to meeting the needs of over 5.5 million people who face GBV-, housing and land property-related and general protection risks in South Sudan. Acted and its national partner, HDC, will adopt a community-based approach to reinforce resilience and self-reliance among concerned communities to contribute to the eradication of protection risks and enhance the sustainability of the positive outcomes of the action.

1. Needs

According to the protections for 2024, 5.5 million South Sudanese are in need of Protection assistance, among who 53% children, 50.4% women, and 15% of people with disabilities due to persistent conflicts and insecurity. Among the women and children in need, 83% women have survived SGBV, while 80% children are at-risk. In addition, more than a third (33%) South Sudanese are internally displaced (IDPs), fleeing to a safer place because of armed conflicts, inter-communal clashes, mass human rights violation or natural disasters. As underlined by the HNO 2023, the local and national governments lack the necessary means to protect the populations and the traditional community leaders are left as the first providers with minimal protection capacities. This situation greatly affects the severity of the protection needs in the whole country with 71% of the protection needs are rated as catastrophic, namely the highest level on severity scale. In the zones of intervention under this, namely Eastern Equatoria and Upper Nile, the situation does not differ from the rest the country with general protection needs being recognised as catastrophic. GBV protection needs are also rated as catastrophic in both states with pockets of severe and extreme needs. In addition, House and Land Property (HLP) risks concern two million of people across South Sudan. The majority of people in need are IDPs and returnees with respectively 507,000 and 401,000 individuals according to the HNO 2023. In Central Equatoria and particularly Juba county, HLP needs are critical with cases of secondary occupation and theft of property which dissuades returnees and IDPs to go back to their location of origins. As for the social protection system, there are weak land governance structures in South Sudan which are characterized by low capacity of the land administration services and lack of awareness on land legislations. This mainly leaves the disputes over lands and properties unresolved with no remedy means. On top of the fragile national context, the regional situation with the conflict in the neighbouring State of Sudan is contributing to the exacerbation of protection needs in South Sudan. In September 2023, OCHA reported that individuals fleeing Sudan experienced instances of violence and GBV on the way to South Sudan which is the third hosting state to displaced populations from Sudan. Since April 15th, more than 369,500 individuals have crossed to South Sudan, among who 50% of women, according to UNHCR and IOM. As per the data collected by UNHCR and IOM, 83% are South Sudanese returnees, 16% are Sudanese and 1% are from third countries. Upon their arrivals, returnees find themselves in a dire situation with little to no services of protection available. The situation is particularly dire in the northern state of Upper Nile where the majority of people fleeing Sudan (84%) have crossed to South Sudan through the the Joda/Renk border entry point. As per a rapid needs assessment done by Acted and its national partner HDC, individuals who arrived in Renk, Maban and Melut all report the need for protection services due to symptoms of trauma, depression and stress. While new arrivals have access to psychosocial support and case management in Renk and Maban, those who are in Melut do not have access to any services. According to the same needs assessment, there are no safe spaces dedicated to women, girls and children which is critical to the prevention of violence perpetrated against them. Acted and HDC also found that the host communities in these three counties do not receive any protection assistance, even though needs have been reported among them. As such, the Protection Cluster estimates that more than 5.5 million returnees, IDPs, vulnerable residents, host communities in South Sudan will be in of protection assistance in 2024.

2. Project activities

In line with the Protection cluster strategy for 2024, Acted will enhance the provision of protection services through their integration at border points in Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria as well as in Central Equatoria and locations with emergency needs identified by the Acted mobile team. Acted will partner with HDC to reach host communities, vulnerable residents, IDPs, and returnees in these areas where there is an assistance gap in HLP, general, and GBV protection. Acted will focus on HLP needs in Central Equatoria and other areas of concern. Acted and its local partner HDC will provide HLP protection via help desks to IDPs residing in camps in Bor and Juba countries, with the aim to enforce and promote ownership as well as property rights. Through these helpdesks, IDPs will be able to access legal assistance and dispute settlement mechanisms regarding land attribution. Acted will also provide extra assistance to targeted vulnerable households whose HLP needs are particularly high. Through cash and voucher assistance, Acted will contribute to assisting these vulnerable households in meeting their shelter needs by securing land and property. In Central Equatoria, Acted and HDC will aim to strengthen HLP mechanisms, hence contributing to establishing an HLP protective environment. Acted and HDC will train administration officials for more effective implementation of HLP legislations at Boma, Payam and state levels. Acted and HDC will also raise awareness among IDPs on their rights linked to HLP in Juba camps and peri-IDP sites. On top of its HLP actions, Acted will contribute to provide general and GBV protection services to people living in border areas and in locations with emergency needs. Through protection scoping assessments (safety audits and focused group discussions), Acted will identify the protection risks with the concerned communities. Acted will share the conclusions with the Protection Cluster and other relevant protection actors to enhance a protective environment though greater humanitarian coordination. In locations with emergency needs, Upper Nile, Central and Eastern Equatoria, awareness-raising workshops will be led by Acted mobile and static teams with HDC on protection-relate topics such as PSEA, GBV, early child and forced marriages, safety of children, women and girls. The sensitisation sessions will be used to mitigate protection risks, particularly alarming among IDPs and returnees impacted by the Sudan conflicts and other armed clashes. Acted will also form and strengthen community-based protection mechanisms (CBPMs) and networks of Protection Focal Points in locations of high needs and in Eastern Equatoria. The CBPMs and Protection Focal Points will provide the primary protection assistance and referral to people in need. At border points in Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria where protection needs are exacerbated, Acted will provide individualised case management to at-risk men and boys as well as women and girls, survivors of GBV. Case management will include first psychological aid and psychosocial support. Vulnerable women and girls in reproductive age will receive dignity kits as part of the case management service. In Upper Nile where there are no safe spaces, Acted will work to establish these centres to facilitate accessibility of protection services in one centralised place for women, girls and children who have crossed to South Sudan from Sudan. In Eastern Equatoria where community centres exist, Acted will train CBPMs to run these structures and provide awareness-raising sessions as well as case management to assist at-risk individuals. Lastly, Acted will aim to strengthen the protection system by training national partners along case workers to assist in a timely manner at-risk children and GBV survivors among IDPs, returnees and host communities.
