Project Summary

This project aim is to improve equitable access to quality life-saving curative and preventive services through timely provision of Nutrition and Health and protection activities through existing health and nutrition system in Bau locality in Blue Nile State among vulnerable male and female, children under-five, pregnant and lactating women (PLW); and to provide emergency assistance to the people affected by the current ongoing conflict in Sudan - in particular in Jebel Awlia Locality in Khartoum State.
The project will reach 59,507 beneficiaries in Blue Nile. The key project interventions and activities are integrated primary health care services that include health system support, communicable diseases, child health, sexual and reproductive health, prevention, identification, and treatment of malnutrition through implementation targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) for severe acute malnutrition (SAM), maternal, infant and young-child feeding in emergency (MIYCF-E) and referrals for SAM with complications.
As part of building resilience, the project will provide IYCF/E counselling for caretakers of children under-five, provide food-based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) for children under two years, provide food-based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) for pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), micronutrient supplementation (Vitamin A for under-fives and MNP), provide iron and folic acid supplementation for pregnant women, conduct screening (MUAC/Oedema) of all children 6-59 months and refer to appropriate CMAM sites for OTPs/TSFPs or SCs. Conduct Growth Monitoring to serve as an early detection of deterioration of nutrition situation in children under 5 years, distribute IEC materials for IYCF and nutrition education and maternal and child health (MCH) during anti-natal care, in maternity wards, delivery room, post-natal care, and adolescent as one package of services that will contribute to the surveillance system. Treatment of childhood illnesses (IMCI) will be integrated with CMAM, referral of under-five malnourished children, PLW with breastfeeding difficulties producing milk or children with disabilities or children with difficulties in suckling and swallowing to the nutrition sites/specialist. GBV and PSEA will be mainstreamed during training of nutrition staff, that will include a mandatory signing of code of conduct, provision of key messages on GBV, establishment of feedback and complaints mechanisms.
To respond to the current conflict that began on 15 April 2023, Medair plans to set up three mobile health teams to provide basic healthcare assistance to the affect population in Jebel Awlia Locality in Khartoum. The response aims to contribute to the reduction of excess mortality and morbidity among the vulnerable population in Khartoum amidst the ongoing conflict. Through the response, Medair aims to improve the access to healthcare services of the people in need and reduce the burden of the open hospitals which are overwhelmed. Medair will aim to provide services to 20,000 beneficiaries during this period through the mobile health teams.

1 - Needs

BNS has some of the highest health needs in Sudan: Damazine, Ar Rusayris, and Wad Al Mahi face extremehealth needs, while Geissan has severe health needs (2023 HRP). Medair’s MSNRA indicates that the majority of the assessed displaced and host communities do not have access to adequate basic free health and nutrition services. In Ar Rusayris and certain areas of Damazine, existing health facilities do not have sufficient stock of essential medicines or medical supplies. The main referral point for emergency cases, including obstetric emergencies and children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with complications, isDamazine town hospital; however this hospital is inaccessible to some IDP communities due to the ongoing intercommunal tensions, so referral of emergency cases is not possible for these communities. Access is further limited during the rainy season, particularly in hard-to-reach locations such as Madina 9, Shammar, and Deiwa (Al Rusayris) communities. In Wadi Al Mahi, intercommunal violence has resulted in the displacement of health workers and a shortage in essential medical and nutritional supplies, which has severely affected host communities. Only six of the 13 assessed health facilities have external support. The estimated health worker ration (based on MSRNA findings) in Wadi Al Mahi and Ar Rusayris is 0.1 per 1,000 people, which is vastly below the nationwide ratio of 0.76 and the WHO global target of 4.45. The main morbidities reported during the MSRNA in all locations for adults and CU5 were respiratory infections, diarrhoea, and malaria, which is consistent with the most recent country-wide annual health report, whichsuggests that these same illnesses were the top three morbidities treated in BNS in 2020 (MoH, 2020). Only one assessed facility provided treatment in line with integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI) protocols, and four reported having measles vaccines available. The majority of the facilities had a Clinic Utilisation Rate of < 1. While most of these health facilities had no outbreak diseases reported in the assessed areas in the past three months (aside from Madina 10, which had suspected cases of Acute Jaundice), the Early Warning, Alert and Response Network (EWARN) system is not well-functioning across BNS, as there is a lack of sufficient capacity and knowledge of outbreak surveillance and alert investigation. There is no recent maternal mortality rate for BNS, however the majority of women deliver at home and do not seek health services for complications. Emergency obstetric and newborn care and ANC coverage is low, with only two assessed facilities providing ANC services and three facilities providing normal delivery services. There are no clinics providing basic emergency obstetric and newborn care. None of the assessed clinics reported incidences of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), however this is more likely to be a consequence of a lack of sufficient SGBV awareness. None of the assessed facilities are providing MHPSS services.For the current conflict in Sudan, it was reported that many hospitals located at the center of Khartoum and other cities have become inoperational because they had been bombed, attacked or used as military bases (Rapid Assessment Report published by Sudanese Development Call Organization (NIDAA), 22 May 2023). Some hospitals have no access to water and electricity, and most are lacking in medicines and medical supplies. A number of medical personnel lost their lives while providing services to the population. In Khartoum, many people have no access to any functioning healthcare services, and they have to seek help from medical personnel at their houses, said the NIDAA assessment report. There are also concerns that the conflict will exacerbate the issues of food insecurity and acute malnutrition, which were already worrying before the start of the fighting.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Following the gaps in health and nutrition across the states as mentioned above, Medair is positioning itself strategically to respond to the humanitarian emergency in a systematic manner based on its vast experience in health and nutrition with focus on integrated primary health care services that includes health system support, communicable diseases, child health, sexual and reproductive health, prevention, identification and treatment of malnutrition through implementation TSFP for MAM, OTP for SAM, MIYCF-E and referrals for SAM with complications. The key health and nutrition project activities are supporting the PHCCs with medical supplies, training of health care providers on integrated management of new-born and childhood illnesses (IMNCI), expanded program on immunization (EPI), clinical management of rape (CMR), family planning (FP), Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC), mental health and pharmaceutical logistics; treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications and referrals for SAM with medical complications for children 6-59 months, treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) among under-fives children, treatment of acute malnutrition among PLW, provide MIYCF counselling for care takers with children under-fives. Other services include provision of food-based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) for children under two years, provide food-based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) for PLWs, micronutrient supplementation (Vitamin A for under-fives and MNP), provide iron and folic acid supplementation for pregnant women, conduct screening (MUAC/Oedema) of all children under five (6-59 months) and refer to appropriate CMAM sites OTPs/TSFPs or SCs. To distribute IEC materials on IYCF and maternal and child health (MCH) during anti-natal care, maternity wards, delivery, post-natal care, and to adolescent as one package of services that support the surveillance system. CMAM services will be integrated into IMNCI and EPI activities to identify and refer children at risk of under-nutrition under-five children.Additionally, Medair will mainstream mental health and psychosocial support, protection and assessment into its programming that will support innovative approach to evidence-based programming and improve quality of care through partnership with ADRA for the provision of WASH and Protection. A functional and efficient monitoring and evaluation system will be set up throughout the project cycle management Behaviour Change Communication (BCC) will be included to improve the practice of key health, nutrition, and hygiene behaviour in the community. AAP system will be established for feedback and complaints mechanisms.To build resilience, Medair will integrate the following activities into its projects as much as possible: conduct counselling of Pregnant women and caregivers of children 0-23 months on MYCN, provide Vitamin A supplementation per child (at least two doses), iron and folic acid to pregnant women during ante-natal, engage mothers of under-fives children to conduct MUAC screening of there children, growth monitoring and promotion at the health facility level for children 0-23 months, deworming of under-fives years children, dissemination of IYCF/E messaging, setting up of MtMSG etc. To respond to the needs caused by the current conlict, Medair will set up three mobile teams in Khartoum and each team will be comprised of a medical officer, a midwife, a vaccinator, and a registrar. The teams, under the supervision of a Medair Health Project Manager, will provide reproductive health services for pregnant and lactating women, integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), potentially vaccination services, conduct nutrition screening and provide basic messages on health, IYCF and hygiene. The teams will refer patients to functioning hospitals and/or other partners as needed for follow up, and in a way that does not put the patients at risk.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Specific considerations will be made at all stages of the project (assessment, planning, implementation, and monitoring registration, verification, distribution and post distribution monitoring) to ensure fair, safe, and equal and meaningful access for all. This will ensure include various individual groups; household engagements of men, women, child headed households; PLWs; PLWDs; and the elderly within the consultation of needs, planning and follow up. Considerations will be made, in coordination with community members and relevant organizations to ensure fair, safe, and equal and meaningful access to registration and distribution activities. This will include risk assessments to establish mitigating measures such as timing, location, layout, function, and support given at the distribution and or service locations. Medair will mainstream protection in all its activities throughout this project that will improve a meaningful access to PLWDs, Women and Girls, Children and all age group. Medair will include protection mainstreaming key topics in its behaviour change communication (BCC) activities that will enhance knowledge of the beneficiaries and improve access.MEDAIR will raise the awareness of services providers, community health workers’ by building their capacity on AAP, GBV and disability and Training of nutrition staff on GBV and PSEA including mandatory signing of code of conduct, provision of key messages on GBV services availability.Medair will partner with other relevant stakeholders to ensure all beneficiaries have access to adequate and quality services.Considering the current fluid and volatile situation in Sudan, Medair will uphold “do no harm” principle in the planning and implementation of the emergency response in Khartoum. Medair will ensure that the response will not cause any further harm to the population, so the response approach and modalities have been carefully considered. Medair will take guidance from the community when determining mobile service locations and all due care will be taken when making referrals to hospitals in order to address safety concerns.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Medair is an active agency in Health and Nutrition Cluster and has a good relationship with UN agencies such as UNICEF, UNFPA, WFP, WHO and others as well as national and international organizations, MoH and HAC at both federal and state level. Medair will work in coordination with inter-agency structures including accountability and feedback measures. This will include reporting cases of, or suspected cases of SEA using the established reporting mechanism. Medair will further participate in and ensure training of all staff members in PSEA, implementing appropriate policies and reporting mechanisms and provide clear information to beneficiaries relating to the identification of SEA and reporting options available to them both internal and external. All Medair staff receive training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, and relocatable staff are trained in protection-sensitive programming as part of Medair’s standard briefing procedures. Medair liaises with existing protection partners in allocations to identify and refer cases requiring specialized protection support.
