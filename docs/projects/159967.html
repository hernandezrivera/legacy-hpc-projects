Project Summary

Narrative to explain the need of the project
The purpose of this project is to prevent and treat acute malnutrition in children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers through implementation of community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) activities in Rumbek Center and Pibor counties.
This project will focus on the management of children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition and pregnant and lactating mothers with acute malnutrition and will be implemented using the national CMAM guideline. Children of SAM with medical complication will be referred to nearby stabilization center (SC). To ensure quality and good coverage of the program, CNVs and mother support groups (MSG) will do continuous screening, referral and follow up cases at community level. Furthermore, preventive activities will be conducted at nutrition sites and at community level and areas of focus will be awareness creation on maternal infant and young child feeding, identification and prevention of malnutrition, sanitation and hygiene, cooking demonstration and kitchen gardening. As part of treatment protocol, all children (if there is enough supply) with SAM will be screened and treated for malaria according to the national protocol. Furthermore, preventive kits (jerry cans, Mosquito nets, soaps) shall be provided to mothers /care takers of children under treatment for in OTP/TSFP sites as these children are at higher risk of getting infected and also a way to promote personal hygiene.
All children who will be discharged cured from OTPs will be enrolled to TSFP programs for full nutritional recovery. When there is deworming and vitamin A supplementation campaign, this project will support to ensure children under five in the county get the service in collaboration with SMOH/CHD and the health partner in the county. Furthermore, children who may deteriorate while in TSFP programme will be enrolled to the OTP programme. PI will implement OTP and TSFP programme together in Rumbek Center but only TSFP programme in Pibor county. In addition to the above mentioned interventions, PI will implement BSFP in five sites of Pibor county.
In total, this project will provide quality treatment service for 30474 children 6-59 months (Pibor:2684 SAM and 7451 MAM children 6 - 59 months; and Rumbek Center: 7109 SAM and 13231 MAM children 6 - 59 months) and 20658 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), (Pibor: 7446, Rumbek Center: 13212) in TSFP programme in Pibor and Rumbek Center counties using the national CMAM guideline. Furthermore, this project will be implemented in integration with health, WASH, and food security and livelihoods activities in Pibor and Rumbek Center counties.

1 - Needs

Based on the Integrated Phase Classiﬁcation (IPC) for Acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition (AMN) analysis of August 2019 (issued 11 September 2019), in August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) were classiﬁed in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Among this number, an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). The overall nutrition situation during the lean season of 2019 is Critical and worse compared to same period in 2018. Based on the IPC AMN, of the 79 counties were analyzed 58 were classiﬁed as IPC AMN Phase 3 and above (GAM prevalence above 9.9%). Most counties in Unity, Upper Nile, Jonglei and Warrap and parts of Eastern Equatoria and Lakes are in critical levels of acute malnutrition. According to the IPC AMN projection analysis (for Sept.- Dec. 2019 period), seasonal improvement of acute malnutrition situation is expected during the harvest and post-harvest period due to availability of food stock at household, reduced morbidity of childhood illness as well as marginal improvement in infant and young child feeding practices. However, due to high prevalence of acute malnutrition experienced at the peak lean season, improvement through shift in phase classiﬁcation might not be noted but rather improvement within the same phase. Based on the FSNMS round 23 ﬁndings, exclusive breastfeeding rates have improved from 45% in 2010 to 74.9% in 2018. But complementary feeding practices in South Sudan remain below standard. Only 47.8% of the children aged 6-8 months are introduced to solid and semi solid food in a timely manner. Only 20% of children aged 6- 23 months received the recommended minimum dietary diversity and 7.1% received the minimum acceptable diet. About two-thirds (66%) of the total surveyed children were reportedly sick from one or more illness(es) in the two weeks prior to the date of the survey. Malnutrition situation in Pibor County continued to be high as shown by the recent SMART survey, May 2018, post-harvest season. The survey found a high GAM rate of 20.7% [17.6- 25.0, 95% C.I.], of which the prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 4.9% [3.1 - 7.4, 95% C.I.] based on weight for height z scores and/or edema. The overall GAM prevalence is indicative of a critical malnutrition situation based on the WHO standards. In addition, the January 2019 IPC analysis, puts Pibor county to “critical phase” (in IPC AMN phase 4) for the May to August 2019 projection period and it is one of the very few counties which was predicted to have populations with a likelihood of experiencing Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) even with the existing humanitarian support. On the other hand, the situation at former Lakes State has remained the same except for Rumbek East County which is now projected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and not Emergency (IPC Phase 4) as earlier projected in January 2019; Rumbek center was classiﬁed as IPC 3 for acute malnutrition in August 2019 classiﬁcation and projected to remain in IPC 3 to for the IPC AMN projection analysis for the period of September- December 2019.Given the continued food insecurity, instability that hamper food production, the poor health infrastructure, frequent diseases outbreak and reGiven the continued food insecurity, instability that hamper food production, the poor health infrastructure, frequent diseases outbreak and relatively low optimal breast feeding and complementary feeding practice, there is the need to continue large scale humanitarian food assistance and ongoing nutrition services in Pibor and Rumbek Counties. This can help to prevent Extreme Critical levels of GAM (WHZ) and excess mortality in some areas. Plan International South Sudan by this proposal therefore expresses its commitment to continue strengthening, scaling up and improving quality of its nutrition program in Pibor and Rumbek Center Counties.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Accountability to affected populations (AAP) is one of Plan International’s key commitments under the core humanitarian standards, and we are therefore committed to ensuring that affected populations are meaningfully involved in projects and have the ability to give feedback and participate in decisions about any activities affecting them. Plan International (Plan) has long-term presence in Pibor and we have built good rapport with communities through our work and transparent and accountable approach, as we actively involve the affected population in consultative and decision-making processes at the design, implementation and monitoring stages of all projects. This includes clearly outlining project objectives and indicators, and providing opportunities for the community to ask questions, provide feedback and hold Plan International to account if they feel the objectives of the project are not suitable or have not been met. Throughout the project lifespan, participants will take part in monitoring activities through regular quarterly meetings between Plan International and the community-based committees and local leaders. The committees will be comprised of representatives selected together with the community and will include men, women, youth and people with disabilities to ensure everyone’s needs are represented in discussions and addressed in the project. Beneficiaries will also be able to provide regular feedback through the use of suggestion books and suggestion boxes, allowing for anonymous feedback if preferred. Such feedback is monitored by our M&E focal points who then relay information back to project staff to address accordingly. This system ensures no issues of concern are missed and that people’s identities are also protected if the issue is sensitive.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The nutrition project targets all communities in the county including IDP,returnees and host. This will be clear to all during discussions with community representatives. Furthermore, in all dialogues with the community, the project ensures that all different community members are represented and their view reflected and addressed.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Nutrition sites are selected through discussion with the county, cluster and partners operating in the county. Hence, gaps are clearly identified to ensure equity in providing the service to all the community member i.e. IDPs, returnees and host, including hard to reach communities. Furthermore, throughout the implementation of this project, further gap assessment and discussion will be made with community representatives, CHD and nutrition cluster and identified equity issues will be solved accordingly.Plan international will station most of the nutrition sites in government PHCUs, PHCCs and hospitals and use locally recruited staffs and volunteers and ensures continuous capacity building.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The objective of this project is fully aligned with 2020 nutrition cluster objectives, i.e. Sector objective 1.To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2020 and Sector Objective 2.To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2020.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Cattle raiding, inter-ethnic conflict, flooding and road damages are common occurrences in Pibor and Rumbek Center counties. Furthermore, changing seasonal patterns and the environment is likely to increase the frequency and intensity of cattle raiding and inter-ethnic conflict may prevent stability and crop production and affect market. Moreover, depletion of food stock may further lead to deterioration of nutrition status of the communities and may exacerbate with infection and poor health infrastructure in place.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Besides, PI will recruit staff and volunteers locally and involve staffs in county health department (CHD) health institutions, CNV s and MSG in different training, review meetings, joint support supervisions and awareness creation campaigns to ensure continuous capacity building and there by grantee sustainability of the project. These activities will help (volunteers, CNVs, MSG) from within the target communities to trickle the knowledge down to other communities not reached by the intervention and continue with recommended nutrition practices.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

From the onset of the project, Plan International will enable the different community groups (men, women, people with disability, ethnic minorities, IDPs and returnees) to participate and know the project and raise if they have any concern. This will continue through project implementation, review and close up.To minimize the risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services, sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance during the nutrition awareness raising sessions, Plan staff will include Child protection and GBV sessions to sensitize the communities. Women/caretakers will also be advised to move in groups to and from their homes when they are coming to collect the supplies. Furthermore, Plan has a Code of Conduct which all staff/associates must sign. It prohibits staff/associates from sexually exploiting beneficiaries and provides a reporting mechanism in which communities can report any SEA concern confidentially. Plan will also orient the communities about our conduct of conduct policy and ‘what is’ or ‘not’ expected from Plan staff.
