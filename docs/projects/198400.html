Project Summary

This project 12 months will seek to address protection monitoring on household and community level (with referrals when needed); protection information sharing and awareness raising; capacity building of partners and duty bearers; provision of specialized protection services (case management, psycho-social support, individual protection assessment, distribution of dignity kits etc.) and legal assistance; establishment of community-based protection networks, safe livelihood, skills acquisition, and empowerment activities, reintegration support for people coming out of inaccessible areas and those formerly associated with NSAGs; and protection advocacy in high severity areas including: Gubio, Dikwa, Ngala and Gwoza LGAs in Borno State. Mubi north, south and Madagali LGAs in Adamawa state and Yusufari, fune and Tamua LGAs in Yobe State.
GSF aim to 141,500 people with protection needs with the highest severity ranking, as per Humanitarian Needs Overview, including 80,000 out-of-camp IDPs, 25000in-camp IDPs, 5000 Returnees, and 30000 HC members across the BAY states.
Advocacy for Ending Violence against Internally Displace people Advocacy will be done with the state assembly members, Court, police department and other concerned government department to make the law and apply it in favor of abolition of violence against IDPs. All forms of violence against IDPs should be ended. Equal treatment of IDPs and Host community by people in authority including freedom of Movement of IDPs

1 - Needs

Approximately 140,000 people cannot be reached by protection partners with populations in Gubio, and Ngala LGAs being hard-to-reach, while high levels of protection needs are assessed among people coming out of inaccessible areas. Persons formerly associated with NSAGs face the risk of exclusion from assistance in reintegration areas. NSAG violence against civilians was uncommonly high in 2022 in both inaccessible and accessible areas, including abductions and mass killings in Dikwa, LGAs, also due to enhanced suspicion of civilians collaborating with Government Forces. Especially the 2.2 million IDPs across the BAY states experience high levels of vulnerability and face severe levels of protection incidents in Geidam, Gujba, and Gulani LGAs in Yobe state, extreme levels in Madagali, Maiha, Michika, and Mubi North LGAs in Adamawa state and catastrophic levels in Chibok, Dikwa, Mobbar, Monguno and Ngala LGAs in Borno state. Approximately up to 150,000 IDPs have been affected by the closure of 8 IDP camps in Maiduguri and government enforced “returns”/relocations to Bama, Gwoza, Jere, Konduga, Kukawa, Mafa, Marte, Mobbar, and Monguno LGAs where they cannot provide for their basic needs with high levels of insecurity, limited freedom of movement and livelihood opportunities, and low levels of government and humanitarian services in over-congested camps, causing secondary or multiple displacement given the unsustainability of their “return”/relocation areas and new humanitarian needs. Refugees were also forcibly returned to Abadam, Bama, and Gwoza LGAs and face a lack of services and severe insecurity with regular NSAG attacks, having caused loss of life. Displaced and non-displaced populations in Bama, Gwoza, Jere, Konduga, Mafa, and Maiduguri LGAs have been most affected by NSAG attacks while going about livelihood activities, including farming, collecting firewood and scrap metal, and herding. Host community members across the BAY states struggle to receive Refugee returnees and relocated IDPs with depleting capacities in the absence of government and humanitarian actors, while they have themselves suffered catastrophic levels of protection incidents in Dikwa and Monguno LGAs in Borno and Hong and Madagali LGAs in Adamawa. Peaceful co-existence has been compromised in some communities, including in Bama and Monguno LGAs. Populations across the BAY states have also been at increased risk of trafficking in persons both internally and transnationally

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming throughout the project activities, the project team will conduct risk analysis exercises prior to activities and periodically thereafter to understand the vulnerabilities, capacities, threats, power holders, gender analysis and service availability in the areas to identify what risks might exist or arise. When the risk is identified, either per-existing or potentially due to GSF activities, specific mitigation measures are assigned (time-bound with a responsible person identified), and the risk itself is measured by the community against the positives of the activity proposed. The proposed project will take into account strict adherence to gender equality and equity throughout the design and implementation phase and the specific needs of children and women survivors are based on age and gender, ensuring for instance that adolescent females are assigned a female staff or volunteer, and those young children are assigned a staff who has experience with, and can easily interact with children of their age. The staff and volunteers engaged in the project will be gender-balanced to help ensure that both girls and boys are able to relate to and talk with appropriate persons. The leadership of the community structures will include females and GSF will facilitate participation from all members, irrespective of their age and gender. The use of women to contribute to awareness creation on CP and GBV directly is a context-specific opportunity to advance women’s participation. The majority of the religious and community leaders are males with only a few female representatives at the community level. During the training and meetings with the community leaders, the project will aim to engage all female leaders to ensure their representation and may if appropriate, include other well-respected women in the community who are vocal about CP and GBV protection Concerns. Supported by the Community Based mechanism, GSF will monitor the overall protection situation in camps, informal settings, and host communities on a regular basis and develop networks and contacts with relevant stakeholders to identify incidents and protection concerns, such as access to humanitarian assistance by communities in general and by persons with special needs, UASC, GBV survivors, gaps in service delivery but also information related to education, WASH, food, healthcare, shelter as well as safety and security including any related incidents. Concerns on reported/alleged violations/protection issues will be documented, recorded, verified and analyzed together to support the identification of priority key protection issues to respond to either by providing direct advice or assistance or by referring beneficiaries to appropriate service providers (in the case of incidents). The methodology for protection mainstreaming will include spot observations, focus group discussions (FGDs) with key informants and monitored populations, individual/household interviews, and area assessments as well as testimonies and complaints collected through the complaint and response mechanism. In addition, as part of the overall mainstreaming strategy, GSF will conduct two-way information sharing with the target population throughout all of its activities, thus providing information to IDPs on return conditions, rights and entitlements while seeking information from IDPs on incidents and protection concerns in camps, host communities and in their areas of implementation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GSF is committed to the core values of IASC framework on AAP by ensuring participation and inclusion, communication and transparency, feedback and response, organizational learning and adoption. We would be accountable to all our beneficiaries and stakeholders from the onset of the project. This requires GSF to conduct child friendly CRM, KIIs and FGDs to collect complaints and provide feedback. This systematic involvement and participation of the beneficiaries and stakeholders would ensure accountability and quality response to the affected people. Information sharing about the project activities through meetings with community-based stakeholders, gender-balanced staff speaking local languages and message dissemination through community leaders, will ensure that GSF interventions have clear, relevant and timely information required to make informed decision and choices. GSF shall conduct location based focused group discussion with influential community based stakeholders such as youth leaders, women leaders, religious and traditional leaders to understand the appropriate means of collecting, disseminating feedback from each project site. GSF would do that by recommending some of the existing feedback and complaint and accountability mechanism such as the complaint box which has overtime proven to be non-effective as advised by other partners, GSF CFM lines to receive direct calls from beneficiaries and focus sessions with community groups to understand what the issues are and concerted, practical steps of resolving the bottlenecks so as to make the project better and more accountable for the persons that we serve. Community based facilitators will support to generate the data needed to ensure responsiveness and accountability in programming through documenting protection issues/concerns including instances/trends in violations, gaps in service provision, lack of information, and other barriers to service access.
