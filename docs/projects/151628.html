Project Summary

The objective of the project is to provide lifesaving emergency water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion services for conflict, drought and flood affected people, vulnerable host communities and people in disease prone areas, mainly women and children including strengthening of communities’ resilience to cope with the impact of emergencies and recurrent shocks. In addition, the project is also targeting to reduce the risk of violence against women, children and vulnerable groups while accessing water and sanitation facilities by providing gender segregate facilities and establishing the facilities within 500 meters’ distance of the settlements.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains critical due to ongoing impacts of drought, flooding, displacement and conflict. According to OCHA Humanitarian Bulletin of 4th October 2018 there are over 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia continue to live in critical conditions which is contributing to high levels of acute humanitarian needs and an estimated 2.9 million people are in need of WASH assistance across Somalia. Flooding is prevalent in Somalia, in March 2018, heavy rains started in large parts of Somalia and continued for over two months resulting in additional displacement and destruction of shelter and water supply facilities including in IDP locations. Cholera is endemic in Somalia, in 2018, as of 7th October, a total of 6,464 cases and 43 deaths reported from 22 districts and 4 regions of Somalia, over 45% of the cases were children under 5 years old. The continued conflict, flooding and disease outbreaks together with nutrition crises and other natural emergencies combined with poor WASH coverage in Somalia highlight the need for UNICEF and humanitarian partners to continue providing emergency services to vulnerable communities. Therefore, this emergency WASH project will comprise of situation assessments, preparedness, emergency WASH response, strengthening partners’ capacity, cluster coordination and information management. The project will focus on emergency water trucking/water vouchers, rehabilitating and upgrading urban and rural water facilities, construction of new water points, provision of emergency sanitation services for IDPs and vulnerable communities, hygiene promotion, promotion and support for sustainable sanitation facilities, WASH in schools and health/nutrition facilities and flood and cholera prevention and preparedness activities to reach the total targeted population of 1.5 Million People with a total of 35.8 Million USD.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Repeated occurrences of disasters in Somalia pose a huge threat to communities and water and sanitation infrastructure resilience. Hence this emergency WASH response will go beyond provision of access to safe water supply and restoring of WASH infrastructure and services and will help to prepare the affected communities to face future hazards. Water, sanitation and hygiene as a critical need and priority during the emergency, early recovery and longer-term recovery efforts in Somalia, the measures to be undertaken by the project will create sustainable WASH facilities as well as build community and institutional capacities. The project is designed in such a way that while providing emergency lifesaving interventions it will also provide longer term sustainable solutions for affected population. To ensure the emergency interventions are linked to durable solutions, the project included construction, rehabilitation and upgrading of water supply systems and promotion of community based sanitation interventions which will provide the affected population with durable solutions and increase resilience of the communities to mitigate the impact of future shocks. The project, with participation of affected population and local authorities, will establish sustainable operation, maintenance and management systems. Depending on the location and local context of each water point suitable management modalities of water supply systems will be established. Two modalities are in practice. First, the direct management of the water systems through affected community committees, or second, by engaging the private sector through Public Private Partnerships (PPP) with consumer/community oversight. The project also focuses on sustainability by promoting innovative approaches including decentralized management of water infrastructures and solar powered water supply systems. Given strong private sector presence in Somalia water sector, UNICEF will encourage greater role for the private sector in achieving sustainable and quality services, including community entrepreneurs and other local private sector actors. For provision of sustainable sanitation solutions for affected population, the project will use the recently developed strategy to adopt community lead sanitation strategies in IDP locations by engaging religious and clan leaders for promoting sustainable sanitation.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Continued conflict and extreme climatic events in Somalia have created large number of displacement of populations in the country. According to OCHA Humanitarian Bulletine of 4th October 2018 there are over 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia with very limited or no access to safe water supply and sanitation facilities. In addition to these vulnerabilities in many locations, land tenancy is a major issue and IDPs are repeatedly affected by evections and humanitarian partners denied permission to construct WASH facilities as the land where the IDPs settled are claimed by others. In addition, the WASH services in host communities are very limited and not able to cope with additional pressure created by large number of IDPs that put IDPs in a very critical situation as they are the last group of people to get services from existing infrastructure. To address these challenges, the project will priorities provision of emergency as well as sustainable WASH facilities and services in areas where large number of IDPs settled and include IDP representatives in the management structures of the water points. For water supply systems serving both host communities and IDP locations, the project in coordination with humanitarian partners will establish a subsidy system for vulnerable IDPs by providing water vouchers to access enough water in both community and private sector managed water supply systems and ensure enough representation of IDPs in the water user Associations. The project will also work with local authorities and community leaders to allow humanitarian partners to provide WASH facilities in the settlement areas by creating dialogue and incentive of improved services for communities hosting IDPs.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Lack of clean water and poor sanitation and hygiene practices not only cause diseases but also create huge burden on women and children as women and girls perform most of the unpaid labor associated with WASH in households and communities which reduces the time they have available for education, economic activities and leisure. Women and girls are often vulnerable to harassment or violence when they travel long distances to fetch water, use shared toilets, or practice open defecation. In addition, lack of proper hygiene supplies like soap and sanitary pads affects dignity of women and girls.The project is designed in such a way that the water points will be constructed close to settlement areas thus reducing the distance traveled by women and children to fetch water. Special emphasis will be on the needs of vulnerable women and girls, the elderly and poor communities. Beneficiaries, especially women and young people, will participate throughout the project cycle, including on site selection, establishing management structures and the monitoring of the progress. Women and women groups will be consulted during site selection and identification of water supply system management entities and modalities. In addition, women will be represented in the Water User Associations and PPP companies and will be part of training and capacity building interventions by the project. In the sanitation component, all the emergency latrines to be constructed in IDP camps and settlements will be gender segregated and with proper locks and access to the households which will be tracked and monitored with a specific indicator developed for monitoring of progress.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Conflict and insecurity is a major challenge for UNICEF and other humanitarian partners to reach the affected population with lifesaving and sustainable WASH services. In addition, competition for limited water resources can also be a source of conflict in many parts of Somalia which increase vulnerability of communities in areas where there is critical water shortages and limited resources. The project will prioritize providing functional, accessible and affordable water supply for drinking and domestic use which is key to peace building and resilience as it creates coherence in the communities, helps and reduce the risk of conflict. To overcome the issue of accessibility and insecurity to deliver WASH services to affected populations, UNICEF uses multiple channels to reach affected populations. These include working with local NGOs, Government, business communities and community leaders to implement WASH projects and capacity building of partners and voluntary self-help groups that have formed during the crisis from inaccessible areas. UNICEF also employs third party verification for monitoring of project activities in inaccessible or partially accessible areas to ensure accurate information for programme implementation and monitoring.
