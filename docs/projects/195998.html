Project Summary

"IOM will adapt and strengthen its GBV program, established in 2017, targeting Rohingya refugees and host communities centered on three pillars: 1) survivor-centered support, 2) risk mitigation, and 3) addressing root causes that perpetuate GBV in line with IOM´s GBV in Crises Institutional Framework.
IOM directly implements case management providing a multisectoral package of support via nine Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) across eight sites and through an emergency safe shelter. To mitigate risks, prevent GBV and empower women and girls, the WGSS provides information, age-appropriate and structured psychosocial support (PSS), roll out Ma Boinor Rosom and Girl Shine Curriculum, awareness and educational activities, life-skills and opportunities, forums for two-way information-sharing and learning, including peer-led curriculum on GBV, sexual and reproductive health, menstrual hygiene and disaster preparedness.
Ensuring sustainability and localization, IOM and PULSE Bangladesh Society, IOM’s IP, will scale up capacity building and support with community groups, advocates, imams, majhis, volunteers and women´s committee members, to transition key WGSS awareness and outreach activities through regular meeting, capacity building sessions and regular supervision as appropriate. This will enhance community ownership and accountability by working holistically with communities to address the root causes of gender inequality. IOM will implement structured and evidence-based GBV prevention activities such as EMAP in the proposed areas. IOM will work closely with the Protection Research Unit to develop peer led evidence-based curriculum working with men, boys, women, girls and persons with diverse Sexual Orientations, Gender Identities and Expressions, and Sex Characteristics’ (SOGIESC) to ensure inclusive approaches. Such strategies will ensure safe and non-stigmatizing entry points to GBV services for male survivors and survivors with diverse SOGIESC.
IOM´s program will feature a greater focus on GBV risk mitigation in the WASH sector through enhancing the participation of women and girls in the design and maintenance of WASH infrastructure. IOM´s primary focus will be on menstrual health education through peer-based models building on the findings of IOM´s summary report Ma Boinor Rosom or “Mother and Sister´s Ways” and its related curriculum, piloted and rolled out as a model of best practice. The curriculum, among other complementary outreach tools (MBR-1, MBR-2) focused on life skills and male engagement, will be further developed using participatory approaches for both Rohingya and host communities.
Empowerment and skills development activities including tailoring and handicraft training for Rohingya and Host community, IT and Arts training (not certification courses) for host community women and girls will become more participatory, structured and diversified. IOM will scale up the production of re-usable sanitary pads working with volunteers. Participants who have graduated from skills-building classes and groups will be engaged as peer trainers and supported with coaching and capacity building. IOM will support Rohingya women to identify opportunities for strengthened women’s participation and engagement through multi-stakeholder approaches and plans to scale up and replicate its Women´s Participation Project, piloted in 2020 and 2019 as a best practice model for fostering social cohesion and ensuring disability inclusion.
IOM is an active member of the GBV Subsector Working Group, GBV Case Management and Dignity Kit Task Teams and Co-chair of the PSEA Network. IOM GBV staff serve as interagency GBV Camp Level Focal Points and as part of interagency emergency and response structures such as the Mobile Medical Teams and the PERUs. IOM will continue work with the DC, UNO, RRRC Office and CiC Offices at the camp level by coordinating regularly and providing capacity building on GBV."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The reduction of humanitarian actors and restrictions in activities due to COVID-19 in the last year exacerbated pre-existing GBV trends for Rohingya refugees and host communities. The 2021 JMSNA findings, ACAPS, and interagency assessments have echoed such findings highlighting that the COVID-19 restriction measures have contributed to new protection concerns for individuals at heightened risk who face discrimination and additional barriers to accessing services such as single female-headed households, adolescent girls, persons with diverse gender and sexual orientation and persons with disabilities. WGSS facilities were closed due to fluctuating lockdown measures throughout 2021 and early 2022. IRC´s 2021 analysis building from its 2020 “In the Shadows of the Pandemic” report, GBVIMS reports, and interagency gender analyses, 2022 ACAPS thematic report on protection implications of polygamy, there is an increase in incidents of intimate partner and domestic violence, polygamy since the onset of COVID-19 and have impacted on referrals and continuity of case management . According to GBVIMS monthly and quarterly reports, most GBV incidents occur at the survivor's or perpetrator’s residence, street/pathway, water point, public toilets/latrines and bathing facilities, highlighting an urgent need for GBV risk mitigation in WASH programming. In-depth research using participatory approaches with Rohingya women and girls highlighted that in this context it is extremely different for women and girls to manage their periods. Social taboos impact how women and girls learn about their periods, their access to and awareness of necessary menstrual products and how they manage their periods in the camps. Building on the IOM and UN Women joint report “Honour in Transition” and recent interagency assessments and reports, gendered norms are shifting in Rohingya communities and impact on the mobility of women and girls making control over resources, information and access to services difficult. As well gender norms are changing in displacement intersecting with new family compositions and demographic changes. A focus on safe and meaningful women´s participation and empowerment are crucial to wider GBV prevention efforts and safe access to services. Scale up of evidence based GBV prevention work will be key priority to challenge harmful norms and ensure access to services for the vulnerable. IOM has also been piloting a Cash for Work initiative that is culturally appropriate to further enhance access to safe and dignified services. Women and adolescent girls are at heightened risk due to greater mobility restrictions in displacement among other contributing factors including poor living conditions in the camps, limited basic resources, lack of income generating activities, insufficient lighting and gender segregated toilets and bathing facilities, distance of water points and insecurity. Many of these risks have shown to lead to harmful coping mechanisms such as child marriage, survival sex and human trafficking. FGDs and reports have indicated that pressure and fear of parents and community leaders as well as the low participation of women and girls in making decisions at household level constitute reporting barriers for survivors and impact on safe access to services. Risks of forced marriage with the perpetrator and not being protected against reprisals are commonly cited concerns. Social stigma and perceptions about survivors, the community leaders’ interference in handling rape cases, lack of protection of survivors, insufficient awareness of consequences of rape, benefits of services and service accessibility could considerably delay survivors’ decision making in reporting and accessing services on a timely manner. Specialized GBV services including multi-sectoral services will require further enhanced to consider the inclusion of male survivors as well as survivors with diverse gender and diverse needs."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Situating protection at the core of its humanitarian response, IOM implements a range of multi-sectoral and integrated activities for the crisis-affected persons in Cox’s Bazar to have unhindered access to their fundamental rights; to no longer be subjected to any form of violence, coercion, and deprivation; and to have the capacity and resources to build resilience against all future forms of harm. Protection is a shared responsibility in IOM that reduces the risk and vulnerabilities by increasing capacity through dignity, safety, resilience and meaningful participation of all crisis-affected persons. The GBV team works through a survivor centered approach guided by the IOM's GBViC intitutional framework as well as follow the ISP of GBVIMS+/Primero.

3.2 - Gender

The implementation of this project will be guided by IOM’s Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises (GBViC Framework), and the Cox’s Bazar Mission related action plan. IOM will ensure that the principles of do no harm, safety and non-discrimination guide activities are adhered to throughout the project. IOM is committed to gender equality and women’s empowerment by mainstreaming across the project through assessment, outcomes, outputs, activities and monitoring and reporting. When applicable, the interventions planned under this project will go through a consultative process where different needs, capacities and priorities of women, girls, men, boys and diverse gender groups including person with disability will be taken into account and the assistance will be targeted to the persons and groups most in need. IOM aims to have a gender balance for staff in the camps who often work in pairs, thus allowing the beneficiary options to establish the best rapport and trust.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

All areas of IOM programming include age considerations, particularly child safeguarding mechanisms. These mechanisms are embeded at the development, implementation and evaluation phase to continuously assess how we can ensure upholding the best interest of the child at all times.

3.4 - Diversity

IOM adopts diversity considerations in all areas of programming to ensure equal access of services.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM´s GBV program is characterized by community-based and participatory approaches to ensure diversity of need and inclusive participation. IOM will actively solicit feedback from the affected population for insight to and feedback on emerging risks and threats, how activities will be carried out, who will be consulted and so on. IOM GBV uses structured questionnaires, CFM desks and case management feedback surveys and facilitates regular FGDs and consultations inside the WGSS and with at the community level including person with disabilities, gender diverse individuals to ensure a community driven and do-no-harm approach. IOM will continue to roll out PSEA training packages, ToT for PSEA Champions next year to ensure IOM staff, IP staff and volunteers adhere to the PSEA guiding principles and mandatory reporting systems. IOM has a strong reporting mechanism in place and IOM will continue to Co-Chair the PSEA network and support the IPs and ISCG with capacity building and advocacy.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"IOM and its IP are part of the Rapid Protection Assessment team, Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) and Mobile Medical Team (MMT) to have immediate response to the crisis and disaster affected people. The always coordinate with the Site Management and the CiCs and support Disaster Management Units (DMU) volunteers with training on DRR, GBV Safe referral. IOM will also continue to raise awareness on Proetction Key Messages to help the community people to reduce the impact of disaster before, during and after it happens. This also includes household to household visit through trained volunteers to ensure Person with disabilities and other vulnerbale populations are reached. IOM and IP PULSE Bangladesh Society will continue to preposition Clothing items and Dignity Kits as part of its emergency preaparedness and response activities. "
