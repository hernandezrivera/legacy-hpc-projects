Project Summary

This intervention aims to enhance access to life-saving healthcare services in flood and conflict-affected communities of Adamawa and Yobe States in Nigeria. Its primary purpose is to provide immediate emergency response, strengthen local healthcare capacities, and ensure comprehensive healthcare services for vulnerable populations, particularly women, children, and those impacted by the ongoing crises. Through rapid deployment of medical teams, capacity building, infrastructure development, and comprehensive healthcare initiatives, the intervention seeks to improve health outcomes and foster community resilience in the face of these challenges.

1 - Needs

The identified needs within the flood and conflict-affected communities of Adamawa and Yobe States align with the findings outlined in the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for Nigeria (https://reliefweb.int/attachments/51148ca6-ea34-412f-a582-6091976d1207/2023%20HNO_Nigeria_v0.8_Latest%20update%2020%20March.pdf). According to the report, both Adamawa and Yobe States exhibit critical gaps in essential services due to the compounded effects of conflict and natural disasters.The HNO highlights the pressing need for healthcare access in conflict-affected areas, with approximately 5.76 million people requiring health assistance in the northeastern region of Nigeria. Among them, there's a substantial number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) facing challenges in accessing basic healthcare services due to insecurity and limited infrastructure.Furthermore, the report underscores the vulnerability of women and children, emphasizing the necessity for specialized care and support. In these affected regions, an estimated 2.7 million individuals are in need of critical health services, including maternal and child healthcare.Infrastructure damage and inadequate facilities also hinder healthcare delivery, with a significant portion of healthcare facilities either damaged or non-functional. This situation significantly limits the provision of essential healthcare services to the affected populations.Moreover, the HNO stresses the importance of addressing mental health needs in these communities, highlighting the psychological impact of the crises. It estimates that approximately 4.7 million people require mental health and psychosocial support due to the protracted conflicts and displacement.Therefore, based on the figures and assessments provided in the 2023 HNO for Nigeria, the proposed project's focus on emergency healthcare response, capacity building, infrastructure development, comprehensive healthcare services (especially for women and children), and mental health support resonates with the critical needs identified within the flood and conflict-affected communities of Adamawa and Yobe States.

2 - Activities

1. Deploying rapid medical teams, emergency supplies, and critical treatments constitute the immediate emergency healthcare response. 2. Capacity building involves tailored training for local healthcare workers, improving their skills and knowledge. 3. Infrastructure development focuses on renovating and equipping healthcare facilities to handle increased demand. 4. Establishing mobile clinics and specialized units addresses remote areas' healthcare needs, emphasizing maternal and child health and mental health services. 5. Community engagement initiatives raise awareness on hygiene, disease prevention, and mental health, fostering active participation. 6. Continuous monitoring and evaluation ensure efficacy and guide ongoing improvements for sustained impact on flood and conflict-affected communities' health.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project implements a zero-tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). It includes comprehensive staff training, community awareness campaigns, and a confidential complaints mechanism. Rapid response protocols address reported cases, ensuring survivor support and holding perpetrators accountable. Continuous monitoring, engagement with community leaders, and transparent reporting mechanisms strengthen protection measures, safeguarding vulnerable individuals within the project area.
