Project Summary

A reduction in economic growth and a rise in unemployment will inevitably lead to a sharp increase in the poverty rate and push more people below the poverty line. The number of vulnerable persons who will need assistance may rise exponentially in 2020, even if for a temporary period. Vulnerable groups in Bangladesh are unlikely to have savings and the resources needed to fend off the impacts of COVID-19. The proposed intervention will support these vulnerable groups to cope with the situation and restore their livelihoods by offering temporary employment, contributing to improved food security and reducing negative coping mechanisms.
Poor and vulnerable households are increasingly getting dependent on external assistance including government and NGO. Substantial number of people have no or limited livelihood options. Given that some form of markers are functional at the local level, direct cash injection in the form of unconditional cash grant will not only increase direct cash in hand, thereby optimizing beneficiary choice and decision making, it will further stimulate the demand and contribute in balancing the wider market system.
Therefore unconditional cash grant support aiming to meet non-feed needs of underserved communities, such as the proposed locations of Chakaria, Pekua, Moheshkhali and Ramu sub-districts will result into immediate economic gains of the targeted communities. The reason for selecting Chakaria, Pekua, Moheshkhali and Ramu is due to the fact that Teknaf and Ukhia sub-district, being marked as host community sites are overcrowded with actors and most of the need for emergency cash and hygiene materials has been covered by relevant actors.
For the UCG, IRC will target 18511 most vulnerable households (92,555 individuals) in the aforementioned sub-district. These households will receive BDT 4500 each for two months.
For CFW, IRC will target 3520 vulnerable women (benefiting 17,600 individuals) who has prior knowledge of swing, have access to swing machine and were engaged in some form of livelihood activities related to swing, mask production, reusable sanitary napkin production, a total of 4500 individuals (22,500 individuals) will receive BDT 7500 each to disseminate COVID-19 related awareness messages for at least 17 days , a total of 1500 Households (7, 500 individuals) will receive BDT 7500 each to work for at least 17 days in to develop homestead vegetable garden. While selected unions of the targeted districts will be prioritized for this activities, relatively poor households will be prioritized who may not have the necessary purchasing power.

Objectives

Sector Objective 1: Expand support to improve food security and compensate for loss of livelihoods of the most vulnerable Bangladeshis in Cox’s Bazar whose livelihoods will be impacted by the pandemic, through food and cash support according to need.

Needs

The IRC’s Economic Recovery and Market Systems sector interventions seek to meet basic needs and food security through temporary income generating activities. IRC will target vulnerable women who were previously engaged in livelihood activities prior to COVID-19 and have lost their source of income. Market systems play an important role in the food supply chain and food security. Due to the government lockdown to help contain COVID-19, production, employment and income has been disrupted, leading to a gap in income and food security. The effect of the containment measures is now visible as small traders, wage earners, agricultural labors are idle and have lost their income; the impacts of COVID-19 on economic growth, job losses and upsurge in poverty are expected to be large. A reduction in economic growth and a rise in unemployment will inevitably lead to a sharp increase in the poverty rate and push more people below the poverty line. The number of vulnerable persons who will need assistance may rise exponentially in 2020, even if for a temporary period. Vulnerable groups in Bangladesh are unlikely to have savings and the resources needed to fend off the impacts of COVID-19. The proposed intervention will support these vulnerable groups to cope with the situation and restore their livelihoods by offering temporary employment, contributing to improved food security and reducing negative coping mechanisms. Poor and vulnerable households are increasingly getting dependent on external assistance including government and NGO. Substantial number of people have no or limited livelihood options. With limited income, people are at risk of increased debt exposure with price hike for daily essentials. Due to the loss of daily work, migrant workers will likely to return to their communities which will have broader social and economic implications as the situation becomes dire. The regular market systems has been distorted: while there is limited supply and availability of daily essentials - contributing to price hike, there is also limited demand and purchasing power due to reduction of income. Given that some form of markers are functional at the local level, direct cash injection in the form of unconditional cash grant will not only increase direct cash in hand, thereby optimizing beneficiary choice and decision making, it will further stimulate the demand and contribute in balancing the wider market system. Therefore unconditional cash grant support aiming to meet non-feed needs of underserved communities, such as the proposed locations of Chakaria, Pekua, Moheshkhali and Ramu sub-districts will result into immediate economic gains of the targeted communities. With cyclone season approaching, the additional cash in hand will further enable the at-risk population to better cope with their vulnerability.

Activities or outputs

1) Unconditional Cash Grants (UCG) for the 18,511 vulnerable HHs benefiting 92.555 household members for two times based on the following criteria-• Households headed by chronically sick or person with a disability; • Female headed households with children below the employment / working age; • Elderly keeping children / orphans; • Child headed households; • Widow, pregnant and/or lactating mothers with no able/earning HH member; • Households with daily wage earners who has otherwise lost their income temporarily due to the shutdown• Households having member of positive COVID-19 cases and currently under isolation and treatment; 2) Cash For Work (CFW) for 9520 Individuals benefiting 47600 household members under the following tasks-• Semi-skilled/unskilled female (who will be provided with training) to produce cloth masks to be distributed among people are at risk of COVID-19• Women and men will be developing homestead vegetable garden• Youth female and male will be disseminating COVID-19 awareness messages among their nearby households
