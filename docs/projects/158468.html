Project Summary

To contribute significantly to the overall health response amongst the cyclone survivors, to reduce the risk and burden of life-threatening vector related diseases and epidemics.
Total Number of Individuals Affected in the Target Area: estimate of 2.3m inhabitants in Caba Delgado. Total Number of Direct Beneficiaries: 900,000.
Strengthening Malaria, dengue fever and other vector borne disease control: • Maximize community acceptance and participation in each prevention campaign.
• Ensure household awareness of the increased risks of malaria
• Promote and sensitize communities to IRS to maximize shelter access and population participation.
• Ensure household maintenance of IRS efficacy, i.e. discouraging washing of walls inside shelters that have been sprayed.
• Promote and sensitize communities to LLINs (received through MoH distributions) to ensure correct and effective use, retention and maintenance (repair of holes, tears, etc.).
• Ensure household awareness of the increased risks of aedes borne diseases
• Promote early diagnosis and treatment seeking for all sick people, especially those with febrile illness
• Promote attendance at ANC and uptake of Intermittent Preventative Therapy (ITP) and use of LLINs for all pregnant women, to help reduce the risk of malaria to their unborn children.
Technical Training (vector borne disease technical training for health care workers): training of hospital health care workers, communtiy clinic workers and community outreach heath workers to improve case management of malaria, dengue, chikungunya, diaroheal disease and trachoma due to increased risk post cyclone. Improve capacity of health care workers to: 1. Conduct differential clinical diagnosis (per national case definitions) of all patients, quickly and efficiently.
2. Conduct confirmatory diagnosis (using rapid diagnostic test kits) of all suspected malaria cases.
3. Conduct clinical and confirmatory diagnosis (where RDTs are available) of dengue fever
4. Provide pre feral treatment of suspected severe malaria cases (where these cases present at peripheral health facilities and need to be referred for inpatient treatment)
5. Provide ACT therapy (as per national protocol) for all confirmed uncomplicated malaria cases.
6. Provide severe malaria therapy (as per national protocol) for all confirmed malaria cases with severe clinical symptoms.
7. Provide supportive case management therapy as needed.
8. Accurately record case diagnosis and treatment in MoH standard format records 9. Understand need for surveillance, particularly for cases of fever with unknown ateiolgy.
10. Collate and report weekly and monthly VBD case-loads (separately recording malaria, dengue, diarhoeal diseases etc according to MoH standardized case definitions, and confirmatory diagnosis where available) according to MoH standard reporting systems and the Provincial Health Directorate.
