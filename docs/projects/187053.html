1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Chad has been experiencing three interconnected crises: food insecurity and malnutrition, population movements, and health emergencies, which have been further aggravated in 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The results of the national nutrition SMART survey carried out in October 2021 indicates that the nutrition situation remains critical with a global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of 10.9% and a severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate of 2.0 % among children under-five year. Young children aged 6 – 23 months are the most affected (15.1 per cent of GAM with 3.6 of SAM against 8.7 per cent of GAM with only 1.2 per cent of SAM among children aged 24 to 59 months).This worrying nutritional situation, which has lasted for years, is exacerbated by multiple recurrent crises, including armed conflicts with large population movements in the South (CAR crisis), in the East (Darfur and CAR crisis), in Chari Baguirmi and Mayo Kebi Est (Cameroon crisis) and in the Lake area (NSAG attack, and BOKO HARAM), with the island populations who are the most affected and the least accessible by humanitarian aid for security reasons. More than 528,000 refugees reside in Chad, and some 402,000 Chadians are internally displaced, a 136 per cent increase since January 2020, including 46,000 people who are newly displaced and 41,478 refugees who arrived in 2021 and require humanitarian assistance. These constant waves of refugees and returnees generate critical humanitarian needs in a context of low level of coverage with basic social infrastructure, particularly for displaced people and host communities. The massive displacements affect disproportionally children and women, with an estimated 13,513 children under five suffering from severe acute malnutrition who will require lifesaving assistance. Outputs: 1.) Procurement and distribution of 13,513 RUTF boxes and others nutrition supplies, materials and equipment to health facilities for the treatment of 13,513 children (6,892 female and 6,621 male) suffering from severe acute malnutrition;2.) Ensure training and refresher training for 36 health workers on the management of SAM and IYCF counselling and accountability for beneficiaries3.) Ensure training for 196 community health workers (CHWs) on MUAC screening of acute malnutrition and awareness raising activities using the promotion of optimal IYCF practices4.) Raise awareness of 6,245 mothers on IYCF practices5.) Conduct monthly monitoring of nutrition status for children using Mothers MUAC strategy for early detection of SAM cases Results 1.) 13,513 children (6,892 female and 6,621 male) suffering from acute malnutrition are treated 2.) 40 Community Health workers trained in the prevention and management of SAM, disaggregated by sex and age 3.) 6,245 caregivers have improved knowledge on IYCF 4.) 26,208 children screened for severe acute Malnutrition
