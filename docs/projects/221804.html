1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project’s overall objective is to contribute to the provision of integrated services to address Gender Based Violence (GVB) and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) needs of the most vulnerable people, both Internally Displaced People (IDPs) and host communities (HC) living in areas affected by the humanitarian crisis in Cabo Delgado, Mozambique. Activities will be implemented in Pemba city. Other sites might be considered based on arising needs, specific cases (eg. high risk cases), security, proximity of GBV and MHPSS related services and logistics capacity. Outcome: - Referral pathway for GBV survivors strengthened and case management improved Output: 1. Strengthening quality life-saving services to address inclusive protection immediate risks and needs (including GBV) of the most vulnerable populations in priority areas. Output 2. Prevent protection risks (including GBV) of most affected and vulnerable populations in priority areas through enhanced preparedness and resilience capacity. Output 3. Strengthen the protection environment and enhance the protection of affected populations – including related to GBV, and through collective and intersectoral action. Output 4. Improving access to community based mental health care and psychosocial support (MHPSS) of most affected and vulnerable populations through integrated, community based MHPSS interventions.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CUAMM develops its projects in close collaboration with local authorities to ensure alignment with regional priorities and active participation from key stakeholders. This cooperative approach secures intervention endorsement while fostering ownership among local actors. Implementation Strategy:- Participatory Design and Implementation: Projects are developed using participatory methods that actively involve community members and beneficiaries. This ensures that activities are tailored to address the specific needs of the target population effectively.- Engagement and Transparency: Information sessions are organized both before and throughout the project’s duration. These meetings, held at the community level and with relevant government services, aim to foster trust, transparency, and mutual accountability.- Community Feedback Mechanisms: CUAMM prioritizes establishing accessible and safe feedback and complaint mechanisms. In collaboration with the Protection Cluster, these systems empower community members to share concerns and provide input regarding project activities. Feedback may be gathered at service points or through focus groups with beneficiaries and local residents.- Linha Verde Hotline Integration: CUAMM promotes awareness and use of the Linha Verde hotline, an inter-agency feedback platform designed to collect complaints, share information, and report sensitive issues such as sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). This ensures an additional layer of protection and accountability for vulnerable populations.These strategies reinforce CUAMM’s commitment to delivering responsive, inclusive, and community-centered interventions while upholding principles of transparency, safety, and accountability.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project aims to facilitate meaningful access to assistance by implementing integrated, tailored strategies that strengthen both the demand and supply sides of the support system. Demand-Side Measures:CUAMM will identify the barriers and challenges different demographic groups face when trying to access services. By working closely with beneficiaries and local authorities, the project will establish common, feasible, and locally accepted solutions to address these challenges. This will include identifying specific needs and co-developing actions to overcome these barriers. Community Awareness and Inclusion:In collaboration with local civil society organizations (CSOs) and activists, CUAMM will lead community awareness initiatives designed to improve attitudes towards gender equality, disability inclusion, and women’s rights. These efforts will also focus on ensuring that women and girls are fully informed about the services available to them, especially at accessible, nearby service points. Supply-Side Measures:CUAMM will partner with local authorities and relevant clusters to streamline the referral and follow-up systems for survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV). This will help ensure that survivors are connected with the appropriate specialized services in a timely manner. Additionally, CUAMM will produce accessible information, education, and communication (IEC) materials and participate in inclusive awareness campaigns. These campaigns will aim to promote the active involvement of vulnerable groups, including women, girls, people with disabilities, and the elderly. Special Measures for Gender and Elderly Inclusion:Recognizing the disproportionate barriers faced by women and the elderly in accessing services, CUAMM will implement targeted measures to ensure their active participation in the project. This may include specific logistical support, tailored activities, or focused outreach to ensure these groups are engaged and their voices are heard throughout the implementation process. These comprehensive, community-driven efforts will ensure that the most vulnerable populations are not only able to access assistance but can also engage in shaping the services that affect their lives.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

CUAMM conducts thorough analyses to identify the barriers, constraints, and challenges faced by various population groups, which hinder their equitable access to humanitarian services and support. Special attention will be given to understanding the specific obstacles encountered by persons with disabilities (PwDs). To guide interventions, a protection map will be developed based on feedback gathered from communities and assessments provided by other partners and institutions. This map will help pinpoint vulnerable groups and areas where risks could undermine the goals of the project. It will also ensure that resources are used efficiently by focusing on priority issues and locations that need the most attention. In executing the project, CUAMM will adopt a human rights-based and people-centered approach, aiming to address the unique needs of PwDs, as well as those of women, men, girls, boys, and the elderly. This approach will ensure that the interventions are inclusive, respectful of the rights of all individuals, and tailored to meet the specific requirements of the most vulnerable populations. This strategy is in line with best practices for humanitarian assistance, which emphasize equity, inclusion, and participation in decision-making.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed program will minimize its environmental impact by implementing several sustainability measures. These include: Training Healthcare Workers and Community Health Workers: The program will prioritize sensitizing both healthcare and community health workers on the safe disposal of consumables, particularly personal protective equipment (PPE). This ensures that medical waste is handled responsibly, reducing contamination risks. Community Awareness on Hygiene and Sanitation: CUAMM will focus on raising awareness in both host and displaced communities regarding environmental and personal hygiene, sanitation, and disease prevention/control. This will be especially important during mass mobilizations (BM), where large gatherings could increase the risk of outbreaks. Reducing Vehicle Movements: CUAMM will promote the integration of activities to reduce unnecessary vehicle movement, thereby cutting down fuel consumption and minimizing the environmental footprint. By consolidating trips and minimizing the number of vehicles and motorbikes traveling to the same locations, fuel use will be optimized. Fuel Storage and Waste Management: To prevent environmental contamination, CUAMM will closely monitor fuel storage and management, ensuring that any leaks or waste are promptly addressed. Additionally, proper disposal practices will be employed to prevent fuel spills and protect surrounding areas. Collaboration with Health Authorities on Waste Management: CUAMM will work with local health authorities to safely manage medical waste from drug store cleanouts and to implement regular practices to avoid any waste being left in the field. This will help prevent contamination of water sources or livestock grazing areas. By integrating these measures, CUAMM ensures that the project’s activities are environmentally responsible while also safeguarding the health and safety of local communities and the surrounding ecosystem.
