Project Summary

The proposed project will address eye care needs of FDMNs people and service gaps exist in the FDMNs camps area located at Ukhia and Teknaf sub-strict of Cox’s Bazar district. Orbis Bangladesh proposes to provide basic eye care services to those who are living in the temporary camps of these two sub-districts in thereby addressing the immediate eye health needs and Orbis will establish vision centers inside the camp and a mobile unit will work at the camp areas to conduct primary screening of children and adults. The project is for one year and the specific key outputs are as follows:
• 50,000 people screened, 55% will be women
• 9,000 children and adults received medicine and spectacles, 55% will be women
• 1,000 adults and children provided surgical treatment. 55% will be women
• Around 120 frontline health workers and ophthalmic personnel will also be trained.
• Setting up one vision centre at the camp and organize 100 outreach eye screening program at the camp locations.
The project will integrate primary eye care into primary health system at the camp and improve the eye health service delivery at the camp to ensure prevention and treatment of the blindness. The project will be implemented through a coordinated approach and implementing partner will be Cox’s Bazar Baitush Hospital (CBBSH).

Objectives

1) Integrating primary eye care (PEC) into the existing primary health care system by training health staff and equipping all health facilities for PEC in UNHCR camps. This will consequently lead to better access to eye services via increased referrals from health facilities to the vision center and/or the secondary eye care hospital, Cox’s Bazar Baitush Sharaf Hospital (CBBSH). 2) Placing and staffing a vision center eye service facility within the UNHCR camps to provide spectacles and other basic (non-surgical) eye services will reduce vision impairment as spectacles are currently unavailable in UNHCR camps. 3) Designing and managing a robust cataract surgery referrals system and fund cataract surgery as needed for UNHCR camp residents, greatly reducing the number of needlessly blind people.

Needs

Preventing blindness and visual impairment can be critical in refugee populations. There is no epidemiological data on avoidable blindness and visual impairment. Orbis collaboration with SEVA Foundation carried a comprehensive situation analysis and found that there is huge need of eye care services for cataract, refractive error and human resources. This report outlines key recommendations for ensuring a Minimum Initial Service Package for eye care in refugee populations to address the needs. Key need includes: a) Addressing preventable blindness. b) Integrating primary eye care (PEC) into the refugee primary health care (PHC) system. c) Establishing guidelines for eye referrals. d) Providing basic eye services (cataract surgery and provision of eyeglasses) without discrimination, as resources allow. According to the initial findings of Rapid Assessment for Avoidable Blindness (RAAB) 2019 conducted by IAPB in collaboration with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and Orbis International prevalence of blindness is high among FDMNs. Major Causes of blindness are Cataract, Corneal opacity, Phthisis and posterior segment. Uncorrected refractive error is high and spectacle coverage is very low even for presbyopia. The Bangladeshi National Eye Operational Plans call for a cataract surgery rate (CSR) target of 3,000 surgeries per million persons per year. Applying this rate to the refugee and host populations in Cox’s Bazar indicates that over 9,800 cataract surgeries would be needed each year in this district. Making provisional conservative estimates on refractive error and presbyopic correction needs indicates that well over 150,000 spectacles would need to be dispensed in this district each year. Additionally, there are likely to be about 980 blind children in the combined population, with more than 300 of these being cataract blindness.
