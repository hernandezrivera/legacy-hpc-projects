Project Summary

The project intends to expand its provision of integrated community-based and individually targeted protection interventions to conflict-affected populations across Northwest Syria. The Project aims at enhancing the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to the protection needs providing protection, and Child Protection and response services to most vulnerable men, women, boys, and girls, prioritizing critical gaps in life-saving protection services, and designed to be people-centered project supports a self-sustainable protective environment for the most vulnerable IDPs, host communities and returnees in targeted areas. MISK Organization will provide child protection services in schools through 4 outreach mobile teams. The project targets 4000 boys, girls. women, and men through mobile outreach teams covering 17 communities surrounding sites (in coordination with Protection and CP cluster). Community in targeted locations will have access to community-based protection services, including awareness-raising over general protection, Child Protection with adherence to cluster SOPs.
The project will contribute to helping to create a safer and more secure environment for children in targeted locations

4 - Needs

A decade of conflict in Syria is increasing the risk of an unamendable impact on millions of civilians in Syria, the violence and indiscriminate attacks have held their brunt on the local communities, IDPs, and infrastructure. As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance. Deepening poverty continues to fuel harmful coping mechanisms and strain the capacities of families and communities to protect children, particularly adolescent girls and boys: child labour, including its worst forms. Children comprise almost half of the affected population.Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited, and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. MISK conducted a rapid needs assessment in 3 camps ( Atshana, Al-Bayan, Al-Karama) in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin in late April 2021. the main findings were as the following: the total population is 4115, 656 HHs. the number of children above 5 is 1260, children under 5 are 730 and 62 PWD. Regarding vulnerable categories in these communities’ children, elderly, and women were the most vulnerable, families, that headed by women was 2%. The main coping strategies the family’s members used during the last days were child labour, all family member works and depending on aid. IDPs also face a difficult path to rebuilding their livelihoods. Poorer households eating less preferred and less expensive foods as well as limiting portion sizes to make ends meet. Child labour is also quite common.

5 - Implementation of activities

the proposed project will respond to the needs of 4115 individuals, 656 HHs (1120 women, 1005 men, 550 boys, 710 girls, and 730 young children) in three locations ( Atshana, Al-Bayan, Al-Karama) in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts. the project will respond to the most vulnerable internally displaced affected population who rely on bad coping mechanisms as child labour in the top. the Project will contribute to Helping to create a safer and more secure environment for children in targeted locations, Main project activities are 1. Conduct rapid child protection assessments in target locations. 2. Run recreational, psychosocial, and resilience activities in learning spaces. 3. Awareness-raising activities for children, parents, humanitarian workers, and community members on child protection concerns, focusing on the following topics (Child Labour, Family tracing and reunification, Basic CP, Child Rights), using IEC materials, home visits, and advocacy with service providers. 4. Provide parenting skills program for parents. 5. Identification, and referral of vulnerable children for sector-based services based on needs. 6. Training on CP, child safeguarding and Psychological First aid for CP and Education staff. the proposed activities will be monitored through a set of activities including onsite monitoring tools and FGDs, random sample satisfaction surveys, and interviews with beneficiaries. feedback and complaints mechanisms will be also in place including direct WhatsApp line, emails, complaints boxes and direct complaints/feedback to the field M&E officer. the M&E team will consist of male and female employees to avoid any inconvenience with the beneficiaries.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Lack of qualification in protection domain in the targeted area.-Lack of protection services in the targeted area especially the specialized one. -Lack of coordination with other sectors like NFI, Livelihood, and other services regarding referrals. -Dealing with huge needs of beneficiaries especially the new IDPs. -Difficulties in dealing with legal services as it’s a new activity. -Difficulties in targeting Men with awareness-raising sessions.Risk MitigationWorking on providing intensive capacity building plans for the new staff. Setting a service map including the main services to have a strong and clear referral pathway. Working on a flexible timetable to be suitable for all beneficiaries especially men. Select a qualified staff of lawyers to have a good service regarding to legal services.-Loss of cash or assets (operational): This continues to be a threat to all areas of operation inside of Syria - mitigation measures: daily inventory, control the treasury better, involve many people in this process to extend the circle of suspicion.-Unforeseen Changes in the Conflict- Conflict is such that an organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Mitigation measures: Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels, as we as direct reports from staff on the ground. “Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space.
