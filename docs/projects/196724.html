Project Summary

The proposed action seeks to address acute education needs for newly displaced children and youth in Sudan. According to the revised 2023 Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), approximately 8.6 million children require humanitarian support to continue or resume their formal education. Approximately 6.9 million school-aged children (6-18 years of age) were out of school during the 2021-2022 academic year. The project will therefore focus on both formal and non-formal education with alternative learning programmes such as accelerated learning and catch-up to accommodate the needs of both in-school and out of school children (OOSC).
NRC will support a total of 141 026 leaners (50 % female and 5% children with disabilities) with access to inclusive quality education in a safe and protective learning environment. First priority locations in line with Education Sector priority one are White Nile, Gedaref and South Kordofan. Second priority locations North Darfur, West Darfur, and Khartoum will only be supported when the security situation allows for safe access and continued programming.
The action will focus on life saving psychosocial support activities under the NRC Better Learning Programme (BLP), a non-specialized, classroom-based psychosocial support program that aims at equipping the children with strategies to cope with stress and promote their psycho-social well-being. It includes a targeted capacity building for teaching staff and community volunteers to ensure sustainability and promote the psychosocial well-being of learners. Through BLP, teachers and learners are equipped with recreational strategies to cope with stress using practical and adaptable exercises that are integrated into classroom activities and includes a targeted capacity building to teaching staff to ensure sustainability and promote psychosocial well-being to students. Schools will be provided with appropriate recreational materials for both boys and girls, and different ages to facilitate play and entertainment to relieve stress. NRC will strengthen school level child protection initiatives and referral pathways, thereby contributing to resilience building and improved child protection.
The action will also incorporate teacher support and capacity enhancement to improve teacher capacity and contribute to improved teaching and learning. The project will support 1410 (705 male/705 female) teaching personnel (teachers and volunteers). Parent and Teachers Associations (PTAs) and Mother Groups will be targeted to participate in school level decision making and promote displaced girls’ access to education.
Project response components will also include provision of education supplies in the form of teaching and learning materials. The project will also focus on improving the learning environment through rehabilitation of WASH facilities, that is, through construction and rehabilitation of latrines and handwashing facilities. Increased access to safe water will be supported. Gender responsive programming will be promoted through awareness campaigns and community engagement, and 50% of project beneficiaries will be female. Adolescent female learners’ menstrual health and hygiene will be supported through provision of sanitary kits to promote their access to education and retention. School level hygiene awareness campaigns will be implemented to contribute to Personal Health and Hygiene Education (PHHE) and schools will be supported with hygiene kits. NRC will mainstream disability to promote access to education by Children with Disabilities (CWDs). This will be through ensuring awareness campaigns equally target children with disabilities and promoting disability friendly learning environments through ensuring provision of disability sensitive latrines, and training of teachers on inclusive education.

1 - Needs

The prevailing armed conflict which erupted on April 15, 2023, between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Force (RSF) in Khartoum spread to other areas such as Al Fasher in North Darfur, El Obeid in North Kordofan, Nyala in South Darfur, and Kassala in Kassala State, resulting in destruction of critical infrastructure, social facilities, and disruption of service delivery of much-needed humanitarian assistance in multiple areas. The armed conflict and resultant displacement has had a significant impact on education for school-going children. Since the outbreak of the conflict, an estimated 404,000 school aged children have been displaced. This includes 294,000 school going children, 150,000 girls and an estimated 15 per cent of children with disabilities. There is limited data on children living with disabilities to adequately analyse their education barriers and promote an enabling environment for increased access to education. The conflict has resulted in schools being inaccessible for both children and teachers due to the destruction of educational infrastructure, and the displacement of teachers and learners thereby reversing the gains already made in supported schools. This will result in a tremendous increase in the number of out-of-school children, with some children at risk of not re-enrolling after the crisis. Many children have been psychologically disturbed and in dire need of psychosocial first aid to deal with stress and restore normalcy. Psychosocial support will help children to cope with the psychological effects of conflict, such as depression, anxiety, and post-traumatic stress disorder, which can have long-term effects on their mental health. Existing challenges prior to the armed conflict: The school environment is characterized by an insecure environment, hunger, dilapidated infrastructure, limited water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities, lack of trained teachers, lack of female teachers, floods and school occupations. This has resulted in poor learning outcomes, as enrolled learners are faced with a poor education system. On the other hand, out of school children and youth are at risk of exploitation and abuse, child marriages and being recruited into armed forces. Education infrastructure in Sudan compromises children’s access to education and safety. Apart from the poor physical school environment, lack of teachers (both trained and untrained) is prevalent, resulting in high teacher to pupils ratios. Due to limited investment in education, most teachers have gone for long periods without in-service training. Working conditions for education personnel are characterized by poor remuneration, persistent industrial actions, and lack of teaching material. This has largely contributed to poor teaching and learning outcomes, with majority of enroled children having extremely poor literacy and numeracy skills, and progressing through the school system without the requisite basic skills to comprehend increasingly more complex subject matter and effectively participate in learning. Limited social and economic inclusion opportunities for adolescents and youth has exposed them to a myriad of risks, including recruitment into armed forces or groups, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, and child labour, with girls facing the additional risks of early marriage and pregnancy. Structural gender inequality and insubstantial social protection measures have exacerbated disparities between young men and young women/young mothers, making adolescent girls particularly vulnerable if not provided with safe environments such as learning spaces. The action will incorporate a gendered lens to programming to promoting equal access to education for girls (and boys in locations where boys are more at risk of exclusion due to negative socio-economic coping mechanisms).

2 - Activities/Outputs

Education mitigates the psychosocial impacts of conflict by providing a sense of normalcy, structure and hope for the future. Improved access to education in Sudan will convey life-saving information to strengthen survival skills and coping mechanisms. Sudan’s education sector functionality is marred by conflict and crisis. Child protection, psychosocial support, gender and disability will therefore be mainstreamed to ensure an inclusive education system in line with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 4 on ensuring inclusive and equitable education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all. The project will also be implemented in line with the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC). The proposed action will contribute to increased access to inclusive quality education for displacement affected children and youth in a safe and protective learning environment through provision of education supplies and support to education personnel. The action will support displacement-affected children to remain connected, protected, and have the opportunity to learn through Child Education Pathways (CEP) emergency curriculum. NRC will work with the community leadership to set up safe and welcoming spaces where the children can access psychosocial first aid, life skills training, reconnect with peers, and find a safe pathway back to learning. Teachers and community volunteers will be equipped with the skills to provide psychosocial support/BLP and child friendly approaches, including identification and referral of children in need to service providers. NRC will support enrolling and retaining newly displaced children into the formal education system by enhancing the learning environment and enabling the absorption of more children. This will be achieved by providing incentives for volunteer teachers, building teachers’ pedagogical capacities as well as training them on protection topics (i.e., child rights, positive discipline, violence awareness, and prevention, etc.) using the MoE’s Teacher Preparedness Training Program (TPTP). NRC will also provide the schools/ learning centres with education supplies (i.e., teaching and learning materials and classroom kits). Improvement of school infrastructure and WASH facilities will be complemented by the provision of hygiene kits to schools and dignity kits to girls, as well as hygiene promotion activities. Enrolment and retention of children in both formal and informal education services will be supported through community mobilization and sensitization of parents, children, and local leaders. All targeted children and teachers will receive psychosocial support through the use of the Better Learning Programme (BLP), a non-specialized, classroom-based psychosocial support program that aims at equipping the children with strategies to cope with stress and promote their psycho-social well-being. It includes a targeted capacity building for teaching staff and community volunteers to ensure sustainability and promote the psychosocial well-being of learners. NRC will also provide targeted psychosocial support to the front-line education staff (teachers and volunteers) so that they can be effective and maintain their well-being while carrying out their duties. This will include capacity building focusing on self-awareness and regulation techniques to deal with stress including breathing and relaxation exercises as well as recreational games to improve well-being and reduce stress in open air settings. The action will target 70% displaced children and 30% vulnerable resident children

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The action will mainstream environment protection in infrastructural construction and rehabilitation. Construction and rehabilitation of latrines, school hygiene campaigns and Personal Health and Hygiene Education (PHHE) will significantly contribute to a reduction in open defecation, thereby protecting the environment and water sources.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

An area level protection risk assessment was conducted to contribute to safe programming. Protection risks analysis will be conducted in selected schools, with the participation of local communities and PTAs. The project incorporates child protection to strengthen children’s capacity to demand their rights and contribute to their own protection, thereby reducing children’s vulnerability to exploitation and abuse. Through training of teachers on inclusive education, the project aims to protect girls and children with disabilities’ right to education. Psychosocial support will also be provided to learners and teachers to contribute to teaching and learning outcomes, improved mental well-being and resilience to future shocks. The action will promote inclusion through creating disability friendly learning environments to protect children with disability from stigma.
