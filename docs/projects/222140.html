Project Summary

Akobo county is located in the north eastern corner of Jonglei State, and borders Nyirol, Uror, Pibor and Pochalla counties in Jonglei State, Ulang county in Upper Nile State to the north, and Ethiopia to the east. Akobo is the county headquarters of Akobo county, which is in the Greater Upper Nile Region of South Sudan. It`s about 450 kilometers (280miles) northeast of Juba, the capital city of South Sudan.
The terrain in Akobo is predominantly swampy, with few distinguishing high points; elevations range around 410 meters above sea level. Rivers include the Gilo River. According to the Atlas of the Ethiopian Rural Economy published by the Central Statistical Agency (CSA), around 10% of the woreda is forest.
The economy of Akobo is predominantly agricultural. There are no agricultural cooperatives, no documented roads, and little other infrastructure. Along with Jekow, Akobo becomes flooded during the rainy season, requiring the people to migrate to the highlands with their cattle until the waters recede; thus raising livestock is the primary source of income in this woreda.
It is clear that the current political crisis coupled with the current flooding and economic crisis in the county has eroded the development efforts from both the government and non-governmental organizations. This has been notable with the setback on infrastructure development, livelihoods and government’s ability to provide services and or implement public policies especially education policies among others to increase access to services by ensuring the citizen have all they need to be safe and protected. As a result, the human resource that has been instrumental in all sectors of lives has also been demoralized due to devastating economic crisis, displacement, inadequate services, un-enabling learning & protection environment, limited access to basic services such as education, Protection, health, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene. Therefore, an inter-sectoral intervention is highly anticipated to address different needs among the target population. In schools and school community, the need for mainstreaming protection, health, hygiene, sexual reproductive health and Gender based violence across different education activities. This approach will help in reconstruction and restoration of dignity, hope and livelihoods of the returning IDPs including cattle keepers.
The most affected group by the flooding are children aged between 3-17 years, Adolescents, youth and Elderly as all sources of livelihoods were all swept away including classrooms leaving the few who are attending classes under the trees in High lands or in the Government administrative units. There is a risk of these children and youth losing more days and turning to criminal gangs & cattle raiders in the communities if not engaged on education and other activities relative activities.
The main objective of this intervention is to ensure that women, men, girls and boys have safe access to basic quality education, this is so relevant in the restoration of hope through multi-sectoral activities. In addition, the provision of integrated services among the IDPs, returnees and the hosts will enhance and promote peace, social interaction and cohesion in and around the communities.
The target groups and beneficiaries anticipated to be reached by the proposed programme are 5, 979 beneficiaries disaggregated as below:

-Children aged 6-17 years =3451 (2076 boys)
