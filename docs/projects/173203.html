Project Summary

NRC will establish a Digital Community Hub (DCH) which will serve as a two-way communication platform with people of concern. The DCH will create bulk and targeted communication channels via SMS and VOIP technologies which will ensure displaced people can better meet their basic needs through increased access to information and services. Information distributed through the platform will include life-saving and life-improving awareness-raising messages as well as information on how to access services. Similarly, people of concern will be able to ask questions, raise emergency alerts, communicate their needs and obtain referrals for services, and voice complaints or feedback via the hub. The platform may also be used to register people of concern for e-voucher smartcards and to distribute multi-purpose cash assistance to meet basic needs identified through the hub. Short surveys and rapid assessments may also be facilitated using the hub.
The primary target group are the 1.44 million internally displaced people living both in camps or camp-like settings as well as out of camps, in South Kordofan as well as North and Central Darfur. The specific localities for intervention in the Darfur region will be determined by upcoming assessments to be conducted in the two states. The DCH will be accessible to all conflict-affected IDPs in the targeted areas who have been individually registered with a phone number. This includes women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities. Indirect beneficiaries include all members of the targeted household including children. The secondary target group are local and national authorities and the broader humanitarian community in Sudan who will benefit from improved communication channels with people of concern.

1 - Needs

In light of the Covid-19 pandemic, communicating with communities (CwC) has become increasingly difficult with frequent lockdowns and limitations on traditional face-to-face communication modalities. In Sudan, the virus comes at a time when 1.8m internally displaced Sudanese people live in camp or camp-like settings with most of these lacking centralized CwC or information management systems. The country continues to face the health and humanitarian consequences of COVID-19. Since the start of the pandemic in mid-March, the government confirmed that over 14,000 people contracted the virus, including more than 1,100 who died from the disease, as of 12 November. In addition, displaced people are susceptible to malaria, cholera, dengue fever, chikungunya, and other novel diseases. Sudan is facing its first polio outbreak in more than a decade, affecting nine states across the country. The increasing number of outbreaks and transmissions continues to put pressure on the country’s fragile health system. In the face of these challenges, risk communication platforms are urgently needed which can reach and are accessible to vulnerable women, men, youth, and persons with special needs. In particular, women, youth, and persons with disabilities tend to face unequal access to information due to cultural and physical limitations. Despite the tremendous efforts being invested in peace, a number of challenges continue to exacerbate the fragility of communities across the southern regions and Darfur. Moreover, in the wake of the changes taking place in the country and the uncertainties of the transitional period, new conflicts are emerging in an environment where resources and opportunities are already under stress. Inter-communal conflict is a recurring pattern, which continues to displace people and claim lives in some parts of the country. Such conflict affected persons are in need of communication channels to raise or receive emergency alerts and communicate with humanitarian actors. In 2021, the CWG will increase efforts to advocate and scale up the use of Multi-Purpose Cash (MPC) as a modality to respond to humanitarian needs, enable the environment for coordination, and harmonization. However, technical challenges remain in scaling up this modality including the lack of data collection tools, assessments and non-food market monitoring mechanisms which can inform interventions across all partners. The digital community hub will serve as an additional data collection tool to inform coordination as well as support the distribution of multi-purpose cash to meet the widespread humanitarian needs of men, women, boys and girls of all ages and abilities. Analysis from the recent MSNA confirmed gaps on accessibility of information due to elements of age, gender, and other diversities. Humanitarian partners have in the past used varied methods to promote accountability and maintain a two-way communication with communities, although inconsistently and in isolation. There is a need for a systematic approach to collecting, analyzing and responding to complaints and feedback to identify issues that require attention at the strategic level. The digital community hub will allow NRC and possibly other local actors to receive and respond to complaints in a manner that will be accessible to men and women of phone-usage age of diverse abilities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The Digital Community Engagement and Assistance project responds to the informational and communication gaps of displaced people in Sudan, the opacity of service delivery and coordination, and the weak recognition of displaced people's agency in addressing their own priorities to meet basic needs. Specific activities will include the following: - Establish and run multi-agency digital communication with communities platform including a complaint and feedback mechanism as well as a service referral mechanism. - Disseminate life-saving and life-improving awareness messages and information on humanitarian services via SMS and VOIP technologies - Create informational products on community settlements and community feedback for dissemination to the humanitarian community- Support the AAC/CE Working Group with regular briefings based on data and information received through the digital community hub- Distribute e-voucher smartcards for service delivery tracking- Distribute multi-purpose cash to meet basic needs via direct cash distribution through banking services or via vouchers

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Before project implementation the project will undertake a gender sensitiveassessment that will consider different information needs and communication channel capacities of men, women, boys, and girls, and of specific groups that may be vulnerable, marginalised, or discriminated against. The project design prioritises participation of vulnerable groups such as women andpersons with disability ensuring that they are able to access services and assistanceand removing any barriers that may prevent full and effective participation. The project itself aims to ensure the voices of people of concern are heard and responded to, and that information reaches those most in need.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NRC will co-chair and participate in the AAP WG and its task teams, which will collaborate closely with the PSEA network. As such, the digital community hub will be used to regularly collect feedback from people of concern which will be shared with the AAP WG. Additionally, NRC will participate in the mapping of existing complaint and feedback mechanisms and support the development of guidelines which can be used by partners in the establishment of their own mechanisms.
