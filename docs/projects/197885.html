Project Summary

This project aims to address the emergency and urgent needs of vulnerable populations in the northwest of Syria, to response and support the GBV risks in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. Through WGSS and ambulance units centre in Azaz city, Afrin city, Idleb city; GBV services are to be provided to community members in selected locations, as well as referral services through the ambulance system that is functioning in selected locations in Aleppo and Idleb governorate.
To strengthen its response to the complex and multispectral needs of survivors. The GBV intervention in this project aims to "Survivors of GBV and individuals at risk of GBV have increased access to safe and confidential GBV services through 3 WGSS.
The GBV staff in will target women, girls in particular, including women and girls who live with disabilities, and also the elderly women.
Violet will ensure to conduct a risk assessment and analysis to understand the safety and accessibility of the services and develop clear and targeted recommendations and action points while establishing GBV services. Also Violet will strengthen privacy and confidentiality for all women who come for health services through infrastructure improvements. Violet will use GBV Case Management Standard Operating Procedures including GBV CM tools, Data Protection Protocols, and update the GBV service map that is available by the GBV Sub-Cluster in NWS and update the relevant referral pathways for multi-sectoral support in the operating area. Violet will do refresher training on the already existing protocol on CMR and ensure compliance with it, for Doctors and Midwifes and other humanitarian workers are already exist in the targeted location. Also, ensure/improve educational materials available to women and girls on topics related to GBV in the WGSS and other facilities. Vıolet will collaborate with the GBV SC to participate in the case management coordination efforts to benefit from inter-agency capacity development opportunities and already developed tools related to case management and data management.
1,685 women, girls, and men will be targeted by Violet GBV intervention within 3 WGSS, starting from train non-GBV staff on GBV core concepts and safety referral. Whereas the GBV staff will be trained on Community-based Protection, PSEA & CSG, and PFA, and in-depth cases management to GBV cases workers and PSS counsellors, targeting a total of 30 staff members.
GBV Case Workers will provide specialized GBV services to survivors and the at-risk cases based on survivor-centered GBV Case Management to a total of 300 women and girls cases.
Violet MHPSS counsellors will provide cultural and age-specific individual and group mental health and psychosocial support sessions targeting GBV survivors and at-risk cases in a total of 1285 women and girls.
Violet Young Mother Club facilitators will provide YMC cycles and sessions for women and girls to address the holistic needs of pregnant adolescents and first-time mothers, targeting 240 women and girls. Moreover, 120 women and community committees members will be trained on GBV basics and Safety Referrals, and Enhancing the community acceptance and support for GBV issues and needs. In addition to response to more than 50 survivors by CVA and small grant opportunities through empowerment sessions and business skills training and coaching. The priorities are for the high-risk cases and also based on the type of the violence and the roots of the problem to ensure ending or reducing the violence against women ad girls survivors and/or at risk-cases. The GBV survivors who living with disabilities and elderlies will be also included in the priorities.
Violet will use the GBV SC M&E Toolkit to 2nd edition 2021 to monitor and evaluate the implements and reports in line with the inter-agency guidance.

3 - Needs

Based on the GBVIMS+ third quarter report 2022, GBV disproportionately affects women and girls: 100% of reported incidents involved female survivors. The decreased % of PWDs this quarter: PWDs still have significant challenges accessing GBV services, with 2.9% of survivors being persons with disability. Perpetrators are primarily people who belong to survivors’ first circle of relation: 63% of reported incidents occurred at the survivors’ home, and 95% of alleged perpetrators are people who belong to survivors’ first circle of relation (Intimate partner/Former Partner, Primary Caregiver/Parent, Family other than spouse or caregiver). IDPs continue to be the population most accessing GBV services: 84.4% of reported incidents involved IDP survivors. The most common forms of GBV experienced by survivors are physical Assault (37.8%) of reported incidents, followed by psychological/emotional Abuse (27.6%) and Denial of Resources, Opportunities or Services (26.5%).The Voices from Syria 2022 report describes that Sexual violence continues to affect women and girls within and outside their homes. Forced and early marriage remains a threat for girls as young as 10 years old and is linked to the family’s economic situation as well as to caregivers’ wishes to protect girls from sexual violence by marrying them off. Women who are widowed or divorced are exposed to continuous psychological violence by both their families and the broader community.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements.Potential risks:1) Misconduct of staff against child safeguarding principlesMitigation: initial and refresher training on CSG responsibilities, behavior training for staff, assign focal point for CSG, child-friendly AAP sessions including CSG information and how to raise complaints, zero tolerance on misconducts and rapid action after investigation.2) SEA misconduct from project staff to parents or childrenMitigation: provide information sessions to parents (sex-segregated if preferred) on expected behavior from staff and humanitarian workers, explanation of complaints mechanism, availability of AAP staff face-to-face for sensitive complaints.3) Request of SEA for selecting BNFs to the servicesMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasize on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative. Violet will conduct FGDs/KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitor


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Border crossing between Turkey and Syria remains open for humanitarian assistanceRisks: Indiscriminate attacks on civilians and health infrastructure (IED,VBIED). Intensification of the conflict resulting in shifting frontlines, mass population displacements or drastic changes of the context. Exodus of professional staff to address the risk of instability, contingency plans are made and include predominantly the emergency evacuation of staff and equipment and shifting the services to where the community moves. Security team carries out regular security assessments of facilities and closely monitors developing security situations to determine if facilities remain open and continue to provide full range of services or limited emergency services during periods of increased hostilities.Assumption: The COVID-19 situation is still increasing Risk: Access loss to GBV servicesMitigation: Violet is addressing by improving the hygiene and wash protocols and materials in the facilities. Decrease the number of beneficiaries in the group sessions, and keep the social distance in both group and individual sessions. Resume the activities through an online approach in-link with all data protection protocol and respect confidentiality, with providing internet cards and use additional resources if needed by other projects, sectors, and donors. Keeping the donor updated with any additional fund required.
