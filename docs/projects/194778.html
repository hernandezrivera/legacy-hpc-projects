Project Summary

UNICEF’s WASH humanitarian response will focus on the provision of and sustaining access to lifesaving gender responsive basic level water supply and adequate & equitable sanitation facilities and promoting personal and environmental hygiene for 3.6 million most vulnerable people affected by the massive SAF/RSF and other armed conflicts, public health epidemics and natural disasters. The project geographic focus will be Khartoum, North, South, East, West and Central Darfur, South, West and North Kordofan, Blue Nile, White Nile, Sennar, Geziara, Kassala, Gedarif, Red Sea, River Nile and Northern States.
UNICEF’s WASH humanitarian response will focus on:
-Provision of and sustaining access to lifesaving basic level water supply for 3,500,000 affected population.
-Provision of and sustaining access to adequate emergency sanitation facilities for 280,000 affected population.
-Promoting personal and environmental hygiene for 3,600,000 affected population.
-Distribution of hygiene promotion supplies for 180,000 affected population
-Building communities' resilience to WASH related recurrent shocks with emphasis on community empowerment of women, girls, young people and people with disability.

1 - Needs

As per the revised 2023 HNO, 14.1 million people need humanitarian WASH interventions (compared with 11.5 million assessed before the SAF/RSF armed conflict). Of these, 11.6 million people are in need of basic level water services, 12.6 million people are in need of adequate & equitable sanitation, and 14.1 million people are in need of hygiene services. The target populations are mainly those impacted by massive SAF/RSF fighting and other armed conflicts, epidemics and natural disasters (e.g. water scarcity and floods). Out of the 189 localities in Sudan, 151 are classified as localities of critical water scarcity with high susceptibility to natural hazards, particularly flooding and prolonged dry spell. Women and girls are more susceptible to GBV while fetching water and/or defecating in the open. Similarly, young, elderly and people with disability have challenges in accessing services. These groups are often ignored or not fully involved in community-based decision making and management processes.The revised 2023 WASH Sector objective is to increase access to humanitarian WASH services for 6.1 million of the most in need people. Of these, 5.1 million will be targeted with basic level water, 911 thousand with adequate and equitable sanitation facilities and 5.2 million people with hygiene promotion interventions with major expansion towards the urban setup in which most of the SAF/RSF fighting are taking place. Special attention will be given to building resilience to WASH related recurrent shocks in affected communities. Capacity building will focus on community empowerment and inclusive services to women, girls and young people with a disability.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Expected Outputs: - Access to lifesaving basic level gender-responsive water supply increased and sustained for 3.5 additional unserved emergency-affected populations with focus on climate change mitigation and adaptation. Of that, 3.0 million will be supported with water trucking, water disinfection and operation and maintenance support for their existing water sources and 500,000 will be provided with new or rehabilitated durable water sources. -Access to gender-responsive and disabled-friendly adequate sanitation facilities increased and sustained for 280,000 additional unserved emergency affected population. - 3.6 million emergency-affected people reached with behavioral change focused hygiene promotion interventions with focus on handwashing with soap. Of them, 180,000 will receive hygiene promotion critical supplies.- 20,000 children in 50 schools and 22,500 visitors of 30 health/nutrition centres will be provided with packaged gender WASH services.- Affected communities’ resilience to WASH related recurrent shocks built with emphases on community empowerment and women, girls and young people enhanced enablers. Activities: Deliver coordinated and integrated gender-responsive lifesaving SPHERE standard WASH services for the affected population and education and health institutions at the hot spots’ areas, existing/new displacement, borders and transit zones to ensure availability and continuity of sufficient quantity and quality of WASH survives as follows: 1- Basic Water Supply:-Water trucking and provision of local water storage and distribution systems-Operation and maintenance support for of existing water facilities and networks-Water disinfection support at water sources, water transportation means and household levels-Rehabilitation and construction of water facilities-Empowering affected populations using Community Management for Operation and Maintenance (CMOM) approach with focus on women, girls, and adolescents. 2- Adequate Sanitation:-Construction/rehabilitation and management of gender responsive (separated, locked and nearby) emergency latrines.- Community mobilization for household latrines construction using Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) approach for the affected population in the protracted emergency areas within focus on women, girls and adolescent’s contribution. 3- Hygiene Promotion:- Conducting interpersonal hygiene promotion interventions with focus on hygiene promotion campaigns, household visits, development and distribution of BCC culture friendly materials .- Procurement, prepositioning and distribution of hygiene promotion supplies as a part of the above overall hygiene promotion interventions, mainly soap, jerricans, hygiene kits and MHM supplies.-Support solid waste management interventions.4- Coordination, technical assistance, capacity building and monitoring:- Strengthening WASH Sector humanitarian coordination forum at federal and state levels with focus on the coordination and harmonization of WASH sector planning, advocacy, implementation, monitoring and resource mobilization.- Activation and strengthening evidence-based M&E and IM systems with gender responsive information using up to date, innovative, monitoring tools. -Institutions and human resources capacity building for sector partners with focus on managerial, technical, equipment, logistic and motivation capacities.- Building the affected communities’ resilience to WASH related recurrent shocks with focus on ensuring suitability of the provided WASH services and community empowerment and management packages for the aim and requirements of the targeted population to cope with the existing and potential challenges with focus on women, girls and young people.
