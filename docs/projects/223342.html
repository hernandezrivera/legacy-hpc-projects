Project Summary

This project will benefit 90,000 ind (15,000 households) in target areas that are experiencing humanitarian crisis in the prioritized counties of Bor South, Akobo East, and Pibor (Jonglei State), and Rumbek Centre (Lakes State), where recurrent acute shocks, especially localized violent conflict or flooding, tend to cause large scale acute displacements within or into these geographies, or where there are large numbers of returnees – driving dire humanitarian needs for these populations. In response to assessed needs of returnees or households displaced by specific acute shocks in these areas, CRS will target acutely displaced or newly returned households, applying vulnerability-based targeting criteria to identify those with the most severe needs, with an aim to prevent morbidity and mortality among those with elevated vulnerabilities (e.g. PLW, CU5, PWD, etc). CRS will provide one-off cash transfer at 100% of the CWG- recommended MPCA transfer value (average figure of $165 used for budget estimation), to provide purchasing power to access life-saving food and other essential needs (shelter, clothing, NFIs, hygiene supplies, medicine and medical services, etc). CRS will coordinate to complement other emergency interventions, particularly food, livelihood, or cash-based assistance interventions, especially those programmed by World Food Program (WFP), IOM (or RRM partners), UNHCR, or FAO.
Based on CRS’ recent experience in target counties, multi-purpose cash is the most preferable intervention because: 1) Markets are sufficiently elastic and safely accessible to meet essential needs 2) Cash is strongly preferred by local communities. 3) Local leaders support use of cash. 4) Participants are already familiar with and using SSP currency. 5) FSPs are available and have capacity to safely deliver cash quickly in target areas. 6) Cash is cost effective and logistically easier than other interventions. Per recent rapid response analysis by CRS, cash-in-hand via third-party over-the-counter delivery mechanism remains the most appropriate, safe, and cost-effective way to implement cash in these counties at this time, and many organizations are now implementing cash-based assistance in the area. Providing cash will support local markets and traders, will promote beneficiaries’ dignity, encourage their agency, and allow flexibility to meet most urgent needs. CRS’ experience (and evidence globally) shows that beneficiaries will prioritize their most urgent needs, while also investing where possible in assets or businesses that will support their food security, self-reliance and increase their resilience to future shocks. Providing cash will also contribute to protecting resilience gains, and encourage early recovery of livelihoods through restoration of productive assets and income.
CRS prioritizes cash and market-based modalities where appropriate in emergency, recovery, and resilience-building interventions. CRS has extensive expertise implementing cash-based assistance (conditional, unconditional cash and voucher programming) in South Sudan, utilizing our Cash and Asset Transfer (CAT) platform and a network of independent Financial Service Providers. Since 2020, CRS has transferred over USD $29,000,000 in cash and voucher-based assistance with funding from USAID, EU, and SSHF among others. A global leader in cash programming, CRS is an active member and hosts the coordinator of the Collaborative Cash Delivery (CCD) Network, and is also co-chair of the South Sudan Cash Working Group. The project will benefit from CRS’ strong relationships with key stakeholders, established presence in the target communities, and strong understanding of each local context.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation for displaced or returnee households and hosting communities in Bor South, Pibor, Akobo East, (Jonglei State) and many areas of Lakes State is severe: characterized by food security crisis and emergency conditions driven by large numbers of IDPs, an influx of returnees (soon to reach 900,000 per IOM/UNHCR), persistent inter-communal conflict, macro-economic challenges depleting purchasing power, five consecutive years of flooding, severe degradation of livelihoods due to recurrent shocks over multiple years, and limited agricultural production. Recurrent and overlapping shocks, particularly localized violent intercommunal conflict and and climate-related shocks including extreme flooding, regularly drive displacement events within or into these counties. Per the Intersectoral Prioritization (Nov 2024), all target counties are ranked Priority 1 or 2 for response (counties surrounding Rumbek Center such as Cueibet, Rumbek North which feed IDPs to Rumbek town are prioritized), indicative of the overall humanitarian crisis in these counties. Within this crisis, displaced persons, returnees, and hosting communities face severely elevated needs compared to other populations; , facing hunger and dramatically elevated risks of malnutrition and communicable diseases, they are often completely dependent on humanitarian assistance for food and other life-saving needs. Children, vulnerable adults such as pregnant or lactating women, and persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected. Target areas have been severely affected by flooding for five consecutive years. In 2024, flooding has affected estimated 1.4Million people, including at least 200,00 in this project’s target areas (OCHA Flooding Snapshot 15 Nov). South Sudan has experienced three civil wars and decades of civil war and multi-level, violent conflict, including local inter-communal violence, due to conflict over resources and ethnic rivalries. Routine revenge killings and cattle raiding displaces communities, forcing them to concentrate in smaller locations with limited access to essentials like clean water, hygiene services, or health and nutrition services. Per the latest IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (Round 15 Sep 2024), Akobo East and West host 14,872 IDPs and 18,792 returnees; Bor South - 86,665 IDPs / 66,303 Rtrnees; Pibor – 30,301 IDPs / 21,404 rtrnees; Lakes State: 115,760 IDPS / 90,760 rtrnees. However, these figures likely undercount many returnees from Sudan or Gambella and those displaced by flooding. There is every reason to anticipate severe emergency throughout 2025: conflict, flooding, drought, and other emergencies will drive displacements alongside continued returns from Sudan; with compound impact on hosting communities that already face humanitarian crisis, while those displaced or returning will face extreme vulnerability and be in need of life-saving assistance to access even the most basic food, shelter, NFIs, and essential services. Widespread need for assistance will continue throughout 2025; it is essential to scale up response to acute displacements to save lives, alleviate suffering, and ensure protection and dignity of the most vulnerable. Based on CRS’ recent experience, multi-purpose cash is the most preferable intervention. Primary markets (Akobo town, Bor Town, Pibor, and Rumbek Towns), and some secondary markets, are functional year-round with re-supply by road or boat, are accessible safely to all vulnerable groups, and have essential life-saving items available to meet multi-sector needs. The primary markets have been consistently rated 50% or higher on JMMI. While assessment and analysis is needed for every acute situation, based on our extensive history programming conditional and unconditional cash at scale in these counties, CRS is confident that in most emergency situations in most times of year, market assessment would bear out the appropriateness and feasibility of cash in these locations.

2. Project activities

In response to assessed needs driven by acute shocks, CRS will provide multi-purpose cash to support life-saving multi-sectoral needs of 90,000 ind. (15,000 households) from crisis-affected populations in target locations. CRS will prioritize one-off cash transfers to vulnerable displaced or returnee populations. In some circumstances, based on assessment data, CRS may advise for multi-month support, or for inclusion of some host community members, but such situations would be anticipated as exceptions. Key activities: STAKEHOLDER AND COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT. CRS builds on a strong set of established relationships and entry points; CRS will develop a wholistic community engagement strategy to ensure strong 2 way communication for transparency and clear expectations on all sides. This includes active and passive feedback mechanisms developed in consultation with communities, and regular engagement meetings (or FGDs) with diverse representative groups of community members as well as leaders and key stakeholders. NEEDS ASSESSMENT: preferably IRNA or coordinated assessment with other humanitarians, RRC, and local communities. GENDER ANALYSIS, PROTECTION RISK ASSESSMENT AND CONFLICT ANALYSIS – building on previous assessments and analysis, and ensuring strong consultation with diverse groups in the affected communities. MARKET ASSESSMENT AND RESPONSE ANALYSIS. Primary data collected will be triangulated with secondary data, particularly the JMMI, WFP data, and ISNA data around market functionality and access. TARGETING: CRS will use a community-based process with clear vulnerability criteria. CRS will prioritize acutely displaced or recently returned households. CRS’ has extensive experience in target areas conducting transparent, vulnerability-based targeting processes that prevent manipulation. CRS will consider de-duplication with other actors implementing cash, food or other relevant sectoral assistance at the local level – to include de-duplication with WFP beneficiaries if appropriate and in accordance with data protection policies of CRS and other actors. REGISTRATION: CRS will use its Cash and Asset Transfer (CAT) system for registration and beneficiary management that improves risk mgmt, monitoring, and responsiveness. CASH TRANSFERS: Pending response analysis of a specific emergency situation and local context, CRS envisions cash-in-hand via third-party over-the-counter delivery mechanism to be the default option. CRS anticipates one-off emergency transfers, with transfer values figured at 100% of the CWG recommended MPCA value stated on the JMMI, which is based on the MSSMEB (est. $165). CRS will consider adjusting this to harmonize with other cash actors in the area with the guidance of CWG. POST-DISTRIBUTION MONITORING: CRS conducts PDM through a randomized household level survey of participants 2-3 weeks after each distribution; this includes questions related to gender and protection concerns. MARKET MONITORING: CRS conducts independent market monitoring before, during and after cash programming. CRS will utilize the JMMI triangulated with field reports to monitor markets and ensure cash remains feasible, appropriate, safe, and dignified. Based on price trends, CRS may adjust transfer values as needed. SAFE AND DIGNIFIED PROGRAMMING: CRS has a strong commitment to the centrality of protection informed by communities’ priorities. CRS adheres to “Do No Harm” while considering unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls. CRS centralizes protection and conflict sensitivity, integrating robust comprehensive gender, protection, conflict sensitivity, and PSEA integration measures such as: consultation with diverse groups; active and passive monitoring and feedback mechanisms; targeting women and vulnerable groups for inclusion; training of staff and all stakeholders; locating service points to reduce risk; comprehensive community sensitization and engagement.
