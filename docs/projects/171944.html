1 - Needs

Entering its seventh year, the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine affects some 5.2 million people, of whom some 3.5 million require humanitarian assistance and protection. Even after six years of active hostilities, there is no immediate resolution to the crisis in sight. Most conflict-affected people live in the Donetska and Luhanska oblasts, which are “divided” by a 427-kilometre-long “contact line”, where active hostilities take place on a daily basis. As of the end of July 2020, there have been 3,367 civilians killed as a result of the conflict activities since 2014, with more than 7,000 injured. The COVID-19 pandemic and the measures imposed to contain the spread of the virus have impacted all of Ukraine, further exacerbating humanitarian needs in eastern Ukraine, and deepening the vulnerability of the conflict-affected population. Prior to the closure of the “contact line” in March 2020, there were an average of 1.1 million crossings of five checkpoints each month – over half of which were made by elderly over 60, who cross to access pensions, social benefits, basic services and maintain contacts with their families or relatives. As of August 2020, despite the partial reopening of three checkpoints in June, this population remains unable to receive pensions or social services, causing increased humanitarian needs among the most vulnerable population in non-Government-controlled areas (NGCA) of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts. The humanitarian situation is marked by: (i) people who are immediately affected by the crisis – particularly those who are living along the contact line, who face the daily risks of shelling and who have lost access to most services and support, as well as those no longer able to access pensions, social benefits, and administrative services; and (ii) people who are indirectly affected by the crisis – as a result of diminishing or limited livelihood opportunities – either because people have been cut off from productive centres by the ‘contact line’ or as a result of the economic impact of the crisis, now exacerbated by COVID-19. The elderly are disproportionately affected and account for 30 per cent of those in need, which is the highest proportion of any crisis in the world. The higher rates of disability and immobility among the elderly make them more vulnerable, including to economic insecurity. The situation is aggravated by administrative hurdles they face when accessing their entitlements, especially pensions. As conflict continues, civilians live in constant fear, and suffer from heightened psychological distress, especially children and youth. Some 1.3 million conflict-induced internally displaced persons (IDPs), whose average monthly income (US$92) is still well below the subsistence level (US$128), still require sustainable solutions.
