Project Summary

The proposed project aims to enhance agricultural production, food, and cash for work of affected households and communities in NWS, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities. The proposed interventions are four interventions:
1-Supporting the wheat value chain;
2. Distribution of free bread in the camps
3. Providing 320 CFW opportunities within the agricultural services provided to wheat farmers.
4. Providing 200 grants to 200 women who head families to establish income-generating projects related to agricultural projects and food processing
4200 farmers will benefit from the distribution of wheat inputs, including seeds, fertilizers, pest control materials, and wheat marketing
Divided into 1000 farmers from whom 2000 MT wheat is purchased from the agricultural season 2022-2023 and 3200 farmers to whom seeds, and agricultural inputs are distributed for the 2023-2024 season
320 cash-for-work workers will be selected to implement activities related to firefighting and integrated pest management
SADAD intervention will be aligned with the wheat crop calendar and improve the purchasing power of 1000 households. 2000 tons of wheat seeds will be purchased; 800 tons of wheat will be used as seeds for the next season’s planting, after sifting and sterilizing. The other 1200 tons of purchased wheat will be supplied to the contracted mill to be ground, and, as a result, 960 tons of flour will be obtained and supplied to the bakery.
Consequently, bread for 5000 households can be produced and distributed free of charge for 6 months as of the end of the wheat harvesting in June 2023.
200 women will be selected as heads of families, training will be provided to increase their efficiency, and then 200 grants of $1,000 each will be provided to establish income-generating projects.
The activities carried out on the sites will be divided according to the following
1- Distributing free bread to the residents of the camps in Jandires and Afrin, where all camp residents will be targeted with free bread for a period of six months.
2- As for the farmers targeted by value chain support, they are from Idlib farmers, in all the governorate aspects specified in the locations (Idleb Al Ma'ra. Harim. Jisr-Ash-Shugur and Ariha ) where the beneficiaries of the activity will be selected according to specific criteria
- Farmers who do not receive support from other organizations in the current or previous season
- Farmers who have experience in wheat cultivation and have stopped cultivating it as a result of the economic and climatic situation have priority
- Farmers who do not have a stable source of income and have people from vulnerable groups among their family members
- A woman heading a family
- Displaced families who can access land by renting
- Farmers whose possession does not exceed 10 dunums
3- Cash-for-work workers will be selected from places close to farmers, for easy access, according to specific criteria
- The beneficiary should be a displaced person in the project area for a period of more than 6 months
That he does not have a fixed source of income
People with mild disabilities have priority, provided that their disability does not affect the implementation of the work
4- For the 200 grants, 200 women will be selected as heads of families within Sheikh Hadid and Raju According to the criteria:
- A woman heading a family
- Displaced for more than 6 months
- There is no fixed source of income
The beneficiaries will establish centres for food processing and small home gardens for field cultivation according to their residence status between camps or villages.

3 - Needs

SHO conducted Wheat value chain assessments in proposed locations. The main purpose was to get a better understanding of the respective domestic agricultural value and supply chains and the challenges that smallholders are facing along the chain.When the assessment was issued, 87% of wheat farmers suffer from difficulty in securing their needs for agricultural inputs due to the scarcity of some materials or their high prices, in addition to that 63% of farmers expressed difficulty in marketing their wheat products.Also,90% of the respondents indicated that women in the Afrin district are unable to secure their monthly needs of food, medicine and clothing, which prompts them to send their children to work in order to improve their living conditionsThe HNO2023 has shown that " The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population, the result shows a 7% increase in the PiN from last year. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of sliding into food insecurity, The alarming increase of people at risk of shifting to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels, is of major concern to the sector"

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sadad has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. Sadad is an active member of the PSEA Network and will collaborate with the PSEA network to support quality implementation in the project. Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers. Mitigation: Sadad ensures to have in a place an accessible mechanism for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse in line with Sadad safeguarding policy. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Risk: Lack of understanding of employees about PSEA issues. Sadad understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, con dent and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about Sadad's position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children.
