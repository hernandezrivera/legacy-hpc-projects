Project Summary

In response to the identified needs of the crises-affected population in South Sudan, DRC will deliver this protection-oriented project aiming at enhancing the protective environment for women, men, girls and boys in South Sudan through facilitating access to services in an efficient and timely manner while strengthening community-based protection initiatives. DRC will implement protection activities across the country, including in hard-to-reach areas, through static, semi-static and mobile modalities. In doing so, DRC aims to contribute to the overarching intersectoral strategic objectives in addition to the protection cluster strategic objectives.
The proposed activities will ensure access to critical services and promote self-reliance through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of the targeted communities and build their resilience to respond to new shocks and crises. During the project, emphasis will be put on coordinated responses through engaging with multiple coordination mechanisms at the national and sub-national level and humanitarian actors on the ground.
The target locations are country-wide, with the emergency responses being activated following the recommendation of the Protection Cluster and the emerging needs.
The proposed components are general protection, GBV, PSS and HLP. General protection activities aim at improving informed and evidence-based programming through regular assessments and analyses which will be shared on a need-to-know basis with partners and clusters to facilitate evidence-informed programming and advocacy.
In addition, GBV prevention and response activities will be implemented in compliance with the minimum standards for GBViE aiming at prevention and responding to GBV and contributing to addressing the root causes of human rights violations.
Under PSS, DRC will implement structured and unstructured PSS activities including the establishment of temporary WGFS, group sessions and engagement of men, women, boys and girls in games and sports activities to help people meet their psychological and social needs due to shocks. Additionally, PFA pieces training and PSS awareness sessions will be conducted to enable people to identify signs of stress for support and referrals.
Under HLP, activities aim at improving access to and knowledge of HLP rights through awareness-raising, information sharing, and supporting the establishment of referral pathways.
Moreover, protection responses will be implemented through a variety of modalities, including cash and in-kind assistance in the form of Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) or Multi-purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA). Cash will be provided based on a risk vs. benefit analysis assessment and on a case-by-case basis to mitigate, prevent, or respond to a protection risk.
Overall, DRC will ensure mainstreaming of PSEA and disability inclusion throughout the project cycle from design to evaluation stage. Awareness raising for the communities and stakeholders on their rights, training for the staff on the afore-mentioned topics and setting up confidential age and gender sensitive complaint mechanisms and inclusion of people with disabilities in decision-making processes will be among the activities carried out to ensure mainstreaming.

1. Needs

During 2024, the dire humanitarian situation in South Sudan driven by the effects of years of conflict, climatic shocks, public health challenges, food insecurity and intercommunal violence continued to deteriorate. The situation was exacerbated by the conflict in Sudan that broke out on April 15, leading to the displacement of an estimated 5.79 million people within and outside Sudan in neighboring countries, including South Sudan, further straining scarce resources, and increasing protection risks, particularly for the most vulnerable groups. As of October 16, the estimated number of arrivals in South Sudan was 317,993 Individuals were recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict. Overall, 91,36% were reported to be returnees, with the majority being women and children. The conflict in Sudan has had a notable impact on the scarce resources within the country and trade between Sudan and South Sudan decreasing the imports from Sudan, which contributed to an increase in the cost of the food basket.According to the HNO 2023, 9.4 million people- including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children- were projected to need humanitarian assistance, representing an increase of 4 percent from 2022. Out of this number, 6.1 million were estimated to need protection services including services for gender-based violence (GBV), which is a prevailing issue in South Sudan, ranging from domestic violence to sexual violence in conflict, placing the country second in GBV prevalence rate in East Africa. Women and girls remain at significant risk of gender-based violence while children and adolescents continue to face inequitable access to education, with 2.8 million children (52 per cent girls) out of school due to socio-cultural norms.While the humanitarian actors continue to provide humanitarian assistance and despite the high need and reliance on these services, physical impediments such as poor infrastructure, lack of roads, flooding, in addition to funding constraints are severely impeding the capacity of humanitarian partners to respond to the emerging needs of the displaced population and the host communities. The country is susceptible to multiple shocks and unaddressed root causes of needs put an emphasis on the significance of designing and implementing humanitarian responses.Additionally, according to HRNP 2024, prolonged conflict, insecurity and climate change have had profound impacts on vulnerable populations in South Sudan. The Protection Cluster's Protection Risk Assessment (PRA) conducted in August 2023 highlighted that GBV is a pressing issue with alarming prevalence rates. The 2023 ISNA findings also underscored the urgency for interventions focusing on GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response. Children face significant risks of violence, exploitation and abuse due to conflict, forced recruitment, displacement, family separation and limited access to education, nutrition and health care. Explosive hazards covering an estimated 16 million m² of land — particularly in Greater Equatoria, Upper Nile and Jonglei — pose a significant threat, limiting access to crucial humanitarian services. In 2024, conflict and severe climate events, including flooding and drought-like conditions, are expected to trigger additional displacement. The upcoming national election will likely bring a risk of election-related violence and instability. Conflict in Sudan has negatively affected South Sudan's economy, resulting in higher food and fuel costs and risks for sub-national violence. Diminishing humanitarian funding may further hinder access to vital services, exacerbating the existing vulnerabilities.

2. Project activities

DRC will implement a comprehensive protection-oriented project focused on general protection and GBV providing direct service provision and referral through static and mobile modalities. The overall aim of the project is to reduce the likelihood and impact of protection risks by reducing the vulnerabilities of the population and the threats they are facing while enhancing the capacities of the communities and local stakeholders.Under general protection, rapid protection assessments, audits and protection monitoring will be conducted to identify protection risks faced by persons of concern and analyse the trends in order to identify the prevailing vulnerabilities, threats and capacities. In addition, protection desks will be established to facilitate access to information and the safe identification and referral of people with specific needs employing updated service mappings and referral pathways. In total, 10,000 individuals with protection concerns will be reached with protection awareness raising and 1000 will be identified and referred to services through static teams, while 5,180 individuals will be reached with CBIs and non-cash-based interventions.The main activities under the HLP component will include monitoring to identify prevailing HLP concerns, recovering lost land tenure documents in collaboration with the relevant ministries, in addition to awareness-raising and basic counseling and referral. DRC will strengthen/establish 2 dispute resolution mechanisms and train 50 local authorities and relevant institution's staff on HLP, dispute resolution and ADR. In total DRC aims at reaching 1,000 women, men, girls and boys through HLP awareness raising and outreach activities.GBV response will focus on the provision of comprehensive case management in women-and girls-friendly spaces (WGFS) employing GBVIMS+ and the provision of cash and in-kind support. Moreover, a range of PSS activities, such as individual and group counselling and recreational activities, will be conducted in WGFS, aiming at enhancing mental health. In total 3,000 women and girls will benefit from specialized services provided at WGFS and 500 women and girls of reproductive age will receive dignity kits. Furthermore, PSS will be mainstreamed into all activities through awareness raising on topics revolving around mental health, building the capacity of the staff and stakeholders on Psychological First Aid (PFA) and the provision of individual and group PSS services targeting 1000 individuals. To ensure self-reliance and sustainable solutions, livelihood activities will be presented in the safe spaces to enhance 200 vulnerable women’s access to income- generating activities and livelihood skills. In order to strengthen GBV risk prevention and mitigation, 1000 women, men, boys and girls will be targeted for awareness-raising sessions on GBV and available services, while 360 individuals will be reached through GBV prevention initiatives through curriculums such as SASA and EMAP. Moreover, community-based protection and localization will be promoted by strengthening the capacity of 6 community-based structures (women networks and CBPNs) through the provision of training and material support, in addition to training of 100 non-GBV humanitarian workers (including local actors) on GBV and GBV mainstreaming and integration in their respective sectors. DRC will continue its advocacy efforts and coordinated responses by engaging with the established coordination mechanisms at the national and sub-national level.
