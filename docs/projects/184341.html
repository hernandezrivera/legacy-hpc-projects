Project Summary

The extended crises in the target areas, resulting from natural and man-made disasters have disproportionately affected women and girls including those with disability. Due to the displacements, social structures have been broken and women and girls’ support systems disrupted, exacerbating women and girls’ vulnerability to shocks and risk of violence both at home and in the community. Drivers of GBV in Somalia include pervasive insecurity, increased vulnerability and displacement—linked both to conflict and climate-related disasters—and deteriorating social and customary structures. Displaced women and girls are among the most vulnerable populations—to extreme poverty, marginalization and conflict and climate-related shocks. IDPs in Somalia are also particularly vulnerable to violence survivors of GBV were female IDPs. IDPs face unique risks of GBV and sexual exploitation due to limited security in the IDP settlements, poor living conditions and limited clan protection. The attendant separation of many women and girls from community and familial support structures, as well as from traditional livelihoods activities, also contributes to an increased reliance particularly of women on marginal, inconsistent, and hazardous livelihood strategies, which often increases exposure to violence.
Generally, GBV is one of the areas that is underreported in conservative Somalia community. It is widely understood that this was not just as single factor that caused it but multiple factors that include cultural orientation, fear of stigmatization, lack of confidentiality, loss of reputation, fear of unknown, poorly defined laws and policies among others. these risks to violence extend in part as women and girls travel long distances to find water, food, livelihoods, and other resources, particularly for IDPs traveling outside formal or informal settlement areas. Women and girls traveling in route to or from IDP settlements, often in search of economic and other support services, also confront serious risk of violence. This link further aligns with global evidence that highlights the extent to which external shocks like natural disasters exacerbate gender inequalities and exposure to violence. Increases in violence are attributed, among other factors, to increased trauma and stress in the aftermath of disaster, as well as losses in economic opportunities and livelihoods particularly for men both in the short- and longer-term. Furthermore, male members of families continue to migrate with livestock to more fertile regions, leaving women and children behind unprotected and increasing their vulnerability to GBV.

1. - Projects activities

CARE will undertake protection prevention and response activities through the GBV AOR and CP AOR as highlighted below:1. Establish physical support centres (safe spaces for women and girls including those with disability): GBV experiences leads to psychological wounds that are harder to heal than bodily injuries Survivors of GBV may have intense negative feelings in the outcome or long after the violence, most of the survivors experience severe emotional disturbance during the violence struggling with anxiety, flashbacks, and trust issues they express instant behaviour change feeling, guilty, shame, isolation and loneliness that leads distress and negative thoughts which likely interferes with daily life functioning or even a bodily sensation such as pain or fatigue anxiety. 2. Community awareness creation on protection issues: The project will increase community engagement on GBV prevention and will host community structures, including community based prevention networks (CBPNs), community leaders’ councils, gatekeepers, and psychological first aid volunteers (PFA, to develop action plans on community mobilization and community sensitization to reduce GBV risks for women and girls. CARE will also continue sensitize community and religious leaders through forums on GBV to encourage them to apply their power and influence in a positive way to promote the safety and wellbeing of women and girls, and support them to access services if they experience GBV. 3. Survivor centred approach to GBV Case Management along with Distribution of dignity kits: This is a multiple response activity which is in line with CARE’s priority (case management, including referral, action planning, and development of safety plans, etc.) and IASC minimum standards (Minimum Standard 6: GBV case management). CARE will provide GBV response services to the survivors of SGBV including case management, psychosocial first aid and psychosocial support, distribution of dignity kits and clinical management of rape among others. The response services will be offered from the women and girls’ safe spaces which are one stop spaces for all CARE GBV services in the community and where a service is not available; referrals are made to other agencies (service providers) in the referral continuum. 4. Case Management for Vulnerable Children to meets their unique needs: CARE will provide case management services to address children and their family’s needs in an appropriate, systematic and timely manner through direct support and/or referrals. CARE will establish Child protection ambassadors and child safeguarding focal persons who will be based in the villages and remote areas of the target district to simplify referrals of the vulnerable children to the help centres. Identification, registration and referral will be done with confidentiality to ensure vulnerable children and/or survivors receive targeted and comprehensive services at the earliest possible moment through identification of risk factors or abuse. 5. Psychosocial support services provision to children, adolescents and caregivers: The project will engage case workers, Mental Health Specialist and Child Protection manager to continually compromise child development, children’s overall health and well-being especially during violence, situations of insecurity and instability of crisis ranging from armed conflicts to natural disasters. The project aims on preventing and reducing of the negative impact of crisis situations on the emotional well-being of children and their families. 6. Parenting support programs: The project will promote positive parenting activities for appropriate parent-child relationships in a way that parents can simply raise their children with discipline and good moral values. Parents will be trained with skills of identifying and accessibility of resources free from stress to avoid maltreatment and unintended neglect to their children.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

During international days, like the 16 Days of Activism campaign, CARE will collaborate with other actors, including the relevant government ministries and local organizations, to mobilize and sensitize the community on the consequences of GBV, referral pathways and available GBV services including case management, clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), psychosocial support), all offered by CARE, and legal aid and safe shelters offered by other protection actors. The project will engage youth networks for accountable practices to address gender, power and the role they can play as agents of change as allies in GBV prevention by providing guidance and mentorship. The project will employ community based approaches that build on existing structures like community based prevention networks (CBPNs) composed of men, women and youth, together with community and religious leaders, CARE will develop and implement survivor-centered practices when dealing with GBV cases, facilitate monthly meetings, establish community protection mechanisms based on the specific risks women and girls face and establish awareness raising action plans for increased coordination of GBV response and encouraging coping mechanisms that foster women’s resilience and address protection issues and concerns. Community and religious leadership will actively be involved in our programing to ensure safety for women and girls and those with disability in the communities. The project will deliberately target minorities so as to ensure there is no exclusion
