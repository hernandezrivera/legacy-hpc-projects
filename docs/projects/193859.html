Project Summary

Through this project, and in compliance with the WASH Cluster Objectives “Improving access to adequate WASH services for the most vulnerable communities in Area C (HRP SO 1 & HRP SO 2)”, UAWC aims to ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient drinking domestic water services in accordance with dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Qusra in the West Bank. The initiative has been endorsed by the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) and it is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) enabling a strong collaboration among local and regional stakeholders in-line with and beyond the central management of the PWA.
The project will directly benefit 1,151HHs representing 5,418 Individuals; among them 1,483 men, 1,461 women, 1,265 boys, 1,209 girls, and living in West Bank, - Nablus, Qusra.
The activities will include: 1) Conduction of a rapid assessment in order to verify the exact needs, security situation and expected objectives of the project; 2) Technical survey for planning and design; 3) Bilateral meetings and field visits with PWA and local institutions; 4) Joint Field visits with the Qusra municipality and the joint village council to the proposed reservoir construction site; 5) Conduction of a post survey, in the final phase of the project, to measure effectiveness and impact of the implemented intervention, establishing also a baseline for any other required future interventions, considering opinions and feedback of all segments of society besides women, men, girls, boys and elderly; 6) Conduct the hydraulic model study and design of the reservoir construction plan, this will include defining the accurate bill of quantities for constructing the reservoir such as land leveling, fittings, water pumps, carrier line and civil operations; 7) Install and construct Water reservoir of 250 m3 capacity for domestic drinking and install the needed carrier lines and connect it to the water network, this will include: land leveling, fittings, water pumps, carrier line, civil operations to connect to the main water network line; 8) Handover of the work between UAWC, Qusra municipality, Joint Services Council PWA and the
contractor.
The initiative aims to complement and support the WASH cluster response plan in its application. All the proposed activities have been coordinated with Joint services council and Qusra Municipality that will support the action through the organization of logistics and the direct relationship with the beneficiaries to reduce the risks and impact of accidents related to settler violence, excessive use of force by of Israeli forces.

1 - Needs

The WASH sector in West Bank is marked by a chronic vulnerability that predates the emergency of May 2022. The Israeli restrictions against the WASH sector’s development, climate change, and local authorities’ limited capacities contribute to the sector’s severe fragility. According to the 202 Humanitarian overview, more than 1.6 million Palestinians in the West Bank suffer limited access to WASH services. Many communities and households were reported at that time to have little or no resilience to any crisis that could affect their access to sufficient WASH services. Therefore, the report says, “crises could shift them to a severe vulnerability that could threaten their lives”. Currently, 178,000 people living in Area C are identified as vulnerable due to the lack of access to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). In June 2021 UAWC conducted a rapid needs assessment in the most prioritized vulnerable communities in the Governorates of Nablus. Based on this assessment Qusra was selected in coordination with WASH cluster team and PWA. The need assessments result show that there is an urgent, humanitarian need in the targeted area which is suffering from the with poor access to safe water. Stakeholders analysis has been carried out with Water joined council for east and south Nablus Governorate and municipality and other active organization. Qusra is a Palestinian village in Nablus Governorate, and classified as area B and C. Qusra village is surrounded by four settlements and has been exposed to aggression and many other difficulties created by the occupation. Most of Qusra lands of are located within areas B and C. Qusra is considered one of the villages that suffers from repeated attacks by settlers on its citizens continuously and throughout the year, in addition to the policy of preventing access to the lands. The area of lands in the town of Qasra is 12,000 dunums, 8000 dunums are in Area C. The findings showed that 100% of the houses in Qusra are connected to the water network, and are supplied with water through the Joint Services Council and the Palestinian Water Authority. There is a shared water reservoir with a capacity of 3000 cubic meters in Qusra that supplies it with drinking water, in addition to 8 other villages, where water is pumped to Qusra every 10-14 days. Where the reservoir is 1,000 meters away from the main water network, and due to the nature of the village and the varying altitudes, specifically the elevated Kreck area, suffers from water scarcity, especially in the summer, water is not available on a daily basis in the village, and there are 1151 HH in Qasra, inhabited by 5418 citizens, The population suffer from insufficient water access, as the water reaches the households in the summer every 14 days, and in the Creek area is the most scarce for domestic water due to its great height and its location in a high mountainous area, which is the highest point in Qasra, the water reaches the area once every 14 days, and the residents of the area are forced to buy water tanks at a price of 20 shekels per cubic meter, and this constitutes an additional burden on the families living in the area, knowing that the Kreck area is the only area in which the people of Qusra can expand urbanely, as All other areas are surrounded by settlements on all sides.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle UAWC ensure protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. The partners consider safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. Overall UAWC seeks to complement its own actions with for instance legal support to inform risk mitigation measures especially in Area C and consequently to increase complementary humanitarian action. For activities that include physical interventions (including constructions of animal shelter and roads, water pipes rehabilitation), UAWC will coordinate with the relevant legal entities to evaluate how this intervention will affect the legal status of the targeted communities. UAWC will do the hard construction works during Fridays and the holidays of the ICA to attract minimal attention from them, using low visibility implementation and coordinating with beneficiaries (especially animal shelter rehabilitation) to cover the new structures while ensuring that the new works will not change the overall appearance of the communities. In order to inform its assessments and implementation UAWC will continuously work on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, UAWC believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. UAWC has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All UAWC staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. UAWC has in place focal points at base and mission levels. as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, all staff received specific in person training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UAWC has conducted Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation. Particular attention was paid to the way UAWC includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of UAWC core values, UAWC thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. UAWC aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, UAWC focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, UAWC started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform UAWC’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, UAWC is increasingly using ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. UAWC is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, UAWC usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, UAWC started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. UAWC has safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanisms in place which allow beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. Beneficiary knowledge of this system are promoted during field assessments and monitoring visits during which UAWC and partners staff explain how the mechanisms work. With regards to access to assistance and in this specific project the partners will 1) Use their respective data and knowledge of the areas to prioritize in a transparent and relevant manner more at need communities 2) Consult widely with community members, community leaders and MoSD, MoL and MoA directorates 3) Call for applications with a fair competition mainly for the income generation activities to select most relevant and promising business to be established and access business ideas based on criteria made public.
