Project Summary

The project aims to improve the food security situation for the most vulnerable families in Aleppo and Idleb covernorate by providing live-saving humanitarian food assistance for 7000 families (42000individuals: 9009men, 9471women, 11540Boys, 11980girls) That will be achieved through two inventions:
1-Distribution of regular food assistance for 1000 vulnerable families (6000 individuals: 1287 men, 1353 women, 1649 Boys, 1711 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates while each family will be supported with monthly food baskets for continuous 8 months. and that will be through 2 modalities:
a- cash modality through restricted e-vouchers with value of 65 USD to address their food needs (500 families/3000 individuals : 644 men, 677 women, 825 Boys, 854 girls)
BINAA will prepare a vendor map and provide the selected vendors with devices and training on how to use these devices. in addition to conducting orientation sessions on using e-voucher cards for the beneficiaries.
b- in-kind modality (500 families/3000 individuals: 644 men, 677 women, 825 Boys, 854 girls) the e-voucher modality provides beneficiaries with the freedom to choose from a variety of healthy food items.
This food baskets will cover at a minimum 70% of the daily kcal intake which is around 1550 Kcal per person per day in a monthly family food ration. Calculation will be made on a reference family size of 5/6 for a period of 30 days.
2-Free bread distribution (The essential food of the Syrian families) for 6000 vulnerable families (36000 individuals: 7722men, 8118 women, 9891Boys, 10269 girls) on a daily basis for continues 6 months in Idleb and Aleppo governorates while each person will receive 330 g of bread per day based on the FSL cluster recommendations BINAA will contract with the closest bakeries to the targeted areas and supporting them by the sufficient flour and the operational cost in order to produce the needed amounts of bread, then distribute it to the beneficiaries on a daily basis. BINAA will take the COVID-19 precautions in all project phases starting from beneficiary selection till the bread distribution according to BNAA COVID-19 Guidance
We are targeting both males and females in this project including all ages. We will also take the elderly and the people with disabilities through the project implementation while we will deliver their portions to their houses
For Bread, the distribution will be blanket distribution in the targeted camps.
For the food baskets: the beneficiary's criteria will be as the following:
1- Disaster-affected households
2- Internally displaced (IDP), refugee household (HH), or recent returnee HH
3- HH with persons with disabilities (PWDs)
4- No income or lowest HH income per person in the village (total HH income/# of people living in the HH)
5- Female-headed households
6- Child-headed households
7- Families with multiple children under 5 years of age
8- Labor-poor HHs (HHs in which there is no one who can work)
9- No food stocks or HHs with recorded malnutrition
10- HHs not covered by other interventions (cash, food aid, etc.) from another agency
BINAA implements rigorous quality control measures throughout bread production, conducting laboratory tests on flour received from suppliers to ensure high standards. Bread specifications are stipulated in bakery contracts, aiming for top-notch quality. Moreover, BINAA assigns bakery supervisors to monitor daily bread preparation, guaranteeing adherence to best practices and maintaining consistent quality across operations.
For cash transactions, we impose restrictions on the items vendors can sell to beneficiaries. Additionally, we hire supervisors to be present in shops during working hours to ensure high-quality items and delivery services.
For in-kind transactions, we establish stringent quality specifications for items and conduct tests on all received items from suppliers to ensure they meet contracted specifications before distribution to beneficiaries

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The food security situation is worsening, with 15.5 million people needing food and agriculture assistance in 2024, including 12.9 million who are food insecure and 2.6 million at risk. The project will include the following activities:1- Supporting 1000 households by food assistance through in-kind food baskets and cash for food using restricted E-vouchers for 8 months, through (Community engagement, Update the prices, Preparing the vendor map, Contract with E-voucher service provider (cash) and food baskets provider (in-kind), Ibenefeciery selection, Distribution of the vouchers (cash) and the food baskets (in-kind), Supervising the voucher redemption process) 2- Supporting 6000 households by free bread distribution for 6 months, through (Community engagement, beneficiaries' selection, Contracting flour providers, contracting with bakeries and providing them with the flour and operational cost in order to prepare the needed amount of bread, Distribution of the bread to the targeted families) 3- Conducting awareness campaigns against COVID-19 and Colera.4- M&E activities including monitoring the distribution process, and conducting baseline, end-line, and PDM visits. BINAA will receive referrals from other organizations and sectors for vulnerable HH to be added to the beneficiary lists. * GBV Risk during distribution and by field workers. Mitigation: distributing earlier, closer to homes, segregating distribution by gender, selecting field teams carefully, and establishing accessible complaint mechanisms. In case of exploitation, actions include suspensions, referrals for psychological support, and investigations by BINAA.* SEA Risks during project implementation. Mitigation: Integrate PSEA into BINAA policies, ensure personnel adherence to the code of conduct, include PSEA clauses in contracts, Appoint a PSEA officer as the focal point for prevention strategies, collect and analyze risk factors, ensure visibility of PSEA materials, receive and track complaints, and share PSEA-related activities with relevant personnel.* Changes in security or authorities in project areas. Mitigation: Regular monitoring, assessing access security, exploring alternative locations.* Attacks on vendor shops. Mitigation: Daily reporting, temporary suspension, changing locations if necessary.* Fraud in redeeming vouchers. Mitigation: Using E-voucher system, daily monitoring, publishing complaint mechanisms, MEAL team oversight.* COVID-19 and Cholera. Mitigation: Organizing distributions to prevent gatherings, promoting social distancing, displaying awareness posters, conducting information sessions
