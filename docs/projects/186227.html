Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in the Southwest region of Cameroon has exposed the local population to large scale protection risks and human rights violations and impacted vulnerable households’ capacity to meet their emergency food needs. Through this Action, the IRC seeks to improve the protection status of vulnerable people affected by the crisis in North West and South West Cameroon, assisting the most vulnerable people to stay safe, access assistance, recover from violence and claim their rights. The IRC will do this through an integrated program of Protection prevention and response activities to mitigate further protection risks resulting from the use of negative coping mechanisms.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Conflict-affected populations in North West and South West are forced to leave their homes and experience specific forms of deprivation, and an increase of protection risks due to the exacerbation of vulnerabilities, the disruption of social dynamics, the reduction of capacities to cope, and the increase of external threats linked to the high concentration of populations and potentially of armed forces. This violence peaked in 2018 and led to a massive displacement of the population. There are more than 347,542 people (female 56%, children 62% and 9% elderly person) are displaced within the two regions. Of the 845 villages assessed in the North-West, more than 59% of the villages undergo pendular displacement, while of the 737 villages assessed in the South-West, more than 29% undergo pendular displacements . Most IDPs are in shock and in need of access to basic services and restoring of a protective environment. People affected by the NWSW crisis need protection and assistance in Cameroon. Conflicts and violence are having knock-on effects, hindering access to social services and challenging humanitarian access and the delivery of humanitarian aid.According the Protection Assessment conducted by the IRC in September 2020, threats, movement restriction, robberies/looting and most villages burned have been identified as the main protection incidents. The proposed action is in line with the gaps and needs identified, with the protection response of DG ECHO, in particular the prevention of gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA); the priority for HRP's NWSW of improving effective prevention and access to protective assistance, including psychosocial, medical and legal support; and put in place coordination and communication systems to enhance awareness. As of August 2021, the crisis in the North-West and South-West regions has caused the displacement of an estimated 119,995 girls and 95,085 boys within those two regions. Adolescent boys and girls, children with specific needs and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) remain the primary categories at risk among all affected groups of IDPs, returnees and affected host communities, especially in rural areas and hard-to-reach areas.
