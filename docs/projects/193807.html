Project Summary

The purposed action aims to ensure the quality of life for children (boys and girls) with cancer aged 6-11 years in the Gaza Strip by promoting protection, wellbeing and access to education and learning support in Gaza Strip. The project will target 250 cancer patient children (boys and girls) and their families as well as service providers including teachers and psychosocial counsellors. The targeted children will be identified from the data information system supervised by Ministry of health and Ministry of Education.
The proposed project is in line with the education cluster HRP strategic Objective 2 – Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children through the following activities/interventions:
1- Provision of essential rehabilitation to schools to ensure that school environments are inclusive and safe for students and teachers.
2- Support cancer patient children including CwD to access education services.
3- Provision of learning support programs (remedial education and catch-up activities) to vulnerable dropout children, students suffering from cancer disease.
4- Psychosocial support and outreach for children
The proposed intervention will contribute to long-term change by improving the educational standards cancer patients in the Gaza Strip. In order to reach the long-term change for the target group, the proposed project will ensure that these three main changes at the intermediate level:
• Increased enrolment of girls and boys who are cancer patients in educational facilities and reduced social barriers and gender inequalities in HHs, schools and community.
• Maintained/institutionalized provision of integrated and organized inclusive services among local state and non-state actors
• Increased provision of inclusive educational supportive services for girls and boys of cancer patients
As well as four main changes at immediate levels:
• Improved equitable access to educational services for girls and boys of cancer patients
• Raised awareness and responsiveness of women, men, girls and boys in how to deal with cancer patient children
• Enhanced coordinated and integrated efforts of relevant stakeholders
• Strengthened capacity and roles of CSOs.
Target Beneficiary
The main targeted population are the child with cancer related diseases The second group are children in the age range of 6–11-Year-old. IRPAL will engage the community CBOs in identifying the children with cancer in cooperation with ministry of health in order to avoid leaving any child behind and ensure that they benefit from this service.
Furthermore, IRPAL will provide the adaptive material for the children with disabilities to ensure their full participation in the project activities. In addition, the project will take into account the different needs of the children according to their sex and age which will be provided through their participation in needs assessments.
IRPAL has already considered these needs in the designing phase where the children with cancer related diseases were assessed through interviews and focus groups which have been conducted with all relevant stakeholders – providing IRPAL with a deep understanding of their requirements.
The proposed intervention is planning to address a core problem in the Palestinian community: “Cancer patient Children have unequitable access to right to education”. The project will contribute in addressing the main causes of the core problem like:
• lack of equitable access to educational services
• Absence of coordination and integration amongst relevant stakeholders
• poor capacity and roles of CSOs
Through effective and efficient components:
• Increased equitable access to educational services among F/M Cancer patients Children
• Enhanced coordinated and integrated efforts of relevant stakeholders
• Strengthened capacity and roles of CSOs
The proposed intervention will address a critical problem among girls' and boys' cancer patients.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip faces a chronic humanitarian crisis, impacting the livelihoods and access to essential services by its two million residents. This crisis has been driven by over 15 years of closure and an unsolved internal Palestinian political divide. The dire socioeconomic situation has been worsened by the COVID-19 emergency which has further exacerbated vulnerabilities. According to the recent statistics revealed to (HNO 2022) that the high rate of unemployment, and lack of economic opportunities, unemployment has reached 44.7 per cent and poverty almost 60 per cent. The education system’s capacity in providing quality education to all children remains a major and protracted challenge in Gaza Strip according to the 2021 HNO. Added another Significant health crisis in increasing the non-communicable diseases in Gaza Strip. Cancer is considered one of the most dangerous diseases and a global killer. According to the WHO, it is one of the leading causes of mortality globally. The number of cancer cases among children under 18 years old was 685 new cases between 2016 – 2020, i.e. 7.7% of total cases. (MoH 2021) Children with cancer are living a difficult reality due to their recurrent treatment which causes a repeated school drop-out that affects patient children's academic achievements that leads to decrease their motivation toward learning. Additionally, some patient children drop out of school because they are not able to go to school every day as normal children which cause a significant low academic achievement According to humanitarian needs overview (HNO 2022). children with and without disabilities are negatively impacted in their access to education due to humanitarian situation in Palestine. In fact, 96 % (around 556,000) of the people in need of education (74 % in Gaza, 26 % in West Bank). According to MSNA 5.7% of school-aged children reportedly dropped out of school during the current school year (OCHA 2021-2022), low achievement is given as one of the main reasons for dropping out of school As mentioned in the mapping study for cancer care in the Gaza Strip by Islamic Relief issued in 2021, there are gaps in service provision such as the lack of integrated and effective coordination mechanisms among the service providers gaps, the study finds gaps in medical supplies, diagnostic facilities, lack of well- organized referral system. Based on the IRPAl mapping study of cancer care in the Gaza Strip study findings, the poverty rate among studied families reaches 81.1%. Only 10% of patients live in rural areas, 26.7% in refugees' camps, and 68.3% in urban localities. Rapid need assessment report: Focus groups were conducted by IRPAL and CTCCM in close collaboration with the educational centers were established through the project of enhance the education right for patient cancer children which has been implemented by CTCCM and IRPAL, this assessment aimed at identifying patient cancer children (girls and boys) needs, circumstances and requirements in order to find solutions for the continuation of the educational process and the accessibility of education for cancer-patient children. The main findings show that Children with cancer face multiple forms of discrimination, leading to their low level of schooling. And lack of adaptation of the educational system, which includes infrastructures, materials, , stigmatizing and discriminatory attitudes of educational staff. This project is to support protected access to schools, reintegration of dropout students into school system through strengthened remedial/catch-up education for Cancer patient Children who have unequitable access to right to education, The project will contribute in addressing the main causes of the core problem like: lack of equitable access to educational services, absence of coordination and integration amongst relevant stakeholders and poor capacity and roles of their caregivers and service providers. It is expected that improving their grades in these core subjects will reduce their risk of dropping out of school.

A1: Provision of essential rehabilitation to schools to ensure that school environments are inclusive and safe for students and teachers. A2: Support cancer patient children including CwD to access education services 2.1 Providing assistive devices, safe and inclusive transportation, specialized and adapted learning materials, activities to address violence and bullying, 2.2 Facilitate the access of patient children and their families for treatment outside Gaza Strip (West Bank hospitals/Israel) by providing safe and appropriate transportation. 2.3. Implement awareness-raising workshops about daily good healthy routine for patient children. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

This project is protection and inclusion oriented, and specifically designed through the lens of P&I to promote equitable access to educational rights for F/M children with cancer related diseases. Safety, dignity and rights will further be promoted through the participation, engagement and empowerment of target population (F/M cancer patient children and their parents) in all project phases starting from the needs analysis, so that the diversified requirements and needs of persons of all genders, age and abilities are identified and well considered along the project lifetime. This will:• Maintain rights to justice and equitable access to services through the well planned satisfaction surveys and 2-way feedback with participants and ensure that their gender, age and diversity requirements are satisfied through relevant adaptation and accessibility planning and ensuring the adapted service. • Disseminate and implement planned awareness and induction activities on safeguarding policies (child safeguarding, PSEAH, gender justice, whistleblowing, complaint and feedback response mechanism CFRM, people-at-risk, dignity at work) amongst project staff, partners and vendors, supported with commitment statements in all contractual agreements • Empowering participants with their rights and entitlements, as well as with all safeguarding policies and mechanisms, through planned awareness and induction activities.• Implement SMART complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) plans targeting project staff, partners, participants and vendors, and sensitize them to the standards and ethics of conduct, their rights and responsibilities, as well as all to the tools and mechanism in an effort to raise/escalate/report safeguarding and other entitlements/commitments rights within the complaint response mechanism.
