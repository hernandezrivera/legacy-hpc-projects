Project Summary

This project is designed to respond to the urgent needs of the targeted groups in relation to the poor WASH facilities and services. This project will provide adequate safe access to water for 10664 Palestinians living in Area C in the West Bank in Umm at Tut- Jenin, Beit Liqya-Ramallah, Khirbet Yarza and Ibziq-Tubas individuals; among them 2329 young girls, 2707 women, 2443 young boys, 2768 men, 417 elder, and 167 PWD. Thus reducing the vulnerability of these four communities located in Area C and who do not have access to adequate WASH services. The targeted beneficiaries of these communities are currently suffering from lack of access to clean and adequate water and hygiene education that in turn resulted in poor economic status. The project will address these key needs which form the basis of all development through participatory, inclusive and effective means.
It is worth mentioning, that the project intervention will benefit indirectly 17334 individuals in 3 village at Ramallah governorate namely; Kharbatha village, Beit Ur At-Tahat and Saffa villages and 2 at Tubas governorate namely; al Maleh and Al Burj community.
The project is designed to provide comprehensive intervention to improve the quality and access of WASH services in the four targeted communities through integrating water supply, facilities, and mass WASH education as a package, and community participation. The project main activities: 1) improving water quality of communal water sources through installation of chlorine-dosing pumps and maintenance works, 2) installing and rehabilitating of water transmission pipelines, 3) Conduct a training course (capacity building) for the community hub members regarding the WASH best practices, and 4) Conduct awareness sessions for the targeted local community.
Since this project is comprehensive, it gives special attention to WASH education among the community members. The project will adopt a participatory approach by establishing a community-based WASH hub that will be responsible for spreading WASH messages to their communities. The community hub members, will receive a training of trainers (TOT) on WASH practices to create transformative change on best practices in their own communities.
This project will be implemented by the Applied Research Institute, Jerusalem (ARIJ), and in partnership with the Union of Agricultural Work Committees (UAWC), and in coordination with the local village councils, and Palestinian Water Authority to reap the benefits of their input and experience in the field. In addition the project team will coordinate with other active local grassroots and organizations working in the related fields.
This project is considered an urgent humanitarian project and part of ARIJ and UAWC Response Plan, and in line with WASH Cluster HNO 2022 and WASH 2022 HRP “WASH Cluster Specific Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians” and the PWA plans. The proposed interventions will result in improving the vulnerable population in the four targeted localities in the West Bank their access to equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed WASH services.

1 - Needs

Approximately over 1.35 million Palestinians across the oPt are affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and will be in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2022 (HNO 2022). According to PWA, Israel controls more than 85% of the water resources in the West Bank. The Israeli Military Order 158 prevents Palestinians from constructing any new water installation without first obtaining a permit from the Israeli Occupation authorities. Therefore, PWA is purchasing 20% (84 million M3) of the Palestinian bulk water supply from the Israeli water company Mekorot. This limited access to water resources reflected in a clear discrepancy of average water consumption per capita in the Palestinian communities in the West Bank (85 l/c/d) and the Israeli settlements in the West Bank (300 l/c/d). In Area C (which represents 60% of the West Bank) communities depend on Israel’s planning system, however, the application process for a construction permit has an extremely low success rate. As described by the UN Secretary General, these restrictive and potentially discriminatory planning policies deny communities access to affordable and safe water, including drinking and domestic water, and water for basic hygiene and sanitation facilities, including toilets, sewage networks, and cisterns. Currently, 178,000 people living in Area C are identified as vulnerable due to the lack of access to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). Moreover, and since the beginning of 2021, 30 demolition incidents against 51 Palestinian WASH structures have occurred, displacing 266 people including 146 children and affecting 794 people. This destruction of WASH infrastructure represents an increase of 40 per cent, compared to the same period in 2020, and an increase of 300 per cent in terms of people affected. In August, 2021, The building inspector of the Israeli Occupation Civil Administration raided Khirbet Yarza at the Jordan Valley, and notified a water line (2 inches*3km) , that transmits water from the reservoir to ‘Einun area. According to notice number 622, the Israeli Occupation claims that the water line is sat up on an archaeological land. The targeted water line was providing water to residential tents belongs to citizen {SM} (name reserved for privacy purpose) who supports a family of 6, 3 of them are children (and 3 females). Noteworthy, this project includes the installation of three chlorination units to benefit the targeted community at Khirbet Yarza and Ibziq in Tubas Governorate through improving the water quality of their water sources / water cisterns. It should be noted that this system will also benefit the population at Al Maleh and Al Burj community (290 individuals). In addition to the rehabilitation of 1 km of water pipelines to support residents’ steadfastness at Khirbet Yarza. Umm al-Tut village suffers from a large water loss due to the invalidity of the main water line connecting from the water source to the village, where the water loss is approximately 40% due to the wear of the line, and the site also suffers from a severe water shortage, as water is pumped to the village at a rate of one day Every week, this in turn affects the lives of the population and their health and social conditions. The project team will work to replace the 4 km internal water distribution network, due to the increased water losses in the site, which may reach 40% of the value of the monthly water bill, as well as extending 0.5 km network to supply water for 15 families, which is approximately 15% of the number of families in the site. Beit Liqya depends on the water of the Israeli Water Company (Mekorot), and water is supplied to it through an 8-inch water line, which needs major maintenance. Also, the flow of the water line is not sufficient to provide the area with drinking water, especially since this line supplies the surrounding villages (Kharbatha village, Beit Ur At-Tahat and Saffa villages). Therefore, the project intervention will result in replacing the main transmission pipe from 8 inches to 12 inches to provide water to Beit Liqya, as well the 3 neighboring communities which are Beit Ur Tahta with a population of 5444 people, and Kharbatha al-Misbah with a population of 6,876 people, and finally Safaa with a population of 4,724 people.

2 - Activities

The proposed activities are expected to improve the overall protection of the targeted communities through improving their access to clean and adequate water and WASH education that will contribute to equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services. In order to ensure the intervention's sustainability, the local councils at the targeted communities who is responsible for managing the selected water resources, will be asked to provide in-kind contribution including the provision of space, water testing and related arrangements electricity and materials to protect the water sources and its surrounding area. Furthermore, an operational and maintenance plan will be developed and technical/theoretical capacity building (on-site training) for the operators will be conducted to ensure the ownership and successful operation of the targeted water sources in a sustainable manner. Activities:1.1: Rehabilitation of (4 km 2 inch) transitional pipeline; in addition to (0.5 km 1 inch) pipeline in Umm-At-Tut for the benefit of 1200 beneficiaries. Replacing the 4 km internal water distribution network, due to the increased water losses in the site, which may reach 40% of the value of the monthly water bill, as well as extend 0.5 km network that supply water for 15 families, which is approximately 15% of the number of families in the site. 1.2 Replacement of old transitional pipeline (2.6 km 12 inch) in Beit Liqya for the benefit of 9304 beneficiaries.Replacing the main transmission pipe from 8 inches to 12 inches due to main reason that the water flow isn't enough to provide water to Beit Liqya with a total population of 9304. In addition this also will benefit indirectly around 17044 people living in three neighboring communities which are Beit Ur Tahta with a population of 5444 people, and Kharbatha al-Misbah with a population of 6,876 people, and finally Safaa with a population of 4,724 people. 1.3 Rehabilitation of 1 km of pipes of Khirbet Yarza main pipeline for the benefit of 31 beneficiaries. Extending the distribution of water network through the installation of 1 km (2 inch) main pipes with required fittings to benefit 31 individuals. 1.4 Connect Ibziq pipe (2 km 2 inches) with Aqqaba Municipality for the benefit of 129 beneficiaries.Installing a new water carrier line to deliver water from the nearest water point (Aqaba Artesian well) with a length of 2 km (2 inches ) to provide 129 people with water. 1.5 Installation of three chlorination units at Khirbet Yarza and IbziqInstalling of 3 chlorination units will benefit the targeted community at Khirbet Yarza and Ibziq in Tubas Governorate through improving the water quality of their water sources / water cisterns. It should be noted that one of the chlorination unit at Khirbet Yarza will be equipped with solar system as there is no electricity near the targeted site. The chlorination units will improve the water quality that is supplied to 160 at Khirbet Yarza and Ibziq . In addition this also will benefit around 290 people living in 2 neighboring communities which are al Maleh and Al Burj community 1.6 community awareness training material Conducting an awareness-raising campaign to raise the awareness of the targeted communities in respect to WASH best practices. The project will adopt a participatory approach by establishing a community-based WASH hub (one in each targeted community) that will be responsible for spreading WASH messages to their communities. The community hub members, will receive a training of trainers (TOT) on WASH practices to create transformative change on best practices in their own communities. Each hub will consist at least of 4 individuals, of which 2 females and 2 males. The hub who will be responsible for spreading WASH messages to their communities. In cooperation with this community hub, the project will distribute awareness material (leaflets) to the four community to ensure better access to hygiene. Raising the awareness of the targeted beneficiaries will ensure that they are able to sustain and protect the scares water sources.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

On a regular monthly basis, MEAL Officer & Project Coordinators, meet with the high level decision makers at the two organizations to discuss the project progress and issues including mainly the received complaints. The main purpose of this monthly meeting is to share experience on how to react with all project issues and discussing the lessons learned and best practices. On the other hand, and to ensure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with the project deliverable and team (internal or external team), the MEAL officer conducts desk review of the project reports and field visits to meet with a random sample of direct beneficiaries as well as with official institution representatives. Also, the two partners commit to address the complaints and feedback within a reasonable timeframe

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

ARIJ- UAWC will adopt different mechanisms of stakeholders’ feedback. They are: verbal feedback to the project team in the field or over the phone, through the field monitoring visits by the ARIJ MEAL Officer as well as email address. ARIJ will also arrange a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries during the project implementation and up to 3 months after the end of the project. This system gives individuals the possibility to report complaints and inquiries in a secured, accountable, transparent, equitable way, as well as to receive a response in a timely manner. All complaints/feedback are reported to ARIJ Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff will be registered in the database, and referred to the relevant staff according to it sensitivity and importance. If the complaint requires follow up, the complaint will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. In addition, end line survey will be carried out to measure the satisfaction and the feedback of the project impact on different vulnerable groups at different levels (women, men, youth, elderly people, PWD).

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

In all of the project activities including field visits, meetings assessments , and focus groups, etc the beneficiaries of the targeted communities will be informed about the complaint mechanism and how to access it in. Also, a paragraph will be included in each agreement with beneficiaries explaining the steps to report any complaint through a beneficiary feedback mechanism which is accessible by phone or email. A service phone number will be distributed to the population in the proposed target communities enabling them to report feedback or complaints relating to project implementation or the partners employees’ behavior.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In order to fulfill the needs and priorities of the targeted citizens, ARIJ and UAWC will ensure having an environment helpful to respect for human rights and to avoid exposing the targeted beneficiaries to any threats and risks especially to gender-based violence. Therefore, protection measures and risk mitigation will be provided during the project implementation to ensure dignity, safety, accountability to them. The project team will establish a Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and will conduct Community based meetings with the participation of the representatives of related stakeholders to enlighten them with protections issues even beyond the end of the project. The project will also inform the beneficiaries about their rights to raise their feedback, complaints, and suggestions and encourage them to report any issues or concern.Both ARIJ-UAWC will ensure that their feedback is well-received and reflected on the ground. Furthermore, ARIJ- UAWC with the related stakeholders particularly the PWA will inform the targeted beneficiaries of the risks that may arise in addition to the technical protection measures integrated in the project activities execution. Do no harm approach will support that all beneficiaries (including the elderly, women, children, and people with disabilities) are targeted in having equitable and impartial access to all project activities and interventions without any discrimination and without to being exposed to any hazards or threats. ARIJ-UAWC has Monitoring, Evaluation and Accountability system and assigned a professional team under the lead of professional person who has extensive experience in project monitoring and evaluation in order to improve the quality of its program and its fulfillment of the agreement commitments. Most importantly, the system focuses on the project accountability commitment to the affected persons through the development of various measures that ensures the principle of "Do No Harm" to the affected people. The M&E staff are responsible for ensuring that the project is going in line with its indicators, quality standards, outputs and outcomes on monthly and quarterly basis. The M&E staff will produce a progress report on a quarterly basis comparing the activities, quality, quantity, and showing the progress of the project according to the planned activities. Any deviation will be raised and solved immediately. Sex and Age Disaggregated Data’ (SADD) will be reported within the produced progress report. Moreover and in order to measure the project progress and outcome, the project staff will update the conducted baseline assessment and will conduct an end-line survey on the project outcome indicators. Random sampling interviews with the beneficiaries and field visits will be conducted to measure the overall project impact and their overall satisfaction on the implementing of the project activities. A final report will be produced to present the final outcomes and shared with the WASH cluster.
