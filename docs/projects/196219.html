Project Summary

Based on the following three strategic pillars resembling three basic needs, HEKS/EPER Syria is planning to implement the following activities:
Strategic Pillar #1 ‘’Bread’’: HEKS/EPER acknowledges the importance of subsidization as a lifeline for millions of vulnerable Syrians and an effective social support system. Therefore, HEKS/EPER Syria mission plans to support the subsidized bread value chain by rehabilitating/improving three public bakeries’ capacities in the southern area: one in Dar’a governorate (20,000 PoCs) and two in Rural Damascus (mostly in Qatana and in Dhameer) (30,000 PoCs each)
The estimated number of persons of concern disaggregated by Age and Gender are as follows (based on percentages in the HNO 2023):

Strategic Pillar #2 ‘’Water’’: HEKS/EPER is planning to provide three solar power systems to three boreholes in Rural Damascus (mostly: Nashabiyeh, Hajar Aswad and Sahnaya) with 10,000 PoCs benefitting from each borehole.
The estimated number of persons of concern disaggregated by Age and Gender are as follows (based on percentages in the HNO 2023)

Strategic Pillar #3 ‘’Work’’: HEKS/EPER is planning to rehabilitate and equip one Rural Development Center in Rural Damascus (Mostly in Dhameer area) with 1,000 PoCS benefitting from the intervention.
The purpose of the project is to enhance the resilience of the population in the southern area by increasing accessibility, affordability and availability of basic services with respect to initial standards.
HEKS/EPER approach is to support duty-bearers (Syrian state) to take responsibility to fulfill their obligations and not create a parallel support system or dependence on humanitarian aid. Therefore, HEKS/EPER acknowledges the importance of the Syrian state as a social entity and works to support the relevant authorities in providing essential services to the population.

This project is following a multi-sectoral approach to support people living in the southern area in an integrated manner to cover the top three strategic pillars/basic needs; Food (Bread), water and work.
HEKS/EPER Syria Country Office is newly established and thus HEKS/EPER did not participate in the preparation of the 2022/2023 HRP. During 2022, HEKS/EPER was able to implement three projects (one under each strategic pillar/ basic need) as follows:
1- Rehabilitation of one Bakery in Ashrafiet Sahnaya area in Rural Damascus
2- Providing Solar power system to one borehole in Ein Terma area in Rural Damascus
3- Rehabilitation of vocational training center for PWDs in Barzeh area in Rural Damascus
During 2023, HEKS/EPER will continue to focus on Rural Damascus and try to expand more toward the south targeting initially Dar’a governorate.
Therefore, this project is considered a continuation of the projects implemented in 2022 with the aim of contributing to covering the three basic needs in the southern area and targeting some areas with multiple interventions such as Ashrafiet Sahnaya, AlDummair and Eastern Ghouta.

3 - Needs

HEKS/EPER Syria is focusing on the following 3 strategic pillars resembling basic needs:Strategic Pillar #1 ‘’Bread’’: According to the drafted Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2023 (HNO 2023), it is estimated that 12 million are facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people are at risk of sliding into food insecurity. Bread is the main food staple in Syria and the public bakeries provide subsidized bread for a price of around 5% of its real thus giving access to basic food to the most vulnerable layers of the Syrian population.Strategic Pillar #2 ‘’Water’’: 13.55 million people are in need of WASH assistance, 2,6% more than in 2022. Furthermore, according to the ICRC report on 01/10/2021, Syria now has up to 40% less drinking water than before the crisis period. All functioning water supply infrastructure depends on electricity in a context where the power generation capacity is down by 60% to 70% and the majority of areas are receiving less than 2 hours of grid electricity a day. Similar to bread, water is also subsidized by the Syrian state as it charges fees that do not cover the operational costs.Strategic Pillar #3 ‘’Work’’: Livelihood support remains one of the top needs with approximately 90% of households in Syria consistently experiencing a gap between their income earned and expenditure required to meet basic needs despite 51% only having one member earning an income signaling the increase in the working poor class.

4 - Implementation of activities

Based on the 3 strategic pillars of strategy, and on HEKS/EPER’s approach to support duty-bearers (Syrian state) to take responsibility to fulfil their obligations, HEKS/EPER Syria is planning to implement rehabilitate and/or provide new production lines for three bakeries, install three solar power systems to boreholes and rehabilitate and equip a rural development center.Since all the proposed activities are hard components and mainly contain civil, electrical and mechanical works without direct interaction, selection, or participation of beneficiaries (in light of the Syrian context & ministries standards) The implementation of the activities will be conducted as follows:1. Area identification in coordination with the sectors and ministries2. Obtaining needed approvals from ministries and security authorities3. Conducting the technical assessments 4. Define the scope of work in coordination with the ministries (MoWR/MoITCP/ MoSAL), local authorities (DoWR/DoB/DoSAL) and local communities.5. Launching procurement process6. Conducting baseline survey (for boreholes)7. Contracting with the selected contractor launching the execution of the works8. Continuous monitoring of the contractor’s works 9. Conducting the functionality testing 10. Handover the infrastructure, equipment and tools to local authorities to be managed by their side11. Conducting the end line survey (for boreholes) or post-implementation evaluation (for bakeries and Rural Development units)

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk #1: Exclusion -physical access - (Likelihood: High, impact: Low) – Mitigation measures: Design the infrastructure to allow for safe and easy access for everyone including PWDs – i.e: creating ramps. Risk #2: Children conduct work on behalf of the humanitarian organization or its contractors.- (Likelihood: High, impact: High) – Mitigation measures: 1- Add zero-tolerance disclosure on child labor in contracts.2- Check civil documentation. Risk #3: Safety incidents during or after the execution of the works- (Likelihood: High, impact: High) – Mitigation measures: 1- Install caution signs around the work sites 2- Contractors sign HEKS/EPER’s safety measures. 3- Install railings and fire extinguishers in the infrastructure Risk #4: Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials i.e: generators.- (Likelihood: low, impact: High) -Mitigation Measures:1- MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.2- Promote cost recovery systems for longer-term maintenance and ownership of infrastructure to promote community ownership. Risk #5: Potential exposure to explosive hazards during assessment/rehabilitation.- (Likelihood: medium, impact: High) -Mitigation Measures:1- Work closely with Mine Action actors2- conduct assessments/rehabilitation works in areas declared as cleared;3- Conduct safety training with staff and contractors.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: beneficiaries exposed to SEA harm - (Likelihood: low. Impact: High) – Mitigation measures: 1- Inform local communities, authorities and contractors that HEKS/EPER has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse.2- Establish a confidential complaint mechanism and distribute hot-line number to key informants and community leaders3- Address any SEA complaints. 4- Train staff on code of conduct, safeguarding principles and GBV risk mitigation measures;5- Raise the awareness of existing service providers where victims can seek help6- Enforce policies to prevent beneficiaries’ exposure to harm; Due to the nature of the planned activities (Infrastructure works), the interactions with beneficiaries are almost entirely non-existent and thus the risks related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse are almost totally avoided.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A. Assumptions;1) Stable security situation2) Consistent humanitarian access3) No major fluctuation in the Syrian Pound value4) No mass movements of populations5) Availability of material in the local market6) Acceptance of community.B. Risks: 1) Shortage of Fuel (Likelihood: medium, impact: high) Mitigation: rely on outsources transportation means, more active remote management and decrease the site visits without affecting monitoring and quality.2) Potential exposure to the explosive hazard (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High) Mitigation: coordinate with MINE actors and conduct works in cleared areas; conduct safety training with staff and contractors.3) Theft of rehabilitation/construction materials (likelihood: low, Impact High) Mitigation: sign MoUs with local ministries to ensure local ownership of materials & facilities to promote cost recovery systems for longer terms maintenance and to promote community ownership.4) Delays in obtaining needed approvals for assessment/implementation. (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium) Mitigation: keep close coordination with the line ministries at different levels to speed up the processes – seek the support of sectors to advocate for approvals.5) Lack of capacity of authorities to operate or manage the infrastructure. (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High) Mitigation: ensure authorities willingness & ability to manage from the assessment phase –provisioning of technical training, spare parts, and tools for public staff.
