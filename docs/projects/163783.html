Project Summary

Inter-communal tensions in the South of Libya have grown increasingly worse since early 2019, escalating into a violent conflict starting on August 4th, 2019. The conflict, initiated by a series of airstrikes, has led to intense urban fighting, identity-based civilian killing, as well as mass displacement from Murzuk to neighboring areas in Libya. According to data in the IOM-DTM Round 27, by October 31, 2019, the deterioration in the security situation due to armed conflict had resulted in the displacement of over 5,643 families (28,215 individuals) in search of safety to surrounding areas in Southern Libya.
In neighboring cities in the South, following the need for safety and security, shelter was listed by key informants (KIs) in the 2019 REACH Rapid Assessment as the second highest priority, and NFIs in third place – with KIs emphasizing the need for basic household NFIs, becoming increasingly difficult to find. WASH and protection needs were also highlighted as key concerns for IDPs and host communities. DTM in support of the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM)* conducted a needs assessment of families displaced from Murzuq to the coastal municipalities in Western Libya, where shelter, food, and non-food items (NFIs) were identified as the top three priority emergency needs.
Through this intervention, ACTED will provide humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support to conflict-affected populations (IDPs, returnees, and host communities) in the South of Libya (SNFI sector Objective 1). This project will ultimately contribute to 2020 HRP priorities through enhancing vulnerable population’s safe, equitable, and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral services to access resources and reduce their vulnerability.
To do so, ACTED will bring its significant programming experience in the South of Libya, where it has established links with the community and an in-depth knowledge of local dynamics. ACTED has helped to meet the humanitarian needs of vulnerable households in Sebha and Brak Shati through the provision of unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) through UNHCR funding in 2018 and 2019. During this timeframe, ACTED carried out community-based participatory needs assessments and implemented Quick Impact Projects in Sebha and Ubari with the goal of addressing community priority needs (health and education) while fostering social cohesion in the South. Between 2012 and 2016, ACTED carried out the EU-funded Civil Initiatives Libya (CIL) countrywide, including in the South, to strengthen the capacity of emerging civil society organizations (CSOs), with the objective of fostering a vibrant, active, and independent civil society in Libya. Since 2017, ACTED has worked as an implementing partner with GIZ for the ongoing project “Support to Municipalities in the Context of Mixed Migration Project” funded by European Union Trust Fund and the French Ministry of Foreign Affairs, which supports the resilience of local communities hosting migrants through improved service delivery.
To implement this project, ACTED will ensure an active participation in the Shelter and NFI Sector, including through participation in meetings and the submission of timely and accurate reporting, to ensure a coherent and coordinated approach with other humanitarian actors in the South of Libya.

1 - Needs

An increased need for shelter and NFI assistance for IDPs has manifested in the South of Libya since August 2019 due to socio-political developments and a resulting deterioration in the security situation. These needs were highlighted to ACTED directly by the Murzuk IDP Crisis Committee in Sebha, as well as the mayor of the municipality of Idri Shati, requesting humanitarian assistance to the large and increasing number of displaced families in their respective municipalities. Shelter: As highlighted in the Humanitarian Access Report published in October 2019, many displaced families continue to face significant challenges related to shelter and housing. KIs estimated that between 10% and 30% of recently displaced IDPs from Murzuk were being hosted by family or friends, with a further 5-30% renting their own accommodation (REACH RA). A KI in Sebha reported that the majority of Murzuk IDPs in the city are staying in collective shelters, rather than staying with relatives. Overcrowded shelters and a shortage of available housing options was reported as an issue for recently displaced persons by the KIs in Sebha and other neighboring cities. However, even some IDPs in Sebha who are financially capable of renting a flat are unable to find one, due to the recent influx of IDPs from Murzuk and Ghuddwa, as well as tribal considerations (some neighborhoods are under the control of conflicting tribes so the displaced persons are unable to rent accommodation in those neighborhoods as they would be vulnerable to raids, making their choices limited). In a consultation with the mayor of Idri Shati, concern was highlighted for the families living in three collective shelters based out of schools in the municipality, and request was supported with rehabilitating the shelters’ bathrooms, windows, doors, and sanitation facilities. NFIs: An increased need for basic household items, particularly mattresses, blankets, cooking pots, and utensils, in order to meet the needs of the influx of newly displaced persons has been reported – however these items in increasingly scarce supply on the local market as a result of heightened demand from the recent IDPs (REACH Rapid Assessment). ACTED Consultations with CSOs conducted in November in Sebha indicated the need for hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene management supplies for women, and supplies for infants. The lack of access to basic goods is further exacerbated by difficulties to withdraw money from banks due to the lack of liquidity, unreasonably high fees to withdraw or transfer money and the lack of ID documents to provide identity to banks, which had been lost or destroyed (Humanitarian Access Report, October 2019). The mayor of Idri Shati highlighted the need for the provision of blankets and other household NFIs for IDPs in the South in preparation for winterization. The needs for NFIs is expected to continue increasing in the winter months, notably for warm bedding and clothing. Furthermore, in December 2019, the OCHA-led Area Coordination Group for the South was approached by the Murzuq IDP Affairs Central Committee, highlighting the urgent humanitarian needs for 170 families who displaced to Jufra and 550 who displaced to Ubari, Bent Baya, Alghrayfa and Wadi Etba – notably their need for blankets, mattresses, and non-food assistance. Until now, no further assistance bas been provided due to limited funding to address these needs.Where feasible, ACTED will prioritize female-headed households, in recognizing that female-headed households face additional protection risks and are at greater risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms. In doing so, it will promote women’s increased access to and control over resources, acknowledging that men usually have primary control over most resources.

2 - Activities

Through this intervention, ACTED will provide humanitarian shelter and NFI support to conflict-affected IDPs and host communities.Activity 1: ACTED will implement collective shelter rehabilitations to ensure safe and dignified living conditions, based on the needs highlighted by site assessments, consultations with the IDP Crisis Committees, municipalities, and FGDs with community members including men, women, boys, girls, and persons living with disabilities. In the set-up phase of the project, ACTED will collect and analyze age and sex-disaggregated data on needs, priorities, community dynamics, and capabilities relating to shelter. Activities will focus on structural repairs, seeking to ensure the living conditions are adequate and able to uphold the dignity of affected populations by making the spaces safer and community friendly—taking into account lighting, ventilation, privacy, and security. ACTED will train local, gender-balanced IDP Crisis Committees—on risk identification and site maintenance works. Activity 2: ACTED will distribute NFIs to IDPs in collective shelters, and those living outside of collective shelters for those hosted by community members or in rented facilities, in addition to vulnerable returnee and host community members. Following an in-depth needs assessment, ACTED will identify the list of commodities needed which will be sourced through local suppliers. The items distributed will be tailored to the needs of the girls, boys, men and women of varying ages and abilities in each household. Key items may include cooking items such as mattresses/ bedding, kitchen sets, cooking/ heating stoves and fuel, clothing, dignity kits, and baby items. The beneficiaries will be selected in coordination with the municipality and the local IDP Crisis Committee. Distribution areas will target multiple locations and towns to ensure the conflict sensitivity of the response and address the needs of all displaced populations.

4 - Protection risk analysis

The security situation in Libya prevents access to beneficiaries: At the time of proposal development, the security situation remains problematic in the South. Travel disruption remains a possibility--periodic outbreaks of violence of all kinds (tribal, military, political, extremist, criminal) have been known to greatly increase the danger of travel between cities in Libya, or sometimes temporarily block travel entirely. Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (SEA): The involvement of humanitarian workers in acts of SEA is a serious violation of the ‘do no harm’ principle, and humanitarian organizations’ obligation to protect populations affected by crises. Sexual exploitation is defined as ‘any actual or attempted abuse of a position of vulnerability, differential power, or trust, for sexual purposes, including, but not limited to, threatening or profiting monetarily, socially or politically from the sexual exploitation of another.’ Sexual abuse is defined as ‘the actual or threatened physical intrusion of a sexual nature, whether by force or under unequal or coercive conditions.’Theft, corruption and diversion: Theft is a risk due to an increasingly tense local economic situation, unpaid workers and liquidity shortage. Large parts of the population are armed and reports of robberies are frequent. Furthermore, humanitarian assistance organizations and agencies must always take adequate measures to mitigate risks of internal corruption or diversion of aid.

5 - Mitigation measures

R1: ACTED Security staff continuously monitor conflict developments in order to anticipate access concerns and modify procedures in advance. ACTED has in place standard operating procedures designed to minimize the exposure of staff and beneficiaries to security threats. Should changes in conflict dynamics cause a deterioration of the security situation, ACTED will potentially adapt the program if needed. ACTED also uses staff locally based in Sebha which increases the capacity to access beneficiaries directly in their locations, while multiple distribution areas will ensure there is limited movement to other areas of the city needed by beneficiaries that may expose them to risk due to displacement outside of their ‘tribal zones’.R2: ACTED’s CRM provides a mechanism for beneficiaries or community members to report any instances of SEA on the part of ACTED staff or partners. Where possible, ACTED will ensure a gender-balanced group of staff for all program field teams. Additionally, selection criteria for program activities and the principles of humanitarian action, including the fact that humanitarian is free and equal to all without expectations of reciprocal favors, will be explained to beneficiaries of ACTED programming. ACTED also has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy that all staff have to abide by, and any violation of this policy results in immediate termination of staff R3: ACTED has strong internal controls and separation of responsibility in place within the finance unit, logistics department, program team and an independent monitoring and evaluation department. ACTED will apply its institutionalized procedures for promoting transparency and preventing and detecting corruption. ACTED staff will also be encouraged to keep a low profile and avoid direct transfers in physical cash to partners. ACTED’s Security Manager conducts ongoing incident tracking to determine areas where threat may be higher, to avoid if possible.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

A key aspect of Accountability to Affected Populations is adequately informing women, girls, men and boys of the available resources and how to influence the project design and implementation. To do so, ACTED has developed feedback mechanisms for women, girls, men and boys as part of its humanitarian programming—notably, its Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), which serves as a core means to ensure accountability towards beneficiaries benefitting from shelter and NFI assistance. The CRM is managed by Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation (AME) staff to ensure independence from programme teams and for which confidentiality is a fundamental principle. Complaints are received via a dedicated CRM phoneline, Whatsapp, email and in the field by AME and programme staff. All complaints are registered and recorded by the CRM Focal Point and categorised based on the level of sensitivity. The CRM Focal Point leads to resolve complaints raised, with the involvement of programme, AME or senior management staff, depending on the case. The CRM Focal Point responds to all individuals who have made a complaint to inform them of the outcome of their complaint. Within this project, ACTED will ensure that all beneficiaries are made aware of the CRM process and that they are able to raise any issues. In this way, the CRM serves as a feedback and response mechanism that both gathers the views of POCs, and incorporates them into project changes, as well as provide a mechanism for beneficiaries or community members to report cases of mismanagement, misconduct, or sexual exploitation.During the screening process, all interviewees are communicated with an ACTED CRM number in case they have any complaint or feedback on the selection process. Information regarding the CRM (what it is, how it works, how to make a complaint, how ACTED processes and responds to complaints) will be systematically communicated to all beneficiaries in this project by the AME unit in partnership with programme teams.During the project implementation, ACTED will monitor the access to shelter and NFI assistance by women, girls, men and boys of varying abilities and backgrounds—including individuals living with disabilities--and will accordingly develop sex and age-disaggregated indicators based on the assessed gaps and dynamics.
