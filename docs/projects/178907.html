Project Summary

NRC’s project will contribute to Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) sector HRP objectives 1, 2 and 3. NRC will implement its ERL activities through a combination of direct and partner-led modalities, with aim to build resilience of displacement-affected population by providing a pathway from acute food needs to their greater self-reliance and livelihood recovery. Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level (including the Cash Working Group), and access. NRC will provide ERL assistance to vulnerable conflict and displacement affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, returnees and members of host communities suffering the effects of the conflict.
1. Emergency Response: Objective: Vulnerable households can meet basic needs and reduce negative coping strategies, as well as to ensure protection of their productive assets.
To ensure that displacement affected households can meet basic needs and reduce negative coping strategies, and to ensure protection of their productive assets:
- Unrestricted Cash Based Assistance, as a primary response modality to empower households to define and attain their own specific needs whenever appropriate.
- Information and Behaviour Change: CBA will be accompanied by information about the importance of dietary diversity and nutritious dietary choices to increase the impact of CBA in promoting FS outcomes.
2. Economic Recovery: Objective: Vulnerable households achieve self-reliance through own-food production and/or income generation.
To facilitate the transition of displacement affected populations from humanitarian assistance to self-reliance. NRC will support economic recovery of households so they can restore their ability to produce food, while for non-agricultural population NRC will use market-based approaches to facilitate access to demand driven income generation activities.
- Local Food Production: targeted grants (cash or in-kind) to support local production of food by conflict affected population. Grants will be accompanied by training in food production techniques.
- Income Generation Activities (IGA): NRC will facilitate access to IGA through: (1) Cash for Work activities that will ensure access to temporary income and employment opportunities, (2) Grants (cash / in-kind) to support income generation and (self) employment, and (3) Basic business skills training.
3. Inclusive Markets: Objective: Households achieve resilience through sustainable livelihoods and inclusive markets.
To promote the development and access to inclusive markets that enable displacement affected populations fully restore their sustainable livelihoods.
- Sectoral Market Based Support: Development of agricultural livelihoods via provision of targeted grants, supported with training, mentoring, and market access.
- Market Access Support: Employment Facilitation, Entrepreneurship, Local Capacity Strengthening and Infrastructure Rehabilitation.

4 - Needs

Across Syria, 14.2 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, with a total of 12.4 million people are considered food insecure, of which 1.3 million severely food insecure, with an additional 1.8 million at risk of food insecurity. Food insecurity in Syria remains primarily affected by the continued conflict causing protracted displacement and loss/lack of livelihoods opportunities, which is contributing to high economic vulnerability levels, hampering people’s accessibility to food. Covid-19 global pandemic, depreciation of local currency (SYP) and economic crises have worsened the situation during 2021, further exacerbating food insecurity and overall humanitarian needs of conflict-affected population throughout Syria. Approximately, 6.7 million people are displaced within Syria, with an estimated 2.96 million people are displaced across Northwest and Northeast Syria, and 2 million people relying on monthly food assistance. Access to food, livelihoods and employment opportunities are the top-three priorities identified by displaced population, regardless of length of their displacement. Over 1.7 million people currently living in last resort camps are among the population groups most affected by the deteriorating food insecurity situation with no access to livelihoods and total reliance on humanitarian assistance. Unemployment figures have risen sharply over the past year, and among the country’s working age population are at approximately 9 million. Accordingly, approximately 50 per cent of Syrians spend more than 75 per cent of their expenditure on food. Highly vulnerable categories include: female-, child- and elderly-headed households, the elderly, PwDs and HHs with no or limited access to income.

5 - Implementation of activities

NRC’s project will contribute to Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) sector objectives 1, 2 and 3 via several interventions addressing the key priorities of displaced population, as follows: 1. Emergency Response: NRC will provide unrestricted Cash Based Assistance (CBA) as a primary response modality to empower households to define and attain their own specific needs whenever appropriate. The CBA will be accompanied by information sessions about the importance of dietary diversity and nutritious dietary choices to increase the impact of cash assistance. 2. Economic Recovery responses will include: Support to Local Food Production: targeted grants (cash or in-kind) to support local production of food by conflict affected population. Grants (and/or inputs) will be accompanied by training in food production techniques. Income Generation: NRC will facilitate access to income-generation activities through: (1) Cash for Work activities that will ensure access to temporary income and employment opportunities for the most vulnerable conflict affected population, (2) Cash and/or in-kind grants to support income generation and (self) employment, and (3) Delivery of basic business skills training. 3. Inclusive Markets: Sectoral Market Based Support interventions will focus on the development of agricultural livelihoods, and will contain targeted grants (or kits) for agricultural livelihoods inputs and/or assets, supported with training, mentoring, and market access support to maximize impact. Market Access Support: NRC will focus on the following core areas to promote inclusive markets for Conflict Affected Syrians: Employment Facilitation, Entrepreneurship, Local Capacity Strengthening and Infrastructure Rehabilitation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (1): Risk of corruption at the level of beneficiary selection, including exclusion of specific people or communities. Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Mitigation Measures: 1) Complaints/Feedback mechanisms are put in place and are accessible to beneficiaries. 2) Various outreach modalities are utilised and own selection criteria is used. 3) Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) includes questions about exchange of aid for services or money, 4) NRC compliance standards and procedures, 5) staff awareness on anti-corruption policies. Protection Risk (2) Requirement of beneficiary vetting by some donors agencies pose number of protection risks to the recipient of the assistance. Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Mitigation measures: 1) Institutional policies in place to guarantee confidentiality of beneficiary data. No beneficiary vetting accepted during implementation. 2) Programmatic tools and processes designed in a manner which guarantees impartial selection of beneficiaries based on needs with full respect of do no harm principle. Protection Risk (3): Centralised, large-scale distributions might create security risks for beneficiaries and staff/partners. Also, they may not be physically accessible for people affected by impaired mobility or those unable to leave the house. Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Mitigation Measures: 1) Prioritisation of household-level distributions where feasible and appropriate. 2) Where centralised distribution sites are required, selection will include risk assessment and alternative distribution methods. 7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC’s Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, and child safeguarding policies. Several sessions took place in 2020 to raise awareness of PSEA and child safeguarding to staff, in addition in sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC participates in PSEA network meetings, regularly reports and shares information on good practice and challenges related to PSEA. NRC’s Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and child safeguarding reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams. In addition to inquiring during post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs) whether people were asked to exchange anything for aid. Attempts by non-traditional humanitarian actors (e.g. local councils and authorities) to interfere in beneficiary selection and eligibility are reported and addressed as they come. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks who recipients of aid think provided the assistance, in order to identify possible areas of concern. Efforts in 2021 to strengthen child-friendly CFM have started and are expected to conclude by mid-2021. Cash/LFS: Issues related to exchange of assistance for sexual favours, threats or actual removal from beneficiary lists

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Threats to health, safety and security of staff, partners and beneficiaries: NRC has dedicated Health, Safety and Security staff, to ensure a close context follow-up and implementation of NRC Security policy and standards. NRC produce regular risk assessments and updated security and contingency plans. NRC staff receive proper security and safety training. - Interference from local authorities preventing NRC to access specific areas and to select independently beneficiary or staff: networking with peer organisations to advocate for unified positions, regular meeting with local key stakeholders to reinforce humanitarian principles, transparent policy and selection criteria, clear red lines identified- Non-renewal of UNSC resolution 2165 after 10 July 2021: Continue negotiations with GoS to expand access from Damascus. Keep strengthening LHP’ capacity to ensure operational quality. Scale up procurement to guarantee access to supplies.
