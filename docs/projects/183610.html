Project Summary

Through this emergency project, and in compliance with the Cluster Objective 1 and 2, ACTED’s aim is to ensure safe and dignity access to adequate WASH services, at households and community level, for both victims of demolition/confiscations and the most vulnerable Palestinians.
ACTED and ARIJ (Applied Research Institute – Jerusalem) propose to cover outstanding WASH needs in the identified 15 most vulnerable communities in 3 governorates, in complementarity with other ongoing and planned WASH interventions, including under Humanitarian Fund funding, as follows:
- Hebron: Isfey al Fauqa, Isfey al Tihta, Khallet Hajar Fauqa and Tahta/Al Buweib, Al Baqa'a, Jinba, Ar Rakeez, Umm Qussa
- Jerusalem: Al Khalayleh Bedouins, An Nabi Samwil Bedouins, Ma'azi Jaba', South Bir Nabala Bedouins
- Ramallah: Deir Abu Masha'al Herders, East Tayba Bedouins, Ein Samiya, Maghayer ad Deir
Activities will include:
- Water network in one community and provision of 3 water sources facilities critical operation and maintenance materials and tools (dosing pump)
- rehabilitation and provision of key materials to ensure the maintenance of WASH facilities in 18 institutions and community structures, including provision of environmental and personal cleaning kits in Health Care Facilities (HCF) in coordination with the MoE, MoH and PWA at each step of the implementation, for approval or information.
- support to 82 most vulnerable households with the self-repair of their WASH facilities at household level through cash assistance and provision of 24 latrines.
- rehabilitation of 30 water wheeled tanks
- In addition, ACTED will respond to outstanding WASH needs in terms of demolitions by rehabilitating up to 5 WASH community infrastructures in affected communities, based on needs arising in 2021 and 2022.
- Creation and training of 8 Community-based Protection Committees in target areas, to ensure protection mainstreaming within communities, communications with communities, and accountability to affected populations throughout the project duration (where needed).
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, the disabled and the elderly. Gender principles will be mainstreamed throughout the entire action and CBPCs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV with a particular focus given to women and children. Finally, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be core to the proposed Action.

1 - Needs

As outlined in the HNO 2022, in Area C, the restrictive planning system enforced by the Israeli authorities prevents development, including even routine repairs and maintenance of water structures, leaving the West Bank with inadequate WASH infrastructure (wells, cisterns, pipelines, etc.). Based on Water Sector Regulatory Council, 620,000 Palestinians in the West Bank reside in areas (primarily in Area C) that are not connected to piped water services or are poorly supplied; consequently, they consume less than 50 litres of water per capita per day, well below the 100 litres recommended by WHO. Moreover, more than 100 communities in Area C still rely on rainwater harvesting cisterns and trucked water to satisfy their water needs, leading to high water prices that can reach more than 30 ILS/m3, as well as water quality issues. Secondly, Palestinians living across the WB are at high risk of demolitions or confiscations by the Israeli authorities. Based on OCHA demolition dashboard, between January and September 2021, 51 WASH-related structures and another 6 WASH infrastructures were demolished or confiscated on the grounds of a lack of building permits, which are nearly impossible to obtain, this affected 3,860 individuals including 1,868 children and 1,003 women. Finally, women and girls are disproportionally impacted by water quantity and quality limitations affecting substandard sanitation facilities, for their own health and as well as in terms of bearing the burden of household duties for all the family. Therefore, inadequate WASH facilities mean gender inequality. The proposed intervention was designed based on the findings of the Multi Sectoral Need Assessments (MSNA) conducted by REACH in 2021 and the HNO 2022. The assessment findings show that 124,910 PIN in area C categorized at Severe, Extreme and Catastrophic class. Also, the findings indicate that more than 4,000 households in WB (majority in area C) are in shortages of adequate sanitation facilities at the household level, thus they are adopting several coping mechanisms, including sharing latrines with other families and using shelter spaces, other than bathrooms, for handwashing or bathing. Furthermore, the intervention is informed by ACTED’s active participation in the WASH in HCFs assessment survey 2021 led by WASH Cluster in August/September 2021, and mentioned in the HNO 2022. The assessment supported the identification of areas of acute WASH vulnerability and served as a key reference for resource allocation and mobilization. Out of 637 surveyed HCFs, ACTED led the assessment in 352. The findings show that 164 HCFs in West Bank suffer from insufficient WASH facilities to meet the standards set up by the Ministries of Health. With regard to educational institutions, at least 129 schools in the West Bank suffer from insufficient WASH facilities (primarily toilets, drinking and handwashing stations) to meet the standards set up by the Ministry of Education. This results in overcrowding the existing facilities and undermining hygiene practices, all of which raise particular concern due to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. Furthermore, for both health clinics and schools, unsanitary and inadequate facilities for women and girls has the risk of discouraging them from going to clinics to receive treatments, and girls from attending school. Lastly, in 2021, ACTED conducted a multi-sectoral vulnerability assessment in WB which covered 802 households representing 4,789 individuals across WB governorates (including Hebron, Ramallah and Jerusalem) who were nominated by either local partners or Community leaders/representatives. Survey results showed that 716 (89.3%) of the assessed households reported that their ability to earn an income has been affected due to COVID-19. In addition, 602 (75%) of the households have been unable to pay any of their bills or cover usual costs due to a lack of money in 2021. The vast majority of the assesses households (93.7%) depend of negative coping mechanism to meet their household's needs.In terms of WASH needs, the assessment findings show that (1) 505 (63%) of the assessed housing units are not connected to municipal water network where it is available. 522 (65%) households do not have a minimum of 3 water sources in their housing unit (1 in kitchen sink, 1 hand wash basin in toilet, and 1 shower mixer tap). (2) 223 (27.8%) households do not have at minimum 1 functioning 1000L water tank. (3) 449 (56%) households do not have at least 1 drinking Water Tank of 200L with faucet, and drainage valve, stored in a safe location away from contamination elements. (4) 421 (52.5%) of the housing units do not have 1 kitchen (inside or outside) with adequate space of minimum 3m2. (5) 318 (39.6%) of the housing units that have a kitchen do not have a solid and cleanable preparation counter and sink, and is separated from toilet by solid wall. (6) 506 (63%) of the assessed housing units do not have a minimum 1 toilet and shower/bathing space per housing unit of minimum 3.5 to 4m2, trapped and vented to the outside, and connected to public sewage system, or to private sewage disposal system (e.g. cesspit, septic tank). (7) 171 (21.3%) of the assessed households do not have any type of kitchen and for those who have one, 546 (68%) highlighted the needs for its rehabilitation. (8) 330 (41%) of the assessed households relay on the trucked water as main water accesses for domestic and drinking. Nonetheless, such tanker trucks are poorly maintained and outdated, thus resulting in water quality concerns during transportation. (9) 348 (43.4%) households reported the need for maintenance/ replacement of the existing storage units. (10) 164 (20.5%) household do not have any type of latrine, and 105 (13%) share a latrine with other households, while 81 (10%) of them reported practicing open defecation as main coping mechanism. The majority of those households that have a latrine use pit toilet with slab/Arabic toilet.

2 - Activities

Act 1. Following the technical assessment of selected schools and health clinics, in collaboration with MoE and MoH and with special focus on needs of people with disabilities (PWD), as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns, Bills of Quantities (BOQs) will be developed for18 institutions outlining rehabilitation and maintenance WASH needs and related action plans. Contractors will be selected through a transparent tender process. ACTED/ARIJ will conduct regular follow-up and monitoring visits to ensure respect of the plan and of the WASH and SPHERE standards, as well as to provide corrective actions where needed. ACTED/ARIJ will provide cleaning staff in targeted schools and HCFs with operation and maintenance training to ensure sustainability. Moreover, HCFs will receive environmental and personal cleaning kits. Act 2. Following the technical assessment, the project team will select 3 water sources with the highest rehabilitation needs to be targeted for maintenance. BoQ and workplans will be developed and validated by the PWA. The aim is to cover the provision of water facilities’ critical operation and maintenance materials and tools, such as chlorine dosing pump. Furthermore, following the dire need in al Khalayleh Bedouins community, for water network installation and extension and HH connections, ACTED engineers will conduct technical field visits and develop BOQ with support of PWA engineers and community representatives. ACTED will launch separate tender process for both sources and network to select contractors to proceed to the works and will conduct regular follow-up/monitoring visits on-site to ensure works are completed as per BOQs and in respect of WASH and SPHERE standards. For the water sources, costs of the rehabilitation will be cost-shared with communities to ensure ownership. Also, ACTED/ARIJ will train the water service providers staff on operation and maintenance to ensure sustainability. Act 3. ACTED/ARIJ will identify the 82 most vulnerable HHs in target communities through transparent application processes and socio-economic/protection vulnerability assessments. Once the 82 HHs are selected, ACTED/ARIJ will develop tailored BOQs with each HH, to establish rehabilitation needs and costs. During the technical assessment, the project team will ensure that specific needs of PWD, as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns, are integrated into the BoQs.A self-repair approach will be used with the provision of restricted cash in 3 instalments. ACTED/ARIJ engineers will monitor progress and address technical issues through regular visits. To confirm the use of funds, assess beneficiary satisfaction and formulate recommendations, ACTED will conduct a Post-Distribution Monitoring involving both male and female household members.The provision of restricted cash assistance to 82 HHs will enable them to adopt adequate WASH practices by facilitating rehabilitation of their WASH facilities, especially those related to hygiene, protection, and health. All HHs will receive Hygiene kits and for those with limited water storage capacity will receive PEtanks.For 24 HHs who do not have latrines, ACTED and ARIJ will provide a new latrine to make sure that the HHs do not share latrines with other HHs or practice open defecation. Act 4. Following the assessment result, the most vulnerable 30 water wheeled tanks will be targeted for maintenance through cash assistance and following a tailored BoQs and workplan. Truck owners will be required to cost share 20% of the works to evidence their commitment to servicing the water needs of their communities and to reduce misallocation of funds. The assistance will be provided in two instalments (60% and 40%, the last on reimbursement basis). Technical staff will conduct follow up visits to the community to ensure that tanker truck owners are implementing their BoQs.For specific items in the BoQs that needs specialized-workers to implement such as epoxy paint, ACTED/ARIJ will consider hiring professional workers/contractors to ensure a no harm approach. In terms of sustainability, on-site awareness-raising sessions for truck drivers and supervisors will be conducted and a check-list for the drivers on the needed requirements produced to have their trucks licenced and on the safe handling of water transportation.Act 5. Upon reception of the OCHA trigger, ACTED will conduct post-incident assessments (community-level structure tool) within 2 days. In cases where community WASH infrastructure is demolished/confiscated, ACTED engineers will conduct an additional technical assessment to develop BOQs. The emergency responses will be provided following a contractor identified through a competitive procurement process and supported by regular follow-up from ACTED field staff to ensure quality completion of the needed works. Based on trends, ACTED is expecting to cover the rehabilitation of 5 water infrastructures following demolition incidents in 2022 or to cover needs arising from pending incidents from 2021.Act 6. Throughout the project, ACTED’s community-based protection approach will be followed to ensure solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee better respect of the safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups. ACTED will set up 8 CBPCs in the targeted communities (of which at least 30% will be women) and train members on the main protection-related topics (protection, PSEA, case referrals) climate change, disaster-risk reduction, legal framework, etc. If similar entities are already established in the targeted communities, ACTED involve them in the implementation and into the above-mentioned trainings. Finally, across all activities beneficiaries will be made aware of ACTED’s complaint response mechanisms and PSEA awareness materials will be distributed. ACTED will ensure that a standard close on PSEA is included in all contracts in accordance with the IASC principles/guid

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

In kind assistance ensures an equitable and harmonized response to all beneficiaries in all areas, following the relevant cluster standards for kits components. ACTED will go through a transparent procurement process to purchase items, to ensure the quality/price balance. Kits will be delivered directly to the field to ensure equal access to all beneficiaries, mainly the most vulnerable such as PwD and the elderly. As noted in the 2021 MSNA findings, cash assistance is beneficiaries’ preferred method, especially for women and PwD, as it provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost-efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will contribute towards restoring the Palestinian economy. Finally, cash assistance ensures multisectoral coverage, while keeping beneficiaries involved all along the activities, as they can choose the more adapted items to cover their multiple needs. Service provider solution is chosen for those activities, such as rehabilitation work or trainings, which require specific technical skills. In this way ACTED will ensure the quality and effectiveness of the results and ensure a no harm approach for beneficiary, especially for the shelter and WASH rehabilitation. For this methodology, ACTED will identify experienced and professional contractors who will be selected through a transparent procurement process thereby ensuring the completion of activities on time whilst fulfilling all technical requirements.

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED and ARIJ will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, whatsapp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co-applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that co-applicants learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, whatsapp, email address, complaints boxes and directly through ACTED staff during field visits. This plurality of means aims to ensure CRM accessibility to all, including vulnerable groups with specific needs. At the first stage of the project, during the assessment, field officers will provide a CRM card to each BNF, including all communication channels mentioned above, and explanations on the CRM, to ensure that all BNF feel comfortable and in measure to reach it if needed. Female staff, part of the team to which BNF will be able to refer to, will notably ensure female BNF feel safe to complain. AME staff will dedicate specific time to vulnerable groups during field visits to re-explain the CRM and emphasise that BNF can directly complain to them during visits - if one person would not have the capacity to use/access a phone/email address or the complaint box (notably children, PwD, and the elderly). ACTED’s commitment to accountability to affected populations is fully integrated into its recruitment process, staff inductions, trainings and performance management and partnership agreements. All ACTED staff working for the ACTED oPt mission are trained on the Code of Conduct, on the Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection Policies. Trainings on PSEA aim to ensure that all staff understand the Code of Conduct and the PSEA policy, their obligation to report any allegation or suspicion and the sanction measures taken for any breach of the Code of Conduct. Staff are also trained on recognizing sexual exploitation and abuse situations and concrete steps to follow for reporting any concerns or suspicions. In early March 2021, all ACTED staff also participated in the induction session on PSEA organized by the Protection Cluster, to strengthen their capacities to recognize exploitation and abuse situations, as well as adopt a good behaviour to report.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED and ARIJ will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary households to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis. Finally, all activities will be conducted in strict adherence of COVID-19 mitigation measures including social distancing and personal protective equipment (PPE) to minimise the risk of COVID-19 transmission among beneficiaries, staff and stakeholder alike.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED’s Protection Officer will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per household will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are taken into account, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, the elderly and other marginalized persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. In addition, CBPC members and ACTED staff will be present to ensure the integration of household concerns into BoQ and workplan design. In the event beneficiaries are dissatisfied with their respective BoQ and/or workplan an appeal mechanism will be offered through the CBPCs and ACTED’s CRM. If the CBPCs or ACTED/ARIJ staff consider the request justified, a new BoQ and/or workplan will be produced by a different agronomist. To mitigate this, all staff involved in the assessment process will undergo extensive refresher training in protection standards prior to the assessments to ensure equal and impartial access to assistance. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. For instance, women involved in farming activities in the targeted areas will be approached and highly encouraged to participate in the CBPCs to ensure that membership remains at 30% female at a minimum. After all, women represent a significant but underserved segment of the agricultural workforce in the target areas. Similar efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBPCs will conduct household visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including elderly, people with disabilities) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
