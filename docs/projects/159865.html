Project Summary

Mercy-USA seeks funding for twelve months for emergency programming in the Central South Zone of Somalia. Water and excreta related diseases such as diarrhea, dysentery, worm infestation, and other respiratory diseases and communicable diseases such as; scabies, skin and eye infections still remain a major health concern in rural settlements of Mudug, Gedo and Hiiraan Regions. These communities continue to incur health-related economic loss due to inadequate hygiene practices resulting from mortality and morbidity of preventable fecal-oral diseases. The most affected population are those with low immunity including children under the age of 5, pregnant and lactating mothers, people with long term illnesses and the elderly. According to Mercy-USA’s rapid needs assessment carried out in Hiiraan, Gedo Banadir and Mudug regions in June 2019, barriers to achieving safe levels of hygiene in this context include cyclical movement of people, lack of adequate water, lack of adequate household sanitation facilities and lack of understanding of the importance of hygiene in relation to family health.
Mercy-USA’s hygiene promotion strategy will seek to build on local knowledge, practices, and preferences in order to prevent diarrheal diseases through the widespread adoption of good hygiene practices. Formative research and observation in the four regions of operation have identified poor hand-washing practices at critical times, poor household water treatment and storage practices and poor disposal and management of excreta as the key risks of diarrheal pathogen transmission to children. The program will aim to improve these identified and prioritized key risk behaviors through hygiene promotion activities that will focus on four key areas: awareness on safe water treatment and storage practices, good hand-washing practices, food handling, and safe excreta disposal. Sphere standards and the WASH cluster guidelines will be used to guide all hygiene promotion activities.
Mercy-USA will target IDP camps in Elwak district – Gedo region, Buloburte District – Hiraan Region and Hobyo Distict – Mudug Region with the construction of 80 VIP latrines and 80 hand-washing stations and rehabilitation of 5 communal boreholes. Families living in these IDP camps are displaced due to loss of livelihoods from ongoing drought and clan conflict. These IDPs face many hardships including poverty, malnutrition, lack of water and large sanitation gaps where women and children do not have adequate access to latrines or handwashing stations, leading to open defecation and exposure of children to fecal oral diseases. Identified IDP camps are located in rural communities and are home to over 5,000 households most of whom consist of women, children and the elderly. Mercy-USA will leverage WASH efforts to these IDP camps through providing adequate sanitation infrastructure in conjunction with hygiene promotion and water supply to ensure that the camps do not become a breeding ground for AWD/Cholera and other diseases during emergencies.
Mercy-USA seeks to construct 3 rainwater harvesting systems and 18 VIP latrines in identified schools that do not have access to adequate safe drinking water and sanitation facilities (1 school each in Hobyo, Mahaas and El Wak districts). Through a rapid needs assessment conducted in July 2019, Mercy-USA observed that most schools in rural settings do not have access to water within the school compound. This has forced children as young as 6 years old to walk for average distances of 1km outside of the secure compound of their school in search of water during break sessions. Observation of available sanitation facilities in the schools assessed show that latrines are few in number, dirty and harbor vectors. Teachers have reported that lack of access to adequate safe water has led to a decrease in school enrollment. Children do not have access to safe drinking water, which has created a poor learning environment in schools.

Project Activities

WATER SUPPLY SUB SECTOR • Rehabilitation and protection of 12 shallow wells (4-Gedo, 4-Mudug and 4-Hiraan regions). • Rehabilitation and protection of 5 boreholes (2-Gedo, 2-Mudug and 1-Hiraan regions). Rehabilitation activities will include, conversion of inefficient generator-powered boreholes to hybrid models by installing solar-powered pumping systems, construction of elevated tanks, piping extensions of water sources and construction of water kiosks in IDP settlements and primary schools in areas with high rates of malnutrition and AWD to improve access to safe drinking water. • Shallow well chlorination campaigns targeting 30 shallow wells (10 in Gedo, 10 in Mudug and 10 in Hiraan Regions)• Training of 17 Water User Associations (WUAs) to ensure that targeted water sources (12 shallow wells and 5 boreholes) remain safe, effective and operational during and after disasters in order to provide safe and free drinking water for vulnerable IDPs. • Construct rainwater harvesting systems in three primary schools which will be connected to the class roofs to collect rainwater during rainy seasonsSANITATION SUB SECTOR • Construction of 80 VIP latrines and 80 handwashing stations for IDP communities in Hiraan, Gedo and Mudug Regions.• Construction of 20 VIP latrines and 20 handwashing stations for rural Health Facilities providing free MCH services in Hiraan, Gedo and Mudug Regions.• Construction of 18 VIP latrines and 18 handwashing stations for primary schools in communities affected by high rates of malnutrition in Hiraan, Gedo and Mudug Regions.• Provide cleaning kits (brooms, buckets, liquid soap, and gloves) to 3 primary schools, 10 MCHs and IDP settlements targeted with sanitation infrastructure to encourage good cleanliness standards are kept at all sanitation facilities. School faculty, Health facility management committee and IDP community will be in charge of operating and maintaining these facilities. • Provide continuous on-the-job training for health workers, teachers and school WASH club members to emphasize the importance of cleanliness of latrines. • Promote sanitation and safe solid waste disposal in households through hygiene education. • Train Ministry of Water staff on the protection of water sources and allowing them to be front and center of program activities. • Provision for appropriate disposal of menstrual material will be provided.• Separate VIP latrines will be constructed for male and female, sited in safe locations and illuminated at night. HYGIENE SUB SECTOR • At the community level, the program will train 10 Hygiene promoters (HP) 50% men and 50% women who will supervise the work of 100 community mobilizers in Banadie, Hiraan, Gedo and Mudug Regions.• All hygiene promotion staff (110) will undergo a 5 day comprehensive training on PHAST. • Distribution of 2,000 standard hygiene kits to guardians of children who have been admitted to the nutrition SFP/OTP program in 10 health facilities in Banadie, Hiraan, Gedo and Mudug Regions.• All health facility staff and teachers will be facilitated to come up with an AWD/Cholera preparedness plan based on their existing resources. Household Water Treatment and Storage (HHWTS) networking group will be formed at each health facility. • Support for one KAP/coverage survey to measure program indicators. COORDINATION • Regular update of 4W matrix; Participating in regional and district level coordination meetings. Initiate district coordination meetings in locations where there are none. • Participating in cluster technical working groups when required. ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH • Construction of one standard incinerator with a waste separation zone and waste bin for each of the 10 health facilities in need of such items.• The operator of the incinerator will receive appropriate training, demonstration and a complete set of protective clothing and equipment.

Mitigation Measures

Communities are forced to leave their place of residence to access humanitarian assistance (humanitarian assistance as pull factor)The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. • Mercy-USA will continuously map the location of beneficiaries to better target IDPs and pastoralists ensuring that services are provided at the convenience of the target beneficiary. • Design of program activities will be guided by the needs on the ground. Mercy-USA will engage with the community to carry out needs assessments that will in turn be used to design program activities. • Mercy-USA will reach beneficiaries who are located far from health centers through the provision of outreach activities that will provide hygiene promotion and distribution of hygiene kits. • Mercy-USA will select CHWs from the local community ensuring that no settlement is left out in the program geographical reach. CHWs will carry out hygiene promotion at the household level. • Mercy-USA will train program staff on the importance of convenient service delivery that does not put beneficiaries at risk or disrupt their household activities. Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistanceThe following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk.• Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to gain an in-depth understanding of marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Mercy-USA will ensure that community selection committees comprise of at least 50% representation of various groups of marginalized people including IDPs, minority tribes and people with disability. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability ensuring that marginalized communities are reached by the program. • Design of WASH infrastructure will factor in the needs of the marginalized communities. For example boreholes will be solar powered to reduce the running cost hence providing free drinking water. Zero tariffs/costs on drinking water will ensure that no vulnerable members of the community are left without water. The WUA will charge a nominal fee for livestock owners. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible my all members of the community especially marginalized communities. Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk.Mercy-USA will have in place three forms of confidential feedback mechanisms to ensure that all community members are reached including illiterate people, those who cannot physically travel and finally those who do not have resources to feedback. The follow mechanisms 1. Suggestion boxes 2. Hotline numbers 3. Focal persons who will established an AAP desk at community level. 4. Community mobilization will be intensively carried out to ensure the uptake of the formal feedback mechanisms established. 5. Mercy-USA will continuously monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of the mechanisms established and make improvements on a continuous basis.

Please provide further explanation

The protection environment at target IDPs is not safe especially for women accessing sanitation and water supply facilities. Mercy-USA will locate water points near households in the IDPs. Mercy-USA will map the vulnerabilities of the IDP communities using GIS to identify safe and suitable location of the water points and sanitation facilities protecting the women, children and vulnerable people. Mercy-USA will seek to ensure that there is a sense of community ownership of the program both at the health facility, community and school levels. Mercy-USA will ensure that the communities commit to the longer term operational and maintenance costs of all WASH infrastructure upon the exit of Mercy-USA’s program. The costs of management, operation and maintenance of all infrastructure are in line with the capacity of the communities to pay, as verified through initial community discussions during the needs assessment process. The communities will establish the mode of payment during program implementation. Community participation in these interventions is important and will be emphasized at all stages of the project, including identification of locations to be supported, supervision of construction work in line with cultural expectations, provision of labor for construction and monitoring of usage and maintenance of the facilities. The community will thus gain responsibility for the success of the project and for behavioral change among their children, and by extension the community at large. Mercy-USA will educate the management committees on all major and minor future needs. Guidelines will also be given on decommissioning of latrines that cannot be rehabilitated. This will allow them to have measures in place for maintenance of facilities. Mercy-USA has also factored sustainability in the design of the latrines; designs are specific to the needs of users (school children and women recovering from child birth). All latrines are conveniently desludgible and quality materials will be used in the construction, including correct curing processes and concrete classes. Mercy-USA will ensure that entitlement of beneficiaries to services and utilities is legally recognized before and during project implementation. Mercy-USA will share with all revenant ministries and local authorities the planned project activities and implementation strategies. Beneficiary selection process will carried out through the community beneficiary committee and selected beneficiaries will be well informed of the services/utilities that they will be receiving.
