Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address the critical educational needs of internally displaced children aged 3-17 in Ngala, Dikwa, and Bama L.G.As of Borno state, considering the unique challenges they face due to the ongoing crisis, climate change impact, and gender-specific concerns. The project will not only provide immediate educational assistance but also focus on sustainability and holistic development.
Target:
The project aims to enroll 10,000 internally displaced pupils in each Local Government Area (LGA) - Ngala, Dikwa, and Bama, totaling 30,000 children. The target population includes boys and girls aged 3-17, with a specific focus on those facing unique challenges, such as children with disabilities, girls at risk of gender-based violence, and boys vulnerable to recruitment by armed groups.
Key Activities:
Infrastructure Improvement: Renovation and construction of classrooms to create safe and conducive learning environments.
Water and Sanitation Facilities: Installation of proper facilities for maintaining a healthy and safe school environment.
Teacher Training and Recruitment: Training of existing teachers and recruitment of additional educators to ensure an adequate teacher-student ratio.
Inclusive Education: Implementation of measures and curriculum adaptations to cater to children with disabilities.
Gender-Sensitive Initiatives: Creation of a secure learning environment for girls, provision of menstrual health resources, and implementation of gender-responsive curriculum.
Community Engagement and Awareness: Conducting awareness programs to promote education and gender equality within the community.
Life Skills and Vocational Training: Introduction of programs to enhance practical skills for adolescents, preparing them for future employment.
Enrollment Process:
Community-based education centers will be established in collaboration with local leaders.
Identification and registration of internally displaced pupils, with a focus on inclusivity.
Promotion of the project through community awareness campaigns to encourage enrollment.
Tailored outreach initiatives to ensure the participation of children with disabilities, girls, and vulnerable boys.

1 - Needs

Access to education for girls and boys of school-age (3-17) has been obstructed by a range of complex challenges, including poor and unsafe school infrastructure, lack of proper water and sanitation facilities, insecurity, attacks on educational facilities, shortage of teachers, lack of premises adapted for children with disabilities and other protection concerns. These challenges have varying effects on different population subsets, for instance, girls are likely face the risk of early marriage, while boys may force to recruitment by armed groups. The crisis has exacerbated the situation for children with disabilities, who constitute 15% of the population (8% of whom are girls), depriving them of essential, needs-based educational services. In fact, 56 percent of all public primary classrooms are deemed inadequate and nearly 1,400 schools have been destroyed since the onset of the crisis in the North-east . The 2022 and 2023 flooding and climate change affected 132 schools depriving 50,490 girls, 48,510 boys and 9,000 children (4,860 girls) with disabilities of access to educational facilities . Climate change related crises will continue as a significant threat to children and causing disruptions in educational services. 9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

The proposed Integrated Education and Development Program for internally displaced children in Ngala, Dikwa, and Bama is rooted in comprehensive assessments conducted by reputable organizations such as the Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA) in 2021, UNICEF, the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC), and the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (UNOCHA). These assessments have provided valuable insights into the educational landscape, challenges, and needs of the affected populations.Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA) 2021:JENA serves as a cornerstone for understanding the educational needs in crisis-affected areas. The assessment identified critical challenges, including poor and unsafe school infrastructure, shortage of teachers, insecurity, and specific protection concerns for vulnerable groups such as girls and children with disabilities.UNICEF Education Sector Assessment:UNICEF's assessment highlighted the multifaceted challenges obstructing access to education, emphasizing issues such as attacks on educational facilities, inadequate infrastructure, and the impact of climate change on the education sector. It underscored the urgency of interventions to address the unique needs of internally displaced children.Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) Findings:UBEC's findings contributed essential data on the inadequacy of public primary classrooms, emphasizing the urgent need for infrastructure improvement. The assessment identified the extent of destruction of schools since the onset of the crisis in the North-east, underscoring the gravity of the situation. https://reliefweb.int/report/nigeria/nigeria-situation-report-12-december-2023,
