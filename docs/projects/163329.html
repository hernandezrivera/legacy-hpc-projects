Project Summary

The overall goal of this project is to improve the living conditions of the affected populations (IDPs, refugees and host communities) through access to essential life-saving items, and by reducing vulnerability and realizing safety and human dignity through WASH, child protection/psychosocial support and early recovery/livelihoods.

Besoins / Needs

Over nine years, the humanitarian emergency in the Lake Chad region remains worrisome and more than 10 million people in these areas (northeastern Nigerian and part of Niger, chad and the far north of Cameroon) continue to face deep humanitarian crisis cause by violent conflicts, extreme poverty and climate change.Adding to this destitute situation, the entire environment of the locality is degraded due to abusive cut of the meager trees of region for firewood. The pressure on the environment becomes higher and higher. The soil is denuded leaving place to erosions and the desert. The refugees and IDPs, pregnant women, aged men and women, children are suffering the natural harsh climate change.Although humanitarian actors are present in the Lake Chad basin, the needs are very high as there are several gaps in service provision. As mentioned above, we have carried out an assessment and have cross-checked the results with the information received while participating in different humanitarian coordination platforms (UN clusters, technical working groups, etc.); and determined that the priority sectors are the following:1. WASH2. Livelihood/Early recovery3. Protection/Psychosocial SupportThis dire situation is further compounded for the conflict affected people with severe vulnerabilities. Therefore, there is a pressing necessity to provide immediate assistance to minimize loss of life and to ensure that the needs of the most vulnerable are covered. Access to water and basic sanitation remains problematic in this community, the assessment shows that the access to water is of 14.2 liters per person per day (out of 20 liters). The physio-chemical and bacteriological analysis carried out on 28 water points revealed that 9 are infected with fecal coli-forms. The epidemiological risks are major with regard to the rate of open defecation which remains high. In order to reduce these risks, drinking water supply and sanitation services must be provided or strengthened for those most affected.Livelihood/Early recovery being transversal, it includes strengthening synergies between sectors and the link between emergency response and people's sustainable development needs, to reduce vulnerability and build resilience.Displaced and refugee affected children and host communities are in great need of psychosocial support as well as prevention and care services. Unaccompanied and separated children need access to coordinated identification, documentation and reunification services while being secured in temporary foster families. Also, the social cohesion and peace promotion groups need to be strengthened both technically and materially.
