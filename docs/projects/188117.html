Project Summary

The projects intends to provide a multi-faceted response in an integrated and coherent way. Shafak's proposal is designed to supporting farmers and livestock herders needs of the vulnerable IDPs, returnees and HCs. Over 2022, the project comprises the following key intervention areas:
Activity1: 5600 Targeted winter crops farmers (wheat and barley) are able to resume and restore agricultural activities, to increase household income and improve consumption through provision of agriculture start-up kit that contribute to the cost of : i) agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; pest control, harvesting and V) extension services, VI) purchasing wheat.
Activity2: 2000 Targeted olive trees are able to resume and restore agricultural activities, to increase household income and improve consumption through provision of agriculture start-up kit that contribute to the cost of : i) agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; pest control, harvesting and V) extension services.
Activity3: 1000 Targeted forage crops farmers are able to resume and restore agricultural activities, to increase household income and improve consumption through provision of agriculture start-up kit that contribute to the cost of : i) agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; pest control, harvesting and V) extension services.
Activity 4: 6500 Targeted households are able to increase household income and maintain livestock activities through: i) provision of livestock inputs (500 kg of fodder per beneficiary 1500 beneficiaries) ; ii) associated training, iii) vaccination campaigns per health care calendar (4 campaigns, 200,000 head per campaign owned by 4,000 households).
Activity 5: 4000 Targeted households stabilize, maintain or increase their income through Cash-for-Work activities, which build and strengthen community related value chain infrastructure , disaster risk reduction, mechanical pest and vole control and olive trees harvesting, purification of wheat fields, and afforestation.
Activity 6: Rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure, irrigation channels, Markets, Mills, bakeries, storage, fodder mixers, feeder roads and silos.
Activity 7: Graduation Business Grants: 500 Small-Micro Enterprises through provision of business skills training and financial or in-kind support with value ranging from 500 to 1000 USD. Selected beneficiaries will have access to financial services, in addition to improving job skills and linkages to the job market. The identified participants will receive Business Development Skills training (BDS)including operations and financial management(food processing, production, agriculture and livestock enterprises.
Activity 8: 5000 HHs to be supported with regular food basket over 8 rounds.
Activity 9 : 15000 HHs to be supported with Cash/voucher based on populations preference and market dynamics
Activity 10 : 15000 RTEs to be distributed as response to emergencies and shocks
Activity 11: 2000 MT of flour to be distributed to bakeries to subsidize bread prices.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.
It is worth mentioning that about 50% of 2022 budget is secured up to date of this submission.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities. In addition to protection threats, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021.Shafak conducted multi sectorial need assessment between Dec 2021 and Jan 2022 In Idleb and Aleppo. The key findings:•The estimated monthly income for the HHs in the assessed areas is less.• A fluctuation in the value of agricultural products and failure to keep pace with the rising costs and the rise in the dollar exchange rate.• Lack of effective quality control over agricultural production inputs such as seeds, pesticides and fertilizers, and entry of non-conforming items.•Furthermore, all the communities are not able to meet their basic needs (food, health shelter.) and most of them (more than 75% are relying on humanitarian assistance to meet these needs. Even though, all communities adopted in negative copying strategies by reducing their food consumption and rely on other CSI (copying strategies).• Most of the households depended on humanitarian assistance. And the most prevalent dependency in the community .

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Losing access to operation area due to absence of authority’s approval: Coordination and good introduction of the project to Local Authorities (LA) and communities, getting approval and signing MoUs before starting the activities, maintaining good relationship with LA.2-Deterioration of the security situationSeeking Clearance from the safety and security department before the planned activities. Suspending the activities and reporting the incident directly to the partner.3-Low participation by women in CFW, SMEs, and training activitiesCommunity mobilization for the project activities with more focusing on women participation. Having female staff. Consult women about time and location of activities. Target beneficiaries from the same location. Include a small percentage of Male to accompany women during CFW, all associated people to be inducted about PSEA and sign code of conduct, increase the daily wages for CFW to secure safe transportation for women as groups.4- Some livestock holders may reject the intervention of the vaccination campaign Shafak’s team will conduct a community mobilization session for the local authorities and community leaders. A media campaign will be held to prepare the community for the vaccination activities. 5-Loss of access to operational areas, and displacement of livestock holders:selection of the locations was for the most stable areas, b) in case of such occasions, the activity will be suspended and a plan B will be in place instead
