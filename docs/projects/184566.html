Project Summary

Malnutrition remains persistently high in Somalia. According to FSNAU SMART survey in July 2021, the overall median GAM rate in Somalia was Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score) in 2021. The drivers of acute malnutrition include high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation, reduced access to milk, and food insecurity. Urgent treatment and nutrition support is required for approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 213,400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Integrated interventions are needed to support recovery and prevent deterioration of the nutrition situation.
This proposed project will provide lifesaving integrated Management Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) Programme to vulnerable communities in Awdal, Bay, Bakool, Lower Shabelle, Banadir, Bari, Galgadud, Hiiran, Lower Juba, Mudug, Nugaal, Sanaag, Sool, Togdheer, Waqooyi Galbed. The project is aiming to reach 594,592 IDPs and host communities affected by drought, conflict displacement and climate change hazards in the targeted locations.
In addition to the CMAM service and Infant and young child feeding, (IYCF-E) services will be integrated with the CMAM. Trained IYCF-E counsellors will provide support to PLWs and care givers of young children with difficulties in adopting the optimal infant and young child feeding practices recommended by WHO/UNICEF. To reach the population in need in target regions with lifesaving integrated nutrition Programme delivery, the project will operate fixed and mobile nutrition facilities.
Save the Children proposes a multi-sectoral, integrated health and nutrition response to respond to vulnerable, IDPs, and marginalized communities in Somalia. We will work with MOH to improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services in static health and outreach sites to deliver an integrated package of promotive, preventive, and curative maternal, nutrition services. SC will build the local capacity by providing training and technical support to local government, health facility staff, the Community Nutrition volunteers, representatives of local communities to raise awareness, promote nutrition, and strengthen linkages between the community and health facilities.
In addition to that Save the Children will increase awareness in the community about the rights of people with disabilities. Children with disabilities are generally hidden from public view including during the active case finding sessions in the community. SC will involve CWDs, providing and affording children the space and opportunity to initiate activities and advocate for themselves.
To sustain efforts to reduce acute mortality and morbidity related to acute malnutrition we will reach and support the following number of beneficiaries (including 5% Persons with Disabilities).
Total: 594,592 (Boys: 139,192, Girls: 148,955, Women: 200,000 & Men:106,445) IDPs: 112,972 & Host communities: 481,620

1. - Projects activities

Save the Children proposes a multi-sectoral, integrated health and nutrition response to respond to vulnerable, IDPs, and marginalized host communities in Somalia. Save the Children will work with MOH to improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services in static health and outreach sites to deliver an integrated package of promotive, preventive, and curative maternal, nutrition services. SC will build the local capacity by providing training and technical support to local government, health facility staff, the Community Nutrition volunteers, representatives of local communities to raise awareness, promote nutrition, and strengthen linkages between the community and health facilities. The nutrition program will be based on the Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach to maximize the coverage of, and access to, life-saving child and maternal focused nutrition services for children under five years and PLW. The CMAM approach empowers community volunteers and care givers trained on and provided with MUAC to screen, identify and refer children and PLW with acute malnutrition for treatment before they become seriously ill, and ensure proper treatment by conducting frequent home visits of children. In addition to the CMAM, service and Infant and young child feeding (IYCF-E) services will be integrated with the CMAM. Trained IYCF-E counsellors will provide support to PLWs and care givers of young children with difficulties in adopting the optimal infant and young child feeding practices recommended by WHO/UNICEF. In addition, SC will support to increase awareness and sensitization on COVID-19 amongst vulnerable populations in target areas using the RCCE strategy. The CMAM and IYCF services will be adapted for the COVID-19 infection prevention as per national and nutrition cluster guidelines, and especially all IYCF counselling cards will be adapted to address breastfeeding in COVID-19 context. Health and nutrition staff will be trained on mainstreaming of COVID-19 interventions in regular service delivery.• Conduct nutrition, health and hygiene promotion sessions and dissemination key messages on health diet, IYCF, COVID19 prevention in the nutrition centers and community • Establishing and training of Mother to Mother and father to father supporting groups for the promotion of optimal IYCF practices • Establish and equipping Mother baby areas for the provision of safe space for breastfeeding and promotion of optimal IYCF practices • Provide micronutrient supplementation to include Vitamin A, MMN and de-worming for children 6-59 months• Integrating nutrition with WASH, health, and Cash sectors by establishing smooth referral mechanism to ensure the synergies to address the underlying cause of malnutrition • Procurement and distribution PPE for nutrition centers to prevent COVID-19 spread in the community and in nutrition workers • Provision of Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme for children and PLW• Conduct pilot study on effectiveness of Family Health workers /CHWs treatment of SAM/MAM children without complication at their home using simplified combined protocol as alternative of empowering the community in the context of COVID-19• Conduct nutrition assessments: SMART surveys, coverage surveys, KAP survey/establish Nutrition surveillance using community health workers • Coordinate activities with the government and Nutrition Cluster
