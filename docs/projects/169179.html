Project Summary

The overarching aim of this project is to ensure the protection of vulnerable populations, to provide access of vulnerable households, for both displaced and host communities, IN ADDITION TO MIGRANT WORKER POPULATIONS, to physically adequate and safe shelters with security of tenure. This is accomplished through the provision of protection-focused shelter assistance that addresses the inadequacies in terms of the safety of vulnerable households.
This project will apply the standards set through technical guidelines on selected shelter activities drafted by the sector working group, UN partners and LRC to maximize impact, complement services and avoid duplication.
In response to the crisis, Caritas Lebanon (CL) will provide cash for shelter activities and minor renovation direct interventions to enable vulnerable households whose shelters were damaged to carry out essential repairs to ensure protection from the elements. The assistance will be provided as direct renovation interventions through a preset renovation methodology and quality assurance guidelines.
The intervention will ensure that rehabilitation of shelters will not lead to evictions or rent increases (do no harm) for the tenants. Finally, this intervention will be accompanied by services provided by other projects that fall under other sectors such as psychological first aid and psychosocial support.
Caritas Lebanon (CL) will organize trainings and ongoing technical capacity building through direct mentoring all along the project’s implementation.
The emergency response will assist 2180 AFFECTED HOUSEHOLDS (approx. 10,900 INDIVIDUALS) in the economically vulnerable neighborhoods of Beirut and Metn. Eligible households will be selected impartially strengthening social cohesion in the affected areas.
The selection criteria will include
- Inclusion: This sector will primarily target the general need of shelter of the vulnerable population affected by the blast while ensuring the inclusion of different age groups, gender and diversity, ESPECIALLY THE DISPLACED AND MIGRANT WORKER POPULATIONS.
- Targeted assistance to the most vulnerable households: Caritas will determine eligibility based on the needs assessment reports set out by UN Habitat and LRC. Special attention will be given to
o Female headed households
o Elderlies
o Household with PWD
o Household with dead family member
o Household with heavily injured member
- Coordination with local actors: Understanding the holistic needs of affected communities requires inter-sectoral collaboration. Shelter assistance will be coordinated between partners within the sector and with activities of other sectors for improved impact and complementarity of interventions where applicable, including but not limited to protection and health concerns.
- Safeguarding social stability and livelihood: Coordination with the above mentioned partners to ensure adequate housing conditions

1 - Needs

Caritas Lebanon was one of the first NGOs to arrive in Rmeil, Burj Hammoud, and Mar Mikhael, to assess the situation and provide urgent emergency relief such as food, water, and medication. The primary needs assessments highlighted the following concerns for the shelter sector:- Temporary acute shelter will need to be provided to fully-damaged households: According to municipalities, many buildings have already collapsed; others are at risk of collapsing. Based on ACTED’s primary assessment, a large part of houses in some areas within 1 and 2-mile radius are turning inhabitable. However, many households have not evacuated yet and are dwelling and refusing to leave their homes, as they cannot afford to move out to another house, or do not have family to host them. Many families visited during distribution of food and assistance told volunteers that they are waiting for the army to declare their buildings at risk of collapsing to leave. Others who have left are returning during the day to look over their belongings, many claiming that many looting incidents have already taken place. BASED ON AN INITIAL RAPID EVALUATION, IOM ESTIMATES THAT 24,600 MIGRANT WORKERS HAVE BEEN AFFECTED ACROSS SEVERAL DISTRICTS OF GREATER BEIRUT. WHILE THE IOM AND OTHER ORGANIZATIONS ARE IN THE PROCESS OF CONDUCTING A MORE IN-DEPTH ASSESSMENT ON THE IMPACT OF THE EXPLOSION ON MIGRANT WORKERS, UNOFFICIAL SOURCES CLAIMED THAT SEVERAL MIGRANT WORKERS DIED DURING THE EXPLOSIONS AND MORE THAN 30 ARE MISSING. - Partially-damaged households will be in need of support to become habitable: According to UN, The Shelter sector estimates that 29,771 apartments (89,313 ind.) have minor damages, 17,100 apartments (51,300 ind.) have moderate damages and 1,144 buildings (30,660 ind.) have heavy damages. Minor and moderate damages could mean broken windows, doors, destroyed appliances, destroyed furniture. Therefore, more than 46,871 apartments could need support and assistance to become habitable. While many families are still residing in their own homes, some have lost their kitchen use and are asking for daily basic assistance. Assistance could mean anything from NFIs such as home mattresses, pillows etc. to home appliances to provide their basic needs such as an oven, a fridge. As part of a rapid assessment with the Governor of Beirut and the municipality of Beirut, LRC has identified Gemmayzeh, Mar Mikhael, Ashrafiyeh and Karantina as priority districts. Door-to-door emergency needs assessment has been undertaken in several socio-economically vulnerable neighborhoods, including Bourj Hammoud, Nabaa, Bachoura and Furn El Chebbek. The Governor of Mount Lebanon has identified Bourj Hammoud, Jdeideh, Sin El Fil, and Jal el Dib (Seaside) and Zalka (Seaside) as in need of shelter support. Based on ACTED’s assessment, widespread structural damage is reported at the capital of Beirut and its surrounding suburbs. - 70-90% of houses in Bourj Hammoud are destroyed or partially destroyed- 70% of houses in Nabaa are only partially damaged and habitable- 80% of houses in Karantina are destroyed or partially destroyed- 70% of houses in Karm el Zeytoun are partially destroyed- Houses in Geitaoui are only partially damaged Caritas Lebanon is awaiting the results of the subsequent assessment being performed by the Disaster Risk Management unit in the Prime Minister’s Office and the Order of Engineers and Architects (OEA) with UN-Habitat, in order to ensure that all households assisted with shelter NFI are cleared of all infrastructural risks. Caritas Lebanon will coordinate closely with the coordinators of the Shelter Working Group on next steps.

2 - Activities

SO2 Contribute to multi-sectoral longer term repair or rehabilitation of areas affected • Rehabilitation of apartments/houses moderately damaged (level 2). Caritas will work with its team of engineers to assess lightly damaged apartments/houses and identify the urgent needs such as windows, doors etc. A group of pre-qualified contractors will be selected to conduct the works following strict guidelines and regulations in addition to pricing based on the market assessments conducted on construction material and labor fees. There will also be the option for the households to conduct the works and CL would make payments to contractors after verifying and confirming completion of works. Minor renovation works are estimated for 1500 USD for 2180 HOUSEHOLDS (approx. 10,900 INDIVIDUALS). i. Conduct market assessment (preliminarily: Emergency Market Mapping Analysis – EMMA) for construction materials by external consultantii. Select households based on vulnerability and damage to shelter iii. Inform beneficiaries about the program, rights and duties, financial service provider, complain mechanisms and basics of shelter rehabilitation iv. Households living in rented shelters sign tripartite agreement between Caritas, landlord, and tenant to protect them from rent increases or eviction and ensure that the repairs are done in sufficient quality to the landlord v. Initiate, conduct and finalize the renovation works under close supervision of engineers (for direct renovation interventions)vi. Monitor outcomes through home visits and post distributing monitoring surveys HAVING A LONG-STANDING PROGRAM DEDICATED TO SERVICES FOR REFUGEES, DISPLACED AND MIGRANT WORKER GROUPS IN LEBANON IN THE SECTORS OF PROTECTION, HEALTH, EDUCATION, SHELTER AND OTHERS, CARITAS WILL MAKE SURE TO CONTINUE ITS MISSION TO SUPPORT THESE VULNERABLE GROUPS BY ENSURING THAT THEY RECEIVE SHELTER ASSISTANCE UNDER THIS PROGRAM THROUGH REHABILITATION OF HOUSEHOLDS DAMAGED BY THE EXPLOSION.
