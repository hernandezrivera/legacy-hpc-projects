Project Summary

Funded by the SCHF, Basmeh, and Zeitooneh have been implementing the Humanitarian Field Officers project in Northwest Syria since 2017, to provide humanitarian actors with highly needed information on the ongoing response in the region and to support OCHA with regular sectoral and inter-sectoral assessments and monitoring activities to track the SCHF-funded projects. Since then, B&Z has expanded its operations to include community engagement and obtain feedback from the community on their needs.
Through the HFOs team that is stationed in different locations of the NSAG-controlled areas, this project will enable cross-border activities from Turkey into Syria to tap into trusted, up-to-date, and local sources of information, and disseminate it to be used by the humanitarian community. In consultation with OCHA Turkey, the team will be overseen by the B&Z organization in Gaziantep, Turkey. The project is built around three components and is intended to provide broad support for the formal coordination system, particularly clusters/ICCG.
1-Information gathering
This component focuses on obtaining data on the ground in order to provide reports on the humanitarian situation and context, as well as to support response planning and advocacy. For use in various platforms and products, the data is shared with different units in OCHA. Clusters will be given information on critical needs and gaps, including urgent situations to be addressed. The HFO will also support on the collection of data related to humanitarian access constraints that help with the trend analysis of the Access Monitoring and Reporting framework in close coordination with the Access Unit.
2-Support the monitoring of SCHF-funded projects.
The HFOs will keep track of SCHF-funded projects. These monitoring activities will be carried out at the SCHF's request and in accordance with the SCHF's monitoring criteria. As projects supported by the SCHF are assessed through the clusters, monitoring them directly increases the quality and accountability of the inter-cluster response. The Humanitarian Field Assistants (HFA) will also receive training on M&E tools and methods to ensure that they collect accurate and relevant data.
3-Community engagement and AAP :
The project team will conduct community engagement activities to strengthen the humanitarian response by facilitating meetings with the affected communities, humanitarian partners, and other stakeholders to obtain feedback from the community on their needs and shed the light on the appropriate response. The project will enhance the capacity of humanitarian partners on AAP, gender, and inclusion.
4- Capacity Building for CSOs and CBOs in Northwest Syria
This pillar will fall under B&Z’s capacity-building program which strategically aims at empowering the Syrian civil society to take a leading role in Syria’s future and more importantly be active agents of change by gaining the skills and knowledge that enables them to advocate for change at the policymaking level.
The project builds on the outcomes of a previous project that is about to be completed in 2022. The information-gathering component is well-established and in recent months the mechanism for sharing specific needs data with clusters was streamlined and has resulted in individual cases receiving assistance. SCHF monitoring activities carried out so far have been well-received, resulting in one of the HFOs becoming largely dedicated to this work and the processes for monitoring becoming more formalized which will be further enhanced. Direct engagement with communities facilitated by the HFOs has allowed senior leadership, including the ERC, to provide high-level advocacy from key constituencies including women and PWDs.

3 - Needs

There is currently a gap and disconnect between the information available in northwest Syria and the information available to humanitarian actors to support day-to-day planning and implementation.In addition, with the attempts to implement projects through the cross-line process, the humanitarian community needs a better understanding of this process to develop their response plans better. This project is built based on the need of bridging the gaps in information gathering, analysis, and coordination functions, particularly given the challenges of remote management. It does so in particular by:• providing real-time information on the ground from impartial sources, which is otherwise diffuse and difficult to compile credibly in a short time frame.• assisting with project monitoring, which is challenged by remote management. • assisting with direct engagement with communities to improve the quality and accountability of the response. The project is built around three components and is intended to provide broad support for the formal coordination system, particularly clusters/ICCG:1-Information gathering2-Support the monitoring of SCHF-funded projects.3-Community engagement and accountability to the affected population:

4 - Implementation of activities

1- Ensure sharing of information collected and analyzed to OCHA Turkey on a daily basis. This will then be shared with relevant humanitarian actors to support principled humanitarian action. 2- Mapping of assigned OCHA HFU projects including identification of implementing partners, locations, and affected communities through an interactive online map that enables the HFAs to follow up on the SCHF ongoing projects per cluster/organization/location. 3- Engage with the local communities and the affected population to gather their inputs, and feedback and share them with OCHA Turkey and the wider humanitarian community. Note and report any specific needs as identified by the population, in particular for people with noted vulnerabilities, e.g. physically disabled; grandparent-headed households, etc. Provide feedback on these activities, and identify opportunities and trends. 4- Empower Syrian local CSOs and CBOs from Syria through an organizational capacity development program that includes training on NGO governance and administration, proposal writing, project management, NGO management, social engagement, communication, and advocacy, coaching throughout the program period, and grant-making to enable them to implement relevant interventions and projects responding to their target community needs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project aims to enable men, women, girls, and boys as indirect beneficiaries of the project in their right to receive humanitarian assistance. Cross-cutting issues and vulnerabilities (such as gender, age, disabilities, etc.) will be focused on by ensuring gender balance among staff members to facilitate outreach and access to both male and female beneficiaries, taking into consideration existing traditions and cultural norms. In this project, 10 of 20 field officers will be female and 1 of 4 full-time Turkey-based staff. This will continue in the new project. In addition, existing staff members have already received training on protection mainstreaming, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Do No Harm, and other related protection and GBV mainstreaming to ensure their awareness of their obligations and duties and to capacitate them in their community engagement. All new staff will also receive the same training upon their recruitment. The HFOs are also bound to sign a Code of Conduct highlighting protection and GBV-related issues. While supporting the cross-border humanitarian community in their community engagement, the HFOs will raise existing protection and GBV concerns and will contribute to raising awareness among local communities about their rights as recipients of humanitarian aid, taking into consideration cross-cutting issues and vulnerabilities (such as gender, age, disabilities, etc.).


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

B&Z maintains a zero-tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse. For this reason, B&Z has a child protection policy, code of conduct, and safeguarding policy where everyone within the organization plays a role and has a responsibility to protect the beneficiaries from any type of exploitation, harassment, or abuse. Therefore, staff and volunteers are required to follow all the guidelines and processes set in these policies. B&Z's zero-tolerance Safeguarding policy reaffirms and strengthens the commitment as an organization towards adults and children protecting them from all forms of harm and abuse. All B&Z’s staff have been informed, received training, and signed these policies to ensure the proper and dignified implementation process throughout the project period.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1- Military operations in the area and clashes based on scenarios anticipated by B&Z that might limit freedom of movement and ability to deliver 2- Limited access granted by authorities on the ground 3- Kidnapping risks 4- Casualty risks 5- COVID-19 outbreak. Assumptions 1- Secure the field staff to fulfill the tasks within the project period 2- Secure the information and equipment within the project period. Mitigations 1- B&Z developed a contingency plan 2- B&Z developed and secured duty of care policy 3- B&Z revised and kept the risks scenarios updated 4- the project field team will limit their meetings and use the internet communications methods (WebEx, Skype, etc.) and in case they are conducting site visits they will use Personal protective equipment (masks, gloves, rubbing alcohol) to ensure their safety and the beneficiaries safety as well. BZ will monitor the safety of practices and locations on an ongoing basis - and adjust project activities when threats to safety are identified in order to minimize risks. In case of severe degradation of the security conditions in northern Syria, the training center in Afrin will be used as a guesthouse where HFOs can seek protection. A safe and secure information exchange system will be established between the project field team and the team in Turkey. OCHA Turkey will support the field team in their engagement with authorities on the ground to ensure their safety and access.
