Project Summary

The project will seek to ensure improved and equitable access to safe and protective education services for 2,473 children (1473 Girls,1000 Boys) aged between 3 to 18 years among emergency and COVID-19 affected IDPs, returnees, and host community including children with disabilities, and youth (girls and boys) in Aweil East and Aweil West Counties in Northern Bahr El Ghazal. The project will increase enrollment and retention of boys, girls and youth in schools, improve the quality of education services through capacity building of Parent’s Teachers Association, teachers and SMCs in management of schools, protection of the wellbeing of all children and to deliver quality services. Specifically, teachers will be trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies, psychosocial support referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19, health and nutrition and impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms. On the other hand, PTAs and SMCs will be equipped with knowledge and skills on resource mobilization and management in schools, peace building, how to support teachers and learners, mobilization of affected communities to enroll and retain children in schools and on disaster risk reduction. Further, the project will strengthen the capacity of the school community and education actors to mitigate the transmission and impact of existing emergencies including Covid-19 and on learners, teachers, and communities. Through the WASH component, it is expected that 2,473 boys and girls will have access to thirty two (32) sex disaggregated, disability sensitive and protective latrines and hand washing facilities established and rehabilitated in line with Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies Minimum Standard for Education in Emergencies and WASH INEE and sphere standards. In the context of Covid-19, the project will seek to reduce congestion in classes by establishing eight (8) new learning spaces and rehabilitating 8 existing classrooms in targeted schools. All established and rehabilitated learning spaces will have corresponding latrines and hand washing. To improve quality of learning, the project will provide teaching materials to teachers, learning and recreational supplies to Early Childhood Development, primary school learners and youth aged between 3-18 years. In order to mitigate transmission and spread of Covid-19 among learners, teachers and school managements, the project will create awareness on Covid-19 transmission routes, social distancing, hand washing as well as distribute Physical Protection Equipments such as reusable masks and soap obtained from Wash and Education Core pipelines. To promote enrollment and retention of girls in schools, reduce early marriages and reduce incidences of girls dropping from school, the project will conduct Back to Leaning and girl child education campaigns and awareness through MoEST, teachers, PTAs, community leaders, local authorities and SMCs. Finally, the project will work closely with health and protection case workers present in targeted communities to enhance the protection environment of learners and teachers who will identify protection risks in learning environments, conduct referrals and create awareness on child protection at community and household levels. The caseworkers will conduct referrals of child protection cases, train teachers in life skills, protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and psychosocial support (PSS).

1 - Needs

According to South Sudan’s Humanitarian Plan (HRP) for 2021, an estimated 3.4 million school aged boys and girls are in need of educational support compared to 3.1 million children in 2020. This means an extra 300,000 school aged children are in need of educational support in 2021 compared to 2020. In 2020, the vulnerabilities of poor and conflict affected population worsened as a result of escalating food insecurity, flooding, and impact of Covid-19 and inter communal violence. According to the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO), Covid-19 has impacted over two (2) million school aged children in South Sudan due to school closure. Prior to Covid-19 outbreak, 2.2 million children were already out of school in South Sudan (UNICEF). Displaced children and their families and people with disabilities are particularly at risks as they tend to live in overcrowded and informal settings. Over 11,000 unaccompanied and separated children including those released from detention and armed forces and armed groups are still in need of family tracing and reunification services. One million children are at risk of psychosocial distress. This is further aggravated by some of the COVID-19 preventive measures such as the close of schools disrupting their routine and social support systems-leading to negative coping mechanisms. Left without access to the protective environment of schools, the risk of children being neglected, abused or exploited increases significantly. Out of school boys are at risk of engaging in child labour, joining local gangs and criminal groups, being recruited into armed groups while girls face risks of being forced to early marriages as well as sexual exploitation. Covid-19 has eroded livelihoods, disrupted trading activities and created unemployment. This has led to decreased sources of income for individuals and families and by extension denying families income that can be used to pay school fees, buy school uniforms, books and other essential learning and recreational materials for children. Covid-19 has also reinforced existing vulnerabilities among children. Of particular concern are the children living with disabilities who might not access radio and remote learning programmes that have been put in place by the Government and humanitarian actors. As far as learning is concerned in Aweil East and Aweil West Counties, majority of schools were destroyed by floods that affected huge parts of the counties between September and November of 2020. During the rainy season, pupils were not able to access learning. Schools in the counties are far apart, a contributing factor to low enrolment especially among girls. Also, majority of the schools lack sanitation facilities, blackboards, benches, and learning and recreation supplies. On teaching, few volunteer teachers majority of whom are males do not receive monthly salaries from the Government. Lack of salaries has led to increased teacher’s turnover with some seeking jobs among NGOs or working as casual laborers to earn a living. Significantly, volunteer teachers lack requisite skills needed to teach, mentor and support learning in schools due to few capacity development opportunities. In the context of Covid-19, teachers, PTAs and SMCs who should be at the forefront of combating transmission of the virus and ensuring protective environments in schools lack requisite training on Covid-19 transmission, prevention and impact . Failure to address these education barriers will deny children education, lead to low levels of development in the counties and aggravate levels of poverty leading to stiff competition for few available resources which in more than one way will lead to conflict. Further, lack of education will amplify negative social norms that perpetuate Gender Based Violence and discrimination of girls including decisions that influence and increase child marriages.

3 - Project indicators

SLI will develop a robust monitoring and evaluation plan to measure outcome, outputs and track project indicators. A baseline survey will be conducted at the start of the project. Midterm and End line data will also be conducted to allow for snapshot comparison and measure success of the project. The project will use Focus Group Discussions, Key Informant Interviews, Questionnaires, individual interviews to collect both qualitative and quantative data. Additionally, indicators measuring overall satisfaction of beneficiaries and timeliness of the response will be conducted through post distribution monitoring and quality surveys. The monitoring and evaluation process will measure the following indicators; Number of children and youth (3-17 years old) accessed and attended schools in affected areas; Number of temporary learning spaces established (in line with Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies- Minimum Standard for Education in Emergencies (INEE-MSEE); Number of school children got access to gender-sensitive WASH facilities (in line with INEE and sphere standards); Number of temporary learning spaces rehabilitated; Number of children (3-5 years) benefited from Early Childhood and Development (ECD) kits; Number of school children got access to gender-sensitive WASH facilities (in line with INEE and sphere standards; Number of school children benefited from rehabilitated gender-sensitive WASH facilities (in line with INEE and sphere standards); Number of children and youth (6-17 years) benefited from quality education and recreational material supplies; Number of teachers trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies; Number of teachers learners trained and updated on PSS and on the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms; Number of teachers and education personnel trained on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19, health and nutrition and Number of community education committee members and Local Education officials trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

On PSEA, SLI will have a full time Gender Officer tasked with mainstreaming protection and creating awareness on PSEA during project implementation. The Gender officer will provide information about PSEA during inception meetings with community leaders, IDPs and host community representative, during focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls, and during selection of beneficiaries, during distribution learning, Recreational, Teaching and PPEs. Also, the Gender Officer will ensure PSEA messaging through banners, T-shirts and brochures during project activities. The Gender Officer will hold biweekly meetings with pupils, teachers, PTAs, SMCs, women and girls representatives to gauge their perception on protection concerns as well as seek their views on how best to safeguard wellbeing of teachers and children. SLI gender officer will also play a prominent role through existing referral pathways in Aweil East and Aweil West Counties. Necessary PSEA referrals will be done if and when they arise by the gender officer.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SLI will create forums where project staff, its Gender and Protection Officer will conduct Focus Group Discussions, individuals and group meetings to obtain information from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on their perceptions regarding project implementation. In instances where a member of a family is disabled, SLI will conduct personalized meetings so that the individual or their caregivers can be able to communicate their preferences, submit complaints and received feedback. Also, SLI will seek to engage women and girls so that their voices, concerns and preferences can be taken into account right from project planning, project implementation, project planning and evaluation. Further, Complaint and Feedback Data will be disaggregated by gender and age in order to measure the level of participation of females and those living in disability in the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project (aim of the project, its objectives, targeted beneficiaries, project duration, project donors, and the kind of assistance and beneficiaries selection criteria) will be provided to community members, leaders and local authorities during inception meetings, review meetings and end of project stakeholders meeting. Project reports including progress reports, post distribution monitoring reports and end of project reports will be shared with community representatives, Education cluster at State and National level and with the community leaders in Aweil East and Aweil West Counties. Information on feedback mechanism will be provided by SLI’s Gender Officer during meetings, training, and selection of beneficiaries and at distribution venues. The Gender Officer will conduct sensitization and awareness about the type of feedback that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit and avenues of receiving feedback. As far as complaint mechanism, SLI will distribute a toll free number which will be under custody of Gender Officer and Protection Officer. The number will also be displayed prominently at registration sites as well as distribution sites. Complaint and Feedback desks will also be availed during selection of beneficiaries and distribution of shelter and non-food items.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SLI will establish eight (8) Complaint and Feedback mechanisms in the two counties to ensure complaints are submitted by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and feedback is provided in a timely and safe manner. AAP Committee will be oriented and trained on the Feedback and Complaint Mechanism, Accountability to the Affected Population, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), inclusion, selection and targeting criteria and basic human and children rights. Information on channels beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can use to submit and receive complaints will be shared during meetings, trainings and focus groups discussions. Gender and Protection Officers will also conduct training on how to use the Complaint and Feedback mechanism as well as provide information on processes and guidelines applied to ensure safeguarding the privacy of complainants.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The risk is considered medium during project implementation. A Safety audit will be conducted prior to commencing project activities in all eight schools in order to map potential risks posed to project staffs, project assets and beneficiaries. The audit will be embedded in all project activities to ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staffs and protection of project assets. SLI will utilize the safety audit report to seek commitments and support from local authorities, RRC and community members in protecting staffs, beneficiaries and project assets. SLI will deploy its Gender Officer to support the project on fulltime basis. The Gender Officer will ensure gender and protection mainstreaming in the entire implementation process. She/ He will conduct training on PSS, GBV, PSEA and will liaise with Community Child Protection Networks, Child Protection Partners and community case workers for GBV referrals. To ensure children living in disability are not miss on assistance, SLI will form a special committee comprising of five community members (3 females/ 2 Males) in each of the counties to map and register all children and youth aged between 3-18 years. The Committee will track the level of assistance provided to children living in disability while at the same time ensure assistance provided is appropriate and meets the specific needs of the disabled children. The conflict has diminished the capacity of women and girls to cope with external shocks and by extension increasing their vulnerability to marginalization and protection risks. Girls are particularly at risk of dropping out of school, early or forced marriage. In light of this, to cushion girls from missing on learning, the intervention will ensure equal participation of girls and boys in project activities either as direct or indirect beneficiaries. Women and women leaders will be included in PTAs membership as strong advocates for girl’s education. BTL campaigns will also focus on encouraging community members to enrol and retain girls in school. Project data will be disaggregated by sex and age in order to measure the level of participation of girls in schooling activities. The project will involve girls in decision making especially when designing, locating and establishing wash and other facilities to ensure their perception of safety and dignity is taken into consideration. Special forums will be organized targeting female community leaders, local authorities, teachers, PTAs, SMCs and MOEST officials to provide them with information on challenges girls and children living in disability face in schools and how to address the challenges. By encouraging increased participation of adult females and girls in project activities, it is anticipated that they will be empowered to participate in decisions that affect them as well as having their issues amplified during meetings, awareness activities and trainings. Also, during interactions with the boys, male teachers and community members, negative gendered norms and the negative impact they have on girls schooling will be highlighted with communities being encouraged to play a positive role in advocating for girls schooling as well as role of female members in education decision making. Finally, the project is designed to ensure equal participation of male and female in project activities.
