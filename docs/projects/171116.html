Project Summary

While all populations of the globe are vulnerable to COVID-19, yet there are those who are the most vulnerable including the elderly, women and children who are generally disadvantaged compared to others and most subject to increased GBV under crises when there is increased unemployment, restrictions of mobility and heightened tensions, fears and anxieties creating more demand for already limited services. Also, as the incidents of violence were increased during COVID-19 emergency situation, there was a need to continue responsive services to GBV survivors and enhance their resilience to the circumstances. The project is a response to the affected women and girls by the emergency situation and COVID-19 impact, especially women and girls with disabilities, and elderly women by providing them multi-sectoral services remotely and physically. The services include health, psychosocial, legal, cash assistance, support to small businesses for GBV survivors, awareness and support for special-need survivors. The project also will focus on providing access to women and girls with disabilities and elderly women. On the other hand, the project will focus on raising awareness and knowledge of GBV topics for service providers in NGOs and ministries, in addition to focusing on PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) during delivering the services and dealing with the survivors.

1 - Needs

Incidents of gender-based violence, including domestic violence, sexual abuse and forced marriage are among key protection concerns both in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank. They are also acute among vulnerable groups such as women and girls with disabilities, female farmers and heads of households. As per 2021 HNO figures on GBV, people in need in Gaza are 135,495, with 127,826 females from (18 to 65), and 9,214 people living with disabilities. Based on the latest Violence Survey (PCBS, 2019), some 29% of currently married or ever married women experienced “at least once” a form of violence (psychosocial, physical, sexual, social or economic) by their husbands in Palestine, with 38% in the Gaza Strip. PWWSD fieldwork from March to May 2020 found that 30 % of abused women were abused by family members. In Gaza, protection concerns have grown as drug use by male family members has increased; increasing women’s exposure to domestic violence, sexual harassment, marital rape and forced prostitution. (UN-Women Gender Alert, August 2020). According to 2017 population census conducted by PCBS and Food Security Cluster (2018) statistics, Female-headed households represent 9.2 % of the Gaza Strip. 31 % of female-headed households are vulnerable to food security challenges, where 62 % of female-headed households in Gaza are vulnerable to food insecurity.When the COVID-19 pandemic struck worldwide, humanitarian organizations in the Gaza Strip sounded the alarm, warning against a health catastrophe that could hit tens of thousands of people. The Strip's high population density, weak healthcare system, and lingering crises did not bode well for efforts to mitigate the health impacts of a possible virus outbreak. Now fears are greater than ever as the first cases of COVID-19 were detected among members of the community in late August, triggering local authorities to impose a new lockdown. In Gaza, nearly 1,200 new COVID-19 cases, 500 recoveries and 11 fatalities were recorded until early September 2020, resulting in 1,780 active cases. Gaza city and the Northern Gaza governorate account for about 85% of active cases, almost all of which are attributable to transmission within the community. The GBV SC meeting in Aug. 2020 stated that there is increased demand from GBV survivors to provide dignity and hygiene kits, food and non-food Items to protect themselves and their families, as during the lockdown and curfew, they can’t get their basic needs from supermarkets or groceries, especially the quarantined families in the buildings or red-areas and women and girls with disabilities. Also, they highlighted that the restriction of movement and restrictions from the government affected the women start-up projects. All the women businesses are stopped now and they can’t produce or even sell their products. According to the Civic and Women Coalition for the Implementation of CEDAW report in June 2020, Women and girls with disabilities have also experienced a double increase in the rate of violence against them. Moreover, SAWA helpline reports during June 2020 showed a 30% increase in calls compared to May. 967 calls have been documented counselling sessions. For Jun.- Aug. 2020, 297 women reported physical, verbal and emotional abuse, while 345 cases asked for MHPSS services. Additionally, based on figures from CFTA, WAC and RCS during September 2020, 1481 female cases reached the 2 helplines in Gaza and their services’ mobile numbers for psychosocial support, legal consultations, RH awareness and support, and health referral. Also, based on PWWSD service data from March to September 2020, total phone consultations provided during state of emergency in Gaza Strip were 2348 cases; where 2178 were female cases. The pandemic has also pushed a greater number of households into poverty and food insecurity, with an increase of at least 10 percent in the poverty rate according to the World Bank’s Economic Monitoring Report (June 2020)Based on these figures, UNFPA started to include the Cash Voucher Assistance as part of the GBV comprehensive services as its pilot through CFTA in June-July 2020 which was successful and with great effect on GBV survivors and vulnerable families affected by the emergency situation. 1033 families (730 women, 303 men) received food and hygiene vouchers to cover their basic needs once during the lockdown in July 2020. 97% of the beneficiaries stated that they did shopping with convenience according to your choices and needs, 95% said that the voucher mechanism was better for their dignity and respect, and 97% expressed that this intervention had a positive impact on their psychological status and self-satisfaction.Based on CARE Palestine WBG COVID-19 Rapid Gender Assessment recommendations April 2020, national authorities and donors/ the international community must strengthen the GBV and protection response as part of COVID-19 response efforts, supporting protection-focused services and supporting the capacity of service providers to provide hotlines, referrals and remote and direct health and psychosocial response services for survivors, while expanding service availability.UNFPA through its three safe spaces in Gaza Strip worked their best to provide multi-sectoral services and emergency response to GBV survivors and vulnerable people. However, UNFPA partners’ monthly feedbacks highlight the increased and urgent need to respond to the increasing demand from GBV survivors asking for services through helplines or by reaching the safe spaces. Therefore, UNFPA designed this project to assure the accessibility of GBV survivors to comprehensive GBV services in different areas, respond effectively to the needs of survivors and service providers as well to enable them to provide the comprehensive services and ensure their sustainability and long-term responses.

2 - Activities

Overall goal: Strengthen resilience and coping strategies, build psychosocial wellbeing and reduce fear and stigma for vulnerable women and girls in Gaza Strip due to COVID-19 Outcome 1: Improved availability of safe and high quality remote and physical multi sectoral services and protection for vulnerable women and girls in the affected communities in Gaza Strip, mainly the quarantined cases through UNFPA three safe spaces in Gaza Strip.Highlight: UNFPA will ensure that all safe spaces have health protocol to follow in case of any COVID-19 suspected cases. PPE materials will be delivered to those service providers to ensure full protection for them when delivering the service, the women beneficiaries accessing the services will also receive needed PPE for health services. Also, safe spaces are within health centres where they will follow the whole sterilization and disinfection procedures from MOH.ACTs:1- Providing MHPSS, case management and legal consultation services to 8000 women and girls at risk of GBV, women and girls with disabilities (WGWDs), elderly women and the affected ones by quarantined through helplines, mobile communication, WhatsApp and other tools in addition to Safe spaces visits through RCS, WAC and CFTA. UNFPA partners have database for survivors WGWDs where they will target 500 WGWDs in this project with different responsive services. Also, the implementing partners have direct coordination with EducAid and Humanity and Inclusion (HI) for targeting and responding to WGWDs needs. RCS and CFTA safe spaces are adapted for PWDs and they have a GBV service directory with Braille language that will be updated based on the updated emergency service directory. Do no harm approach will specifically apply here in light of COVID-19 to equip women with proper skills to protect themselves and their families from getting infected and be well prepared in case of COVID-19 infection. UNFPA partners received training on remote service provision through CFTA in September 2020 based on the Safety and Security Guidelines for Remote GBV Service Delivery approved by GBV SC.2- Providing protection services and reintegration support to women victims and survivors of GBV in shelters in Gaza which are Hayat Centre and Beit Al-Aman Centre in Gaza. This activity is mainly to expand UNFPA support to shelters to provide continuous high quality psychosocial services, vocational training activities in addition to protection tools such as PPE, mainly for the ones who will be accessing health facilities, food items, and communications. 3- Provide 100 GBV survivors with chronic illness with psychosocial well-being, and needed therapy. Outcome 2: decreasing vulnerability to protection risks through the provision of CVA and food and non-food items for women and girls, mainly the headed households and caregivers.ACTs:1- Provide regular cash assistance for food and hygiene items for 1000 families in Gaza Strip, mainly headed by women, the affected by the lockdown and quarantine in their buildings, and 200 families from them for WFWDs based on CFTA, UNFPA partners’ and MoSD database. All protection measures including physical distancing, wearing mask and time scheduling will be considered to ensure no gatherings in closed places. UNFPA partner-CFTA- had excellent experience in implementing CVA, where they will coordinate with UNFPA partners in all governorates to ensure wider coverage for GBV survivors. Also, with MoSD, FSS and UNRWA to avoid overlapping. 2- Provide support and guidance for 100 women with small businesses that were affected by the COVID-19 emergency situation through coaching and small grants to retrieve their work through WAC, RCS and CFTA according to their targets of economic empowerment in previous UNFPA projects. Outcome 3: Enhanced capacity of the NGOs and governmental service providers and awareness of the community towards the impact of COVID-19 on GBV.Highlight: The trainings and sessions can be face to face with considering protection measures or remotely based on previous experiences.ACTs:1- Strengthen the capacities of virtual service delivery, including running help line, to support and refer GBV survivors through 6 training sessions/group for 120 (70F, 50M) personnel from UNFPA partners RCS in North Gaza and Gaza city, CFTA in Buraij Health Center and Wesal Network in Khan Younis and Rafah, and WAC. The training will be according to the Safety and Security Guidelines for Remote GBV Service Delivery approved by GBV SC. The training also will target 10 service providers with disabilities in coordination with EducAid and will include a session about targeting survivors with disabilities.2- Strengthen the capacity of local organizations on PSEA by conducting PSEA training for 30 GBV SC organizations and services providers, strengthen complaint mechanisms and monitoring capacities and increase awareness on PSEA among local communities through media campaigns, animation videos, social media, and publications. UNFPA will be directly responsible for providing technical guidance and support through its focal points who received PSEA TOT training conducted in August 2020 by the PSEA network through OHCHR.3- Conduct training for 100 staff (80 M, 20 F) from MoH, and MoI who are engaged in responding to households under home isolation/quarantine on GBV detection and referral. The training will be conducted based on GBV SOPs, minimum standards, and UNFPA material for non-GBV specialists through RCS in coordination with Protection Cluster based on previous experience in June 2020.4- Provide awareness to 2000 community members (1000F, 1000 M) on how to access services during COVID-19 pandemic and the restriction of movement effects on vulnerable groups through social media and protection networks in Gaza Strip.5- Publish fact sheets/ research papers on the effect of COVID-19 and restriction of movements on health, socio-economic and other streams in Gaza Strip by WAC.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according toUNFPA minimum standards and all partner GBV service providers know how to applythese principles in their work with GBV survivors. The service providers have beentrained to protect the identity and safety of GBV survivors by not disclosing anypersonal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’sexplicit permission and consent. Also, GBV service providers have robust experience inensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological andemotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or fromtheir family members due to notions of family honour.All UNFPA partners are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centred Approach to provide meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors. UNFPA will continue to provide capacity building to partners on theseprinciples including on GBV Minimum Standards and safe GBV remote service delivery.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed to provide safe and equal access to adequate multi-sectorialservices for women and girls and for the ones with disabilities through UNFPA implementingpartners of GBV service providers, who are specialized in providingcomprehensive services for GBV survivors and the staff received multiple trainings on “HawaZahra” manual that focuses on dealing and including people with disabilities, inaddition to adapting their safe spaces to be accessible for WGWDs. Additionally, the service directory for GBV service is now printed in Brailleز Also, the project focuses on reaching the vulnerable beneficiaries from different areas of Gaza Strip. Additionally, the project will target women who are cancer and kidney-failure patients to provide them with responsive services in addition to some special needs and treatment. Moreover, based on UNFPA study about violence on elderly, the project will target elderly people mainly women to ensure their equitable access to health and other services.On the other hand, the awareness, outreach initiatives, advocacy campaigns andthe social media materials will cover wide areas to ensure all targeted communities have abetter knowledge about GBV, link of COVID-19 and GBV, rights of PWDs and other important topics.
