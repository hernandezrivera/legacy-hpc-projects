Project Summary

Project Title: Provision of timely and appropriate access to safe and adequate water, dignified Sanitation, and enhancing safe hygiene practices for the Internally displaced population (IDPs), Returnee and crisis affected communities through integrated WASH interventions, reinforcing protection and enhancing resilience and communities coping mechanisms and sustainability of livelihoods.

Project Summary : In this project, UCDC aims to provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water, improved sanitation, and improving hygiene knowledge of the Internally Displaced Population (IDPs) and most vulnerable host communities in Wau, Jur River and Raga Counties focusing on WASH emergency intervention to reduce morbidity and mortality resulting from WASH-related diseases and malnutrition through integrated lifesaving WASH activities mainstreaming protection, accountability to affected population (AAP) and strengthening the capacity of affected community on copping mechanism and resilience through training of different groups of people in the community such as pump mechanics, Water management committee, women groups, community leaders, and the AAP focal persons. The project will target 15,000 (12,000 direct, 3,000 indirect) beneficiaries mainly the internally displaced population, most vulnerable host communities affected by conflict and natural disasters, with limited access to safe water supply, hygiene, sanitation practices and food insecurity with supported coordination and complementation of WASH gaps which other implementing partners in the targeted areas are limited to tackle through their interventions due to continuous increase on the caseloads. UCDC purposes to address WASH gaps related to currently arising emergency needs assessed to achieve Sphere standards, the core humanitarian standards/principles and mainly the WASH cluster objectives contributions to 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan.
UCDC targets vulnerable and crisis affected population with a structured, effective preparedness and response programme through improving access to safe water, enhancing community awareness on safe hygiene and sanitation practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases through:
• Repairing and rehabilitating 20 identified boreholes, measurable by WASH cluster standards for water supply.
• Selection and Training of 30 pump mechanics, 100 water management committees, 30 hygiene promoters, 40 water point attendances, the 40 food vendors, and 15 AAP focal persons being deeply involved in the participatory M&E.
• Conducting hygiene promotion through house-to-house, focused group discussions, and public outreaches.
• Identification and registration of 1500 most vulnerable IDPs/host communities households targeted for the WASH NFIs, 500 women and girls for the menstrual hygiene kits.
• Distribution of the WASH non-food items, menstrual hygiene kits, and IEC materials.
• Identification, registration and distribution of hand washing facilities, garbage bins to 40 food vendors.
• Conducting continuous coordination for complementation, integration and mainstreaming purposes.
• Conducting continuous rapid monitoring and evaluation (M&E) through the UCDC’ AAP incorporation methodologies and the Post Distribution monitoring (PDM).
• Improving household waste management system through cat-methods, digging garbage pits and installation of 8 garbage bins in the public places.

1 - Needs

Protracted conflict in South Sudan since 2013 has caused erosion of physical and social infrastructure, death and massive displacement in Wau,Jur River and Raga counties Westren Bar ghazel state, South Sudan, echoed to tribal clashes, cattle raiding, mass GBV and protection issues Wau,Jur River and Raga counties Western Bar ghazel state are among the most affected with recurrent attacks often causing more displacements, loss of lives and destruction of infrastructure. Coupling, the national financial crises have worsened the situation. REACH conducted a rapid assessment in Wau and Raga counties in 6/7/2019, OCHA led IRNA Assessment of March/2019 in western bar ghazel indicating urge WASH gaps, where 84.9% of the people practices open defecation, 49.2% people use water only for hand washing while 6.7% only use water and soap for hand washing; and only 16.2% caregiver’s wash hands at critical times, 78% population with limited access to safe drinking water.The Inter-Cluster Working Group (ICWG) debriefings for 10 October 2019, indicates massive displacement up to 15000 or more in Jur River and Raga counties as the situation are still being assessed, Consecutive shocks (cattle raid, livestock disease outbreak, atypical livestock sales to cover consumption gaps, additionally ICWG findings indicates recent seasonal improvements (different lean season due to pastoralism) - but deterioration expected with upcoming pastoralist lean season (peak during January – April), IPC findings shows that in both projection periods IPC Phase 5 populations (‘Catastrophe’ expected) due to deterioration expected with upcoming pastoralist lean season (peak during Jan – April) and highly recommends immediate scale-up within January 2019 in the identified geographical hostpots of Jur River and Raga counties. 6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

UCDC has a legal framework that elaborates the recognition of the rights of beneficiaries in all the project locations. All project staffs are bound to operate under these protocols and a monitoring team constituted by community members shall be put in place to make sure there is no violation of the beneficiaries’ rights.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project implementation strategy shall make sure displaced people are included in the beneficiaries receiving WASH NFIs and MHM kits and be part of the project staffs as pump mechanics and water management committees. They will also participate in the planning, implementation and monitoring the project progress and give feedback on the quality of the services offered.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

UCDC will ensure that the project will be introduced to county WASH department and make sure they understand the objectives and targets of the project. The national WASH guidelines and protocols for department of water and sanitation shall be followed during implementation and monitoring. The activities shall be supervised by the county water and sanitation department according to the protocols and reports shall be shared with the county WASH department.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. The project will be aligned with the national objectives since it will be implemented according to national WASH guidelines and all the reporting and monitoring frameworks shall be aligned with the existing WASH implementation structures as per the WASH cluster and the line ministries.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

UCDC follows standard security protocol, updated regularly. UCDC staff members, contractors and donors are instructed on everyday security risk mitigation measures, also in terms of traveling, communication, conflict resolution etc. UCDC team follows all the procedures and systems to provide security to its compounds (people and assets). UCDC has procedures for travels and field trips; screening the locations regarding security and local dynamics, liaising with OCHA for further information on the context, coordinating with partners on the ground including the local authorities as important UCDC’s safety and security due diligence and standard procedures (SOP). Maintaining close relationship with local communities, as well as informing local authorities and other stakeholders about the activities allows UCDC teams to perform their duties in a relatively safe environment in the field.UCDC liaises closely with the Security SFP at NGO forum, NGO partners SFP, UNDSS and UNOCHA on information gathering and evacuation of staffs.Wau,Jur River and Raga Counties where UCDC’s WASH intervention will be implemented is not exceptional to incidences related to cattle raiding, age-set clashes and GBV/protection issues, considering NGOs are often affected by robberies and as the conflict is more active, NGOs are affected by violence and unpredictable waves of insecurity. This pattern is linked to economic crisis, no political reasons were identified in the actions when analyzing major incidents affecting aid agencies. Varying casual labor rates in field is increasing and its affecting agencies operations. Most member of intervention team are based in project location and Wau Town. There is currently wave of youth violence across some regions in the country, protesting recruitment of humanitarian workers in their areas from other regions, this has weakened humanitarian operations.
