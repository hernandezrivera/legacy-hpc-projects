Project Summary

Over a period of 12 months, VSO will employ 3 National Professional Volunteers, 20 National Graduate Volunteers and 2 international volunteers who will live and work with 50 community volunteers to deliver an early recovery project designed to build back better living conditions in 20 wards within 7 Local Government areas in Borno Adamawa and Yobe. The chief targets in this action are females heading households, children heading households, returnees, unemployed youth, vulnerable host community members and children with disabilities. VSO plans to use the relational volunteering model that creates social cohesion, that builds long lasting relationship, and which promotes long term and sustainable change. Our volunteer rational model ensures that vulnerable and excluded beneficiaries are empowered to realize rights and full potential by building up and on their assets, skills, resilience and agency. Our Relational Volunteering Model empowers project beneficiaries,,making them leaders of their part of the development bargain. It places them in the driving seat and facilitates their decisions on their future, whilst strengthening social and community ties. Through the model, the project will promote deep seated participation, create harmony and lead to a joint implementation of project objectives. The model promotes inclusion laying great emphasis on disability, age, gender identities whilst addressing discrimination and harmful social norms and practices the foster exclusion, stigma, denial and discrimination.
The proposed project seeks to revive and strengthen protection mechanisms for a total of 44, 000 persons mainly girls,, women, boys faced with grave protection concerns. VSO will conduct awareness raising sessions, link up survivors with the appropriate agencies for trauma counselling, healing as well as psycho social support. VSO working through local community volunteers will revive and build on local community mechanisms for supporting survivors. We will work to make these very functional and responsive to the demands of the project beneficiaries. Community Volunteers will systematically and regularly provide follow up with survivors and continue to encourage their integration into the community , be they host , returnee or survivor of abuse.
VSO will use an asset and capability appeal, support women to group and begin the process of healing, and to generate incomes to pay for education, health care and household commodities. Women will be provided with skills trainings through vocational training centers, supported to group, how to mobilize local scarce resources and then encouraged to save as little as they deem convenient with a view to encouraging a saving culture.
Beyond the savings women will have opportunity to access support for the start of small scale business. VSO livelihood technical lead supported by the International volunteers and the National Professional Graduates will provide a series of training on vocational skills to young boys and girls. These skills giving sessions will seek to open up opportunities for the target groups currently frustrated by the lack of employment concerns. VSO will provide a select of technical and vocational trainings on tailoring, hair dressing, mobile phone repairs, catering and shoe repairs
The project will reach a target of 79, 837. VSO expects that at the end of the one year of delivery, social cohesion will be improved for 10, 000 host community members and returnees, girls, women and boys would have received sufficient local support to be part of the local community protection systems, livelihood systems would be restored for 15, 000 individuals. Perceived and real tensions, within communities of the project would be minimized through peace building sessions. VSO contributes to resilient livelihoods of the poor, enhance their ability to reduce vulnerability. Improved capabilities and resilience of beneficiaries,communities and systems in fragile settings

1 - Needs

Insecurity in the North East has resulted in loss of employment, destruction of property, forced migration, displacement and disruptions in education calendars. The Boko Haram insurgency has led to the deaths of 600 teachers, displaced 19000 teachers, and destroyed 1200 schools settings. (RBPA 2016) Schools have been forcibly occupied and used as IDP camps and where camps have been relocated from schools, parents and children have found it difficult to go back to the same schools. The reintegration of children especially the girl child is a challenge as they face marginalization, discrimination and rejection by family and community members due to social and cultural norms (VSO SGEA 2019). It is estimated that of the 8.7 million children out of school, 60% are girls. Drop-out rate is high in general, with more girl drop-outs in the north, forced into early marriage. In Borno, 18% of secondary school age girls attend formal school but at the primary level, meaning they are behind for their age and at greater risk of dropping out (MICS, 2011).The prolonged conflict adversely impacted on the lives of communities, destroying livelihoods, denying children especially the girl child of her right to a decent education, further denying women, boys and girls their rights to protection, worsening the conditions that favor the abuse of children, girls and women. a situation that persists even today. The resultant outcome is that even at this phase of early recovery 56% of the impacted populations is still without a secured source of livelihood or an income, millions of IDPs have returned to what was once home, but they make a return to where a land that no longer fully recognizes them, they have to start life anew. Host communities feel the stress as IDPS continue to benefit of the limited social services that exist in these communities. Large population of unemployed youths , unaccompanied children, teenage mothers, single mothers, females headed households and girls are at risk of Sexual exploitation because they are unemployed and cannot find employment. The insurgency fractured an already fragile educational system, bringing the infrastructure to its knees and worsening vulnerabilities of the girl child and other marginalized persons such as those living with disabilities. It destroyed livelihoods and deepened the protection crisis, Women and girls have been subject of rape, were denied their rights to shelter, a home, a livelihood an education and the right to a safe and healthy motherhood. The crisis has created many households headed by children, a growing population of single mothers, large numbers of teenage mothers, an unusually high number of Female headed households. There are also concerns about the significant numbers of unaccompanied children. Returning IDPs find they have lost their asset base, their social standing and are struggling to re integrate into a society that they once called home. Host communities are struggling to provide for their families and returnees or IDPS. There is a stress on the limited social services and infrastructure, leading to tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities. Data indicate that there are very high numbers of households (above 50% across the BAY states ) that do not have a regular source of income. The International Community has invested hugely in curtailing the humanitarian crisis in the North East, with many INGOs providing lifesaving services, At this phase there are huge gaps of recovery that need to be met. The gaps are largest in protection, livelihoods and education. A recent VSO assessments of the needs in the NE indicate that VSO unique relational volunteer approach to working with community volunteers, supported by national volunteers will help lay the foundation for a health early recovery programme. This project is built around community volunteers as key actors for building back better their own devastated communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

VSO will establish a feedback mechanism that primary actors to openly make their concerns known. We will hold quarterly feed back sessions to hear out any issues. We will encourage the use of Community score cards to gauge community appreciation of our work and the quality, impartiality, effectiveness and rapidity of the the services provided. We will encourage the use of the existing VSO hotlines and emails for reporting cases , suspicions around the violation of primary actors right. We will display at all community meeting , in our offices and in our partner office, the various contacts for reporting cases of protection, fraud, abuse and how primary actors can seek redress. We have built into the project various awareness raising sessions on the rights of our primary actors to access information and the mechanism for complaints. We will increase awareness sessions for primary actors, on their right to complain and most importantly assure them of their protection. We will investigate and report back and take actions on any complaints. The lessons from the investigation will feed into improving our processes.
