Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges – intensified by the global economic downturn, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict – all adversely affect the food security of South Sudanese families. South Sudan: Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No.15
Upper Nile region of South Sudan has for a long time faced protracted emergencies both man made and natural disasters of complex magnitude including perennial inter and intra tribal conflicts, cattle raids, chronic flooding, diseases and drought. Upper Nile State is currently facing the influx of returnees who fled the wave of violent conflict in 2022. U.N. agencies Reuters December 7 2022 Violence in South Sudan's Upper Nile displaces 9,000 -U.N. agencies
According to the UNHCR, since the start of the Sudan conflict in April, over 1 million people have been displaced from Sudan to neighboring countries, of whom 364,818 arrived in South Sudan as of 7 November. Of these, 83 per cent are South Sudanese returnees and 16 per cent are Sudanese refugees. Fourty nine percent (49.6%) which translates to 180,949 of the 364,818 new arrivals are female; 49% are below the age of 18 and 3.5% are over 60. Many women were subjected to sexual violence in Sudan, testimonies from women in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State revealed systematic use of sexual violence by armed men along the road into South Sudan, calling for immediate action in strengthening gender-based violence (GBV) response capacity, including clinical management of rape (CMR), psychosocial support (PSS), GBV case management and basic needs assistance. South Sudan: Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 9 as of 1 September 2023. A joint partner’s assessment also revealed that with increasing numbers of vulnerable at risk returnees coming in, there is growing concern on the rise of PSEA cases as vulnerable majority women and girls continue to access lifesaving assistance like food, among other intervention services
GCAF intends to intervene with Key prevention and response priorities/activities to ensure that there is dignified access to life saving humanitarian assistance, that crises-affected population protection risks are mitigated, and finally, increasing capacity of protection structures. To safeguard inclusion, strategic action will be taken to ensure that PSN and PWDs are engaged in the consultations, planning and implementation of response.
With a budget of 250,000 USD GCAF plans to provide prevention and response services to vulnerable IDPs, HC members, and returnees arriving in Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile State. GCAF will target 4311 vulnerable persons, Returnees 2155, HC 863 and IDPs 1293 will be targeted for response, with consideration to the age, gender segregated as follows, Women 2069 , Men 1380, Girls 1380, Boys 344, while 5% (216) of the above targeted population will be persons with disability. Target population will come from the Counties of Ulang 1437 (Female 862, Male 575), Nasir 1437, (Female 862,Male 575), Maiwut 1437 (Female 862, Male 575). The targeted at risk and vulnerable persons will be reached with specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, WGFS programming, women, men, boys and girls will be supported with individual and group PSS including outreach activities, awareness-raising activities will be conducted on GBV risks, available response services, GBV prevention initiatives (e.g. SASA, EMAP, community cares programming), distribution of dignity kits to vulnerable women and adolescent girls, GBV risk mitigation interventions (Income generating activities, fuel efficient stoves, integration of GBV by other clusters and humanitarian actors) strengthening the GBV referral pathway and the training of non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral response.

1. Needs

Food security outcomes are likely to deteriorate, and humanitarian needs to rise, including for host communities in the already acutely food-insecure areas. Households are likely to resort to negative coping mechanisms. Longochot/Nasir has already been classified Intersectoral Priority Classification IPC severity priority1, while Ulang, and Maiwut are priorty level 2, meaning that they face food insecurity challenges which poses a risk for women and girls accessing food support, meaning the distance traveled to access food support raises protection concerns from the risk of SGBV for women and girls due to gender roles, further more PSEA concerns especially adolescent girls and widows accessing services remains a threat. GBV needs in Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut in Upper Nile are a result of multiple factors, including, post conflict shocks, ongoing impact of floods which destroyed livelihood and community resilience, ongoing and concurrent arrival of returnees from Sudan, going through Renk Transition Center to their final destinations, secondly there are also cases of vulnerable internally displaced persons who are not yet in their final destinations, and thirdly vulnerable members of the host community of Nasir, Ulang, and Maiwut.A joint rapid Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) risk assessment, conducted from 15-19 June 2023 in Renk, highlighted that risks of SEA and other misconduct by humanitarian staff in Renk and Juba boarder, the assessment identified adolescent girls, single women, and single female‑headed households as the most vulnerable to SEA.There are challenges of child headed, and female headed households that are failing to cope up with the devastating consequences of displacement and post conflict shocks, many lost their parents and husbands and have taken over responsibility of siblings and children respectively. they are however surrounded by high risks of Gender violence, especially exploitation and abuse, risk of SGBV and GBV, Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), recruitment and abduction of children into armed groups and armed forces (CAFAAG), Mental Health breakdown, Diseases, lack of access to services, malnutrition, Early and Forced Child marriage due to power exploitation and weakened and overwhelmed protection monitoring system. OCHA South Sudan: Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 16, 20 October 2023Given the larger numbers of women and girls, and adolescent mothers coming in, there is priority need for distribution of Dignity kits to maintain hygiene of young girls and young mothers to deter chronic infections which can exacerbates the health risk in the community at large, proper waste disposal is also a major factor in this response.There is need for MHPSS, Many women were subjected to sexual violence in Sudan, testimonies from women in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State revealed systematic use of sexual violence by armed men along the road into South Sudan, calling for immediate action in strengthening gender-based violence (GBV) response capacity, including clinical management of rape (CMR), psychosocial support (PSS), GBV case case management and basic needs assistance. South Sudan: Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 9 as of 1 September 2023.There is dire need for critical Protection assistance and services, including specialized response through WGFS providing MHPSS, PSS and recreational activities, Couselling, referrals, among other reponse to provide access and mitigate risks through awareness raising and capacity strengthening of local protection structures in Nasir/Luakpiny, Ulang, and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile South Sudan. GCAF seeks to work in three counties of Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut, with regular field visit to the bomas and parishes responding in the HRP Strategic Objectives areas of facilitating meaningful Access, Protection risk mitigation, and Protection prevention and emergence response. Seperated children will require FTR services and referrals.

2. Project activities

The Key response priorities/activities are aligned to the Project Strategic objectives derived from the three HRP Strategic Objectives as maintained by the Protection Cluster, ensuring that there is dignified access to life saving humanitarian assistance, that crises-affected population protection risks are mitigated through centrality of protection, and finally, increasing protection structures capacity. Project Strategic Objectives; 1. Provide vulnerable and at risk women, girls and boys and men with critical Protection assistance and services, including specialized response with key consideration to AGD approach, and Disability inclusion, in Nasir/Luakpiny, Ulang, and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile South Sudan2. Support and strengthen community protection structures to prevent, mitigate, advocate, and respond to the problem of traditional and culturally perpetuated violence against women and girls in Nasir, Ulang, and Maiwut Counties in Upper Nile State, through Dialogue, awareness raising, radio talk shows, and school Child Rights Clubs (CRCs) programs among others3. Strengthen capacity, support and coordinate community based protection structures in Ulang, Nasir and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile, to advocate, prevent and respond to GBV and human rights violations, through training of men and women leaders in the community in GBV Protection in emergencies.To achieve the above strategic project objectives, GCAF will implement activities that will directy respond to the key objectives and indicators, as agreed upon by Protection Cluster and GBV AoRThe priority response are; diligent 1. Reach women, girls, boys, and men through awareness-raising activities on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting2. Reach women girls boys and men through GBV prevention initiatives (e.g. SASA, EMAP, community cares programming)3. Support women girls boys and men with specialized GBV response services ( GBV case management, referrals, & WGFS programming )4. Support women girls boys and men with individual and group Psychosocial support including recreational activities5. Distribution of dignity kits to vulnerable women and adolescent girls in need 6. Support vulnerable women, girls, boys, and men in emergencies with cash and voucher assistance to facilitate special needs. 7. Support women and girls with GBV risk mitigation interventions (Income generating activities, fuel efficient stoves, intergradation of GBV by other clusters and humanitarian actors) 8. Update the GBV referral pathway in Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut Counties in Upper Nile State 9. Train non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral responses. Expected Outcomes:Increased access to life saving humanitarian assistance, and direct services for 3000 vulnerable individuals in Nasir Ulang and Maiwut. Reduction in gender violence and sexual gender based violence against women, girls, and boys, and men, as a result of mitigation of protection risks by strengthening coordination of centrality of protection with partners, through dialogues awareness raising, outreaches among othersQuick and quality response to protection needs in Nasir Ulang and Maiwut, through capacity building and strengthening of protection structures
