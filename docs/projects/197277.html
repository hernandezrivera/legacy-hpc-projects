Project Summary

The project aims to educate the population in best practices related to maternal and child nutrition, birth registrations, routine vaccination of pregnant women and children less than 5 years, and ideal WASH practices. From which it will address the problems of maternal malnutrition by providing iron and folic acid and deworming services and awareness raising in exclusive breastfeeding, complementary feeding practices and WASH will be addressed to reduce malnutrition in Minowa camp.
Through antenatal consultations (ANCs) and community interactions, pregnant women will be screened for malnutrition and nutrition counselling provided to those in need. Micronutrient powders (MNPs) will be provided for children to diversify their food diet. Screening of children 6-59 months old will aid in detecting the deterioration of children’s nutritional status where bespoke interventions will be provided to reduce severe malnourishment and limit the possibilities of medical complications. The malnutrition preventive activities will be carried out at both the supported Integrated health facilities (HF) and the community level at the Minowa camp.
At the health facilities, antenatal consultations will be delivered to screen pregnant women for malnutrition using MUAC tapes and identifying oedema. Follow-up of acutely malnutrition women via counselling will help to optimise their nutrition and better their WASH practices. Iron and folic acid tablets will be provided to all women attending ANCs and pregnant women will be dewormed. During post-natal consultations there will be continuous provision of iron and folic acid up to 6 months after childbirth to prevent nutrient deficiencies in infants and children. During ANC/PNC women will receive education on maternal, infant and child nutrition, optimum IYCF practices, WASH, procedures of birth registration, importance of vaccination and nutritional demonstrations. CHWs will be present at the HF to train on key messages from the health personnel.
At community level malnutrition prevention interventions will be carried out by CHWs, health promoters, key community actors like IYCF support groups, husband school members who are model husband working towards the amelioration of maternal and child health by educating other men. This network of community actors will provide social, and behaviour change communications for the adoption of health habits concerning maternal, infant and child nutrition as well as basic WASH practices. Nutritional demonstrations including cooking demonstrations will be led by health promoters and CHWs to teach the population on how to complement young children and equally how to adjust diets of pregnant and lactating women. Trained CHWs will distribute MNPs bimonthly via household of each eligible child 6-23 months and provide key messages on the use of MNPs. Home visits will be organised for the follow-up of children receiving the MNPs. Routine monthly screenings for malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months will be carried out for a close follow-up of the nutritional status of beneficiaries.
In close collaboration with Health promoters and CHWs IMC UK will work to implement activities such as: nutrition screening, active case finding of defaulters and absentees in the community, referrals to the appropriate nutrition programs at the health centres and informing the community about malnutrition.
Children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications will be referred to the stabilization centres (SCs) at the district hospital or a CMA (Medical centre of the municipality).

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Far North (FN) region is the most vulnerable regions in Cameroon, impacted by the Lake Chad Basin crisis, floods and intercommunal conflicts. It is reflected by the lowest indicators for maternal and infant nutrition, and general health status according to MICS 20141. According to UNHCR statistic report 20222, the FN 133,575 Nigerian refugees, including 74,972 in Minawao Camp as well as 377,877 IDPs and 130,609 returnees. More than 90% of the population movements are driven by insecurity and attacks from non-state armed groups (NSAG), leading to internal displacement of local communities scattered across different places of the region including the Mayo-Tsanaga division. This has led to increasing needs and vulnerability in the FN. According to the EDS 20183, women of childbearing age (15-49 years) suffer from anaemia (43,2% in FN). Only 70% of pregnant women have initial access to the recommended iron and folic acid intake for at least 90 days and only 27.8% have continued access after 90 days. One of the main causes of malnutrition in infant and young children is diarrhoeal infections due to poor WASH practices. 13% of population in FN and 17 % in North region practice open defecation and they represent up to 80% of the country’s population. In the National Food and Nutrition Policy 2015-2035 (p.17)4, the promotion, support, protection and encouragement of maternal and child nutrition from the time of conception to 5 years which is a national priority. This objective is aligned to contribute towards child's survival, growth and development through the improvement of infant and young child feeding and improving nutrition for pregnant and lactating women in Cameroon by 2035.
