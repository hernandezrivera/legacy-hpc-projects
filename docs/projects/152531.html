Project Summary

The findings of county level nutrition assessments conducted in 2018 reveal that 68 per cent of the 32 SMART surveys had global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent. This signals a critical nutrition situation in South Sudan in 2019 which will require scale up in nutrition intervention by UNICEF and implementing partners. The purpose of this nutrition project is to support preventive and lifesaving nutrition interventions through treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), provision of maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN), capacity building and robust information systems in all the ten states of South Sudan including use of integrated rapid response mechanism (IRRM) modality to deliver nutrition services in the hardest to reach locations. UNICEF will also work closely with other sectors to deliver integrated nutrition sensitive interventions through an array of cross-sectoral linkages with education, health, water and sanitation, child protection interventions to contribute to reduction in stunting and acute malnutrition. In response to the humanitarian needs in South Sudan, UNICEF together with implementing partners plan to reach 984,701 pregnant women and caregivers of children below the age of 24 months and 220,680 children with SAM. The total cost of delivering UNICEF front line project is estimated to be USD 33,283,200 for a duration of twelve months.

Needs

According to county level nutrition SMART surveys conducted in 2018, about 68 per cent of 32 surveys had global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent. The overall situation of acute malnutrition has slightly improved in 2018 compared to the same period in 2017 with no county reporting extreme critical levels (GAM above 30%) of acute of malnutrition in 2018. A total of 31 counties in the states of Warrap, Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei reported `Critical' (GAM 15.0 - 29.9%) levels of acute malnutrition while 20 counties in Lakes, Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states reported `Serious' (GAM 10.0-14.9%) levels of acute malnutrition. Most the counties Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria states recorded 'Alert' (GAM 5.0-9.9%) and `Acceptable' (GAM <5.0%) levels of acute malnutrition. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to improve marginally in October - December 2018 due to seasonal availability of local production, increased availability of fish, milk at the household level and seasonal improved access to markets and services. However, levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 due to the onset of an early lean season and the depletion of remaining cereal stocks.In response to the highlighted humanitarian needs in South Sudan 2019, UNICEF in collaboration with about 40 nutrition implementing partners will address the needs of about 984,701 pregnant women and caregivers of children below the age 24 months and a further 2,070,000 children under the age of five years including 220,680 children with SAM. The target beneficiaries will have access to critical nutrition services, preventative nutrition services and life-saving treatment of children with SAM both in IDP camps, PoCs and host communities. UNICEF and cluster partners to respond to nutrition needs using different interventions namely nutrition assessments (screening and nutrition surveys), community based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM), Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition interventions and micronutrient supplementation specifically vitamin A supplementation and deworming.UNICEF will also work closely with other sectors to deliver integrated nutrition sensitive interventions through an array of cross-sectoral linkages with education, health, water and sanitation, child protection interventions to contribute to reduction in stunting and acute malnutrition. Linkages with the health sector will include nutritional screening integrated community case management and treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition, distribution of long lasting insecticide treated Nets (LLITN), malaria testing & treatment, immunization of children under the age of five years attending OTP sites and integration of Vitamin A supplementation and deworming into national immunization days (NIDS) where feasible. Linkages with WASH will substantially complement efforts to reduce diarrheal infections through improved access to water and sanitation at the OTP sites, provision of WASH related non- food items and creating communities that are open defecation-free. In addition, where UNICEF is working with implementing partners, partnership agreements will include ensuring cross-sectoral programming as per the partner’s capacity. Linkages with the existing conditional social protection schemes especially as part of the exit strategy from the CMAM program cushions children from relapse once they are discharged from the OTP. Working together with education and child protection sector to provide child play materials and ECD kits for stimulation of children at either at the OTP or SC.Importantly protection mainstreaming, community engagement, accountability to affected population, gender based programming, protection from sexual abuse and gender inequalities will remain central to different to nutrition programs implemented by UNICEF and implementing partners in South Sudan.

Activities

UNICEF and implementing partners will respond to nutrition needs using different interventions and activities. The identified humanitarian needs will be addressed through a mix of the following activities:• Community mobilization and active screening for early case detection of children with SAM• Provide integrated treatment to children with SAM both at static facilities and through community outreach. • Nutrition care and support for pregnant and lactating women during emergencies• Train health and nutrition workers on MIYCN at national, state and community level• Provide individual and group IYCF counselling to caregivers of children aged below 24 months.• Support advocacy and promotion of MIYCN activities through world breastfeeding week events both at national and state level.• Train health and nutrition workers on CMAM to improve care for children with malnutrition. • Provide integrated care for children with SAM (OTP/SC) in health facilities, WASH services in nutrition.• Rehabilitate OTP structures and storage facilities for nutrition supplies. • Provide integrated on-job coaching, mentorship on SAM treatment, supplies management and MIYCN.• Monitor the progress and quality of nutrition interventions and services at the static nutrition facilities and outreach sites.• Conduct nutrition assessments through SMART surveys and FSNMS.• Routine management of nutrition program data and information.• Malaria testing and treatment at the OTP sites.• Distribution of WASH NFI (non- food items) and hygiene promotion at the OTP sites.• Distribution of education ECD kits at the OTP sites.• Create awareness and provide GBV referral services at the OTP sites.

Strategic Objective 1

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

UNICEF nutrition program is designed to work closely with the communities from project inception through implementation and monitoring of results. UNICEF will ensure active consultations with the beneficiaries or affected populations during onsite monitoring which allows for feedback from beneficiary. Similarly, nutrition assessments conducted at the household level provided useful feedback from the beneficiaries. UNICEF also ensures high level financial accountability is delivered in line with UN HACT Assurance systems so that resource reach the affected population.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages are context specific and usually depends on the nutrition intervention and target beneficiaries while taking into consideration gender and age group needs. UNICEF’s Nutrition programme mainly targets boys and girls under five years with severe acute malnutrition. Therefore, the programme design and implementation duly considers the needs of the target groups . UNICEF also considers gender specific needs like Maternal infant and Young Child Nutrition needs of pregnant mothers and caregivers of children between 0-24months who receive key messages on prevention of malnutrition and vital knowledge on key child caring practices .In addition ,UNICEF disaggregates all nutrition program data CMAM data by gender in all the interventions. All the nutrition assessments and analysis incorporate gender considerations highlighting nutrition needs of boys and girls. Finally UNICEF gives due consideration to gender during recruitment of staff

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All UNICEF staff working on the nutrition project must take up a course of sexual exploitation and be certified as part of mandatory orientation package at the time of employment. UNICEF staff also sign up a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries as well as report any suspected case of sexual exploitation and abuse. The beneficiaries are also provided with toll free helpline for reporting cases of abuse and exploitation.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

UNICEF works closely with community leaders and beneficiaries from project design, inception throughout implementation and monitoring of results. UNICEF collects feedback from communities and affected populations during onsite monitoring visits. Similarly, nutrition assessments conducted at the household level provide useful feedback from the beneficiaries on the nutrition services. Community volunteers working in UNICEF projects also conduct follow up home visits to children on program.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

UNICEF nutrition project will ensure community sensitization system is in place to represent different interests and needs; establish quality and accountability to affected population (QAAP) committee in which Mother to Mother support groups are part of it; establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community ; provide training to the nutrition programme staff to understand how to serve best the beneficiaries using the QAAP approach; Raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the nutrition sites ; Involve the communities in design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation on the planed or on-going nutrition project.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will ensure highest level of community engagement through involvement of community members in needs assessment, community feedback sessions and focused group discussions . The community volunteers will also conduct follow up visit to monitor progress of beneficiaries.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In collaboration with the child protection section, UNICEF will ensure that children and their mothers are provided with psycho-social counselling to ensure that women can continue to care for their children. Gender based violence (GBV) cases encountered will be referred for appropriate support. Children undergoing nutrition treatment at the inpatient Stabilization Centre (SC) will be provided will toys and play materials for stimulation as part of SC treatment. Monitoring and reporting of situations through state and community based facilities and mechanisms to follow up on population movement, protection risks, trends and incidents to prevent atrocities, grave child rights violations.Efforts will also be made to link all the children discharged from CMAM to existing social protection schemes.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Access constraints is a constant challenge in all the ten states where the project will be implemented, UNICEF will therefore engage technical support of an access specialist who will negotiate access for effective project implementation. Additionally, nutrition services will be set up proximity to beneficiaries to ensure safe access by the beneficiaries. The IRRM modality will be employed to ensure services are taken closer to the beneficiaries as much as possible even those in hard to reach areas. UNICEF will use survival kits response mechanism in case security does not allow access for normal IRRM activities to be implemented.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

UNICEF will work closely with protection actors to incorporate community awareness to UNICEF will work closely with protection actors to incorporate community awareness to address inequalities or protection threats for example preferential feeding practices where women do not always have the same access to food as men and children. Sensitization during project activities to address cultural inequalities where men may be fed first and receive the biggest and best portions. Similarly, women themselves may choose to give food to their children first and eat last, in such instances pregnant and lactating women are especially at considerable risk of malnutrition. Sometimes (women/ adolescent mothers) who have been raped may discontinue breast feeding). Some of these inequalities will be addressed through mother support groups sessions.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project scope will include coverage surveys and bottleneck analysis to identify barriers to access for different nutrition interventions and identify solution to barriers of access to service delivery and uptake.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Most of locations where the project will be implemented in South Sudan are fragile , therefore safety and security will be monitored before setting up nutrition site and service delivery points. This will ensure to ensure protection of lives , nutrition supplies and materials . In most insecure locations, UNICEF will use IRRM modality and the mission teams will be accompanied by a security and access specialist to the mission fields. Additionally, women and children will be provided with safe and child friendly spaces so they can play and care for their children.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The conduct of UNICEF staff and partners will be trained and monitored on how they attend to the needs of the beneficiaries with respect, honor and use of appropriate language.
