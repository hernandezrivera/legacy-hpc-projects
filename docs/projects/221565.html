Project Summary

The project will deliver both static and mobile Primary Healthcare services to 20,000 beneficiaries (4,325 children U5 years of age and 15,675 adults) to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard to reach populations and is expected to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services and generally improve the health of the population of communities in Lainya and Morobo Counties.
The project will complement ongoing interventions by SSUHA in supporting essential life-saving healthcare service provision by improving equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations through provision of essential drugs/equipment to support the growing numbers of returnees in 20 static health facilities and10 mobile sites. It will be implemented through the already existing Government -MoH systems to enhance sustainability through strengthening of the health system to offer quality health services. It will support the existing health facilities through capacity building for health facility staff, provision of additional essential drugs/equipment and provision of additional staff to conduct mobile outreaches. This, combined with emergency preparedness and response, will ensure service continuity and will play crucial role in achieving the Health Sector Development Plan’s overall objective of "a healthy and productive population of South Sudan living a dignified life with emphasis on maternal and child health with attention to effectiveness, efficiency, and equity". Proposed interventions will directly contribute to Cluster objectives 1, 2, and 3.
SSUHA will seek to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services, reduce excess morbidity and mortality, enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. Proposed activities will seek to contribute to improvement in the physical and mental wellbeing of 20,000 beneficiaries including 710 U5 years vaccinated for pent3, 1,200 children 6months to 15 years vaccinated for measles, 946 persons supported with mental health services, 2,088 persons who are affected by conflict and displacement supported with PSS services, 814 persons with disabilities are supported with rehabilitation services and 50 survivors of GBV are supported with CMR services. 20 Static facilities and 10 mobile outreach teams will be strengthened through provision of medical supplies and improvement of staff capacity. Remote villages will be reached through mobile clinics; community mobilizers and Boma Health Workers. Among the activities that SSUHA will conduct includes; supplemental vaccination campaign to 4,325 children U5 years and Women of childbearing age; Provision of fuel to facilitate integrated mobile outreach since there are already 2 vehicles supported under HSTP. Provision of FANC and PNC to pregnant and lactating mothers, strengthening physical and mental healthcare system. Treatment and control of communicable diseases targeting 4,325 children U5 years and 15,675 adults; Strengthening of referral system between Primary Healthcare facilities, the community (remote villages) and the regional hospitals; Creating Disease Surveillance and Response plan to monitor morbidities and mortalities; Training of 80 health workers on CMRS, IMCI, BEmONC and disease surveillance and response. Conducting disease alert verifications within 24 hours. Training will be conducted for Boma health workers on integrated community case management on communicable diseases such as pneumonia, diarrhea, and malaria.

1. Needs

South Sudan has some of the worst health outcome indicators globally, and Lainya and Morobo are no exceptional. There is poor access and utilization of health, nutrition and WASH services due to the many years of insecurity and conflicts, neglect and limited funding for social and health services. These are coupled with an acute shortage of health workers in recent years among other factors. South Sudan’s Maternal Mortality Rates (MMR) are among the highest in the world, presenting over 1,223 per 100,000 live births (MMEIG report, 2022). According to the Jan- Oct 2024 DHIS2 data, average ANC4 coverage in the two counties is very low (at 43.4%); lainya (54.8%) and Morobo (32.0%). The low ANC attendance implies limited access of pregnant women to life saving services which negatively affect healthy pregnant outcomes and survival, and also the health of newborns. There is need to work towards increasing the number of pregnant mothers attending the MOH recommended ANC visits, availability of skilled health workers with necessary equipment and supplies supported with appropriate community initiatives. According to DHIS2 Jan-Oct 2024 data, the leading causes of morbidity in <5 across the 2 counties was Malaria (67.6.0% Lainya) and (53.4% Morobo) of total <5 consultations. Many of these occurred due to lack of basic quality health care, vaccinations, proper food, clean water and sanitation compounded by fragile security situation which barred efforts to address preventable communicable and infectious diseases. Pentavalent vaccine coverage in the 2 counties is 33.0% (DHIS2 Jan-Oct 2024). These figures are very low compared to the national figures of 73% and 84% respectively (WUENIC and MOH). The low vaccination coverage coupled with inadequate disease surveillance and response, increased population movements and displacements all make these areas vulnerable and at risk of outbreaks which needs to be addressed. RI, disease surveillance and response activities need to be strengthened at all facilities in addition to being integrated into planned mobile outreach activities. Average estimated wasting among children <5 years in Lainya and Morobo counties is 10.90% ,(2023 Nutrition Pin). The causes of malnutrition are attributed to limited food production due to insecurity and conflicts, destruction of food crops due to cattle keepers invasion, and high influx of returnees from the neighboring countries of Uganda and DR Congo. The situation is likely to change hence SSUHA needs to put more effort to provide BPHNS with integration of Nutrition, WASH, FSL and GBV to prevent malnutrition. The project will be implemented through in coordination with the existing Government -MoH systems (CHDs) to enhance sustainability through strengthening the health system to offer quality health services to address the immediate health needs of the population and will contribute to the realization of National HSSP 2023-2027 vision of a healthy and productive population of South Sudan living a dignified life, and will further strengthen the health system and partnership to overcome barriers to effective delivery of BPHNS and efficiently respond to quality and safety concerns of communities through integrated health and nutrition service delivery and health systems development.

2. Project activities

South Sudan Health Association will provide life saving health interventions which are in line with the South Sudan Ministry of Health National Health Policy 2023-2027 which conforms with the health cluster strategic objectives 1, 2 and 3. The outreach/mobile clinic will improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations, reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases as well as improve the quality of life of 20, 000 beneficiaries (4,325 children U5 years of age as well as 15,675 adults) by providing access to health services in remote and conflict affected hard to reach areas particularly access to maternal child health, general consultation, screening for malnutrition and routine immunization services and enhancing resilience and promoting humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. The main activities will include; disease prevention, control and treatment of communicable and non communicable diseases including epidemic-prone diseases in 20 static health facilities and 10 outreach/mobile sites. • Conduct disease consultations both at static and mobile outreaches targeting 20, 000 beneficiaries (4,325 U5 children and 15,675 adults).• Conduct training of 80 health workers on IPC and provision of IPC materials targeting 20 health facilities and 10 mobile sites. • Provide routine immunization targeting U1 years for vaccination as well as support national vaccination campaigns in addition to routine immunization services including growth monitoring, IYCN and malnutrition screening for under fives, and pregnant and lactating mothers targeting 1,200 children of six months to 15 years with measles vaccination. • Provide rehabilitation services targeting 814 persons with disabilities.• Provide mental health services targeting 969 beneficiaries • Conduct skilled health facility deliveries targeting 1,300 skilled births and provision of family planning, essential drugs/equipment and referral of critical cases both at outreach and fixed health facilities to 20,000 beneficiaries.• Provide Clinical management of rape services targeting 50 survivors• Provide psychosocial services targeting 2,088 beneficiaries • Support disease alert verification targeting 90% of all alerts to be verified within 24 hours.• Provide IEHK health kits to 20 supported health facilities to conduct mobile outreaches.• Conduct community health events (health education) targeting behavior change• Establish community feed back mechanisms to all the 20 supported health facilities.• Conduct community consultation meetings quarterly to identify community needs.•Provide risk communications and community engagement to communities to demand for services through a network of Boma Health workers and community committees.
