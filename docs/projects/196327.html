Project Summary

"WHO is lead health sector agency to support the MoHFW and coordinate health partners to support the beneficiaries for essential health service delivery, protect people from various health hazards including emerging and re-emerging diseases to provide a better healthy life at Cox’s Bazar
WHO’s response strategy is aligned with the overall Health Sector Strategy 2023-2024 and aims to deliver on critical priorities based on the issues highlighted by the SAG and the JRP 2022 monitoring framework. WHO factored into considerations previous MSNAs, Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment May 2021, Comprehensive Gender Analysis within Rohingya and Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh (March 2022), Health Sector Rationalization exercise, 2022, and the WHO HeRAMS, 2022 and Population In Need (ISCG, 2022)
Rohingya; 52% female and 48% male in 33 camps. 16% are children <5 years; 36% are children 5 to17 years; 44% are adults and 4% are elderly (>60%) and 1% are persons with disabilities. Host, 14.7%,38.5%,44.4% and 4.6%, respectively. 7.1% of people in Bangladesh have functional difficulties- higher in males. Changes in gender roles and practices have been noticed which were more favorable in host communities. Equal rights;60% of Rohingya and 68% for host community. Participation in community decision making was at 45%, 50% of the host community. Childcare, a role for women; 70%of Rohingya, 60% of host. Women needed permission of male at 95% for both.
In 2022 and beyond, WHO will uphold its support to the Government of Bangladesh and partners towards the monitoring and utilization of essential service delivery through standardized monitoring with Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) interventions; containment and prevention of emerging diseases, upsurges and outbreak of diseases within the camps and adjacent host communities; preparedness, readiness and response to multiple hazards including disaster risk reduction , resilience and contributing to addressing climate change, advocating on behalf of the Health Sector on inadequately addressed issues including rolling out of HeRAMS and rationalization exercise to enhance efficiency pivotal in ensuring quality and accountable service utilization of women and girls, boys and men.
The health-card (booklet) for all Rohingya, strengthening routine immunization system will be additional priority, while continuing to deliver an effective health response to the Rohingya crisis through increased investment in public/local institutions and capacity development of local government, health care partners and health facilities staff, at PHC facilities, Field hospitals, UHCs, and other secondary level facilities on delivery of various elements of the minimum essential package services, referral and secondary health care services.
WHO intends to continue providing regular and timely information on public health risks and requirements and filling gaps through effective coordination, communication, collaboration and effective response ensuring access to life saving interventions. The project will maintain capacity to prepare for and respond to needs that result from health emergencies and outbreaks, rapid response training, prepositioning of kits, diagnostic capacities, among others.
The underlying principles of WHO’s work remain and will be reinforced to liaise with Government on the governance and leadership through Civil Surgeon’s Office and MOHFW Coordination Centre, support in developing and adapting standards, guidelines and protocols, extending of national programs to the refugee settings and; avail health workforce, vaccines and essential technology, health information, for neonates, children, boys and girls and women and men in both the Rohingyas and host community. WHO, as the lead of the Health Sector, will continue to work towards equity and quality of services, strongly engage government in localization efforts and stimulate documentation and publication.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The following needs have been identified that will guide WHO response in Cox’s Bazar. These include the need;• to monitor and invigorate access to healthcare services and treatment at primary healthcare center and clinic by taking initiatives to eliminate barriers• for coordinated health response with the ministry of health and health partners providing essential health services to the affected population in Rohingya camps and host community. This has to ensure minimal duplication of efforts, fill critical gaps on availability as identified by various assessments and the Health Resources and Services Availability Monitoring System, safe access and quality of health services for men, women, boys, girls and at-risk groups including the rehabilitation services for those with disabilities• to reverse the negative impacts impacted to the essential health services under the COVID-19 outbreak on health-seeking behavior and increasing capacity to develop as a more resilient health systems within the camps and adjacent host community.• to utilize the risk perception exercise and strategic tool for assessment of risks, health facility safety and resilience tools, among others to address vulnerabilities of the Rohingya weather-related and other emergencies to mitigate effects and strengthen resilience to incidents which also impact disease trends disproportionately for men, women, boys, and girls. • for continued coordination of emergency response and preparedness in relation to monsoon and cyclone to minimize health impact on the affected Rohingya population and host community.• to continuously training health humanitarian workers on various aspects of the essential health care packages• to sustain improvements in immunization coverage and quality of services to achieve coverage of >95% and dropout rate of less than 5% with catch up campaigns and Supplementary Immunization activities.• to improve health worker and patient safety through implementing IPC program with adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities .• to support referral pathways for comprehensive emergency obstetric care with services, improve quality of newborn care, family planning provision (particularly increasing uptake of long-acting methods) and be responsive to adolescent health needs• to strengthen prehospital and critical patient care management systems for girls and boys, women and men.• for continued strengthening of health facilities to deliver GBV care based following several capacity building initiates that have been undertaken to bridge identified gaps. Dedicated supportive supervision. • for additional and continued advocacy effort of extending national health programs (such as EPI, TB/HIV/AIDS/Hepatitis) to Rohingya men, women, boys and girls. • to address shortages in NCD management, laboratory capacity and build on community health promotion and lifestyles• to address mental health and psychosocial support training gaps, advocacy of child and adolescent mental health services and address access to information on substance abuse • for health care waste management solution including safe disposal of expired medicines and enhanced water quality surveillance in collaboration with DPHE, and WASH actors.• to advance rational use of medicines to reduce risk for antimicrobial resistance through Anti-microbial stewardship, antimicrobial surveillance and other interventions under one health.• to address risks for communicable and neglected tropical diseases, strengthen and sustain system for prevention, early detection and response to outbreak prone diseases and impacts of climate change• Need to increase preventive measures to reduce harmful practices, empower individuals (men, women, and other diverse genders), more male change agents' capacity on gender equality and gender issues like SRHR."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"This project is aligned with the rationalization principles in WHO’s strategic intervention, which are further being materialized through the Health Sector Strategic Advisory Group. The rationalization exercise guided by the essential services packages and services availability, which has also been published by WHO through the HeRAMS. WHO maintains a lean coordination structure in concert to the needs of the response at Cox’s Bazar and the many camps, often integrating as much as possible. WHO continues to engage in inter sector discussions on integration and establishing linkages with nutrition sector, WASH sector and others for complimentarily and efficiency in the delivery of aid assistance to the Rohingya and host community. The support to the Civil Surgeons’ office with a Ministry Of Health and Family Welfare Coordination centers staffed to support the humanitarian response as well as the host community in Cox’s Bazar is bold step to enhance government led approaches in the delivery of services, coordination and monitoring and sustained partnerships. With a well-coordinated partners network, WHO continues to ensure there is prioritization of required support to meet immediate humanitarian response needs, response to concurrent upsurges and outbreaks of diseases, while streamlining health service delivery for refugees and host communities at Cox’s Bazar, building on investments made over the past years to ultimately contribute towards building a resilient health system that is predictable and easy to sustain/continue. Training needs and service assessments will be conducted, as needed. In 2021 and 2022, WHO training benefited participants- 35% from HPs, 47% from PHCs, and 18% from secondary and dedicated disease treatment facilities. Analysis of medical commodities provided to partners over last 3years; 41.15% to secondary health facilities,40.5% to PHCs and 18.3% including to referral Govt facilities. Continued rationalized support will be guided by analysis

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

WHO has a PSEA policy in place and sensitizes on this policy. The related trainings are mandatory for all staff, consultants and volunteers. WHO has PSEA focal person, and within the health sector ensures cross-cutting issues are addressed including training on protection mainstreaming in collaboration with relevant sectors. Moreover, WHO has put a system in place on response mechanism for victims and ensure all supports required for victims. In order to engage implementing partners and contractors who align with our policy, Request for Proposal templates and the Invitation to Bid templates incorporate policy directive on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Also reflected in Agreement for Performance of Works (APW), the Technical Services Agreement (TSA) and Purchase Orders. WHO harness IP assessments through the ISCG to guide decisions on partner engagement. Additionally, Direct Funding Cooperation with Government are done with an implementing partner assessment

3.2 - Gender

WHO maintains technical staff recruited specifically to support bridge the gaps identified in the analysis, a GBV consultant, with supporting components of access to services. WHO supports capacity building for health service providers on clinical management of rape (CMR) and intimate partner violence (IPV) to address the needs of victims of GBV and ensure delivering quality services through use of GBV toolkit in health facilities; Moreover, adolescent SRHR training capacitates the efficiency of health staff to ensure access to services. WHO collaborates with GBV sub sectors partners who jointly support implementation of activities transforming gender roles/relations, like adolescent SRH care training and others. WHO will capitalize on supporting gender perspectives within health facilities and supporting establishing necessary linkages. WHO through the health sector ensures that facilities have at least one female doctor.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

WHO through the health sector will regularly monitor reporting in EWARS; DHIS-2 and 4Ws and adherence to SADD by partners and working groups including situation reports, health sector bulletin including Under 5 years of age and above for males and females data disaggregation. Staff undertake training - United to Respect: Preventing sexual harassment and other prohibited conduct and are expected to conduct themselves in a way that shows their commitment to provide assistance on the basis of need alone and without discrimination, in particular any person under the age of 18, including discrimination or other forms of punishment on the basis of many diversities including birth (or other) status; or the status, activities, expressed opinions, or beliefs of the child's parents, legal guardians, or family members. Conduct that undermines the protection and safeguarding of children is prohibited. Conduct that violates international legal requirements with regard to children is prohibited

3.4 - Diversity

Common to all UN agencies, all staff are to undertake a United to Respect: Preventing sexual harassment and other prohibited conduct. Staff, consultants and contractors are expected to conduct themselves in a way that shows their commitment to provide assistance on the basis of need alone and without discrimination against any person including discrimination or other forms of punishment on the basis of race; colour; sex; language; sexual orientation; gender identity; religion; political or other opinion; national, ethnic or social origin; disability; birth (or other) status; or the status, activities, expressed opinions, or beliefs of the child's parents, legal guardians, or family members. WHO offers technical guidance on tools on disability inclusion in health projects and participates in inter-agency coordination mechanisms. Integration of disability in the results framework of Health Sector is ensured and monitoring on quarterly basis in terms of access, ramps and rails etc.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"Activities related to WHO’s direct involvement in Health Sector coordination have been incorporated into this project. More attention is given to addressing the delivery of essential health services, implementation of Guidelines for Protection Mainstreaming-2021, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) Framework, Health Sector Gender Action Plan (GAP) 2021. WHO will offer technical guidance to the Risk Communication and Community Engagement Working Group and incorporates AAP principles into discussions with Community Health Working Group. The planned interventions are be guided by periodical service availability assessments. External independent reviews and assessments will be used as an opportunity for the Rohingya and host community populations to review the work of WHO"

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

WHO developed a standard procedure for health care waste management in Cox’s Bazar and will continue to capacitate health service providers at all levels of the health system and monitor facilities adopting effective management of health care waste and thus minimizing the impact of health services on the environment and on climate change. Promotion of the usage of renewable energy sources-solar in the health care facilities is part of WASH FIT Programme which will jointly contribute to build awareness on environmental and health linkages when possible. The upcoming phase of a community risk perception exercise, which also assess environmental risks, will further advise mitigation and response measures to disasters that affect the environment. More attention will be accorded to engagement on environmental conservation and implementation of health emergency and disaster risk management framework to contribute to reduction in effects of climate change at community level
