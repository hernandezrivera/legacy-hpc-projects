Project Summary

In line with the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) sector Strategic Objectives (SO1 & SO 2), part of the revised HRP 2023, which was done mainly to respond to the current crises (15 April 2023) and due to this emergency, the cost of the revised 2023 HRP will only focus on lifesaving activities. This project will aim to improve access to lifesaving assistance for food-insecure IDPs affected by recent crises. The project will target around 80,000 vulnerable people (16,000) HHS (34,400 women, 25,600 men, 11,200 girls, and 7,200 boys) as direct beneficiaries who are identified to be in need facing extreme and catastrophic severity levels (category 4 & 5) food insecurity in Khartoum, North Darfur, Kassala and Blue Nile states by various interventions including general food distribution (full ratio) for 6-months, general food distribution (half ratio) for 6-months, agricultural support in terms of seeds and tools, livestock support in term of material distribution treatment and kits, livelihoods support in term of cash and vouchers. The ultimate beneficiaries of the project are mainly women and men, IDPs, small farmers, pastoralists, and poor residents who will be provided with lifesaving assistance through promoting access to environment-friendly agricultural productivity inputs and services. The project will support FSL strategic objective one (SO1), improving the food security status of the insecure by life sustain interventions through providing life-saving assistance to meet the food needs of the affected population during the crisis and offering emergency agriculture life-saving assistance to ensure food production. The timely emergency life-saving livelihood in the form of crop seeds, tools, and livestock support, will facilitate the vulnerable population to support during the lean season and help in prior planning of planting season during or soon after the rains. This will rapidly increase food production while safeguarding livelihood sources.
Besides the affected people & IDPs, many targeted populations in targeted areas depend mainly on natural resource utilization for cultivation, livestock rearing, fishing and related businesses.
The project will support the self-resilience of the affected by community priorities by building their productive assets and income-generating opportunities to save and sustain lives (SO2).
The project will be implemented in 4 states, covering 14 localities. The project activities are the Provision of general food distribution -full ratio- for 4000, most of the need HHs, and a half – ratio for 3000 HHs. Provision and distribution of agricultural support (drought-resisting crops and vegetable seeds and seedlings package, farmers’ schools, horticultural diversification and agroecology production, post-harvest reduction techniques, community-based women and men agricultural extensionist training) to 3000 small-scale farmers. Provision and distribution of livestock support (establishing livestock migration routes, support vaccination campaigns, poultry raising establishment, livestock health awareness services, livestock watering and migration routes security) to 1000 pastoralists. Provide livelihood support in terms of cash assistance for agriculture support to 5000 to most in need from the IDPs affected by the current emergency; these activities will be delivered in participatory with all relevant stakeholders. Practical Action has a long presence and great legacy in working with communities in the project operational areas. Besides, it's capacity and capability to deliver multiple projects.

1 - Needs

Clashes between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) have continued for about 40 days, especially in and around Khartoum, as of 14 May, killing at least 700 people and injuring 6000 since the fighting began, according to the Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) as of 21 May 2023, this crisis along with the other reasons such as economic decline and inflation, floods and poor harvest in 2022, high food prices and conflicts across targeted states, was affected the food security negatively and lead to continued deteriorating. The macro-economic situation affects all states through inflation, and about 40 per cent of the population in phase 2 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) could slide into phase 3. Also, the disruptions in supply chains and markets have caused the price of food and other essential commodities to increase by 400 per cent compared to before the conflict (revised HRP 2023). Before the current crisis, 16.2 million people were food insecure across Sudan; according to the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment, which represented 34 per cent of the population, the number of People in Need increased to 19.9 million (OCHA). Besides other reasons, such as the tribal conflict in the Blue Nile assumed hundreds of lives and left thousands displaced. Reported sporadic tribal clashes, car hijacking, robberies and crime continued in North Darfur, with over 900,000 displaced across the state, according to IOM. In Kassala state, Floods coupled with dry spells, insecurity and poor post-harvest crop management have negatively affected annual harvest and food/livelihoods security in the three targeted states. The IPC analysis 2022 - 2023 estimates that the early harvest of 2022 is below the yearly average by 35% leading to an expected deficit in cereal production, leaving 64% of country households without enough stocks to carry them through this year’s lean season. The general socioeconomic situation reflects on food and livelihood security. OCHA states profiles (August and September 2022) show high malnutrition rates across the three states. Prevalence of global Acute malnutrition among children under five reached 18.5% in Kassala and 28% in North Darfur, and 11% in Blue Nile states. This is coupled with poor access to essential services, particularly for women and girls, where 36%, 51% and 48% of young women 15 to 24 years of age are literate in Blue Nile, Kassala and North Darfur states, respectively. Women also face severe health and nutrition-related problems related to early marriage below 18 years of age, reaching 51%, 48%, and 47% in Blue Nile, Kassala, and North Darfur states. The limited organisational, networking and advocacy capacity of CBOs & national NGOs is a sign constraint to their playing a more decisive role in coordinating and effectively delivering humanitarian support to the affected populations. Women lack adequate representation and have a low voice in community decisions. Strengthening the capacity of these institutions in various areas will enable them to plan, prepare and effectively respond to future food insecurity and livelihood hazards. It is worth noting that Practical Action has extensive experience through different projects in developing the organisational, institutional, and technical capacities of civil society organisations and government institutions. Environment: Climate change and related natural disasters (floods, droughts, and dry spells) strongly affect the socioeconomic situation of communities and households and can contribute to the number of people requiring humanitarian support. Environmental degradation (e.g., land and forests) and unsustainable use of natural resources are both contributory factors and consequences of the humanitarian situation in Sudan. The degradation of natural resources has forced most of the rural population to overgraze range lands and expand cropping in marginal.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Practical Action recognizes that hard climate conditions and the mismanagement of the natural resources contribute to poverty in Sudan. Most of Sudan's people are reliant on their farming for their livelihoods, despite the fact that Sudan is one of the most fertile lands in Africa, yet the productivity is low due to rain scarcity, desertification, deforestation, absence of proper irrigation methods, and a lack of knowledge and awareness on natural resources management and climate change. Practical Action through its "Energy that transforms" Change Ambitious have invested in activities that promotes safe impact of environment such as introducing clean cooking stoves for households in Darfur, BN and Kassala states, which improved women and girls' health and reduced the burden on the environment. The "Farming that works" Change Ambitious helped reducing conflicts over limited resources at a local level, enabled farmers and pastoralist adapt to new climate reality and improving their resilience. "Cities fit for people" Change Ambitious has contributed to environment-friendly waste disposal practices, in a holistic community-focused approach to improve sanitation and waste disposal.Therefore, Practical Action 3 Change Ambitious are realizing and focusing on imposing positive impact on environment, addressing climate change challenges, building the local capacity and awareness on adopting best practices related to environment conservation and climate resilience and mitigation. Throughout this project, farmers and vulnerable households will be trained on seed farming for climate adaptation, soil drought resistant and soil organic carbon, agroecology, the impact of deforestation and desertification, natural resources management, disasters risk reduction, pollution and waste management among other activities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Practical Action ensures understanding and commitment to the safeguarding policies package by all employees, partners and contractors, which states Zero tolerance to all types of sexual exploitation and abuse especially against children, women and vulnerable adults. Complaints system will be put in place to ensure safe and effective communication channel is active for all beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The system includes different tools including hotlines, emails, complaints boxes and others. As communities will be well-oriented and engaged in initial consultations and need assessments, Practical Action will ensure inclusion of the needs of different community groups including women and disabled people to assure that no one is left behind! Doing No harm principle will also be maintained to ensure no negative implications will arise from the project interventions that violate people safety, security, dignity or create damage to the resources. The project will enhance communities’ protection through provision of market-based livelihoods and income generating activities that will reduce tensions and competition over natural resources, raising the public awareness on conflict resolution and peaceful co-existence.
