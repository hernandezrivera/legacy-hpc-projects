Project Summary

The current economic crisis in Lebanon has presented itself on the CP sector through the increase of working children in different facets.
This has been reflected by CP sector dashboard shared by UNICEF during February 2022, which states that CP sector partners reported, through (RTM) survey, Child Labor (52% of responses) was the most predominant risk throughout 2021, Violent Discipline (19%) and Street-Connected Children (18%).
The UNHCR has shown in its report that there has been 23% increase in children receiving Case Management in 2021, in areas including Tripoli and Akkar. This states the need for a call of act towards protecting these children and preventing the increase of cases.
By the proposed Programme Al-Fayhaa association (AFA) is willing to apply for a Child Protection program targeting Lebanese, migrants, and Palestinian in North and Akkar.
At the beginning of the programme, AFA will hold an outreach activity to target the most vulnerable children who will participate in the program’s different Pathways, on top of that AFA through different community engagement initiatives and awareness sessions will make sure to fill in the vulnerability criteria checklist to be able to refer Out of School Children to Formal education, non-formal education, protection or any other needed services, .
The programme focuses on both girls, boys and women from Lebanese, migrants and Palestinian backgrounds that reside in marginalized areas in North. Moreover, vulnerable Lebanese, migrants and Palestinian children still experience physical violence, abuse and child labor that contributes with the need of case management, Focus Psychosocial Support and community based Psychosocial Support activities.
AFA will implement case management, for 150 cases of children involved in child labor and street connected children in Akkar, Tripoli, Minnieh-Dinnieh, and Zgharta.
Moreover, AFA will conduct Community Based PsychoSocial Support to low and medium case children at risk of child Labor, early marriage, or any other type of risks, this target will be divided to two age groups, 200 Children aged (6-11) and 200 children aged (12-17), in addition to 400 Medium to high-risk Cases benefiting from Focussed PsychoSocial Support sessions, including Focus actions on meeting the immediate identified needs of the child and the services they are willing to accept and provide information on other services or supports that are available to meet their needs.
To establish the purpose of the program and to provide a holistic approach to all the family, 600 caregivers will participate in positive parenting and focus psychosocial support sessions to provide children’s caregivers the best methodologies and parenting skills to deal with their children and protect them from any possible risks and harm.
Moreover, AFA team will implement several community-initiatives related to Qudwa toolbox, which engages the community for the aim of finding solutions and raise awareness on the Violence Against Children, Child Protection, Violence Against Women, Child Labor.
AFA has gained a high reputation in the community where its centres have become safe spaces for many children and their families. AFA has the potential to plan, implement and follow-up CP activities since the organization has successfully completed a CP program in collaboration with DRC under the fund of UNICEF, with the following activities: Case management (child labor), Focussed and community based Psychosocial Support activities. As for Positive Parenting, it was directed to female caregivers to help them learn more about their children’s needs and feelings, as well as equip them with positive parenting tools. AFA has the potential to respond to CP matters with its trained staff on SOP. Other activities have reinforced positive attitudes and social norms among parents through using the Qudwa caregiver’s toolbox.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Many of the identified children at risk will be girls’ survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) or at risk of GBV, those vulnerable groups alongside with caregiver/parents that are GBV survivors will be provided with sensitive and critical services to offer the best support. These principles include, but are not limited to: ensuring survivor-centered approaches and do no harm.Facilitators, staff and volunteers will be trained on the do no harm approach as well as on Al Fayhaa Association PSEA and child protection policy and code of conduct. Inclusive trainings will be cascaded to ensure delivery of our programs respecting the do no Harm to PWSN. On the other hand, parents will be trained on positive-parenting approaches where topics to increase their self-esteem will be implemented. A Gender Focal point will support all the beneficiaries and affected population claiming their rights and reporting correctly the incidences.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Through the implementation of its projects, AFA distributes assessments among the beneficiaries aiming to gain feedback from the affected populations on their needs and thus design awareness sessions catering for their requirements and ensuring accountability while implementing our projects. The above ensures retention of the targeted beneficiaries allowing room for mutual feedback and project. A tool for feedback is kept at centers at all times, where individuals can fill in feedback surveys and submit them confidentially (complain Box). From the believe that feedback from beneficiaries is key to sustainable outcomes that ensure greater impact; the feedback is usually gathered through different means such as questionnaires, surveys, evaluation forms.Also, to follow up on all complaints/cases in line with AAP policy, AFA will make sure to have an Accountability Focal point throughout this project to ensure and monitor the do no harm approach and the proper delivery of our services.
