Project Summary

Malaria is endemic in South Sudan and the start of the transmission season coincide with the onset of the rainy season. Morbidity and mortality due to malaria is very high with 50.2% of cases and 39.4% of deaths attributed to malaria The proposed project will work toward achieving the Health Cluster Objective 3: “Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases.” This objective is linked to Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 of the 2024 HRP. The project will protect the target population from malaria and other Vector Borne Diseases (VBD) through implementing Integrated Vector Management (IVM) activities. Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS), mosquito larviciding and fly control measures will be employed as tools to disrupt disease transmission together with educating the community on the diseases itself and how to prevent infection through adopting desired positive behavior. The project will also train nursing and medical staff on malaria and other VBD differential diagnosis and case management. This will improve the quality of health service delivery in the target areas. MENTOR has been a member of HRP for three consecutive years and has successfully implemented IVM interventions in the refugee camps and IDP sites in South Sudan with significant results achieved. However, some areas have not received consistent funding or support for IVM interventions, leading to an increase in malaria cases. These areas include: the host community in Maban, which has been regularly affected by the floods since 2019, the host community in Rubkona, Bentiu IDP camp and refugees and returnees in Renk.
The project will therefore seeks funding for similar IVM interventions to be carried out in these areas to prevent and control malaria and other VBD, which are expected to increase after the rainy season and the floods.
This program will aim to support 148,000 individuals, a number which includes 58,000 people living in Bentiu town, 40,000 people living in flood-affected host communities in Maban and 50,000 people living in Renk who have fled or been affected by the conflict in Sudan.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, due to a multitude of factors, including the environment (recurrent periods of flooding and drought), security issues such as intertribal conflicts that cause displacement, poor governance that hinders people’s access to essential services, and the influx of refugees and returnees fleeing the conflict in Sudan which add additional strain on already stretched resources. The government relies heavily on partners and UN agencies for provision of health and other service funding from different donors. UNOCHA recently estimated that around 9.4 million people in South Sudan — including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children and 337,000 refugees — will need humanitarian assistance and protection services in 2023, which reflects 76% of the population. One disease benefiting from the overburdened systems in South Sudan is malaria, which is endemic throughout the entire country and a major public health problem in South Sudan, where it accounts for 66% of outpatient consultations, 50% of admissions and 30% of deaths, according to the WHO African Regional Office. The country also has one of the highest malaria incidence rates in the region, with an estimated 8,750 cases and 20 deaths per day, as reported by the World Malaria Report 2022. Some of the worst affected locations South Sudan are Maban, Renk, and Bentiu. MENTOR has been operational in all four refugee camps in Maban since 2014, implementing vector-borne disease (VBD) prevention on an annual basis. However, the host community could not get consistent funding for the same interventions except in 2018, 2020 and 2021. It has been observed that health facilities serving both the host and refugee communities have seen an increase in malaria cases after the activities have been stopped in the host community due to lack of further funding and the geographical proximity of the host community to the camps. Another location, Bentiu PoC, which currently hosts more than 100,000 IDPs, has historically been badly affected by VBD) as well as water- and food-borne diseases, and the health facilities have struggled to cope with the large numbers of people seeking treatment for those preventable diseases. Lastly, Renk County in Upper Nile State has seen a recent influx of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan. As of 16th October 2023, 347,249 people have entered the County. 300,717 people have entered Renk through the Joda border crossing, of which 10,000 stay in a Transition Centre at any point in time, with people moving in and out, 15,000 have settled in informal camps, and 20,000 have settled in Renk Town. The influx is expected to continue. A main concern is the high number of vulnerable arrivals, which include unaccompanied children, the elderly, disabled individuals, and pregnant women, all of which are at a high risk of contracting malaria. Due to the nature of the camps and Transition Centre, where poorly constructed shelters are overcrowded, exacerbating poor living conditions, these communities are more susceptible to disease, and VBD in particular. As almost all shelters are simple mud and tarpaulin hut, they are exposed to malaria mosquitoes and other disease-transmitting insects, such as sandflies. The vulnerabilities increase during the rainy season where there is a marked increases in the mosquito population. The heavy rains further increase poor sanitation, as latrines overflow and water is contaminated through open defecation. MENTOR therefore seeks funding for IVM interventions to be carried out in the flood- and VBD-affected community in Bunj Town and in Renk, where refugees and returnees have fled to after the conflict in Sudan broke out, to prevent and control malaria and other vector-borne diseases. In addition, MENTOR proposes VBD activities in Bentiu PoC, which has historically suffered from a large VBD burden.

2. Project activities

The MENTOR Initiative proposes to respond to the Health Cluster Objective 3 “Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases” which is linked to the Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 of the 2024 HRP. MENTOR propose the following activities in the Maban host community, Bentiu PoC and in Renk. • Indoor Residual Spraying: The control of adult mosquito disease vectors through IRS is key in reducing the risk of malaria infection. MENTOR will procure WHO-prequalified insecticides for IRS for use in all target locations. Local community workers will be recruited and trained in implementation of IRS by following WHO guidelines. To ensure an optimal level of protection in line with WHO recommendations, MENTOR will aim to achieve at least 85% coverage of shelters prior to the onset of the rainy season. Impact of the IRS intervention will be measured through analysis of weekly malaria incidence reports collected from health facilities in the target locations. • Larviciding for mosquito control: Complimenting IRS, reducing the vector breeding capacity through Larval Source Management (LSM) ensures that the disease transmission capacity of Anopheles, Aedes as well as Culex mosquitoes is limited through preventing larval development. The WHO-recommended larvicide will be applied to all stagnant water containing mosquito larvae. The prevention field staff will routinely assess all potential breeding sites and 90-95% will be treated during three LSM rounds. • Fly Control: Flies can act as mechanical vectors for a multitude of diseases. The WHO-recommended safe chemical Pyriproxyfen will be used for application in latrines, rubbish piles and open defecation sites. The prevention field staff will routinely assess all potential fly breeding sites across the IDP sites and target host communities and 90-95%will be treated in 3 rounds of activity. Staff will also work closely with the WASH cluster and WASH partners to coordinate the timing of intervention. • Capacity building for healthcare workers: The capacity of health care workers will be improved by providing training on differential diagnosis and case management of malaria and other VBDs. They will be trained on the pathology, symptoms, correct differential diagnosis, and case management of VBDs that contribute to morbidity and mortality amongst the target population. On the job training will be provided to serve as follow-up supervision visits. • Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE): This will be implemented through door-to-door RCCE campaigns as well as health education sessions for community leaders. Integrated RCCE will focus on delivering a variety of health messages, such as the promotion of bed net usage, signs and symptoms of VBDs and promotion of early treatment seeking behaviours, prevention of mosquito breeding sites, good waste management, good hygiene and sanitation practices to reinforce the relationship between WASH and VBD control.
