Project Summary

The ongoing Great March of Return (GMR) demonstrations further deteriorated the health sector in Gaza, on top of the devastated socioeconomic conditions compounded by the blockade, energy crisis, salary cuts for PA employees; high unemployment and volatile security situation in Gaza.
The public hospitals are overstretched with urgent treatments for GMR related cases, and expanding the capacity of the health services to absorb increasing needs is necessary to ensure access of vulnerable population to health services. Furthermore, the people in Gaza are in need of psycho-social support to cope with the impact of current and potential man-made and natural disasters. Meanwhile, the Agency's ability to respond to emergency health needs in Gaza is greatly constrained by the tight funding situation compromising its ability to respond as needed.
In light of these conditions, this proposed intervention aims to support the provision of critical healthcare for Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip through (1) supporting health interventions received outside UNRWA facilities, (2) expanding the healthcare services within UNRWA facilities by providing lifesaving medicines and hiring emergency surged staff to cope with increasing demand, (3) providing necessary assistive tools to children in need, and (4) providing mental health and psycho-social support especially women as well as trauma and GMR-affected cases.

1 - Needs

With 80 per cent of its two million of citizens depending on humanitarian assistance, Gaza remains under protracted humanitarian crisis. The process of gradual deterioration under the blockade has left essential services in Gaza barely able to function, thus directly impacting people's basic right to health access, which has been further stretched since the beginning of the Great March of Return. Since 30 March 2018, Gaza has witnessed a significant increase in Palestinian causalities in the context of mass demonstrations taking place along Israel's perimeter fence with Gaza. According to OCHA report, more than 34,440 people have been injured including 7,927 children and 2,576 as of 31 August 2019. This massive number of inquiries has exposed the public health services to unprecedented burden overwhelming its medical and human resources with high workload. Hence, it is getting more and more complicated to access healthcare due to the following; 1. Restriction in terms of people access: The tightened humanitarian space is making the delivery of healthcare challenging. As an example, the first half of 2019, only 67 per cent of patient applications for a permit to exit Gaza through the Erez checkpoint for hospital appointments were approved. In addition, the waiting time for non-elective surgery at Shifa Hospital, one of the largest hospitals in Gaza, has increased from five months in January 2017 to 12 months in September 2019 with a total of 8,400 cases currently on the waiting list; and 2. Restriction in terms of good access: The healthcare system in Gaza has been suffering from increasing strain due to lack of medical supplies, spare parts and equipment needed for maintaining and upgrading health facilities. According to the Ministry of Health, the shortage in essential medical drugs exceeded 52 per cent as of May 2019, leaving the supplies stock to less than one month on average. This shortage is seriously threating the quality and availability of healthcare provided to thousands of patients seeking healthcare services. Furthermore, Gaza has been facing with a chronic electricity deficit for over a decade and has massively impacted the availability of essential services particularly health, water and sanitation services. For instance, emergency health and diagnostic services are at risk, including intensive care operations. In addition, the need of the mental health services has increased significantly, especially for those who were injured or who experienced the loss of their close relatives in recent events. As a result of the current crisis, the WHO estimated that approximately 210,000, or over one in 10 people (10% of the population) will have severe mental health problems and about 54% of Palestinian boys and 47% of Palestinian girls aged six to 12 years reportedly have emotional and/or behavioral disorders, and the overall disease burden for mental illness is estimated to account for about 3% of disability-adjusted life years. This is in addition to the accumulated psychosocial support needs identified in the past years. A 2016 survey conducted by UNRWA found that 55% of sampled patients attending UNRWA health centres demonstrated poor mental health and wellbeing, with 70% being identified as being potentially depressed. UNRWA data has further shown that 80% of women who seek counselling are also experiencing GBV. Due to the problems mentioned above, Gaza's health sector, of which capacities were already overstretched before the crisis, is struggling to cope with the mass influx of causalities while facing acute shortage in medical supplies, overburden on public healthcare services and increase of mental disorders among the Gaza community. Therefore, the needs in terms of supporting the public health sector and strengthening the health services already provided by UNRWA to 70 per cent of people across the Strip should be met rapidly in order to cope with the ongoing crisis.

2 - Activities

UNRWA provides primary healthcare to over 1.3 million Palestine refugees across its 22 Health Centres in the Gaza Strip. The deteriorating socio-economic conditions have seen the UNRWA health system overstretched, and the continuing energy crisis is compelling the Agency to rely on fuel powered generators to minimize interruption of service delivery due to power outages. The Agency is deeply concerned about the overburdened public health services which are barely able to absorb the increasing demand due to the restricted capacities. According to recent records, the average waiting time of elective surgeries (the majority of whom are refugees) has reached to twelve months while refugees are becoming less able to afford the cost of secondary and/or tertiary healthcare provided outside UNRWA. UNRWA is mandated to provide primary health care services to the Palestine refugees; however, the agency aims to scale up its hospitalization services provided to Palestine refugees to alleviate the burden on the public health services and leave the first priority to emergency cases, while referring the vulnerable refugees who are not able to afford the private hospital based treatment. This is being provided through partially covering the hospitalization cost of medical treatment received at hospitals with which UNRWA has established agreements, or through reimbursing the patients for the medical treatment costs they incur. It is in line with the Health Cluster’s criteria to prioritize the provision of comprehensive care for trauma care across the trauma pathway, by relieving a part of influx of patients from public health services, as well as cluster’s objective of ensuring access of vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip to quality and affordable health services. In addition, UNRWA is expanding its response capacity within its primary health care services in order to respond to increasing demand by providing lifesaving medicines to vulnerable Palestine refugees and hiring emergency surged staff to cope with the increasing needs. Since the beginning of GMR demonstrations, UNRWA’s Health Programme has recorded an increase in injury-related cases, with patients often presenting severe and long-term needs for injury care and mental health support, on top of already existing and increasing needs in primary healthcare as well as psychosocial and mental health support due to the prolonged humanitarian situation in Gaza. Emergency surged staff will be able to fill the gap in the area of increased health care needs within UNRWA services, including proper medical screening of school children, and to provide necessary assistive devices that vulnerable families are not able to afford. UNRWA’s mental health and psychosocial support at health centres targets vulnerable Palestine refugees identified as in need of support, including women of reproductive age and children, the elderly and people with disabilities including GMR related cases. As a result of the accessibility to health centres for women, 80 per cent of mental health interventions support women including those who are experiencing gender-based violence. The intervention is a comprehensive, structured and child/person-centred model of care with the psychoeducation sessions being the first line of interventions which is recommended by the mhGAP model and align with the mental health needs for people in Gaza; in addition to group counselling intervention and an individual counselling approach. The health counsellors are critical in supporting patients who present serious mental health difficulties that require more focused and structured counselling, while they are playing a critical role in addressing GBVs. This intervention has been one of UNRWA’s priority emergency interventions, and it is continued to be in need due to ongoing situation in Gaza, as the Health Cluster also described as one of its response approaches.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The agency is taking the principles of “Do No Harm” seriously within its operations to ensure safety and dignity of all served refugees including women, girls and disabled people. In general, health services are provided in a manner that keeps all service receivers away from any potential to face any hazards or threats, physically and emotionally. The UNRWA health centres are equipped by proper equipment and provide spaces for women and girls to seek treatment and support comfortably without being harmed or exposed to any danger. For this specific intervention, the below arrangements are in place for ensuring safe, adequate and decent health services are provided to the targeted refugees : 1) Hospitalization services: The UNRWA’s Heath Programme manages to keep all confidential details on the hospitalized cases unrevealed to any non-concerned party. The Agency also, as the hospitalization services are provided with contacted hospitals, periodically checks the quality of the services inside the contracted hospitals and ensures all protection measures including protecting the sensitive information and provision of safe, comfortable areas are in place where services are provided.2) Mental Health and Psycho-social support: The provision of MHPSS inside UNRWA’s health centres is provided by qualified team responsible for handling cases with optimum protective manner. The counselors play vital role in guiding the cases towards “No more harm” and keep all details confidentially stored at UNRWA’s MHPSS central database. The sensitive cases e.g. GBV are paid more focus, care and follow up to ensure 100% safe support. Some complicated cases are referred to specialists for follow up in coordination with head of MHPSS and other external parties.3) Provision of physiotherapy, speech therapy and lifesaving drugs: The provision of such services is done within the overall UNRWA health system which guarantees safe access, confidentiality and respect, as well as provision of separated areas for males and females seeking the service. All details on the treated cases are kept on e-health system with limited access by concerned staff. The aim behind that is to keep every detail away from the potentiality of being revealed.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all Palestinian refugees in need for healthcare, without any discrimination inside its 22 health centres deployed across the Gaza Strip. Location of health centres takes into consideration of safety concerns, especially for women and girls, as well as catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. The health centres take into consideration specific vulnerability concerns and adopted specific SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize healthcare to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A specific follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow specific concerns and potential protection cases. Further, health centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Staff in health centres, whom is regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection mainstreaming and gender, are able to refer specific protection cases to MHPSS counselors for follow up. A crowd control system is in place and a specific calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance. Finally, a complaint response mechanism is accessible in health centres and mechanism to provide timely feedbacks are available through specific response committees.
