Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to evidence-based decision making for humanitarian operations in Somalia through IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM).
Under the coordination of the Information Management and Assessments Working Group (IMAWG) and in collaboration with all clusters, DTM will provide reliable and up to date information on the number of displaced populations, their location, profile, displacement trends, reasons for displacement, and needs disaggregated by age and sex to better assist and inform the governmental and humanitarian response. In particular, DTM’s data will feed into the 2021 roadmap of the IDP Working Group in order to provide regular updates on the IDP stocks across Somalia.
DTM tools have the potential to be tailored to take into account changes in the national context as well as information needs of responders working across the HDP nexus.

Project Activities

1. Regular collection of data on populations affected by displacement through implementation of the DTM’s Baseline Assessment. The Baseline Assessment aims to provide a country-wide mapping and quantitative estimates (stocks) of the presence of IDPs, returnees (from abroad and from internal displacement), absentees (people who left the location and have not returned since) and residents at the lowest possible administrative level (neighbourhood/wah in urban areas, villages/tulo in rural areas, and IDP sites). Information on population figures will be disaggregated by age/gender, and for IDPs the tools will also capture reasons and dates of displacement. Conducting the exercise with regularity will allow to observe dynamics in populations mobility. 2. Profiting from the information collected through the Baseline Assessment, DTM will conduct Multi-Sectorial Location Assessments (MSLA), which will inform the humanitarian community about gaps in service provision and will contribute to the accountability of the humanitarian partners. MSLA is highly customizable, allowing not only to capture sectoral data (water, hygine and sanitation, education, protection, nutrition, health) but also indicators that inform the HDP nexus, as well as COVID-19 specific data. 3. Data analysis and dissemination - Information will be shared regularly with national authorities and humanitarian partners through reports, dashboards, datasets, and maps. Customization of data analysis will be possible at the request of partners as priorities dictate. While all data collected aims to be publicly available, IOM will follow guidelines of the IMAWG/IDPWG with regards to data sharing, as well as its Internal Data Protection policies.

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1: Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) Mitigation Measure: While exclusion and protection risks remain a major concern in Somalia, this project specifically targets IDPs in order to identify their location, profile and needs, including needs related to insufficient protection related services. This ultimate aim of this project is to ensure that the relevant partners have access to the information required to guarantee that IDPs are fully accessing services that their needs. Finally, the DTM forms include a comments sections, allowing each key informant to express concerns and urgent needs, including in regards with protection and exclusion. Risk 2: Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation Measures: The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) primarily collects information through the use of key informants (KIs). As such, the choice of KIs has a significant impact on the level of inclusion and representation of each group into the exercise as well as the fair inclusion of their needs in products shared with partners. In order to prevent exclusion, IOM will work, when possible, with KIs representing all the groups within the community respecting age, gender and diversity. These KIs may include representatives of Women’s Committee, Youth Committee, Elder Committee, Education Committee, as well as gatekeepers, site managers, Women's Community representatives, Community leaders, and other knowledgeable individuals. IOM requires that assessments be conducted with at least three (3) KIs for a location, each of them representing different groups of the community. Finally, the DTM includes surveys conducted through household or individual interviews, for which enumerators are asked to interview a similar number of female and male head of households. Risk 3: Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. Mitigation Measures: Enumerators recruited for data collection will be from targeted communities. The enumerators will be required to go through a mandatory training on IOM’s Data Protection Policy. This will support them in understanding their obligations regarding data management, and will equip them with practical skills for collecting and storing this data in a secure manner. In addition, enumerators are required to request for the KIs’ consent prior to collecting any information, ensuring that KIs understand the exercise and willingly participate; while this does not directly reduce the risk, it provides KIs with an opportunity to refuse participation to the project in case they perceive it as a potential risk. Beyond data collection, IOM counts with strict Data Protection Policies about which all staff have been sensitize, and which breach is a ground for termination. Risk 4: Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries Mitigation Measures: Humanitarian data is politically sensitive. IOM acknowledges that a number of external stakeholders could have an interest in misrepresenting data in order to manipulate assistance in favour of a particular group. To mitigate this risk, all DTM staff are sensitized on humanitarian principles. While data collection is closely coordinated with external particies such as government counterparts or donors, IOM collects the data independently through locally hired enumerators. Concerns over the quality of data collected, including intentional misrepresentations aimed at manipulating humanitarian assistance, is a ground for immediate termination.
