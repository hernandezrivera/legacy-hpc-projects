Project Summary

The emergency response, which began in November 2019, is expected to unfold until the end of 2020, taking into account increased lean season needs exacerbated by the current crisis as well as the time needed for WFP to progressively include beneficiaries in new areas and scale up distributions. As such, during this response, a total of 700,000 beneficiaries will receive four months of general food assistance (in-king, cash or a combination of the two) in line with a gradual geographic expansion of WFP’s assistance.
Acknowledging the country-wide deterioration of the food security situation, WFP’s emergency assistance will be geared towards IPC Phase 4 households across the country, ensuring the overall humanitarian response meets the acute food security needs and stops any further decline in people’s food security status. WFP will adopt a particular focus on areas with a higher proportion of people in IPC phase 4 (roughly 20%) and comparatively lower partner capacity, while promoting complementarities with other actors, including UNICEF and FAO, to ensure the provision of a comprehensive package.
WFP will target 700,000 of the most food insecure people, in line with the HNO, corresponding to approximately 70% of the population classified in IPC Phase 4. A specific focus will be given to vulnerable households with pregnant and lactating women and girls, children under 5 years, elders and people living with disabilities. Complementary regular programming (school feeding, resilience – not within the scope of this Con Ops) will be maintained to support other populations in need, such as IPC Phase 3. These numbers are to be revisited along updated IPC projections.

1 - Needs

The October 2019 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) results reveal a generalized deterioration of the food security situation across the country, with 1.05 million people in the emergency phase 4 (of which 207,000 in urban settings) and 2.63 million in the crisis phase 3 (of which 649,000 in urban settings). According to IPC projections, from March to June 2020 the number of people in phase 4 and 3 with further increase, with 1.2 people in emergency (of which 228,000 in urban settings) and 2.9 people in crisis (of which 740,000 in urban settings). The October 2019 IPC is the first edition to capture food security data in urban settings, while in rural areas figures indicate a 47% increase in people living in phase 4 since last year. Though not disaggregated below department level, the January 2020 SMART survey carried out by the Ministry of Health and UNICEF showed increased GAM rates, particularly with respect to SAM in urban areas.WFP will target 700,000 of the most food insecure people, in line with the HNO, corresponding to approximately 70% of the population classified in IPC Phase 4. A specific focus will be given to vulnerable households with pregnant and lactating women and girls, children under 5 years, elders and people living with disabilities. Complementary regular programming (school feeding, resilience – using other sources of funding) will be maintained to support other populations in need, such as IPC Phase 3. These numbers are to be revisited along updated IPC projections.Acknowledging the country-wide deterioration of the food security situation, WFP’s emergency assistance will be geared towards IPC Phase 4 households across the country, ensuring the overall humanitarian response meets the acute food security needs and stops any further decline in people’s food security status. WFP will adopt a particular focus on areas with a higher proportion of people in IPC phase 4 (roughly 20%) and comparatively lower partner capacity, while promoting complementarities with other key actors, including UNICEF and FAO, to ensure that a comprehensive assistance package is provided. The scale-up of WFP’s assistance will use a stepwise approach, geographically beginning with existing areas of coverage before progressively reaching 9 out of 10 departments. The only department not to be included in WFP’s emergency response is the Sud, an area not prioritized by IPC data, where WFP maintains resilience programming and which will be the focus of other partners’ interventions. WFP’s greater focus will be given to the areas of Nord-Ouest, Artibonite, Ouest initially, gradually expanding to Grande Anse, Nippes, Nord and urban beneficiaries in Port-au-Prince, among others (resources permitting).

2 - Activities

The emergency response is expected to unfold until the end of 2020, taking into account increased lean season needs exacerbated by the current crisis as well as the time needed to progressively include beneficiaries in new areas and scale up distributions. As such, a total of 700,000 beneficiaries will receive four months of general food assistance (in-kind, cash or a combination of both). Ensuring access to basic food commodities via in-kind or cash-based transfer (CBT) assistance will free households’ diminished resources to purchase other available produce to complement their diets as well as to cover other basic needs such as health and education. Modalities and transfers will vary as follows: Urban (Port-au-Prince): 100,000 people to receive four months of CBT assistance.Bar code enabled vouchers and mobile money are expected to be the principal transfer mechanism in urban settings given security and protection concerns. Market functionality has been initially confirmed through existing and ongoing assessments, while a WFP retail assessment mission has supported the identification and selection of adequate retailers. Paper voucher distributions are set to be carried out in Cité Soleil. WFP will explore opportunities to transition towards e-voucher mechanisms when and where possible. WFP will also pilot new mobile money enabled solutions in Croix des Bouquets, exploring the possibility of testing phone to phone and phone to trader transfers in this area. A contract with the largest commercial telecommunications provider in country is already in place, enabling the design of similar e-money transfers in these and other peri-urban areas of the country with appropriate network coverage. Rural and Periurban: 600,000 people to receive four months of assistance, transitioning from in-kind to CBT after month 1 or 2, according to feasibility.This approach responds to immediate food needs and minimises potential inflationary pressures on local markets, before progressively moving to CBT approaches in line with beneficiary preferences. Feasibility assessments will confirm the precise timeline for a potential progressive transition to CBT to promote local economy (transition expected to take place after the second month of assistance or earlier, conditions permitting). In the limited areas where markets are not expected to be functional, such as the island of La Gonave (15,000 beneficiaries), four months of in-kind assistance are planned. WFP has extensive experience in the provision of direct cash distributions using cash-in-envelope, deemed to be the most appropriate and effective transfer mechanisms for rural and peri-urban settings. WFP partners with a dedicated Financial Service Provider, with a nationwide delivery footprint and sufficient liquidity. A tender process to contract other FSPs is currently ongoing, to provide alternative options and minimize the risk of default. Malnutrition prevention: 85,000 Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls and children 6-59 months will receive specialized nutritional products for a period of 4 months In selected areas of higher risk for increased GAM prevalence, together with general food assistance, WFP will deliver blanket supplementary feeding rations to pregnant and lactating women and girls and children 6-59 months. If and where further prioritization is required, WFP will assign priority to children 6-23 months as well as pregnant and lactating women and girls. Designed to establish a continuous link between the prevention and treatment of malnutrition, WFP will also strengthen its efforts to refer malnourished children to health centres, while reintegrating families of discharged children back into food security interventions. In addition, WFP will scale up its Social and Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) activities, using theatres and radio spots, as well as networks of health community workers to disseminate context-specific messages on nutrition and hygiene.

7.3 - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

Under the project, WFP plans to implement HDN activities accross the country through the Social and Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) component. Through a participatory approach, WFP will develop context, age and gender specific messages focusing on nutrition and hygiene practices. Through different communication channels, WFP will disseminate such messages in the targeted communities to promote dietary diverisfication and age-appropriate healthy eating habits. WFP and its cooperating partners will also work in close coordination with the Ministry of Health and Population (MSPP) to ensure that Community Health Workers (ASCP) are better equipped to screen acute malnourished children and provide nutrition sensitization sessions. Moreover, with other sources of funding, WFP will continue implementing resilience activities with a specific focus on North-West and Artibonite departments.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Throughout the project, WFP's internal "hotline" will be used to collect beneficiary feedback/complaints regarding activities in progress. The hotline is operated exclusively in-house, which means that beneficiaries get, in most cases immediately, a direct reply. If the question cannot be answered immediately, a follow-up can be done within less than a day. The hotline operator is also trained on SCOPE, WFP's beneficiary registration database, and can therefore confirm with a caller if s/he is part of a WFP programme and access the beneficiary information to assist him/her immediately. The hotline also serves to inform beneficiaries of their rights and entitlements, e.g., the ration they should expect and distribution dates. It also allows beneficiaries to report any issue encountered. The hotline operator also supports outbound campaigns to monitor programmes. The toll-free hotline number is available on beneficiary cards and is displayed on banners during distributions and other activities. Moreover, WFP plans to set up community-based complaint mechanisms to ensure that everybody has access to them.
