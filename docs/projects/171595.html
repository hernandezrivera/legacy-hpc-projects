Project Summary

About 586,000 people (in Awdal 136,000, Woqooyi Galbeed 223,000, Bari 161,000 and Nugaal 66,000) are facing acute food security Crisis (IPC 3 and 4) or worse and urgently need immediate humanitarian assistance and livelihood support through December 2020. About 822,000 people are stressed (IPC2);(in Awdal 195,000, Woqooyi Galbeed 337,000, Bari 193,000 and Nugaal 97,000) and require livelihood support to prevent them falling into crisis. Approximately 151,000 People including IDP- in Awdal 37,000, Woqooyi Galbeed, 67,000, Bari 33,000 and Nugaal 14,000- are experiencing extreme food insecurity and clarified to be in emergency (IPC4). They suffer greatest level of malnutrition with median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of 10-14.9%. The most affected are about 849 900 children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden) with 143,400 of them likely to be severely be severely malnourished through 2021. The most affected areas currently have or are projected to have a Critical GAM prevalence, of 15-29.9% or by a MUAC below 125 mm threshold and require immediate access basic food rations to improve nutrition include; East Golis Pastoral, Bosasso IDPs, Garowe IDPs, Galkacyo IDPs, Hiran rural, Beletweyne urban, Shabelle Riverine, Mogadishu IDPs, Bosasso IDPs, Baidoa IDPs, Juba Riverine.
The Action targets specific regions Northern Somalia (Somaliland and Puntland) because of high vulnerability of the population, inadequate Humanitarian Assistance, long lasting impact of Sugar Cyclone ,insufficient 2020 Gu rainfall ,impacts of desert locust on pastures and COVID-19 on household economic activities(FSNAU, September 2020).
To address identified needs above,VSF Germany proposes an integrated intervention to enable vulnerable households access immediate food and also build their livelihoods.The action targets 72,270 beneficiaries (in 12,045 households), in IPC 3&4( including IDPs) with; i)unconditional cash transfer for immediate food access-27,000 (4,500 hh); ii)cash for work for-6,000 (1,000hh) (iii) Protection of productive livelihood assets for those in IPC 2 - support for fodder production 3,000 beneficiaries (500 hhs), provision of supplementary livestock feed -9,000 beneficiaries (1500hh), animal health services , 27,000 beneficiaries(4500 hhs,) and iv)capacity building of livestock institutions (for sustainability)-270 (45 hhs), v) training on participatory disease surveillance(for enhanced early detection and control of disease)-35 CAHWs/Animal Health Auxiciliaries(AHA) ,(each rep a hh)-for livelihood protection and recovery.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfers have the potential to give crises affected communities greater flexibility and choice. Opposed to in-kind this intervention chooses to use cash transfer modality because it will allow selected beneficiaries to decide what they should spend the money on. This enables people to choose what they most need, and allows for this to vary from person to person. Cash transfer is also preferred mode by the fact that it allows beneficiaries to make their own choices thereby increasing their power and control over how they respond to the effects of the ongoing shocks. The dignity of the beneficiaries will also be upheld especially by the fact that the method of transfer will be through e.mobile which is safer and faster.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Through CFWs, significant cash will be injected into the local economy which will provide local business more purchasing power and capacity to manage high volume of goods. The injection of cash into the local economy will help revamp the local market even in during disruptive climatic changes like droughts.

Mitigation Measures

Mobilization and community awareness session will inform community on the selection criteria to identify the most vulnerable beneficiary to be registered and benefit on distributions. The process will be done during the day enabling beneficiary’s time to travel home before nightfall. E.mobile system will be the safest distribution mode that minimizes the beneficiaries risks of theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion.This will also ensure beneficiary privacy is uphold because they are the only one who are aware of cash transfer and they can withdraw anytime .Additionally, VSFG has PSEA policy that staff/implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. Additionally all staff directly implementing the distributions and are in direct contact with beneficiaries will be trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).The distribution teams will work on rotational basis to different locations to avoid one team regularly visits the same locations. The project will organize awareness raising sessions on GBV for beneficiaries and spreads information regarding the available referral services and focal points.The beneficiaries will be informed not to receive food assistance or be selected to the intervention in return of favor.The project will ensure that the beneficiary selection committees is representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members will be trained on protection and gender inclusion.Feedback mechanisms will include but not limited to PDM surveys, feedback box at distribution site, and toll-free phone lines. The project will ensure that the adopted feedback mechanisms are accessible, confidential and well understood for feedback. In order to ensure community are aware, feedback mechanism process will be shared during implementation forums. The beneficiary’s lists and other personal data will be kept confidential and in safe places where password only known to project implementer s are used and will strictly be available for only intended use. The project will apply organizations operational data protection measures. The project will ensure that the assistance and services benefit the most vulnerable by assessing, identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups.

Please provide further explanation

Livestock supplementary feed distribution: This will be a short time intervention to support the livestock owners during the dry season. Involvement of the Ministry of Livestock in each target location and other government agencies is a step of ensuring continuous verification of the need for the support of livestock dependent pastoralists. Livestock owners will be trained on proper feeding of productive animals during times of forage scarcity to sustain animal production. The livestock owners will sensitized and trained on fodder production, harvesting and storage (grass, herbs and crop residues) for supplementary feeding of their animals during dry seasons when feeds are scarce. Regeneration of pasture due to relative good rains received in any rainy period-Gu and /or Dyer could provide an opportunity for fodder growing, harvesting and storage. Animal Health: The activity will be undertaken with full involvement of Government veterinarians and private animal health service providers (veterinarians, Animal Health Auxiliaries and Community Animal Health Workers). After treating the livestock in the selected/ target villages, the project will have linked livestock owners to animal health service providers in their respective areas for continuous service provision as well as regular support and supervision from Ministry of Livestock. Unconditional Cash Transfer (UTC): The beneficiaries (who are mainly IDPs and destitute pastoralists) will have opportunity for recovery from devastating food insecurity and be able to rebuild their livelihood. From previous similar interventions, the beneficiaries of UCT support had showed ability to use part of their cash to rebuild their usual livelihoods. Some of them used part of their cash to run small businesses e.g. tea shops in their places of residence .By the end of the project; they were able to their foot to build their livelihoods. Those who will participate in cash for work will have the opportunity to quickly rebuild their livelihood, including IDPs. VSF G will refer those IDPs and pastoralist dropouts from the programme to other recovery programs undertaken by other humanitarian and development actors.
