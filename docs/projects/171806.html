Project Summary

Conflict, drought and other natural disasters persist in Somalia leading to continuous displacement and exposing people especially women and children to multiple risks. Actions of armed actors and perpetrators of violence continue to threaten the lives of many, abuses against civilians, including wide spread gender-based violence (GBV), recruitment of children, physical attacks, and forced displacement remain. Vulnerabilities have been accelerated by displacement as IDP communities have less access to basic services, justice, shelter as well as socio-economic opportunities among others. Women, children and marginalized communities are particularly at higher risk. Capacities to cope with displacement related shocks are low as most IDPs lack livelihoods, proper housing and protection among other factors. Their social networks have been disrupted and thus not ideally reliable to offer protection. Children make almost 45% of the population in Somalia and thus the need to strengthen protection environment at all levels.
The objective of the project is to strengthen the protection of most vulnerable girls and boys, affected by conflict and climatic shocks by reducing risks and vulnerabilities and increasing community capacities. DRC will employ participatory and right based approaches, age, gender and diversity (AGD) approach and community-based child protection approaches which will involve, inclusion of children from refugee’s/asylum seeker communities, migrants, IDPs and most vulnerable host communities in addition to local authorities in the project cycle. The project aims to build and strengthen local capacities within the community and support them to establish a protection framework to prevent and respond to violations. It will focus on improving capacity for immediate response to violations and remedial action as well as strengthening the protective environment in Somalia.
On immediate response, the action will aim to address acute protection needs of girls and boys stemming from violence and child rights violations, as well as coercion, and abuse. DRC will establish child friendly spaces through which psychological first aid support will be provided. On environment building, DRC will reinforce formal and community institutions for better legal and community-based protection of vulnerable boys and girls from violence, coercion and abuse. Key to achieve this objective, activities will include awareness-raising and outreach among communities on protection issues, capacity building and training for duty bearers, service providers and humanitarian workers on Child protection. On advocacy, DRC will strengthen coordination of child protection services and enhance advocacy on protection with relevant stakeholders.
The project will take deliberate measures to ensure gender parity of the beneficiaries and will strive to reach equal number of boys and girls while considering the gender specific needs of the beneficiaries. Key activities will include provision of comprehensive and confidential case management services including MHPSS, establish and support community-based child protection structures including children with special needs, manage child friendly spaces, strengthen referral pathway to provide timely and adequate services to children in need and those at risk, tracing and reunification of UASC, awareness messages on key child protection messages among others.
The proposed action will reach 59,100 people (13,200 girls, 13,300 boys,16,300 Women and 16,300 Men).

Child protection case management. Girls and boys with child protection risks will be identified and referred through case management. Those with special needs will receive specialized services.

Mitigation Measures

1. Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of marginalized community’s and minority groups: Minority groups in Somalia are rarely targeted in humanitarian project cycle and are mainly marginalized and invisible. Women and children and persons with special needs among minority groups are even more sidelined and excluded and do not benefit from interventions that accords them some degree of protection. The environment in Somalia as a result of ongoing armed conflict and cyclic often deem minority persons and groups more vulnerable. In addition, at least 15% (10% children)of the population are people with disabilities who face heightened risks and exclusion from services. To mitigate on this risks, DRC will intentionally target and empower, protect and promote the rights of minority communities, with a focus on women and children who experience multiple-discrimination on account of their gender and minority status. DRC will follow a selection criterion for beneficiaries that include members of minorities as vulnerable groups that require special attention. Selection of beneficiaries will be through participatory methods in collaboration with trained community representatives. Through protection monitoring, DRC will identify trends of exclusion and marginalization. Data collected will be used for targeting and prioritization, ensuring that no one is left out. DRC will consult people with disabilities and/or their representatives during the design and Identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities. Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of women, girls and boys Women, girls and boys remain vulnerable to protection risks and harmful cultural practices, GBV, child recruitment and hazardous child labor. Their ability to access justice and remedies and to enjoy rights is often limited and further weakened in case of displacement. Furthermore, stigma related to abuses often deters survivors from accessing assistance or reporting incidents. To address this, DRC will include specific needs of women, girls and boys when designing and implementing the project. DRC will seek ways to increase the women and girl friendly spaces providing opportunities for recognizing their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes. 2. Mitigation the risk of external stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection There are significant number of displaced communities and people in locations that are not easily accessible to humanitarian assistance, this could be due to insecurity and people living in far to reach areas. In addition, there are instances where external stakeholders demand targeting of particular groups or prevent others from receiving assistance. To mitigate on these challenges, DRC will use beneficiary selection criteria that considers vulnerability as an entry point to assistance. DRC will engage in consultation processes adequately gathering relevant information including power dynamics from the communities and other stakeholders and taking these views into account when selecting beneficiaries and implementing the action. DRC will work with existing or revitalized community committees among the target groups that will be part of the beneficiary selection.3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: DRC will involve the target groups from designing the projects, selection of beneficiaries to service provision and they will be capacitated on the project and its deliverables. DRC has a comprehensive complains and feedback mechanism which will guide the project to ensure all community members can access information on the project and share their feedbacks and complains which will be effectively addressed. DRC has different avenues of collecting such information including our complains hotline 332 that is manned by a dedicated staff who in turn receives all complains and feedback and directs them to respective projects manager

Please provide further explanation

Recognizing that the project is set in a protracted and complex emergency context, the project will partially contribute to durable solutions. The project will aim at addressing all 3 dimensions of safety, key to the achievement of durable solutions – physical, material and legal. Material safety will be addressed through integration with DRC response in other sectors in which protection will be mainstreamed. The program is designed taking into account the needs and perspectives of the communities that ensuring that the intervention will contribute to physical, material and legal safety of the targeted communities. DRC will conduct conflict analysis before and during project implementation and identify among other things, root cause of conflicts and other factors that may lead to conflicts. The information from conflict analyses studies will inform program design and re-design. Social cohesion and peaceful coexistence will be supported analyzing how targeting can minimize intercommunity conflicts. The action is directly linkage to linked to government processes and will aim at building long term protective environment through building capacity of government officials on human rights which will at the end improve governance. In addition, community based structures will be strengthened through training and awareness creating leading to a more empowered community that can prevent and respond to violations on their own.
