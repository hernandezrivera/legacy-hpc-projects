Project Summary

The project’s aims to provide basic and improve livelihoods of 2,367,500 displaced people, returnees and overburdened host community people at risk within the highly neglected sub-districts in the aforementioned areas Located in:
Atareb, Daret Azza / Aleppo Governorate
Homs / Homs Governorate
Deir Ezzor / Deir Ezzor Governorate
Dar'a, / Dar’a Governorate
Sabkha, Madaan / Ar-raqqa Governorate
Hama / Hama Governoarate
Tartous / Tartous Governorate
Lattakia / Lattakia Governorate
As-Sweida / As-Sweida Governorate
The consequences of the conflict and breakout of covid-19 have been particularly severe and have converted many parts of the different governorate into hard-to-reach areas, where people suffer from lack of basic needs, due to the enormous destruction including education and medical facilities and socio-economic infrastructure being cut off from access to markets, loss of livelihood and income, the rise in the unemployment rate, and the sharp depreciation of the currency. With this Early Recovery and livelihood education support, the living conditions and socio-economic condition, in addition to build resilience of 2,367,500 people in areas with high rates of displacement and Scaling-up interventions to contain impact of COVID-19 crisis and recent economic deterioration on livelihoods, through Enhancing resilience-building through Vocational Training and toolkits, rehabilitation of education facilities to ensure that children have access to education, rehabilitation of health facilities to improve the health facilities access for all people. In addition to rehabilitation of local economic infrastructure such as (markets, storage, warehousing and processing canal rehabilitation), repair and rehabilitation of social infrastructure and rehabilitation roads and related drainage systems. Moreover, remove debris and waste.
It worth to mention that the procurement of the toolkit will be handled by RSRP and there will No cash OR Voucher components to the beneficiaries.
Furthermore, It should be noted that the project is in line with HNO 2021 and the Sector Strategic Objective for the ERL (SO1 and SO3) and the priorities:
Strategic Objective 1: Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs.
Strategic Objective 3: Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities

4 - Needs

A decade of the humanitarian crisis resulted in 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance. Nearly 11.6 million people lose their access to local infrastructure including education and health facilities, social facilities. 74% of available basic socio-economic infrastructures are damaged. The vulnerability of the population has been exacerbated by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic compared to last year. Moreover, the severity of humanitarian needs has become more extensive, overall poverty is estimated close to 90%. In addition, 78% drop in the value of SYP in just one year (to SYP 3,030/US$1 on the informal market (WFP, December 2020)). unemployment is estimated at 50%of the working-age population, and over 60% of youth, where 200,000-300,000 jobs lost since March 2020 (UN, COVID-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment, August 2020) near 75% of persons with disabilities are without access to economic, social, and medical support. (HNO 2021)According to HNO 2021 and the conducted field assessment indicate, targeting areas have reported 7,082,144 (PiN) of Early Recovery and Livelihood support. Worth mentioning that Livelihood support consists of 50% of total needs. Therefore, RSRP addresses decreasing dramatic loss of livelihoods and improve socio-economic resilience for affected households, Priority will be given to Scale-up mainstreaming of PLWD and female-headed HHs support to enhanced access to livelihoods opportunities and areas with high severity of need, where access to basic services is most limited, with the highest rate of people in need and population density, highly destroyed/ impoverished, as well as where spontaneous returns/high influx of IDPs occur.

5 - Implementation of activities

RSRP's activities are in line with the ERL sector’s response strategy adhering to the needs-based approach, identification of PIN within the framework of 2021HNO to sub-district level, and adhering to HRP2021 strategic objectives (SO1andSO3). RSRP proposes the activities mentioned in the project cost section. The main pillars followed to achieve the proposed responses in coordination with the MoLA, STD, ERL sector and other relevant stakeholders:-Obtaining approval in coordination with STD-The hiring process.-Announcing about registration for vocational training through local community and social media.-Identify 13500 beneficiaries in the targeted areas according to vulnerability selection criteria considering female HHs, persons with disabilities without income, HHs without any income resources and youth in need. The registration process will be implemented through the registration form on KOBO.-Procurement process.-Identify 90 education facilities, 9 health facilities, 18 local economic infrastructures, and 18 social infrastructures that needed rehabilitation through need assessment in the targeted areas according to damage ratio. -Rehabilitation of 9 education facilities, 9 health facilities, 18 local economic infrastructures, and 18 social infrastructures.-Rehabilitation of 45k, of identified damaged roads and remove 180.000 m3 of waste and debris.-To ensure quality, regular field visits will be conducted and field staff reports and collecting and collating the progress on ERL initiatives through the submission of 4Ws regularly.-The accountability to the affected population will be guaranteed through the establishment of a complaint mechanism and the regular evaluation of feedback.
