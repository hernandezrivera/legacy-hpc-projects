Project Summary

The project aims to provide life-saving WASH assistance to 140,000 IDPs, returnees, non-displaced, migrants, and refugees affected by the humanitarian crisis and COVID-19. The project will preposition essential WASH supplies that is sufficient to respond to around 30,000 people in case of emerging humanitarian WASH needs across the country in a timely manner. The project activities includes:
a) provision of safe drinking water through water trucking, minor rehabilitation of water networks, installation of water storage tanks, provision of household water treatment material.
b) provision of adequate sanitation services through installation of emergency latrines, emptying septic tanks, rehabilitation of existing toilets, disinfection/fumigation of potential areas of contamination, decommissioning of emergency latrines.
c) Hygiene awareness, prepositioning, and distribution of hygiene items including COVID-19 related supplies as well.
UNICEF WASH Programme will also include support to already established Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM), which is designed to deliver immediate, life-saving supplies to affected families, most notably food (immediate Response Rations), drinking water and hygiene kits, dignity kits with a focus on women and girls of reproductive age, NFIs, mother/baby kits, basic lifesaving maternal health care kits and vaccines. These services will be provided to persons on the move or displaced due to the armed conflict, hard-to-reach areas, caught at checkpoints or stranded between front-lines within 72 hours of receiving DTM reports, or DTM verification of the needs highlighted by government or non-government partners in letters of assistance submitted to the consortium. The overall goal of the Libya Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism is to save lives and mitigate negative impact of acute crisis on lives and dignity of affected groups with emphasis on vulnerable groups especially women and children. It is expected that RRM will be able to reach affected population with hygiene kits along with other necessary critical supplies at the early onset of a crisis. The intervention will be a geographical focus based on a vulnerability in given locations of displacement and will precede or complement the sectoral response, but delivery will remain affected by insecurity, accessibility, logistics…etc.

1 - Needs

Recent multi-sectorial assessments conducted by REACH and other agencies highlighted the dire humanitarian needs of the displaced population due to the protected crisis in some parts of the country such as Murzouq, Sebha, and the southern neighborhoods of Tripoli. The main WASH needs of conflict affected population include life saving WASH assistances in collective centers and the areas of return and essential hygiene items for the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees families. Moreover, COVID-19 is also having a negative impact on the lives of most vulnerable population in Libya. Likewise needs for enhanced WASH services is of extreme importance to combat COVID-19 and other communicable diseases. It has been observed and analyzed that the needs for prevention and control of infections is not achieveable without critical WASH services.

2 - Activities

Provision of safe drinking water through the below activities:- Water trucking where there is no access to a safe water source,- Minor rehabilitation of water systems. - Installation of storage water tanks.Provision of sanitation services through the below activities:- Installation of emergency latrines that are separated by sex.- Emptying septic tanks/ decommissioning of emergency latrines.- Disinfection/Fumigation Provision hygiene through the below activities:- Hygiene awareness-raising and distribution of basic hygiene items (including Covid-19 and women specific materials) through male and female hygiene promoters/volunteers.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Coordinate with local authorities in terms of humanitarian response efforts and to facilitate hosting for the IDPs and migrants, like using schools and dormitories as a shelter for the most vulnerable groups. Designing a hygiene kit for families to meet the needs of all the members males, females, children, and elders. Working on rehabilitating the WASH facilities in the collective centers and detention centers, which will be separated toilets that serve all the groups.UNICEF and partners abide by PSEA regulations to ensure beneficiaries are protected from sexual exploitation or abuse. Training to partners on PSEA and awareness campaigns among beneficiaries on ways to report sexual exploitation and abuse. 4.2 - UNICEF intends to carryout programmatic activities inline with all humanitarian principles particularly "do no harm". During the implementation phase regular meetings are held with local authorities to increase the understanding of progress and discuss challenges. UNICEF tries to integrate the COVID-19 risks in its programming from the assessment phase throughout the program cycle. In addition, UNICEF internally works closely with the Health unit within UNICEF to understand the impact of rehabilitation of WASH facilities in DCs, Schools, PHC. Engineers are also trained on COVID-19 preparedness aspects and follow WHO guidelines to ensure this risk is being mitigated. 4.7 - Various programs implemented by UNICEF will continue to advocate for the alternatives to detention and improving living conditions in urban areas where migrants live. UNICEF will regularly advocate for migrants in line with the principle framework for migrants. 4.13 - While directly implementing through female and male staff various activities to address the most vulnerable groups' needs, UNICEF will mainstream protection as an integral part of its overall intervention and identify the needs of women and children. UNICEF will also pay special attention to ensure safe and dignified access of all sex and age groups and will consistently consider mitigation of protection risks, including of GBV. 4.17 - UNICEF will look at gender aspects and verify the needs of persons with disabilities when it comes to the rehabilitation of schools to ensure an inclusive approach is being used. 4.20 - UNICEF will liaise with local authorities and other coordinating humanitarian actors to understand which project areas are prone to mines. UNICEF will include advocacy messages on the risk of mines when working on IDPs willing to return. 4.21 - For WASH infrastructure UNICEF will use a transparent tendering procedure based on a SOP with fixed period of advertisements and applications processing, to be able to award contracts based on quality standards as localized as possible. This will avoid disruption of local markets.
