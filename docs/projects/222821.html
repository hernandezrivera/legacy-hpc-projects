Project Summary

The International Organization for Migration (IOM) is dedicated to addressing the needs of both Rohingya refugees and host communities in Bangladesh. In 2025, IOM will implement a series of activities aimed at enhancing the skills and livelihoods of these communities, fostering self-reliance, and promoting social cohesion. These activities will be implemented in close collaboration with other UN and development agencies to avoid duplications and improve quality.
IOM will provide Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) programs to 1,500 Rohingya individuals. These programs are designed to equip participants with essential literacy and numeracy skills, enabling them to better navigate their daily lives and access further educational and vocational opportunities. This is particularly important as one of the major challenges for vocational training is the literacy and numeracy levels of Rohingya communities.
In addition to AAL, IOM will offer vocational training to 1,000 Rohingya individuals. This training, spanning 360 hours, will be based on the Livelihoods Skills Development Strategy (LSDS) selected trades and curriculum in line with the Government of Bangladesh- United Nations Framework on Skills
Development for Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities. The goal is to build Rohingya Refugees' skills and capacities in sectors commensurate with opportunities available in Rakhine State.
Recognizing the importance of diverse skill sets, IOM will also provide non-formal training to 3,000 Rohingya refugees. This 120-hour training will cover various crafts such as dressmaking, shoemaking, and other artisanal skills. These activities not only enhance the participants' abilities to generate income but also preserve and promote their cultural heritage. To further support the newly skilled individuals, IOM will facilitate in-kind support (such as the provision of raw material for handicrafts) or refer 1,000 Rohingya volunteers to different sectors. This initiative aims to integrate skilled volunteers into various community projects, thereby enhancing their practical experience and contributing to the overall development of the camps.
IOM's efforts will also extend to the host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf. IOM will offer non-formal training to 800 individuals from these communities. This training will focus on skills that are in demand locally, fostering economic growth and reducing dependency on external aid. In addition, market linkages for the host community will be strengthened through the establishment of shops/ kiosks, product fairs, etc. IOM will also provide in-kind support (such as raw material for bamboo craft, sewing machines) to 800 beneficiaries from the host communities who have already received non-formal training. This support will help them to further develop their businesses or improve their employment prospects, ensuring the sustainability of the initial training investment.
The selection criteria for all activities will be based on eligibility criteria such as female-headed households, income below the poverty line, and widows. IOM will also focus on disability inclusion throughout the selection process to ensure inclusive support for livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities. Women empowerment, leadership, and decision-making will be promoted by incorporating income opportunities, entrepreneurship opportunities, business development training, and financial literacy for women in the host community. Additionally, self-reliance opportunities will be provided for women in the camps. Through these comprehensive activities, IOM aims to build the capacities of both the Rohingya and host communities, promoting resilience, self-sufficiency, and harmonious coexistence. The project underscores IOM's commitment to inclusive development and the well-being of all affected populations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In Bangladesh, socioeconomic conditions have been gradually deteriorating due to a combination of political developments, inflation, and price hikes. These factors have significantly impacted the livelihoods, food security, income, and employment opportunities of the host communities. In July 2024, Bangladesh experienced the highest inflation rate in the last five years, reaching 11.66% as per Trading Economics (tradingeconomics.com). This economic strain has not only affected Bangladeshi host communities but has also had a profound impact on Rohingya refugees. Funding priorities for other global emergencies have diverted resources away from Bangladesh, reflecting negatively on food security and cash-for-work opportunities.The 2024 Skills Supply Demand Analysis report from the International Labour Organization (ILO) emphasized the significant skills gaps and systemic constraints in the Chattogram-Cox’s Bazar region, affecting both the Bangladeshi host community and Rohingya refugees. The report identified several challenges in the skills sector, including a lack of job sector-specific data, inadequate monitoring and evaluation mechanisms, poor coordination between Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) providers and industry, and limited access to work-based learning opportunities. These challenges have resulted in 49 hard-to-fill occupations across five high-growth sectors: construction, tourism and hospitality, transport, manufacturing, and agri-food processing. For instance, the construction sector faces a shortage of skilled workers, with 65% of land used for cereal crop production and only 1% for homestead gardening.The Inter Sector Needs Assessment 2024 report reveals that 73% of the households have at least one income, of which 79% HHs have single sources of income and 55% earn only 5,000 BDT/month. Women also need support for income opportunities as only 2% were found to have contributed to household income. The dependency on humanitarian aid (94%) underscores the critical need for targeted livelihoods and skills development support to address the vulnerabilities, skills gaps, and socio-economic challenges faced by both the Bangladeshi host community and Rohingya refugees. Enhancing employability through improved training, better coordination between TVET providers and industry, and increased access to livelihood opportunities is essential for building resilience and promoting sustainable development in the region. It is noted that only 15% reported having access to training, and among them 36% were able to utilize the training.The FAO Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis (RIMA) 2024 report highlights the extreme vulnerabilities faced by both communities, exacerbated by natural disasters, economic shocks, and funding shortfalls. In 2023, 86% of Rohingya households and 57% of Bangladeshi households reported facing at least one form of shock. The report underscores the importance of improving adaptive capacity and asset ownership to enhance resilience. Key drivers for resilience include household income, crop diversity, technology adoption, and capacity-building/training. The average monthly income for Rohingya households was BDT 3,288, significantly lower than the BDT 24,331 for Bangladeshi households.According to the Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA) 2024 Report, 97% of Rohingya households and 69% of Bangladeshi households were found to have moderate to high vulnerability in 2023. Economic vulnerability among Rohingya households increased by 3% compared to 2022, with 24% unable to afford the minimum food basket. The report also highlights the significant reliance on humanitarian assistance, with 83% of Rohingya households falling below the food Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) without aid. The Bangladeshi community also faces high economic vulnerability, with 40% of households below the MEB in 2023.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM will ensure the mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of activities. The guiding principle for these activities is "Do no harm," especially for vulnerable people. The privacy of beneficiaries and the confidentiality of shared content will be maintained during the design and implementation of the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness-raising sessions to protect them from any incidents giving rise to protection concerns. In case any protection concerns arise for the beneficiaries, IOM will follow referral pathways.IOM will deliver services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, ensuring cultural appropriateness and avoiding any unintended negative consequences. Assistance and services will be provided based on needs, without discrimination based on age, sex, gender, nationality, race, or ethnic allegiance. Services and assistance will be offered on an adequate scale, within safe and easy reach of beneficiaries, at locations known and accessible to all groups, including medical cases, Persons with Disability, and discriminated groups.To ensure that activities are participatory, livelihood counselling will be conducted within refugee and host communities to identify specific needs and preferences; the project will also involve community members during the monitoring of project activities and results to foster transparency and accountability. Through these participatory methods, the project will ensure that community voices are actively shaping LSDS activities, making programs more relevant and impactful.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Age and disability are cross-cutting components mainstreamed throughout all of IOM’s work with its beneficiaries. As a technical partner in the Age and Disability Technical Working Group, IOM promotes age and disability inclusion. IOM's beneficiaries under this project are primarily individuals aged 18 years and above, excluding child beneficiaries. The organization plans to include youth and adults as direct beneficiaries of self-reliance and livelihoods activities. However, these activities will indirectly benefit all age groups within the targeted households. Interventions will be designed to safeguard the children of female-headed households. Training centers will be equipped with breastfeeding corners and child-friendly spaces, allowing mothers to attend training courses while their children are safely accommodated. To mainstream age and disability into program, IOM will collect age disability disaggregated data, and use the information to plan the activities. Accessibility of all groups and disabilties will be ensured by tailoring the program activities. The monitoring and reporting tools will also include age and disability data. The staff and beneficiaries both will be sensitized on the specific needs of all groups and types.All planned activities for the year will consider individuals from various races, ethnicities, persons with special needs or disabilities, and the elderly population. The aim is to benefit all individuals in need. IOM will place special emphasis on minority groups within the community to help integrate them into society. Activities will be tailored to their specific needs and skills based on the outcomes of needs assessments. At least 3% of the beneficiary list will include persons with disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability is a vital and integral component of this proposed project. During the needs assessment, the affected populations, including men, women, gender-diverse groups, and persons with disabilities in all targeted locations, will be consulted on their needs and priorities. They will also have the opportunity to contribute to the design and planning of trade selection, opportunities, and market analysis, with support from IOM, and access to information-sharing and data-collection sessions. The affected population will lead in making decisions on their roles, local resource mobilization, and livelihoods/self-reliance skills, considering safety, security, and gender dynamics. Specific project activities will be planned based on their needs, priorities, and context. No one will be left out of skills development opportunities. IOM’s Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is already established at project sites to receive regular feedback from beneficiaries regarding the activities. The privacy of beneficiaries and the confidentiality of content will be ensured, and responses to their feedback will be delivered regularly with diligence.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM maintains a Zero Tolerance Policy toward sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), binding all staff, implementing partners, contractors, vendors, and service providers to high standards of behavior at all times. SEA against affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result in disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution.A dedicated PSEA Officer supports ongoing efforts to combat SEA, ensuring adherence to PSEA principles. IOM continues to roll out PSEA training and Training of Trainers sessions for PSEA Champions, aiming to equip all IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers with essential knowledge of PSEA guidelines and mandatory reporting systems.During the project’s initial implementation phase, SEA-related risks are screened, and mitigation measures are identified to enhance safe programming for affected populations. IEC materials are developed to raise awareness among communities about their rights to free assistance, reporting mechanisms, and available referral services. The PSEA Officer, in collaboration with the program team, conducts regular consultations and focus group discussions with beneficiaries to improve awareness and address concerns about SEA.IOM has an online reporting platform, WEAREALLIN.IOM.INT, for reporting SEA incidents. Recognizing potential access barriers, IOM has also established alternative complaint and feedback mechanisms to receive SEA-related concerns from beneficiaries and community members. IOM’s specialized investigation team ensures follow-up on SEA complaints, with access to protection and support services for survivors provided in coordination with its MPHSS-Protection team, the PSEA network, and existing GBV and Child Protection referral pathways.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Host communities will be made aware of and encouraged to follow seasonal cropping practices to avoid and minimize damage to crops from flooding, erosion, land use practices, salt water incursion and high tides. In addition, IOM will support communities to construct temporary drainage canals in their agricultural fields to ensure the effective channeling of rainwater so that damage to standing crops can be further avoided/minimized. IOM will support the community to implement small-scale structural flood mitigation works such as the construction of drainage system, reforestation, etc. under its cash for work initiatives, to mitigate the impact of floods and cyclones in settlement and agricultural areas.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

IOM maintains a Rohingya Response Business Continuity Plan (BCP), and contingency stocks to support overall EPR efforts for host communities and refugees via relevant Sectors and ISCG. The project focuses on the market linkage for sustainability and business continuity. The host community beneficiaries will be linked with local and national markets whereas, the Rohingya beneficiaries will be connected with camp markets. Besides, trade fairs and product promotions will help to connect with consumers. Beneficiaries received vocational training will be given apprenticeship opportunities to engage in employment for sustainable income source.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Engaging with youth is a priority for IOM. The Organization is committed to developing activities targeting youth and young people on the move, addressing their needs and vulnerabilities and promoting meaningful youth engagement. IOM applies IASC Guidelines on working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises. Under the project IOM will engage beneficiaries over 18 years old who also represent the youth group. These beneficiaries will receive non formal and vocational skills training to contribute to their family and livelihoods opportunity.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project focuses on building the self-reliance and resilience of the Rohingya refugee by providing vocational and skills training in addition to in-kind support, strengthening their ability to earn income and contribute to their family. Moreover, project activities aim to build Rohingya Refugees' skills and capacities in sectors commensurate with opportunities available in Rakhine State, so that they will be able to use their skills following their return to Myanmar.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Aligned with the priorities of the District Development and Growth Priorities (DDGP) of Cox's Bazar, the project activities aim to support income generation, building market linkages and economic growth. Through skills-building and job-training programs, the DDGP emphasises the importance of providing local communities with long-term economic possibilities. The goal of IOM's project is to make people more employable and give people in the community the skills they need to take part in a wide range of business activities. IOM's project plan includes steps to improve market linkage by building local infrastructure to facilitate market access by local companies. This will give local communities access to bigger markets, which will help them generate income. IOM's project also focuses on supporting business and income generation over the longer term, which aligns with the DDGP's goal of making people more employable and strengthening the economy.
