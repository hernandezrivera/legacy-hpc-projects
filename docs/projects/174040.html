Project Summary

More than 1.5 million individuals are exposed to the risk of Explosive Remnants of War, landmines of an improvised nature, and other Improvised Explosive Devices in Borno, northern parts of Adamawa, and eastern parts of Yobe. After over a decade of the ongoing conflict in North-East Nigeria, intense fighting between the Nigerian army and Non-State Armed Groups resulted in the widespread contamination of the area by explosive hazards. The presence of explosive hazards is affecting the population and humanitarian actors, restricting freedom of movement, safe access to villages, and rebuilding of key infrastructures, and arable land. Given the widespread explosive ordnance (EO) contamination and prevalent use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs), to include landmines of an improvised nature, by non-state armed groups, the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Assessment identifies 1.5 million people in need of mine action in north-east Nigeria. The majority of people in need are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis. The presence of explosive hazards was a barrier to access land for agricultural activities. The lack of access is exacerbating food insecurity and will continue to do so until mine action activities can be undertaken. Many IDPs in Borno State are intended to be resettled to their places of origin by the government of Borno. The Borno State Return Strategy emphasizes “IDPs and returning refugees must be protected from violence, (…) mines, and unexploded ordinances and any other threats to their rights and security”. Minimal conditions include inter alia, that they must be protected from explosive hazards. This year alone, in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, 224 explosive incidents were registered, 195 IED incidents, and 29 Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) incidents, resulting in 145 civilian casualties. Nigeria has been reported in the landmine monitor (data 2019) as the country with the fifth most Explosive Ordnance casualties. UNMAS Nigeria supports national capacities, including the security sector and civil society, and coordinates the mine action response in northeast Nigeria as it is crucial that the immediate threat posed by explosive ordnance to civilians and humanitarian and development actors is mitigated. To ensuresustainability, UNMAS is also committed that relevant national and local entities should be able to manage the explosive threat, based on an accurate understanding of the scale and scope of explosive ordnance contamination. In response to these challenges, UNMAS developed a project that aims at providing prevention of incidents, through capacity building of national and state authorities as well as local NGO/ Civil Society Organizations via Training of Trainers (ToT) for Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) and Non-Technical Survey (NTS), enhancement of existing national capacity by providing targeted training for security forces on Improvised Explosive Device Disposal (IEDD), and response to incidents through Advanced Emergency First Responder Course (EFR) and Individual First Aid Kit (IFAK) training and victim assistance (VA) referral mechanism.

1 - Needs

IEDs, mainly as improvised landmines, have been the weapon of choice by Boko Haram factions, and the extensive and indiscriminate use of IEDs affects the population and is a threat to the humanitarian community as they remain active over time if not cleared. The capacity of the Nigerian federal, state, and local authorities as well as civilian organizations to conduct mine action activities to protect the affected civilian population is currently limited. These limitations include conducting non-technical and technical surveys, mapping and analysis of contamination to strategically plan effective and efficient mine action, delivery of life-saving EORE messages, to conduct IEDD, support of victims and survivors of explosive incidents, including Emergency First Responder medical attention. In this regard, it has been established that it is crucial to support the capacity development of the Nigerian national and state authorities and local NGOs and civilian organizations to mitigate threats of landmines and explosives, as well as to mitigate immediate threats for refugees, IDPs, returnees, and communities as well as humanitarian actors. The COVID-19 pandemic has raised the concerns of congested camps, which make the refugees, IDPs, and returnees increasingly susceptible to outbreaks. The Decongestion Strategy identifies that 80,000 families are with limited personal space at camps and camp-like settings. The situation is exacerbated with refugees from the neighboring countries of Chad, Cameroon, and Niger arriving in Nigeria further congesting camps. As there is a need for camp expansion and decongestion, surveys of the areas for mines and explosive contamination, as well as EORE to ensure the safety of civilians, are critical. Furthermore, in light of the Borno Resettlement plan to return all IDPs to their area of origin by the end of the year 2021, it is crucial that land for resettlement and socio-economic activities as farming, shepherding, firewood, and water collection are surveyed and ensured to be free from explosive hazards prior to return. The need for EORE and NTS therefore has increased, with 1.16 million people in need in Borno, more than 200,000 in Adamawa and 176,000 in Yobe. Lands, where contamination is suspected according to reliable information, should be non-technically surveyed to confirm the presence or the absence of explosive ordnance. Data collected since 2016 indicate that more than 56 percent of all incidents in the BAY states happened in 7 Local Government Areas (LGAs). Three LGAs, namely Gwoza, Konduga, and Bama, account for more than 35 percent of the whole of known incidents. In addition, there is no functioning referral mechanism for the victims and survivors of explosive incidents, making it hard to access immediate and long-term medical, phycological and social support, which is crucial especially for the people with disabilities who are the most affected by the conflict, displacement, and explosive incidents.

2 - Activities

UNMAS Nigeria intends to continue supporting national capacities, including the security sector and civil society, and coordination of humanitarian mine action response in northeast Nigeria, towards the vision of National authorities protecting civilians from the threat of explosive ordnance in northeast Nigeria.In order to effectively respond to the risks and threats of mines and explosives in the North-east, UNMAS Nigeria has developed a strategic and comprehensive multi-donor approach, in line with the UNMAS Strategic Plan 2019 -2023 and UNMAS Nigeria Country Programme Strategy (2020-2021), as well as strategies of UN Nigeria, including the “Centrality of Protection Strategy”, “Multi-Year Humanitarian Response Plan (2019-2021)”, “Borno State Return Strategy of 2018" and “Decongestion Strategy" (draft).The project revolves around 2 outcomes, mainly focusing on developing capacities of the national and state authorities and the local NGOs and CSOs to plan and deliver effective mine action intervention based on information and mapping, and on mitigation of immediate threats for refugees, IDPs, returnees, and communities as well as humanitarian workers. Activity 1: Nigerian authorities in Borno Adamawa and Yobe states have increased capacity to locate and respond to IED threats and incidents. UNMAS will enhance the existing national capacity by developing and installing information management system to document the scale and scope of contamination, and by providing targeted training for front line members of National Security forces through the delivery of Improvised Explosive Device Disposal (IEDD) and Advanced Emergency First Responder Course (EFR) training to enhance sustainability and national ownership.Activity 2: A VA referral mechanism and national VA strategy and work plan are established. UNMAS Nigeria plans to conduct a survey and mapping of explosive incidents and the victims and survivors of those incidents, including their families and communities, to identify the situation, challenges and needs of victims and survivors, including physical challenges and mental stigmas. This project plans to design and establish a referral mechanism, which enables access to effective immediate and long-term support, with particular attention to the needs of women and girls. Such a referral mechanism can be shared by other UN, international and local organizations, and enable well-coordinated and efficient protection assistance. Activity 3: National entities have increased capacity to conduct Non-Technical Survey (NTS) to confirm the presence and absence of explosive ordnanceActivity 4: National, state authorities, national NGO/civil society organizations have increased capacity to deliver Explosive Ordnance Risk Education in BAY States. UNMAS will provide Training of Trainers (ToT) in order to enhance sustainability and national and local ownership, through the development of national and local capacity. Activity 5: Educational materials respond to the risks faced by women, men, children, and vulnerable groups in north-east Nigeria are designed according to the needs of each group of the 5000 beneficiaries for EORE and conflict prevention and preparedness based on gender, age, location, displacement status, and other factors, in consultation with affected communities in line with “Community Engagement Strategy and Action Plan for north-east Nigeria”, “Borno State Return Strategy”, and “Decongestion Strategy (draft)”. Activity 6: Civilians and humanitarian actors in north-east Nigeria have increased capacity to mitigate the explosive threats through the delivery of EORE sessions to enhance knowledge on risks and threats of mines and explosives and foster safe behaviour, reaching 40.000 beneficiaries as well as provision of Explosive Ordnance Awareness (EOA) sessions for 400 humanitarian actors operating in north-east Nigeria.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Explosive ordnances are indiscriminate, but specific threats and impacts vary according to gender, age and other aspects of diversity. Mine action actors maintain a comprehensive and inclusive approach to planning, implementing and monitoring programmes using local sources to include expertise on gender and protection relevant to the community and to ensure protection for all and to guarantee no one, including women or marginalised groups are left behind.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Community members are involved in determining their own risks and priorities for mine action assistance, which ensures the project addresses community needs and priorities and participation in design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of operations. Systematic measures are in place to collect feedback from affected people to understand the usefulness of the messages and to understand how these messages might be effective in changing behaviour of those who live among or will return to areas with Explosive Ordnances. Two-way information provision or CwC, feedback mechanism and community participation have always been part of the mine action approach in Nigeria.
