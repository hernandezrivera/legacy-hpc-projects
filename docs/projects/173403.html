Project Summary

The project aims to address specific needs of 16,355 boys, girls and children with disabilities of ages 3 to 24 years in the Rohingya community, and ages 4 to 24 year in the host community. Overall at least 51% are girls.
In order to ensure inclusive access through equitable, gender-responsive learning, the project will construct single and double storied learning centres for the Rohingya learners with proper drainage and gender segregated WASH facilities. 700 differently-abled children will be identified and provided with PWD devices as per their need, and learning centres made disable-friendly with support structures. Host community will continue to be supported through pre-primary, non-formal primary centres, learning opportunities for adolescents and youth through adolescent centres and multi-purpose community learning centres. This will contribute to increasing the number of school going children and skills development among the adolescents. Involving the host community children in different learning activities will contribute to ease tensions and improve social cohesion between the communities.
In adherence with the Education Sector standards and frameworks, quality education will be ensured through: staff capacity development - teachers basic, refresher and, subject based training in LCFA & Myanmar Curriculum, Teachers Learning Circle (TLC); construction of training centres. Through participatory approach in material development, so that contents are gender and culturally sensitive. Rigorous monitoring will be key along with coordination with ISCG and relevant government authorities to ensure smooth operations. Awareness sessions and C4D activities to sensitize the community on different social and education issues will be done to minimize pre-existing socio-cultural norms hindering children’s education and ensue community’s ownership. BRAC’s partner organizations will be capacitated as required, to scale interventions and impact children’s education in both communities. The Sector’s contingency plan will be contextually updated and applied to prepare for natural calamities and other incidents.

1 - Identified Needs

Needs of Rohingya communities: 86% Rohingya children have access to attend home learning despite the COVID-19 pandemic with existing learning facilities and infrastructure in the camps. Despite this achievement during the pandemic, 9% of age group 3-24 are reported may not go back to learning facilities after the lockdown on education activities is lifted. In the Rohingya community 40% female and 43% male of age group 3-5 years , 24% female and 20% male of the age group 6-14 years were already not attending any formal learning before COVID-19 (JMSNA, 2020). Furthermore, alarmingly, 97% female and 72% male of age group 15-18 years and 99% female and 96% male of age group 19-24 years were not accessing educational activities (JSMNA 2020)The issue of gender-inclusiveness is yet to be addressed as 99% female of age group 19-24 are still out of reach, especially due to social stigma and the lack of menstrual friendly WASH segregated facilities in the learning centres. Children with disability have the additional barriers of accessibility (camp, road, and geographical terrain) and lack of inclusive teaching-learning materials and are less likely to attend learning facilities (10% to 40% less than the children without disability). Pre-dominated socio-cultural barriers (23%), marriage (36%) and domestic support (20%) are the main factors preventing children and youth to fully access education opportunities.During COVID-19 lockdown, at least 64% households with children that attended LCs before reported having spoken to a teacher since the LCs had been closed (JMSNA-2020). 27% households report loss in access to education during the pandemic. Compared to the previous year, education situation has improved in the camps, however a large number of children and parents are still reluctant to pursue education which needs to be addressed.Needs of host communities: Before COVID-19, 40% of individuals aged 4-24 were reported not to have attended any formal learning in the host community (JMSNA-20-2020). The Rohingya influx of August 2017 had further impacted the host communities and schools of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazila, whereby enrolment and retention are among the lowest in the country. Beside this pandemic directly impacted children’s retention or enrolment and the quality of education has also significantly decreased due to community para-teachers seeking higher-paid jobs in the camps with the humanitarian community.Barriers to education for children and youth range from being engaged in household chores or economic activities due to the high inflation in the area (32% - JMSNA 2019) to increased sentiment of insecurity (criminal activities, road traffics). As reported only 26% youths are attending formal education during this academic year.
