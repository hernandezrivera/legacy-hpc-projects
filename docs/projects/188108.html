Project Summary

This project aims to respond to the needs of conflict-affected and vulnerable groups by providing life-saving assistance through winter vouchers and also providing people in need with wider and more dignified assistance, giving them the flexibility to choose what to purchase based on their heating stoves are being used ( wood, pomace, fuel, blankets, clothes). It also results in more aid directly reaching beneficiaries, which ultimately ensures the maximum impact for those in need and better value-for-money. The winterization vouchers contribute to meeting a Minimum Winter Needs for one family for 2021 as recommended by Shelter/NFI cluster to be 150$ per household,
The distribution of the winter vouchers will be through vouchers to be redeemed through the money-transfer agency for 150$ USD, where all beneficiaries will receive cash vouchers which they can redeem at selected money transfer vendors and receive cash. This project intends to reach 3,000 households (18,000 individuals approximately) in the highly prioritized locations where the coverage percentage is ZERO according to S/NFI cluster gap analysis, focusing on IDP sites such as unfinished buildings, collective centers, and informal settlements.
The beneficiaries will be selected based on a set of vulnerability criteria with a focus on the newly IDPs women-headed households, elderly, orphans, pregnant and lactating women, and other hardship cases like those who are in dire need of Shelter.
Amal Organization has a technical voucher and cash Standards Operation Procedures SOP in place to deal with cash and vouchers in such risky and sensitive context, and this SOP is in line with the cash working group guidelines as well as all the international NGOs who have Cash-Transferring Programs.
By the end of the project and during the years "2022-2023", 3,000 HHs (18,000 individuals) will secure all winter needs to cope with the harsh winter conditions.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people, triggered one of the worst displacement crises of our time, and led to the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply, and irrigation systems. According to HNO 2022, 14 million people in Syria need humanitarian assistance, with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline.Syria is not considered to be among the most vulnerable countries in the world to natural hazards, several environmental risks remain, mainly due to its arid climate, including drought, seasonal flooding, and wildfires.Destruction and structural damage have also severely affected the housing stock across the country, leading to inadequate housing and shelter conditions for millions of people in Syria. Shelter needs particularly pronounced during winter, remain high amongst the over 2 million IDPs in over 1,760 informal settlements, planned camps, and collective shelters in North-west and North-east Syria. IDPs in these last resort sites live in particularly inadequate, temporary shelters such as tents and unfinished buildings which provide limited protection against the elements and require frequent exchange and/or repair. 5 - Implementation of activities

Amal will announce vacancies to start the staff hiring process. After employment, Amal will start signing the needed MoU with local councils, organizations, and other stakeholders related to the implementation of activities.Amal will announce a competitive tender to contract with the Money-Transfer Agency that will be responsible for redeeming the BNFs' vouchers.The field team will conduct a community mobilization through meetings, social media, and posters, then door-to-door BNFs’ registration and verification process will be completed in order to prepare the initial beneficiaries list,The MEAL Team will complete the selection process based on the selection criteria to reach the final beneficiaries list and start the distribution process.The distribution team will be consisted of: Two staff members and one community member per desk. One to check beneficiary ID card against approved distribution list registers cash vouchers given to the beneficiary and request signing of the cash voucher receipt and distribution list. Second staff to give the voucher to the BNF and stamp the voucher. Community member to support BNF identification.Finance and M&E staff monitor distribution sites randomly to check distribution site conditions, safety and potential issues in the area and accurate documentation and distribution by the distribution teamThe beneficiaries will redeem their vouchers at the next table by the money-transfer agent and the staff finance officer

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will engage the target BNFs in the design process, planning, and activities implementation. So, the project targeted both the host community and IDPs in all project stages.the BNFs are from both women and men with priority for the following families: • Female/widow headed household(HH)s• Child-headed HHs (head of HH must be less than 18 years old)• HHs headed by someone with a disability (limitation to perform a specific task) • HHs with two or more members with a disability (mental and/or physical) • Elderly-headed HHs (over 60 years old) • HHs with more than 5 children • HHs without a breadwinner • HHs with pregnant and lactating womenthe project will consider gender issues during the beneficiary selecting, verifying, and vouchers distribution by involving females in the project staff, coordinating with the community leaders, and families before and during activities implementation.Amal will emphasize ensuring access to complaints channels for all members of society, both women and men, and emphasizing the importance of these channels to solve problems in the right way without embarrassing anyone.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project will follow strict procedures during the implementation periods, by engaging females in the project staff, and engaging all stakeholders with targeted families in all project activities especially the distribution process.the project will select offices inside the community near the public facilities and main road for easy access.the project will recruit qualified MEAL staff to ensure high quality of monitoring, evaluation, and transparency.All these steps will conduct by the project are to avoid and prevent any kind of abuse or sexual exploitation.Amal organization will make sure that all the staff has attended the PSEA training, also banners will be shown at the distribution locations about the PSEA reporting mechanism.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Interference and Aid diversion by Armed Opposition Groups. Mitigation: 1- Share the distribution plan with the safety and security department to get clearance and advice.2- Train staff to make them prepared for like scenarios and how to report like incidents directly with suspending the distribution and leaving distribution sits.Risk2: The Tension between the host community and IDP's. Mitigation: Clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders, and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the activity. coordinate with local authorities in the targeted area as well as be active in field coordination.Risk3: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers, and low knowledge on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. Mitigation: Amal has policies, procedures, and other measures in place that relate to preventing sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. So, Amal will work on the following: 1- train the staff on code of conduct, PSEA, CSG and follow them periodically2- Amal ensures to have in a place an accessible mechanism for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse in line with Amal safeguarding policy.Risk4: Covid outbreaks or related risk. Mitigation: Amal is highly considering the COVID-19 outbreak through all its projects. So, there will be more than one distribution point where possible, and the team will do door-to-door distribution for those who are elderly, have chronic diseases ..etc.
