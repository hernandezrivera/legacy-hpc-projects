Needs

UNDP, OCHA, UN Environment and Government of Bangladesh (GOB) have conducted need assessments on environment, social cohesion and livelihoods. Restoration of ecosystem was identified as a primary and urgent need. The large makeshift camps in the Kutupalong-Balukhali and other areas has made substantial direct impact on the available forest resources in Ukhiya Forest Range where forest land that has already been cut by 30-40% by the ongoing deforestation process. From Ukhiya alone 43,567 tons of biomass carbon were removed. Trees on hHills were cut for constructing settlements, thereby removing/disturbing the top soil and local ecology. The value of lost benefits/opportunity cost due to change in land use to accommodate the makeshift camps is significant, and the continued demand for fuel wood is further putting pressure on the forests and plantations. Moreover, the local ecology and topography does not allow for much food production and people are dependent on petty trade and daily labor to meet their needs. This fact, together with the market impact of the refugee influx (driving prices up, lowering wages and employment opportunities), has increased food insecurity in local communities. Host communities report increasingly using negative coping strategies like borrowing food and relying on assistance from neighbors, an indication that that food security may deteriorate further. Other negative coping mechanimsmechanism such as trafficking and early marriage, which diproportionatelydisproportionately affects women and girls, are also on the rise. The local host and refugee communities are also at higher risk from natural disasters as a result of the deforestation and less able to deal with the shocks with deteriorating livelihoods. Women and children bear greater risk during natural disasters in Bangladesh. Women, particularly female headed households, have less ability to put in place self-protection mechanisms to reduce the impact of natural disasters on their household – largely due to the economic and physical requirements to do so, and struggle more than men to recover following natural disasters.The environmental and livelihoods impact has also given rise to socio-economic conflicts and disharmony between host communities and Rohingya refugees.

Activities or outputs

This project has two main componetscomponents to address these needs. 1. The project will support will support the livelihoods of the host communities affected by the influx of Rohingya refugees by creating or restoring productive assets, encouraging self-organization of local economic actors, improving technical skills and financial literacy.the livelihoods and resilience of the host communities affected by the influx of Rohingya refugees. The project will support communities to develop risk reduction action plans with a focus on creating, protecting and restoring productive assets as well as improving community infrastructure, shelter and WASH facilities, thereby making communities and households more resilentresilient to shocks. The project will have with a specific focus on women and highly vulnerable households, with a targeted 80% of direct beneficiaries to be women. In order to maximize impact, individuals may be involved in multiple activities – for example, all beneficiaries of asset building assistance will be encouraged to join a savings group, in order to further build their financial management skills. Cash support will be provided as an immediate source of income, so that the most vulnerable households are not tempted to use their cash grants for livelihood assets they receive, in order to meet the survival needs of the household. Caritas will coordinate with other organisations assisting the refugee populations living in the target communities, in order to mitigate any tensions between both communities. Risk reduction action plans will take into account the needs of men, women, boys and girls by including them in participatory processes and prioritization. Needs based on age and ability will also be incorporated. 2. The project will restore forest ecosystems which will rejuvenate declining forests and waters sheds with favorable impact on agricultural productivity and thence food security. This includes: homestead gardening of vegetable seedling by the Rohingya peoples; Road side plantation by the participation of Rohingya peoples; Bamboo sapling plantation for preventing hill slop erosion by the Rohingya peoples; Capacity building training on rightly planting and caring means; among others. Both women and men will be targeted.
