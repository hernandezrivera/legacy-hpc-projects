Project Summary

World Vision Diffa Emergency Response seeks to partner with sector agencies in an inclusive WASH intervention in other to contribute to improved menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and also guaranteeing women economic empowerment in Diffa and surrounding communes.
The Dignified Menstruation project will focus on improving Menstrual hygiene management through the creation of an all an inclusive friendly space for reproductive health information, production of reusable biodegradable pad, research and advocacy unit dubbed MHM-Hub. Institutional latrines with menstrual hygiene management components in school, health centers and other public places. Design and pilot Mobile changing Booth for public spaces which will incorporate female friendly minimum requirements for menstrual hygiene. This will includes waste collection point which will be conveyed by sanitation taskforce to the final treatment site. There will be institutional latrine construction in communities.
• Design and test mobile changing booth for piloting in 5 public spaces
• Construction of latrines in communities and in schools adapted to MHM requirement for women and girls.
This will target vulnerable population in Chetimari, Diffa, Maine Soroa, and Gueskerou Communes largely drawn from both refugees, returnees, internally displaced people and host population. A total of 700 households from the selected communities will be reached over a period of 12 months .

Besoins

Many interventions are being deployed to cover the most basic needs of the displaced people including, improving their nutritional status, livelihoods, water, sanitations and hygiene. However, for some reasons, very few of those intervention take into account the crucial aspect of menstrual hygiene for women and girls. Like in many African Countries, menstrual hygiene is still a taboo subject in Niger. The statistics continuous to show that women suffer in silence and dehumanized as a result of socio cultural and religious restrictions affecting the management of menstrual period. About 40% of women and girls are restricted in mobility and lack access to appropriate WASH infrastructure. In the context of Diffa, women and teenage girls lack private and secure spaces for menstrual hygiene management (MHM), they have inadequate information on menstrual hygiene, suffer coupled with anxiety of bloodstains in public due to the use of used rugs and pieces of cloth for menstrual period hygiene management. This situation exposes them to serious health risks. Girls using rugs for MHM risk bloodstain from leakage which may result in low self-esteem because it obviously a sign of poverty, increases their vulnerability to gender based abuse. Extreme poverty is indeed widespread in Niger. Being a woman and above all a displaced woman increase economic vulnerability. Women and girls cannot afford hygiene pads, and most of them don’t even know how to use them. , low self-esteem and associated health risk. Interventions around this sector in Diffa by humanitarian agencies have been limited to the distribution of dignity kits. Failure to respond to the biological needs of women and girls in this context can therefore have significant health implications.The emphasis will largely be on menstrual health and hygiene using a play based animated curricular to educate girls and boys in and out of school on reproductive and menstrual health. Content will be developed in collaboration with Education Unit in the commune.
