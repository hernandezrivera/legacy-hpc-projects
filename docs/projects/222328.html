Project Summary

The 2025 project is anticipated to provide critical and Lifesaving basic Services to Vulnerable Populations in hard to reach areas especially in Akobo, Pibor, Nyirol counties of Jonglei State including Panyijiar and Leer counties of Unity state where CIDO has physical and programmatic presence. CIDO intends to provide timely and tailored Shelter and NFIs support to vulnerable targeted beneficiaries mainly returnees, IDPS and host community through in kind static response and cash based initiatives where feasible. This is in response to conflict ,displacement and floods that forced many of the target population to their current displacement sites.
Host communities in these areas are face with challenges on how to cope with the number of returnees who are in need of their support and care with the little resources that they have. CIDO will provide lifesaving shelter and NFIs in-kind support in response to specific needs to 65,000 IDPs, returnees and conflict affected populations broken down as Akobo county (15,000 people) ,Pibor county (20,000 people) Nyirol county (10,000 people) Leer county ( 10,000 people) and Panyijiar county ( 10,000 people) While adhering to the SNFI cluster targeting criteria and in light of the limited resources ,CIDO will consider the specific needs, concerns and priorities of vulnerable women and girls, men and boys of different ages and with specific needs such as the elderly, children heading households, people with disability, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerabilities with the aim of incorporating protection principles during the project implementation. The intervention will clearly define the protection concerns taken into consideration. Contextual feedback mechanisms will be established in line with the distribution method and the location dynamics. Beneficiaries will be identified using approved South Sudan SNFIs cluster criteria of selection. In addition, CIDO will endeavor to give priority to child-headed households, women, persons with disability, the elderly and other vulnerable people in the community who will benefit from Emergency Shelter and NFI distribution. CIDO will aspire to conduct joint missions including joint assessment and responses when needed in order to better respond to the complex needs of the beneficiaries, pull resources together and minimize overlap.

1. Needs

Humanitarian needs in South Sudan continue to be mainly driven by years of inter communal conflict natural disasters like floods, increasing underlying vulnerability and which has been further compounded by the impacts of climate change. This compounded by severe flooding in 2024 late July to date. Floodwaters have destroyed critical infrastructure and have limited humanitarian access to heavily affected populations. Barriers to movement, imposed by flooding and insecurity, have also limited the ability of vulnerable groups in affected areas, particularly women, to implement traditional coping strategies already weakened coping mechanisms and exacerbating vulnerability. In May 2024, the Sudan and Ethiopia conflict had major impact on people, resulting in massive number of returnees additionally the most recent flooding reported as from July 2024 has left thousands displaced in the above aforementioned locations which has further led to overcrowding and loss of access to markets and basic services .There are reports of negative coping strategies as a result of homelessness. According to an Inter-Agency Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) conducted on August 2024 by humanitarian agencies in Ganyilel and Nyal Payams of Panyinjar County in late October 2024 Humanitarian partners and including CIDO, conducted an IRNA in all the four payams (Diror, Barmach, Walgak Center, Buong and Kuonykew Payams), over twenty-four (25) villages were displaced away from their homes as a result of floods. Part of the findings are that 5,154 HHs (30,924 individuals) of the population are affected, team as well conducted an IRNA in Akobo East County and the situation is worrying since the floods affected population are in dire need of SNFIs assistance. The situation requires immediate humanitarian response to provide lifesaving SNFI assistance both in kind and through cash based interventions. Conflict dynamics appeared more pronounced in Akobo East and Akobo West and Nyirol County as a result of the inter communal conflict, flooding continue to strain access to the very limited basic servicesand disrupted local markets in Pibor, Akobo, Nyirol, Leer and Panyinjar Counties. Sub-national violence in Pibor, Akobo and Nyirol Counties resulted in relocation of some humanitarian partners, looting of lifesaving supplies and targeting of humanitarian workers based on ethnic identities. The humanitarian country team in South Sudan has since recommended immediate humanitarian response scale up in the counties Pibor, Akobo and Nyirol County. Greater Pibor Administrative Area has been marred by protracted shocks for many years, marginalization; conflict and floods in the past years; desperation cattle-raiding. Despite improvements in shelterconditions for internally displaced persons (IDPs) across the state, IDPs in flood affected counties in July 2024 reportedly continued to mainly live in improvised shelter types.According to OCHA South Sudan snapshot October 2024, about 1.4 million individuals are displaced across the Country 377,000 individuals floods internally displaced persons seeking shelters on higher ground , partners are reporting limited resources with key ES/NFI gaps highlighted. This project will target 65,000 IDPs, returnees,IDPS and host communities affected by shocks and identfied in accordance to SNFI cluster targeting criteria broken down as Akobo county (15,000 people) ,Pibor county (20,000 people) Nyirol county (10,000 people) Leer county ( 10,000 people) and Panyikiar county ( 10,000 people) . They will comprise of mainly pregnant and lactating women ,People living with disability, child headed households,elderly and female headed households.

Conduct distribution of ES/NFIs materials to 65,000 beneficiaries 5.Conduct training of staff members on PSEA and AAP 6. Procure local shelter materials and distribute to registered and verified beneficiaries7.Construct shelters to the identified beneficiaries 8. At the beginning of the project ,Conduct consultations and community engagement to initiate a project implementation committee to support the response cycle with representation from local community members, beneficiaries, and NGOs 9. Initiate a safe, child and gender sensitive complaints and feedback mechanisms with clear reporting channels in each project location. 10 Conduct market assessments in the proposed counties 11. Provide cash based interventions where feasible in the proposed counties12 Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring assessments using AAP tools. Conduct awareness sessions on disaster risk reduction to the targeted population. 13.Establish and strengthen Disaster Management Committees 14.Develop Climate Change Adaptation Plans
