Project Summary

The project aims to mitigate the immediate effect of displacement due to natural disaster or conflict through preparedness of 7 public schools in Gaza and North Gaza Governorates to serve as 4 Designated Emergency Shelters (DES) and post-emergency Collective Centers according to agreed Shelter/WASH standards. The action aims to strengthen the ability of vulnerable IDPs in the Gaza and North Gaza Governorates to cope with protracted threats and shocks.
The project activities are aligned with the Country Strategic Objective 3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
This project responds to the Shelter Cluster Objective 3 “Increase capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner” through the preparedness and upgrading of DES.

Needs

During the height of hostilities in the 2014 conflict, 485.000 people were internally displaced. NRC’s IDP-Assessment (2018) shows that the first destination of Gazans who fled their home were Emergency Shelters (51%), host families (24%) & rented units (22%). The ones who sought emergency shelters went to 93 UNRWA schools (285,000 IDPs) & 24 public schools (48,000 IDPs) which were not prepared to function as designated emergency shelters (DES) providing safety & protection during/post-emergency. As of October 2018, there is still a gap in the preparedness of DES. A Gaza 2014 humanitarian response review conducted by the Gaza Emergency Operations Center & lessons learnt gathered by the Gaza Inter Cluster Coordination group (ICCG), concluded – in coordination with MoEHE and MoSD – that UNRWA and PA public schools are the most feasible option to accommodate IDPs during emergencies. The OCHA-led IDP working group in cooperation with UNRWA, MoSD, MoEHE & relevant clusters set selection criteria for the initial list of 65 schools suggested by MoEHE based on location, capacity, access, existing facilities/infrastructure, security/safety. A multi sectorial technical taskforce (TTF) including NRC, Oxfam, ACF, PUI, UNICEF, GVC, UNDP & others, in cooperation with relevant authorities assessed each school in April/May 2016. Key informant interviews with relevant stakeholders were held. Considering UNRWA shelter standards, the TTF developed two tools: 1) A technical assessment tool to assess the security, capacity and suitability of the selected schools. 2) SoPs considering all sectors’ needs, were developed in consultation with all clusters, mainstreaming gender & protection in line with IASC guidelines. Based on the assessment, 32 schools were prioritized according to hosting capacity, geographical DES-coverage and proximity to available facilities and services. As a result of the assessment analysis by the TTF, final outputs (scope of work, budget, design & project time frame) were presented to the ICCG & shared with main stakeholders after Cluster revision.Gender & protection concerns were reflected in the scope of work. Based on the experience of female & male IDPs of all ages using emergency shelters during 2014 escalations, overcrowding was considered a main challenge. It disproportionately exposed women, girls, older male and female adults & other at-risk-groups like PWDs to protection threats. Especially WASH facilities & overcrowded shelter-rooms in DES can increase women’s, girls’ & other groups’ vulnerability to sexual & other forms of GBV especially after dark. This resulted in the inclusion of specific considerations:- Gender-separated WASH facilities need to be central, accessible & well-lit to ensure safety, privacy, dignity & minimize the potential for GBV. To ensure proper hygiene management especially of girls, safe disposal options in WASH facilities as well as sufficient & safe water for washing hands & personal hygiene needs to be available. Using WASH facilities after dark can hinder the ability of women & girls to practice appropriate hygiene during their menstrual cycle & can raise GBV threats. - All IDPs need safe and equitable access to WASH facilities. Hygiene facilities are critically important for PWDs & older people of both sexes as they are more susceptible to infections.- Shelter rooms need to ensure sufficient privacy & safety especially during nights. Out of 32 schools (20 DES), 14 schools have still not been prepared as DES while 18 schools were either fully or mostly partially prepared. The total number of beneficiaries is estimated at 22,500 IDPs in the 32 schools with an average of 703 IDPs per school. Also students & teachers of the target schools will benefit from part of the preparedness activities (ramps, backup-generator, toilets, essential maintenance) with a total of 34,885 students (19,396 girls, 15,489 boys) & 1,469 teachers (825 female, 644 male).

Activities

Out of the 14 schools, NRC proposes to upgrade 3 (2 DES) located in Gaza Governorate (Arafat school for talented I & II) & North Gaza Governorate (Al Hirtani school). Out of the 18 partially prepared schools, NRC proposes to close outstanding preparedness gaps of 4 (2 DES). The latter were previously partially prepared by NRC (North Gaza Governorate: Al Falujah & Hafsa schools) & UNICEF (Gaza Governorate: Al Buraq & Al Aqsa schools). The proposed intervention covers WASH, Shelter, food security, and energy components. To minimize protection threats for women, girls, older male & female adults & other at-risk-groups like PWDs, activities are tailored to meet identified gender & protection needs.The action includes (1) maintenance/repair works of existing Shelter/WASH infrastructure & facilities, (2) upgrading works, (3) procurement of equipment. The activities are implemented through a bid-winning contractor with daily site supervision & monitoring by NRC Shelter Staff. The project is coordinated with the OCHA led DES working group, MoEHE, MoSD, respective municipalities. Maintenance/repair works include windows & roof maintenance & insulation from climate; maintenance of existing WASH facilities; repair of doors to ensure physical protection & privacy; maintenance of handrails to ensure safety for children & elderly; Fixing of electrical installation & outlets to avoid security risks; working lighting systems inside classrooms, corridors & along the path to and from toilets to ensure adequate access to toilets during night especially for women, girls, boys, and PWDs.Upgrading works include temporary partitions (fire resistant fabric) inside classrooms to ensure privacy, especially for breastfeeding mothers, males & females to pray & change clothes. When used as Collective Center post-emergency, family-size partitions ensure privacy & dignity & minimize protection threats. A window protection film mitigates the impact of broken glass during escalation. An adequate DES-entrance with suitable sized gates & emergency exits facilitate movement and safety of IDPs including wheelchair users as part of evacuation procedures. WASH components include the construction of latrine & shower units separated for women/girls and men/boys and suitable for personal hygiene, with sufficient water storage tanks, and solar heating systems for hot water during winter and to ensure handwashing and personal hygiene. To reduce GBV in/around WASH facilities, security measures such as lighting, internally lockable doors, and privacy fencing is installed to completely shields users. For wheelchair-users, access to WASH facilities is ensured by a ramp and doors of adequate size as well as a separate PWD-fit toilet and shower units within each WASH facility. Taps, soap dispensers, and light switches are at reach for users of all ages. Procurements include supply & installation of fire extinguishers & solar system for emergency lighting, backup electrical generators with fuel tanks, desalination plants with connection to constructed/rehabilitated water well to ensure drinking water supply in case of shortages in municipal water supply. In the school yard, a sun-shade with concrete seats is installed, which is essential during post-emergency when DES are used as Collective Centers.

Indicators

NRC’s M&E frameworks ensure that relevant M&E tools are used to meet planned targets with quality. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E unit, ensures that a tracking and reporting system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes. An indicator matrix records data from the field on a monthly basis to measure progress and provide corrective measures where needed. NRC supports quality control measures and accountability towards beneficiaries. In addition, the M&E unit conducts regular field visits to monitor project progress and feedback key findings to project teams. NRC conducts a post-project lessons learnt exercise to continuously improve efficiency and effectiveness in implementation. A complaint and feedback mechanism will be accessible to the communities in which the DES are located.On a technical level, NRC staff prepares detailed technical plans, trainings plans and matrices, specifications, BoQs, and tender documents and monitors tightly the contract implementation of the bid-winning contractor through a monitoring and reporting plan that includes 1) daily supervision by the field engineer; 2) documentation of daily reports, site instructions, casting & works approval, sample approvals, site and laboratory testing, 3) Weekly field visits by NRC Shelter management, and bi-weekly field meeting with contractor, 4) technical field engineer from MoEHE will participate in monitoring visits, 5) Tracking of legal contract compliance, 6) Tracking of phased payments, 7) Formal committees from NRC and MoEHE to approve samples, variation orders, and financial payment; 8) Documentation of all project documents, shop drawings, as-build drawings, and photos throughout project implementation; 9) Site hand over to MoEHE and MoSD.Community outreach & engagement during the design-phase is ensured by NRC’s local partner. The community will be informed about the main project information through community meetings and information leaflets which will also inform them about how to access NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism. Previous IDPs (male & female of different ages, families with PWDs) & municipal forum members representing female/male community members of different ages will be invited to walk through the public schools in their respective community which have already been prepared by NRC to function as a DES. An FGD will be conducted to discuss their feedback with regards to whether their protection & gender concerns & special needs are adequately addressed in the upgrade of the DES based on emergency-standard. Relevant feedback will be considered in the design and upgrade of the DES proposed for upgrade. Also, communication with neighboring communities adjacent to the DES will be consulted and their concerns addressed during the design phase.
