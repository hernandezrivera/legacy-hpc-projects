Project Summary

The project seeks to deliver quality life saving and life sustaining, inclusive and gender responsive interventions to 165,000 crisis affected populations in need, across Aljazeera, East and South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan States. This will be achieved through the following thematic areas;
• Provide crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to emergency water services.
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to emergency sanitation services
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to hygiene services
• Provide outbreaks affected and ‘at-risk’ population access to minimum WASH for Health package
The response targets three localities in Aljazeera state (Madani El Kobra, El Hasaheesa and El Kamleen), five localities in East Darfur (Eldaen, Bahar Alarab, Abujabra, Alsalaya and Elfirdos) five localities in Blue Nile (Baw, Geisan, Damazine, Kurmuk and Rosaries), Kadugli and Delling localities in South kordofan and five localities in South
Darfur (Beliel, Nyala South, Nyala North, Mershing, and Um Dafoug)

1 - Needs

The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene situation (WASH) is deteriorating due to worsening economic crisis, political instability, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, a five-fold increase in operation and maintenance cost of water infrastructures, insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation by the Government, and limited external investments. The recent armed conflict that erupted in the country on 15th April exacerbated the situation, increasing the IDP case load, damaging WASH infrastructure, disrupting supply chain for WASH inputs and limiting Government attention and funding for WASH. Most affected WASH population groups are those impacted by the ongoing conflict, existing IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations battling food insecurity and extreme poverty who cannot afford WASH services and practices. As per the revised 2023 HRP, 14.9 million people (excluding Refugees) who fall under the above categories need urgent humanitarian WASH assistance. Conflict especially in the Darfur region and South Kordofan led to displacement of people from their homes to rescind in IDPs.The upcoming rain season and potential flooding is likely to put more pressure in an already bad WASH situation in affected areas. These multiple shocks severely impact on livelihoods, escalating the vulnerabilities of the crisis affected populations. This response project targets eleven (18) localities in four states, two of which are ranked as priority one (Aljazeera and South Darfur) and the other two as priority two (East Darfur and South Kordofan) in the revised HRP owing to the current conflict. Three of these states (ED, SD & BN) also host the bulk of the vulnerable people in need . The three states were in protracted conflict for about 17 years and therefore also host majority of IDPs in Sudan. The abrupt nature in which the ongoing conflict erupted means that people were caught unawares and unprepared. Most left with the minimum they could carry and therefore WASH NFIs are greatly required at gathering points to support the affected populations to access WASH services. Dignity kits for adolescent girls and women of reproductive age are equally a critical need among the displaced population. The conflict impacted bank opeartions and hence cash flow challenges leave the vulnerable people more exposed and in need of humanitarian assistance. Limited sanitation facilities at gathering points have led to open defecation, which poses a high risk of disease outbreaks. The needs were obtained from a number of assessments done by WASH partners, IOM DTM updates, OCHA humanitarian updates and the revised WASH HRP for 2023.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The response will focus on live saving and live sustaining interventions targeting mainly populations affected by the ongoing conflict. This will include water trucking services to IDPs in gathering points, provision of WASH NFIs and dignity kits and repair/rehabilitation of damaged WASH infrastructure. This will cover both IDPs as well as the host communities. However, part of the response will support the escalating needs of existing IDPs, returnees and Sphere standards will be applied in the design and implementation of the proposed interventions to ensure that the target program participants receive assistance that meets the set humanitarian standards. Sudan WASH sector standards will also be followed to the extent possible, within the context of emergency response. Water safety will be a high priority for this project. Detailed water quality analysis will be conducted for all sources identified for rehabilitation to ensure that the water availed to beneficiary meets WHO drinking water standards or at the minimum the country standards as may be applicable. Robust Free Residual Chlorine monitoring will be maintained from the source, drawing points, health care facilities and household level. Proposed water points will be located in secure places to ensure they are safely accessible, especially for women, children, the elderly, and those living with disabilities. Emergency shared/communal toilets will be constructed at gathering points to help prevent open defecation and the associated risks. World Vision will work with the local administrative authorities to improve solid waste management, which if not properly handled can lead to disease outbreaks, pollution of water sources and general environmental degradation.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Uncoordinated use of natural resources can have detrimental impacts on the environment further contributing to climate change. Climate change has become one of the major issues threatening the sustainability of WASH Infrastructure development in the world. Changes to weather patterns, including the reliability and predictability of seasonal rainfall and the impacts of extreme events, now place unprecedented pressures on water resources, especially in flood- and drought-prone contexts like Sudan. Climate change threatens water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities and services, as these are intimately linked to the water cycle and are vulnerable to changes in the quantity and quality of available water resources. Floods and droughts, which pollute and reduce water delivery respectively, have now become a perennial issue to deal with in Sudan. We witnessed the destruction of WASH infrastructure when flash-floods were experienced across many states in Sudan in recent months.One of the Five Principles of Sustainable WASH is the element of environmental sustainability which entails placing WASH interventions in the wider context of the natural environment and implementing an approach of integrated and sustainable management of water resources. This is the approach proposed to be used in this response, to protect watersheds and over-exploitation of ground water. Through IWRM, WV will ensure harmonization of water use with land use to enhance the resilience of water and sanitation services to disasters as an effective strategy to reduce disaster impact, enhance ecosystem services, minimize pollution hazard, minimize over- pumping of groundwater aquifers, promote the sustainable use of natural resources for WASH infrastructure, and source materials as sustainably as possible. Borehole sitting will be done through geophysical surveys accompanied with Environmental and Social Impact assessments to ensure correct siting. Through a participatory approach with the target beneficiaries, potential environmental impacts from proposed interventions will be identified during ESIA processes and mitigation plans developed, implemented and monitored. Green energy solutions like solar pumping and gravity schemes will be optimized as much as possible. Ground recharge technologies like sand dams will be adopted where feasible. Use of locally available appropriate materials will also be promoted for sanitation facilities as opposed to transported brick and mortar.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed into planned activities. The project will ensure that each activity will not expose vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be linked to protection agencies (Government entities or other organizations dealing with protection issues) to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults. The project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups (like minority groups, PLWD, among others) within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, department of welfare in the Ministry of social welfare to ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staf or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While the project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children. To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staff will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries. Sanitation facilities in schools will ensure gender segregation, be located in well lit safely accessible spaces especially for girls and be designed and constructed to offer safe and private dignified access to boys and girls. Care givers will be educated to ensure that children only carry age appropriate jerricans when they have to help in fetching water.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to beneficiaries will be two-fold; to direct beneficiary groups and accountability to host community leadership. Accountability to direct beneficiaries will be through transparent engagement, communication and dialogue sessions with group members and regular joint review of progress. Accountability to host community leadership will be through MoUs that WVI represented by ‘Village Focal Points’ - will sign with community representatives. The MoUs will spell out the roles of the organization as well as the role and contribution of the communities. In addition to being accountability tools; MoUs enable WVI to communicate decisions such as criteria for selection of beneficiaries and project locations. MoU are reference points for issues that arise during implementation. MoUs have enabled WVI hold communities accountable for commitments they make at the onset of projects.Participation: This program will be designed in consultation with IDP and host communities leadership and government entities. In addition to regular consultation as part of ongoing interventions, IDP , host communities and government leaders were informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries and prioritizing interventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level will be facilitated by project staff to further publicize the program and communicate opportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institute feedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and ‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully in Darfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularly women – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamper participation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanisms and / or through or discovered through interactions. The project staff will work towards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements, ensuring that have timely access to relevant and clear information and they are satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the response. The program staff will consult with beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and accept complaints, and communicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it can address. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages
