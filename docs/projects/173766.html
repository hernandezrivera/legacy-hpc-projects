Project Summary

ThThe education needs for boys and girls in the conflict-hit NWSW regions of Cameroon have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. The hard-gained learning progress from the conflict has been reversed as formal schools and non-formal learning spaces have been closed. Both host community and IDP boys and girls and all children living with disabilities are affected. Primary and Secondary school children have become more vulnerable and are further exposed to a myriad of protection risks. Both teachers from schools and facilitators of non-formal learning spaces have been impacted negatively as they do not have accurate information on the spread and preventive measures of COVID-19. All children, teachers, facilitators and communities at large require gender and age appropriate information on what is COVID-19, how it is spread, preventive measures and what to do in the event that they suspect they are infected.
The project is to target 16,662 (9200 girls and 7,462 boys) children between the age 4 to 17 years whose aims will focus on creating and maintaining the protective learning environment for children and teachers, as well as strengthening their knowledge and skills cope with threats and attacks. In some rural communities on the contrary, there are no schools and the community has taken upon themselves to create community learning structures (CLS). However, these community learning structures face some challenges ranging from inappropriate learning spaces (lack of WASH facilities, crowdedness, poor benches, lack of good teaching/writing materials), to inability to recruit or train qualified teachers and community schools’ facilitators.
The project will facilitate a holistic educative response to the needs of the children, boys and girls, affected by this double crisis. The activities will integrate a strong capacity building of all the actors by strengthening the abilities of teachers and facilitators to provide psychosocial support to the children, by sensitizing at community level on the importance of protecting and preventing schools from attacks, by distributing schools’ kits to boys, girls and teacher/facilitators. Also to improve the quality of learning spaces, the project will ensure access to WASH facilitating with the construction/rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, provision of wash-hand stations, extension of water in feasible areas, provision of waste bin for waste management and the carryout of hygiene promotion including MHM sessions with the adolescent girls.
The HRP 2021 will include standard curriculum for basic subjects and life skills subjects uploaded USB devices and solar-powered radios to get these lessons to crises affected children in the communities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Numerous reports of attacks on education facilities and personnel, including the kidnapping of students, the burning of schools and the military use of non-operational schools. This was likely linked to the general school reopening planned for 5 October. From January to September 2020, ten attacks on education were reported, including the killing of a teacher and injuring of primary and secondary school pupil/students. The majority (9 out of 10) of incidents took place in the NW region. The number of incidents reported in 2020 is less than in 2019 however this is likely due to schools being closed because of COVID-19 and therefore not being targeted. NSAGs reportedly kidnapped four primary school children in Keyon at Elak-Oku (Bui division) in the NW. All four of them were rescued the same day. However, during the rescue a father of one of the children was shot and injured. The alleged burning of a non-operational primary school building being used by military at Nchum (Mezam division) in NW region was reported. Another similar incident of military use and damage of non-operational school buildings was reported in Bafut (Mezam division) in the NW.
