Project Summary

Analysis of humanitarian needs in 2023, the standard of living and well-being of an estimated 6.1 million people will be affected because of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Despite the replenishment of major waterways for four consecutive long rainy seasons, 42 per cent of the population in South Sudan do not have access to an improved water source and 45 per cent need to walk for more than 30 minutes to access its main water source. This is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities, as well as for women and girls who are increasingly exposed to GBV.

Project Scope
This project will be for a period of one year, starting from January 1st. 204 to December 31st, 2024, and will be implemented in Imehejek and Lurgilo Payam in Lafon County in Eastern Equatoria State. The target population is 25,800 people drawn from the host communities, IDPs and returnees. Each Payam will target a population of 12,900 participants. The project will construct and rehabilitate four institutional latrines, rehabilitate (Major and minor repair) four boreholes, train 20 pump technician, 20 hygiene promotors, 20 CLTS including 20 community leaders. 20 water users committees/hand pump caretakers, awareness raising, radio talk shows, and procurement and distribution of WASH items in each County. The project will also provide AAP services such as workshops for AAP stakeholders, community information sharing feedback sessions, sensitization, and training of community leaders on AAP, AAP volunteers and setting up of complaints and feedback mechanisms. There will be joint monitoring visits to measure project progress, identify challenges for mitigation and provide recommendations. At the end of the project, there will be a project review meeting with project stakeholders and participants to review the overall performance.
To intervene in case of emergencies crisis during the project period, the project will include budget for modification to address the crisis. This budget is part of the overall project budget.

1. Needs

Limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, such as water points and latrines, poses multisectoral impacts. Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as evidenced by the high prevalence of malnutrition in the country. At the national level, the GAM prevalence for children between 0 and 59 months is at 16 per cent, while SAM was at 5.9 per cent according to the preliminary FSNMS and 2022 SMART surveys. The presence of water-borne diseases such as diarrhea in children under age 5 is also indicative of poor WASH conditions. Increasing levels of insecurity, both ethnic and politically motivated conflict, localized violence and disputes over land are the underlying drivers of WASH conditions in almost all states, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services and restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure. Conflict has caused families to retreat to remote areas with limited WASH infrastructure, increasing pressure on the existing WASH services in the host communities and contributing to elevated inter-community tension. Insecurity has also disrupted livelihoods, trade, and supply chains, further perpetuated the economic challenges, and reduced the household income for WASH goods and services. Conflict- and/or climate-driven displacement has also led to the formation of new IDP sites that require comprehensive WASH services to ensure sufficient access to water and operation of WASH facilities.According to OCHA’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2023, around 79,487 people in Lafon County have humanitarian needs (Up from 54,700 in 2021). The figure represents over 50% of the estimated population for the County. The County is projected to continuously be under crisis Phase 3 as of December 2022 to July 2023. Communal fighting, cattle raiding and highway robbery are the primary threat to the communities of this County. During dry sessions, herders from Budi and Kapoeta North Counties to the South-East frequently travel to the flood plains of Lopa/Lafon for grazing, making the area prone to conflict. In addition, and historically the sedentary Pari tribe tended to clash with the arriving Lopa herders in the County South. Of recent, the Murle and the Toposa groups have become a problem to the communities of the area. This situation in the area has resulted to unstable settlement of the communities and displacement. This internal movement and displacement of the Lopa/Lafon people have increase capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water for the people, with the situation worsening when more people are returning home. Insecurity has also affected access to water and Sanitation facilities across the Payam in the County. Access to improved sanitation is below 10 percent where open defecation is practiced due to lack of gender segregated pit latrines. In addition, recurrent flooding regularly damages the existing sanitation infrastructure and with high number of returnees and IDPs, there is great need for the communities to invest in, reconstruct or repair sanitation facilities. Limited sustainability of the WASH response in these County due to funding shortage will increase the likelihood of waterborne disease and outbreaks, including Hepatitis and cholera.
