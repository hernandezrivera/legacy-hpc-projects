Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to alleviate the impact of the Syrian crisis on the most affected people, local communities and IDPs, in Aleppo/Al Bab and Afrin areas. The most important needs identified related to Mental Health & Psychosocial Support, MHPSS will be addressed by two axes: Acute Mental Health Inpatients Unit in Al Bab area in addition to Fixed MHGAP Unit in Afrin Area. The most affected people will benefit from specialized MHPSS services - which are not available effectively in the area to respond to the high needs of those people by establishing an Acute Mental Health Inpatients Unit in Al Bab area in addition to a Fixed MHGAP Unit in Afrin area. The main goal of the project is to provide specialized mental health services qualified to address more severe mental health disorders alongside with Psychological First Aid, PFA and referral of more severe and serious cases.
The Acute MH Inpatient Unit: will provide an acute inpatient service for more urgent presentations of severe mental health disorders, like schizophrenia (acute psychotic episodes), bipolar disorder (manic episode), severe depression, suicidal risk, severe substance misuse disorder and severe behavioral disorder related to mental health problems which cannot be managed in the community.
In addition, providing a multidisciplinary mental health service in the community (outpatient psychiatric units, psychiatric supervision, follow up and case management, individual, couple and group psychotherapy and tele-mental health supervision), and receiving referrals of more severe and complicated psychiatric cases from other agencies and health providers.
The Fixed MH Unit: will provide assessment and diagnosis of cases; women, men, children and adolescences presenting mental health disorder’s signs and symptoms according to the mental health disorders which are addressed in the MHGAP program (Depression, epilepsy, Psychosis…. Etc.) in addition to the provision of proper interventions including specific kinds of psychotropic medications.
In addition, in both clinics, there will be a team of 2 PSWs who will provide the intake assessment for cases in the outpatient clinic and refer the cases in need to the MHGAP doctors in addition to psychoeducation and awareness sessions for beneficiaries, families and caregivers.
Also, in both areas, group sessions and individual consultations with follow up mechanisms will be provided by the psychosocial workers (PSWs) supervised by the MHGAP doctors. In the projects, IDPs camps, schools and home visits will be targeted by a team of outreach PSWs who in turn will provide PSS activities for children and adolescences and awareness sessions for families on protection and MH concerns.
Objectives of the Project:
• Cases with severe and acute MH disorders will receive specialized MH interventions.
• Cases with different ages presenting signs and symptoms of mental health disorders will receive interventions based on MHGAP program.
• Beneficiaries have better awareness and knowledge on mental health and protection concerns.
• Children improved coping skills dealing with the stressful circumstances with better.
• Beneficiaries with different ages have access to other services provided by other humanitarian actors in the area.
• The project staff of PSWs have better knowledge and skills of protection minimum standards and stress management and detecting signs and symptoms of mental health disorders alongside with PSS principles and activities.

Needs

Despite the relief efforts by humanitarian stakeholders, with the massive influx of IDPs and returnees in addition to the local communities of Al Bab and Afrin areas, numerous challenges remain to be addressed to provide adequate response considering the scale of needs of Al Bab and Afrin populations, especially regarding specialized MHPSS services. Barriers persist in terms of access to specialized MHPSS services for all populations of concern since response provided mainly focuses on a facility-based approach, with beneficiaries benefiting the few services they know of and which are accessible to them such as awareness sessions and PSS activities for children which are provided by trained and non specialized persons who tend to either leave cases in need for specialized interventions or refer them to other service providers in other areas such as Azaz area. According to the survey conducted by UOSSM MEAL team in Al Bab area:Al Bab host communities are approximately 61,725 people with approximately 68,625 IDPs. In addition, Al Bab city has two camps: • Turkish Vakfi Camp which consists of 110 tents and contains 475 people, 98 families all are IDPs coming from Al Ghota.• Doyof Asharqia Camp (the largest camp in Al Bab); it contains 2480 people, 585 families all are IDPs from Al Ghota and few IDPs from Homs. the survey shows that Al Bab community contains:- 6 PHC centers (Free of charge) - 3 Supported Hospitals: Al-Hekmah – The new Hospital – Al-Farabi (Free of charge)- 3 Private Hospitals: Al-Majd – Al-Fateh – Assalam (Not Free of charge)- UOSSM Ambulance system (Free of charge)- SRD physical therapy center (Free of charge) Also, according to the survey, it is noticeably that Al Bab community has no specialized MHPSS services except for 2 nonspecialized centers available in the area: o Qyam foundation, which provides cultural lectures and cultural courses, supports Al-Qalem private school where they provide PSS for students and sometimes free of charge open days targeting kids from both the IDPs and the host community. The foundation does not have any CHWs nor PSWs. o Baitona special needs center which provides services to help with (Autism – Dyslexia and delay speech – Physical therapy for children with special needs – Down syndrome), with social and educational activities to help children integrate with the society.According to the WHO Needs Assessment Report / Aleppo Governorate – Afrin District August 2018:• The total population of areas out of governance control are approximately 4.5 Million (HNO 2018).• The estimated number of MH and Substance Use Disorder (SUD) patients is 11.64% * 4.5 Million = 523,800.• On Average 2%-5% out of total MH patients, except substance use disorder, need a hospitalization, keeping them in a health unit for one to three weeks on average till recovery (Dr. Satto, MHPSS Coordinator, UOSSM-North region).• On average 30%-35% out of total substance use disorder patients are addicted and need a hospitalization.• Each MH-SUD Center includes two units MH and SUD, which can serve 720 patients in a hospitalization section and 4,400 in OP mh-units in a year; the total number of beneficiaries is 5,120.By analyzing the previous values and indicators, the overall needed MH-SUD Centers can be obtained as following:  Needed MH-SUD Centers = Total Number of patients / MH-SUD Center beneficiaries.  Needed MH-SUD Centers = 523,800 / 5,210 = 102.  On average, only 5 to 7 percentage of these patients accept to visit thus kind of services due many reasons; the main reason is due reputation. Implementation of activities

The Acute MH Unit will contain:• A Psychiatrist: who will provide psychiatric management by prescribe, direct, or administer psychotherapeutic interventions or medications to manage mental, emotional, or behavioral disorders. And also to supervise the MHGAP doctors. • 3 MHGAP Doctors: who will provide assessment and diagnosis according to the mental health disorder’s signs and symptoms presented by the cases referred to him/her by the psychosocial workers, PSWs. In addition, he/she will refer severe and more serious cases to the psychiatrist in the acute MH unit. • 7 Psychiatric Nurses: who will follow up the intervention process for the patients in the hospital since the intake and during the residency reaching the discharge. • 1 Chief Psychiatric Nurse: who will be responsible for the psychiatric nurses’ team by training - coaching – supervision in addition to coordination between the management and administrative parts. • 4 Psychosocial Workers (PSWS): 2 PSWs will be based in the acute MH unit and provide the intake assessment for cases in the outpatient clinic and refer the cases in need to the MHGAP doctors. In addition, they will follow up cases referred to them by the MHGAP doctors in addition to provide psychoeducation, awareness sessions for patients, caregivers and families. And 2 PSWs for the mobile team who will reach out accessible and reachable IDPs camps and schools and home visits and facilitate PSS activities for children and adolescences. The acute unit will contain 20 beds for severe and acute cases, all cases will be above 15 years old. The hospitalization period arranges from 15 days to one month. The Fixed MH Unit will contain:• A General Practitioner doctor trained on MHGAP program, who will provide: mental health services starting with- Providing assessment according to the mental health disorder’s signs and symptoms presented by the cases.- Providing a proper diagnosis of the cases according to the mental health disorders which are addressed in the MH GAP program (depression, epilepsy, psychosis…. Etc.) - Providing the proper interventions as they are addressed in MHGAP program including specific kinds of psychotropic medications when necessary.• 3 Psychosocial workers (PSWs): who will provide:o One PSW based in the MH unit provides follow-up the MHGAP doctor clients and patients in individual or group sessions, and provides consultations under supervision of the MHGAP doctor.o A mobile team of two psychosocial workers to reach out accessible and reachable IDPs camps and schools and home visits as well, and facilitate PSS activities for children and adolescences. In each, the acute MH unit in Al Bab and the fixed MH unit in Afrin, there will be:• Data Entry: who will be responsible for the patients’ information, coding the files and keeping them in a safe place. • Unit HR Assistant: Responsible for helping the HR Organizing & Development Coordinator• Meal Assistant: will help on implementing the monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning system within the project, data collection with verification and evidences support, drafting reports and filling tools, sharing them with the designated MEAL officer. • Unit Accounts Assistant: Responsible for assisting admin accounts coordinator. • Safety and Security Assistant: who will Submit periodic and emergency reports collected from regional safety and security coordinators and inspect and monitor implementation of security and safety policies and procedures.• Service Staff: who will be Responsible for cleaning the offices and bathrooms. • 2 Guards: who will conduct visitors` control and pay attention to the area around the office.The project will be supervised and followed up by the MHPSS Coordinator in addition to the MHPSS Regional Officer who will be based in the Units.

Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: high, effect: highMitigating Measures: To mitigate and prevent these issues as possible, there will be separated rooms, for men and women, in both the acute MH unit and the fixed MH unit. Also, the waiting area, in the outpatient units will be separated, a part for men and a part for women and both will be controlled and monitored by the receptionists. In addition, for baths and toilets will be also isolated and separated for men and women and also for the staff. The acute MH unit and the fixed MH unit will be also provided by a complaint box reached by beneficiaries. Above all, all the staff members will sign a code of conduct. For further control and supervision of the violence, gender and exploitation issues, the role of M&E will be activated during the project cycle. Also, there will be a client satisfaction survey conducted each three months by the PSWs for beneficiaries. 2- Neglecting the most vulnerable (children, women, the elderly and the disabled): possibility: moderate, effect: highMitigating Measures: Taking into consideration the context and the environment roles, traditions and customs, the staff in both sites will be mixed of the two genders, males and females. Also, the working hours in the units, especially outpatient units will be during daylight which enable women reach the services available. In addition, the building will be equipped by facilities for disabled beneficiaries such as ramp, wheelchair and modified toilets. Above all, the PSWs in both sites will facilitate PSS activities targeting children and adolescences in schools and community centers in the area on a weekly or biweekly basis.3- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: moderate, effect: highMitigating Measures: All the services provided in both sites will be based on humanitarian perspectives, UNHCR Humanitarian Principles, for both host communities and IDPs, and to achieve this goal many things will be taken into accounts; the location and place of the units will be safe and accessible for beneficiaries. Also, activation of coordination and collaboration with humanitarian stakeholders and other service providers in the targeted areas regarding referral pathways. In addition,to activate the participation of the local communities in the area, a better understanding of its mental health problems should be reached, and that’s can be achieved by an outreach activities starting by conducting focus group discussions FGDs within the community tackling their challenges and problems they are facing in terms of mental health, accordingly and based on the FGDs outcomes, an information/education /communication IEC materials developed and regularly distributed by the psychosocial workers PSWs illustrating the services provided in both units. In addition to facilitate awareness sessions for children and adolescences at schools or community centers available in the areas. Also, awareness campaigns are conducted for families and caregivers on mental health concerns.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Since UOSSM is already registered in Turkey, it has a governmental validation from the Turkish Ministry of Health for the implementation of its activities inside Syria especially in the areas controlled by the Turkish Government, this will facilitate access to work in Al Bab and Afrin areas and it is also maintained in an acceptable security conditions since both areas are stabilized, managed and controlled by the Turkish Government. In addition, based on the services and interventions UOSSM provide inside Syria since 2012, local authorities, service providers and humanitarian stakeholders are familiar with those services leading to effective collaboration and coordination. Above all, since it is already acting inside Syria in many locations and areas, UOSSM has developed security management measures to limit its staff exposure to risk and also for proper respond regarding any unexpected risks as the following:• A Security Manager who has an extensive network of contacts in the areas of interventions allowing to collect information and to issue rapid alerts. • The Security Team also supervise the security monitoring of the targeted areas and regularly conduct evaluations upon the need. • Regular trainings for staff acting inside Syria by the Security Team to build their capacity and raise their knowledge to anticipate, mitigate and react to potential security threats• Moreover, exit strategy developed by the Technical & Operational Team for each project.
