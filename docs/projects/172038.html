Project Summary

The project aims at strengthening protection of the conflict-affected population including through the physical presence of human rights monitors in conflict-affected areas, which could deter further human rights violations and serve in itself as a protection mechanism. By engaging directly with victims and witnesses of human rights violations, this will allow to refer victims to life saving humanitarian services, promote an environment that is conducive to the promotion and respect for human rights and contribute to protect the humanitarian space.
A stable presence of the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine (HRMMU) in the conflict-affected areas on both sides of the contact line would allow for an establishment of continuous monitoring of the situation with rights of the conflict-affected population with geographical coverage throughout the Donetsk and Luhansk regions, including the ability to cover the grey zones.
The analysis stemming out of the human rights monitoring will be utilised by key humanitarian actors, and thus contribute to shape their humanitarian response to the protection needs of the conflict affected population. A human rights approach including gender analysis to the humanitarian response is vital where the population is suffering due to lack of protection and failure of accountability mechanisms.

1 - Needs

More than six years since its onset, the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to cause deaths, injuries and damage to civilian objects, and to disrupt civilian life in general, affecting 5.2 million people across the country (those resident in the conflict zone and IDPs). Continued increase in conflict-related civilian deaths and injuries (131 from 1 January to 31 October 2020) reflects the volatile situation and sustained atmosphere of insecurity and anxiety. The conflict amplified existing human rights challenges with serious human rights violations including arbitrary detention, torture and ill treatment, conflict-related sexual violence that continue to be recorded on both sides of the contact line. As a result, access to socio-economic rights as well as to justice and accountability has deteriorated. In that context, human rights monitoring is critical to document human rights violations, to identify critical human rights violations and subsequent need of protection services and design protection/humanitarian responses, including remedy and reparation to victims. There is a need for a stable OHCHR presence in the field. Field offices will make it logistically easier for the population to access the Monitoring Mission as it can reach out to the most remote or marginalized parts of society. HRMMU’s field presence will be able to maintain a direct and regular contact with local authorities and greater access to a larger number of communities, which can enable prompt local responses. It also enables the establishment of more systematic communication with both the humanitarian community and the authorities to be used for advocacy for change. The following groups need to be paid particular attention: women and men in conflict-related detention; women and men at risk of torture and ill-treatment, including conflict-related sexual violence; older men and women, and boys and girls whose social and economic rights have been impacted by the conflict, especially those resident in the isolated communities along the contact line; women and girls living in close proximity to military personnel (risk of sexual exploitation); women and men, girls and boys resident near the contact line being at high risk of shelling, SALW fire and of mines and ERW (where men and boys often at higher risk); human rights defenders and journalists in areas lacking rule of law and where space for civil society and freedom of expression are limited.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

1) Civilian Casualty recording (deaths and injuries with gender and age disaggregated data) to advocate and inform parties to the conflict on measures to be taken and their obligations as per International Humanitarian Law (IHL), and to inform humanitarian actors of where the needs are greatest, e.g. need for Mine Risk Education in areas with high number of casualties due to ERWs. 2) Recording of damage to civilian infrastructure (except for civilian housing). 3) Promotion of remedy and reparation to civilian victims of the conflict through the adoption of relevant legislation, administrative mechanisms and budget allocations. 4) Daily interviews, field visits, monitoring and reporting of the human rights situation contributing to protection by presence. Identifying particular risks of men and women will be part of the monitoring work. 5) Utilising the findings from the monitoring to track trends; informing advocacy actions, early warning and providing referrals to the humanitarian community for a follow up. This includes both Government-controlled territory and territory controlled by the armed groups. 6) Utilizing the findings to raise awareness of human rights violations among national stakeholders and parties to the conflict, the UN system and broader international community. Supporting human rights approach in humanitarian coordination mechanisms, and working to inform humanitarian actors of the human rights situation allowing humanitarian actors to respond to needs of persons whose rights have been violated. 7) Addressing relevant authorities with urgent interventions such as arbitrary detention, enforced disappearances, and torture and ill-treatment, including conflict-related sexual violence. 8) Developing capacity of local stakeholders to identify and address human rights violations and vulnerabilities and how to address such challenges. Expected outcomes: 1) Government of Ukraine, armed groups of the self-proclaimed ‘Donetsk people’s republic’ and ‘Luhansk people’s republic’, civil society counterparts, the UN and wider international community are better informed of the human rights situation in the east of Ukraine, in particular in the Donetsk and Luhansk regions. 2) Increased understanding and capacity of the civil society counterparts and authorities to address human rights violations; Government of Ukraine, armed groups of the self-proclaimed ‘Donetsk people’s republic’ and ‘Luhansk people’s republic’ and civil society counterparts have better understanding of accountability mechanisms and the conduct of effective investigations of most egregious human rights violations, including conflict related GBV and sexual exploitation. 3) Humanitarian actors use human rights analysis as a basis for designing a response with human rights based approach to the protection needs of the conflict-affected population. 4) A reduction in human rights violations due to the overall work of the HRMMU and its partners, e.g. individual follow-up on cases leading to the release of arbitrarily detained persons, or advocacy based on civilian casualty recording leading to decrease in civilian casualties. 5) National mechanisms for remedy and reparation to civilian victims of the conflict have been further developed.

3 - Indicators & targets

1) 5 interviews/monitoring visits per week (total: 260); 2) 5 individual follow-ups with authorities and referrals to humanitarian partners per week (total: 260); 3) 2 periodic and 2 thematic reports on the human rights situation in Ukraine in 2021; 4) 1 monthly meeting with humanitarian community per office / 12 x 4 offices (total: 48); 5) 1 monthly meeting/ 12 x 4 offices with authorities and parties to the conflict (total: 48); 6) monthly civilian casualty updates (total: 12); 7) 1 bi-monthly meeting x 4 offices to strengthen local capacity (total: 24); 8) 5 changes to policies/institutional frameworks related to rights on matters where advocacy have been conducted.
