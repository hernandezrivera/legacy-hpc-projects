Project Summary

The Back to School Campaign through provision of food aims to encourage return and resumption to education in areas affected by natural disasters, with the aim of reducing barriers in access to primary school for boys and girls while supporting their families to meet their basic food needs at home. Targeted beneficiaries will receive fortified wheat flour of 25 kg (bimonthly) as take-home ration, conditional upon enrolment and regular attendance at schools or temporary learning centers notified by the Government. The project will be implemented in collaboration with Education and Literacy Department, Government of Sindh and target 71,480 children (35,740 boys & 35,740 girls) enrolled in primary boys’ and girls’ schools with 3,574 MT of Wheat Flour in affected areas of districts Badin and Sajawal, Sindh. The project is in alignment with the objectives of Sindh Education Sector Plan and CM’s forthcoming Bokh Mitao Programme and will implemented in areas where WFP’s other interventions (nutrition, food assistance, DRR and social protection) are ongoing.

Justification:
According to UNDP’s latest Report on Multi-dimensional Poverty in Pakistan the incidence and severity of multi-dimensional poverty is high in the southern part of the province. For instance, approximately 84% of peoples live in multi-dimensional poverty in Badin while it is 68% for Sajawal district. Similarly, according to RNA 2020, 72% of assessed population in Badin and 63% in Sajawal reported inability to buy food and in the case of any emergency the average food stock for their own consumption may last for 5 days only. These districts also host many out of schoolboys and girls of age 5-16 years, and school completion rates are drastically low. Displacement, school damages and discontinuity of educational activities resulting from floods and other disasters further deteriorates education situation in the affected areas.
Disasters’ negative impact on lives, livelihoods, livestock, agriculture, infrastructure, access to social and life-saving services and income generation opportunities is significantly high on the children, women, disabled and elderly peoples. Among others, the incidences of less availability of food for children and women at home, engagement in labor and income generation activities, children dropout from school and early marriages of girls are common in most of the cases.
WFP’s experience of emergency response in Pakistan suggests that school feeding programmes can play an instrumental role in bringing children back to school while reducing their chances of drop out, engagement in child labor or early marriages. Provision of take-home rations- a means to transfer resources to vulnerable households and subsidize families’ food basket, motivate families to send and retain their children in schools and hence, contribute to increase enrolment, retention and attendance at schools.
Effectiveness of school feeding is evident from flood response in 2011-12. WFP field monitoring reports of school feeding under indicated that almost 97 percent of students were retained in schools in Sindh. Similarly, analysis of education indicators in WFP assisted vs non-assisted schools in Erstwhile FATA showed that enrolment rates increased at 15% in WFP assisted schools as compared to 3% in non-assisted schools. Moreover, the retention rate in WFP-assisted primary schools in 2016-2017 was as high as 80.1 %, against WFP’s target of 70 % (compared to 24% in all primary schools in erstwhile FATA).

1- Project Activities

The overall objective of the intervention is to improve return and resumption to schools by providing food as safety nets for boys and girls of age group 5-11 years, belong to poorest and disasters affected families. The specific objectives are:• To increase daily attendance at schools • To maintain retention and reduce drop out rate• To bring out of school children in schools who were not enrolled or dropped out from targeted schools during last year• To contribute to maintain food consumption level of assisted children and their families The project will be implemented in collaboration with Education and Literacy Department, Government of Sindh with certain activities run jointly by the NGO partner and transporter for food supply chain management. A rapid need assessment of affected areas will be conducted to identify, select and cluster areas for project response. The priority will be given to the most vulnerable areas. In the case of damaged schools, intervention will also consider Temporary Learning Centers (TLCs) where scholastic activities is ongoing as project location. The project will target all boys and girls from grade 1 to 5 enrolled in Government boys’ and girls’ primary schools and assist with 50 kg fortified wheat flour in two cycles during the project. The assistance is conditional upon 20 days attendance in a month in targeted school. WFP will also organize community mobilization activities with help of Government and sister UN agencies, school management committees and training sessions on project modalities will be organized for effective implementation of the project. Project will be integrated with WFP’s ongoing interventions and education sector partners projects ongoing in the targeted areas and all the stakeholders and development partners will also be taken onboard for integration, synergies, complementarity and maximum impact of the project.WFP ensures accountability to the affected population through established beneficiary feedback desk (BFB) WFP Country Office to receive complaints/observations directly from beneficiaries and individuals regarding the project activities. Information of this mechanism will be provided, and banners will be placed at all locations to ensure that all the beneficiaries are informed of their entitlements and have access to approach WFP for any feedback. The project is carefully designed so as not to create harm or exacerbate the existing vulnerabilities of the beneficiaries. The intervention is primarily designed for implementation in schools where children do not have to travel distances to get WFP assistance and face safety and security risks. Project implementation also prioritizes dignity, self-esteem and safety of the beneficiaries through targeting of all schools and children falling under project selection criteria regardless of the race, gender, religion and political affiliation. Similarly, project interventions are based on human rights-based approach and linkages with international and national legal frameworks including Convention on the Rights of the Child (OUNHCHR, 1989), and constitutional obligations of the Government of Pakistan. It contributes to the implementation of Article 25-A, 37-A and 37-B of the Constitution of Islamic Republic of Pakistan. The project objectives are in alignment with the Government priorities for education sector in the area and WFP will further advocate with the Government and partners for sustainability and continuation of the project activities.
