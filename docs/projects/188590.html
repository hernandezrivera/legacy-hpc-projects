Project Summary

Overall, the Food Security Sector (FSS) estimates that 12 million people in Syria are facing acute food insecurity, which is at least 55.3% of Syria population. While the food insecure figures have slightly declined by at least 4% compared to last year’s record increase, the number of people facing acute food insecurity remains extremely high and 51% higher than in 2019, pre-Lebanese financial crisis period and before the COVID-19 outbreak. Moreover, drought-like conditions in Syria are affecting agriculture production capacity, thus affecting farmer’s livelihoods and local food production. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 240, 000 small-scale farming households (equivalent to 1,440,000 people) in Syria, with the objective of protecting and enhancing the livelihoods of vulnerable farmers and IDPs through the restoration of critical irrigation infrastructures and improve water use efficiency to increase household food production and food security.
. The project outputs include:
• Output 1: Critical small-scale irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are assessed and light rehabilitation undertaken.
• Output 2: Capacity of technical staff and relevant stakeholders including farmers strengthened to increase water use efficiency and establishment of Water Users Associations (WUAs)
A training programme on enhancing irrigation management, water use efficiency in small-scale irrigation, agricultural land management will be based on FAO standards and approaches. It is expected that stakeholders and farmers will participate in the proposed trainings.
This project is expected to contribute to increasing access to irrigation water for crisis-affected farmers and their families in the targeted areas through the light rehabilitation of critical small scale irrigation infrastructure. Crop cultivation and other agricultural practices, including livestock and animal feed production, will consequently benefit from the improved water supply plans as an emergency response to the prevailing crisis and drought-like conditions. The proposed intervention will strengthen household food production capacities, skills and knowledge and address immediate food security and nutrition needs, through increasing local availability and supply of food. This may in turn promote flourishing of agricultural based livelihoods in the targeted rural communities, which would contribute to social cohesion, peace and stability. The project seeks to utilize intensive labour in order to maximize community participation and inject cash into the local community.

4 - Needs

Agricultural infrastructure has suffered extensive damage, theft and neglect since the conflict started in 2011. Persistent dry spells and drought has further worsened agricultural conditions. The result has been the disruption of large sections of the agriculture system with consequent substantial weakening of its components, including farmers who rely on irrigation systems. The Ministry of Water Resources (MWR) estimates that Syria’s average annual water requirement is about 17.743 billion m3, of which 15.613 billion m3 (88%) is required for agriculture. The average annual deficit (about 1.533 billion m3) increased to 5 billion m3 in the recent dry years. It is estimated that 25% of the irrigation infrastructures are damaged, especially in the major wheat producing areas. The water scarcity, damaged irrigation infrastructure and the low rainfall level has forced most farmers, especially in Rural Damascus and Rural Aleppo, to rely on surface wells to cover part of their irrigation needs. This will have negative impact on groundwater decline and depletion, leading to high pumping costs. The low production of wheat and other crops during the 2021 harvest season has resulted in a significant gap in supply and demand, mainly for wheat and other crops such as legumes. The wheat and barley production in 2021 harvest was much lower than the previous years in NWS, due to drought, reduction of the agriculture areas and lack of productive inputs to farmers. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiary selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example: IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation measures: Establish a community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do no harm principles, including GBV and PSEA risk mitigation measures. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because of the being selected for the project. Women and youth will be prioritized, inputs and trainings will take account of the different requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted so that re-settlement is supported. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well to limit possible friction between the two
