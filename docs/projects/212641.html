Project Summary

After 12 years of relentless conflict, Syria grapples with an ongoing humanitarian crisis, with 15.3 million people needing aid in 2024. In NWS, many of the population faces dire needs. The assessment highlights requirements in CCCM. The intervention focuses on reinforcing camp management committees, identifying rehabilitation activities, and offering income-generating activities and business start-up grants to enhance livelihoods. camps infrastructure facilities require rehabilitation due to bombings and EQ. There's a high demand for risk management and mitigation training and trained teams to respond to emergencies. Livelihood interventions are crucial to improve living conditions and support income-generating activities inside the camps, including women and PWDs in vocational training programs and start-up grants to improve livelihoods situation inside the camps.
Providing life-saving support to people in camps throw: Reduced vulnerability of displaced individuals (2000 families) through the provision of safe shelter units inclusive of basic humanitarian infrastructure and assistance for the duration of # months including:
Improve measures and light humanitarian infrastructure.
The camp's effective governance and management mechanisms.
Effective data and information management established ensuring women and men, boys and girls.
An inclusive and accessible Help Desk, confidential complaints, and feedback mechanism to report allegations of intimidation, coercion, violence sexual exploitation, and abuse experienced in receiving assistance are established.
The Main Activities: 20 camps are provided with light administrative humanitarian infrastructure rehabilitation (fire points (40), administration offices (20), and WASH facilities 60 latrine, 300 lighting poles. Conduct 40 IVT for 800 BNFs. Conduct 20 SME training 400 BNFs.
Start-up grant 300 grants.
POINT will form community committees within the targeted sites to enhance community engagement, participation, and social cohesion.
PRA: Gather data on existing protection risks.
Identify the most significant risks and vulnerabilities.
Assess the potential interaction with the project.
Safety audit for any risks related to protection and seeking to mitigate or confront them. For example, most of the roads inside the camps and between sectors do not have lighting, as well as toilet blocks, especially for women. These issues are considered risks that threaten the protection of IDP and therefore must be referred to service providers at the camp level according to each sector for immediate intervention.
PSEA strategy: Signing PSEA and CSG policy by all POINT staff and beneficiaries.
PSEA Sessions for all staff.
Assumptions:
A willingness by targeted individuals to participate in Informal Vocational Training (IVT) activities.
PWDs have the capacity and secure safe access to training facilities.
Work environment secures against Protection from PSEA and Child Safeguarding (CSG) issues.
Permission and approvals from concerned local authorities are obtained.

Clear communication about the IVT to manage expectations and sign agreements with them.
CRM system will be in place to ensure that all complaints are well addressed.
