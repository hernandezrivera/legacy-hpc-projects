Project Summary

Through this project, Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) is scaling up the provision of lifesaving WASH and Nutrition response in Tonj East, Tonj North and Rumbek North. The project will ensure community response will prevent children dying from common preventable illness, while addressing underline causes of mortality through improving WASH interventions. The project contributes to the reduction of morbidity and mortality from common communicable diseases and acute malnutrition, improve access to safe and clean water and improve hygiene and sanitation practice, while increasing access to protection and psychosocial support services. The services will provided through an integrated response approach delivering critical and life-saving assistance for populations in most critical WASH Severity Classification 4 and catastrophic WSC 5.
CCOSS will reach the most vulnerable populations with an integrated package of life-saving services that includes WASH and nutrition. Integrated WASH and nutrition interventions will focus on delivering a minimum package of services at the nutrition centers, at the household of patients, and in the community. Nutrition activities at household level include context specific WASH ‘small doable actions’ and establishing means for minimum standards of WASH. The WASH ‘small doable actions’ include handwashing with water and ash, disposal of faeces including child faeces and treatment and storage of water.
CCOSS programmes will be informed by a conflict-sensitive approach, with a strong focus on social cohesion, embedded in an analysis of socio-political-ethnic dynamics as the main drivers of violence. In its approach to service provision, CCOSS will be accountable to affected populations, will ensure equitable representation of men, boys, women and girls in all community feedback and complaint mechanisms, and will target the most vulnerable people, including internally displaced persons, survivors of gender-based violence, and persons with disabilities. Men, women, boys and girls will be engaged throughout the project cycle. We will secure appropriate means (community desks) for affected populations to provide feedback and to complain about their experiences and perspectives on our programmes and responses, including on sensitive issues. These mechanisms will ensure that information is regularly collected, analysed, integrated into decision-making and acted on, and that the results are communicated back to people in a meaningful dialogue.
The project will assess, identify and respond to protection risks and vulnerability of affected populations and communities by ensuring safe and reliable mechanisms are in place to access assistance and to raise concerns about sexual exploitation and abuse issues, and other protection issues such as gender based violence. The project staff have been trained on PSEA, the project volunteers such as hygiene promoters will also be trained on PSEA.
This project will target 211,789 individuals (Men: 52,591, Women: 42,735, Boys: 59,545 and Girls: 56,918) Tonj East and Tonj North Counties of Warrap State. Per county, the numbers are as follows:- Tonj East 140,149 individuals (Men: 35,892, Women: 24,596, Boys: 41,148 and Girls: 38,513) and Tonj North 71,640 individuals (Men:16,699, Women: 18,139, Boys: 18,397 and Girls: 18,405). 15% of the target population (31,768 individuals) will be Persons with Special Needs.
Prior to project implementation, CCOSS will conduct, focus group discussions (FGD) with women, men, boys and girls at targeted community settlements will be conducted to get feedback on design, implementation, and monitoring of sanitation facilities, ensuring safety and mitigation of Protection / GBV risks, especially for females.

1 - Needs

Warrap state has remained a priority location since the start of the conflict, continues to have a significant IDP population, and affected host population that remains vulnerable under the current circumstances. The convergence of multiple shocks in already vulnerable areas in 2021 could lead to severe outcomes. Crucial information gaps remain a challenge, with poor access to many parts of Warrap due to insecurity and inadequate infrastructure. Warrap state continues to remain one of the most difficult locations, especially for humanitarian agencies to operate. This is due to drivers and multipliers of crises that have remained present over time. These include insecurity and violence, local and inter-communal violence (IVC), ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, flooding, economic instability, and insecure access to food and livelihoods. Warrap state broadly faces a myriad of WASH-related challenges emanating from accessibility challenges by partners, the influx of IDPs as a result of forced displacement due to cattle raids from neighbouring Cuiebet that spills over the Warrap State border into Manyangok Payam. The majority of residents drink water from unprotected sources, almost no existence of pit latrines thus open defecation, while acute watery diarrhea is the main health challenge. Open defecation is a common practice of disposal of human excreta and the community knowledge on good hygiene practice is very low. This situation has exposed the populations to a high-level risk of vulnerability to water-related illnesses. Having to defecate in the open undermines the dignity and safety of women, children, and people living with disabilities, and poses them to high risk of violence and discrimination. During the rainy season, most counties in Warrap (Tonj East and Tonj North) are affected by heavy and flash floods which continue to strain access to the very limited basic resources. Flood-induced displacement limits access to WASH infrastructure and places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. The limited access to WASH services impacts people across all population groups, but the needs are the most severe among the newly displaced population, and in areas hosting recently returned refugees and IDPs Malnutrition has been a chronic concern in the communities of Warrap state, especially in Tonj East with persistently elevated levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) exceeding the emergency threshold of 15%. Studies indicate that these high rates are attributed to sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) among children, chronic food insecurity, increased seasonal incidences of diseases like malaria and diarrhea, access constraints to health and nutrition services, poor water, hygiene, and sanitation and social services. IDPs and Host Communities in these locations have the highest severity of needs. Within these population groups, some of the vulnerable groups that may have specific needs include children, women at risk, the elderly, people with disabilities, single-headed household members, and the extremely poor. High WASH severity can be attributed to a heavy reliance on surface water, lacking sanitation facilities, and the repeated presence of insecurity in these locations. Women and girls face an increased risk of harassment, assault, and sexual violence when collecting water, using communal latrines, and accessing menstrual hygiene products. Additionally, appropriate and dignified washing locations remain critical needs.

2 - Project activities

a. Conduct focused group discussions at targeted community settlements to get complaints feedback and inputs on appropriate design, implementation, and monitoring of project activities. During these sessions, views of women, girls, boys, men including persons with disabilities will be taken into consideration.b. Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to nutrition facilities through rehabilitation of non-functioning boreholes, and provision of handwashing facilities equipped with water and soap after conducting gender audits. c. Provide adequate portable water and soap to OTP/TSFP mobile sites at each distribution d. Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water ) in communities with high GAM and WASH vulnerability through rehabilitation of non-functioning boreholes, and provision of handwashing facilities equipped with water and soap after conducting gender audits. e. Provide timely WASH NFIs kits to caretaker/SAM child f. Conduct hygiene awareness campaigns each month of implementation to improve hygiene practices with focus on behaviour change communication WASH-related diseases. The awareness shall also provide knowledge on appropriate behaviours to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases and encourage good hygiene practices. g. Conduct training for nutrition and health workers on basic knowledge on WASH related diseases including transmission routes h. Promote, demonstrate and facilitate appropriate household water treatment and storage. These includes; sun exposure for 8 hours, boiling water, store drinking water in a clean container inside the house, cover the drinking water container and use a narrow necked container for water. i. Promote and facilitate appropriate handwashing behaviours such as handwashing with water and ash (or soap when available), and use of tipitap at household levelj. Promote and facilitate appropriate disposal of feces of babies, other household members. this will be through use of the ‘cat’ method, dig and dumb method or constructing local pit latrines

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Complaint /feedback mechanism will be agreed upon between CCOSS and the project beneficiaries and established prior to commencement of the project. Monitoring of the complaints and remedial action will be documented during the project implementation cycle. This will be done with the goal of promoting community engagement and accountability. The field project team will act upon complaints and feedback received immediately. Community participation in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of the post-distribution will also be solicited. For instance, the community will be involved in the identification and selection of community volunteers to help in the distribution of WASH NFIs. After the completion of the distribution, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the relief commodities, as well as to collect further

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Complaint and Feedback desks will be set in strategic places in the community. Some of these desks will be manned by some community members who will assist other community members about the issues they want to raise. Illiterate community members will be assisted with those who are able to read and right by documenting their complaints and feedback. Households with members with disabilities will be visited and their complaints and feedback received. CCOSS will also train staff, community leaders and community members on AAP methodology. This will ensure that AAP/CCE will be incorporated in all the stages of the project cycle. The feedback received will be incorporated in future organization plans.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will assess, identify and respond to protection risks and vulnerability of affected populations and communities by ensuring safe and reliable mechanisms are in place to access assistance and to raise concerns about sexual exploitation and abuse issues, and other protection issues such as gender-based violence. The project staff have all been trained on PSEA and signed up to the Code of Conduct. The project volunteers such as hygiene promoters will also be trained on PSEA. The information on PSEA will then be incorporated and disseminated to the community by the hygiene promoters

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Prior to project implementation, Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan will conduct, focus group discussions with Persons with Disabilities (PWDs), women, men, boys, and girls at targeted community settlements to get feedback on the design, implementation, and monitoring of sanitation facilities, ensuring safety and mitigation of Gender-Based Violence risks, especially for females. These focus group discussions will also be utilized to trigger community action towards poor hygiene practices such as not washing hands. At least 15% of the target beneficiaries will be Persons with Disabilities. Through these community engagement forums, the project beneficiaries and other key stakeholders will be informed about the project.
