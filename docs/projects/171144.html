Project Summary

This project aims to ensure that there is increased equitable access to health services at health facility, at community level and through integrated outreach activities conducted by mobile teams among IDP and Host (rural and urban) populations in South Central Somalia. The project design takes on a targeted approach with a focus on: (1). Control of communicable illnesses (diarrhea, acute respiratory illnesses malaria and measles). (2). Integrated management of common childhood illnesses (Eye, ear, skin, worm infections, anaemia and chronic childhood diseases) (3). Expand access to EPI, contributing to control of vaccine preventable diseases (4). Expanded access to reproductive healthcare services for women of reproductive age. (5). Community Disease surveillance and response – An emergency Rapid response component is built in to allow quick scale up of lifesaving health services (e.g. through CTCs/ORT corners/mobile ORT and isolation wards) during rapid onset emergencies. (6). Increasing access to Emergency Primary Health Care including managing non communicable diseases (diabetes) and mental, neurological and substance use (MNS) disorders at OPD level. (7) Strengthening psychosocial support services through locally recruited and incentivized front line psychosocial workers who will be trained on psychological first aid, basic counselling skills and WHO- Problem Management plus (PM+) intervention. (8) Provision of community based mental health services and referral of people with mental health conditions to the health facilities to ensure they receive key psychosocial wellbeing awareness messages.
The service will be provided across all supported health centers. In this way, IMC aims to contribute to reduction in mortality, human suffering and socio-economic effects among disaster and conflict affected populations in Somalia. IMC proposes to continue to use a multi-layered approach to mitigate excess morbidity and mortality in south-central Somalia. It includes: (1) Bringing health services closer to the communities to address the urgent health needs of conflict and disaster affected and other vulnerable population in south-central Somalia through uninterrupted delivery of free, high quality integrated health, nutrition, protection and WASH services. (2) Positively influencing individual, household and community behavior change to boost the absorptive and adaptive capacity of vulnerable people to manage implications of risks/shocks through innovative culturally acceptable interventions. (3) Strengthening local capacity to respond to emergency primary health needs in South Central Somalia by working with community structures like health committees and coordinating with other humanitarian actors and the MOH Somalia to complement and cover gaps in health, nutrition, protection, and WASH services. (4) Improving capacity of non-specialized general health care staff by providing training in selected priority mental, neurological and substance use disorders using WHO’s mhGAP–Humanitarian Intervention and WHO- Problem Management plus (PM+) Guides. (5) Enhancing collaboration with stakeholders at the local level to improve coordination, prudent use of resources and increasing synergy between various actors for the maximum benefit of the target communities. (6) Provision of community based mental health services and referral of people with mental health conditions to the health facilities to ensure they receive key psychosocial wellbeing awareness messages. (7) Identifying the gender excluded and the reason for their exclusion from access to services and opportunities in the Health sector, designing policy and/or project-level responses that attempt to address the barriers in the project cycle implementation, carry out monitoring and evaluation (M&E) to check whether planned resources and actions have reached women, men, boys, girls, the poor and all the excluded and adjusting/redesigning on continuous basis basing on M&E findings.

Project Activities

1. Provide diagnosis and treatment and necessary follow-up of common illnesses including Mental Health & psychosocial support, in women, men, boys and girls form both IDP and host populations. 2. Ensure access to skilled birth attendance through quality Basic Emergency Obstetric Services (BEmONC) for women of reproductive age. 3. Provide training and ongoing supportive supervision to male and female healthcare workers at facility and community level on integrated management of childhood illnesses. 4. Expand coverage of vaccination in children aged 5 years and below. 5. Deploy male and female CHWs to facilitate health education/promotion at health facilities and FHWs at community level. 6. Improve access to vital ANC and PNC services at community level to improve early access to maternal and reproductive healthcare. 7. Form representative (gender, clan, IDP and host balanced) community health committees/health center committees to provide oversight of implementation activities. 8. Respond to disease outbreaks through stand-by emergency rapid response teams and mobilize community to respond to mitigate impact of disease outbreaks. 8. Ensure gender inclusion in project design, implementation, reporting and closure, e.g. via organizing different stake holders meeting (from men, women, youth (boys & girls), elderly) in the life cycle of the projects.

Mitigation Measures

On the risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.), IMC will enhance protection mainstreaming in health care services. IMC through its Community Health workers (CHW), Female Health workers (FHW) and Community Liaison focal person will engage community stake holders on its available services and as well enhance community education, mobilization and awareness on available Protection services in its areas of operation. IMC as well via its other funding sources will ensure referral pathways is available for protection survivors. On the risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities; IMC will engage health center committee, community elders, local authority and other relevant stake holders in project design, implementation and closure as well as in response to emergencies. For beneficiaries with disabilities and or with limited mobility, mobile health and nutrition teams will make weekly home visits. Those needing transport for specialized treatment will be transported using IMC ambulances. IMC will ensure there is adequate integration and linkages with Nutrition/WASH/Protection services in the targeted areas to minimize distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services. Standby Emergency Rapid Response Teams (ERRTs) comprising of key staff will be maintained to enable quick respond to trauma emergency/outbreaks including cholera, AWD, measles when a threshold is attained. Beneficiaries seeking treatment regardless of age, sex, clan affiliation, workplace and household will be treated equally and in line with national treatment protocols and guidelines. Different sociodemographic groups will be consulted on their specific health needs. During treatment and mobile outreach, beneficiaries with disabilities & other special needs, will be identified and given priority. All health facilities will have ramp/easy access paths to accommodate persons with impaired mobility. Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive (use of visual aids) to ensure that all beneficiaries know about health and mobile outreach times/sites. CHW and Mobile Health and Nutrition and Health Facility teams will be recruited from beneficiary communities and will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, marginalized ethnic groups). IMC will form gender and ethnic group balanced Community Health Committees/Health Centre Committees to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes, promoting a sense of ownership and improving the relevance and sustainability of the health services. On the risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, IMC maintains a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained and logged. Throughout the project cycle, IMC will ensure that it communicates with affected populations in formats and media that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate.

Please provide further explanation

This project links directly to Strategic objective 5 of the Somalia Resilience and Recovery framework to improve basic service delivery. The project is modelled along the Ministry of Health EPHS framework of tiered health service provision and follows, Somali Treatment Guidelines. Sustainability is built in to the project through integration of community health committees and health centre committees who provide community oversight of health activities implemented.
