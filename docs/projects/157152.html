Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the reduction of malnutrition cases and preventing malnutrition depletion of vulnerable communities through responding to immediate Health and nutrition needs of the target population in South Darfur,East Darfur , Blue Nile and South Kordofan States. The main objective of the project is to contribute to improve health and Nutrition status of vulnerable conflict-affected populations including Children under five years Old boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women as well as general wellbeing in South Darfur, East Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan States through integrating health and nutrition interventions. In nutrition, a Stabilization Center and several OTPs will be established, MUAC screening will be supported and the local capacities will be improved through training of male and female volunteers in CMAM establishment of mother support groups to promote IYCF, as well as the awareness of the target population will be increased through development and distribution of IEC/BCC materials particularly on IYCF to men and women. Health interventions include support to health and nutrition facilities through provision with facilities, drugs and equipment and strengthening of the available capacities and systems. The capacities of female and male Community Health Workers and Community Health Promoters will be built for both male and female to assist in increasing the communities’ awareness and enhance service provision. As well as surveillance, early warning and rapid response systems will be strengthened to serve both gender.
World Vision Sudan strategy is to increase coverage and access to quality primary health care and nutrition services in the project area, while improving and consolidating the overall quality and ensuring continuity of those services that are already being provided at established health centers. World Vision Sudan places a strong emphasis on capacity building and training of national staff, and empowerment of communities in order to ensure sustainability of its actions.
During project period of December January 2019 to December 2019, World Vision Sudan plans to continue lifesaving Nutrition activities in the above stated areas as well as expand community based management of Health and malnutrition through prevention, integrated Community case management and improved access to quality treatment .
World Vision will work in collaboration with State Ministry of Health and Federal Ministry of Health at state and national level respectively.
In addition, World Vision Sudan is also working towards empowerment of families and communities through strengthening & capacity building of community health workers/networks in health promotion and prevention for both male and female
In all of its activities, World Vision Sudan works to promote adherence to the national standards and policy frameworks established by the Ministry of Health.

Needs

Health:The protracted crisis in Sudan and inadequate funding have had a major impact on all levels of health care, especially in Darfur, South Kordofan ,Blue Nile and ED states.About 36 per cent of the Primary Health Care (PHC) facilities across Sudan is not fully functional, either due to staff shortages or poor physical infrastructure. Only 24 per cent of functional health facilities offer all main service components of the PHC package (42 per cent in Darfur is due to NGO support). (HNO 2018)Sudan has experienced high levels of violence and unrest, particularly in Darfur. Extending coverage of a minimum basic package of primary health care services (BPPHS) to facilities in all Sudan is a need that will be addressed by the Health Sector in coming years. Continuing population displacement from neighboring countries and access constraints will likely increase the need for strong preparedness and timely response to communicable disease outbreaks including measles, acute watery diarrhoea, malaria , hepatitis E and scabies. Maternal and infant mortality will continue to be a priority problem particularly among vulnerable populations especially under five boys and girl children and pregnant and lactating women.In addition, the prevalence of non-communicable diseases is on the rise. The AWD outbreak that started in August 2016 in Kassala state has spread to all 18 states in Sudan. Only a third of the population has an adequate number of midwives providing reproductive health services, and there was no full vaccine coverage of Penta 3 Vaccines. (HNO 2018)The sector needs to ensure continued access to basic and life-saving primary healthcare bakeges and referral services to girls, boys, men and women, while improving maternal and child services in localities with significant health needs.Absence of functional community based health services, poor education and information of communities on communicable and other diseases creates huge gaps on health seeking behavior and best practice of personal and environmental hygieneNutrition:Malnutrition being underlying contributing factor in 45% of child deaths (WHO fact sheet No.178) According to 2014 S3M report, prevalence of GAM in South Darfur (15.9%) is above WHO emergency threshold with El Salaam even worse at 29.1% and Katayla 22.3%. Severe Acute Malnutrition is 10.4% in El salaam and 4.5% in Katayla, Half (47.2%) the children in Al Salaam are stunted while Katayla has 26.2% stunting. High under-nutrition burden is further exacerbated by internal conflict cycles in the region given the increased risk created by population displacement and disruption of essential health and WASH services. The burden for treatment of acute malnutrition has grown country wide. In 2014, 70% (UNICEF data 2014) of children needing treatment for SAM did not receive it due to lack of services. Children with SAM have a ten times higher risk of death with case fatality rate for untreated SAM being up to 50% (2003 WHO Guideline
