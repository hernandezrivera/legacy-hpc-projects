Project Summary

South Sudan enters in its 5th year of conflict, the humanitarian crisis that suffers the country continues intensifying and expanding: violence is increasing and the economic decline threaten health, security and subsistence of the population. The conflict has had a major impact in the health system of Jonglei State, affecting the wellbeing of the population, both host and IDP population live together and mixed in the community and therefore both of them will benefit equally of the project.
South Sudan has a fertility rate of more than 4 births per woman, and 90 percent of women give birth without the assistance of a skilled professional. In 2013, the average neonatal mortality rate was 39 per 1,000 while the infant mortality rate was 64.1 per 1,000 live births . Much of women and girls give birth at home. Inadequate child spacing, poor feeding habits and the lack of food affect the general health of the community; this situation is similar in Jonglei State
In South Sudan, only 22% of the country’s healthcare facilities are operational and the absence of services contributes to morbidity and mortality. The ongoing conflict and insecurity limit access to medical assistance and hamper the timely treatment and control of disease. Women, who are in a subordinate position, are particularly affected. The conflict has exposed the Jonglei State population to multiple protection risks at home and within communities. Women and girls can be kidnapped, injured and/or killed, or become victims of gender-based violence (GBV) including sexual violence; men and boys are at risk of being recruited by armed groups, detained and even killed.
Doctors of the World-Médecins du Monde (MdM), in close partnership with the Ministry of Health (MoH), has been supporting the Bor State Hospital (reference facility for the whole Jonglei State) since November 2017, reinforcing maternity and paediatric services. Yet it remains short-staffed, in need of technical support and capacity strengthening as well as medical supplies. The regular needs assessments carried out by MdM outline the need to strengthen sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services as well as children health services in terms of capacity building, resources, coordination and supervision.
MdM is the main health organization in Bor South and the only one present at the state hospital, this means that the organization has direct access to a vulnerable population, victims of gender-based violence and abuse, and that it can easily establish a coordination system with other organizations related to protection and violence issues, as well as offer clinical management of situations of gender violence, always including the MHPSS component.

Needs

Two assessement missions were carried out along 2018. One in River Bank area (hard to reach area). The second assessement focused on PHC, MHPSS and Gender needs in Bor South and Pibor counties in Jonglei state, to determine the needs in these areas and formulate this proposal.

Activities

The Project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality of women and children affected by the conflict in Jonglei State. To do so, the Project will reduce barriers that prevent vulnerable women and girls from accessing and receiving quality SRH services and will support children health services. It is also important to reinforce their capacities to overcome the consequences of facing great violence as a key to their survival To achieve these goals, the Project will implement several activities.First of all, in order to increase access to SRH services, the capacity of health institutions to provide quality and timely services to women will be strengthened with sufficient, operational and trained staff. Importantly, services provided by MdM will be free of charge to enhance universal health access and coverage for every person and community, without discrimination, offering adequate and quality health services, with special attention to vulnerable groups of population. 1. Comprehensive SRH services including obstetric and newborn care services, family planning, counselling and referrals. 2. Care for survivors of sexual violence, GBV and violence in general will be improved. Activities will also include clinical and psychosocial care for sexual gender-based violence (SGBV) survivors, coordination activities to improve the referral system, training for health workers in CMR and PFA, prevention activities addressing other forms of GBV and the expansion of psychosocial care for survivors.3. The gender dimension of HIV will be addressed. Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs), HIV and GBV are interconnected issues. And, gender inequality places women at risk of HIV and STIs within their marriage. In that sense, the Project will improve the HIV/AIDS services provided at the Bor State hospital and selected PHC. This will be done by raising awareness on prevention, care, treatment and services, providing free available condoms and supporting safe and rational blood transfusion practices.Children health services will be critical as they have a significant impact on morbidity and mortality. The Project will reinforce coordination and referral systems and involve other actors to ensure a holistic and integrated approach. MdM will fill current gaps such as:- MHPSS activities. Psychological distress due to violence and trauma in pregnant and lactating women has a major impact on child development. These actions will reinforce the existing local health system and will be implemented in close partnership with the Ministry of Health (MoH).The Project will also address community structures and increase the population awareness of their rights, specifically their right to health and how to exercise this right. Action will be conducted where change is most needed, through existing women lactating groups, local community health workers (CHW) and community volunteers. To facilitate access to that network and contribute to long term changes in the community, MdM will work in partnership with Sudan Medical Care (SMC), a national NGO present in the area with extensive experience in community mobilisation and primary health care (PHC) services and support.To achieve the expected change, community-based interventions will reach all levels of society, using community participation processes to engage women, men, girls and boys and ensure diversity. The activities will focus on:1. Training, support and supervision of these CHW and volunteers in order to expand access to SRH services.2. Psychosocial support to GBV survivors. CHW and volunteers will ensure that activism and change is happening within their community. MdM will remain the supporting entity, but the community members themselves will own the changes happening.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The Project will also address community structures and increase the population awareness of their rights, specifically their right to health and how to exercise this right. Action will be conducted where change is most needed, through existing women lactating groups, local community health workers (CHW) and community volunteers. To facilitate access to that network and contribute to long term changes in the community, MdM will work in partnership with Sudan Medical Care (SMC), a national NGO present in the area with extensive experience in community mobilisation and primary health care (PHC) services and support.To achieve the expected change, community-based interventions will reach all levels of society, using community participation processes to engage women, men, girls and boys and ensure diversity. Community Health Workers and volunteers will ensure that activism and change is happening within their community. MdM will remain the supporting entity, but the community members themselves will own the changes happening. They will run awareness raising campaigns, education and sensitisation, referrals and information sharing about the availability of free health services in the area. Support to existing women’s groups will be provided in order to promote local women’s initiatives and access to information. Healthcare workers will receive financial and technical support. The team will assess training needs with the aim of providing higher quality healthcare services for rights-holders.Advocacy actions will be directed to the United Nations system, international donors and the government of South Sudan, to raise awareness of the humanitarian emergency and health situation, provide information on the specific needs and gaps in health services and the emergency response; and to obtain funds and supplies of health products to provide sexual and reproductive and child health services. MdM will bring special attention to coordination among international organizations in order to reduce gaps in interventions and avoid duplication.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The ongoing conflict has resulted in the breakdown of the social fabric, reducing protection for women and girls and forcing them into new, multiple roles. Traditional gender constructs have been substantially altered: the role of men as main providers for their families has been eroded and women and girls have new responsibilities related to care, work and participation in new productive roles. This extra workload limits girls’ access to school and women’s access to other activities (social, economic, leisure and political).Women are in a subordinate position in households and communities where decisions are mostly taken by men, including those about access to health services. This is aggravated by limited access to education, information, training and employment opportunities for women.MdM aims to enhance the right of women and girls to health by supporting their free access to integrated and quality health care, including psychosocial support (PSS), gender-based approaches, SRH services and care for GBV survivors through the clinical management of rape (CMR), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and Psychological First Aid (PFA). MdM will also reinforce HIV/AIDS services at the Bor State Hospital and selected Primary Health Care (PHC) and raise awareness on prevention, care and treatment services available. Community mobilization will be done with the support of community structures, existing women’s groups, community health workers (CHW) and community volunteers using participatory processes to engage women and girls.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety of beneficiaries and their communities should guide all project decisions.Due to the core focus of this intervention (pregnant and lactating women, children, SGBV victims ) specific safety and ethical considerations should be taken. Protecting confidentiality is essential to ensure both women's safety and data qualityFieldworkers should be trained on do not harm and safety principles.Processes and criteria for CHW, volunteers and women groups recruitment should be carefully considered, transparent and described in a way that can be easily understood by those involved in the project. The provision of services should maintain a minimum standard of care framed in international standard guides as IASC.Project workers have an ethical obligation to monitor and measure harms, or threats of harm, that may occur during the implementation.

Feedback mechanisms will be put in place to guarantee the continuous revision of protocols, methods, approaches and methodologies used and to ensure that they promote and assure dignity of the beneficiaries.
