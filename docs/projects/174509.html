Project Summary

UNESCO's response to education in emergencies (EiE) in the Far North region of Cameroon is part of its overall education strategy aimed at meeting children’s educational needs in emergencies to improve access to quality, inclusive and safe educational opportunities. To achieve this, UNESCO proposes to expand its current education interventions in distance learning that have been successfully undertaken in NWSW regions and through the national platform by targeting 16,000 (7,000 girls, 9,000 boys), refugees, returnees and host children in 4 districts that include; Mora (Mayo - Sava), Mokolo (Mayo Tsanaga, , Mora ( Mayo - Kani), Yagoua, Goundaye and Gobo (Mayo Danay). UNESCO will integrate protection, Psychosocial support (PSS), GBV, gender and sexual & reproductive health (SRH) for adolescents and particularly girls, as critical cross cutting issues in the communities. Specifically, the project will target the following components: i) Enable access to distance learning in community learning spaces using online/offline learning materials. This will include mobilizing parents and respective community leadership to ensure out of school children are enrolled in safe learning spaces within the communities. Additionally, UNESCO will train and monitor learning facilitators on the organization, structure, content and delivery of the distance learning including use and maintenance of the IT Equipment to ensure smooth learning with minimal interruptions. The facilitators will be trained on use of the UNESCO online learning platform, which will ensure that at all times learners have the option of using both online/offline learning materials effectively. ii) UNESCO will train teachers, learning facilitators and selected PTA’s on Psycho Social support, Disaster Risk Mitigation/Reduction, Early warning systems so that they are able to provide and/or facilitate learners and teachers with the required support. iii) In addition to distribution of learning materials (IT Equipment, mobile learning units and materials) UNESCO will distribute supplementary educational materials in selected mother tongues, Using the series of stories, in comics, from 'Bouba & Zaza': this collection of children's books, initiated by UNESCO, Éditions Michel Lafon and the Association for the Development of Education in Africa (ADEA), is aimed at children from 3 to 8 years old and aims to sensitize them on important social themes in the community like Violence, HIV/AIDS, Peace while improving reading and comprehension skills. This material will be integrated by the programming of a radio program (approx. 1 hour per week, spread over several sessions of 20 minutes max each), in mother tongues, to promote the stories distributed and encourage access to literacy / learning children (4-12 years); this last complementary component will make use of community radios that UNESCO already has and is installing in the Far North (operational in 2020), as part of its national program for community radios. iv) UNESCO will train selected community leaders, youth and implementing partners in Education in Emergencies, Disaster Risk Reduction/Early warning systems, Protection and Psychosocial support for learners and teachers. v) Through this action UNESCO will also seeks to mitigate deterioration of livelihoods due to the ongoing conflict and COVID-19 to improve the living conditions of at least 600 vulnerable youth through the provision of tailored life skills and livelihoods activities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon continues to be negatively impacted by the Boko Haram violence in the Lake Chad Basin. Various reports indicate an estimated 527,000 persons have been displaced as a result of the conflict and violence and continue to face significant trauma and protection risks . In the Far North, the Boko Haram insurgency, compounded by chronic vulnerability, the growing impact of climate change, and COVID-19 have left more than 1.2 million people in need of urgent assistance, with the majority being women, adolescent girls and children (HNO 2020). Beginning of 2019, the this region region saw a sharp upsurge in violence and the ongoing hostilities have displaced 527,000 people (112,000 refugees,6 297,000 IDPs7 and 117,000 returnees and continue to steadily push more people to flee their villages (HNO Report 2020). The number of IDPs increased from 228,000 in October 2018 to 297,000 in December 2019. While girls and women are particularly vulnerable due to the combined effect of gender discrimination and socio-economic vulnerability caused by the crisis, leading to specific threats against them (in particular gender-based violence), boys and men are more exposed to arbitrary arrests, forced recruitment and physical violence. Both genders therefore live with psychosocial trauma related to violence, including sexual violence and rape. Besides Conflict and the Boko Haram challenge, other crises include Flooding that affected more than 38000 pupils from July to October 2020, and 104 primary and secondary schools which were affected, in addition to the 67 primary and secondary schools which had already been closed due to insecurity and armed conflicts (OCHA SitRep October 2020). These multiple crises have a common denominator: children are in distress and teachers lack knowledge and competence to deal with crises affected students. Children are highly affected by conflict, exposed to violence, abuse and exploitation, family separation issues and have significant psychosocial needs. As a result these children easily drop out of school, face early and forced marriage, engage in petty commerce, child labour and other negative coping mechanisms for survival. With the COVID-19 crisis, teachers are being forced to learn and adapt IT skills and/or learn to work in a remote way, while children continue with home schooling. This adds additional burden on the education system especially in these conflict areas where structural challenges were already inherent, - particularly in the Far North. Conclusively, UNESCO will focus on the following actions in this proposal- i) Expand access to Protective Learning Environment, ii) Improve the quality and relevant education services provided by teachers and education personnel in both formal and non-formal learning institutions iii) Provision of Teaching and learning materials, iv) Capacity building of teachers on Pedagogy, EiE, PSS and Protection. UNESCO will also learn from other agencies that continue to do great work in this region for the benefit of children, and v) Provision of livelihood skills to vulnerable youth for the income generation.
