Project Summary

The crisis in north-east Nigeria continues to disrupt and limit access to livelihoods of millions of people, undermining opportunities for durable solutions. The intensification of attacks has resulted in prolonged insecurity, exacerbating the plight of vulnerable civilians and triggering waves of forced displacement as well as violations of human rights. Conflict affected populations continue to suffer from a continued deterioration of living conditions, a disruption of pre-existing protective mechanisms and a lack of access to essential services. They have accumulated protracted stress and experienced extreme violence, and loss of family members, social connections, and property. Although the need for essential life-saving assistance is still widespread, IOM recognizes the urgent need for early recovery interventions, which restore livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, in areas of protracted displacement and in areas of spontaneous return to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations, especially girls, youth, women, and the elderly, against future vulnerabilities and shocks. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. Without those pre-conditions, a premature return of displaced populations may lead to secondary displacement, deepen IDPs’ vulnerability, and exacerbate tension and social polarization between IDPs, host communities and returnees. The inadequate capacity in areas experiencing return is by IOM considered a direct risk to the overall progressive resolution of displacement situations.
This project seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience-building of conflict-affected populations through a series of community support interventions. As people continue to return to their places of habitual residence, there is urgent and significant need to restore infrastructure, promote early recovery of economic means of livelihood and facilitate a robust resilience building process. Livelihood interventions under this project will include Cash for Work, engaging IDP, returnee and host communities. In addition, vocational training and skills enhancement activities for youth will be organized as per labor market assessment to create human capital as necessary for the early recovery phase, with a special focus on youth. Each targeted beneficiary will also receive business start-up capital to establish small scale business. CFW and skilled labor schemes will support activities such as comprehensive neighborhood upgrades and light community infrastructure rehabilitation. This project will also work towards enhanced coordination and complementarity between humanitarian, stabilization and development partners, through a people-centered approach spanning across the HDN. It aims to provide technical and logistical support to community peace committees’ operations as well as building the capacity of local governance mechanisms to promote local recovery, restore social cohesion, and prevent and respond to SGBV crimes in compliance with human rights standards and through a victim-centered approach. IOM will align its response plan to the 5 strategic objectives of Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector as follows: •Support Livelihood Stabilization •Promote inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in Host Communities, Returnees, and Host communities •Support Restoration of Local Governance Administration and Services •Improve Access to Infrastructure and Basic Social Services through Rehabilitation and Reconstruction of Public Service Facilities. •Increase capacity of local partners, communities to identify analyze and respond to early warning signs of violence, conflicts and disaster.

1 - Needs

In 2019, 7.1 million people in Nigeria continue to be in need of humanitarian assistance. As their needs are unlikely to diminish any time soon, concerted efforts seeking to address the root causes of displacement and vulnerability have become ever-more pressing as the provision of immediate humanitarian support in resolving the negative effects of the crisis. The severe damage to key public infrastructure and service provision has compounded the long history of marginalization and chronic under-development in the north-east. While over 1.9 million people remain displaced in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe States in north-east Nigeria, a significant number of IDPs have returned to their communities of origin since the start of the crisis. As of July 2019, IOM recorded 1.64 million returnees across the three worst-affected states. However, scarce resources in communities of origin are often insufficient to meet the basic needs of returnees in addition to those of the host community. Severe damage to or destruction of community infrastructure, as well as the lack of access to adequate basic services, ultimately affect the sustainability of return and reintegration, as demonstrated by past and current waves of secondary displacement. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, which restore livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, in areas of protracted displacement and in areas of spontaneous return to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. According to REACH Multisector Need Assessment 2019, 43 per cent of households across Borno state displayed recovery and livelihoods needs. Livelihoods needs were highest in Central Borno, with 63 per cent of households in need. On the LGA level, the highest needs were reported in Damboa (72%), Monguno (68%) and Konduga LGA (56%). In the state of Adamawa, 48 per cent of households were found to be in need of recovery and livelihood support. Across Yobe state, nearly half of households had recovery and livelihoods needs (47 per cent), with returnee households being of highest concern (66 per cent). Specific measures to restore livelihoods include short-term employment creation, reinforcement of market-based skills through vocational training, and micro and small enterprise recovery, as well as recapitalization of small enterprises for youth and women. This is essential to address negative coping strategies to which many households have already resorted, including borrowing food, buying food on credit, selling of productive assets, borrowing cash from others and spending savings, and protection concerns including early marriage, child recruitment, child labor, trafficking, and sexual exploitation and abuse. In addition, areas of return, whose relative stability may also render them attractive to persons whose displacement persists, must begin recovery efforts in order to ensure that return does not constitute a secondary displacement and that returnees, IDPs and host communities residing in areas of return are able to rebuild while absorbing additional residents. As such, efforts are needed to restore a sense of security, social cohesion and access to basic services engaging both local governance mechanisms and affected communities in joint prioritization exercises. These efforts will contribute to mitigating potential tensions that may arise due to strained resources in areas witnessing last-scale returns, a restoration of local governance capacity and services, and a restoration of security through promotion of freedom of movement, safe routes, removal of UXOs/IEDs including from housing, agricultural land and market/trading routes, and the re-establishment of civil governance including the presence of police and judiciary.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities are factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the complaint and feed-back mechanism team and tools . Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
