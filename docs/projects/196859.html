Project Summary

Oxfam will support the resilience building in the crisis-affected agricultural communities through rebuilding their absorptive, adaptive and transformative capacities to address future risks, fragilities and uncertainties. While the project will help the communities to address their immediate and short-run needs through the project duration for 2 years, it will help them to recover their resilience pathways. In other words, building the links between emergency and development phases. The project will rely mainly on the existing systems, knowledge and capacities to make positive change that will foster positive transition. In full community participatory way, Oxfam will identify the critical risks and concerns and the main causes of vulnerabilities in the agricultural communities and the existing capacities and plans to address them immediately, both in the short and long terms.
While Oxfam will target the most vulnerable HHs, by providing them with the needed assets and capacity building training like agricultural and livestock kits, it will also help enhancing the community’s capacity as a whole. This will be done through rehabilitation of irrigation system and bakeries. Provision of agricultural inputs through various modality Also, by building the community mechanism and advocating with different stakeholders to change the market environment to be more suitable for women assess and participation, and rehabilitation of the irrigation channels through labor-intensive schemes when feasible.
Besides, the project will link the most vulnerable and willing families with the market systems in a sustainable way by supporting market driven income-generating activities (IGAs), including well-adapted vocational training. This will come after performing a thorough market and livelihood assessments in order to identify the existing opportunities and capacities in the local market systems.
Oxfam has acquired tremendous experience in using market based approach . Thus ,the action will conduct market functionalities and labour market assessment to explore feasible opportunities and delivery mechanism of assistance to the target communities . Oxfam will use standardize tools which adaptable to the context to undertake the market and labour market assessment
The project will be implemented in full gender and climate sensitive manner; therefore, gender aspects will be mainstreamed into all the project aspects in order to encourage women economic empowerment and participation in the communities decisions. Risks and vulnerabilities related to climate change will be prioritized and addressed.
The geographical targeting will be done after close coordination with other humanitarian actors in order to ensure the highest possible level of complementary and avoid overlapping. additionally , the project will work closely with other sector and working groups like early recovery and livelihoods , Agriculture working group and Cash working group to enhance coordination , integration and linkage among various intervention and initiative among the sectors to bring desire positive impacts on the live and livelihood of target people . This approach will also build the resilience capacity of the target communities to absorbe shocks .

3 - Needs

The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68% ofthe total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100% food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels.The impact of Syria’s crisis continues to hinder people’s ability to meet their immediate food needs and livelihoods, in a context marked by very complex drivers and contributing factors. Based on several recent assessments performed in Aleppo, Rural Damascus and DeZ,Oxfam found that farmers have lost their capacity to restart and sustain their agricultural activities due to: the destruction of the irrigation systems, very high prices of farming inputs; livestock and fodder, high prices and shortage of fuel, suspension of the government subsidies programs due to lack of resources, Therefore, farmers have relied on several negative coping strategies that will contribute to worsening the agriculture sector more in the near future like, drilling of wells close to Euphrates the matter that causes selling of their livestock, migrate to urban areas looking for livelihood opportunities ,joining military groups

4 - Implementation of activities

- Context analysis to identify the main causes of risks and vulnerabilities, the existing capacities and the potential pathways for resilience building.- Vocational and skills building of the most vulnerable farmers and livestock breeders- Provision of agricultural kits (seeds, fertilizers, livestock, fodders ..) to the most vulnerable HHs through the appropriate modality - rehabiliation of irrigation canals, wells , bakery and wheat flour mills to enhance access and avaliability of foood for most vulnerable HHs through the appropriate modality, Supporting small-scale food production through cash or voucher. Giving that the amount of money for the BFs will be based on the sector guidance 2022- Post Distribution Monitoring to monitor the performance of the established businesses, their sales growth, existing problems, and difficulties.- Support establishing micro and small businesses after ensuring enough linkages with the market system, special focus on women and PWD economic empowerment, as well as for those at protection risks that is believed to be reduced by economic empowerment. - Rehabilitation of existing irrigation systems and bakeries, supporting the communities to build gender sensitive water management.- Post Distribution Monitoring to monitor the functionality of the rehabilitated irrigation systems and bakeries and whether women and PWDs have equal and dignified access to the irrigation water and produced bread.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.Community tension arising from community perception of unfair eligibility criteria or unfair geographic/beneficiary targeting/selection: Community-based beneficiary selection, Effective communication, include communication materials in local language, Conflict-sensitivity analysis, protection risk analysis to inform final design of project and mitigation measures 2.Diversion of assets: risk-informed selection criteria, DDM, PDM, CFM 3.SEAH: Oxfam’s PSEA and Child Safeguarding policy, safeguarding focal points in all hubs, training, refreshers, CFM, reporting lines in place, Manager’s responsibility to provide healthy working environment. 4.Risk of jeopardizing commitment to meaningful access standard: Market assessments include specific disability, age, gender-sensitive questions; to capture facilitators/barriers to engagement of AGD groups in specific labor markets. This is essential to ensure female and PWD beneficiaries are not excluded from the market after completing vocational trainings (VT) or receiving assistance. 5.PWDs will be identified at interview stage prior to engagement in VTs to ensure preparedness to accommodate their diverse needs safely, coordination to ensure recipient of referrals from DPOs. 6.Youth (18-24) are highly exposed to conscription, hindering their ability to learn from VT: HH-level vulnerability assessment points constraints to engage and/or sustain the assistance received e.g. micro businesses, VT, to ensure assistance is relevant.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA triggered by unequal powerdynamics between e.g. staffresponsible for applying beneficiary selection and communities in need of assistance: Oxfam’s PSEA and Child Safeguarding policy; safeguarding focal points in all hubs; training and refreshers on the topic delivered to staff and partners; CFM; reporting lines in place; Manager’s responsibility to secure a working environment conducive to reporting SEA-related concerns; multi-layer verification of the list of selected beneficiaries against Oxfam’s vulnerability criteria and MoVs, in-house capacity to operationalize Oxfam’s reporting and investigation routine.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Limited equipment and supplies available in the Syrian market due to instability, sanctions and transactions delays prevent the implementation of project activities. - The USD to SYP Exchange rate fluctuations could result in either big gain at end of project period with no time to appropriate the additional funds, or not enough funds to fulfil commitments and project objectives. - Potential of fraud and aid diversion, amongst Oxfam staff, suppliers and partners - COVID19 spread puts at risk the health and wellbeing of affected communities and of humanitarian workers delivering assistance. - Assumptions: - No interference by conflicting parties in the implementation putting at risk independence, and impartiality. the local authorities will allocate enough production inputs for the rehabilitated bakeries. the community will have the capacity to manage the work in the rehabilitated bakeries sufficiently. The social norms and traditions will enable women to participate in the communities’ decision-making process.Mitigation: Oxfam will perform rapid market assessment to check the availability of the required items and their prices. All Oxfam and partner’s staff will be inducted on Oxfam’s standards on safeguarding and accountability. Very clear accountability mechanism will be put in place prior to the implementation. Oxfam will go for NCEs to absorb the surpluses that it might have as a result to an increase in the exchange rate.
