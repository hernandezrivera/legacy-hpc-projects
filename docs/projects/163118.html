Project Summary

Zimbabwe is currently reeling under economy instability, social, political and climatic induced shocks that puts the nation on virtual cycle of food insecurity. The proposed project in Buhera district will adopt a holistic approach in addressing food insecurity and building community resilience through intervention mix. The project is targeting 600 poorly resources food insecure farmers who are in Integrated Phase Classification 3 and 4 (IPC 3 and 4). The implementation of wide range of crops and livestock production activities in this project will minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration by improving access to basic crop and livestock inputs. Moreover, the project will enhance access, functionality and availability of critical community assets for crops and livestock production through infrastructure rehabilitation and construction. The project also is designed to optimize the benefit of agricultural support through extension and advisory services to capacitate farmers with knowledge on crops and livestock production in Buhera district by December 2020.
The district lies within natural region iv and v which receives annual average rainfall between 450-600mm which is not sufficient to sustain dryland agriculture practices. During the 2018/19 agriculture season, the district receives below normal rainfall and crop yield potential was compromised leading to the district positioned on 5th position in Zimbabwe in terms of food insecurity as evidenced Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) report of 2019. About 76.5% of the population (199,725 people) were projected to be food insecure. The district is in crisis and emergent need of support (IPC 3 and 4). The food insecurity situation in the district was worsened by Tropical Cyclone Idai which left a trail of destruction on infrastructure, food security and livelihoods. Poor performance of the country’s economy is also contributing to food shortages in the district. The livestock situation in Buhera district has also been deteriorating and the mortality rate of 20.3% for cattle reported by ZimVAC report of 2019 is alarming as it is far above the nation’s recommended rates between 3-5%. Also, the goat mortality rate of 20% for the province which is higher than the country’s acceptable rates of 8-10% is a cause for concern.
According to the situation in the district there is need to intensify climatic smart agriculture techniques, production of drought tolerant crops (sorghum and millet) and venture into diversified small livestock and gardening activities for improving food production and livelihoods options. The proposed project will complement with agriculture and livestock input support as well as rehabilitating productive assets and capacitating farmers with complementary trainings. These activities will be facilitated through the government departments of Crops and Livestock Production, and Veterinary Services. Community based targeting process will be used to select the beneficiaries of the project in IPC 3 and 4. Selected farmers should have access to land and water resources. Special considerations will be given to vulnerable and marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed.
Zimbabwe Council of Churches (ZCC) has extended its operations in Manicaland Province during the beginning of Cycle Idai response in March 2019. It has supported victims of the disaster with emergency food assistance and shelter support. ZCC has signed agreements with the Provincial Administrator for the Province to conduct livelihood recovery activities in the province.

1 - Activities

The project will conduct multiple activities to prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population. The project will provide emergency agriculture support aimed at ensuring they can achieve food security and resilience to repeated exposure to multiple shocks and stressors. Six (6) water sources will be rehabilitated and solarised for supplying water for nutrition garden at 6 gardens that will be established. Each nutrition garden will have an average of 50 beneficiaries who will be supported with nutrition garden inputs in the form of 20g tomatoes, 20g carrots, 20g leaf vegetable rape and cabbage, 100g butternuts, 20 g cucumber, 20g onions and 500g beans or peas per farmer. For the livestock feeding component, the project will provide long term solution in the form of 20 cuttings fodder. The project will also conduct small livestock restocking activity in which each farmer will be provided 6 hens, a cock indigenous chickens and 50 kg poultry feed per farmer, 2 females goats per farmer and 1 buck in community at a ratio of 1buck per 13 females. The project will further rehabilitate 3 dip tanks and livestock handling facilities. As the district is prone to seasonal droughts, the project will provide drought tolerant crop seeds in the form of 5kg maize drought tolerate variety, 5 kg sorghum and 5 kg millet per farmer. hectare. As the project has realised the need to capacitate farmers on best agriculture practices for crops and livestock production, it will conduct farmer trainings on nutrition garden production; livestock fodder production, livestock production and animal health; climate smart agriculture, diseases and pest management; small grains value chain development, post-harvest handling, storage, processing and marketing. All the proposed activities will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, food security humanitarian organisations and local leadership. Government technical department will offer technical expertise in facilitation of project activities. The project will coordinate its activities with other programs in the district to minimise duplication of efforts and ensuring that the most vulnerable people are targeted. The organisation will attend all the meetings that will be invited through the Food Security Cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be determined by measuring the number of households receiving season sensitive emergency crops and livestock inputs, critical community assets for crop and livestock production rehabilitated and built as well as the number of vulnerable small holder farmers receiving extension and advisory services to manage crop pests and livestock diseases.
