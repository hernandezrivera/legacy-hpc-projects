Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the preparedness environment through increasing capacity of humanitarian shelter actors as well as communities, which includes the application of standard actions and positives approaches on gender, people with disabilities, protection, and environment while being accountable to the affected populations. The areas of intervention consider a combined analysis of risks, vulnerabilities, historical disaster profile, and average of people in need.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

More than 20% (317.125) of the people affected by the rainy/cyclonic season 22/23 had their houses destroyed. Shelter NFI and shelter assistance distribution reports from the shelter cluster partners, showed a reach of about a 3% of people affected, which is a 13% of the people with destroyed houses. Damaged or destroyed houses together with flooding situations also impacts the life inside the dwellings compromising the domestic life in terms of available core items to sleep, cook, water and food storage, and sources of energy (fuel to cook, illumination). This project includes preparedness actions for natural disasters through the preposition of key supplies such as shelter and NFIs kits, the dissemination of education messages and the capacity building at community level on safer house reinforcement and reconstruction. The preposition of core supplies (Shelter & NFIs) aims to cover 60,000 people (12,000 households, 31,800 females. 28,200 males, from which 34,200 are children, 22,800 adults, 3,000 elderly and estimated 9,000 with disabilities), the target number is based on the shelter partners capacity to respond during the rainy/cyclonic season 2022/2023. The anticipatory actions aim to build capacity through the positioning and awareness of key IEC materials in listed accommodation centers and training on safer reinforcement and rebuilding of shelters. These actions target 44,137 people (8,827 households, 23,393 females, 20,744 males, from which 25,158 are children, 16,772 adults, 2,207 elderly and estimated 6,621 with disabilities).

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The key activities include:1_ Procurement and preposition of appropriately designed shelter and NFIs supplies, based on needs identified, contextual appropriateness and value for money in terms of transportation and speed of delivery. Considering the broad affectation of natural disasters in terms of geographical area, the preposition of supplies aims to cover all the provinces of the country.2_ Distribution and placement of fixed IEC materials on shelter reinforcement and safer reconstruction on listed accommodation centres. After TC Freddy´s second landfall, according to DTM, 96k people were sheltered in 210 accommodations centres across the country. The Shelter Cluster identifies these sites as hotspots for the dissemination of lifesaving messages. 3_Carry-out trainings to community leaders, young adults (men and women), and general community members in BBS construction techniques. Leaders and general community members play a key role supporting the shelter reinforcement and reconstruction activities. It is neccesary to strengthen theirs skills through updated and tested safer practices that can make the difference in terms of shelter resilience. 4_Carry-out post distribution monitoring and analysis to inform future programming. For all the activities areas are targeted based on people at risk and in need figures, residual needs from 2022/2023 rainy and cyclonic season, as well as a shelter severity analysis of living conditions in the country.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM is implementing the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) which enables thehumanitarian community to identify priorities and activities through multi-sectoralassessments that take into consideration gaps in humanitarian service coverage,including health, WASH, protection and shelter services. The DTM displacementmonitoring and emergency tracking tools will further inform humanitarianprogramming of protection risks and needs for better targeted and evidence basedinterventions ultimately contributing to more secure settlements for affectedpopulations.Regarding project design, IOM ensures that the shelter Kit content is tailormade to mitigate protection risks and takes into consideration the needs of vulnerable groups.Greater protection concerns are mainstreamed within the Shelter Cluster partners and into the multi-sectoral teams (in addition to having protection representation within IOM structure), who conduct referrals and services specifically targeted for those vulnerable persons. IOM is an active member of the Protection cluster and shelter-protection assessment visits inclusive of HH surveys that focus on vulnerability criteria and protection aspects areperformed to ensure integration of these aspects into the design of the program activities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Shelter Cluster partners with support from other clusters, will conduct multisectoral community engagement exercises which include focal group discussions, visits to the affected communities, community and household assessments, in order to understand their most immediate pressing shelter and NFI needs and for those displaced, the intentions of the communities to resettle into sites either set up by the Government (to be verified that have meet the minimum standards), or plots provided by the Government within the host community. Communities will be engaged in on-the-job training exercises with the intention to give guidance on the use of the shelter materials and improved construction techniques. A right- and community-based participatory approach is essential to create meaningful involvement by displaced residents in protection and assistance activities. The community’s leadership, but also other representatives of men, women and youth, elderly and persons with disabilities will be included in the design of programming and assistance activities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities will be targeted and have a higher score while selecting the most vulnerable houses. These people, if with no labor capacity, will receive technical and labor support for the construction of their shelters. And estimated 20% of the population with disabilities will have been assisted under our programing.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

IOM will seek strict adherence to the Sphere Standards for Humanitarian Assistance which identify the environment as a cross-cutting issue to be incorporated to all humanitarian assistance. In that sense, IOM will encourage the use of assessment tools to assess the environmental impact of the construction related program activities. This tool focus from the selection of the shelter sites, the construction of structures reviewing the possible negative environmental impacts, the management considerations while occupied and also considers the possible decommissioning or dismantling of the structures to reutilize the resources. The use of this tools will engage local actors and communities to understand the different conditions particular to the working areas.
