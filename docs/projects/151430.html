Project Summary

The proposed action will be implemented through the already existing Government-MOH systems to enhance sustainability through the strengthening of the health system to offer quality health services to the target populations; IDPs and host community during humanitarian crisis. The action will aim to enhance sustainability through strengthening of the health system to offer quality health services to the target populations. Target locations are Baki and Lughaya districts of Awdal region, Burco and Odweyne districts of Togdheer region both in Somaliland, Garoowe, Burtinle and Eyl districts of Nugaal region in Puntland and Baydhaba, Qansax Dheere and Buur Hakaba districts of Bay region in South Central Somalia. The interventions seek to increase access, utilization and coverage of essential maternal, new-born and child health services through strengthened health system for better service delivery through; capacity building to the health care workers on safe motherhood, BEmONC and CEmONC, EPI and cold-chain management, integrated disease surveillance and response (IDSR) and mass casualty management in order to improve their capacity to deliver quality health services; provision of essential medicines including vaccines thus avoiding stock outs and ensure availability and accessibility of supplies; strengthening emergency obstetric and new-born care through adequate staffing to provide both BEmONC and CEmONC services 24/7; scale up of immunization services both routine immunization and campaigns during disease outbreaks. Through this action also, mitigation of impact and response to shocks to the affected populations as a result of conflict and public health emergencies will be enhanced through supporting the MoH prepare emergency response plans (ERPs) at regional health authorities level and have rapid response teams (RRTs), who are trained and can promptly investigate rumors, confirm outbreaks, notify WHO and MoH, mount an aggressive response, and report promptly. Referral health facilities will be selected strategically and equipped to enable management of mass casualties during catastrophe and staff trained on mass casualty management. It is envisaged that through this interventions, target populations will have improved access to quality healthcare services during humanitarian crisis and therefore reduce excess mortality.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains in need of humanitarian interventions. The 2018 post-Gu food security and nutrition assessment by FAO’s FSNAU and FEWSNET confirmed the overall food security situation has improved. However, the total number of people in need still exceeds levels prior to onset of the drought crisis in 2016. This has been attributed to the increase in internal displacement that occurred during the drought as people in inaccessible, rural areas sought assistance in populated areas humanitarians could reach together with recent floods, ongoing conflict, forced evictions, Gender Based Violence (GBV) &heightened levels of child recruitment. UNOCHA Aug 2018 report indicated that 4.6 million people including an est. 2.5 million children are in need of humanitarian assistance. Of these, 1.5 million are in crisis or emergency. The IDPs still remain the most vulnerable and are in dire need of immediate and long-term assistance. Health outcomes in Somalia still remain low and have been complicated by several issues especially insecurity, displacement, weak government systems and limited WASH interventions, amongst other factors. It’s estimated that up to 5.4 million people in Somalia are currently in need of emergency health services, hence necessitating sustained effort in prevention, detection and response to water and vector-borne diseases, including AWD/cholera and malaria (UNOCHA). The number of maternal deaths continue to rise; maternal mortality ratio of 732 deaths/100,000 live births. With under-five mortality rate of 137/1,000 live births, only 1/4 of Somali women can access ANC services and only 1/3 of births are attended by skilled medical personnel. Many diseases contributing to high morbidity&mortality are vaccine preventable yet DPT3 coverage is <50%(UNICEF 2016). The health infrastructure is fragmented with weak institutional capacity leading to poor quality service provision; ratio of qualified personnel est. at 4/10,0000(WHO).

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Women, men, boys and girls, persons with disabilities, and marginalized communities are impacted differently by the crisis and displacement, having distinct humanitarian needs and facing specific protection concerns. Therefore, integration of age, gender and diversity principles in service delivery and assistance will be promoted. Protection risks in IDP sites include widespread GBV, child recruitment, physical attacks, forced early marriages, forced evictions, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices. The project will strengthen inclusion by instituting an accountability mechanism for effective protection of affected populations i.e robust complaints and feedback mechanism for instance a toll free telephone line.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender segregated data will used throughout the project. The project will that the health facilities are accessible and appropriate for all in the communities. This will take into gender specific protection needs into account. This will include but not limited to making of availability of rooms for privacy, gender-segregated latrines and handwashing facilities in the health facility. The latrines will be equipped with locks. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to women and girls to address their menstruation need. While gender disparity in Somalia often exclude women from accessing information and making decision, the project will not only ensure health and hygiene promotion will include both men and women in the event, it will also intentionally include both male and female in the various health committee so that they can have equality opportunity to take part in decision making on health issues in the community.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Using ‘Do no harm principles’, WV will seek to avoid physical or psychosocial risks that would be detrimental to project beneficiaries, and will serve all people irrespective of age, sex or clan. Given vulnerabilities associated with issues related to clan dynamics, WV has done a thorough analysis of the context and has conducted the Do-No-Harm Assessments for target districts. Competing over access to basic commodities and services are often one of the major causes of community conflict. To avoid provoking fraction among communities, the project will closely engage community throughout the project cycle, ensuring they are involved in site and community health committee selection, fully informed of project details, and making sure accountability system is in place to allow them voice their concerns and complaint. WV, throughout the project cycle, will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff during program. This will ensure timely provision of services to prevent late travels by the staff and beneficiaries which might expose them to security risks. Throughout the project duration, world vision will monitor the security situation and also collaborate with the local administration and the community to ensure that the beneficiaries and the staff are well aware. WV has the security team that closely monitors the security situation within the target areas, hence able to advice and prevent staff and also beneficiaries from being affected.
