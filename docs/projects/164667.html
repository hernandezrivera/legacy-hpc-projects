Project Summary

The project will take place in two different geographical areas in term of the political situation; North West Syria NWS, where the access to this area is mainly from Turkey as it is controlled by Turkish-supported armed groups and HTS, and North East Syria NES, where the access is from Iraq - Kurdistan, and is controlled by Syrian Democratic Forces SDF. Bahar has an oﬃce in Erbil and Erbil-based staff can oﬃcially cross the border from Kurdistan to NES when needed.
The model of the intervention will be the same across the two different areas and will be consisted of CMAM, IYCF, and IMCI activities. Bahar has health facilities to support OTP care services in both areas and stabilization centers situated rural Aleppo with a capacity to respond to the elevated numbers of severely malnourished cases fled from the escalated areas. Bahar also is a part of an inclusive referral system for health and non-health services to the linkage of the beneficiaries to the nearest (health, nutrition, CP, GBV) care providers, and will be utilized to complement the nutrition intervention.

4 - Needs

4.6 million are in need of Nutrition services during 2020 Acute malnutrition remains relatively high among internally displaced children and mothers in the conflict areas, about 240,000 CU-5 and over 50,000 PLW are in need of urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services. At least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of health complications, including death, with higher numbers of acute malnutrition amongst infants under 6 months of age. Chronic malnutrition is evident in most sub-districts throughout Syria – notably in the Northwest of Syria (Aleppo, Idlib) and North East Syria (Deir Ez Zor, Al-Raqqa and Hasakeh). Chronic malnutrition is also on the rise with almost half a million children stunted (low height for age) in 2020. While at least one out of eight children in Syria is chronically malnourished, this ratio is worse in areas affected by displacements, returns, and over-burdened communities such as North East Syria, Idlib (1 out of 5 children), and Aleppo (1 out of 4 children). To prevent chronic malnutrition, strengthening optimal infant feeding practices and maternal nutrition throughout the first 1,000 days of life is critical. In Syria, at least one out of every three mothers and children are anemic. And that could lead to negative outcomes such as increased maternal mortality and morbidity, particularly in conflict-affected, and underserved areas. Currently over a million children and over three million mothers in Syria are in a need to treat and prevent anemia. Otherwise, the cycle of chronic malnutrition in Syria will continue, over 90% of mothers will develop complications, and 5% of them could possibly die.

5 - Implementation of activities

Nutrition activities including, CMAM, IYCF,IMCI, micronutrients distribution, SAM and MAMmanagement, and capacity building. The activities will be implemented in an integratedmanner with WaSH, protection, and health. Bahar will implement integrated CMAMprogram included all pillars (community mobilization, TSFP, OTP, Inpatient care (SC), MBAs) andIYCF program for people in need providing outreach and facilitated services in both NWS (Afrin,Al BAb, Jarablus andA'zaz), and NES (Hassakeh, Ar-Raqqa, and Deir-ez-Zor). Nutritionists and CHWs will provide services at the fixed health facilities and at the community level, visit homes and camps and will provide the following activities: 1. MUAC screening for acute malnutrition of all children (6-59 months of age)and PLW. 2. Referring identified malnourished cases to receive the treatment. 3. Providingappropriate IYCF counseling that will take place either at the target house or at the facility. 4. Distribution of micronutrient supplementation. 5. Following up the SAMand MAM cases. 6.Provide outpatient care services at Bahar's PHCs to manage SAMwithout complications cases. 7.Provide inpatient care services at Bahar's Hospital- Afrin tomanage SAM cases with medical complications. 8.provide capacity building and trainingto the community health workers, doctors, nurses, midwives, nutritionists, and volunteersto detect and manage malnourished cases properly and the optima IYCF-E life-saving practices.9. all the detected malnourished cases will be referred throughout over than 20 cars and ambulances of Bahar. Bahar will rely on nutrition cluster criteria and forms to register the beneficiaries. To address the gender issues in the target communities, the main providers of the services will be female CHWs to consider theexisted culture and habits in the area as the counseling will target PLW.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk analysis:Some risks cannot be fully or significantly mitigated.Thus, Bahar organization staff will be managed to carry out a risk-to-benefit analysis to see if it the risk taken is justifiable compared to the benefit gained. Throughout the conducted nutrition activities some risks are considered by BO teams:1.Attacks including airstrikes, bombs or shelling, on nutrition workers in the health facilities, direct attack on nutrition worker might occur.2.Poor families may try to ensure the nutritional needs of their daughters are met by arranging child marriages. and underfed females may be at heightened risk of exchanging sex for food.3.Children receiving nutrition services are over-exposed to the risk of harm by nutrition personnel.4. Aid workers or associated people (humanitarian workers or local council members) use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people, or harassment. To mitigate such risks BO aims to:1.Apply the designated de-confliction mechanism for health and nutrition facilities, Provide preparedness trainings for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents.2.train all IYCF counselors on how to supportively engage with GBV survivors and provide information in an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and access care as applicable to their respective role, and ensure referral mechanisms to specialized GBV support, health care, MHPSS, and child protection are in place. MBAs will be launched especially in the health facilities, collective centers, and camps if possible.3. Bahar will conduct participatory assessments to better inform nutrition program design or to improve programming in regards to child protection. And will seek technical advice and help from the Child Protection Coordinator. Community Nutrition workers will receive regular PSS sessions.4. (PSEA) policies will be in place, practiced and monitored and all the employees in GZT office and in Syria will be trained.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Conflict can potentially affect the ability to deliver assistance by limiting access to nutrition services with a high impact, Bahar will ensure that staff are sensitized to understand the two-way interaction between the intervention and the context and will be aware of the source of data, reports, social media and rumors and counsel staff to avoid external sharing of unverified information.Additionally, Beneficiary data and other information is not sufficiently so, Bahar has a clear policy to protect information that may be sensitive or could put people at risk. This should include when equipment, paper and/or electronic data and documents should be relocated or destroyed.Finally, Humanitarian staff, volunteers or associated personnel are not able to make informed decisions. Hince, Organization has clear Duty of Care policies that clarify the organization’s responsibilities and obligations to its staff, volunteers and associated nutrition personnel.
