Project Summary

The project will support 10,000 vulnerable and underserved children and 350 teachers
through the provision of emergency education supplies and school-based psychosocial
support. It will also provide remedial education to vulnerable school-aged children
including training of teachers on the home-based learning methodology. Furthermore, it
will support conflict-affected students with disability with assistive devices to ensure their
equal access to inclusive quality learning opportunities.

1 - Needs

The protracted and recurrent conflict, the Israeli blockade, the use of school facilities as shelters and for military activities as well as the attacks on and demolitions of schools in the Gaza strip have been severely impeding access to quality education in a safe andchild-friendly manner for both children and teachers. These threats also negatively affect students` general well-being and their school performance and completion rates. Save the Children`s (SCI`s) research conducted in February 2018 that some 63% of children in Gaza experience nightmares and 68% have sleeping difficulties. Some 95% of children have reported depression, aggression, hyperactivity and a preference for being alone. Aircraft sound is cited as the single biggest source of fear by 78% of children. Nearly 60% of all caregivers interviewed (90 out of 150) state that the blockade has a significant impact on their children`s daily lives with the conflict and constant insecurity being major sources of stress. The chronic and widespread electricity shortage compounding the above threats also prevents children from learning and concentrating effectively both at school and in their homes. In SCI`s research, children have often expressed feeling “angry”, “anxious” and “alone” when the lights have gone off. Caregivers have stated feeling concerned about the economic impacts of the power shortage and the impact on their children`s learning. The funding cuts to UNRWA resulting in the downsizing of its activities and staff has put both the UNRWA schools and provision of education at risk as well as many Palestinian caregivers employed by the Agency. The double and triple shift system applied by UNRWA schools (70%) and Ministry of Education schools (63%) further limits the time available for studying to four hours a day and impedes remedial education, co- and extracurricular activities. According to the Gaza Education cluster, the deterioration in the humanitarian situation has pushed more families into poverty, with 54% cent of the population in Gaza undermining the affordability of basic education supplies and transportation costs, and approximately 50% of all students are failing to meet their educational potential and facing reading and writing difficulties. These also erode children`s resilience and leave them at the risk of school drop-out and a number of protection risks as some families have allowed their children to work in order to support the family livelihood according to NRC EiE rapid assessment report. UNICEF`s recent report on Out-of-School Children in Palestine (July 2018) has found that about 8.8% of all boys and 11.9% of all girls at the age of five do not attend school. Out of school rates decrease at the ages of 6 to 13, while sharply increase for boys from the age of 14 (with 11.6% of boys and 2.7 of girls) not attending school. The increase continues for boys, with 22% and 29% of boys aged 15 and 16, respectively being out of school. For girls, the same figures are lower at 5.6% and 13.3%. Adolescent boys, aged 14 and 15, make up nearly half of all children, up to the compulsory school age of 15, who are out of school. This shows that there is a gender-specific pattern of missing education affecting adolescent boys in particular. Children from vulnerable households, children with disabilities, and 14-15-year-old boys are all affected by this system-wide barrier related the inclusiveness, quality and equity of education services in Palestine as reported in the OOSC_SoP report. Moreover, children with disabilities are still facing a lot of challenges to access quality education. The Educaid needs assessment on EiE and IE stated that inappropriate resources and furniture, absence of diagnosis tools to identify children learning difficulties, limited capacity building opportunities for teachers in term of inclusive education and mainly shortage of donation related to providing assistive devices for children with disabilities.

2 - Activities

The project will center around two main components in direct support of the strategic and cluster objectives of the 2020 HRP. Responsive activities will include 1) provision of emergency education supplies to vulnerable and underserved students and teachers and 2) provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by education-related violations. Remedial activities will include 1) provision of remedial education to vulnerable school aged children and 2) supporting conflict-affected school children with disability with assistive devices. The project will target a total of 10,000 children (5,000 girls and 5,000 boys) whose learning outcomes are affected by high level of distress and low level of motivation resulting from the violence, and poor infrastructure and overcrowding in schools. Following a rapid needs assessment, the 8,000 children will be provided with home-based learning opportunities and recreational activities. This will include training for 150 home/community-based learning teachers (75 female and 75 male) who will benefit from a cash for work support. These teachers will deliver lessons in the communities as alternative form of education when schools are used as shelters or are not functioning. 2,000 children (1,000 girls and 1,000 boys) living in very hard conditions will receive student kits in the form of e-vouchers that support students at risk of dropping out due to poverty or disability through the provision of value vouchers for school supplies, learning materials, uniforms, and children’s clothing, in addition to assistive devices as needed. 200 students (100 girls, 100 boys) with sensorial and physical disabilities will receive assistive devices to attend schools. 10 schools (5 boy, 5 girl schools) located along the ARAs and pre-identified and used as shelters and/or UNRWA schools that will be closed down as a result of downsizing will be targeted. 200 teachers (100 female and 100 male) in these target schools will receive training on Stress Management and Psychological First Aid provision for children to support the learning of children facing mental health challenges. Referral pathways, for identified/disclosed protection concerned to service providers (health, case management, mental health/pss, security/legal etc), will ensure that protection needs identified for specific children are addressed by relevant service providers. Girls and boys will have equal access to project activities and gender-specific needs and risks. The project activities will help enhance access to quality education interventions for children who are negatively affected by the double and triple-shift school system and /or come from most vulnerable and poorest households and/or live with disabilities. SCI will place a specific focus on children requiring psycho-social and remedial education support as a result of education violations, children at the risk of school drop-out and children with disabilities. Target locations will include the Gaza strip including ARAs with most vulnerable schools prioritized. These will include locations where students have to cross checkpoints and/or militarized and 􀂡ring zones, where children have to cover long distances to reach school, where schools are at risk of being used as shelters during an emergency and where there is shortage of school infrastructure and staff. Save Youth Future Society as project partner and long-term strategic partner for SCI will directly implement the home-based learning project component and recreational activities for the children. The partnership will both support SCI`s overall localization and capacity-building strategy, further local ownership and sustainability as well as facilitate access to local and most vulnerable communities Throughout the project, SCI will work in close cooperation and consultation with the in-country Education cluster, the EiE Working Group, Protection Cluster, the MoEHE, UNICEF and the UNRWA.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

• SCI will ensure “Do-no-harm” through our project intervention in terms of mitigation of potential social tensions in communities resulting from community members feeling excluded.• Interventions are based on needs assessments and transparent selection criteria. Appropriate targeting of beneficiaries will be the result of quality assessment of design and planning processes, coordinated through regular meetings with the partners and communities during the course of the implementation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

• To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks.
