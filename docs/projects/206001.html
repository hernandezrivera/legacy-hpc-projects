Project Summary

In the proposed project, Save a Life International (SLI) plans to target 3,190 beneficiaries, among them being 1894 Girls and 990 boys within Aweil South County. In addition to that, SLI will identify 100 more new volunteer teachers to be recruited, trained with skills to support ECD and Primary school learning in targeted 47 schools. Further, 150 SMCs and 56 PTAs will have their capacity built to enable them to support Education activities in schools and in their respective communities. To complement on the proposed project successfully, SLI will rehabilitate the previous 7 TLS constructed in Aweil South County to reduce congestion and allow for new enrollments in order to enhance quality access and safe education to all boys and girls. To improve hygiene and sanitation, SLI plans to rehabilitate 100 pit latrines that were previously constructed across 47 schools within the County. All rehabilitated latrines will be equipped with hand washing facilities and will be segregated by gender. To increase enrollment and retention of out of school boys and girls, SLI will conduct back to learning campaigns both at the Boma and Payam Level in close coordination with teachers, school management committees, parent teacher’s associations and Ministry of Education officials with the key massage; “Education is the Key to success”. The campaigns will be sustained for a period of twelve months to ensure enrollment and retention of boys and girls who are currently out of schools. Further, SLI will provide learning, teaching and recreation materials for pupils and teachers in all the targeted 47 schools. To reduce incidences of school drop outs, SLI will work with community leaders, PTAs, Teachers and SMCs to monitor cases of school dropout, make follow up on cases and ensure readmission and retention in schools. Noting that adolescent girls who have reached the reproductive age miss schooling due to lack of menstrual hygiene training and materials, SLI will ensure girls are provided with reusable sanitary pads and soap. For an integrated response, SLI will work closely with child protection partners to conduct school and community-based child protection monitoring, awareness on child protection concerns, making of referrals for specialized services.
To reduce SGBV cases in schools, SLI will coordinate with GBV partners for information sharing in schools, GBV awareness and referrals. Prior to rehabilitating existing learning spaces and latrines that were affected during flood, SLI will conduct in-depth consultation with Camp Coordination Mechanism and Housing, Land and Property (HLP) partners, community leaders and local administrators to address any underlying land issues and ownership before proceeding to rehabilitate the facilities. In the current HRP call, FSL and Nutrition partners are expected to establish farming and nutrition demonstration sites/gardens. As such, SLI will coordinate with FSL and Nutrition partners to encourage pupils and teachers to participate in FSL and Nutrition activities conducted in demonstration gardens and school kitchen garden located within schools. This will help children grow variety of vegetables that will help boost their nutritional value during school feeding programme that is supported by WFP. WASH partners will be engaged in during hygiene promotion in schools and in the community. SLI will participate in Education assessments and activities supported by the Ministry of Education, cluster and partners in Aweil South to ensure education needs that arise during implementation are identified and met as long as available resources allow

1. Needs

MoGEI and the South Sudan Education Cluster (SSEC), co-led by UNICEF and Save the Children, undertook a national education needs assessment (ENA) across 442 schools to provide an overview of the current education situation in South Sudan and conduct a situation analysis of the functioning of South Sudan’s education system, particularly given the impact of over seven years of conflict and displacement as well as the COVID-19 pandemic and subsequent interruption of education in South Sudan. Worse still, of the children who were attending schools, the head teachers reported that only 2.3% were attending school regularly. After schools were opened in Covid19 context, the main reasons head teachers cited for boys not reenrolling were inability to pay fees (24%) and cattle rearing (13%) while for girls the reasons were pregnancy (25%) and inability to pay fees (15%). Further, 15% of head teachers reported that all children in their school had writing materials, 24% reported their teachers had a full set of textbooks for the subjects they were teaching while 2% of the 322 schools assessed reported having children with disability having enough mobility or other assistive devices. The ENA report also notes that 64% of head teachers reported that children in their school did not get a meal at school or food to take home, while over 60% reported that the majority of learners were not getting food before attending school. Prior to Covid19, Northern Bahr El Ghazal had a prevalence of 49% (female) and 53% (Males) of out of school children. A recent report by the Education Cluster on schools affected by flooding has listed 37 schools as partially destroyed by floods and 6 schools as completely destroyed by floods. This would mean that after flooding has receded, majority of these schools will require rehabilitation work to restore latrines, classrooms and hand washing facilities to functionality. For schools completely destroyed by floods, there will be need to establish new temporary learning facilities. Failure to do so will mean pupils remain out of school or they attend schooling under trees where they will be exposed to elements of weather. According to MoEST officials, they cite non-functionality of schools due to insecurity; destruction of schools during flooding, school closures; attacks on School; limited accessibility to the schools and remoteness, poor school infrastructure, limited availability of WASH facilities, negative cultural practices where communities prefer educating boys and girls, weak capacity of teachers and Education support structures, lack of teaching, learning, recreational and Menstrual Management supplies, low capacity of teachers in implementing the current curriculum, delayed or lack of salaries for teachers as key factors leading to massive dropouts. Further, officials noted few female teachers in schools which has a negative impact on girls enrolling and remaining in schools. Failure to enroll and retain children in school means that boys will continue to be at risk of recruitment into armed groups, gangs and child Labor while girls face the danger of forced or early marriage. In order for Aweil South to contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 3 and 4, targets and indicators by 2030, a lot of effort needs to be put in place to attract and gainfully sustain pupils in schools through inclusive, safe and quality learning.

2. Project activities

Community sensitization, mobilization and back to school campaigns. One child, one teacher, one book, one pen can change the world. SLI will emphasize more on the importance of education through back to school campaign and creating awareness within the community and the targeted schools in Aweil South. The objective of back to school campaign is to increase the school enrolment. Distribution of learning materials. SLI will directly improve the retention of children in schools by addressing the quality, safety, and relevance of their instruction. The program will be emphasized on early grade literacy, gender equity, conflict mitigation, and engaging marginalized populations, and used a community-based approach to reach out-of-school and marginalized boys and girls through the distribution of the learning materials. Distribution of hygiene kits to girlsThe provision of dignity kits to adolescent girls and women plays an important role in helping maintain their dignity, confidence, and self-esteem. This will also enable girls not to absent themselves from attending school lessons and promote regular attendance. Rehabilitation of Temporary learning structuresSLI will aim at expanding education opportunities that will be inclusive and promote social cohesion. To accomplish this objective, SLI will gear this component focusing to improve and expand safer education services for children and youth through rehabilitation of 7 TLS to avoid overcrowding in schools so that learners can have enough learning spaces. SLI will also enhance relevance of education and promotion of learner well-being and social cohesion; and improve quality of management of education systems through local structures within the 47 schools in Aweil South county. Rehabilitation of pit latrines.Poor sanitation and hygiene are the main contributors to acute watery diarrhea which is one of the lead causes of mortality in Aweil South. Lack of sanitation contributes to many communicable diseases and has a direct impact on the health of young boys and girls. To provide safe learning environment at schools, SLI will ensure that all the pit latrines in the 47 schools within the targeted county of Aweil South are in good use and are gender desegregated for male and female to ensure that they are all in use. Training of SMC, PTAs and volunteer teachers. SLI finds it very significant for progressive training of the SMC and PTAs together with volunteer teachers. During the project implementation, building the capacity of teachers is essential to achieving the quality of education and academic experience of our beneficiaries. The key is to provide children with the tools to think for themselves. To do so, SLI will instill a sense of commitment in teachers and ensure that they have the skills to bring the best out of children while understanding the varied learning methods required to meet the needs of every child. Through extensive regular capacity building, teachers receive trainings on student-centered teaching methodology, social and emotional learning as well as trainings that enable them to make effective use of equipment, teaching aids and technology to ensure that each child gains as much as they can from the learning center. Training of girls and boys. The various contributors provided the technical knowledge and experience from the field. As part of the development process it will be important for SLI to work hand in hand with protection partners to continue equipping girls and boys on protections issues, skills and conflict resolution and management and GBV. Boys and girls will also be taken through child rights, responsibilities and importance of education
