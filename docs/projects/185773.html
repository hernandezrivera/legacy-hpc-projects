Project Summary

In 2022, Tdh will ensure an evidence-based health package which fulfils the essential health services for refugee and host communities in an equitable manner while ensuring accessibility and quality. Tdh primary health care facilities will avail health promotion, preventive services curative services, and access to secondary/ tertiary referrals. Systematic community-level health promotion and preventive services will continue to be rolled out through community health workers linked to the primary health facilities. Curative services will encompass IMCI, SRHR which includes, ANC, PNC, Normal delivery, B-EMONC, Preterm new-born and sepsis management, post-abortion care, Menstrual regulation, Td vaccination IFA supplementation, obstetric fistula screening and referral, voluntary counselling and testing for PMTCT and MHPSS as a multi-sectoral approach with tdh cp response which includes psychological first-aid, identification and referral of persons with mental needs, management of mental health priority conditions by mhGAP trained clinician, referral of severe casse, psychosocial interventions provided by a trained psychologist, counsellor’s, community awareness workshops, psychoeducational activities such as self-care and risk factors reduction, group psychosocial activities and individuals psychosocial services, in addition to general communicable and non-communicable disease curative services, which includes standard triage including nutrition screening, first aid, treatment of emergency cases, outpatient services. Referrals are sustained to ensure intra-camp and outside camp referrals for secondary and tertiary health care. For child health Tdh, in coordination with GOV, will provide routine immunization and individual growth monitoring and provide IYCF counselling to mothers and systematic detection of acute malnutrition among children 6 to 59 months and PLWs. Additionally, clinical diagnosis and management of anaemia among the targeted beneficiaries and referral of PLW, SAM and MAM children 6 to 59 month to OPTS or TSFPs according to nutritional status and treatment guideline will be actioned. Tdh will also continue to provide a family planning service that includes short- and long-term methods together with the community sensitization outreach program, in line with Tdh’s child protection response. Other services, such as Oral and eye care, will be explored to be provided in an integrated manner in close collaboration with primary and secondary health care providers. An important focus will be on the vulnerable groups including pregnant and post-natal women, new-born, under 5 years child including those with malnutrition, and persons with disabilities. Tdh health program also focused on adolescent health which includes adolescent-friendly service, IFA supplementation, Menstrual Health Management (MHM) through a gender-responsive, adolescent-friendly PHC service, in coordination with Tdh’s Child protection team. As Tdh’s commitment to mainstreaming GBV prevention and response messages on community-level, the Health response has a particular focus on GBV survivors, including survivor identification through referrals, self-reports, accompaniments, health provider suspects injury or condition that can be linked to violence, assessment and care for survivors of associated minor injuries, clinical management of rape survivor, also the treatment of prophylaxis of STI, emergency contraception, post-exposure prophylaxis, TT and Hepatitis B vaccination, follow up and consent-based referrals for other services. B laboratory services will also be provided among the beneficiaries. Tdh health program also looks after health care waste management.

1 - Identified Needs

Health facilities in the camps continues to be challenged, in part due to the large caseload. As per the health sector, gaps analysis identified includes essential health services; comprehensive emergency obstetric care; newborn care; family planning, and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape as well as first line support of GBV survivors. By end of September 2019, the proportion of women accessing facility-based deliveries had improved 47% as compared with 37% at the start of the year, but this remains below target and is subject to geographical variations within the camps. Risks for communicable disease persist due to living conditions in the camps. Immunization coverage among children under 1-year-old improved from 67% at the start of 2019 to 78% by end of September 2020, but substantive improvements are needed to achieve more than 95%. Capacities for non-communicable disease management, laboratory diagnostics; mental health and psychosocial support services, limited access of referral pathways from community health facilities to higher levels of care, and gender inequalities in access to health outreach, remain a major impediment to adequate treatment as observed by Tdh outreach team. Finally, the gradual increase in COVID-19 positive cases as of early March 2021 will has had a multi-dimensional impact on the refugees, host community, and humanitarian sector in terms of restricted population and logistics movements, fear of beneficiaries to access services and, associated morbidity among others. The needs analysis was informed by two core assessments, The Host Community Multi-Sector Needs Assessment conducted by Tdh in 2019 and The Rohingya Refugee Annual Multi-Sector Needs Assessment 2019, cross-referenced against secondary sources. This analysis has been updated using the 2020 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), 2020 Joint Response Plan (JRP) and other secondary sources.

2 - Activities or outputs

Provision of outpatient and inpatient curative care at the PHCs. This includes the services for disaster affected population. Provision of all basic Maternal and Newborn Health services as per minimum packages of PHC facilities. Provision of Child Health services including IMNCI, Routine immunization, growth monitoring. Provision of Adolescent health services including MHM and health promotion. This will be carried out through camp based youth centres. Provision of Family Planning services such as family planning counselling, short acting methods, referral for long acting methods. Provision of services for Gender -Based violence cases - assessment, first line support, care, clinical management, treatment and referral. Provision of Nutrition services - clinical diagnosis, IYCF corner, Referral of PLW, SAM and MAM children. Identification of Communicable diseases, management and referral as necessary. Identification, psychological first aid and referral of persons with mental health needs (special attention to vulnerable groups e.g. children, GBV survivors, persons with disability. Provision of screening, risk assessment and referral of Non Communicable disease patients. Emergency referral mechanism (24x7) including patient back referral system as per the sector referral protocol. Provision of Laboratory and Pharmacy services referring to the minimum package of essential health services for PHC facility. Maintenance of Health care waste management and IPC system in the PHC premises. Sustain community health program linked to the facility complying with the CHWG ToR and standards. Information management and accountability to DHIS - 2, EWARS reporting and patient feedback mechanism/health committee.
