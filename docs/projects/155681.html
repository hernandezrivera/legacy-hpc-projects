Project Summary

UNHAS started operation in Cameroon in November 2015, following a request from the Humanitarian Coordinator as the provision of assistance by aid organizations was hampered by vast distances, insecurity and poor road infrastructure. In the years that followed, the security situation further deteriorated and made access to northern parts of the country, particularly the Far North extremely difficult. The introduction of UNHAS flights has therefore been vital in enabling humanitarian organizations to scale up their response and reach more people in need. Since its inception, UNHAS transported 20,725 passengers, 62 MT of light cargo and served more than 50 organizations.
For 2019, the Global Humanitarian Overview (GHO) identified 4.3 million people in Cameroon in need of humanitarian assistance (a 30% increase compared to 2018), majority of whom being women and children. In order to support the planned humanitarian response and enable access to the populations in need and project implementation sites, UNHAS aims:
1. To provide NGOs, UN agencies, donor organizations and diplomatic missions in Cameroon with safe, effective and efficient access to beneficiaries and project implementation sites;
2. To transport light cargo, such as medical supplies, information and communication technology (ICT) equipment;
3. To provide adequate capacity for medical and security evacuations of humanitarian staff.

Needs

Poor road conditions and long distances delay the provision of humanitarian aid. In addition, insecurity in the northern parts of Cameroon makes road travel along some key routes dangerous, further constraining humanitarian access and service delivery. For example, it takes one day driving from the capital to the north location of Ngaoundere, and to reach Garoua it would take a two-days. UNHAS services remain essential to ensure a reliable link in-country between Cameroon's capital and locations in the Far North, North and Adamawa regions.The situation in Cameroon remains fragile and prone to extreme deterioration with little or no fore warning. In such an event, humanitarian actors may have to be temporarily moved to parts of the country with lower risk levels until appropriate mitigations are in place or improvements observed. In the event that this occurs, there is currently limited means to safely move staff quickly. The health infrastructure in northern Cameroon has limited capacity and UNHAS medical evacuation is therefore a crucial service to the humanitarian community. The deployment of UNHAS would thus enhance the ability to relocate or evacuate staff quickly and safelyThere is a commercial airline, CAMAIR-CO, that operates in the same areas, however, humanitarian actors have in the past complained of poor reliability and regular cancellations as factors affecting their ability to reach programme implementation locations on time. According to the results from the provision of access satisfaction survey conducted in November 2018, most of the respondents indicated that they rely solely on UNHAS services for air transport to reach populations in need of assistance. Therefore, UNHAS services will remain essential for the humanitarian community to reach beneficiaries and their project implementation sites in 2019.
