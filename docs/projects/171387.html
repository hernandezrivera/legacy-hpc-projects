Project Summary

Due to the ongoing humanitarian crisis, Shelter and Non-Food Items (SNFI) needs remain consistently high, with an estimated 3.1 million Somalis with SNFI needs according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, especially in Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) sites where many households (HHs) live in makeshift shelters. Oftentimes, these makeshift shelters do not provide adequate privacy and protection against weather elements, whilst a lack of tenure security, random evictions and vulnerability to fires further exacerbate the shelter needs of IDPs. It is therefore estimated that around one-third of the Somali population lack essential NFIs.
Therefore, SNFI needs are the subject of much collaborative efforts; Collective Outcome 2 of the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) aims ‘to reduce the risks and vulnerabilities, and build resilience of displaced and host communities to reach durable solutions’, whilst Specific Objective 2.2 aims to scale-up and provide CCCM, WASH, education, health, nutrition, shelter and NFI services for 1.6 million people in established IDP sites across 68 districts by the end of 2021.
For the 2021 HRP, 75,000 individuals (12,500 households with an average of 6 individuals per household) will receive NFI support through a mixed-methods strategy of in-kind contingency stocks provision to be undertaken with contingency stock partners and direct implementation of Cash-Based Intervention (CBI) for NFI needs. A further 75,000 individuals will receive shelter support through emergency shelter, in-kind shelter kits, and CBI for shelter needs. Finally, 15,000 individuals will receive infrastructure support, both in-kind and through CBI. The transfer mechanism for CBI will be evidence-based and linked to market assessment and needs analysis. The breakdown of the planned intervention is as follows: (1) the provision of contingency stock with in-kind NFI and shelter kits for 10,000 households (NFI: 7,500, Shelter: 2,500), available for distribution nationwide by shelter cluster partners where need arises; (2) emergency/transitional shelter (Emergency: 7,500; Transitional: 2,500) and NFI (5,000) support to newly displaced IDPs in the Gedo and Bay region targeting 15,000 households; (3) infrastructure support to 2,500 households in Gedo and Bay region.

Project Activities

Activity 1: carry out detailed needs and vulnerability assessments in identified and targeted communities. Activity 2: Procure and manage contingency stocks to support Shelter Cluster Partners.Activity 3: Shelter and NFI support through cash based interventions to targeted beneficiaries.Activity 4: Strengthen evidence-based programming through post distribution monitoring (PDM).Activity 5: Provide small infrastructural support to households to improve resilience of structures against natural calamities. IOM beneficiary selection guidelines are designed to provide evidence base for prioritizing humanitarian interventions to assist the affected households by defining and targeting the most vulnerable. IOM beneficiary selection method takes holistic view of vulnerability indices, sector specific needs, assist in selection of modality (in-Kind/CBI) and the context around intervention needs. The process builds on community-based selection of beneficiaries. In the context of Somalia, this refers to the identification of households/individuals eligible for assistance by the participation of the affected community in absence of formal identification. The overall objective of the beneficiary guidelines is to improve the selection method and ensure safety and security of vulnerable communities in rural and urban settlements. To ensure that assistance reaches the most vulnerable, a multi layered selection method is applied collecting information related to Demographics, Personal vulnerability, Physical vulnerability, Hazard Exposure and Severity of impact - Change in shelter adequacy / habitability. The data collection tools (needs assessment, distribution, beneficiary registration and complaint feedback mechanism tool/register) should provide sex and age desegregated data.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Using CTP instead of in kind provides beneficiaries with the flexibility to opt for the items that are needed to complement items they may already have, hence providing them with the option of making choices that suit to their circumstances.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

IOM cash-based interventions are preceded by market assessments that determine the availability of items, cost and safety of beneficiaries to receive cash support. During a market assessment, a minimum of 10 individual interviews with local service providers are undertaken. These providers are informed during the interview that participation does not guarantee that the interviewee will receive a contract offer. These interviews capture data such as whether they have access to a Somali bank account and government approved documentation; areas where supplies are procured, modes of transports, and whether they have any links to trader associations or other traders; their current stocks; and what their capacity is for working with a commodity-voucher scheme where delays of up to 3 weeks are expected.

Mitigation Measures

•Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.Likelihood: Low Impact: HighMitigation measures: (1) rotate distribution teams so that it is not always the same team constantly visiting the settlement; (2) provide clear information to distribution actors and affected populations explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services / favours to anyone in exchange for receiving SNFI assistance, ensuring that this information is communicated to women and girls. • Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA.Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation measures: (1) ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers involved in the distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse; (2) ensure all staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign, and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries; (3) in cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of community SNFI workers to monitor, report, and refer protection concerns (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. • Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation measures: (1) collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming; (2) ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable, identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community, and preventing discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups; (3) note which group may have greater difficulty finding or renting accommodation or more prone to eviction; (4) apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries for distributions; (5) ensure a lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. • Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area.Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation measures: (1) take into account the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. Advocate with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent; (2) obtain permission before using or building on any land or property, in writing where possible; (3) avoid community tensions by providing the local/host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of non-food items; (4) consult both displaced and host communities about shelter and NFI needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment, or conflict between the two communities; (5) keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use – apply effective data protection measures.
