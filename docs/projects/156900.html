Project Summary

This project will reach out, in coordination with FSL cluster, to 20375 individuals of most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in the most food insecure districts in Idleb and Aleppo with life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance and livelihoods support.
This intervention aims at assisting 75 of the most vulnerable families in the community (headed by women or disabled people) in Al-Bab, and Jebel Saman Aleppo; where the prolonged conflict has led to a decrease in livestock shrinking food security, especially for the most vulnerable men, women, girls, boys.
The project will provide each family with a dairy cow in addition to equipping sheds by providing yogurt and cheese production equipment, 50% of the needed fodder for 6 months (while the other 50% will be covered by the selling the produced milk), drugs, sterilizers and tools. Householders will receive training on cattle breeding and care mechanisms in addition to protection from hazards and diseases. Regular vaccinations will be organized in addition to artificial fertilization. Beneficiaries will be trained on feeding choice to render the best milk production in cooperation with specialized veterinary staff of experts to guarantee sustainable results.
Beneficiaries will also be trained by experts on processing milk to produce yogurt and cheese in addition to marketing mechanisms in a 3-month course in order to enhance their livelihoods. A part of the produced milk will be utilized in the training center to train families to make milk products for 3 months. After training is over, families will receive tools to produce yogurt and cheese. After 3 months of training, trainees will be able to produce milk products.
At the end of the project, the beneficiaries will keep the cows and they will get contracts signed by Local council as proof, after making sure that they are qualified enough to take care of the cows and utilize their products.
Moreover, this proposed intervention targets 1000 of the most conflict-affected families and those headed by women in addition to families with undernourished men, women, boys and girls in areas of Al Bab and Afrin, where the prolonged conflict has decreased the agricultural activities and food and agricultural security opportunities, especially for the most vulnerable families by enhancing livelihoods and resilience for the most vulnerable groups through offering home garden grants, at an average of 500 m2 for each household, by providing agricultural inputs, seeds, pesticides, fertilizers, tools and fuel (to provide necessary water), in addition training families on cultivating lands, care and protection measures to avoid hazards like frost and pests. This will be done in cooperation with a specialized team of experts and agronomist to provide guidance and ensure the success and sustainability of the project. The project covers the winter seasons starting from September.
This project also aims at urgently supporting 15000 individuals (3000 HHs) of the afflicted families in Al-Bab, Afrin and Harim in case of emergencies like mass displacement or rain and storms, especially affecting the most vulnerable families, women and children. The proposed project will contributes to saving lives and alleviating the suffering of the most vulnerable persons by providing life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable families.
During our past experience, there is a great need for food baskets and hot meals in such cases. The project will provide each family with one food basket with diverse nutrition value according to FSA cluster standards and recommendations in addition to hot meals for 30 days. Distribution will done coordination with cluster, competent authorities and other organization targeting the same area to ensure complementarity and avoid duplication.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. 57.2 percent of food-insecure households reported as having outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in 2017/2018 winter season in Syria with the lowest wheat production since 1989. The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019; restoring protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen the resilience of Syrians. In reference to the same report, AlBab, Jebel Saman, Afrin and Harim are among the districts with the highest severity of needs with al-Bab reported as severe (level6 severity) needs, and Harim and Afrin reported as of major severity (level5 severity). Jebel Saman has the biggest PIN in the whole country with 872,769 food insecure PIN, Harim comes in the second place in northwest Syria with (266,199 PIN), Al-Bab came in third place with 204,608 PIN, while Afrin occupies third place in the northwest in food insecurity prevalence (40%) at (76491 PIN) and, according to WoS Food Security and Livelihood Assessment, Afrin has zero (0%) food secure population. Moreover, Harim, Afrin and al-Bab are borderline or relatively safe areas, where IDPs are expected to arrive in case of any escalation in the conflict or any change in the control map leading to new influxes of IDPs. Most vulnerable and at-risk groups include children under5, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly, people with specific needs, and those people chronically ill due to their need for nutrition and health concerns, so this project will aim to specifically prioritize families which include these vulnerable groups.

Implementation of activities

SDI, through its field team, will engage with local community leaders and other actors and stakeholders in the project area to inform them of the project, coordinate beneficiary selection and involve them in designing actitivies. SDI will review beneficiary lists and pay home visits to verify they meet the following criteria: Direct beneficiaries have been affected by conflict and are in need of assistance, prioritizing the most recent IDPs; female, child, disabled, and elderly-headed households; and then families with children under 5, elderly, undernourished or disabled members and pregnant and lactating women.Concerning the cow grant, we will announce the cow grant, receive applications, make home visits to verify their living condition and that they have a piece of land and a proper place to keep the cow and ask for a recommendation from the local council before choosing the family. Concerning homesteads grant, the beneficiaries should have access to a 500m2 agricultural piece of land.All items will be procured in a transparent and competitive manner according to SDI’s policy and procedures to ensure impartially and rule out fraud. Cow beneficiairies will receive one cow per family in addition to equipping sheds by providing yogurt and cheese production equipment, 50% of the needed fodder for 6 months (while the other 50% will be covered by the selling the produced milk), drugs, sterilizers and tools. For agricultural inputs distribution activities, each household will receive seeds, pesticides, fertilizers, tools and fuel (to provide necessary water) to cover 500m2 of land, in addition to training families on cultivating lands, care and protection measures to avoid hazards like frost and pests. SDI's technical team of agronomists and veterinarians will supervise the process of taking care of cows and cultivating lands and train beneficiaries on needed work utilize their assets for income generating acitvities in order to ensure success and sustainability of the project. For the distribution of food baskets and hot meals, distribution place and time will be agreed on with local authorities, will be conducted in a clearly advertised, efficiently run manner, and will consider the protection of the beneficiaries. Materials will be delivered to houses/places of beneficiaries who cannot come to distribution centers. For all activities, female staff will be recruited to ensure more protection and access for girls and women. Complaint mechanism is in place to receive feedback, complaint numbers and email will be well-advertised at workplaces; brochures of safety and security and best practice will be distributed to beneficiaries. Training will be organized to enable cow and land beneficiaries to utilize their assets in the best way.An M&E plan is in place including beneficiary verification, PIM and focus groups to ensure quality implementation and project compliance with the adopted plan, indicators and outputs. Progress reports, M&E reports will be prepared in addition to a final report that sums up all achievements, financial info, impacts and lesson learned.
