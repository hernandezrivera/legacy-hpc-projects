Project Summary

-The targeted sub-districts in Aleppo, Raqqa, and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates are on a severity scale of 3-5.
-An estimated 5% (at least) of targeted beneficiaries has a disability.
-Approximate cost per beneficiary is 44$, including school rehabilitation and distributions.
-Total funds received in 2022, including carry over from 2021: approx 2.7mil USD.
-The number of beneficiaries reached in between 2020 and 2022 is over 120.000 individuals, including the rehabilitation of more than 75 schools.
-The project is partially funded at this stage.
-WWGVC is an active member of the Education Sector, including multiple working groups and sub-sectors in Aleppo and Deir-ez-Zor.
-The project directly contributes to Sector HRP objectives and activities. WWGVC will report on the 4Ws for all activities implemented within this project.
Considering the needs and in line with the No Lost Generation Initiative, context analysis, and demographic trends, the intervention aims at re-establishing basic education services in the targeted communities, which are return, underserved, and newly accessible areas. The Action aims at increasing access to safe, inclusive, and quality education for children and adolescents, relying on WW-GVC expertise in NFE (Non-Formal Education), solid reputation, and knowledge of the areas.
Over the course of the project, WW-GVC will implement the following activities. Firstly, in order to increase availability and access to education:
-Rehabilitation of at least 225 classrooms, benefitting at least 11000 students; When rehabilitation is not feasible, the project will consider implementation of pre-fabbricated classrooms or the use of other public spaces for educational purposes;
-Rehabilitation of 18 WASH in-school facilities to ensure access to safe water and sanitation to at least 11000 students;
-Back To Learning (BTL) campaigns targeting 15000 individuals, including door-to-door visits to identify out of school children (OOSC) and the main barriers to education access; interactive theatre events with OOSC; Focus Group Discussion with community members on the importance of inclusive education; distribution of awareness materials;
-Self Learning Program for 4000 OOSC; Remedial Classes for 6000 students (RCs); both including the distribution of school materials;
-Vocational trainings and distribution of start-up kits, benefitting 400 students, including the production of materials conducive to a safer school environment, such as reusable PPE, desks, chairs, other school furniture, training on solar systems and the implementation of solar panels; furthermore, vocational trainings will prioritize households with OoSC in order to increase income and favour re-enrolment;
-Distribution of furniture, small repairs to existing structures, school supplies (including the distribution of vouchers), heating equipment, School in a Box kits, and schools bags, benefitting at least 15.000 students;
In parallel, to improve the quality of education, WW-GVC will:
-Train 800 teachers in Active Learning (AL), PSS, Hygiene Promotion (HP) and Risk Education (RE);
-Deliver school awareness campaigns in HP, RE, and inclusive school model for 12000 students;
-Distribute recreational materials benefitting at least 2.000 students;
-Distribute incentives for teachers to support the transportation costs, benefitting at least 300 teachers working in rural areas;
-Carry out life-skills development activities throughout SLP and RCs programs, supporting the wellbeing of 10,000 OOSC and students at risk of drop out.
Finally, to improve the capacity of the education system of the targeted areas, WW-GVC will:
-Provide training to DoE staff on data management and IT, targeting at least 80 DoE staff,
-Implement PTA at community level, targeting at least 15 communities.

3 - Needs

Intervention sub-districts are selected based on the Sector’s HNO, OCHA severity scale, assessments conducted by WW-GVC including infrastructure analysis and an evaluation of completed projects, and secondary data (e.g. DoE, other INGOs, MoE, etc). All selected sub-districts fall under severity scale score 3-5. Main problems for the incoming year include: lack of safe learning spaces, high rate of non attendance, significant decrease in attendance for children older than 12yo, high percentage of children lamenting unclean and unsafe WASH facilities, child labour, presence of mines/IEDs, overcrowded classrooms, incidence of early marriage and other protection issues. During the most recent BTL campaigns, WWGVC identified more than 1500 OoSC in rural Deir-Ez-Zor and more than 2000 in Aleppo. The main reason for the lack of school attendance is economic, with 48% of families citing this as the main reason for drop-outs. Further assessments identified a high rate of drop-out among CwD, both due to social stigma and physical barriers. Discussions and KIIs with education staff highlighted the lack of materials, lack of trainings, and difficulties in covering the cost of transportation, especially when working in rural areas, leading to understaffed schools in the most vulnerable communities. Finally, lack of facilities and materials for secondary and TVET education is leading to drop-outs and loss of livelihoods.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project is preliminarily discussed and approved by the Ministry of Education (MoE), carried out in cooperation with the Directorates of Education (DoEs) and Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC), and constantly monitored by WW-GVC in each governorate thanks to WW-GVC presence in the field with local offices in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor. The MEAL Department in Damascus manages monitoring and follow-up tools to ensure supervision and evaluation of activities’ impact, informing about possible ongoing adjustments of modalities and contents. In particular, the MEAL team is composed of a MEAL Manager and a MEAL Officer based in Damascus, and two MEAL Officers based in Aleppo and Deir-Er-Zor respectively. Rehabilitation works are supervised by WW-GVC Engineers and assistant Engineers, ensuring that selected companies deliver quality and timely works; possibly, rehabilitations will be conducted out of school time to avoid affecting the regular and safe implementation of classes. NFE activities will be conducted by trained teachers, using contents/time schedules and modalities agreed at national level (by MoE and Education Sector). School awareness campaigns will rely on standard contents made available by national and international duty-bearers (e.g. DoH/SARC/UNICEF/UNMAS). For the distribution of incentives, close cooperation with DoE and monitoring of the beneficiaries is key. Across all the Action, WW-GVC will collect data of beneficiaries in databases for analysis and monitoring.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WWGVC has a child safeguarding policy aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence. The policy contains a set of standards that all WWGVC staff must take into consideration, including: • placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations • reporting any concerns cases of suspected child abuse or concerns about the welfare of a minor • planning and organizing project activities whilst minimizing risks for children • avoiding culturally inappropriate physical contact with children WWGVC is committed to create and maintain an environment where children’s well-being is promoted in every aspect of its operations. Preventions measures include: • AWARENESS RAISING: All new staff is introduced to the Child Safeguarding Policy. • TRAINING: WW-GVC commits itself to conduct periodical training in relation to this Policy to adequately inform the staff, raise their awareness and enhance their capacity in preventing, and dealing with possible events. • RISK ASSESSMENT: WWGVC endeavors to reduce these risks to a bare minimum. Regular risk assessments are carried out in order to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization’s operations. • SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AS. -Security is stable -Access is safe for staff/companies to conduct rehabilitation works/activities -If schools are not accessible anymore because of security, DoE will select new schools. -Schools are accessible for SLP and RCs and beneficiaries can easily reach them -Relevant authorities approve the activation of NFE programs -Local communities in the areas of intervention are positive with regard to the services provided -Prices are not subject to variations than those already foreseen and materials are available on local market RISKS:1.Security situation may deteriorate, preventing implementation of activities. Mitigating measure: coordination with partners/international agencies (UNDSS) to monitor the situation; WW-GVC adopts a security plan and SOP in case of major accidents; 2.Shrinking of operational space due to military operations. Mitigating measure: policy environment is monitored by attendance to Clusters, WGs and coordination mechanisms 3.Major demographic changes in the areas of intervention requires modification in the strategy. Mitigating measure: WW-GVC monitors population movements and liaise with other agencies and with the Sector 4.Major change in attitude of authorities/partners toward planned intervention. Mitigating measure: WW-GVC ensures good relationship with authorities, informing them of activities. It ensures a transparent and accountable way of working 5.Diversion of aid from staff. Mitigating measures: WW-GVC direct monitoring of activities.
