Project Summary

Medair has been implementing a multisectoral programming in Sudan since September 2020 that integrates WASH, Health, Nutrition and NFIs interventions in Khartoum State specifically in Jebel Awlia locality. The above-named project targets the IDPs and host and Refugee populations in Blue Nile and Khartoum States. Medair intents to ensure provision and improve in the access of safe water while strengthening existing management systems, support in safe human waste disposal and management through sanitation facilities and promoting better hygiene promotion activities focused on handwashing at critical time, personal hygiene targeting 34,219 persons in need (IDP and host)

1 - Needs

Due to unexpected flooding’s, internal conflicts and outbreak of epidemics the COVID-19 pandemic, provision of and accessibility for WASH services has been severely disrupted. With approximately 68% of the Households in Sudan having access to both improved water sources and sanitation (MICS 2014) in Sudan. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2021, COVID-19 is one of several key drivers that has resulted in 9.1 million people including 1.14 million refugees lacking access to sufficient WASH services. This includes 1.4 million lacking access to a sufficient quantity and quality of water, 3.7 million lacking access to basic sanitation and 5.03 million lacking access to hygiene materials and facilities. According to the Multi hazard Preparedness and Response Plan for April 2020, a total of 156,886 persons in entire Blue Nile State were affected with by floods(1.6%),,WASH related diseases (97.8%) and others hazards (0.6%) .As the context improves with regard to accessibility, With the improving context in terms of accessibility, there is anticipated come back for increase in returnees from South Sudan and Ethiopia refugee camps, internally displaced persons to Bau, Geissan and Kurmuk localities with reported cases in Ullu village (near South Sudan border). These locations have been reported to have major gaps for accessibility of WASH services. Enormous displacement of communities was evident resulting to in host and IDP set populations increasing the strain on existing water, sanitation, and hygiene infrastructures. Limited and /or inadequate safe water supply caused by dilapidated, non-functional water supply sources, unreliable water sources, and damaged drainage systems and weak institutional frameworks for managing water resources. All these has resulted to community moving longer distances to collect water and accessing unsafe protected sources while creating protection concerns to children and women. Both high- and moderate-income earners can opt for purchasing of water from water vendors at 150 SDGs (for a barrel of 220 litres) while low- or no-income earners who cannot afford, rely on water from unprotected sources like rivers and Haffirs. Inadequate water collection and storage containers was observed and cited by the community especially in locations where water sources are unavailable Sanitation coverage has stagnated with many families practicing open defecation for households, depriving the majority of the population of their basic human right. On 2 September 2019, the first case of suspected AWD or cholera was reported in Roseires locality in Blue Nile. Overall, 202 cases were admitted in Blue Nile and 132 in with eight deaths recorded respectively. Crude latrine coverage for different locations differs within the villages communities with majority population practicing open defecation, especially the IDPs. During the wet season, most of the constructed latrines by the community collapsed resulting to community sharing facilities and others practicing open defecation up to date. Communities interviewed are aware and acknowledge the need of having safe human waste disposal. Improper hygiene practices is the greatest concern in Blue Nile, poor handwashing, personal hygiene, unsafe water handling and storage, etc and which has been occasioned by minimum water available for hygiene promotion practices. During the focus group discussions for women and girls, an understanding on practicing good hygiene practices as the only way of preventing outbreak of WASH related diseases in the community and institutions (health facilities, schools). Additionally, menstrual hygiene was cited as a great need to women and girls with lack of safe hygienic kits to be used during the menstrual period. Increased absenteeism for school going children was evident depriving the right to education for the girl child.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Water SupplyProvision and access to safe water supply for community targeting 16,468 (Host and IDPs) in Algeri, Alazaza villages to access at least 15 liters/person/day having 0/100ml Fecal Coliform with Free Residual Chlorine ranging 0.2 to 1.0mg/l at the water point or household level. The rehabilitation non-functional Handpumps, treatment and strengthening the capacity of Water management committees on operation and maintenance in support with the Water and Environment Sanitation department. Additionally, we will look for ways to increase transparency, accountability, and cost effectiveness through the use of technology to monitor the performance and use of rehabilitated handpumps. Medair intends to pilot remote monitoring technology for data collection and tracking of 12 handpumps which includes training and liaising with WES and respective committees. WASH NFIsSupport and distribute needed WASH NFIs to 1,300 vulnerable households the affected population(host and IDP in Algeri(500) , Alazaza(300) villages and Jebel Awlia (500) in and which will could include such items as water collection/storage containers, water treatment tabs, P&G water purifier ,ceramic sand water filters point of use water filters, etc. With households relying on water from unprotected water sources like Haffirs that is consumed without any treatment methods, the training on the use and distribution of water treatment tabs for a period of 3 months and ceramic point of use filters or filter clothes will be help in ensuring safe consumption of water especially for drinking purposes. SanitationSupport and promote sanitation messaging on safe excreta disposal facilities as strengthening and follow up on Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) approach. The communities will be encouraged to newly construct and/or rehabilitate excreta disposal facilities (individual household or family shared) using locally available materials with routine sanitation promotional messaging conducted to in the targeted population (practicing open defecation. with continuous awareness creation past CLTS. Additionally,1,400 vulnerable households will be supported with handwashing facilities (ibriks or tippy taps) as an incentive to encourage their construction of their latrines and support in handwashing. Hygiene promotionMedair will partner with local organization to continue with awareness creation and dissemination of basic hygiene promotion messaging on handwashing at critical times, personal and environmental hygiene, safe water handling and storage through house to house sessions and planned campaigns using hygiene promotion approaches targeting the 34,219 people affected population in Algeri ,Alazaza and Jebel Awlia locations. Any behaviour change communication will seek to build on previous efforts and to tailor the messaging specifically for the community through such approaches as RANAS10 and WASH’em In ensuring behaviour Change Communication approach is properly designed, The RANAS approach in will be used in combination with the training if relevant actors including Ministry of Health, national NGOs and WASH partners.
