Project Summary

ALIGHT formerly known as American Refugee Committee (ARC) nutrition response will focus on preventive and curative nutrition interventions in Morobo, Kajo Keji and Aweil West Counties. The response will primarily target children under five years both boys and girls as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) among the host community, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees in the above-mentioned locations. ALIGHT is present in Kajo Keji County implementing integrated Nutrition, Protection and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) programmes whereas in Aweil West County, ALIGHT is implementing nutrition programmes. It is important to point out that ALIGHT will scale up nutrition interventions to Morobo County in January 2023.
The proposed interventions include prevention of acute malnutrition through behaviour change communication to improve Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices in the community; prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns targeting children under five years; prevention of malnutrition through provision of Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) to children aged 6 to 23 months and PLW; Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) through the Stabilization Centre (SC), Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) and community mobilization where the community volunteers will be engaged to identify and support enrolment of children and PLW with acute malnutrition before their condition deteriorates. In addition, ALIGHT will work in coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, Education, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Health to strengthen integration and linkages of services with the goal of preventing acute malnutrition and to enhance recovery of beneficiaries undergoing nutrition rehabilitation. The proposed interventions seek to reduce morbidity and prevent mortality associated with acute malnutrition and subsequently improve maternal and child health.
The proposed interventions are in line with the nutrition cluster objectives which seeks to (a) increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women; (b) increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition; (c) Enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information, and (d) Increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2023.
ALIGHT will operate thirty-two static nutrition sites in Aweil West County, nine static and ten outreach nutrition sites in Kajo Keji County, and four static and nine outreach nutrition sites in Morobo County. The project aims to reach 7,081 children aged 6 to 59 months with severe acute malnutrition, 23,960 children aged 6 to 59 months with moderate acute malnutrition, 16,045 PLW with acute malnutrition, 44,287 caregivers/mothers with children under two years through individual MIYCN counselling sessions, 9,804 children aged 6 to 23 months and 8,170 PLW through BSFP in Aweil West County, 94,109 children aged 6 to 59 months with Vitamin A supplements and deworm 77,501 children aged 12 to 59 months with Albendazole tablets. The proposed interventions will be implemented in accordance with the Ministry of Health's CMAM and MIYCN protocols.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC report released on 3rd November 2022, an estimated 2.1 million people, including 1.4 million children under five years, and 737,812 PLW in South Sudan, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2023. The high burden of malnutrition is attributed to elevated levels of food insecurity owing to unsustainable livelihoods due to localized conflicts, high inflation resulting to exorbitant food prices and climatic shocks (severe dry spells & flooding); high prevalence of diseases; poor sanitation conditions; sub-optimal maternal, infant and young child feeding practices and limited access to healthcare. Moreover, 6.31 million and 7.76 million people in South Sudan are expected to experience severe acute food insecurity from December 2022 to March 2023 and April to July 2023, respectively. ALIGHT is currently implementing preventive and curative nutrition interventions in Kajo Keji and Aweil West Counties. Additionally, Alight will scale up nutrition interventions to Morobo County in January 2023. Kajo Keji and Morobo are among the most affected Counties in South Sudan by the continued localized conflicts that has led to massive displacement of the population within these Counties and to the refugee camps in the neighbouring Countries. However, from 2019, the number of returnees has increased owing to the improved security situation in South Sudan following the formation of the revitalized transitional government of national unity, testified reduction of food ration offered in refugees camps, unemployment and livelihood opportunities, and limited access to basic services in the Countries of refuge. An increase in the returnees is likely to overstretch the existing basic services and food reserves among the host population, leading to poor household food security. In addition, the population in these Counties are exposed to the risk of acute malnutrition due to food insecurity attributed to recurrent conflicts and invasion of cattle keepers from neighbouring Counties that has disrupted normal livelihoods coupled with other aggravating factors such as inadequate safe water, poor sanitation facilities, and limited access to health care services, all common during displacements. This is consistent with the results of the IPC report released in November 2022, which projected that Kajo Keji, and Morobo would experience worse acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) between December 2022 and July 2023. Aweil West County has had better political stability as compared to other parts of the Country; however, it has been significantly affected by the consequences of protracted conflict in the Country which has resulted in severe economic crisis and subsequently high inflation. Moreover, erratic weather conditions i.e., severe floods and dry spells have resulted in poor harvests. High inflation and poor harvests have adversely contributed to food insecurity and subsequently the high burden of acute malnutrition. This is consistent with the results of the IPC report released in November 2022 where Aweil West is projected to experience serious acute malnutrition (IPC Phase 3) from November 2022 to February 2023 and deteriorate to critical acute malnutrition situation (IPC Phase 4) from March to June 2023. In addition, Aweil West is projected to experience worse acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) between December 2022 to March 2023 and the situation is projected to worsen from April to July 2023 and the population is likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Malnutrition can have long-term negative effects particularly during the first 1,000 days of a child’s life i.e., from pregnancy through to two years of age, therefore, prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition among children under five years as well as pregnant and lactating women through an integrated multi sectoral response programming remains a significant need in the targeted locations.

2 - Project activities

ALIGHT will implement the following activities to achieve the objectives of the project:• Prevent acute malnutrition through behaviour change communication approach by conducting MIYCN individual counselling and group education sessions at the nutrition facilities and in the community as well as cooking demonstration sessions to mothers/caregivers with children aged 6 to 23 months using locally available foods to educate on appropriate complementary foods and facilitation of mother-to-mother support groups.• Prevent micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns in collaboration with the State Ministry of Health and the County health department. • Conduct nutrition screening targeting children under five years as well as PLW in the community through Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and strengthen the family led MUAC screening approach to enable timely identification of malnourished cases and referral for treatment at the nutrition facilities.• Treatment of severely malnourished children under five years with medical complications through the Stabilization Centre (SC) in a health facility as per IMSAM guidelines.• Treatment of children 6 to 59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications through the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) as per the CMAM guidelines.• Treatment of children 6 to 59 months with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) through the Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) as per the CMAM guidelines.• Treatment of PLW with acute malnutrition through the TSFP as per the CMAM guidelines.• Conduct home visits for children and PLW enrolled in the CMAM programme through the CNVs.• Conduct exit interviews to programme beneficiaries to establish their satisfaction, obtain feedback and areas for improvement on the quality of services provided at the nutrition sites.• Promote community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery.• Conduct routine support supervision and monitoring of MIYCN and CMAM activities at the nutrition facilities and in the community to identify capacity gaps and subsequently conduct on the job trainings and mentorship sessions to address the gaps.• Participate in the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Surveys at the County level. • Collect, compile and submit monthly nutrition reports to the nutrition cluster through the Nutrition Information System.• Support the roll-out of the data quality assurance tool at the County level.• In collaboration with the WASH cluster, provide WASH kits to children with SAM at the nutrition sites.• Sensitize households on the correct use of aqua tabs through the CNVs.• Sensitize the community and programme beneficiaries with optimal hygiene and sanitation practices.• In conjunction with the health cluster, provide LLITN for malaria prevention to children and PLW with acute malnutrition at the nutrition sites.• With support of the health partners, test and treat malaria among children under five years with acute malnutrition at the nutrition sites. • In partnership with the health partners, manage diarrhoea among children at the nutrition sites through provision of zinc and ORS.• With support of the FSL cluster, establish and maintain kitchen gardens at nutrition sites or within the vicinity to demonstrate to the programme beneficiaries the production and utilization of nutrient-dense foods.• Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM by equipping the nutrition sites with child play materials.• Train nutrition workers on gender and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) including safe referrals for GBV survivors to facilitate identification of GBV survivors at the nutrition sites for support.• Together with the GBV partners, conduct GBV safety audit in each nutrition site to identify and address protection/safety needs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ALIGHT will ensure that the most vulnerable members of the community are engaged at all phases of the project life cycle i.e. project design, planning, implementation and evaluation to obtain their contribution and ensure their unique needs are cared for. Furthermore, to avoid marginalization of vulnerable community members, more than one channel of communication will be available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Examples include one-on-one meetings at the nutrition sites and office, and discussions with a staff member during community events. In addition, to eliminate communication barrier, ALIGHT will engage staff from the local community who are well versed with the local language.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ALIGHT is committed to strengthening accountability to the affected population by listening to feedback and addressing grievances from those who participate, support or are affected by the program by giving both a voice, and the opportunity to influence key decisions. The aim is to build strong relationships with the community and to allow community feedback to shape and improve programming ensuring that interventions are relevant and appropriate. ALIGHT has already established a relationship of two-way communication and transparency with target communities. Program plans will be communicated to the communities during inception and will continue throughout the implementation period through open forums and focus group discussions. In addition, ALIGHT values complaints and feedback because it helps in improving programming. Focal points will be identified and oriented at all sites to lead on community complaints/accountability response mechanisms, including listening to and reporting feedback and complaints, ensuring timely response, and sensitizing communities on use and purpose of these mechanisms. Furthermore, ALIGHT through the Project Management Committees in each nutrition site, will provide a means for beneficiaries to voice complaints and provide feedback on areas relevant to operations in a safe and dignified manner.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ALIGHT condemns and prohibits any behavior on the part of an ALIGHT employee or volunteer which constitutes any form of sexual misconduct, including sexual harassment, sexual exploitation, and sexual violence towards any other staff member, beneficiary, or other individual participating in an ALIGHT program or activity. All new employees and volunteers will receive an initial orientation and periodic refresher training on protection of sexual exploitation and abuse policy. Employees and volunteers will also sign a document verifying their understanding of the policy. Subsequently all employees, volunteers, and beneficiaries will be made aware of how to report complaints of sexual exploitation and abuse. The beneficiaries will be made aware of ALIGHT’s code of conduct and compliant mechanism through meetings and discussions conducted through on-going activities for instance during sessions held before distributions, community mobilization sessions etc. throughout the project implementation period. ALIGHT will investigate all allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse while keeping it confidential, and violations of the policy will lead to immediate termination of employment.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ALIGHT will engage the local authorities, community leaders, representative of different groups e.g. people living with disabilities, influential members of the community such as religious leaders etc. and the targeted beneficiaries to outline project’s plan and purpose during project inception and throughout the implementation period through open forums and focus group discussions. In addition, ARC will conduct exit interviews to programme beneficiaries to establish their satisfaction, obtain feedback and areas for improvement on the quality of services provided at the nutrition sites. The feedback obtained from the current project progress will be used to shape and improve programming, ensuring that interventions are relevant and appropriate and meets their unique needs. For instance, identification of safe and accessible distribution points for the targeted beneficiaries for nutrition services, jointly planning distribution schedules so that times are convenient and safe for women and children.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ALIGHT will adhere to the IASC international standards and guidelines while delivering the proposed humanitarian services. ALIGHT will uphold the “Do No Harm” principle and as much as possible minimize any negative impact the proposed interventions may have on the beneficiaries and ensure their safety, dignity and respect is prioritized during service delivery. ALIGHT will also uphold the principles of impartiality, neutrality and participation in delivery of services by giving equal opportunity without any biasness to the members of the community regardless of their status, age, gender or affiliations etc. ALIGHT will regularly conduct gender based violence safety audits in nutrition sites to identify and address protection/safety needs. Additionally, ALIGHT will sensitize the nutrition workers and volunteers on gender and gender based violence, and safe referral ways for gender based violence survivors so that they are able to identify and refer survivors for support. ALIGHT will also work with all program beneficiaries to plan the schedules so that times are convenient and safe for women and children. ALIGHT will provide services in a manner that reduces the time spent at, travelling to and returning from nutrition service points. For isolated beneficiaries who take over a day to travel to the nutrition site, an appropriate ration will be considered after verification with community nutrition volunteers or establishment of an outreach/mobile site in the respective area. To mitigate gender-based violence, both men and women will be included in the process of selecting a safe and accessible distribution points where applicable for nutrition services.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

An investment in disaster risk reduction and climate change adaptation is needed to avoid recurrent floods. ALIGHT will work with other stakeholders at State and County level to reduce the vulnerability to floods as well as build the resilience of the community to cope. ALIGHT will advocate and contribute to the flood preparedness plans with clear climate early warning systems to prepare people before the floods strikes, conduct a feasibility assessment of suitable locations for a possible relocation of the current flood prone settlement areas. The assessment will include identification of high grounds and evaluation of land use for other purposes such as farming and grazing. In addition, ALIGHT will mobilize the community to build dykes to prevent overflowing water from settlements areas. Furthermore, ALIGHT will incorporate disaster risk reduction with focus on floods in trainings as well as sensitize the communities on the same.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

ALIGHT will sensitize the nutrition workers and volunteers on gender and gender based violence, and safe referral ways for gender based violence survivors so that they are able to identify and refer survivors for support in a timely fashion. In addition, the nutrition workers and volunteers will work closely with their counterparts in the Protection sector to ensure that any project's beneficiary that need support either in the community or at the nutrition site is attended. Also, the programme beneficiaries will be informed about referral pathways for GBV survivors.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

ALIGHT will strengthen coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, GBV, FSL and Health to enhance integration and linkages of services to improve treatment outcomes of beneficiaries under nutrition rehabilitation and after discharge from the programme to prevent new cases of acute malnutrition. ALIGHT will train project staff and volunteers on promotion of kitchen/home gardening. The nutrition workers and volunteers will thereafter sensitize the targeted beneficiaries and the community at large on kitchen/home gardening and encourage them to establish kitchen gardens at home. Kitchen/home gardening will build the resilience of the population through growing a variety of foods for consumption at household level which will increase food production and preservation of the surplus for future or selling for income. Besides, food produced will be sold to buy essentials that cannot be grown at the household level.
