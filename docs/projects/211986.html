Project Summary

Out of the 4,5 million people living in Northwest Syria, an estimated 4.1
million are in need of water assistance. There is no government
infrastructure in this area, thus the main part of the population relies on
informal and unprocessed water sources for their daily needs, according to
a recent humanitarian situation report by UNOCHA. This region is highly
vulnerable due to armed conflict, earthquakes, and a recent cholera
outbreak. Water resources and infrastructure have been damaged, leading
to devastating consequences for civilian populations impeding access to
safe and reliable water. Overcrowded reception centres lacking proper
water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities increase the risk of communicable
diseases, further undermining cholera control efforts. The region has
already reported a significant number of suspected cholera cases,
particularly in Aleppo and Idleb. Harim and Idleb in Idleb governorate, as
well as Azaz, Jebel Saman, and Afrin in Aleppo governorate, are the
districts most affected by cholera. The situation is equally alarming in
camps and urgent WASH assistance is required. The potential non
renewal of the UN resolution on humanitarian cross-border operations from
Turkey to Syria worsens the already critical situation in Northwest Syria.
While some organizations provide WASH services, there is a significant
gap to be addressed. Distributing water filtration systems faces logistical
challenges, including dependency on imports and increased prices of
construction materials in local markets, which affect ongoing and planned
activities. Technical difficulties in adapting devices to specific water
sources and communication challenges with manufacturing companies
outside Northwest Syria hinder effective utilization. The current water
filtration systems also face operational challenges, such as reliance on
conventional electricity supply, lack of spare parts and high costs for
repairs and maintenance. The absence of renewed cross-border aid
further restricts humanitarian assistance and intensifies the water crisis. To
effectively tackle the crisis, finding local solutions and empowering the
local community is crucial. Thus, there is an urgent need for innovative
solutions to address these pressing challenge: • Providing clean water to
communities facing extreme scarcity of this vital resource. • Reducing the
possibilities of waterborne and spreading water related diseases (e.g.,
cholera) due to the lack of proper water sanitation/treatment systems. •
Lack of knowledge/competence and means of the local communities in
these disaster areas to address the water challenge themselves to be
more self-sustained. • Reduce dependency on external solutions like
transporting water systems and mitigate the extreme difficulties in cross
border transport impeding the populations access to safe and reliable
water.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

This project is an innovative one; It will follow the Bottom Up innovation approach for finding the specific solution, we are going to follow the below : Local innovation: encourage and support the establishment of local manufacturing facilities for water filtration systems and related equipment, by leveraging Field Ready Türkiye’s experience and expertise with an existing Innovation Lab in Northwest Syria and based on utilizing technological assets and prototype designing to develop alternative and local based solutions. Operation optimization: explore innovative and costefficient solutions to sustain and operate water filtration systems. For example, consider powering the filtration systems with renewable energy sources, which can help reduce operating costs. Additionally, implementing early malfunction diagnosis systems can minimize repair costs associated with the power system. Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs): Foster collaboration between the private sector and local authorities through PPPs to drive sustainable water management initiatives. These partnerships combine government oversight and regulatory frameworks with private sector efficiency and investment. By leveraging the strengths of both sectors, sustainable solutions can be developed and implemented. Technology Adaptation: Collaboration with the private sector to adapt existing water filtration technologies to suit local conditions and available resources. This may involve modifying or optimizing filtration systems to effectively treat water from local sources, considering specific water quality parameters and regional challenges. Working with local technology providers and experts enables customization and better alignment with the local context. Material Sourcing: Engage with the private sector to identify and utilize locally available materials for constructing water filtration systems. This includes exploring local sources of filtration components and other materials that meet quality standards. Involving local suppliers and utilizing indigenous resources helps establish a sustainable supply chain and reduces dependence on imported materials
