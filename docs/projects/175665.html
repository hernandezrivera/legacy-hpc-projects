Project Summary

this project will serve the local and host community in our project locations. the aim of this project is to alleviate hunger and destitution among the communities that we serves. some of the activities that will be implemented to achieve this goal includes cash based interventions, distribution of livelihoods inputs and youth economic empowerment activities such as skills training and access to finance. this project will be implemented in some counties of the six state that Plan International has presence including Lakes, Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Western Equatoria, Upper Nile and Jonglei.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is undergoing a serious political, economic, social and environmental crisis. These multiple factors have caused serious economic, social and psychological suffering through loss of assets, displacement, deaths , lack of proper health and education services. All these are compounded by high poverty levels among the people caused by many years of war that led to deprivation, marginalization and neglect. Due to all these factors the vulnerability of many people has increased and therefore their coping strategies have shifted from normal to abnormal coping strategies, thus affecting their resilience to most of the shocks and stresses. The shocks and stresses have affected the populations differently. Children, women , the youth , the disabled , people living with HIV/AIDS and the elderly have been worse affected. It is projected that between December 2020 –March 2021 , the food security situation is likely to deteriorate with 5.8 million people severely food insecure (IPC 3,4,5) according to the latest IPC report. About 108,000 people are in catastrophic (IPC phase 5) . This figure is projected to rise to 7.2 million figure during the April-July- 2021 lean season. Reliance on subsistence rain fed agriculture exposes farmers in Upper Nile, Jonglie, Eastern, Central and Lakes States to local climate related shocks such as delayed/erratic rains, drought, soil degradation and flooding. Poor access to inputs, training and financial services particularly for women contributes to low production. In 2020 in the States (lakes , Jonglei, Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Western Equatoria and Central Equatoria), faced threat of climate , economic , health and conflict related shocks , including delayed rains , prolonged dry spell, locust infestation and worse floods in Jonglei that affected the crop yield leading to hike in food prices of. violent conflict drove widespread displacement, blocked farmers from crop-fields, and scorched farms. Cattle raiding in Greater Kapoeta, Jonglei and Lakes states have led to loss of livelihoods assets. The slow implementation of the revitalized peace agreement has led to increased insecurity in some parts of the country and therefore, continued people displacement, loss of assets, disruption of livelihoods including market and adaption of destructive copping strategies such as cutting trees for fire wood and charcoal, child labour, abandonment. In order to prevent a dramatic deterioration of food insecurity, malnutrition and associated social crisis and child protection risks, the need to provide timely and appropriate recovery and resilience building humanitarian and livelihood assistance in 2021 is more needed and important than ever. Based on our experience in programming in our areas of operation informed by the various assesments and review meetings done with various stakeholders plus discussions with communities, other NGOs and local government, the following needs were identified: 1. The immediate need is to continue providing safe access to food through direct food distribution through in-kind and/or Cash/Voucher to the most vulnerable housleholds. 2. Getting support to protect and rehabilitate their livelihood assets through provision of Livelihoods inputs to enhance their capacities to cope with recurring shock and reduce their reliance on food aid. In other needs that come up inlcudes creation of community assets through cash for work projects 3. Communities and youth needs to be food secure and build sustainable livelihoods through youth economic empowerment initiatives such as skills training for the youth, formation of SACCOs and VSLAs, business start-ups and creation of access to loan services.

2 - Project activities

Main activities for each objective Objective 1.provide sustainable lifesaving food assistance through cash and voucher to most vulnerable 15,800 individuals (51% Female, 49% male). (Response to cluster objective 1) 1.1 Distribute cash or voucher to purchase food and basic needs to 15,800 vulnerable Individuals Objective 2. Protect and stabilize livelihoods for 11,170 vulnerable HHs (female/male 51/49%) (60,000 individuals) through provision of Livelihoods inputs (seeds and tools plus fishing gears (Response to cluster objective 2) 2.1: Distribute Livelihoods inputs(crop, vegetable and fishing kits to 10,000 HH (68,020 individuals)2.2: protect livelihoods through creation nd rehabilitation of assets through cash for work activities for 1,170 HHs (7,020 individuals) 2.3: Train 1,000 individuals on Appropriate agronomics practices 2.4: support provision of veterinary services through community based initiatives to 20,000 livestock. Objective 3: Communities and Youth become food secure and build sustainable livelihoods through creation of access to finance and markets for 2,000 young people (51% Female, 49% Male). 3.1 train 800 youth on specific skills as per the local needs. 3.2: form 30 VSLA and 4 SACCOs targeting 1000 individual. 3.3: support mentorship and business incubation for 1000 youth and VSLA members.
