Project Summary

South Sudan has been in political conflict since December 2013. This conflict has seen immerse suffering to the people of South Sudan and great humanitarian crisis. There has been mass displacement of populations and according to UNHCR there are over 3.1 million people who have sought refuge to the neighboring countries.
This crisis has greatly hit the health sector and affected service delivery to the people of South Sudan. Magwi County has been largely affected by the crisis especially after the June 2016 fighting which spread rapidly to areas that were relatively calm. Mass displacement of the populations and destruction of infrastructure was witnessed.
The signing of the peace agreement between the warring parties on 12th June 2018 in Addis Ababa Ethiopia has seen relative calm return in the country and has also seen influx of returnees from the neighboring countries and from the IDP camps.
This therefore calls for integrated humanitarian response in the county. There is risk of disease outbreaks since the county is a bordering Uganda and the movement of populations to and from the county increases the risk of outbreaks. In February 2018, a suspected meningitis outbreak was reported in Torit County, which borders Magwi County, and it claimed lives of 15 people and affected around 34 others (WHO meningitis Sit rep, 2018 March). It is therefore prudent to have integrated health interventions in the county to avert such outbreaks.
Due to the ongoing Ebola Virus outbreak in democratic republic of Congo, Magwi County remains a very high risk County and therefore needs more attention and high community sensitization to evert possible Ebola outbreak in the county.
Nasir County in Upper Nile State has also been greatly affected by the humanitarian crisis which hit the county since 2018. Immunization services have not been offered in this county for the last 5 years according to the county health Department. This has been partly due to the unavailability of cold chain facilities and closure of health facilities who could offer essential healthcare and routine immunization services.
This being a border county also, there is increased chances of disease outbreak and therefore increasing the infant mortality and morbidity rates.
This project therefore wishes to address the health challenges in these two counties by supporting primary health care units and having two mobile health clinics in each county to offer integrated health services to the vulnerable and affected populations.
The projects also wishes to improve response to outbreaks by timely reporting and investigating suspected disease outbreaks within 48 hours in order to avert possible spread of outbreak prone diseases.
The project will also integrate WASH messages to the communities in order to avert diseases related to poor sanitation conditions like cholera and acute watery diarrhea.
Screening of cases of malnutrition and referring them to the OTP and stabilization centers will be done in order to help reduce cases of malnutrition in the counties and also help reduce mortality and morbidity rates caused by acute malnutrition.
IDSR and EWARS reports will be sent on time to the Ministry of Health and the health cluster to help them plan well for health services in the county monitor disease trends and help respond to possible disease outbreaks.
All funds received will be reported to the financial tracking system in order to inform the cluster of the funding gaps for proper resource mobilization in order to improve health service delivery.

Needs

Magwi county in Eastern Equatoria State and Nasir county in Upper Nile State are border counties and risk outbreak of diseases from the neighboring countries. There is need for immunization services and proper disease surveillance systems to be put in place. This project will address the issue of Expanded programme on Immunization by conducting routine immunization services to the vulnerable populations and also mass vaccination campaigns to the returnees to ensure that all the children from 6 months to 15 years are vaccinates against vaccine preventable diseases like polio and measles.Magwi County is at risk of a possible Ebola outbreak and therefore a lot of community sensitization on the ways of preventing Ebola will be addressed in this project.Provision of essential health services in the two counties remains a big challenge due to the fact that key infrastructure like the health facilities were destroyed during the political instability in the county. This project will address this need by rehabilitating the facilities which were destroyed during the crisis. There will also be provision of mobile health interventions in the two counties in order to reach the most vulnerable populations in their villages.Water borne diseases like cholera are also prone to these counties. The project will ensure that key WASH messages are disseminated to the communities and integrated in the health education talks, in order to sensitize the communities on the importance of drinking clean water and importance of avoiding water source contamination. Water quality testing in the health facilities will be conducted in order to ensure that the water provided to the patients is of good quality for drinking.Malnutrition remains a big challenge in the two counties with the current September 2018 IPC analysis placing Nasir county in IPC phase 4 and Magwi county in IPC phase 3. The GAM levels in the country remains far above the WHO emergency threshold of 15% with other counties in the country having GAM levels of up to 29%. This project will address this need by ensuring that cases of malnutrition are screened and referred to the necessary treatment centers for management in order to reduce cases of malnutrition in the county.Poor disease surveillance which results to poor health planning is a major gap in the country. This need will be addressed by ensuring that timely reporting, investigation and response to outbreak prone diseases which will then reduce spread of diseases therefore drastically reducing crude morbidity and mortality rates.Maternal mortality rates in South Sudan remains one of the highest in the world with figures of 749/100000 live births according to WHO 2017 report. This project will need to address this by ensuring that all pregnant mothers attend ANC services through the mobile health interventions and are delivered by skilled birth attendants. Referral pathways will also be improved in order to ensure that mothers are well taken care of and therefore improve this bad maternal indicator.

Activities

1. Strengthening EPI servicesThis will be done by ensuring that routine immunization services are improved in the two Counties. All Mobile health clinics will ensure that immunization services are done routinely. Mass immunization campaigns will also be done to target returnees aged between 6 months and 15 years in order to prevent vaccine preventable diseases. 2. Rehabilitate destroyed health facilitiesThis will ensure that the health facilities present in the counties are in good position to receive patients and improve the number of consultations and management of diseases. This will address the need of good infrastructure which was destroyed during the crisis.3. Patient consultationsThis will be done through static stations and the mobile clinics. To achieve this, the project will ensure that we hire qualified medical personnel in order to give quality medical services to the vulnerable populations. There will also be established 2 mobile clinics in each county and support one PHCC in each county to ensure that health services reach the most vulnerable in the society.4. Awareness creation on outbreak prone diseasesMagwi county and Nasir county are border counties and are at risk of disease outbreaks from neighboring countries. Magwi County in particular is at risk of Ebola virus disease outbreak. There will be awareness creation on ways of preventing spread of the diseases and also ways of improving disease surveillance.5. Malnutrition screeningThere will be screening of malnutrition cases in the two counties and referral to the OTP or stabilization centers the malnourished children, pregnant and lactating mothers for management in order to reduce cases of malnutrition in the counties.6. Testing of water quality in the health facilities.The testing of water quality in the health facilities will be done in order to ensure that the water in the health facilities is safe for drinking. Messages on the importance of clean and safe water for deinking will be disseminated through health education talks in order to sensitize the community on the importance of clean and safe water.7. Provision of antenatal and postnatal care.This will be done in the health facilities and the mobile health clinics to ensure that mothers attend ANC clinics, deliver in the health facilities attended by skilled health personnel and also attend post-natal care in order to reduce maternal, infant and child mortality rates. The project will hire skilled personnel to ensure effective delivery of this noble task.8. Timely reportingAll the reports will be done in time and disseminated to the relevant authorities i.e. the ministry of health and the health cluster in order to help identify disease trends and plan for health service delivery.
