Project Summary

Approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan continue to struggle with shelters as they experience the destruction of their housing and essential household goods brought about by the compounded effect of the complex protracted humanitarian crisis; intermittent sub-national, endemic violence; displacement; and recurrent long and heavy seasonal rains (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2022). IOM aims to reach 350,000 people across the country through frontline and static teams in Juba, Wau, Bor, Malakal, and Bentiu. Based on IOM's extensive experience in implementing Cash and Voucher Assistance, IOM seeks to reach 126,309 individuals with CVA in locations with functional markets like Wau, Raja, Bor, Kajo-Keji, Bentiu, Nasir, and other locations. In addition. In 2022 IOM assisted over 120,000 people through MPCA, restricted vouchers, Cash for Work, and sector-specific cash. The settlement program in Wau, Bor South, Raja, and other locations will support the long-term shelter needs of the returnees and host communities through innovative shelter construction and environmentally friendly practices, and the use of local materials. IOM aims to implement a durable solution project in Wau, targeting 2,059 households residing in the Naivasha IDP camp. The Housing, Land, and Property team supports mainstreaming HLP in all shelter and community infrastructure projects while ensuring conflict sensitivity and assessment in contested areas. As SFP for the Naivasha IDP camp, IOM will provide SNFI assistance and shelter upgrade to people living in IDP sites. In 2023, IOM will further strengthen integrated and multisectoral interventions in coordination with WASH, CCCM, Protection, and Food Security and Livelihood.
IOM focuses on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse through coordination with protection actors, referral, and training and prioritizes the needs of persons with disability, minority groups, and child and women-headed households through needs assessments and special support. IOM interventions are aligned with cluster strategy, priorities, and overall objectives. Focus on return and stable locations; the settlement program approach will enhance resilience, peace, tranquility, and community development and decrease dependence on assistance.
As S-NFI Cluster lead, IOM continues to coordinate responses, build local organizations' capacity and promote joint and integrated responses with other Clusters (WASH, CCCM, Protection, and Food Security)
IOM continues to lead SNFI Cluster, and host and co-lead National CWG, and the HLP. IOM mainstreams HLP rights, AAP, CFM, GBV, disability and inclusion, communication, and community engagement, PSEA, seeks to enhance community capacities resilience and reduce dependence on assistance.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC classification of October 2022, about 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54%), are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse between October and November. Of those, 2.2 million people are experiencing worse conditions in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and an estimated 61,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Akobo of Jonglei State; Pibor County in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area.In 2023, approximately three million people in South Sudan will struggle to meet their basic needs in SNFI due to the continued destruction of houses and loss of essential goods from the ongoing floods and conflict. The increment is attributed to the current economic crisis in the country, new displacements in Tonga, and ongoing floods and conflict, mainly in Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile, Unity, part of Western Bahr el Ghazel and Western Equatoria States. This presents an increase of 0.5 percent from 2.4 million people who needed SNFI support in 2022, excluding the increasing number of returnees from the neighboring countries and the displaced people in the PoC and IDP sites. Of the total population in need of SNFI assistance, 58 percent are IDPs, 33 percent are returnees and 12 percent are vulnerable host community members (Draft HNO 2023) Inadequate or lack of shelter negatively affects physical and mental health, education, livelihoods, and food security, which can generate increased protection risks, particularly for women and children. People with inadequate or no shelter, particularly women, girls, and PWD, experience greater levels of violence than those with minimal shelter, which exposes them to negative coping strategies. (Draft HNO 2023) In 2022, IOM conducted several needs assessments in counties like Rubkona, Leer, Mayom, Jur River, Raja, Wau, Pibor, Bor, Fangak, Twic East, Fashoda, Malakal, Nasir, Tonj East, South, Tonj North, Nagero and Aweil East. All such studies observed high needs for shelter and NFIs. There are over 15000 IDPs displaced due to recent conflict in Fashoda and floods. Over 30,000 IDPs in Bentiu continue to need support for Shelter and NFI. Similarly, IDPs from Abyei will require shelter and NFI support on a continuous basis.

2 - Project activities

1). Needs Assessment: IOM's shelter and NFI interventions precede a detailed needs and risk assessment as the initial steps of the assistance. Upon consultation and endorsement of S-NFI Cluster leads and state Focal Points (SFP), IOM and partners will initiate plans to conduct needs and risk assessment in the target locations. IOM conducts technical flood assessments, uses standard SNFI Cluster tools for data collection, and develops tools for DRR, and CWG tools. During needs analysis, the teams consult with RRC, local authorities, men, women, PWDs, youth, and minority groups to understand their needs. The needs analysis process includes a review of existing reports, data, DTM updates, and other IRNA reports.2). Provision of in-kind Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) and NFI. Upon endorsement of the needs analysis reports by the S-NFI Cluster and SFP, the project partners request for the release of items from the S-NFI Core Pipeline and coordinate with the Logistics Cluster to transport the items to the distribution sites. IOM S-NFI team this project will follow the Cluster standard packages for the distribution of ES-NFI items to the conflict and flood-affected communities. Prior to the distribution of the kits, IOM and partners will conduct verification and registration of the most vulnerable in the target locations, where possible project will collaborate with protection partners and DTM during the verification of affected communities. The standard ES-NFI package includes plastic sheets, ropes, blankets, and mosquito nets. 3). Shelter upgrade in IDP camps: The she team will respond to shelter rehab needs in IDP camp and Collective sites. The CCCM cluster forwards shelter-related complaints to the Shelter team for assessment and shelter repair. IOM has trained IDPs in shelter construction to engage in shelter rehab and construction through CFW modalities. The need for new shelter construction in the IDP camp and CC will depend on the arrival of the new influx. In consultation with other sectors, SNFI will receive information about the caseload and shelter requirement of new arrivals. Once the caseload is defined, IOM will request S-NFI Cluster for approval and release of construction material. 4). Cash and Voucher assistance: CVA responses will be provided in areas where markets are functional, accessible, and/or have the capacity to entertain the intervention needs. The CVA includes cash for work (CFW), multisectoral cash grants, and restricted cash grants, depending on needs and market assessments' findings. The project will verify the beneficiaries as per the set criteria and engage Financial Service Providers for the beneficiaries' biometric registration to avoid duplication. In the case of restricted vouchers, market days will be organized. 5). Durable Solutions. IOM will form committees and construct 2,059 low-cost shelters (one-room shelters, and toilets) in Wau and provide each household with solar panels to meet household energy needs. 6). Coordination and technical support: IOM co to lead SNFI Cluster, co-lead and host Cash Working Group, lead CWG in Unit, WBeG, and serve as SFP for WBeG. In addition, IOM will dedicate resources for capacity building of SNFI Cluster and CWG partners on response methodology and CVA.7). Procure and transport Shelter and NFIs to partners: IOM continues to manage SNFI Pipeline by procuring SNFI items and pre-positioning items in strategic locations so that HRP partners can respond to the needs of affected communities.8). Awareness of HLP, GBV, PSEA, and disability inclusion: IOM will ensure that Housing, Land, and Property is mainstreamed in all interventions and due diligence/assessment is completed using the standard checklist. IOM all activities will ensure that GBV & PSEA risks are identified through safety audits and mitigation measures are taken. Under the current HRP, IOM will mainstream DRR activities into other activities and will focus on awareness-raising activities only.

3 - Project indicators

Based on IOM's experience in humanitarian assistance, the project will have robust monitoring and evaluation systems to monitor and evaluate project activities. IOM Shelter and Settlement unit hosts a dedicated M&E team comprising an M&E Officer and two M&E Assistants. Furthermore, the implementing partners have trained M&E staff and budget to monitor the activities as the first layer of monitoring. The M&E team is aware of the response methodologies and uses comprehensive, tested data collection tools to monitor the quality of response from assessment to post-distribution/construction monitoring. IOM Programme Support Unit provides technical backstopping and collates information at the program level on key performance indicators. IOM has developed different data collection tools depending on the type of activity and has a pool of trained enumerators to collect data in deep field locations. Wherever possible, project M&E uses a standardized checklist for rapid monitoring of the distribution and cash-based intervention. The M&E officer conducts exit interviews of the beneficiaries relevant to the entire distribution process, including selection, communication, accessibility, and quality of the intervention. The team monitors distance, locations, access, community engagement, protection risks, and status of complaints. In addition to rapid monitoring, the M&E team will conduct post-distribution monitoring of randomly selected distributions. IOM and national partners use standard Cluster tools, which include household interviews, focus group discussion guides, and critical informant interviews. The household sample will be identified based on Cluster guidelines; 90-95% confidence level and 5% margin of error. The M&E team will conduct PDM after two to four weeks of the intervention following standard Cluster guidelines. Under the project, IOM aims to conduct at least one PDM in each location besides rapid monitoring.In addition, IOM will maintain sex and age-desegregated data for the program beneficiaries. In addition to standard indicators, IOM developed a list of custom indicators that will support IOM to track program performance. 5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

CVA depends on local market functionality, availability of financial infrastructure for secure, accountable, responsible cash delivery, and target populations' preference and financial literacy. The market assessment is the first and foremost step for any CVA. IOM has the wise country data of market status in different areas, and members of JMMI, IOM support CWG for data collection. In addition to primary data, IOM will collect secondary information through existing reports and analysis. Services of financial service providers are important, and IOM is already in consultation with Aidonics to digitize the beneficiary data and transfer cash using mobile wherever infrastructure exists.As a member of the Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives (JMMI) and Cash working group (CWG), IOM monitors the market status, vendors' behaviours, prices, goods/services availability, restocking periods, supply routes, and traders' willingness to participate in CVA response. Before any CVA response, IOM conducts a feasibility assessment then compares it with the in-kind response to make an informed decision and modality and the type of the financial service provider. IOM has implemented CVA in locations like Bor, Pibor, Bentiu, Wau, Raja and Kejo-Keji and will continue to expand the cash assistance to new areas based on the market functionality. In 2023, IOM aims to support CWG for FSP mapping and support JMMI data collection.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

IOM shelter and settlement cash and Voucher Assistance response is informed by detailed market and needs analysis, context, and capacity; hence, the right modality is decided based on an in-depth understanding of the operation's needs, context, and environment. If there is proper market functionality, access and capacity, IOM will prefer CVA. As per IOM's needs assessment and PDM, more than 80% of individuals prefer cash over in-kind. Based on the feasibility and market analysis recommendation, the appropriate CVA modality is decided in the form of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA), conditional cash assistance (cash-for-work), or restricted vouchers. CVA will stimulate the local economy as it will have a multiplier effect. IOM believes that CVA empowers affected communities to have freedom and autonomy in choosing the appropriate good and services that meet their immediate needs using available local resources, contributing to enhanced resilience.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IOM Shelter, NFIs, HLP, and DRR activities are tied to an exhaustive all-inclusive assessment before the responses. Based on the outcome of the assessment, IOM shelter and settlement interventions ensure equal access to all Shelter and settlement assistance provided to the crisis-affected vulnerable populations, ensuring the dignity, safety, and well-being of affected communities. Community members, IOM staff, and volunteers will be oriented to attend to the specific needs of vulnerable beneficiaries throughout the project cycle.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IOM Shelter and Settlement will ensure beneficiaries actively participate in the processes and activities that affect them. Project teams will conduct detailed needs analyses using mixed methods of data collection (qualitative and quantitative data collection tools). Project teams will continue to use Cluster-approved tools for accountability to the affected population (AAP) for mainstreaming AAP during the interventions including distribution and assessment checklist, response rapid monitoring tools and debrief tools after each intervention. The use of AAP tools will help IOM teams form Community Committees involving women, men, boys, girls, and elders’ representatives of different groups for assessment and distribution, ensure appropriate targeting, identify needs and context-specific project risks, and enable community feedback. At all stages of the project, the beneficiaries will freely provide feedback and raise complaints through CFMs that will be established for each response. IOM shelter and settlement will keep a record of each complaint and update the status of the issue. PDMs tool has an important section to monitor the CFM desk's performance, which will inform on the performance and recommend measures in case of any changes. IOM will also continue to explore the use of technology for CFM in locations where phones and the internet are functioning. In the settlement program area, IOM established village development committees and placed Complaint Boxes to gather feedback on service provision. Following Do No Harm principles, IOM will prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on affected populations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IOM’s overall objective is to support crisis-affected persons in accessing lifesaving assistance and support by integrating the principles of doing no harm, promoting safety, participation, non-discrimination, and empowerment across all programs. In line with the GBViC framework, IOM will: 1. Ensure all IOM staff and implementing partners are trained on IOM's Code of Conduct, PSEA, and AAP. PSEA training for all staff, enumerators and casual labor is mandatory. IOM will dedicate time and resources to ensure all staff is trained on PSEA principles, protocols, and reporting mechanisms. 2. Uphold PSEA protocols and reporting mechanisms throughout the humanitarian system. 3. In coordination with IOM GBV, translate PSEA educational material in IOM offices (into English and Arabic). 4. Educate beneficiaries (women, girls, boys, and men) about their rights, how and where to report SEA incidents, and confidentiality of information.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IOM’s CVA and SNFI assessment processes are majorly informed by feedback from affected communities. IOM consults these communities through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and household interviews. IOM shelter and settlement consult beyond the gatekeepers in the affected communities and with all social groups, the Women, men, boys, girls, elderly, vulnerable groups, and their leaders, hence the information gathered from the extensive community engagement is used to inform design and implementation of projects. Through the long-term programming where infrastructures are constructed, IOM consults with the potential beneficiaries or the affected communities on the design so that it is culturally appropriate and acceptable, once changes are recommended, IOM modifies the design based on the feedback. IOM’s longer-term and resilience programs such as the settlement and DRR programs are people-centered, participatory, and designed based on feedback from the affected communities, IOM will continue to engage communities in the initiation, design, execution, monitoring, and evaluation stages of the project cycle. Robust CFM is used to collect feedback; hence feedback is provided in a timely manner.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

IOM is committed to ensuring a safe environment for the registration and distributionprocess. IOM conducts safety audits and selects distribution sites based on accessibility,safety, and availability of other services. To ensure the safety of beneficiaries, IOMselects locations that are close to the majority of the potential beneficiaries and easilyaccessible to beneficiaries, IOM follows the cluster methodology for beneficiaries'registration and distribution. To mitigate the risk of violence, IOM ensures clearchannels of communication with the communities. IOM established distributioncommittees before each intervention and inform local authorities before eachintervention. Before any response/intervention, IOM conducts safety audits andcollaborates with protection and GBV partners to ensure that the GBV associated risksare identified and incorporated into the overall project cycle. IOM works withcommunities to build confidence and regain trust through equitable access to servicesand gender. Community participation is ensured at every stage of intervention. IOMshare selection criteria with the community and ensure that vulnerable households areincluded in the selection process – qualifying the selection criteria. IOM purposivelyselects persons with disabilities, women-headed households, and other minorities groupduring needs assessment and post-distribution monitoring. IOM also establishes aCFM desk during assessment and distribution. The CFM desk is placed in a prominentlocation and every hour, the distribution team, through the announcement, informsbeneficiaries to contact CFM for any complaint. IOM conducts rapid monitoring duringthe distribution and refers issues to CFM desks. IOM conducts safety audits prior toany intervention and by ensuring strict adherence to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelinesthrough assessment and response. IOM also incorporates HLP related issues in theresponse planning and beneficiaries' selection criteria. IOM employed a full-time HLPexpert to support HLP related issues both and the field and policy level.
