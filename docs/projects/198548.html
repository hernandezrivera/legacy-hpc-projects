Project Summary

Plan International Nigeria will implement a 12-month nutrition response focusing on the provision of life-saving emergency nutrition services through the strengthening of the existing government health services in BAY State. The project will strengthen the capacity of the government healthcare providers on the management of Acute malnutrition in the community, OTP, Stabilization Centers (SCs), TSFP/BSFP and MIYCN to promote the sustainability of these critical services in the government existing health facilities and Communities. The project will treat children with complicated SAM and/or failed appetite tests in established SCs , uncomplicated SAM at OTP, and MAM at TSFP. The project will also provide brain stimulation services such as psychosocial support to the children and caretakers of children with SAM admitted to the SCs/OTP and behavioral change communication on Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN).
Furthermore, the project will integrate gender-responsive messaging and child safeguarding in its community engagement processes, share information, encourage discussions and address gender and safeguarding issues that impact on Maternal nutrition, optimal Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and others. The project will also strengthen the management of acute malnutrition through supportive supervision and monitoring and through the provision of logistical and technical support in collaboration with the Ministry of health and nutrition sector. The project will focus on supporting the most vulnerable population groups, specifically children under five years of age, caretakers of SAM children, PLW, caretakers of SAM children who are living with disabilities and other vulnerable groups. Plan International will integrate management of Acute Malnutrition into existing health services in each general hospital and in the LGA health system using State Primary Health Care and Development Agency (SPHCDA) and hospital staff and activating the LGA nutrition coordination meeting in all targeted LGAs.
At project inception , Plan International Nigeria will prioritize identifying Acute malnutrition to facilitate access to life-saving services for children under five years of age and to enable management of these SAM cases in collaboration with SPHCDA and Hospital Board Management (HBM) staff. PLAN will strengthen the capacity of the government health service providers as well as parents, guardians and caretakers to prevent malnutrition, respond timely the treatment of SAM and MAM cases and we will work closely with government and nutrition/other partners working in each LGA. The project will strengthen accountability to beneficiaries through the establishment and strengthening of an inclusive and appropriate community and facility -based complaints and feedback mechanism that will allow women, girls, men, and boys to safely report concerns of the emergency nutrition response in their respective Hospital. The project also will ensure gender sensitive monitoring and evaluation is undertaken by dis aggregating all data by sex, age, disability and creating complimentary qualitative and quantitative indicators.

1 - Needs

According to the global intercluster famine statement released in June 2022, increased in conflict, displacement, persistent drought, water scarcity, and spikes in food prices are threatening lives and driving a massive humanitarian crisis across multiple countries around the globe. Recent analysis shows an alarming deterioration of water, food, and nutrition insecurity conditions that are adversely affecting the most vulnerable, including women, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), young children, and internally displaced people (IDPs), putting them at risk of diseases and malnutrition and adopting extreme negative coping mechanisms. The year 2022, marked a record-high level of a global food security crisis, with over 40 million people facing emergency or worse (IPC/CH Phase 4 or above) conditions. Nigeria has been highlighted as one of 20 hunger hotspots in 2022 by FAO-WFP early warnings on acute food insecurity report, and among those as one of the six countries where urgent humanitarian action is critical to prevent starvation and mortality. The report projects that 19.5 million people in Nigeria are already experiencing acute food insecurity. Twelve years into northeast Nigeria’s large-scale humanitarian crisis, the needs are generally as severe and large-scale as ever. The crisis is not abating, and the situation of affected people is not improving, and they still live with great unpredictability and privation that goes far beyond chronic poverty and daily threats to their health and safety, many of which could prove fatal or inflict irrecoverable harm. A major part of these manifests in the form of protection violations. According to the 2022 HRP, around 8.4 million people in the northeast states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022, only slightly fewer than a year ago. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods, and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. In latest joint Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in the BAY States December, 2022 depict that the overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate in Borno 12.3% & 1.9%, Yobe state is 10.6% and 1.5% while Adamawa State has a GAM rate of 7.2% and SAM rate at 0.8% respectively. Adolescent girls face increased vulnerability to food insecurity and malnutrition in North East Nigeria due to the prevailing gender roles, power dynamics/relations, and the related barriers to accessing assistance and support. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance for Northeast Nigeria conducted in October 2021, malnutrition among women has significant implications for both their health and that of their children. In the same report, the prevalence of malnutrition was reported to be almost five times higher for adolescents (15 to 19 years) than adult women (20 to 49 years), 42% compared to 9% and 6 times higher for the severe form at 29% as compared to 5%. According to the Nutrition Sector, this rate in this age cohort is due to early forced marriage and subsequent adolescent pregnancies in the context of inadequate access to a balanced diet and poor health care.The Integrated food security Phase Classification (IPC) Acute Malnutrition analysis issued in November 2022 showed that the projection for Karasuwa, and Damaturu LGAs in Yobe State, and Madagali LGA in Adamawa State from January-April 2023 is phase 2 (Alert). This projection is due to the persistent and deteriorating security situation and the related access issues, leading to decreased food accessibility, and safe water access with possible outbreaks of acute watery diarrhea, measles, and malaria.
