Project Summary

In Syria, where 14.9 million people require healthcare aid, approximately 2.2 million reside in NES, facing weak, disrupted health systems compounded by public health emergencies. According to HNO2024, various emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases, along with vaccine-preventable and neglected tropical diseases, such as influenza-like illnesses, measles, meningitis, and waterborne diseases like AWD/cholera, hepatitis A, and leishmaniasis, continue to pose a significant threat to affected communities throughout Syria. These diseases account for more than 16% of the total consultations reported from the health system through the surveillance system. The cholera outbreak, declared by the Syrian Ministry of Health in September 2022, is expected to persist in 2024, particularly in NES, with seasonal variations in caseloads. Socio-economic challenges, poor infrastructure, and inadequate WASH conditions hinder access to quality healthcare, particularly in Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, and Deir-ez-Zor. NES camps lack sanitation, exacerbating health risks and gender-based violence. Challenges there include severe shortages of medicines and medical supplies, inadequate availability of healthcare personnel, and insufficient referral capacities, with over 1,000 patients classified as "cold cases" unable to obtain life-sustaining specialized services due to a lack of resources and services within the camps.
In 2024 and 2025, the IRC will maintain its focus on three response priorities determined through health system assessments, monitoring, and community feedback. The IRC will deliver comprehensive primary healthcare services and provide support to secondary-level health facilities through direct implementation and partnerships. In 2024, these health services will benefit 299,794 vulnerable individuals affected by conflict in Raqqa, Al-Hasakeh, and Deir ez-Zor governorates through 20 healthcare interventions, including 7 static primary healthcare clinics and the pediatric unit, 10 mobile medical units, 2 Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) facility, and 1 field hospital. Primary healthcare delivery, including malnutrition screening and treatment referrals, will integrate primary, reproductive, MHPSS, and community health across service delivery points to ensure comprehensive curative and preventive services. The IRC will contribute to disease surveillance through systematic data collection, analysis, and reporting of health data, utilizing the Early Warning and Alert Reporting System (EWARS). BEmONC services will be supported 24/7, and CCSAS services for GBV survivors will be linked with existing protection programs. Life-saving pharmaceuticals, PPE, and medical supplies will be provided to all supported facilitie
Given the high risk of GBV in the Syrian context, IRC and its partners staff facilitate safe referrals for GBV through inter-agency referral pathways. Additionally, efforts are made to enhance coordination on protection responses and capacity building within the Protection Cluster and sub-clusters. Ensuring gender sensitivity and privacy in service delivery is paramount. Health facilities are equipped with private rooms to ensure confidentiality during examinations and counseling, and a secure data recording system is in place. Orientation sessions on GBV are conducted for staff, and specialized training on the Clinical Management of Rape is provided to selected personnel. A comprehensive strategy is implemented to mitigate the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials are provided to beneficiaries, outlining reporting channels for incidents of PSEA. Referral mechanisms and disease surveillance systems are in place, along with protection measures to address risks like service disruption, stigmatization, and access barriers.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The IRC will continue to enhance its MHPSS services in alignment with IASC standards, with each facility equipped with an MHPSS counselor, psychoeducation, psychotropics provided by psychiatrists, and referrals. IPC and quality assessments will be conducted at facilities, with refresher training for health staff. The IRC has established an internal referral system between health, integrated protection, and early recovery and development, with a case management team for referral follow-up. Ambulance services will support external referrals, and cases will be followed up by medical focal points. Inter-sectoral and between-service referrals will be facilitated for primary and secondary healthcare. IRC maintains a robust internal code of conduct to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse, ensuring a safe environment for reporting. Collaboration with partners is emphasized to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and accountability. Regular updates are provided on staff training and complaints are received through feedback channels.
