Project Summary

The project aims at providing equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (0-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases as well as Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups (marginalised women, girls and children as well as people with disabilities) focusing on improving nutritional status through provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions through the most the most appropriate feeding and nutrition treatment for 13476 children (5160 boys and 5160girls) aged between 6-59 months and 3156 Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) through appropriate selective feeding and nutrition treatment and further increase evidence based BNSP services linked to Nutrition, Health, WASH,
Food security and protection in the targeted locations in Galgadud, Hiraan, Lower
Shabelle, Middle Shabelle,Bay and Banadir Regions Somalia. Malnutrition rates among children
aged under 5 and PLWs is very high in IDP camps with GAM prevalence around 13.8%
(FSNAU and FEWS NET report of May, 2020). The situation is attributed to impacts of
“triple threat” Covid-19 pandemic and eventual containment measures, serious flooding
and desert locust invasion that not only aggravated shocks but also worsened
malnutrition caseloads owing to associated impacts such as food insecurity, diminishing
purchasing power and increasing morbidity due to water borne diseases and low coverage
/measles vaccination and vitamin A supplementation. The Situation is exacerbated by
poor breastfeeding patterns among lactating women and poor diet diversity and
complementary feeding as well as limited access to nutrition and health services coupled
impacts of conflict and insecurity in Lower Shabelle and Middle Juba disrupted trade and
productive activities. The above plight calls for emergency Nutrition services through
treatment of acute and moderate malnutrition and preventive therapeutic nutrition
services as well as very strong community support for the project.
Of particular concern are women, girls and people with disabilities that are further affected common cases of violations and discrimination when accessing humanitarian assistance hence need for their increased protection and improved consultation and inclusion of people with disabilities in the project planning phase as well as other subsequent implementation stages including monitoring to ensure that their needs are covered and that they equally benefit from the project.

Project Activities

1. Screening of 13476 children (5160 boys and 5160girls) aged 6-59 months and 3156 PLWs and treated through OTP/SFP program 2. Promotion of optimum IYCF targeting 3156 PLWs in project locations 3. Prevention and management of common diseases (anemia, diarrhea, malaria etc) through Vitamin A supplementation, deworming, multiple micronutrients, supplements, iron/folate supplements, and dissemination of hygiene promotion messages and practices to the targeted 13476 children (5160 boys and 5160 girls) aged between 6-59 months and 3156 Pregnant Lactating Women 4. Training of 80 OTP/SFP staff (40 men and 40 women) as well as volunteers in case detection through MUAC measurements and weight measures 5. Training of 120 Community health workers (60 female and 60 male) on nutrition education and to enhance their knowledge in Malnutrition to mitigate increasing malnutrition cases among children aged 6-59 months and PLW 6. Conducting 12 Community education sessions for 120 community nutrition volunteers (60 female and 60 male) to increase awareness on malnutrition

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and service: Mitigation Measure : Enhancing security at the project sites by hiring security staff/ additional guards as well as ensuring that Nutrition services are located at centralized places.This will limit risk associated with long distances by women and girls that are vulnerable to GBV incidences.2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation Measure: Ensuring all staff sign PSEA code of conduct and adhering with it as well as conducting awareness on SEA and the reporting procedures that doesnt expose the victims to the perpetrators.3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.)Mitigation measure: Conducting awareness on protection assistance for survivors of protection incidences4. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activitiesMitigation measure: Conducting comprehensive awareness for persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls and ensuring their inclusion and participation in decision making processes as well as active participation on all project phases/stages right from initiation throughout implementation including mornitoring, till completion5. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanismsMitigation measure: Establishing beneficiaries complain and feedback mechanisms and conducting awareness targeting the beneficiaries for utilization of the same
