Project Summary

The proposed project entails to contribute to reducing the incidents of water raltes (borne, based and washed) diseases and diseases caused by poor and inadequate sanitation and hygiene practices. This can be achieved by increasing access to safe drinking water, safe excreta disposal and improved hygiene practices among displaced and other vulnerable communities in targeted states.
HA-UK with its local partners plans to target 37,725 individuals through providing sustainable access to safe water supply, improve sanitation coverage and enhance hygiene practices to IDPs and people effected by the ongoing conflict BN and WK economic crisis, climate change (flood. drought), malnutrition, and food insecurity in Blue Nile and South Kordofan states. HA-UK will ensure appropriate and cost effective technology products and clean energy such as Solar energy are installed for safe drinking water. Safe handwashing facilities will be provided in schools, hospitals and villages. Demo latrines will be constructed for the most vulnerable families to encourage all the families in villages to have latrines and avoid open defecation. There will be monitoring of water treatment at the source to ensure safe water is provided to the communities. Most vulnerable families will be provided hygiene kits. Massive hygiene promotion campaign will be launched in schools, villages and health services.

1 - Needs

Blue Nile and South Kordofan states remain one of the affected states in Sudan. Moreover, the deterioration of economy, Corona Virus Pandemic, food insecurity and climate change have resulted in increased the number people in need in the target states. The HNO 2022 indicated that in Blue Nile state At Tadamon - BN ranked as the lowest locality have access to improve water sources and sanitation coverage and Kurmak ranked the second lower locality. Total of 29,685 people in need (IDPS / vulnerable communities) were estimated in kurmuk locality while ElTadmon locality not indicated the number in HNO -2022. For South Kordofan State HNO -2022 estimated 36,309, and 36,169 people in Abu gbieha and Abu krshola respectively were targeted for WASH services. This target includes IDPS, vulnerable communities and returnees. WASH SDG- 6 planning and budget report estimated that in Blue Nile state access to BASIC water supply for the state’s schools is estimated at 63.0 percent while access to BASIC sanitation is estimated at 73.4 percent. Out of the state’s 331 schools, only 209 have BASIC water and 243 have BASIC sanitation facilities. On the other hand, the report stated that in South Kordofan state access to BASIC water supply for the state’s schools is estimated at 57.5 percent while access to BASIC sanitation is estimated at 61.7 percent. Out of the state’s 601 schools, only 346 have BASIC water and 371 have BASIC sanitation facilities. The latest S3M - 2018 survey estimated that in Blue Nile the proportion of house hold with access to improved latrines is 44.29 percent, 37.37 percent non-share improved latrines and 20 percent practice open defecation. On the other hand, South Kordofan had the lowest percent in access to sanitation facilities. Whereas proportion of households with access to improved toilet facilities is 16.48 percent, 10.07 percent non-share improved latrines and 49.49 percent open defecation. Low access to basic hand washing facility have recognized into tow states. The percent of access to basic hand washing facilities estimated by 3.36 in Blue Nile state and 0.12 in South Kordofan state. Furthermore, S3M identified low knowledge of 5 critical hand-washing times among the communities in both states.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project, making sure at‐risk groups’ specific needs are taken into consideration and risks mitigated where and when possible. These groups (who could be constituted of women and girls, but also men and boys, elderly, unaccompanied children, minorities, IDPs are at risk of conflict‐related or sexual violence, forced conscription, looting, destitution and general insecurity. The crop and vegetable kits distributed through the program are designed to be lightweight to enable women to carry/transport them back to their homes easily. HA staff will ensure compliance with the five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) defined by the IASC Task Force As part of its strong commitment to AAP, HA is engaging IDPs and other persons of concern in the programme cycle through formation, training and strengthening of community-based structures and networks who take leading role in project implementation. HA has distinctive community based and AGD approaches that promotes the active participation of targeted population particularly girls, women, disability and minority and ensures engagement of all people of concern across the interventions levels. The people of concerns and other stakeholders are also able to share their opinions and complaints through feedback mechanism. This ensures greater accountability to people of concern and support the project sustainability. The opinions and feedback are regularly collected, analyzed and processed to inform project adjustment and improvement. This has largely reflected in building trust with the people of concerns and host communities as well as led to client satisfaction. Through its presence and capacity support to counterparts.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

HA-UK has established multi-sector Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project location (Blue Nile and South Kordofan) through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the HA-UK. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the HA-UK to handle any compliant report and feedback. The reporting procedures will be displayed in a visible place in the project location and complains and feedback boxes will be placed at distribution points and project site offices for those who are literate to use. Views and feedback will be collected from women, girls and community beneficiaries including those living with different disabilities and the minority, authorities from the start of the project to the intervention and monthly thereafter. Feedback and complaints will generally be collected using FGD’s with groups of men, women, boys, and girls; during community sensitization and awareness sessions; and through the use of suggestion boxes. HA’s community feedback mechanism allows for a range of complaints to be received and managed, including a mechanism for confidentially fast-tracking concerns of a serious nature such as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Through this discussion, the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries of the HA-UK will explain its procedures, decisions and allow women and girls to hold HA-UK accountable. Awareness raising on existing PSEA Networks or Focal Point(s) will be included to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms.
