Project Summary

The project aims at responding to the health life-saving needs across Sudan by providing access to regular malnutrition screenings, pediatric primary and secondary health care services to children aged 0-14, and maternal and reproductive health activities. It will take place in Port Sudan (Red Sea State), Nyala (South Darfur), and Mayo area(Jabal Aulia district, Khartoum). The Paediatric Centres in Port Sudan and Nyala are primary health facilities with integrated secondary health services. Both hospitals have a triage department, outpatient rooms for consultations and follow-up and auxiliary services such as a pharmacy and a laboratory. Patients with medical complications and severely malnourished children will be hospitalised in the wards, each of them containing 18 beds. Patients that are out of the Centre’s admission and treatment criteria will be stabilised and referred through an ambulance service to public facilities and feeding centres, if needed.
In Mayo, EMERGENCY is running a primary health facility which provides pediatric services to children under the age of 14 and maternal and reproductive health services to women living in the catchment area of the facility. The Center, working five days per week, is composed of an observation ward, a pharmacy, a laboratory, an isolation room, a vaccination area, and an obstetric sector. In Mayo, EMERGENCY will strengthen the referral services with feeding centers for severe malnourished children (SAM) and with higher-level health facilities for children with medical complications. Additionally, EMERGENCY will ensure maternal health care, addressed to women in reproductive age living in Mayo area, through a comprehensive set of reproductive health services such as ante/post-natal care and family planning. On top of this and to ensure long-term sustainability and as a retention strategy, the intervention will also focus on capacity building of national health staff.
Health and nutrition promotion sessions in the facilities and in outreach will ensure long-lasting behavioral changes at local level and ensure the participation of the population in building socially and culturally appropriate practices to improve the general health of the beneficiaries. Further, EMERGENCY will encourage active participation of the local community including women in the implementation of the proposed project and will consult women, girls, men and boys, where possible. EMERGENCY has already developed well structured cooking classes in Mayo for moderate malnourished children (MAM cases) and aims to expand this activity to Nyala and Port Sudan.
Thanks to EMERGENCY long-standing presence in the targeted areas, EMERGENCY has developed a strong cultural and social understanding based on a constructive relationship with the main stakeholders, which strengthened the community acceptance and buy-in.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan remains complex: there are 8.6 milion of people in need. In this context, health remains an extremely important life-saving sector. According to data gathered in need assessments and meetings with SMOH in Red Sea, South Darfur and Khartoum States there are major gaps in the provision of health services. Moreover, the recent macro-economic and social shocks are making the population increasingly vulnerable and forcing the local communities to adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as reduction in health expenses, to withstand shocks. Currently, paediatric and maternal health services are critical, including the provision essentials druges, vaccinations (measles), malnutrition screenings and referrals.Further, health and nutrition education activities are needed to promote positive behaviors that can reduce malnutrition and the risk of vectorborne and waterborne diseases.

Children from under the age of 14 have access to primary and secondary health services.The beneficiaries will be girls and boys under the age of 14.Outpatient activities will include malnutrition screening,triage, visits and medications. Patients identified as being severely malnourished, if without further complications, will be referred to the feeding center. If other medical complications are present, the child will be transferred to the hospital (for Mayo Clinic) or will be admitted in EMERGENCY’s wards in Nyala and Port Sudan. Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) will make sure to follow-up with them once they are discharged from the hospital/feeding centers. EMERGENCY will also provide immunization services to children in order to boost their immunity against public health preventable diseases.2. Increased access to maternal and reproductive health services, including antenatal and post-natal care, and family planning services in the Pediatric Clinic in Mayo (Khartoum State). EMERGENCY will target Pregnant and Lactating women and support maternal healthcare, benefitting women through the provision of comprehensive Antenatal Care (AC) visits focusing on the most useful components such as identification and management of conditions such as infections and anemia through laboratory services. Additionally, the ultrasound for fetal heart rate measurement will be carried out, and if needed women will receive tetanus vaccination. Post-natal care will be provided to all new-born and mothers either coming to Mayo PC or at household level. Women who receive ANC services in Mayo EPC will be advised on the importance of post-natal care for the health of their children. Family Planning (FP) services, including counselling, evaluation and provision of short and long-acting FP methods will be ensured to women. FP counselling in clinic will be addressed to pregnant and non-pregnant women in childbearing age and women who request the service. 3. 5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

In all locations EMERGENCY ensures proper medical waste management to protect the patients, the communities and the environment. Additionally, following the floods in August 2019, EMERGENCY’s staff observed a further deterioration of the environment situation in Mayo: the lack of waste management provides habitat for harmful animals and insects, drainage channels have been clogged, preventing dirty water from flowing and creating breeding ground for insects. All of this can aggravate the endemic/epidemic potential for outbreaks of vector-borne diseases, such as malaria, and communicable water-borne disease contracted through direct contact with polluted waters. Therefore, during the health promotion activities EMERGENCY will promote hygiene messages that can raise awareness on the negative environmental impact of some.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Ensuring access to high quality and free essential health care is a way to support and strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities protecting them from adopting harmful coping strategies that increases their vulnerability to exploitation and abuses (especially women and children).Additionally, the presence of health facility that are able to respond to health threats such as increased morbidity or potential outbreaks also ensure the protection of communities.
