Project Summary

With established child protection agencies working in compliance with CPSS strategy and CPMS, the project will address multiple protection risks faced by refugee children through a comprehensive package of activities for preventive and remedial actions in the best interests of children.
Specialised AGD-sensitive case management services considering 'Do No Harm' principle will be provided to children at risk, with provision of appropriate alternative family or community-based care for UASC and tailored service and referrals to competent service for survivors of Gender-Based Violence and children with disabilities.
Strengthened information management through inter-agency collaboration and CPIMS+ reports for trends analysis will support the response monitoring and evidence-based data for evaluation. UNHCR and partners will engage with parents, girls and boys and key community representatives at multi-layered levels to promote meaningful participation in project design and implementation and strengthen the community-based child protection mechanism/ structures, including women and girls’ organisations to ensure effective risk-mitigation for children and sustainability. This will be coupled with family strengthening activities to build up their resilience and reduce potential risks for children.
Multipurpose Children and Adolescent Hubs will enable children and adolescents to engage in age-appropriate recreational and psycho-social activities in safe and dignified spaces with close involvement of Community-based Child Protection Committees while serious cases of trauma or psychological distress are referred to specialized services.
Effective integration with other sectors, including Community-based protection, education, GBV, Nutrition, MHPSS, Legal protection to enable the provision of services such as menstrual hygiene kits in adolescents’ hubs, Sexual Reproductive Health, support for children in contact with the law and various forms of support to caregivers.
Capacity of partners’ staff and community volunteers will be strengthened through training, mentoring, coaching considering CPMS, CPSS and IASC guidelines and UNHCR Framework. Child protection tailored trainings will be provided to Government staff including CiCs and law enforcement. Awareness raising sessions targeting the Community will be organized on various topics, including on gender equality/risk, prevention of child labor, child marriage in addition to mainstreaming child protection across sectors.
Child friendly communication on DRR and COVID-19 prevention will be mainstreamed into regular activities. Strengthened community structures will work to prevent temporary separations in case of emergencies. Funds will be dedicated for upgrade and repairs of CP structures affected by natural disasters.
Partners will complement the communities’ response during disasters by participating in the PERUs and ERTs. The greening projects where children at the multipurpose hubs plant and care for vegetation will continue as efforts to protect the environment.
The project will ensure social cohesion between host and refugee communities, opening activities to children from the host community with particular focus on peace-building activities and appropriate child friendly feedback mechanisms in line with AAP principles and mechanisms such as consultations, FGDs and surveys. UNHCR prioritizes SEA prevention and response activities with internal SOPs and office focal points. PSEA and child safeguarding are embedded trainings provided to partners. The Code of Conduct is displayed in facilities and includes a referral mechanism for complaints and protection hotline.
In the 34 camps, UNHCR will support through assessment and recommendations and registration of child-related challenging cases such as new-borns without documentation.
The project will be implemented in close collaboration with the government structures and stakeholders involved in child protection.

1 - Identified Needs

The protection assessment carried out in October 2021 in Rohingya camps revealed the priority child protection concerns in the community were Child Labour (64%), neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%), and risks of trafficking (32%). These were followed closely by depression (27%), separation of children (25%), and physical abuse of the child (23%). However, the data on violence, particularly gender-based violence (12%), does not correlate with what is generally considered to be extremely prevalent in the camps. The complex protection environment, with deteriorating security environment due to clashes among refugees, (with 10% of households reporting deterioration of the safety and security situation in their area of residence compared to the previous 12 months), the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 (noting 66% of households reported degrading living standards), closure of education facilities (reported as 23% of unmet needs) as well as the monsoon affected safe access to services for all children from April to September 2021, particularly those with disabilities. All these have increased the vulnerability of children and their families. With limited access to education and/or livelihood opportunities adolescent boys and girls remain at high risk with some of them exposed to the worst forms of child labor. With 14% of the population between the ages of 12-17, a lot more has to be done to deliver protection to adolescents and to positively engage them. Psychological distress increased the exposure of children to trafficking, negative coping mechanisms by adolescents, violence, and exploitation remain then concerns in the family as well as in the community. Girls, face threats both within the home and in the community. Girls remained vulnerable to sexual exploitation facilitated by some family members and corrupted community and religious leaders, early pregnancy, while boys continued to have limited access to GBV services and resorted to child labor. Even though the 2021 draft J-MNSA reports only 3% of girls aged 10-17, and 1% of boys aged 10-17 as having been married, it underlines correlation with the vulnerability of families confirming that married children were found at greater proportions in households with persons with disabilities, as well as large households, compared to households without persons with disabilities, or small households. Child protection concerns are often underreported especially gender-based violence due to cultural sensitivities surrounding these issues, while neglect related to the overall condition in the camps is the most visible, leading sometimes to recurrent drowning and road traffic accidents. Social norms and practices also contribute to child protection concerns, requiring longer-term efforts to impact change. Response, mitigation, and prevention of child protection risks identified during consultations with children and communities should match with Child Protection Sub Sector’s strategy aiming at working in partnership at various levels advocating and mainstreaming child protection in view of priorities response considering strengthened Community-level child protection, Systems strengthening, Justice for children, case management and working across sectors.

2 - Activities or outputs

SO4- Activity 1: Specialised case management service considering age, gender, diversity, and disability provided for children at risk, alternative family or community-based care for UASC scaled up, BIP-BID for complex cases; quality services for children most at-risk. Follow up on old cases (9300) and identifying new cases (3000).SO4- Activity 2: Life skills and prevocational skills, resilience activities and peacebuilding provided including online sessions.SO4-Activity 3: Structured psycho-social support will be provided besides non- structured PSS. Serious cases of trauma or psychological distress will be referred to specialized services, including MHPSS to receive counselling, therapy or other psychological/psychiatric intervention.SO4-Activity 4: Multipurpose Children and Adolescent Hubs will enable children and adolescents to actively engage in age-appropriate recreational, psycho-social and development with the close involvement of CBCPC. Disposal of menstrual hygiene kits and Sexual Reproductive Health sessions and awareness on gender equality/risks issues will be provided. Mobile team and facilities upgraded to facilitate access for children with disability.SO4-Activity 5: Positive parenting skills sessions provided to caregivers and community members that include: Positive Discipline, Everyday Parenting & responding to children’s psycho-social support, which ensure communities and families understand the risks and actively prevent children from being exposed to abuse, exploitation, violence, and neglect.SO2-Activity 1: Community-led initiatives supported around issues that the community identifies as affecting children and actions for prevention as well as participation on social activities in the community.SO2-Activity 2: Raise awareness of the community, including children on key child protection-related matters including gender equity/ risks, disability inclusion, prevention of child labor, child marriage, drugs, drowning, RTA, COVID-19, and related mitigation measures. Activities will be linked with CBP structures. For DRR, strengthened CBCPCs participate in emergency response plans/PERU and work to prevent family separations during emergencies and mitigate risks for children. Referral pathways for reunification developed and staff and community trained. Child-friendly communication on DRR mainstreamed. CBCPCs participate to repair affected facilities. To protect the environment, ‘greening’ projects implemented with children.SO3-Activity 1: Training and capacity building of social workers, local NGOs, community leaders and governance structures, including law enforcement and CiCs - Mainstream child protection at all levels to ensure skill-sharing and smaller scale partnerships with CBOs through the Implementing Partners. Impact will be monitored through supervision using CPSS quality benchmarks.SO3-Activity 2: Capacity building of volunteers on child protection to prevent and respond to through community-based awareness raising interventions and support to interagency case-management of children with extreme protection concerns including provision of life-saving assistance on an individual needs basis- mainstreaming- PSEA/ PFA- PSEA and child safeguarding is embedded in all trainings.SO3-Activity 3: Establish/support community-level child protection structures and ensure at least 30% are led by women and girls. The ongoing training of community outreach members in child protection approaches will support the improved prevention, identification, and referral of children in need of services. CBCP Committees and clubs will be strengthened in refugee camps and targeted host communities to reinforce social cohesion. Support to Registration on challenging cases related to children such as new-borns without documentation, recomposition of families with dispute on care responsibility, or transfer of children from one family card to another. This include cases in camps not covered by UNHCR CP and partners.
