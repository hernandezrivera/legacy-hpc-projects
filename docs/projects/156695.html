Needs

The ongoing conflict in Syria has resulted in the deterioration of traditional community coping mechanisms. Nearly 89 per cent of people are now living in below poverty line and 68 per cent are food insecure. Local Economy has been crippled by the ongoing conflict, leaving households without access to income-generating opportunities, due to that many families seek alternative income through dangerous survival strategies that include child labour. The damage in infrastructure has led to significant disruption and deterioration of basic and social services and skills gap of the human capital. Which also contributed to hostilities and destruction to the civilian foundation.The protracted nature of the crisis in Syria has challenged humanitarian agencies to restore and protect agricultural and livestock-related livelihoods and value chains to increase the self-reliance and strengthen the resilience of Syrians.Securing a livelihood is one of the most critical challenges faced by nearly all Syrians. Of the 5 million jobs that existed before the crisis, over half are no longer there. Around three in four people of working age are not involved in any economic value generation activities (World Bank 2017).

Implementation of activities

• Provide In-Kind Grants (IKG) to support business set up and business enhancement• Provide Vocational Training (VT) to selected beneficiaries (male and female)• Provide Farmers Training (FT) to selected beneficiaries (male and female)• Provide Business Development Training to selected beneficiaries to build their capacity and skills toward entrepreneurship and to encourage productive economic activities.• Implementation of Community Service Projects (CSPs) to improve access to basic and social services in the communities through light construction/ rehabilitation of infrastructures and/or provision of specialized equipment.• Set up short-term employment opportunities related to community public services• Establish of Community Engagement Teams (CETs), and build their capacity on Community Engagement, Participation and Mobilization• Capacity Building of Community Engagement Teams (CETs) and Community Volunteers (CVs) on Social Cohesion, and Supporting youths’ initiatives (male and female) to promote Social Cohesion and Community Engagement.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:1. IOM response activities unintentionally lead to intra-community tensions arising from the beneficiary selection or limited resources and response capacities.2. The principle of “Do no harm” is not guaranteed due to unclear objective based on the community needs, validated through a transparent community consultation and participation process.3. Possible SEA cases conducted by humanitarian workers male-dominated, gatekeepers and potential other beneficiaries 4. Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurring (crowd control)5. Cultural and social norms may prevent women from participating in livelihood generating activities (IKG, VTs)6. Livelihood opportunities may increase risks of GBV due to changing family dynamics7. Established Community Engagement Teams (CETs) might not be accepted by some Local councils with a weak women presence. 8. Expectations of income may be unrealistic and might create dependency9. Sexual abuse and exploitation by the service provider or by other actors in the community10. Pressure on the service provider from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the service. Mitigation measures:1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response, ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation and that this is openly documented and transparent. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the community or communities concerned and keep them informed of the project implementation. Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’ by keeping the local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress. Have a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension. 2. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community board, or through well-selected key informants making sure that there is equitable representation of diverse gender and age groups among them. 3. Establish a feedback mechanism and train staff on the inter-Agency coordination in PSEA community-based complaint mechanisms. Recruit female staff and train them. Train all staff on code of conduct and include in PSEA section in all contracts (staff, daily workers and suppliers).4. Ensure registrations/distributions are well organized i.e. (identification of sites/environment, set up several spots, do joint distributions with another partner).5. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the programme. 6. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the programme. Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs7. Members of the Community Engagement Teams (CETs) represent the community and are socially accepted.8. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the local authorities and communities concerned to manage expectations.9. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report 10. Establish good relationships with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1. Security situation in Aleppo and Idleb doesn’t allow for viable IOM response activities to be undertaken.2. Local Councils influence and driver IOM response activities.3. Beneficiaries sell their In-Kind grants and toolkits to gain immediate cash assistance instead of starting their own business. 4. Shortage of imported supplies may occur intermittently and non-compliance from suppliers regarding deadlines. 5. The community needs might not be reflected properly through the needs assessment stage.6. Dis-engagement of communities with the social cohesion activities. Assumptions:1. Security situation in Aleppo and Idlib allows to implement IOM response activities.2. Activities to be implemented in coordination with the Local Councils.3. Avoid the provision of pre-tailored In-Kind Grants and Toolkits. 4. All procurements shall be made from local suppliers inside Syria. 5. The Community Service Projects (CSPs) are planned and developed based on the community needs assessments.6. Promote Social Cohesion and Community Engagement. Mitigation measures:1. Establish a security and accessibility plan for the targeted areas, and a process that allows to consult the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment, and develop a ‘duty of care’ strategy for local partners.2. Ensure that Local Councils are represented in the Community Engagement Teams (CETs) and have a participatory role overall the process.3. In-kind grants and Toolkits are designed based on the beneficiaries’ businesses’’ requirement s and submitted in their business plans.4. Well-designed market assessment, enough time to guarantee the procurement process and penalty section to avoid delays and withdraws. 5. Raising awareness of the community on the importance of leaving no-one behind and engaging community members from different gender and age groups.6. Ensure the involvement of the Community Engagement Teams (CETs) and Local Councils together during the whole process.7. Plan the Community Service Projects based on well-designed assessment tools that reflect community needs, contexts and issues.8. Building the Community Engagement Teams (CETs) and Community Volunteers (CVs) capacity on Social Cohesion, and Supporting their initiatives to promote Social Cohesion and Community Engagement.
