Project Summary

This intervention is designed to improve immediate access to food while protecting and restoring livelihoods related to food and income sources 75,000 acutely food insecure pastoral, agro-pastoral and IDP men, women, boys, girls, including persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and marginalized persons in IPC phase 3 in Baardheere, BeledXaawo, CeelWaaq, Doolow, Garbahaarey and Luuq districts, Gedo region. The project duration is one year from January 2021. Food assistance will be provided through local procurement of 5,400 goats and sheep (S&G) from 2,700 female and male pastoralists, slaughter, inspection, and weekly distribution of 2.5 kg of the meat per HH to 1800 HH IDP and vulnerable host households HHs to benefit 10,800 men, women, boys and girls for three months. Acutely food insecure persons will improve their ability to meet their immediate needs for culturally acceptable protein and micronutrient rich animal source food (ASF), provide market for pastoral livestock that is negatively impacted by COVID-19 pandemic, inject cash to the local economy. Emergency veterinary services (e.g. curative treatments and deworming ) provision for core breeding and lactating stock for 10,000 vulnerable female and male pastoral and agro-pastoral HHs, especially women headed HHs will protect livestock assets during drought and increase the availability of milk for domestic consumption and sale thus, contributing to improved HH food and nutrition security and income. 800 female- and male-headed agro-pastoral HHs will receive agricultural tools, seeds and training on basic agronomic and business skills, market price variations, seasonality of crops and sustainable land use practices to scale up fodder, food, dual-purpose and horticultural crop production, conservation and utilization. The fodder and food will be used at home and sold to generate income. Desilting of 20km communal irrigation canals through cash for work (CFW) will target female and male 200 CFW work laborers from the vulnerable IDP and host population thus, providing employment, generate income for CFW labourers to buy food and other household needs for their HHs. The canals will directly benefit 720 agro-pastoralist HHs whose irrigation canals will be rehabilitated. 50 fisher folk will receive fishing equipment and training on sustainable fishing methods, storage, processing and basic business skills to diversify livelihood income sources. 3 women groups comprising of 65 members, will be trained on poultry production and management including disease control, feeding and housing to diversify household food basket and income. 300 vulnerable female, male and youth pastoralist and agro-pastoralists actors along the camel milk value chain (MVC) will be trained and equipped on camel husbandry, milk hygiene, marketing, value addition and basic business skills to increase HH income sources and capitalize on the camel’s resilience to drought to build resilient livelihoods. These integrative approaches create synergies and complementarities with other ongoing humanitarian efforts in addressing immediate and long-term food security gaps in the region and contribute to improved nutrition and protect lives of the vulnerable target population.

Project Activities

Project introduction to local administration; site selection; community mobilisation/sensitisation; beneficiary identification, selection, verification, registration; social agreements signature with local government/technical ministries, communities, men, women, youth, elders, VSF-Suisse. Project baseline survey, monitoring, end-term evaluation. Output1: Immediate access to food for men, women, boys and girls improved. Activity1.1) Procurement of 5400 goats and sheep from groups of 2700 male and female pastoralists/agro-pastoralists; equipment provision for hygienic slaughter/meat handling, slaughter respecting local practise, inspection by the Veterinary/Public Health Departments, and meat distribution to 800 male and female IDPs and vulnerable male and female host HHs (10,800 people) in Doolow and Garbahaarey, pit for preparation proper condemned parts/carcasses disposal. Each livestock seller will sell 2 animals and receive payment through mobile money transfer. Each meat distribution beneficiary HH will receive a quarter goat/sheep carcasse (about 2.5 kg) per week for 3 months, helping HHs with vulnerable groups e,g, children <5 years of age and PLWs to meet immediate food needs and and consume high value protein and micronutrients. Activity1.2) Desilting of 20 km communal irrigation canals in Doolow through CFW targeting 200 female and male labourers. Each of the 200 CFW labourers will work for 35 days in three months earning $7 USD/day (Total=USD 245). CFW payments inject cash to the local economy as the money will be used to buy food and other HH needs for 1200 men, women, boys and girls. Irrigation canals will benefit 720 irrigation farmers (4,320 men, women, boys and girls). Using 30 trained lead farmers as ToTs, the 720 farmers will be trained for 3 days in groups of 25 persons on basic agronomic practices, irrigation equipment use, DRR and EWS to optimize on productivity. Output2: Livelihoods related to food and income sources protected and restored: Activity2.1) Using 32 lead farmers trained as TOTs, 800 fodder (e.g. Sudan grass, Mucuna), food/dual purpose (e.g local maize, beans, sorghum) and horticultural crop (tomatoes, onion, spinach, kale) producers will be organised into groups of 25, receive appropriate agricultural tools, seeds and training 6 days (initial) and 3 days (refresher) on technical and business skills, seasonality of crops and sustainable land use practices, linkages with quality inputs and service provider in Doolow, Baardheere, Luuq and BeledXaawo. Activity2.2) Emergency veterinary services (supportive treatment) to 10,000 female and male pastoralists/agro-pastoralists benefiting 60,000 men, women, boys and girls in Doolow, Luuq, BeledXaawo, Garbahaarey and CeelWaaq. 600,000 livestock (mainly sheep & goats) will be treated/dewormed by County-based veterinary teams (1 veterinarian, 3 Animal Health Assistants, 1data recorder) working closely with ministry of livestock, for 35 days/district. Activity2.3) Emergency feed distribution (range cubes and locally-sourced hay) targeting 5 core breeding and lactating goats/HH (Total=10,000 animals) for 2000 vulnerable pastoralists HHs in Doolow, CeelWaaq, BeledXaawo and Luuq. Each animal will be fed (200g of range cubes and 0.5kgs of hay per day). The feed will be utilized for a period of 2 months, ensuring livestock assets protection from short-term feed resources disruption critical for their survival,during drought, while ensuring milk production continuity for HH consumption. Activity2.4) Fishing equipment provision and training 50 fisherfolk in Luuq and Doolow on sustainable fishing methods, group dynamics, handling/preservation, business skills for diversified livelihood/income sources. ActivityA2.5 Pilot poultry production for 50 women in Doolow and Luuq towns. Activity2.6-Training and equipping of vulnerable female and male pastoralists (240-Producers, 30-Bulkers,30-Traders) on husbandry, milk hygiene, marketing, business skills.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The decision to use cash transfers was based upon careful consideration of the type and urgency of needs identified, results of market assessments, seasonality and needs of existing livelihoods, labor capacity, predicted effectiveness and gender issues. The primary purpose of using cash for work in this project, is to provide short-term income boost to vulnerable disaster affected populations in IPC phase 3 and above to be able to access food and other basic needs from local markets and in the process rehabilitating community productive assets (irrigation canals) which will contribute to improved agricultural production and food security of the agro-pastoralists. The impact of heavy Gu rains, from early March to mid-May had a negative impact on critical supply chains, which was further aggravated by closure of land borders and limited maritime transportation due to COVID-19 (Somalia Cash and Markets Quarterly Dashboard, April-June 2020). Consequently, there was an observed higher increase in the full Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) cost as compared to the same period in 2019, however, prices in most regions started to normalize with the resumption of ground transportation. Thus, this intervention will mitigate the socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19, especially since there was a decrease in employment opportunities, livestock exports and external remittances as a result of COVID-19 mitigation measures (https://fews.net/es/east-africa/somalia/food-security-outlook/june-2020). The income earned by the cash for work laborers will improve their household incomes and contribute to food security and nutrition. The project will establish protection and gender mainstreaming to ensure safety, dignity and integrity of the target beneficiaries while instituting transparency and accountability. Selection of beneficiaries will use a participatory approach, where women and other vulnerable groups (e.g. elderly, persons living disability) from labor-poor households who cannot work will be allowed to nominate a member of their household to work on their behalf. The beneficiaries will be provided with the necessary tools (spades, wheelbarrows) and protective gear (gloves, gumboots). Wages will be provided to cover basic food and non-food items whose market accessibility and price will be adjusted and informed by the Somalia Cash and Markets reports as well as internal VSF-Suisse market assessments. For this, CFW labourers will be paid USD $7 per day for 35 days (USD $245 per CFW labourer for entire period) which takes into account the monthly MEB in Gedo and will enable households to cover the cost of basic food and non-food baskets for an estimated 3 months. Moneys will be paid directly to the beneficiaries using mobile money transfers, which is in line with VSF-Suisse audit and financial requirements.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project design considered the vital role formal and informal markets in enhancing people’s livelihoods, promoting food security and enabling them to meet their essential needs. It encompasses baseline market analysis taking considering core humanitarian standards, to determine access to markets, financial capital, livelihoods and economic vulnerability and their link to commodity prices, income-earning opportunities and wage rates which all affect food security. Therefore, all aspects of market functionality have been guided by the Somalia cash and markets quarterly dashboard reports (April-June 2021) through data sources from Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU Market Update, 25 August 2020) on price data, and the World Food Programme on supply chain and port data (Joint Market and Supply Chain Update, 4 October to 11 October 2020). The project factored in the socioeconomic impacts brought by COVID-19 pandemic and the current recommendations for both increases and decreases that was based on expected increase of food supply starting in July with the post Gu harvest and normalization of movement. Consequently, the interventions will accommodate and address any market constraints, such as access, price increase, seasonal changes, disruption of supply chains and taxes, inter alia.Animal slaughter for meat consumption is a feasible activity as a humanitarian response activity in the context of Somalia. With USAID/OFDA funding, VSF-Suisse procured 2,490 sheep and goats from 2,490 pastoralists, slaughtered them under veterinary supervision (SOWELPA) and distributed 25 tons of meat to 2320 vulnerable HHs (14,843 individuals) in Dollow and Luuq districts for 6 weeks in 2017 (between 23rd September and 4th November) and for three weeks in 2018 (18th June to 8th July). Community members were sensitised on selection criteria of livestock sellers and meat beneficiaries. Selection criteria of the livestock sellers included: Must be a resident of the targeted village, households owning livestock, women-headed HHs prioritized, drought affected livestock owners, the sick, elderly, people with disabilities and households with high number of children. The selection criteria for the meat beneficiaries included: Must be an IDP or resident of the targeted village, Women-headed HHs prioritized, the sick, elderly, PLWD; HHs with high number of children/ risk of malnutrition and vulnerable households. Healthy animals still in fair body condition were procured from verified livestock sellers on a weekly basis, each HH allowed to supply- once. The livestock sellers were paid US$ 50 per sheep or goat supplied, in cash, after passing the inspection. The animals were slaughtered humanely, hygienically under supervision of the meat inspectors (from the technical departments). Community based animal slaughter and meat distribution committees were formed to facilitate the process of identification/selection of livestock sellers and meat beneficiaries, as well as helping during slaughter and waste disposal. The committees oversaw a transparent distribution of meat from the slaughtered sheep and goats The meat from 1 goat/sheep was shared between 4 households on weekly basis, for a period of 8 weeks. Field monitors played the role of registration, documentation and post distribution monitoring. Beneficiaries of meat distribution shared the meat with neighbours and relatives and helped other PLWD or sick to access the meat. Meat inspection was undertaken by members of SOWELPA (both ante-mortem and post mortem. The project ensured that the slaughtering process was totally community managed adhering to Somali cultural and religious norms. Since there are no butcheries in the IDP camps, communal meat distribution was used for the slaughter destocking, ensuring proper disposal of condemned parts in disposal pits. The community provided specific sites slaughter-destocking and documentation of the exercise undertaken.

Please provide further explanation

The protection environment remains a significant challenge in Somalia. Limited government authority, continuous conflict throughout many parts of Somalia coupled with rampant impunity and recurrent environmental disasters has resulted in mass displacement of people within Somalia and to the neighbouring countries. Gedo region continues to face conflict, extreme poverty, demographic pressure, environmental stress, weak infrastructure and institutions, and insufficient resilience to food crises. The region borders Kenya and Ethiopia, where a lot of cross-border trade takes place between the three countries. With the security situation still volatile, restricted movements of communities by complex clan conflicts, military offense and the fear of the militia creates fear and interferes with the coping mechanisms of the communities. Launched in 2016 by the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS) and the Deputy Special Representative of the Secretary General, Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator (DSRSG/RC/HC), the Durable Solutions Initiate (DSI) is based on the premise that durable solutions to displacement can only be attained through strong government leadership and collective efforts from humanitarian, development and state-/peacebuilding partners and with the inclusion of displacement-affected communities themselves. Therefore, the project design ensures centrality on protection, aligns with DSI, NDP and will utilize existing guidelines and standards from FSC, SPHERE and LEGS during implementation. Community based participatory approaches will allow populations of concern to actively participate in the planning and management of the project thus, ensuring the inclusion and protection of the socioeconomically marginalized groups. Focus on knowledge transfer will be based on learning by doing with practical demonstrations applied for non-literate participants and those with low literacy levels e.g. women, to anchor knowledge and create sustainability. Long-term operations in Gedo, has strengthened VSF-Suisse working relationship with local authorities, community groups and government ministries who are involved in the project, from site selection, beneficiary identification, selection and verification, project implementation, monitoring and evaluation together with the feedback mechanisms. The project will rebuild and restore existing livestock based livelihood assets resulting in improved animal health and production which will contribute to food security and nutrition, increased incomes, recovery and resilience. This will mitigate against displacement in search of food and other needs by rural pastoral and agro-pastoral communities. It can also lure IDPs and refugees wanting to return and settle in rural areas. Lessons learnt in this project will be captured through a robust M & E and documentation shared with authorities and beneficiaries for out- and up- scaling of good practices. Targeted beneficiaries who are pastoralists, agro-pastoralists, IDPs and their host communities, will benefit from animal health services, meat distribution, crop and fodder activities as well as new livelihood development. VSF-Suisse participation in national and regional coordination fora such as the NGO consortium and FSC, will allow information exchange and adoption of core humanitarian standards. Therefore, this project will adopt transparency and accountability to enhance a peaceful working environment. VSF-Suisse will collaborate with respective line ministries, local authorities and experts and ensure broad stakeholder involvement in all the interventions.
