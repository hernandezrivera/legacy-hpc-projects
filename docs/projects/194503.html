Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to contribute to the reduction in malnutrition related mortality and morbidity, and improve access quality lifesaving emergency nutrition interventions for the most vulnerable populations targeting SAM and MAM U5 children including pregnant and lactating women affected by floods in Duk County.
According to IPC analysis of 2020, Duk County was classified (IPC Phase 4). The FSNMS Round 27 results, where JDF implements its Nutrition Programmes had Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates above the WHO threshold of 15%. The key drivers for the high malnutrition levels include Flooding (which is a key driver for displacement), Conflict (which leads to displacement as well as service disruption), Supplies stock outs, Food insecurity and Morbidity. The county with a population of 196,576 (Nutrition Cluster PIN targets for 2022), have one of the highest malnutrition burdens in the Country. The county has a total of 25609 and 14,981 children under the age of 5 with Severe Acute Malnutrition and Moderate Acute Malnutrition respectively. In addition, there are 34,519 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) with Moderate Acute Malnutrition. The burden for Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition within Duk county is 26, 581 for Vitamin A Supplementation is 59,075 and for Deworming is 51,174. The unprecedented flooding being witnessed in parts of Duk county has exacerbated the population displacement situation. There is also a high number of returnees and IDPs within the Duk county (13,983 and 11,123 of whom 5,659 and 3,896 are children under the age of 5 years respectively). The flooding has also destroyed crops, cut off communities from accessing Health and Nutrition facilities, markets and other vital commodities and led to deaths of livestock which are the lifeline of the population in the affected counties. The destruction of food crops, loss of livestock and reduced access to markets will lead to severe food insecurity and the attendant malnutrition among children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women in 2022
This project targets 10,431(M=4,631, F=6,800) SAM cases, and 15346 (M=6,966, F=8,380 MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively, MAM PLW cases 24,326, MIYCN 21,230, VAS 47,768 and Deworming 44,230 (PIN 2023). These targets are focusing on the vulnerable people among IDPs, returnees and host communities with limited access to Nutrition interventions.
The proposed project objectives will be:
1) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk, especially girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in Duk County in Jonglei state by the end of 2022.
2) To increased equitable access and utilization of quality Maternal Infant and Young Child preventive nutrition specific service delivery to prevent under nutrition for children, adolescents and women in Duk County in Jonglei state by the end of 2022.
3) To improve program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information to enhance planning for nutrition response in Duk County in Jonglei by the end of 2022.
4) To increased equitable access to integrated Nutrition, Health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in Duk County in Jonglei state by the end of 2022.

1 - Needs

In 2022, it is estimated that about 2 million people in South Sudan will be at risk of acute malnutrition, including 302,078 children (148,018 boys and 154,060 girls) with SAM, 1,039,214 children (509,215 boys and 529,999 girls) with MAM, and 675,548 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition. In spite of JDF intervention in Duk county that had shown decrease in GAM rates below the WHO threshold of 13% (WHO 2021), however, it is within the borderline that requires more intervention. The key drivers for the high malnutrition levels include Flooding (which is a key driver for displacement), Conflict (which leads to displacement as well as service disruption), Supplies stock outs, Food insecurity and Morbidity. The county with a population of 196,576 (Nutrition Cluster PIN targets for 2022), have one of the highest malnutrition burdens in the Country. The county has a total of 25,609, and 14,981 children under the age of 5 with Severe Acute Malnutrition and Moderate Acute Malnutrition respectively. In addition, there are 34,519 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) with Moderate Acute Malnutrition. The burden for Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition within Duk county is 26,581 for Vitamin A Supplementation is 59,075 and for Deworming is 51,174. The unprecedented flooding being witnessed in parts of Duk county has exacerbated the population displacement situation. There is also a high number of returnees and IDPs within the Duk county (13,983 and 11,123 of whom 5,659 and 3,896 are children under the age of 5 years respectively). The flooding has also destroyed crops, cut off communities from accessing Health and Nutrition facilities, markets and other vital commodities and led to deaths of livestock which are the lifeline of the population in the affected counties. The destruction of food crops, loss of livestock and reduced access to markets will lead to severe food insecurity and the attendant malnutrition among children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women in 2023. In addition, and more worryingly, flooding has hugely impaired access to health and nutrition services in Duk. Roads have been flooded or destroyed and most of the affected facilities can only be accessed by canoes, therefore making transportation difficult and expensive. The flooding and the associated poor access to Health and Nutrition services will further impact negatively on the Status of malnutrition across the County. Lack of access to information and communications by women & girls and access constraints (distance, security and finances) are a common challenge that has impacted access to nutrition services. Poor access to water and soap for hand washing, and LLINs for malaria prevention continue to contribute to high morbidity and mortality among children with malnutrition. The effects of COVID-19, economic crisis, currency devaluation and reduced market access and functionality are also expected to play a role in the deterioration of the food security situation in 2023. Incidents of insecurity related to inter-communal violence and cattle raiding within the county is likely to be experienced during this period, further contributing to the projected severe acute food insecurity and the associated malnutrition.This project targets 10,431(M=4,631, F=6,800) SAM cases, and 15,346 (M=6,966, F=8,380) MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively, MAM PLW cases 24,326, MIYCN 21,230, VAS 47,768 and Deworming 44,230 (PIN 2023). JDF will continue with management of SAM and MAM, Preventive nutrition interventions through MIYCN/IYCF-E, community mobilization and monitoring, surveillance and analysis focusing on static nutrition sites as well as mobile services ensuring continuity of care. JDF will focus on integrating activities with other sectors like Health, WASH, Education, FSL and Protection

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

JDF staffs will ensure safety of project beneficiaries during implementation of project activities in communities. Safety audits will be conducted to critically assess safety risks such as thefts, intimidation and extortion and engage local actors and protection actors to ensure safety of beneficiaries. Women and girls will be consulted on the routes they travel and time taken to ensure the distribution sites are as close as possible to vulnerable beneficiaries’ households and where possible transport and accompaniment will be provided. Interventions will take into consideration the approach of do-no-harm, where activities are done with adherence to zero effects on community lives for instance, distribution venues must be in safe location where beneficiaries have access to a proper shade with access to drinking water. Need assessment meetings with community members and the time of beneficiary registration will involve awareness sessions on various issues like child protection, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), and Domestic violence. As communities may be exposed to the risks of explosive hazards, they will also be sensitized on how to protect themselves and how to pass information of suspected hazardous objects to agencies to support. This will be done by ensuring that the local project committees that are formed in each area to champion project activities are empowered with knowledge on protection of men, women, boys, girls and disabled beneficiaries. They will be assigned to continuously disseminate key messages on protection to keep their community members aware. Additionally, JDF will work closely with UNMISS and UNDSS to respond to emergencies or cases that are related to explosive hazards. JDF will implement this project in close collaboration with SNFI cluster at national and state levels to minimize overlaps of activities, and most importantly, to reach the most in need people. JDF will achieve this by participating in cluster meetings like the operations working groups where cluster interventions are discussed. Post Distribution monitoring will help to gather feedback from beneficiaries to inform JDF on the effectiveness of the project and where there will be need by changes in the design of future project.
