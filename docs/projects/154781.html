Project Summary

Oxfam Protection response adapts the community-based Protection approach targeting IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities across, Anbar, Salah Al-din, Ninwa, Diyala and Kirkuk. Oxfam’s 2019 Protection project will aim to target up to 13,664 beneficiaries. The Programme will consist of Mobile Protection unit that will jointly work with Oxfam Country Humanitarian (CHT) team to conduct community-level Protection assessments. This will be in-line with the Protection Cluster Strategy 2/2 ‘to strengthen data collection and impact monitoring to enable strategic programming and evidence-based advocacy to address identified protection needs and risks, and to ensure the Protection of civilians.’ In addition, five Protection teams will be stationed in the areas of intervention to strengthen access to humanitarian assistance and public services through service mapping and dissemination of information during awareness raising activities. Oxfam will work with Local Partners and community-based Protection structures to strengthen their capacity to identify, mitigate and respond to Protection risks responding to their community and individual Protection needs.
In order to inform referrals, individuals identified through this intervention who require specialised assistance will be referred to relevant organisations, emergency cash will be provided to facilitate access to specialised services.
Under this response up to 300 staff of Local NGOs and government offices, in particular Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MoLSA) and governorate authorities will be targeted.

Needs

Iraq’s ongoing internal conflict that displaced 3.4 million people, is estimated to have placed almost a third of its population in need of humanitarian assistance, half of which are women and girls. Since the GoI declaration of retaking ISIS territories in 2017, the general protection needs have shifted to include violations of families with perceived affiliation to extremist groups. Reports highlight that families with perceived affiliation are increasingly isolated, stigmatised and systematically excluded from the general community. The affects this had on women and children has led to alarming Protection concerns. The Iraq Gender Profile developed by Oxfam and UN-Women, indicate that survivors of sexual violence and abuse who lived under the captivity of ISIS, and more broadly women who undergone violence, receive limited adequate support. Forced evictions, returns and confiscation of legal documentation occurring in an environment of impunity is hindering communities vision about their future. Adolescent men and boys are amongst the most vulnerable groups where expectation to live up to their roles as main breadwinners exacerbated during conflict and promoting forced recruitment. This response focuses on raising the community’s awareness on accessing their rights and voicing their concerns adapting a context specific approach. The community-based assessment that will be conducted by Oxfam mobile Protection units and the CHT will obtain information about the Protection concerns and desired solutions to feed into multi-sector needs assessments, at least 20 MSNA will develop under this project. There will be two Mobile Protection units covering five areas of intervention, based in Baghdad and in Erbil for timely access to areas in need of assessments. The information obtained will inform Oxfam’s advocacy calls on evidence-based protection needs. This will be in-line with the priorities outlined in the HRP Protection priorities, activities/indicators. The analysis will be shared with the National Protection Cluster as well as other cluster/sectorial departments as per necessary.Oxfam will deploy Protection teams in the targeted governorates, who will continue to regularly map services and disseminate information through awareness raising activities. Existing facilities, such as community centres, learning centres, health and other public facilities will be venues for awareness raising activities, aiming to target up to 7000 girls, boys, women and men across the five governorates. Though most of the project focuses on general Protection, in Diyala and Kirkuk it will include specific activities on SGBV prevention and promotion of SRHRs targeting up to 3000 people. In-line with the GBV sub-cluster Objective 1, the programme will work with protection partner teams to focus on positive behaviour change, will promote safety and dignity, empowering women, girls, boys and men to access livelihood opportunities, and claim their rights including SRHR. The Project will aim to support up to 250 women, through referrals to Oxfam and other humanitarian agencies livelihood programmes. Influential community figures will be identified and engaged for GBV prevention and response, this project will target at least 60 community leaders. Oxfam Protection and Gender teams will collaborate with GBV specialised organisations to organised round-table discussions, with the community leaders focusing on GBV and SRHRs awareness raising and advocacy. The Project will engage at least 900 adolescent boys and men through interactive context specific activities, such as role plays (theatre) and other focused on positive community engagement, this will be done in collaboration with Child Protection agencies and Oxfam livelihood teams, one of the goals would be diverting youth vulnerability from forced recruitment. Oxfam will establish and work with existing community-based Protection committees. The committees are considered as partners and volunteers where at least 10 CBPCs will be trained on, Humanitarian Principles, Protection principles, child Protection, GBV, SoPs for referrals and roles and mandates of different organisations and governmental bodies. The project aims to engage the committees in short-term peaceful co-existence activities, that will be implemented in areas of displacement targeted under this project, aiming to reduce inter-communal and sectarian tensions amongst the communities. Up to 300 women and men government staff will be trained on key Humanitarian Protection principles and dialogues between the community committees and local government bodies will be organised to enable the community to articulate their issues, needs and recommendations on how to improve their living conditions. Oxfam will provide emergency cash assistance (Cash for Protection) to facilitate safe and dignified access to services. The emergency cash will assist to reduce, mitigate and/or responds to protection needs.

Activities

1. Conduct community-level Protection Assessments. Mobile Protection units will be deployed to conduct rapid multi-sector assessments jointly with Oxfam CHT in areas with new displacements, returns, and with emergency needs for vulnerable host communities across Iraq. The information obtained will inform advocacy calls to address identified protection needs and risks. 2. Refer vulnerable individuals with specific needs to specialised services and assistance. Oxfam Protection teams, will identify individuals with specific protection needs and refer them to specialised actors. In the absences of specialised actors, concerns will be raised to the Protection Working Groups. 3. Provide emergency Cash assistance to prevent, mitigate, or respond to Protection needs. Oxfam will assist 300 cases by providing cash assistance to prevent, mitigate and/or respond to Protection needs. The cash for protection will be applicable in areas with existing specialised protection service and will support accessing and receiving these services. 4. Deliver awareness raising sessions on rights, entitlements and access to assistance, including disseminating information materials. Oxfam Protection teams will map services and disseminate the information with the communities through outreach and during awareness raising activities conducted by Oxfam programme teams. 5. Capacity building of Community members on protection approaches. Oxfam Protection teams will engage with the community to identify existing community structures, and establish in the absence of some community committees. The committees will be trained by Oxfam and partner staff on humanitarian principles, Protection principles, accountability, transparency and advocacy. 6. Establishing community groups to identify, mitigate protection risks, through short-term peaceful coexistence activities in areas of displacement within Diyala, Kirkuk, Anbar and Salah al-Din. Interactive activities will be held together with local Partners including civil society organisations, Protection committees and volunteers with women, girls, boys and men. This activity will be delivered and monitored jointly with the Oxfam gender teams. 7. Capacity building of government staff on Protection approaches. Under this project 300 staff from government bodies across will trained on Humanitarian Principles and Protection Principles. Oxfam will liaise with humanitarian Protection agencies working in the same locations to deliver joint training. 8. Conduct local and high-level advocacy on Protection issues including, freedom of movement, confiscated documents, armed actors’ presence in camps, violations against persons with perceived affiliation to extremism. This activity will be delivered and monitored jointly with Oxfam advocacy team. Monthly meetings will be held with Oxfam protection teams and local partners to discuss development of protection issues. The information obtained will inform Oxfam advocacy Protection interventions. 9. Provide recreational activities and vocational training. Under this project recreational activities will continue in Hamam Al-ali 2 camp in Mosul. The ideasbox materials will be used to deliver the activities. Including filming making, film screening, photography, magazines, recreational arts, sports and vocational targeting with women, youth girls, boys and men. In Diyala and Kirkuk governorate, Oxfam will hold interactive activities with women, adolescent girls, boys and men applying the Gender Action Learning System Methodology on positive behaviour change. 10. Provide livelihood opportunities (through referrals) Through Protection activities, individuals will be identified and referred to Oxfam livelihood teams and Other government/ humanitarian livelihood programmes targeting women, men and adolescent groups.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. The proposed programme covers IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities residing in and out of camps sittings, some areas are concentrated of multi-ethic multi- sectarian communities. There is a risk of tensions between target communities2. Areas with political and high security level might put staff members at risk of being caught in unexcepted clashes, or block access to beneficiaries 3. Engaging in Protection issues may put Oxfam and partner staff at risk 4. Resentment from segments of the community towards families with affiliation to extremist groups may put the target groups at risk of further violations and place Oxfam staff at risk. Oxfam will ensure that its activities do not increase or add to these risks5. Authorities requesting Oxfam staff to share beneficiary data with them 6. Environmental and hazardous risks

Mitigation Measures

Oxfam will ensure to conduct through risk analysis prior to any activity, taking into account potential risk to beneficiaries, communities, staff, partners and Oxfam. Will ensure providing identifying ways to mitigate the risks- that will be evidence-based and context and trends will be clearly articulated. The level of real risk to the Oxfam, individuals, communities and partners will be made clear. 1. Oxfam will ensure to engage with the communities in the target areas in consultations, the programme design includes short-term peaceful coexistence activities in areas with displacements, will ensure appliance of this approach from the initial stages of implementation to work closely in transparency with the target communities. Will take into consideration the contextual nature specific to the areas of intervention, will have female staff to work with women groups and follow a risk matrix plan throughout the project cycle.2. Oxfam will undertake a though risk assessment to the areas of intervention prior to access. Will negotiating access to civilians with the local authorities, and will ensure to prioritise the safety and security of Oxfam and partner staff and the target communities.3. Oxfam will ensure that staff are trained on Safe Programming, incl. conflict sensitivity, principles of do no harm, safety and timely access, accountability, community engagement and empowerment, and will ensure that all activities adhere to the latter.4. To ensure that activities do not increase or add to existing risks the programme will work closely with the humanitarian community to promote a safe and dignifying approach towards stigmatised or excluded community groups. 5. Oxfam follows humanitarian principles and acts a member of the humanitarian protection response in Iraq, will continue to reinforce its stance to local authorities, and explain the principles of confidentially we adhere to. 6. Oxfam Iraq has an established security department, that reviews environmental and hazardous risks, as well as security situation of areas of intervention prior to issuing clearance for access.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Oxfam has strong principles and protocols in working together with affected population in all stages of project implementation (project assessment, designing, planning, monitoring and evaluation). Particularly for this project, a standard accountability and transparency mechanism comprising of series of activities such as community sensitisation/community mobilisation, formation of project support committee, community feedback and response mechanism (CFRM) will be established to ensure quality and continuous community involvement in project design, project implementation, decision making and maximising representation of women and marginalised segment of population. At the beginning of project, Oxfam will conduct a needs assessment to identify Protection threats, vulnerabilities and the communities coping mechanisms. Will ensure the activities designed will consider ways to improve and not compromise the communities’ safety. Therefore, will ensure informed decisions and choices, are made by affected population and dialogues with affected population are facilitated. Oxfam will train all programme and partner staff on safe Programming, to enable them to identify protection issues in the communities the programmes are being delivered in. This will be done through regular programme monitoring. Findings will be regularly coordinated with other agencies, in position to respond, will inform local and national advocacy, and programmes approached will adapt a way to help communities avoid Protection threats. The beneficiary selection criteria will be refined jointly with the communities to ensure the relevance and acceptance of targeting. A gender balanced project support committee (PSC) will be formed to guide beneficiary identification, oversee and guide project implementation process and take part in the activities of the project. Further, establishing effective CFRM will ensure community feedback and complaints are received, addressed and streamlined. CFRM will have three system components comprising of a) providing information to communities and beneficiaries, b) receiving feedback and documenting and c) responding to feedback. While setting up CFRM cultural norms, accessibility (in person, Oxfam hotline, IDP call centre), safety and confidentiality will be considered, for an example sensitive complains will directly go to CFRM focal person from MEAL team, protection concerns will be referred to protection team and beneficiaries will have flexibility to register complains and feedback anonymously. The views of affected populations will also be consolidated to improve relevance in programming through various means such as Oxfam hotline, UNOPS IDP call centre, post distribution monitoring (PDM) reports and end line results. Baseline, end line and PDMs will also be conducted to measure the impact of project interventions on lives of beneficiaries served. Suggestion boxes will be installed in project location to receive feedback from targeted audiences. The feedback received from various sources will be collected, analysed and informed to adopt any changes in programming. Besides, Oxfam will closely work with UNOPS call centre to address the complaints and requests received through referrals. Lastly, Oxfam will work together with protection actors, local authorities, community leaders and other concerned stakeholders and ensure humanitarian safeguarding principles are followed and no harm is made to socioeconomic, environmental and cultural aspects of society.
