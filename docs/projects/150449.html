Project Summary

The proposed intervention will contribute to safe access & inclusive quality of education for vulnerable school children in the Khan Younis ,
Over All: The goal is to contribute to the improvement of the quality of education for vulnerable primary school students in Khan Younis through a holistic, all encompassing approach supporting their right to a safe and positive learning environment in schools.
Specific Objective To improve the academic performance, psychosocial well being and building the resilience of 600 school children.
Primarily through the provision of psychosocial support and accessible remedial classes that will target 600 children of both genders (300 girls and 300 boys including a minimum of persons with disabilities. The project will include the developing of alternative teaching methodologies and tools, centers & school-based education support, and structured psychosocial support that includes the child and his/her care takers like parents or big brothers and sisters, school teachers etc.
Special attention will be given to students with disabilities as well as the students who were affected by the March of Return events. So far, CFTA has worked with 28 (3 girls) students who were injured and around 200 who were affected by the March of return indirectly. in this project, CFTA will continue to work with this target group which is considered more vulnerable. The target group expected is 600 students (300 girls & 300 boys including students with disability). they are between the age of 8 to 15 years old.
In the process of providing this activities, teachers and parents will be provided with interactive skills development support (learn while doing). Children enrolled in the project will implement child led advocacy initiatives that will include child led research, child led fact sheet production, child led photography, child led radio online, videos and other tools that can support children in highlighting the message which will focus on education (students at the risk of dropping out of schools) as the main theme. both girls and students with disability will be participating at the leadership level of the advocacy led campaigns. The aim of the advocacy is to find solutions for them in cooperation with the ministry, schools and families). The selection criteria for students to be enrolled in the project will ensure that vulnerable students are prioritized, this will include gender balanced selection (300 girls/300 boys), inclusion of students with disabilities, students at Al Mawasi and ARAs, students affected by the Great March of Return and students who are at the risk of dropping out. A screening tools for tailored activities will be implemented to guide the activities for the children for example: Accessibility needs and respond, psychosocial, remedial classes, distribution of materials etc. The selection criteria for the project will give special attention to the 4th grade students as well as they will be in the process of being in a transformation level. In addition, UNRWA students who are no more have access to remedial classes and education support because of the current crises.
the project uses participatory approach where children are involved in all aspect of the project through different tools that are tailored for their age groups. this will include, the design of the project, the implementation, the follow up on the participatory indicators and the final evaluation. the project will make the learning experience inclusive, safe, attractive and will facilitate improved education on the longer term. Equally, in order to effectively address the root causes of poor academic achievements of school children, psychosocial support intervention with families are needed to improve the overall psychosocial well being of school children which can, in return, help better their school performance. The project will use Participatory MEAL approach which will directly involve the target group in the monitoring and evaluation all along the project.

Needs

Given the deterioration of the humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip lately, Education has been undermined and directly affected. The main challenges for school children are lack of access to safe child-friendly learning environments, quality schooling, and educational materials. Schools continue to be overcrowded. According to the Ministry of Education and Higher Education for the academic year 2016–2017, there were approximately 38 students per classroom, which is considered high compared to international standards that average 25 per classroom. Added to that, overcrowding at the same classroom affects student focusing on their studies, teachers losing control of the class and may raise the levels of violence. In addition, there is limited time available to support slow learners, and provide remedial education or extracurricular activities. The situation continues to be worsened by the population growth resulting in an average of 16,394 additional students joining public schools each year. n entire generation of Gaza Students has grown under conflict and on-going deterioration of their humanitarian situation. The electricity cuts of up to 20 hours a day has negatively impacted children right and ability to access education at school and at home and increased the drop-out rate according to OCHA report (May 2018). the social and cultural context of the south is considered more conservative than other areas which has a direct impact on girls, also attitude towards disability is still challenged with stigma. the project will ensure that girls needs specially at the age of early adolescents as well as students with disabilities are met,. Child labour has risen sharply in Gaza as a result of the deterioration of the humanitarian situation. According to the latest fact sheet issued by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) 2017 Child labour marked 3.4% of children aged 10-17 years in Palestine. It is estimated as 9,700 working children in the Gaza Strip, which in the opinion of experts consulted is likely a conservative figure and under-reported. Indeed, child labour is very much affected by the deterioration of the humanitarian situation in Gaza. It is used as an alternative household’s loss of income, poverty or loss of primary wages. Unfortunately, children are doing types of work that are considered harsh or dangerous. According to Terre Des Hommes 2018 Child Protection Rapid Assessment Report, 81% of the children included in the assessment are involved in selling goods as street vendors, 66.5% collecting waste (66.5%,), 60.7 collecting rubble, 59.2% are doing farm work and begging is 59% which is considered as child labour. The report also stated that approximately half of all key informants were aware of children working to transport other people and goods, cleaning cars, working in factories, digging tunnels and engaging in construction work. Less than a quarter of all Gaza key informants said they were aware of children employed in domestic labour. therefore, the project will give priority to students at risk of dropping out. A study conducted the defense of children’s rights in June 2018 revealed that 95% of the young people surveyed in Gaza had symptoms of deep psychological distress: depression, hyperactivity, aggression, sleep disorders, anxiety, incontinence, a preference for being alone, sometimes going into a vegetative state. In largely peaceful demonstrations associated with the “Great March of Return started on 30th March 2018; the State of Palestine: Humanitarian Situation Report, January – June 2018 indicated that 135 people have been killed including 17 children. A total of 2,947 children were injured, comprising 19% of total injured, with 50.6% of injuries were to the lower limbs, 7.2% to the head and neck and 48.9% of injures were from live bullets. CFTA psycho-social reports over the last 8 months shows an increasing needs for psychosocial interventions in ARA areas among children.

Activities

- Preparing the baseline for each student enrolled in the project by CFTA and the school- Preparing the selection criteria for the remedial classes insuring access of students in ARA/Almawasi area, students with disabilities and children at risk of being dropped out of schools - Carry out remedial classes for 600 school children throughout the project; each of the remedial class will last for 2 hours including a break and refreshment. Teachers will use informal education methods to implement the class and will provide the students with all educational tools, materials and stationary needed to conduct the class. - Peer to peer learning support will be part of the class techniques, - Producing creative and interactive accessible education tools/games by CFTA’s educators, the school teachers and leaders-students - Carry out refreshing interactive and in-job learning including the use of new creative educational tools based on the module developed and tested by CFTA - Monthly monitoring of each child in the remedial classes based on the monthly results exams and the overall pre-test exam at the start of the project. Each child will have a file that includes the progress of her/his result, projects, monthly exams and feedback from the teachers - Implementing an individual psycho-social assessment for student to determine the intervention needed. - Providing psycho-social support activities including individual and groups counselling - Provide groups psycho-social support: 4 months for each group, including 15 children in each. This will include Drama as Therapy and expressive art activities such as puppetry, drawing and creative writing. - Provide case management for those who need referrals - Integrating extracurricular recreational activities for more enjoyable experience for students at the school. Conducting regular meetings and sessions with parents and teachers aiming at supporting the children enrolled in the project - distribution of school bags, stationary and uniform to most vulnerable students - scanning and distribution of assistive devices and accessible means for students with disability - Organizing child led advocacy with three different groups of students 45 students. the advocacy will target the MoE&HE, UNRWA. Each group will include 15 students who will be working together on education advocacy campaign . this will include Child led research, child led photography and child led factsheet production, Child led Radio production online.
