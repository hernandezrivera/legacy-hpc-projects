Project Summary

Provide up-to-date, relevant and comparable information on the multi-sectoral needs of households across South Sudan in order to inform evidence-based strategic planning of humanitarian response activities.
The MSNA specifically aims to:
● Provide a comprehensive evidence base of the diverse multi-sectoral needs among IDPs, returnees, and host communities to inform the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for South Sudan and 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP).
● Provide an analysis of the South Sudanese population’s needs in 2020.
● Facilitate coordinated joint analysis across and between sectors.
● Through technical oversight, support the continued shift to multisectoral household level data collection in South Sudan
This assessment will build upon the support that REACH provided as a data collection partner for the FSNMS, MSNAs conducted in Maban and Jamjang refugee camps, and on REACH’s existing 14 bases across South Sudan with experienced data collection teams. REACH will also utilise lessons learned from REACH’s global experience in carrying out MSNAs, in particular drawing from its Africa MSNA experience. With more than 291 references in 7 HNOs/HRPs and RRPs in 2018, 2018 MSNAs helped inform protracted humanitarian crises that represent 35% of global funding requirements. In 2019, REACH supported ten MSNAs globally .

1 - Needs

The re-ignition of conflict in South Sudan in December 2013 and July 2016 resulted in the geographical spread of conflict to all ten states. As conflict spread across South Sudan humanitarian needs have been on the rise: Protracted conflict has depleted populations resilience, and continued shocks such as displacement as well as macro-economic and climatic shocks continue to place populations at risk of the worst humanitarian outcomes. The 2020 HNO estimated 7.5 miillion people within the country to be in need of humanitarian assistance. It therefore becomes more important than ever to have reliable data on humanitarian needs across South Sudan. This information allows for the effective design and implementation of sectoral and inter-sectoral programming. Furthermore, while needs assessments have been regularly undertaken by humanitarian partners in South Sudan, multi-sector assessment initiatives serve to reduce assessment fatigue and burden on communities, while enhancing inter-sectoral analysis. A single multi-sectoral assessment, aligned with the humanitarian programme cycle, provides a strategic planning tool for evidence-based prioritisation through comprehensive coverage, consistent methodology, common framework for joint analysis, and buy-in of findings. While an MSNA is intended to support detailed sectoral assessments through analysis of key needs and response gaps, the multi-sectoral nature of the assessment allows for deeper analysis and understanding of key inter-sectoral concepts to support humanitarian planning, including the calculation of inter-sectoral severity scores and People in Need (PiN) numbers.

2 - Activities

For the 2020 MSNA, as part of a coordinated effort with OCHA and the cluster partners involved in the MSNA within the framework of the MSNA-TWG, REACH proposes to carry out country-wide, county-level, household data collection in South Sudan. This methodology will result in a single, representative, and comparable dataset that is more robust than the data utilised for the HNO/HRP in previous years. The findings will be statistically representative at the county level and can thus be generalised to different geographic areas, allowing for meaningful comparison between different counties, states and regions. For more nuanced anaylsis and comparisons of the needs of different population groups, the data will be representative to at least the national-level for demographic groups, such IDPs and returnees. Moreover, the data will be disaggregated by location type (rural, urban or peri-urban) to allow greater understanding of specific needs and challenges for these locations. This type of disaggregation can also be carried out for vulnerable groups , where sample size allows. Methodology: The assessment will employ a mixed method approach with a quantitative assessment using a household-level survey and focus group discussions to provide qualitative data helping to explain key findings. For the survey, a two-stage random sampling will be carried out on the basis of 95% confidence level and a 10% margin of error at the county level. This will be approximately 101 household interviews per county. This is similar to the sample size of FSNMS, and similar methods will be employed, such as cluster sampling and working with data collection partners. The sample unit will be the household and the sample will be drawn from communities, camps, and informal sites. It is anticipated that approximately 9,000 household interviews will be collected through this process. Proposed Activities: REACH activities for the 2020 South Sudan MSNA will include: • Input and review of survey questions, in partnership with relevant sectors;• Sampling, including proposing sampling options and carrying out the sample draw;• Tool testing and piloting;• Developing training guidelines for enumerators;• Training of enumerators in coordination with other data collection partners;• Developing and carrying out data cleaning and following up with both REACH and partner field teams;• Data collection in coordination with other data collection partners;• Production of anonymised cleaned, final dataset;• Initial sectoral and inter-sectoral descriptive analysis;• Joint analysis workshop with all key stakeholders; • Provision of support to sectors in the calculation of their severity scores and PiN;• Provision of support in the calculation of the inter-sectoral severity score and PiN;• Production of sector factsheets based on the findings;• Production of inter-sectoral MSNA report based on the findings Timing: in order to feed into the HNO and HRP process, including the calculation of cluster and intersectoral PINs, the data collection will take place in June to July, with the analysis being available to the clusters by mid-August. Coordination with other assessments: A key added value for the MSNA is the provision of household level data in sectors where this is not currently used for the whole country, that is to say all sectors except FSL and WASH. This will enable a direct, evidence-based comparison of needs across the country, which can inform the prioritization of the most vulnerable locations. A second benefit is that having multiple sectors in one assessment enables intersectoral analysis to help identify the most vulnerable households who have high, overlapping needs in multiple sectors. REACH will work closely with WFP and FAO to minimise duplication with regard to the FSNMS survey, with discussion ongoing. Possibilities include combining the two assessments or not including FSL/WASH in the MSNA, as these are covered in FSMS.
