Project Summary

This project is a roll over from 2019 HRP HPSE19-SHL-150758-1.The project overall objective is to ensure that most vulnerable households with protection concerns due to their substandard housing condition are supported to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity by providing rehabilitation/adaptation assistance (Shelter and NFIs Cluster Objective 2). Providing essential shelter support meets basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation (Strategic Objective 2) and strengthens their capacity to cope with the protracted crisis (Strategic Objective 3). 250 families (1,400 individuals) with the worst combination of socioeconomic vulnerability and housing conditions are selected for support. Factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability include socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD headed), income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets. Factors reflecting shelter vulnerability include gaps in core shelter items based on the Shelter Cluster agreed Minimum Standard for housing in Gaza. The rehabilitation/adaptation support provided by NRC includes core items that ensure physical & climate protection, privacy and dignity, safety & security and reflect gender & protection concerns & special needs like those of people with physical disabilities and access-concerns. Affordable off grid and easy to repair solar systems will be provided to beneficiary households to meet essential household including WASH needs and reduce protection risks. Complementary and based on need, essential household NFIs such as water storage, household assets and equipment, bedding set, kitchen set, dignity kit, hygiene kit are provided.

1 - Needs

The collapsing financial situation, electricity crisis & impact of the Great March of Return(GMR) severely impact the ability of vulnerable households to afford their right to safe &adequate housing, mitigate protection threats & meet basic needs. Most affected arefamilies living in acute poverty and exposed to protection threats due to living insubstandard shelter conditions. This includes GMR-affected with long-term disabilitiesand exhausted coping capacity whose shelters are not safe and adequate toaccommodate their lack of physical mobility & wheel-chair use. Families living in acutepoverty NRC engaged a sample of male and female households especially youth andelderly as well as PWDs through interviews and household vulnerability assessments toreflect shelter and NFI needs including gender, age, protection, and special needs. Basedon data from MoSD (Sep 2018), 539 hardship-households are in urgent need for Sheltersupport. In Oct 2018, NRC conducted household vulnerability assessments of a sample of41 out of these 539 households in 3 vulnerable areas in Middle Area (Deir Al Balah AlBerka neighbourhood), Gaza (Al-Shati refugee camp) and North Gaza (Jabalia refugeecamp). In addition, NRC conducted KIIs & household vulnerability assessments of 10 male& female GMR-affected households in Gaza and Khan Younes Governorates (Sep/Oct2018). The conducted assessments show the following findings with GMR-injured asmost vulnerable in most aspects Overall vulnerability of the assessed households ismoderate (62%), high (7%), severe (5%; GMR: 10%). Socioeconomic vulnerability is largelymoderate (48%) to high (11%; GMR: 20%) - Heads of household: 21% have physicaldisabilities (GMR: 30%), 5% mental disabilities (GMR: 10%), 12.6% widowed (GMR: 30%),73% unemployed (GMR: 100%) - 32% live in overcrowding conditions with negativeimpacts especially for women (see GAM) - 59% are dependent on food assistance - 57%live in shelters with poor roofing (asbestos, corrugated sheets, combined) - Large numberof families lack basic household assets, such as furniture, household equipment, kitchenset, bedding set - Largely similar coping strategies are used: selling household andproductive assets, receiving assistance from family/friends, stopped paying utility bills,accumulated debts, spent past savings, begging/child labor. The shelter vulnerability ismoderate (25%), severe (7%), high (6%) - Major gaps in core shelter-items necessary toprovide a minimum level of physical and climate protection, safety, privacy, dignity, health& wellbeing of male and female family members of all ages and especially familymembers with physical disabilities (doors, walls and roofs, windows, water supply andstorage, shower/toilet, kitchen, electrical connection, access to housing unit & WASHfacilities). Another key finding of KIIs conducted with GMR-injured is their lack of legalinformation on their rights, available legal procedures to seek justice and/orcompensation, or legal actions to receive adequate medical treatment. Female widows asa result of the GMR are now more vulnerable to eviction as their HLP rights and security oftenure depended on their husbands, vulnerable to overcrowding & loss of privacy,vulnerable to increased responsibilities as they will be taking care of injured or disabledmembers of the family, and to shifting roles to become the family breadwinner.

2 - Activities

The identified protection threats in poor shelter conditions are the result of lacking safety& security, privacy & dignity, especially for those households with special needs reflectinggender, age, and disability. To mitigate identified protection threats which acutelyvulnerable households with exhausted coping capacities are exposed to, NRC will provideshelter rehabilitation/ adaptation support for most vulnerable 250 families. It includesitems that ensure physical & climate protection, privacy & dignity, safety & security andreflect gender & protection concerns & special needs like those of people with physicaldisabilities and access-concerns: sealed roof, external walls and door (physical andclimate protection), bedrooms and bathrooms with lockable doors; door width suitable forwheelchair access; separation of sleeping and living spaces; sleeping space separationconsidering sex and age (privacy and dignity), handrails for stairs and roof to protectchildren and elderly, LED emergency lighting to reduce risk of accidents and injuries atnight and reduce GBV; solid lockable external door and lockable windows; entrance rampto housing unit for PWDs and barrier-free access to WASH facilities to ensure hygienicconditions for injured (safety and security), smooth and cleanable floor and wall surfaces;proper ventilation; hygienic food preparation and cooking space; separation of kitchenand bathroom; access to water supply (hygiene). Affordable off grid and easy to repairsolar systems will be provided to beneficiary households to meet essential householdincluding WASH needs and reduce protection risks. Complementary and based on need,NRC provides essential household NFIs such as water storage, household assets andequipment, bedding set, kitchen set, dignity kit, hygiene kit. In case of needs andprotection threats to which NRC cannot respond, NRC project staff is trained to refer casesexposed to protection threats including GBV. This includes children in families with GMR injuredwith post-traumatic stress. NRC engages male & female beneficiaries of all ages indesigning the best technical response to each households’ specific needs and protectionconcerns & supports them in prioritizing their needs considering budget limitations.Beneficiaries are involved in identifying the most suitable modality of implementation(self-help approach/contractor). NRC’s post-project-surveys of male and femalebeneficiary-households indicates that the large majority of households prefer theconditional cash modality for repair/rehabilitation/adaptation while in certaincircumstances, especially female, widow/divorced/elderly/disabled-headed householdsprefer a contractor-approach. Based on social hardship cases registered in MoSD, themost vulnerable in terms of socioeconomic & shelter vulnerability will be selected forsupport based on a detailed household assessment. The factors reflecting socioeconomicvulnerability include socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristicsof the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD-headed), income anddependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, thestatus of household assets. The factors reflecting shelter vulnerability include gaps incore shelter items within 12 set categories based on the Shelter Cluster agreed MinimumStandard for housing in Gaza. Those with the worst combination of socioeconomic &shelter vulnerability will be prioritized for support. Synergies with NRC’s Information,Counselling, and Legal Assistance Programme will be ensured for legal protection. Thiscan consist of raising awareness about HLP rights and responsibilities and support togather proper ownership documentation especially for women who are disadvantaged inaccessing their property & inheritance rights. Both the head of household and thefemale/male partner sign the contract with NRC to ensure equal access to assistance &information.

3 - Indicators

NRC’s M&E framework ensures that relevant M&E tools are used to meet planned targets with quality. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E unit, ensures that a tracking and reporting system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes. An indicator matrix records data from the field on a monthly basis to measure progress and provide corrective measures where needed. NRC supports quality control measures and accountability towards beneficiaries. In addition, the M&E unit conducts regular field visits to monitor project progress and feedback key findings to project teams. NRC conducts a post-project lessons learnt exercise to continuously improve efficiency and effectiveness in implementation. On a technical level, NRC shelter field and office staff tightly monitor the progress in implementation and provide continuous technical support and monitoring visits to target-households during implementation. Additional technical support is provided if heads of households do not feel comfortable/able/experienced to undertake the shelter works themselves. Community outreach and engagement: during the design phase of the project, male and female households of vulnerable and affected communities especially youth and elderly as well as PWDs were consulted to reflect Shelter & NFI needs including gender, age, protection, and special needs. At the start of the project, essential project information including selection criteria and access to NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism is communicated to the target communities through coordination with municipalities, community representatives, neighbourhood committees, key community members, and local NGOs/CBOs. Project information is communicated to both male and female family members in direct communication and through information leaflets which also informs them about NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism. NRC engages male and female beneficiaries in its regular monitoring visits throughout project implementation to ensure the quality of the intervention. NRC conducts post-distribution monitoring and post-project surveys with beneficiary households to measure the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance and the satisfaction of male and female beneficiaries of all ages. NRC’s local partner IDCO is conducting the activities related to the communication with communities at the beginning of the project, and supports the post distribution monitoring and beneficiary satisfaction survey at the end of the project. A close coordination with the Shelter Cluster partners ensures to maximize efficiency and impact in target locations and avoid duplication of assistance. NRC keeps the Clusters informed about the implementation, achievements, and lessons learnt. In the monitoring and evaluation stage, post-distribution monitoring surveys, baseline and endline data collection considers sex and age. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In order to prioritize safety and dignity of vulnerable families and communities over other considerations, NRC will conduct protection risk assessments (Do no Harm), adopt a conflict and context sensitive approach in dealing with beneficiaries, ensure informed beneficiary consent and acting on instructions of beneficiaries, securing confidentiality and data protection. In addition, NRC will use additional protection technical expertise to supplement its interventions by coordinating and referring cases to specialized protection actors.NRC’s Protection Policy, Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and guidance notes and tools including Core Competency Quality Assessment tools and the Protection Resource Pack all contribute to reinforcing and providing guidance on how to action the protection policy. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners and associates (e.g. contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff. Enhancing protection is a key pillar of the proposed activities and will be achieved through mainstreaming protection in the provision of household assistance and achieving protection results with its Shelter/WASH activities.The protection risks are mainly related to safety and security, privacy and dignity, especially for those households with specific needs regarding gender, age, and disability. The proposed assistance toIn the rehabilitation of substandard housing unit’s activities, households will contribute to mitigate the identified risks by targeting households with the worst combination of socioeconomic vulnerabilities and housing vulnerabilities. During the beneficiaries’ selection process, all project information, regarding provided assistance and services, beneficiary selection process and selection criteria, will be shared with the target communities and municipalities. The supported populations will be empowered to request information, raise concerns, and provide feedback related to project activities, which will strengthen the capacity of affected populations to claim their rights. The shelter response will be designed together with the target households. Specific protection and other special needs will be identified and addressed during the intervention. Field staffs are sufficiently trained in identifying protection concerns of vulnerable households. As part of the baseline/endline assessments, target households will be asked protection-oriented questions to assesstheir feeling regarding safety and dignity improvement. The self-assessment of the level of safety and dignity is not only asked in relation to shelter as a product but also to shelter as a process e.g. during the event when contracts are signed, bank cards are received, shelter/WASH responses are designed, implemented, and monitored. A confidential referralprocess is in place to ensure support to identified cases encountering protection risks.The strong community involvement and the feedback mechanism in the project will prevent harm in the first place and will allow project management to quickly react in case of concerns of beneficiaries throughout the project duration. The dignity of target households will be also reflected in using the cash modality (when applicable) and a self-help approach that empowers households to resume their capacity in leading the solution to their concerns rather than being considered as a mere recipient of support.NRC respects confidentiality and safeguards personal and other sensitive information, and apply tight protocol when sharing beneficiary’s information or during the referral of protection cases to the related cluster.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NRC programs are designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to-reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, to reduce their vulnerability and build resilience. This project prioritize most vulnerable families living in substandard and overcrowded conditions, female-headed HHs with low incomes, and HHs with persons with special needs, in order to alleviate financial burden, avoid overcrowded situations without age/gender-based separation in bedrooms and protect them from harsh weather. The beneficiaries’ selection process and the scoring system communicated to the target beneficiaries during the project mobilization period, the scoring system will prioritize the HHs who have the highest socio-economic and housing status vulnerabilities and limited coping capacities.This will be done through coordination with community representatives, neighbourhoods committees, key community members and local NGOs/CBOs to provide a wide range of communities with the opportunity to be involved in project activities.In addition; the Beneficiaries’ selection process will utilize raw data from MoPWH, MoSD cross-checked with UNWRA databases to avoid duplication of assistance. At community level municipalities, CBOs and beneficiaries themselves will be informed about the adopted data validation and beneficiaries’ selection process.
