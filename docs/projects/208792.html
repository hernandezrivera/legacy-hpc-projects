Project Summary

Nigeria is among the top 10 largest food crises in the World and the largest food crisis in West Africa, south of the Sahara, despite its remarkable potential for food production. According to the preliminary result of October 2023 Cadre Harmonise (CH), approximately 4.4 million people in BAY states are projected to experience high acute food insecurity (CH Phase 3 or worse) during the peak of the lean season (June to August 2024), a period that coincides with the rainy season. The situation could have been worse due to the lack of dedicated funding for livelihood interventions. The main drivers of the prevailing food insecurity in Nigeria are largely conflict and insecurity, i.e., the protracted armed insurgency in the northeast led by Boko Haram, banditry, kidnappings, and natural resource-based conflicts in the northwest and north-central. The situation has been exacerbated by the continued increase in food prices as a consequence of the ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflict and new government economic measures, including the removal of fuel subsidies and the continued devaluation of the country's currency (Naira). This has worsened rural households' capacity to access food and agricultural inputs. The most affected are vulnerable groups such as internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, and within those groups, more especially, children, women, and persons with disabilities. Without sustained and scaled-up food availability, facilitation of food access, and livelihood support, it is estimated that a significant proportion of the population in BAY states will continue to endure food insecurity and economic hardship. In addition, negative impacts of climate change and variabilities such as floods and frequent dry spells, pests, and diseases potentially cause severe crop losses.
This year, the northeast states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) have experienced a prolonged dry spell (June- July 2023), affecting food production and food insecurity. Climate variability negatively impacts people living in conflict areas, further compounding the vulnerability of the food production system, which results in poor crop yields and livestock productivity loss with a direct negative impact on households’ livelihood, income, and food security and nutrition.
The greatest burden of food insecurity is in the northeast, in BAY states which are being affected by protracted conflict. The armed insurgency in the northeast BAY states has been ongoing for over a decade and is responsible for severe hardship in most communities. Insecurity led to massive displacement across the BAY states with over 2.2 million people IDPs, of whom 82 percent in Borno, 10 percent in Adamawa, and 7 percent in Yobe (IOM DTM Nigeria Displacement Report 43, February 2023) . A significant number of IDPs (58 percent) are hosted in communities, while approximately 42 percent live in camps or camp-like settlements.
Also, the continued risk of child mortality due to acute malnutrition is alarming, as the number of children suffering from acute malnutrition has increased from 1.74 million in 2022 to over 2 million in 2023. According to the latest IPC Acute Malnutrition Report for May 2023-April 2024, over 1.53 million children under the age of 5 years are acutely malnourished (GAM), with over half a million severely malnourished (SAM). In addition, more than 200,000 pregnant and lactating mothers across BAY are acutely malnourished .
The attacks on civilians have gradually decreased, and in line with the Government’s policy of closing IDP camps and facilitating resettlement in their place of origin, approximately 2 million people have returned to communities of origin, according to IOM.

1 - Needs

In 2024 FAO will continue to support people in need by improving food security, nutritional status and incomes among vulnerable conflict affected households in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states in Northeast Nigeria. The now more than decade-long insurgency and climate change has decimated agricultural livelihoods upon which more than 80 percent of the population relies as a primary source of food and household income. The prolonged dry spells and other climate change variabilities has further compounded this already highly vulnerable humanitarian context, generating interconnected vulnerabilities that negatively affect availability and access to food, agriculture inputs, finance, farming land, extension services, markets and the fundamental viability of agricultural livelihoods. It therefore remains evident that support to the restoration and protection of agricultural livelihoods is key to improving food insecurity and nutrition outcomes as well as mitigating exposure to protection risks.

2 - Activities

Support to Food Production - Support to land preparation including distribution of rainy season and dry season kits (seeds and fertilizer) for crop production as well as essential inputs for backyard vegetable gardening to restore/protect agricultural livelihoods and promote household nutrition. Rainy season crop beneficiaries will also benefit from a Fresh Food Voucher to prevent premature consumption of food stocks during the lean season. - Training of existing seed producers on improved production techniques, good crop production management practices, post-harvest handling, storage and record keeping, including establishment of community-based seed out-growers to promote the production of improved quality and climate-resilient seeds through seed multiplication - Creation of market linkages between seed producers and buyers (agricultural support projects, farmer cooperatives, government) Support to Livestock-based Livelihoods including animal restocking, value chain development and veterinary support. The support will specifically entail: - Animal restocking targeting women and youth with goat and poultry kits for income generation and improved nutrition. Targeted beneficiaries will also benefit from FAO’s ‘cash+’ approach through which they will receive a cash transfer aimed at protecting the livestock assets from premature consumption or sale, and from market linkages. - Vaccination campaign to control animal diseases aimed at protecting livestock-based livelihoods, including biosecurity/biocontainment training for disease control, food safety and animal welfare to key associations/groups involved in livestock production/processing. - Building the capacity of state veterinary services and Community-based Animal Health Workers (CBAHW) to provide animal health services to project beneficiaries and other vulnerable livestock owners. Support to Fisheries - Traditional fishing households that have lost their livelihoods owing to the conflict around the Lake Chad basin will be supported through: - Provision of fish farm starter kits (tanks, fingerlings and feed) for aquaculture production, mainly targeting male youth, including a cash transfer for protection of assets. - Establishment and/or scale-up of private/community-based fingerling production enterprises in close collaboration with the Federal College of Freshwater Fisheries Technology, Baga. - Establishment of local fish feed manufacturing cooperatives through local products assessment and capacity building programme. - Promotion of good hygiene and handling practices through capacity building activities and provision of handling goods and tools to enhance the quality of fish products at market, thereby increasing sale potential. - Provision of fish processing kits (drying, smoking) and trainings to women’s groups to maximize the utilization of fish catch, limit production losses and increase the shelf life of fish products. Protection - Establishment/upgrading of fuel-efficient stove production centers and briquette production centers for income generation. - Distribution of fuel-efficient stoves to households facing heightened protection and health risks; mitigate poor energy access and adoption of negative coping strategies. Increasing Access to Credit To enhance access to credit among vulnerable groups – particularly women – Group Savings and Loans Associations (GLSAs) will be established to facilitate access to financial services through group-mobilized funds. This will promote upscaling and resilience-building by enabling beneficiaries to procure productive assets and develop micro-businesses with the loans and productive resources generated. Food Security Coordination, Data Collection and Analysis - Critical support to the functioning of the Food Security Sector for coordination of food security activities in the BAY states - Critical support to food security data collection and analysis to enable evidence-based programming by food security actors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

FAO will undertake a broad scale protection risk analysis to understand the risks of specific groups across agricultural value chains in the northeast and is well placed to design projects which consider the specific risks facing at-risk groups. FAO has field experts including female field assistants and staff who speak the common local languages in the BAY states and are frequently deployed during trainings, distributions, monitoring and planning missions. To greater understand the protection risks faced by HHs, FAO will undertake a protection risk assessment prior to the implementation of the activity. The Organization will also reinforce training to staff on GBV, PSEA, AAP and CwC. By adopting a CBA, ensuring beneficiary participation in decision-making, and involving local and traditional authorities, the project will promote accountability, capacity building & empowerment. Five percent of the budget will be allocated for the recruitment of a protection expert, training of stakeholders communication and advocacy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO will ensure that feedback mechanisms such as toll free numbers, complaint boxes, complaint desks are available throughout the life cycle of the project and that vulnerable community members have access to these mechanisms. The organization will also ensure that the views and experiences of the most vulnerable are integrated in post-activity monitoring and reporting. FAO will engage a wide cross section of stakeholders to identify the most-vulnerable and those least likely to utilize feedback mechanisms . These individuals will be identified and reached in person where possible and their specific requirements and needs will be taken into account in project design, implementation and appraisal.
