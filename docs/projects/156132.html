Project Summary

The objective of this program is to ensure and improve the food security for the conflict affected populations in north-west Syria through the provision of agricultural and livestock support that would contribute to securing the food needs for the beneficiary farmers and livestock keepers. This program comprise the following main components:
1. provision of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment). Target: 6000 farmers (reached as of mid-year: 3700.
2. distribution of animal feed (fodder/barley) to the sheep and goats of vulnerable livestock keepers. Target: 2000 livestock keepers (reached as of mid-year: 1157).
3. conduct livestock vaccination campaigns immunizing the livestock against different livestock diseases such as: (Enterotoxemia, Foot and Mouth Diseases, and Brucella). Target: 200,000 livestock heads (reached as of mid-year: 2000 heads).
4. rehabilitation of main infrastructure related to agriculture such as (canals, irrigation systems, markets, storage facilities, bakeries, etc.) Target: 20 projects and 2000 CFW workers (reached as of mid-year: 5 projects and 200 workers).
5. supporting farmer groups/initiatives. Target: 10 groups/initiatives (reached as of mid-year 3 groups).
6. support the provision of extension services. Target: 5000 farmers/livestock keepers (reached as of mid-year: 2500 farmers/livestock keepers).

Needs

As per the reported needs in the draft HNO 2019, the scale, severity and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive. This is the result of continuous hostilities in localized areas, new and protracted displacement, increased self-organized returns and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience during close to eight years of crisis. Across Syria, an estimated 11.7 million people require humanitarian assistance, with certain population groups facing particularly high levels of vulnerability. Whilst there has been a reduction in violence in many parts of the country over the past year, the impact of present and past hostilities on civilians remain the principal driver of humanitarian needs in Syria. The proportion of people who are food insecure at national level is 5 percentage less compared to the 2017 HNO. This can be attributed to the significant scale of food and livelihoods/agriculture assistance provided by Food and Agriculture Sector partners that accounted for 5.1 million people being reached on average on a monthly basis with food, and 2.4 million people reached with livelihoods and agriculture assistance from January to October 2018. The agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 25 to 30 per cent of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. For these reasons, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen the resilience of Syrians. Shafak Rapid Needs Assessments showed that the most challenge for agriculture and live sectors are the following:- Unavailability of agriculture and livestock inputs,- High cost and prices of the agriculture inputs.- High cost of transportation and closure of routes.- |Low quality of the agricultural and veterinarian inputs,- Lack of agricultural and veterinarian extension services., - Weaknesses in the value chains regarding marketing and damage to the livelihood infrastructure.As well as a big needs of income generating activities and livelihood.

Implementation of activities

Shafak's implementation approach includes the following steps: - Conducting value chain analysis, and needs assessments as well as baseline study.- community mobilization and signing MoUs with local authorities in addition to beneficiaries registration and verification. - vendor contracting, which will be in compliance with Shafak procurement policies. - voucher design and printing - Distributing agricultural inputs to vulnerable farmers (seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, agricultural tools and cash for fuel and harvest) to enable them to plant winter crops, summer and winter vegetables, and fruit trees, through voucher system. - Forming farmer schools, and provision of extension services and capacity building for local farmers (on Best Agricultural Practices).- rehabilitation of agricultural infrastructure through temporary income-generating activities (Cash for Work) such as cleaning and rehabilitation of: irrigation canals, silos, mills, seed stores, ..etc. to also support marketing - supporting irrigation through enhancing the utilization of alternative energy sources, via the provision of solar-powered irrigation systems, and rehabilitation of: pumps, generators, and irrigation networks. - Supporting community participation in the agriculture sector through the establishment of initiatives (Community-Based Organizations (CBOs)) focusing on the importance of cost-sharing; this includes supporting women food processing associations, water user association, as well as machinery associations. - Linking the wheat farmers with wheat/flour markets and improving the integration with mills, and bakeries to serve bread distribution activities. At the inception phase of the project, Shafak conducts community mobilization activities, where Shafak staff meet with the community representatives and the local authorities, and conduct orientation sessions about the project activities, project approaches and assistance modalities. Shafak accountability team conduct information sharing sessions, and orient the community on the feedback mechanisms. The received feedback from the different gender and age groups in such sessions and in the similar previous projects are taken into consideration while designing the current program with specific focus on the vulnerable groups of women, youth, adolescents and elderlies. Shafak accountability team maintain the collection of feedback throughout the projects, and have the feedback collected by sex and age to be able to analyse the impact of the project on the different groups. Similarly, Shafak MEAL team collects satisfaction surveys about the provided assistance after its delivery and collect these data disaggregated by gender and age.

Protection Risk Analysis

- limitations on movement and physical security risk due to conflict activity.- Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girl’s mobility. - Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. Shafak will mitigate the above risks through the community orientation sessions, in which the Shafak staff ensure that equality concepts are conveyed to the community.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Looting by Armed Groups (Low); involving the LCs and stakeholders of the society in the whole process, to avoid this matters. Putting all security procedures in place before starting distribution. 2. Aggression (by dissatisfied beneficiaries) (Medium); involving the LCs in the whole process, to avoid this matters. Awareness session and community mobilization. 3. Market Price Increased, lead to the increase in agriculture inputs prices (Medium); signing the contract with the suppliers including all the list of items with fixed prices. 4. Beneficiaries re-displaced (High); By making two lists of beneficiaries Most Vulnerable and less vulnerable, to replace the displaced beneficiaries with next eligible non-beneficiaries. 5- Losing access to the targeted location(s) (High): relocation and proposition of the activities to safer area after coordinating with the donor. 6. Bombardment activities by GoS: (Medium): suspend the project’s planned activities for two days until situation can calm down.
