Project Summary

According to the 2024 Protection Cluster and its Areas of Responsibility (AoRs) in South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Response Plan (HRP), 9.18 million people or 81% of the 11.29 million affected population (including IDPs, returnees and residents) face at least one of the top five protection risks. In particular, returnees emerged as the most vulnerable group, with 98% (840,000 people) exposed to protection risks, followed by IDPs at 81% (1.58 million). ISNA highlighted that 36% of women and girls avoid areas due to safety concerns, while 53% of households face barriers to essential services. Limited GBV services were reported in 35% of districts, with an estimated 2.75 million people in need of GBV interventions. 7.1 million (56.3% of the population) projected to face food insecurity. This has been exacerbated by extreme flooding that has affected more than 78 counties in the country. IRNA assessment in Northern Bahral El Ghazal September 2024 revealed that, floods devastated 27 out of 29 payams in the four counties of Aweil East, West, South and North, displacing 246,048 people to higher ground. More than 67,000 feddans of crops and 17,000 head of livestock were destroyed. Community infrastructure, including 29 health facilities, 171 schools and 350 latrines, was either submerged, damaged, or destroyed by the floods. The crises disproportionately affect persons with disabilities, exacerbating exclusion and barriers to access. HI will provide integrated protection and health services to flood- and conflict-affected communities in GPAA and Aweil. The project will address protection risks, including GBV and human rights violations, by providing case management, psychosocial support, life skills training and capacity building for frontline workers and community structures, including OPDs. Under protection, HI will focus on general protection and GBV prevention and response services. Targeted support will address the specific needs of persons with disabilities and survivors of GBV to improve their physical and mental well-being. Activities will include the distribution of dignity kits, building the confidence of adolescent girls through the Girls Shine programme, and engaging men in responsible practices (EMAP) to prevent GBV. Health interventions will focus on MHPSS and functional rehabilitation, such as mobility aids, facility-based stimulation therapy and physiotherapy, to promote active participation of PWD in social life and facilitate their access to essential services. HI will provide WHO PM+ based individual and group counselling to support recovery from trauma, depression and stress for people with and without disabilities. HI will ensure inclusive access to services through barrier assessments and the establishment of referral systems linking protection and health teams. . As co-leader of the GBV AoR in GPAA, HI will strengthen coordination between GBV actors and humanitarian partners for a harmonised response. This initiative aims to meet immediate humanitarian needs while promoting long-term recovery and resilience through inclusive service delivery, capacity building and strategic partnerships.

1. Needs

South Sudan is the second country with the highest Inform Risk Index 2024, after Central African Republic and Somalia. South Sudan is also ranked as the 13th country in the world with acute food insecurity at levels 4 and 5 (famine) by (IPC). 2024 Protection Cluster and its Areas of Responsibility (AoRs) in South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Response Plan (HRP), 9.18 million people or 81% of the 11.29 million affected population (including IDPs, returnees and residents) face at least one of the top five protection risks. Women compounded crises has increased the heightened protection and GBV risk on women and young girls, who are often more vulnerable in unstable environments.Humanitarian partners estimate that half the population of Pibor - 105,540 people - could be affected by floods and food insecurity. In addition, the disruption of emergency food distributions between September 2024 and December will lead to severe food security outcomes in parts of western Pibor, where chronic localised insecurity and lack of equipment have undermined the mitigating effects of agriculture and fishing. In 2024-2025 IPC Between April and July 2025, Pibor is classified as IPC Phase 5. This includes 12,000 people (5% of the population) in IPC Phase 5 (Disaster). August 2024 Rapid needs assessment conducted by HI and ForAfrika in GPAA highlights critical protection concerns, including sexual and gender-based violence, robbery, domestic violence and child abduction, with women, girls and persons with disabilities being particularly vulnerable. The rapid needs assessment also revealed widespread GBV incidents affecting the lives of women and girls, particularly women and girls with disabilities. Armed and unarmed perpetrators target women on the streets and in public spaces, with physical, emotional and economic abuse being common forms of violence. Intimate partner violence is common but under-reported due to cultural stigma and the perception of violence as a private matter. Harmful practices such as child marriage and traditional groupings increase the risk of GBV. Key drivers of GBV include socio-economic constraints, alcohol abuse, communal conflict and inadequate access to basic services such as safe water and sanitation. South Sudan faces a severe mental health treatment gap, with 99% of people in need of care unable to access it. Despite the estimated 204,000 people suffer from severe conditions such as psychosis and major depression, while over 1 million suffer from mild to moderate disorders such as anxiety, fewer than 10,000 people a were served a year. HI's 2023 Multisectoral Needs Assessment also revealed that 92% of respondents reported MHPSS shocks in the past two years, and 98% have not recovered enough to lead normal lives.

2. Project activities

To address the above needs,HI will provide integrated preventive and responsive protection and health services to flood and conflict-affected communities inGPAA and Aweil. The intervention will address the underlying causes of protection risks, including GBV incidents, such as human rights violations, by providing case management, psychosocial support, cash assistance, life skills training, capacity development on protection and GBV prevention and response issues to frontline partners and community structures, including OPDs. Under health, HI will provide MHPSS and functional rehabilitation services as an integral part of this project, helping people to recover from trauma, rebuild their mental and emotional wellbeing and regain physical functioning. These services are essential to enable beneficiaries to overcome the physical and psychological effects of conflict and disaster-related disabilities and impairments, and to improve their ability to participate actively in family and community life. HI will ensure inclusive access to essential services, including GBV case management, MHPSS and rehabilitation for women, men, boys and girls, with and without disabilities. Persons with disabilities will be directly targeted with tailored activities such as functional rehabilitation where HI will provide assitive devices, facility based stimulation therapy and phsyiotherapy. WHO PM+ based Individual and group counslling and capacity building for health service providers' staff. HI will conduct barrier and facilitator assessments at the start of the project to ensure barriers that might hinder from accessing service are removed. The project will create internal synergies by establishing a referral system between the protection and health teams to support persons with disabilities and GBV survivors to access services in a timely manner.As co-leader of the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR) in the GPAA, HI will strengthen coordination with GBV actors, clusters, by reporting to GBVAoR 5ws regular and and humanitarian partners to ensure a harmonized response. Through the proposed intervention, HI will address the immediate needs and fostering long-term inclusive recovery. To achieve this, HI will implement following activities. GBV:- A1: Provide safe, inclusive and survivors centered GBV case management and psychosocial support to survivors. - A2: Strengthen GBV coordination mechanisms and developing functional referral pathways.- A3: Provide technical support on inclusive humanitarian action and conduct barrier and facilitator assessments for accessibility.- A4: Distribute dignity kits for women and girls with disabilities to enhance hygiene, safety, and dignity.- A5: Implement the Girls Shine program to build confidence in adolescent girls and address early and forced marriage.- A6: Engage Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP) as main prevention strategies.- A7: Map and update and conduct regular safe audit assessment. GP:- A1: Provision of one individual protection assistance (IPA) cash to vulnerable individuals with and without disabilities. - A2: Conduct targeted community awareness sessions on rights, protection mechanisms, and available services. - A3: Training humanitarian staff, community-based protection committees, OPDs and local stakeholders.- A 4: Coordinate and collaborating with partners, protection cluster, CP and GBVAoR. Health:- A1: Conduct a barrier-facilitator assessment to identify facilitators, gaps, and barriers.- A2. Provision of mobility aids and physiotherapy sessions for persons with disabilities. - A3: Training of health workers in basic functional and physical rehabilitation.- A 4.: Provision of individual psychotherapy to males and female with more psychological problems. - A5. Provision of group counselling to both male and female with and without disabilmale and female with and without disabilities. - A6. Provision of capacity development to Health workers on MPHSS.
