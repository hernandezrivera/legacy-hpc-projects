Project Summary

The project is designed to integrate protection services (GP,GBV,CP) in 6 targeted sub-districts Afrin/Azaz/Albab /Dana /maartTamsrin/Bennsh for 1 year.
GP intervention: providing quality protection services, with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness-raising, community initiatives, and psychosocial well-being through help desks and outreach mechanisms that aim at support vulnerable children, women & girls at-risk groups, PwD, and affected populations to easily access available services within the targeted areas (Albab,Dana,MaartTamsrin and nearby camps for 3500 BNFs( 1750W,1750 M).
GBV interventions: The project is designed to continue 4 existing WGSS that aim at improving access to quality and life-saving GBV response services for GBV survivors and W&G at risk and put measures in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV through providing referral services to GBV-related, medical, and specialized PSS services according to the referral pathway mechanism provided by GBVSC, plus MWL's GBV officers will develop the GBV service mapping referral pathways, and coordinate with partners who provide these services on the ground. Training of 50 GBV workers, in line with GBV SC (GBV SOPs, PSS, Women empowerment, and CM)and participation in the training organized by the GBV SC when available to ensure alignment with SC GBV standards.
And enhance strategies to empower women and girls, with a particular focus on most at-risk groups (adolescent girls, divorced and widowed W&Gs, W&Gs with disabilities) to prevent GBV and to change negative attitudes and beliefs that foster harmful social norms,.. (including on associated GBV risks and GBV key messages)for 5000 BNFs(900M,3000W, 200 B,900G) at 4 sites.As well as promote GBV risk mitigation in all aspects of the humanitarian response through training 50 non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV risk mitigation according to(IASC GBV guidelines, GBV SOPs). Develop GBV safety audits in the camps and establish focal points (IASC GBV guidelines).
CP intervention:the project is designed to continue 8 existing CFSs that aim at increase and more equitable access for 20700 BNFs(4500W,3500M,6350B,6350G) to quality child protection interventions in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action and targeted the most vulnerable groups (Orphans, separated children, adolescents girls,CwD,...) through providing community-based child protection by engaging 6000 children in structured,semi-structured PSS and recreation activities, and 4000 (2000W,2000 M) in parenting programs, awareness-raising, and community events to prevent and respond to child protection issues for 9000 Adults and children, providing specialized child protection services, case management including ECF 1700(850G, 850B) based on CM guidelines and CPSC SOPs and Referral of child protection cases to specialized services (child protection needs) according to the referral pathway mechanism provides by CP SC, plus MWL's CP officers will develop the CP service mapping referral pathways, and coordinate with partners who provide these services on the ground. Integrating of PwDs with the community by a specialist for PwDs and integrating them into activities where
the centers are equipped with special latrines' with hand railings and ramps. Training of 100 CP workers in line with child protection minimum standards.
The implementing activities will be according to the Operational guidance note for GP, CP,and GBV Activities.
As MWL will report to monthly Protection cluster 4Ws.
MWL will conduct community engagement and coordinate with leaders and key community members throughout the assessment, planning stages, implementation, and close-out of the project.
Several tools (Surveys as Kobo and hard copy) will be adopted to monitor the project activities. Focus group discussion (FGD) on a voluntary basis will be adopted to measure beneficiary satisfaction focusing on PWD, Children, and CwDs considering gender and age

3 - Needs

Families and communities in NW Syria continue to experience violence, displacement, poverty, and the consequences of Covid-19. Data from HNO 2023 show the long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9 million IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over two million people in 1,760 informal settlements and planned camps compound protection risks,Main Findings – HNO 2023 - GBV mentioned that still women and girls suffering from family violence (psychological, restriction of movement, deprivation of resources). Voices from Syria reported that women and girls wished for a greater number of WGSS: “Furthermore, they should increase the number of women protection centers within the camp“.MWL FGD findings in the assessed camps: W&Gs highlighted the need to provide PSS, support for GBV survivors, WGSS, and empowerment activities. where GBV, particularly sexual harassment, early marriage, and domestic violence continues to be pervasive in the lives of women and girls.CiN-2023 estimated children comprise almost half of the affected population 6.01 m children (55.8%% B,44.2%G) as psychosocial distress among children is reported in 26% of surveyed HHs, MWL FGD’s key findings in the assessed camps highlighted the high occurrence of child labor that prevent school attendance by obliging them to leave school or requiring them to combine school attendance with excessively long and heavy work as also clarified in CPM (65% of interviewed in Dana, 70% in Maaret Tamsrin, 74% in Afrin).
