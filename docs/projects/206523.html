Project Summary

Venezuela is currently undergoing a complex humanitarian situation, deeply affecting peoples’ ability to fully enjoy and benefit from their human rights. Actors seeking to respond to needs are also exposed to the challenges emerging from this context, thus limiting their capacity to prevent, mitigate and respond to needs of some of Venezuela’s most vulnerable communities. It is clear that ensuring humanitarian access is intrinsically linked to the protection of civil society actors, including within the humanitarian space.
This project seeks to increase actors’ capacity to confront some of the challenges they encounter in their day to day, focusing on the provision and implementation of tools and strategies towards improved well-being and self-protection. In addressing this, OHCHR also aims to take into account the differentiated impact of protection risks on most vulnerable groups and/or exposed actors, including from within the LGBITQ+ community, elderly persons, persons with disability and indigenous people. Protection risks are also significantly gendered, including gender inequality, gender-based violence and limited access to sexual and reproductive rights and essential services, calling for a holistic and tailored response, in the spirit of Leave no one behind.
This project is framed within the Protection Cluster Logical Framework 2023 and is linked to strategic objectives 3 (3.1.2), and OHCHR Venezuela already benefits from a pre-budgeted Gender Officer and Gender Unit to support gender mainstreaming throughout implementation.
GOAL 1: Strengthening self-protection and security strategies of civil society actors within the humanitarian space.
This objective seeks to complement OHCHR’s ongoing efforts to build capacity of civil society actors in Venezuela, and to ensure sustainability of outcomes. Self-protection has been raised by civil society actors as a key need to support their ability to, in turn, respond to the needs of Venezuela’s most vulnerable communities.
Activities include:
-Provide training to civil society actors working within the humanitarian space, including women, indigenous women, LGBTI persons, on integrating best practices on security, prevention and risk management, including self-protection with gender and human rights approach;
-Establishment of accompaniment mechanism to support implementation of self-protection plan and towards tailored orientation.
-Creation of tools and guidance for secure and inclusive replication of activities
GOAL 2: Venezuela’s humanitarian preparedness, response, and recovery efforts is inclusive of a human-rights based protection.
Capacity building of civil society actors operating within the humanitarian space must be accompanied by comprehensive mechanisms that support early identification of risks. Thus, OHCHR aims for human rights-based protection becomes an integral part of Venezuela’s humanitarian preparedness, response, and recovery effort.
Activities include:
-Enhance visibility of specific challenges encountered by civil society actors operating in the humanitarian space, with emphasis an intersectional gender perspective and other vulnerability criteria
-Support the function of mechanisms for prevention and risk mitigation, as well as towards response to threats and risks faced by Civil Society actors operating within the humanitarian space
-Strengthen the knowledge of humanitarian actors on human rights standards, tools and the international mechanisms of protection including gender and human rights perspective.
-Monitoring, analysis and reporting of human rights trends and patterns that are specific to CSO actors operating within the humanitarian space, including gender perspective and other vulnerability criteria

3 - CENTRALIDAD DE LA PROTECCION
3.1. ¿Cómo las actividades del proyecto garantizan la seguridad, acción sin daño y el acceso efectivo y sin discriminación a los servicios ofrecidos en el proyecto?

The activities of this project have for main objective strengthening protection and self-protection mechanisms for civil society actors operating within the humanitarian space. It seeks not only to provide the tools and instruments for actors to build their capacity to prevent and respond to risks, but also to provide tailored accompaniment and to ensure replication of activities. OHCHR operates in full respect of do no harm, conducting regular risk analysis prior, during and after its interventions. Ending discrimination is a key human rights objective, and OHCHR strives to adopt inclusive and participatory methodologies to ensure no one is left behind from its interventions.
