Project Summary

In 2019, ADRA will continue to support protection for the SSR by strengthening community based self-protection structures such as Inter Communal Dialogue Groups (ICDGs), Women’s Groups (WGs) and forming Youth Groups. This action will strengthen accountability towards decision makers and other actors. The aim is to institutionalize the groups to be part of the former camp governance system. This action will build the capacity of the SSR and host community (HC) to improve social protection systems and increase the groups’ awareness of and linkage to referral mechanisms.
This action will reduce protection risks associated with negative coping mechanisms by implementing cash for work and distributing charcoal vouchers. More specifically, these cash based interventions will reduce exposure to GBV during firewood collection, supported by production and distribution of Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES).
Particular attention will be given to trainings on stove usage and environmental issues and cash for work will include targeted reforestation as well as FES production, thereby strengthening the linkage between activities, aligning interests of SSR and HC and creating space for the SSR and HC to jointly address protection issues.
In Khartoum the project aims at supporting refugee children enrollment, as well as female targeted literacy programs. The programs will be conducted hand in hand with protection and community engagement programs in the same area.

Needs

According to the Commission on Refugees (COR), as of December 2018, the camps in White Nile currently host a total of 39,355 displaced households, which represents an increase of 5,533 HH from 2017, and is further forecasted to increase to over 46,000 in the coming year if the situation remains unstable in South Sudan. Among the SSR arriving in Sudan, 88 per cent are women and children. Having usually travelled long distances to seek safety and protection, these people need food, water, shelter and health assistance, as well as livelihood opportunities. The protracted refugee crisis, in addition to the deterioration of the economic situation of the host community, has further exacerbated the suffering. According to the latest UNHCR SENS data, the overall nutritional status of refugees in Khor Alwaral, Um Sangour and Al Redis (1 & 2) was found to be critical with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates exceeding the critical threshold. Access to food and nutrition is very limited, and the highest undernutrition rates were reported in the three camps of Khor Alwaral (19.4 %), Um Sangour (16.1%) and Al-Redis (18.8%). Females are especially impacted by the crisis, and field observations and assessments show that females prioritize male members for feeding. To help meet the basic household needs, the population in camps have resorted to negative coping mechanisms, such as risky activities and begging. Um Sangour has the highest rate of negative coping mechanisms such as selling alcohol, tree cutting and harmful/unlawful activities that involve boys and girls (e.g. early marriage and smuggling), as well as selling food and assets. The use of negative coping strategies is also confirmed by ADRA’s current projects where beneficiary households report that they have sold food rations in order to help meet their basic household needs. Some of the coping mechanisms also reported during PDMs is selling the food they receive from the monthly distributions, collecting firewood and an increased number of children skipping schools to undertake seasonal agricultural work to be able to provide for necessary commodities. Even though there is an ongoing intervention supported by ECHO to tackle the need for firewood, which has decreased the number of trips outside of the camps, firewood remains a major source of cooking fuel as well as access to cash. A UNHCR mission during April 2018 found that fuel-efficient improved stoves had replaced other types of stoves in most households, and refugee women claimed that wood consumption had been reduced by 50 percent. As of now, ADRA (as the only humanitarian actor providing FES) has produced and distributed 19,750 stoves, which represents a coverage of 48% of the current total population of the camps. Considering the expected new arrivals, the gap will continue to widen throughout the project period.

Activities

 Classroom construction and Furbishing: ADRA Sudan will construct 35 new classrooms in camps and surrounding localities of Al Jabalain and Al Salam localities.  New Uniforms through cash for work: Furthermore, the project will provide 10,000 uniforms for the new enrolled children in grade one as well as new arrivals.  Text books and school materials: Based on UNHCR recent assessment 2018, it was found that need for textbook and notebooks remains high. ADRA supported with UNHCR and in coordination with MoE and localities in WN will provide 26,633 textbooks and 40,218 school sets (notebooks and students learning materials) to SSR and host communities’ students in Al Jabalain and Al Salam localities  Teacher’s offices: Referring to 2018 UNHCR assessments it was found that there is gab of 14 teacher’s office need to be filled in WN state for the SSR camps.ADRA supported by UNHCR will construction 4 teacher’s offices with teacher’s supplies and cupboards. INEE training: ADRA will conduct INEE workshop training targeting 50 participants of the project education partners in WN state. Fuel Efficient Stoves-Cash for work: The project will continue to address the fuel needs through the production and distribution of 5,000 FES to SSR and HC HHs, thereby reducing fuel consumption and expenditure. Community groups:), ADRA will strengthen the existing community groups and establish new groups, including youth groups, as one of the main platforms for community representation, lobbying and advocacy, to address and/or refer issues/incidents as they are brought to their attention by the community members.  Charcoal Vouchers: In the current action, ADRA will distribute charcoal vouchers to 5,500 households on a monthly basis for six months. The charcoal cash vouchers will target SSR HHs, both existing and new arrivals, based on vulnerability criteria.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The environmental impact of the project will be adequately assessed. Communities will be involved in decision making process and in identifying more environmental friendly solutions in order to promote more ownership towards the environment. For instance when installing water tanks in camps and jerry cans, ADRA will work on providing efficient and sustainable taps to avoid water leakage and replacement of taps. As in the fuel efficient stoves, ADRA will work in promoting more efficient technologies as well as tailored approach that would help in having refugees and communities acceptance those technologies.
