Project Summary

Food security remains a matter of serious concern in South Sudan. With approximately 73% of the population living in poverty, many households remain vulnerable as climate related shocks, high inflation rates, sustained conflict and the spillover effects of Sudan crisis continue to worsen the situation. According to the IPC AMN (Oct 2023 to Mar 2024), Ayod is among other 45 counties in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). The same report projects that between July 2023 and June 2024 1.65 M children under 59 months will suffer acute malnutrition with 480,000 suffering from SAM and 1.16M expected to suffer from MAM, 870,000 women are expected to suffer from MAM. 72% of this malnutrition burden will be concentrated in five states including Jonglei.
UNICEF’s Humanitarian Situation Report No. 6 reported in the first half of 2023 149,119 children between 6-59 months were admitted in OTP with the highest admission reported in Jonglei at 20% which is a 28 percent increase as compared to the same period in 2022.
This project will emphasize on prevention of malnutrition by applying Social and Behavior Change Communication strategy with the primary audience for this intervention being pregnant women, lactating women and caregivers to children under 59 months. The RAO nutrition team will carry out the summarized activities: One on one Counselling: priority groups for each behavior will be counselled by MIYCN counsellors and CNVs. Formation of Mother To Mother Support groups who will have weekly sessions to interact and have group counselling, Conduct outreach visits house to house to counsel and motivate mothers of children under 6 months for exclusive breastfeeding, trace defaulters and collect information on reasons for absenteeism and defaulting as well as conduct education on key hygiene messages. Conduct early risk assessment and information sharing about breast related conditions while counselling mothers who have challenges introducing complementary feeding for their children, coordinate with the CHD to conduct behavior change trainings for health personnel to promote their critical role in ensuring babies are breastfed within first hour of life, skin to skin contact for baby and mother and ensure they are enabled with knowledge to provide advice and emotional support to new mothers. Conduct cooking demonstrations with the lead mothers from MTMSG. Positive Deviance/Hearth (PDH) Model will be applied to identify mothers who will demonstrate preparation of complementary food for children 6 months and above, using the locally available highly nutritious foods. RAO nutrition team will also integrate their intervention with other sectors including WASH partners (PASS, CMD, CAO and ADA) to provide hand washing lessons and equipment and standard WASH kits to caregivers of children admitted in CMAM program. Coordination with FSL partner (CMD, CRS, ALSI and PASS) to link vulnerable children and their caregivers to ongoing programs of GFD, Food for Asset and promotion of kitchen gardens and vegetable gardening. RAO team will also coordinate with UNICEF, MOH and the nutrition cluster to provide other reminder channels and IECs like Posters, billboards, banners to be placed at strategic locations like health facilities, distribution points and markets so that a maximum number of people can see them. This will act as cues and will reiterate and remind the audiences of key messages. In addition, a series of activities will be planned to engage the communities on the Word Breastfeeding week. Community outreach component will be implemented on a large scale in the county to identify children with acute malnutrition on time and refer them to the nutrition OTP and TSFP centers operated by CMD. This will be achieved through use of MUAC tapes and referral forms (the family MUAC approach will be applied).
RAO has been responding to the increasing humanitarian needs in Ayod County where we have static presence providing peacebuilding and ESNFI services.

1. Needs

According to an interagency needs assessment conducted on 2nd to 9th August 2023 in Ayod County the humanitarian situation is worsening due to influx of returnees fleeing the Sudan crisis. The local authorities reported over 7,000 persons have returned from Sudan. There is an acute food shortage with a measles outbreak among IDPs in Pajiek Payam this has caused panic among local populations with fears of spread to entire county. The returnees and IDPs are integrated within the resident community and lack proper shelter with many of them observed to be seeking shelter under trees and in abandoned community structures. This has exposed the most vulnerable members of the community like children, PLWs and the elderly to harsh environmental conditions. KIIs conducted with key community contacts revealed food, medicine and shelter materials as the most pressing need. Ayod continues to record high GAM rates with Ayod town Kuachdeng, longor, Niwniw Yian, Panyang and Wunbiem nutrition sites closed down due to funding gaps. This has forced PLWs and care takers for malnourished children to walk long distances to access services.Other needs identified during the assessment was the need for WASH services like gender segregated latrines, dignity kits water storage and treatment items and rehabilitation of water points. SGBV is rampant in Ayod with 3 rapes cases reported in Paguong and Ayod town among the IDPs population. This makes child protection and GBV services an essential need in Ayod, since women, girls, young boys, and elderly are exposed to many forms of violations including domestic violence. Health and Nutrition needs have increased and there’s needs for scale up and replenishing stock. According to the IRNA report the main driving factors are mainly attributed to the influx of returnees from Sudan and Gambella region of Ethiopia.Access to basic services like health, nutrition and WASH services has been severely affected by a wave of floods which has hit the county. Though the water levels had reduced during the short dry spell of October and early November the recent rains have caused water levels to rise up again completely inhibiting vehicular movement and making access to services almost impossible. The most at risk among affected people are those with pre-existing vulnerabilities, such as PLWDs, lactating mothers, pregnant women, elderly and those with mobility challenges, and other health issues. GFD program implemented by CRS doesn’t reach IDPs and returnees and it only covers the resident population. This is making the food shortage more acute and may be a source of conflict among the community. As a coping mechanism a large proportion of the population is relying on wild fruits, green leaves, sharing the small available food and selling HH assets to purchase food. Fishing is also a way of survival for communities along the river banks but they lack fishing gear.

2. Project activities

The RAO team will implement the following activities to achieve the stated goal and objectives: Contributing to CO1: Promote MIYCN by conducting individualized and group counselling on breastfeeding and complementary feeding reaching 12,000 PLWs and caretakers of children under 5. Provide vitamin A supplementation to 17,200 children and deworm 14,000 children. Contributing to C03: RAO M&E team will support the nutrition specialist to conduct one smart survey and MIYCN barrier analysis to advise on priority groups that will be targeted in the SBCC intervention, the surveys will also provide data on GAM rates and the common MIYCN practices.Contributing to cluster objective 4: The RAO team will link 500 most vulnerable children discharged from CMAM program to partners implementing CVA in the county. The RAO team will also integrate ECD in 10 nutrition sites where the team will engage children with locally made toys for stimulation and catchup growth. RAO protection officer will conduct one GBV risk analysis in 5 nutrition sites the findings will be used in quality programming and provide information on protection risks experienced by women and children when accessing services. The protection officer will also train all the CNVs and MIYCN counsellors on GBV basics, PSEA, RAO code of conduct and how to appropriately respond and handle GBV disclosures. Finally, the RAO nutrition team will work with FSL partner and MtMSGs to establish kitchen gardens with locally available highly nutritious veggies and conduct cooking demos at nutrition sites in liaison with CMD.
