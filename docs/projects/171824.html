Project Summary

Based on the analysis of the conflict situation in Banaadir, Lower Shebelle, and Gedo region, NoFYL intends to scale up its protection program to contribute towards enhancing protection, increasing safety, and promoting the dignity of conflict-affected women, men, girls, and boys, in target regions. These areas are currently concerned by prolonged drought, displacement, and voluntary return movements and, with the intensification of the military operations around AS control areas, especially in Lower Shebelle and Jubaland regions. It is expected that a high number of newly displaced people and or newly accessible people will be imminently in need of critical assistance. Medical and psychosocial services available to survivors in the target locations are insufficient, inappropriate, and below the expectation of survivors. These coupled with low levels of community awareness on issues of GBV stigma and other cultural barriers that encourage impunity for breaches of in-laws and convections. In target locations, there has been a steady increase in GBV cases since December 2017. These have been attributed to the increasing levels of conflict, which is one of many active military zones, and the presence of military front-lines in the identified locations, and a high number of IDP camps adjacent to the military camps. Increased forced evictions within the camp have also exposed more women and children to the risk of GBV in the camps hence increasing their vulnerability.
All the project activities will be carried out to maximize community participation. Regular meetings and capacity building activities at the existing women and girl safe space will seek to engage the community in project activities and support the establishment and development of community networks to strengthen local mechanisms for prevention and response by providing support to existing community structures, the project will also seek to strengthen the safe spaces for women to improve protection and to widen support networks, through the deployment of specialized mobile teams composed of Social Worker, Case Manager, Community Health Worker, and Nurses for increased identification and service delivery. Medical support and psychosocial support with components of material support (dignity kits and solar lantern) will be the backbone of this program.
The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of women, girls, boys and men will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender and disability inclusion will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with beneficiaries when undertaking protection assessments through staffs accessing them and identifying their needs. Cognizant to promote equity, NoFYL shall endeavor to provide equal chance, inclusiveness and participation of minority and majority community members as well as disabled groups in the locations and entrench transparency in recruitment of beneficiaries, capacity building, empowerment and resource sharing to achieve a comprehensive community engagement.
The present intervention will also focus on reaching vulnerable groups of individuals, such as extremely vulnerable individuals, children and adolescents at risk, female HHs, elderly, disabled, etc. Active participation of individuals with special needs will be pursued to identify, support and strengthen the coping mechanisms that beneficiaries often create to protect themselves against threats to their safety and dignity, and also foster beneficiaries’ self-esteem and development of skills.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1.1: - Provision of Comprehensive Case management services to GBV survivors (sexual violence, recruitment, abduction, and maiming), including specialized clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), will be provided by trained nurses and case management team within 72 hours. Central to the health service providers will be a strong referral mechanism that will help survivors of GBV in accessing a comprehensive package of integrated services for survivors. Activity 1.1.2: - Provisional of psychosocial support, basic emotional support, and mental health services. Psychosocial support to GBV survivors will be provided by trained counselors and caseworkers who will offer to support if the survivor experiences any post-traumatic incidents. Activity 1.1.3: - Provision of material support through the distribution of dignity, hygiene kits, and solar lanterns as an emergency response targeting women at risk of GBV. This project will provide dignity hygiene kits and solar lanterns to 2000 women and girls of reproductive age within the project locations. Activity 1.1.4: - Provide capacity building to 400 members from GBV AoR members and other NGO staff, service providers (e.g; social workers), and duty-bearers (e.g from the line ministry) particularly, those supporting emergency response teams. Training will be on CMR (4 days targeting 160 participants) SCA, (2 days each targeting 160 participants), and PFA (2 days each targeting 60 participants). The PMS training will target 20 NoFYL staff and, will be led by the protection cluster trainers. Activity 1.1.5: - Adopt the harmonized GBV AoR IEC messaging, print, and distribute through teams of GBV Outreach Workers in the target locations with a target population of 11,700HH combined. Through door-to-door awareness-raising and weekly community outreach, the community caseworkers each will be able to distribute over 12,000 IEC materials on GBV prevention and response for GBV, referral pathways, and available services in the local language. Activity 1.1.6: - Supports economic empowerment and skills development of vulnerable women - 200 women will benefit in tie and dye skills training for GBV Survivors in target locations. The skills training program puts emphasis on decreasing vulnerability of GBV woman survivors and vulnerable women through participation in livelihood generating activities. The objective is to empower, educate and raise their awareness in the protection measures against GBV and to reintegrate them into the society in a sustainable and dignified ways. Interventions include technical and skill trainings combined with a startup kit at the end of training to enable them to establish small business enterprises, beneficiaries will also undergo literacy and numeracy training. Activity 1.1.7: - Establishment of Women and Girls safe space in each of the target locations according to UNFPA/IRC global standards. 5,120 direct beneficiaries will regularly access the safe spaces through service provisions, 5,883HH will be reached indirectly through access to awareness services through the women and girls safe spaces constructed in the vicinity of their camps. The established safe space will provide a space where women and girls can go to at any time to feel safer and empowered and have access to information, skills building activities, support and services. The safe space will be used for various activities such as: psychosocial support, safety planning and risk reduction, skills-building, dignity kits and solar lantern distributions, literacy and numeracy training, skills training on tie & dye.

Please provide further explanation

The sustainability of the project will be ensured through community ownership of the project. For all activities reaching the communities, IDP community leaders and host community leaders, including women and youth leaders will be consulted in order to adapt the activity to the context and ensured community participation, Where possible community members will be encouraged to volunteer and participate in the project. Collaboration with both the host community and IDPs will ensure the project adheres to do no harm principles. Using community participation leverage will ensure a long term impact of the project. Establishment of community structures and its capacity-building aims at ensuring the community focal persons have the capacity to continue support referrals from the communities to other service providers beyond the project durations, established service mapping will also provide a long-term network between the communities and service providers thus ensuring sustainability.
