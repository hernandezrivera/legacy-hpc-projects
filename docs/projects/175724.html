Project Summary

This emergency recovery and resilience building project for the most vulnerable food and nutrition insecure households will be an interwoven response project in nature that will be implemented in a span of 12 months. Main goal of the project is to enhance food and nutrition security and resilience among the most vulnerable food insecure households which promotes sustainability and fosters peaceful co-existence in multiple counties in Unity, Upper Nile and NBeG States respectively reaching 10000 households (5500 female, 4500 male) this translating to 60000 individuals (33000 female, 27000 male). The beneficiaries will be targeted basing on a vulnerability matrix tool to be agreed on with the stakeholders within the project locations.
To achieve this, the project will support the immediate nutritional needs of vulnerable households through the provision of cash/vouchers transfers. Using cash transfers, HHs will access suﬃcient food to meet daily caloric and nutritional requirements. This will address the immediate impacts of food insecurity, reducing the incidence of malnutrition and the related mortality and morbidity. Transfers will be both unconditional and conditional for constructing communal assets that have the potential to enhance longer term food security and livelihoods. In addition to meeting basic and immediate needs, short term cash transfers will ensure HHs have the energy and time to participate in activities that enhance their ability to deal with disruption and change. By improving the availability and accessibility of these productive assets, the project will strengthen long-term food security by improving agricultural productivity.
To enhance and sustain emergency food production, main season agriculture seeds & farm tool, ﬁshing gears and fast maturing vegetable & crop kits shall be distributed. This project will also contribute to the reduction of pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication.
Further, SPEDP throughout this project lifetime shall ensure Covid-19 SOPs and guidelines are observed and adhered to in order to prevent its spread and transmission.
Adaptive and adsorptive on-farm trainings and extension support shall be part and parcel of this project where demonstration plots will be established at nutrition centers where beneficiaries will be taught how to prepare nutritious food in a hygienic manner; SPEDP will also use a community based participatory approach where beneficiary identification and registration process will involve community members.
Project management committees will be established and will take an active role in the beneficiary selection, monitoring implementation and also handling complaints and feedback mechanisms and monitoring of the project. Stakeholders will be invited during the launch and all project details will be shared to foster accountability to the affected populations.
SPEDP will strive to integrate with other projects like health, WASH and nutrition through passive referrals and also establishment of demonstration plots at nutrition sites. Foristance, SPEDP is running a 5 year Right to Grow project in Panyikang,Panjiar and Manyenditand therefore will link this project beneficiaries to this project for support. Through skills development and provision of livelihood inputs, beneficiaries will be in a apposition to become self-reliant and develop resilience against shocks.
SPEDPs added value is that the organization has an already established presence in NBeG since 2012 and in Upper Nile and Unity since 2016 to date has implemented a number of projects since then in different thematic areas. SPEDP is currently implementing in Panyikang, Panyijiar and Mayendit SUN (Scale Up Nutrition) a 5 year EU funded project and as well in Rubkhona implementing SSHF 2020 WASH project and so is familiar with the local settings and in mutual cooperation with authorities and communities which is key in humanitarian programming

1 - Needs

According to IPC SS (Oct 2020-July 2021), between October and November 2020, a typical harvest / post-harvest period in the UpperNile State, an estimated 847,000 people, (58.0% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 612,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 235,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, Panyikang was amongst the five counties that were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, whereas the rest were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. In Panyikang County, food insecurity was driven by reduced incomes, high food prices, lack of markets and low livestock holdings that affected access to milk. Between December 2020 to March 2021, the food security situation in Upper Nile State will not change much and an estimated 224,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During the lean season period between April and July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 1,034,000 people (70.8% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 688,000 people will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 346,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020 (with limited recovery from the previous floods), and the much lower than required humanitarian food assistance in the face of increased needs, will likely result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, especially during the lean season In Unity State, between December 2020 and March 2021, 140,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in Unity State. During this period, all counties will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, except for Panyijiar County that will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During the lean season period of April to July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 205,000 are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Rubkona counties will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), meaning there is a need to restore their food security situation through timely intervention. In NBeG, between December 2020 to March 2021, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with an estimated 179,000 people likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity with Aweil South being classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During the lean season period of April to July 2021, an estimated 201,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 7,000 people are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Aweil South County. During the lean season, all counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, except for Aweil Center which is classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. The key drivers of food insecurity are low crop production caused by prolonged dry spells, crop pests and diseases, and shortage of seeds and agricultural tools, high reliance on markets at a time when cereal prices are at their seasonal highest and most households have depleted their harvest stocks, as well as reduced volumes of fish; the COVID-19 restrictions and insecurity along the border mostly for Aweil that are likely to disrupt the trade supply routes and lead to high food prices; reduced access to livestock products as animals migrate in search of water and pasture; the economic crisis and the resultant erosion of household purchasing power.

2 - Project activities

This project proposes a woven approach covering a wide range of activities geared towards meeting the food security and dietary diversity of most vulnerable households in these selected counties. The following are activities to be undertaken and strategy;Unconditional and conditional cash & voucher based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives - Main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits distribution - Fishing kits/ rapid response kits (fishing kits/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) distribution - Disease and pest surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication - Conditional cash transfers (social skills and safety nets)/ Conditional cash transfers (investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees - Support value chain addition e.g. through milk and fish preservation and marketing - Training and capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills, To implement the above activities, SPEDP will employ a range of strategies including the following; Carrying out Rapid Market survey and risk analysis, this will help in determining local level capacities of commercial sector to source and provide food to the beneficiaries. • Sensitization and selection of traders/vendors, given the location context, this will be done through less formal mobilization of vendors. Vendors sign an agreement acknowledging full understanding of the voucher process—no guarantee of sales, vendors ensures security of their own merchandise, respect for beneficiaries as a humanitarian intervention. • Set up of Community Committees for beneficiary selection, a Community based approach will be used since its effective both in terms of providing a mechanism for implementing a relief operation in a relatively short period of time, and in terms of meeting the principles of transparency, accountability and fairness. This will have a maximum of 12 members • Selection, registration and verification of beneficiaries, this will be done by the enumerators with recommendation from the community committees. Registration lists will be finalized using vulnerability and targeting criteria. • Sensitization of beneficiaries and vendors on voucher redemption process, for beneficiaries this is primarily to underline key aspects of the approach such as families being represented by the female adults, the use of the coupons • Community participatory identification of productive assets for rehabilitation • There will also be training of beneficiaries on basic agronomics through demonstration plots and modern methods of fish preservation. Lead Farmers will be identified and trained, besides, 25% of the beneficiaries will also be trained. - Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring exercises and post-harvest surveys will be conducted to ascertain the impact of the project at household and Community level and broader economic level

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

SPEDP noted that in these proposed counties, there is at least a partially functioning market and project staffs jointly with World Food Programme (WFP) and the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) shall monitor markets and identify opportunities for a market-based response. SPEDP is also aware that even in the deep bush people come together at established or impromptu marketplaces provided there is demand. In all these proposed counties, most households buy more than 60% of the food they eat at market which are also significant places where they can come together peacefully although some traders in markets generally have constraints to increasing inventory due to lack of financial capital, compounded by limited access to credit; customers’ low purchasing power; price variability dependent on seasonality; and external shocks, such as flooding and conflict, Inflation and the sharp depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound (SSP)

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Most people/beneficiaries prefer cash to in-kind food assistance because it allows for more choice; it can be saved and transported more easily during displacement; it can be used for more purposes with dignity and also helps to revamp local markets as opposed to In-kind transfers that have a lot of logistical challenges in delivering assistance, requires active involvement of the public administration, which is generally unaccountable to people and is marked with various forms of corruption and leakage, supply of substandard quality, and to the extent local governments are involved, political bias in distribution

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP staffs are aware, trained and adheres to the UN Secretary-General's Bulletin on Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13) This project staffs will strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising sessions around PSEA with women, girls, boys and men in communities of concern as well as with other stakeholders and design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities. Beneficiaries will be made aware of their rights and entitlements through community meetings, focus group meetings and information sheets distribution to beneficiaries.In particular, SPEDP will identify and support feedback channels that communities prefer while ensuring that feedback mechanisms are sensitive to age, gender and diversity (AGD) and accessible to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities and others at high risk. Besides, SPEDP will also coordinate complaint collection and responses with other partners within these proposed project locations and establish clear tasks and duties (who will do what, where, and how) since it is critical to put in place an effective interagency complaint mechanism that permits and encourages individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially, and safely.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. The PMCs will deliver a mechanism that enables the most vulnerable members of the beneficiaries and community to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of these beneficiaries in relation to the project in a manner that is inclusive, safe, non-discriminative, non-threatening and accessible to allThe CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee’s to implement, with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries).All rights or opinions of the beneficiaries/project stakeholders at all levels of the project will be fully acknowledged and recognized based on developed settings through contact mechanisms established. The Project Management Committee (PMC) will be established at both head office and field offices in order to register complaints from community and give immediate feedback to the complaints if it is a case that can be handled at field level but if not, the case will be referred to head office for action. Non-discrimination and meaningful access to assistance and services will be encouraged: all beneficiaries in the project will be accorded equal opportunity to access information on the project from start of the project implementation. Concern for the safety and dignity of children, youths, women and men for example will be central in this project. Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will entail reduction of risks in all the project activities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly on real time. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicize and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints. PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints.
