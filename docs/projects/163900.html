Project Summary

As part of Medair FLER interventions to vulnerable HHs in areas that are Hard to Reach, Newly Accessible, or with recent or sudden population movement to meet HHs in urgent need for basic shelter-related assistance, Medair intends to reach around 20,000 beneﬁciaries (excluding overlap) with this SHE/NFI intervention through:
i. Rehabilitating HH-level shelter units in damaged buildings via tailored rehabilitation to improve physical safety and security, privacy, and provide more digniﬁed living conditions to a total of up to 190 HHs, targeting the most vulnerable households according to Medair’s vulnerability criteria.It is expected that 10% of HHs are in need of disability adjustments, which will be provided under the next activity.
ii. Providing additional adaptation for elderly or disabled people, Medair will follow accessibility and universal design principles to complete adaptations for up to 62 households with members who live with mobility impairment, with disabilities, or older people wherever this is possible within the constraints of the building structure.
iii. Distributing NFI, Winterization, and Female Hygiene kits to up to 3,831 vulnerable HHs. NFI kits aim to address the emergency and ongoing shelter needs in selected areas, Medair will provide NFIs to vulnerable HHs to improve climatic protection and privacy, and provide more digniﬁed living conditions, the most vulnerable HHs or communities will be prioritized. Winterization kits are distributed to help target families cope with winter weather, which will improve resilience in the upcoming and subsequent winters. Female Hygiene Kits (FHKs) are distributed to women of reproductive age (14- 49), addressing protection and sanitation needs of women.
Medair teams have been able to conduct independent house-to-house assessments

jointly with SARC, using Medair’s own vulnerability and needs assessment criteria to identify the “most vulnerable” HHs. As Medair uses a multi-sectoral approach, these needs assessments will cover both Shelter/NFI needs, as well as WASH needs. Distribution activities for NFI/Shelter will happen in parallel with distribution activities for WASH. Details on WASH interventions can be found in Medair’s WASH submission. Interventions are customized for each HH according to speciﬁc technical needs or HH size. Medair will conduct FGDs wherever possible prior to distribution to obtain beneﬁciary feedback in order to customize interventions perbeneﬁciary needs and prioritize their involvement for the particular community.
It is assumed that there will be a 90% overlap between the NFI and winterization kits, and 100% overlap with the FHK. Therefore, these numbers have not been included in the totaldirect beneﬁciary count, and the total number of HHs receiving kits is up to 3831
While Shelter rehabilitation activities focus more on the longer term shelter needs, the distribution activities are part of Medair’s FLER. Medair does not plan to have an overlap between the beneficiaries for shelter rehabilitation and distribution activities.
The selection of project locations is based on one of the 4 primary conditions for life- saving interventions:
1) Newly Accessible (≤ 3 months) – where achieving continuity of services, or at leastminimizing the gap in basic services, is a key priority; 2) Hard-to-Reach; 3) High and acutepopulation needs, with few to no actors; 4) Recent or sudden population movements.Medair’s proposed intervention will target vulnerable households and communities inGovernment Controlled areas within Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor City, Dara and Idleb, whereMedair already has presence. Ar-Raqqa are also included as a potential area oﬁntervention (areas under GoS control), pending context development and access.

4 - Needs

Large-scale and even multiple displacements continue to be a deﬁning feature of the crisis. In May-July 2019 alone, an estimated 578,000 people were newly displaced. (HNO draft, 2020). Assessments have indicated that 3.42M people remain in need of NFI assistance requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance (HNO draft, 2020). IDPs entering HAS, Ar-Raqqa and DEZ are in high need of core and winter NFIs (NES Syria Crisis Emergency Plan, October 2019). As the political climate continues to shift, Hama; Menbij and Ain Al- Arab (ALP); and Tabqa and Ein Issa (RAQ), many will return in need of NFI, and particularly winterization assistance. Medair assessments in DEZ and Idleb show that over 94% of HH do not own a stove. In Idleb over 97% of HH had no means to insulate their houses;60% of HHs were unable to furnish two or more rooms with carpets.Additionally, over 80% of HH do not own enough mattresses and blankets for each member of their family. Medair’s WASH and Shelter needs assessments in Dar’a found many people to be living in damaged buildings with some even staying in tents. Not only were 99% of households vulnerable, but at least one in seven included a HH member with speciﬁc protection needs such as disability, chronic illness, older age, females as headsof HHs, PLW, children under ﬁve and HHs with more than three children under 12 years. Aleppo city suffered from signiﬁcant shelter and infrastructure damage as a result of the protracted offensive, forcing returnees and IDPs to live in overcrowded spaces and poor living conditions. Living in unﬁnished or damaged buildings poses privacy concerns for women and girls including a lack of safety and security. Medair's most recent assessment data in Khanaser (Aleppo) shows 99% of the households have at least three household vulnerabilities with at least one relating to a protection concern.

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair will carry out up to 190 shelter rehabs responding particularly to protection needs of the most vulnerable. Owner-occupied houses are targeted where possible to avoid issues with landlords and rental contracts after completion. Detailed technical assessments are done to deﬁne basic required rehabilitation in each location, after which interventions are designed case by case, (consulting male and female HH members separately). Rehabilitation focuses on basic house repairs, addressing protection, security, and WASH needs. For each apartment, at least two rooms, kitchen and WASH facilities will be rehabilitated, taking into account the minimum area required/individual according to Sphere standards. Tailored HH adaptations for up to 62 HHs with elderly and PLWMI will be conducted according to protection and mobility needs. 10% of the shelter rehabs will receive these tailored adjustments, leaving 43 HHs that do not overlap with the rehabs.. Technical Advisors ensure compliance to sector and international standards, and after implementation PPM and beneficiary interviews will take place to evaluate the intervention. Medair will provide NFIs, WK, and FHK assistance for up to 3,831 HHs as FLER to areas that are newly accessible or with recent or sudden population movement (e.g. Idleb, DEZ). Beneﬁciaries are identiﬁed through an assessment conducted directly by Medair where possible, or through SARC or local partners. Eligibility is based ﬁrstly on vulnerability, then need, incl.HH size. Items in the kits are selected based on sector recommendations and previous beneﬁciary feedback. Medair distributions are in coordination with SARC and local municipalities. All Kits are in accordance with Sector Standards and delivered directly to HHs. Quality assurance teams examine kits prior to distribution to verify supplier deliverables, monitoring teams verify contents with beneﬁciaries during distribution. PDMs will be undertaken at least one month after the distribution

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Internal training undertaken across all staff (male and female) to ensure awareness of protection issues and the code of conduct, as well training on reporting and clear reporting mechanisms. 2.Conﬁdentiality of sensitive beneﬁciary data is breached, risking stigmatisation. Data collected using ODK is encrypted; awareness raising among staff of data protection principles. 3. Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees, as well as within communities. Ensure distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. Clear vulnerability criteria for HH selection;independent and targeted Medair assessment or veriﬁcation by Medair of any 3rd party assessment; explanation in advance to communities; including contact info for feedback. 4. General Aid Diversion. Ensure local context is well understood and all stakeholders mapped in assessment. Establish strong links with authorities; joint planning and written agreements on intended beneﬁciaries 5. Distributed materials do not beneﬁt women or elderly within households. Clear messaging about purpose of interventions and need for protection of particularly vulnerable groups. FGDs prior to intervention will identify the most relevant items to be distributed. Providing individual FHKs to all women of reproductive age in a household ensures each woman in the household beneﬁts.6.Children at risk from accidents during building works. Explanation in advance to beneﬁciary communities; Contractual requirements for safe work sites. Medair employed site engineer to oversee contractors and suppliers day by day. Spot checks by Medair project staff. 7. Inequity of access by people with disabilities. Customised adaptations of shelters and distributions directly to households that consider physical access for the disabled and elderly.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: - No significant change to the political and security situation inside Syria and the GoS keeps control over recently-retaken governorates - Security situation remains stable enough to allow project and support functions to be set up and to continue -Market systems remain relatively stable - No excessive fluctuations affecting the value of the Syrian Pound and any inflation will be able to be offset against hard currency (USD) -Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during interventions- Continued cooperation and coordination among relevant authorities and partners.- No major deterioration in logistical conditions, the supply chain for essential supplies and equipment remains reliable, and necessary goods are approved for transport RISKS: - Medair cannot secure ministry approvals within the timeline outlined in the work plan, delaying project delivery- Significant injury to team members within Syria either due to security incidents- Central Bank regulations or restrictions which stop or markedly constrain Medair’s ability to maintain adequate cash flow- Medair staff or contractors commit fraud or do not comply sufficiently with procurement guidelines. MITIGATIONS:- Conduct clear, regular context analyses as part of security management-Continue up to date and regular security planning and monitoring, training a crisis management team for each location including close liaison with local stakeholders on security issues and a clear briefing to all staff and volunteers that safety comes first-Remain flexible in project locations if access to a particular location is temporarily unavailable or access is refused- Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders- Training all new staff on procurement guidelines, conduct systematic internal and external audits to assess adherence to all guidelines and check for any fraudulent activity
