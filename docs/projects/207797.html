Project Summary

The advent of the humanitarian crisis in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon since 2016 brought the inhabitants of both regions face-to-face with continuously degrading living conditions and aggravating humanitarian needs. Referred to as the ‘‘The Anglophone Crisis’’ it is rated as one of the most neglected humanitarian crises in 2018 and 2019 and the second in 2020 (Norwegian Refugee Council), (https://www.nrc.no/news/2018/december/conflict-in-cameroons-anglophone-regions-forces-430000-people-to-flee/). This crisis has had a significant impact on children with approximately 1.5 million in need and 1.2 million of them deprived of their right to education (HRP 2023-2025, Cameroon Education Cluster). This situation has exposed children to protection risks amidst other dangers. In the 2022/2023 academic year, 54% of the schools were closed (Gov. statistics 2022) thereby compelling humanitarian actors and education stakeholders to explore alternative and innovative approaches to provide education to children in the conflict affected and host communities. However, there has been a slight improvement in the 2023/2024 academic year, with the closure rate dropping to 36% (HRP 2023-2025, Cameroon Education Cluster) indicating that more communities are gradually accepting formal education. Despite this progress, restrictions on education imposed by Organized Armed Groups (OAGs) are still in place, limiting the operation of schools in most red zones to lay private and confessional education institutions where cost is prohibitive to the average family in an already very fragile economy. Humanitarian actors in an attempt to increase access to education are therefore caught up with emerging and increasing education needs which include; the need for vocational education for over-aged out-of- school children, provision of school kits to children, payment of school fees, support to temporal learning spaces, provision of school feeding as a retention strategy, provision of psychosocial support specialized services to children/teachers and lack of learning opportunities adapted to the educational levels of out-of-school children. This situation is exacerbated by inadequacy in the number of teachers trained on adaptative teaching methodologies that meet the unique learning needs of conflict affected children. In addition, attempts made by education actors to facilitate learning in temporal learning spaces is challenging as there is no harmonised approach or curriculum that facilitate subsequent transition of children into formal education.
Cognisance of the fact that objective 1 (access to education & quality education) of the Education HRP 2023-2025 emphasizes that learners should be taught by teachers trained in child centred pedagogy…, and objective 3 lays emphasis on access to non-formal/alternative and continuous learning, there is need for education actors to take action. Their focus should be towards a tailored and harmonized approach to teaching/learning that will facilitate the acquisition of basic literacy and numeracy skills, subsequent transition and retention of children into formal education in the conflict affected and host communities. Therefore, a holistic and harmonized approach is necessary to support teachers and children acquire fundamental skills and improve learning outcomes, especially for those who missed out on learning and find themselves in classes that correspond to their age but not their learning levels.
It is within this framework that LUKMEF is soliciting funds to provide a holistic response in Improving Access to and Continued Learning Opportunities for Conflict Affected Children in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon for a period of one year. This project indicators will be contributing significantly to the Cameroon 2023-2025 HRP as 1.2 million of the 1.5 million persons in need are out of school children.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The gradual uplift of the ban on education has encourage many communities in the NWSW to open up their doors to both non-formal and formal education. However children accessing non-formal and formal education have unique challenges adapting to the current education system and teaching/learning methods. This situation is aggravated by the fact that many teachers and education partners have not been trained on a tailored approach that will facilitate and accelerate the acquisition of foundational skills for conflict affected children. This limitation causes children to underperform and some dropping out in the course of their learning process. This evolving situation needs a paradigm shift from traditional school practices to an accelerated, SMART, unique and harmonized approach to teaching/learning that meets the psychosocial, protection, health, nutrition and educational needs of children in conflict affected and fragile context. Objectives 1, 2 and 3 of the HRP (access to education & quality education, Protection and wellbeing and Access to non-formal/alternative and continuous learning… respectively) provide great opportunities to train education practitioners in both the formal and non-formal sector on child centred pedagogy (Teaching at the Right level approach) and Psychosocial support. Such knowledge acquisition provides teachers the capacity to help children learn foundational skills taking into consideration their mental health and learning levels. Martin Luther King Jr. Memorial Foundation (LUKMEF), haven successfully piloted this holistic approach, has proven data to its meaningful potential in view of effectiveness in service delivery and long-term impact. This is justified by the fact that children who went through this process greatly improved in their literacy and numeracy skills and were able to adapt and understand the mainstream curricular when transited and integrated into formal education.The constant request by education actors for capacity building on a tailored, unique and harmonized approach to non-formal education in Temporal Learning Spaces (TLS) aligns with the HRP goal and LUKMEF’s objective of building education stakeholders’ capacity. The Teaching at The Right Level (TaRL) is a child centred approach that matches pedagogical content to pupils' educational level and can be used as a harmonized approach in TLS.In previous years, limited attempts at increasing access to inclusive quality education and school retention was exacerbated by the lack of prioritization of funding by donors and other decision makers given that education was not considered as a priority need. However, results of the MSNA (OCHA,2023) indicates that education is a priority need as it ranks second after food need thereby requiring more funding for a holistic response.In addition, the heavy presence of humanitarian activities within the NW/SW regions of Cameroon and the fact that civilians are receptive to humanitarian aid and perceive them as a major support to their survival eases out limitations associated with access for service provision. Therefore, introducing a holistic approach that targets formal and non- formal education settings, protection and nutrition in communities is possible
