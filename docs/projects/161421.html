Project Summary

The current flooding in greater Tonj Counties has displaced an estimated 11,142 people according to an assessment conducted by CHEP and CHD from October 13th – 17th, 2019. (71%) of the persons displaced are women and children (IDPs- 4,641 women, 3,269 children, 3,231 men) Host community (women, children, men) within Tonj North. CHEP proposed intervention aims to increase the well-being of vulnerable IDPs and host communities affected by floods and conflict through the provision of life-saving primary health care. This project will provide life-saving emergency integrated primary health care (PHC) and outbreak response services through static and mobile clinics, as well as through rapid response teams (RRT). The interventions planned will provide urgent emergency health services to improve access to life-saving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services as well as to emergency sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal and adolescent health care. It will support the prevention of and response to outbreaks of diseases with epidemic potential and other health emergencies. Protection components will be part of the joint project specifically for persons at heightened risk of GBV through women’s empowerment and participation. Using HRP support, three primary health care units (PHCU), two primary health-care centres (PHCC) and four mobile units will continue to provide the basic package of health and nutrition services to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and conflict-affected host communities in Tonj North serving a population 2,500(1,000 women, 400 girls, 900 men and 200 boys) beneficiaries over a period of 12 months. CHEP is to contribute to cluster objectives 1, 2 & 3.

1 - Needs

The current flooding in greater Tonj Counties has displaced an estimated 11,142 people according to an assessment conducted by CHEP and CHD from October 13th – 17th, 2019. (71%) of the persons displaced are women and children (IDPs- 4,641 women, 5,180 children, 4,321 men) Host community (women, children, men) within Tonj North. The condition of the IDPs is labeled as dire characterized by lack of health services, poor water sanitation and hygiene practices including open defecation, lack of clean drinking water, poor shelter and inadequate food which facilitate active disease transmission, increase population vulnerability and the potential for communicable disease outbreaks. The assessment team identified lack of access to health services, shortage of qualified personnel, absence of drugs and lack of referral systems as critical in the delivery of health services to the population. The health indicators reported by the ongoing assessment are suggestive of an active communicable disease transmission with Malaria as the leading morbidity at 40% followed by Respiratory Tract Infections (RTIs) at 16% and diarrheal cases at 27% of all cases, skin infection 16%. This recent challenges add to an already precarious situation in Tonj North County with only 40% of the community who have access to health facilities within a 1hr walking distance to get health services. CHEP, through this project expects to provide life-saving interventions serving a population 2,500(1,000 women, 400 girls, 900 men and 200 boys) beneficiaries in Tonj North, with emergency health services through mobile out-reach for period of 12 months.

2 - Activities

1.1 Mobile Clinic activities in areas with poor access to health, offering basic medical services and tests for STDs, vaccinations and visits for pregnant or post-gestational women.1.2 Supply to governmental PHCCs/Us of essential micronutrients, in particular Folic and iron, as well as vitamins, mosquito nets, and Sulfadoxine + Pyrimethamine (Fansidar) to pregnant women, for the prevention of diseases in new born children. 1.3 Provision of expert health personnel in 1 PHCC and 2 PHCUs and support to the health staff already present in the other 2 governmental PHCCs/Us. 1.4 Strengthening the therapies for malaria cases in the governmental PHCCs/Us 1.5 Management of a unit for prenatal and postnatal care and prevention in 1 PHCC and 1 PHCU. 1.6 Support to the County Health Department and the Ministry of Health of Tonj State, for large-scale vaccination. 1.7 Training workshops for local health workers of PHCCs/Us on: - Early diagnosis and disability referral, including epilepsy; - Management of pregnancy problems and obstetric emergencies - Correct and proper management of a Health Facility and proper rationalized use of resources 1.8 Continuous supply of drugs and rapid tests for disease screening (malaria, syphilis, etc.), essential nutrients for life (vitamins, folic acid, iron) to 5 PHCCs/Us in partnership with the Ministry of Health of Tonj State. 1.9 Screening of the diseases of the visual apparatus, STD, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis among IDPs, and referral of the most serious cases to specialized structures. 1.10 Continuous screening, among IDPs and in the host communities, of cases of disability of the developmental age 1.11 Support for PLW through the distribution of vitaminized milk1.12 Sensitization meetings of IDPs and in the host communities, on the issues of disability, nutrition, STDs and pregnancy. 1.13 Informative meetings with IDP Camps leader and with the IDP population over the health facilities run by the MoH or other NGO/Agencies. 1.14 Hygiene Promotion events for the promotion of good sanitary practices, and distribution of chlorine soap and tablets for water purification. 1.15 Nutritional support, through WFP in kind contribution, at the PHCC for children with MAM and referral of SAM cases. 1.16 Pharmacological management of cases of epilepsy and support for treatment in the PHCC

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Instances of exposure of displaced and conflict affected women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements is to be mitigated by the project team working closely with protection committees when issues of GBV or child abuse are encountered. Sexual exploitation and abuse have two dimensions, from the community or by the implementing partners. CHEP has a clear code of contact and policy on sexual exploitation/abuse, all our staffs are always oriented and provided with the policy document and any offender will be punished accordingly. CHEP will be working with protection actors on the ground to mitigate barriers to health care for SGBV survivors, mapping of existing services and referral pathways available to SGBV survivors and orienting health care workers on the needs and services of SGBV survivors. The risk of Contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazards is to be addressed through close collaboration with the protection cluster in creating awareness about safety measures to take regarding UXOs within the project areas.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CHEP approach of operation normally focuses on a person as a whole by providing psychosocial/physical, emotional, economic and social support. There is normally accountability and feedback mechanism in place to ensure that needs of the affected people are taken into consideration while developing and implementing a project. CHEP had a number of consultative meetings with the leaders and the affected people (women/men, girls/boys of different age groups) in the target county.
