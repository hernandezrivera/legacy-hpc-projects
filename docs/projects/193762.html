Project Summary

The project responds to the Shelter/NFIs Cluster’s Objective n. 2: “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”. This is linked to HRP 2022 SO.2; “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services, and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”.
The overall project objective is to ensure that 150 most vulnerable HHs (855 individuals) from Gaza, North Gaza, and middle area Governorates are supported with adequate durable shelter solutions that meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity.
The proposed response will be tailored on the HHs’ needs and will be including the provision of:
1) Rehabilitation and repair of 150 sub-standard housing units up to the minimum shelter standards, and provision of technical support to the most vulnerable households. Based on the Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2022, the Ministry of Public Works and Housing, performed a field assessment of 45,000 housing units in Gaza during the period from June to December 2019. From the total assessed housing units around 21,300 inhabited units across Gaza are classified as substandard since they do not meet the Shelter minimum standards for repairs and upgrades developed by the Shelter Cluster. They lack minimum requirements for adequate living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities and privacy, and require different degrees of rehabilitation. From the already received cases, NRC had assessed and identified 100 cases in urgent need of rehabilitation support. The case load consists of households with socio-economic vulnerability, elderly, FHHs, people living in overcrowded living conditions, marginalized groups living in flood prone areas, with high socio-economic vulnerability, living in substandard housing unit. Additionally, 50 cases were identified as referrals from the NRC protection program (case management) and the Gaza Protection Consortium and include households with severe protection issues in need of extended shelter support as a positive copying mechanism for their wellbeing.
The provided support for all cases will ensure durable shelter solutions that meet basic needs, enhance coping mechanisms, improve physical protection, mental wellbeing, and reduce Gender Based Violence risks (GBV) through safe and dignified shelters.
The required support will be provided through restricted cash transfer with active technical support and supervision, or -in exceptional cases- through a contractor approach if the beneficiaries are unable or not willing to manage the required coordination to complete the agreed works. Finally in case of a need for short term relocation of the household members until required rehabilitation works are completed NRC will be supporting into the identification of appropriate rented accommodation and provide short term cash for rent to facilitate the relocation. 2) Provision of case-by-case restricted cash assistance for essential household items. The support will be tailored according to needs and may include items such as bedding items, gas stoves, heaters with gas cylinders and fuel, emergency lighting system with batteries. Following the Do No Harm principles in case of heater provision all necessary safety measures will be applied and required items such as fire extinguisher, fire blanket awareness raising, and training will be provided.3) Provision of hygiene kits and hygiene awareness material for the targeted families throughout the project cycle. This project is designed in consultations and coordination with NRC local partner, the Shelter Cluster, GPC, Protection Cluster and the MoPWH and coordinated with active shelter partners to avoid duplication and ensure fair distribution of the limited humanitarian funds among affected communities across Gaza Strip.

1 - Needs

Due to May 2021 and August 2022 escalations and according to MOPWH currently 12,300 units of partial damage need repair. The MSNA 2022 assessment has found that 59.8% of the assessed households in Gaza Strip reported existing shelter damage, defects, or issues at their shelters, 42,7% cracks in some walls 30.3%, openings or cracks in the roof ,17,3% broken or cracked windows, 12,7% lack of or bad condition of kitchen and 9,2% lack of or bad condition of bathroom. Furthermore 21,500 existing housing units across Gaza are classified as substandard and do not meet the minimum requirements of living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities, and privacy, and require different degrees of rehabilitation. 150HHs are selected for basic shelter rehabilitation and repairs based on the needs assessment. 36% of the caseloads are female headed HHs,48% of the total caseload are living in overcrowded conditions. 16% don’t have age/gender-based separation in bedrooms leading to lack of minimum level of privacy for changing clothes, sleeping or personal hygiene, 4% have physical disabilities, 4.7% have mental disabilities, 10% have chronic illnesses and, 13% are under the age of 8. The assessed shelters did not ensure the minimum level of climate protection, safety and security, privacy and dignity, health and wellbeing. 60% of the households live in shelters with roofs made of corrugated sheets or asbestos or combined roof, while the roofs in 57% of the assessed houses are not sealed and therefore do not provide basic climate protection. 29% of the HHs do not include any room insulated against hot and cold weather; 72% lack sealed windows to protect against cold weather, 23% of the HHs do not have proper ventilation, 13% of the HHs lack essential furniture, while 59% have poor or insufficient furniture. 85% lack basic household equipment (refrigerator, washing machine, stove, gas cylinder), 45% lack kitchen assets, 39% lack bedding sets, 62% lack safe electrical connections and installations, 19% are not connected to the municipal water network and, 68% lack the minimum required water sources at the housing unit. Similarly, 34% lack the minimum of one functioning 1000L capacity water tank, and 56% lack the minimum of one functioning source of heated water. In addition, 64% are without adequate toilet, 78% do not have sufficient and safe drinking water storage, and 46% lack sufficient amount of domestic water, and 30% of households have poor hygiene conditions.

2 - Activities

To protect the vulnerable HHs living in substandard shelters, NRC will provide basic shelter rehabilitation/ adaptation support for 150 of the most vulnerable families. The proposed activities aim to provide physical and climate protection to targeted HHs, considering privacy and dignity, and reflect gender and protection concerns; It is highlighted that NRC shelter team is including Protection Mainstreaming in all its activities and is coordinating closely with GPC and NRC protection team in the identification of extremely vulnerable households with protection concerns to address their basic shelter needs and provide a holistic support. NRC’s support will aim to: 1) improve physical climate protection, safety and security, privacy, dignity and wellbeing; 2) improve access to basic needs and resilience against shocks from manmade or natural crisis; 3) improve learning environment and household economies through ability to address priority household needs and prevention of livelihood erosion; 4) improve capacities to achieve household strategies and priorities – such as strengthening positive coping mechanisms and prevention of negative coping strategies; Implemented repair and rehabilitation activities include the maintenance of un-sealed roof ,external walls and doors and ensuring that bedrooms and bathrooms have lockable doors; separation of sleeping and living spaces taking into consideration sex and age (privacy and dignity); handrails for stairs and roof to protect children and elderly; installation of LED emergency lighting to reduce risk of accidents and injuries at night; ensuring that entrance ramp for housing unit with PWDs is in place and barrier-free access to WASH facilities , ensure that appropriate toilet fixtures are in place for PWD and also that handrails, hearing and visual adds are available. It is highlighted at this point that NRC is coordinating with Organization focused on PWD to better design and address identified challedges.Finally NRC will be ensuring that kitchen and bathroom are appropriate according to WASH standards and all networks are operational. NRC will implement the mentioned activities through restricted and phased cash payments. The design and implementation of activities will include a) case-by-case needs assessment and BoQs for the proposed repair/upgrade activities, which will include discussions with the targeted HHs to prioritize the most urgent needs, and to choose most durable materials, b) signing the agreements with beneficiaries which clarifies the beneficiaries’ and NRC's rules and responsibilities, including payments schedule, conditions, and the monitoring process. The cash payments will be provided through bank transfers or bankcard system; the beneficiaries will be informed to collect the transfers through text messages. The cash transfers will be distributed on three cash instalments for each beneficiary (40%, 40%, and 20%). c) direct supervision during the actual implementation through the NRC and the local partner engineers, d) activate information and complaint/feedback mechanism to ensure active communication with targeted HHs and smooth implementation, e) accomplishment and final handover of works and post assessment. NRC in coordination with local partner will be providing case-by-case restricted cash assistance for essential household items. The support will be tailored according to needs and may include items such as bedding items, heaters with gas cylinders and fuel, emergency lighting system with batteries. Following the Do No Harm principles in case of heater provision all necessary safety measures will be applied and required items such as fire extinguisher, fire blanket awareness raising, and training will be provided. Furthermore, hygiene kits and hygiene awareness material for all the targeted families will be provided throughout the project cycle.All NRC staff and local partners staff receive an information on code of conduct, PSEA and CFM. NRC will also include binding conditions in all procurement documents and procedures with suppliers to ensure their commitment. In addition, NRC protection team will develop and disseminate PSEA awareness raising material. NRC has zero tolerance of PSEA and all new project personnel and implementing partners are mandatory to attend a series of PSEA sessions as well as refreshing sessions to existing trained personnel ahead of community engagement as well as during the implementation of the project.NRC will provide case-management services to the cases identified with protection risks/concerns through the NRC protection team.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Cash enables beneficiaries to have a choice, flexibility, independence and dignity in negotiating the services for their upgrades and encourages beneficiaries to use available resources to complement. It benefits the local economy through using local service providers and local labours, is more efficient for the programme than renting storage or hiring contractors and enables women to be involved in the decision making around the use of the cash.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Through the Complaints and Feedback mechanism, NRC is committed to strict standards of transparency and accountability across all core competencies where everyone has the right to file a complaint and receive a response. NRC’s CFM focal points in West bank and Gaza respond to all requests/complaints directly or within 14 days as per SOP. The complaints and feedback received by NRC is confidential and closely monitored to be used to improve our working methods making our programs more responsive, effective, and appropriate to the needs of the affected populations. Across core competencies, the CFM team, based on the feedback or complaint received, have the responsibility to inform program teams to respond and act to bring positive change, and continuously inform the community on the appropriate actions taken to respond to the feedback or complaint. NRC CFM contributes to organizational learning, and continuous improvement of accountability and quality of NRC’s work to be gender-sensitive, safe, and confidential, avoiding any risk, harm, or retaliation to our affected population. CFM and M&E units compile, and analyze data collected through various learning exercises and then share the learning and findings with program management. This includes findings and instances of learning from all M&E exercises, field staff observations, outcome surveys, recommendations from evaluations, and recommendations from complaints or feedback received. Program management are accountable to ensure that the learning takes place, and recommendations and findings are incorporated into projects as much as possible. Furthermore,The mechanism will process complaints according to the type: non sensitive such as complaints on service delivery or sensitive such as PSEA or breach of code of conduct.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The rehabilitation/adaptation support provided by NRC includes core items that ensure physical and climate protection, privacy and dignity, safety and security and reflect gender and protection concerns and special needs like those of people with physical disabilities and access-concerns. LED lighting system with batteries will be provided to reduce protection risks. Complementary and based on need, essential household NFIs such as water storage, household assets and equipment, bedding set, kitchen set, dignity kit, hygiene kit are provided. The shelter response will be designed together with the targeted households. Specific protection and other special needs will be identified and addressed during the intervention. Field staffs are sufficiently trained in identifying protection concerns of vulnerable households. As part of the baseline/endline assessments, target households will be asked protection-oriented questions to assess their feeling regarding safety and dignity improvement. The self-assessment of the level of safety and dignity is not only asked in relation to shelter as a product but also to shelter as a process e.g. during the event when contracts are signed, shelter responses are designed, implemented, and monitored. A confidential referral process is in place to ensure support to identified cases encountering protection risks. The strong community involvement and the feedback mechanism in the project will prevent harm in the first place and will allow project management to quickly react in case of concerns of beneficiaries throughout the project duration. The dignity of target households will be also reflected in using the cash modality (when applicable) and a self-help approach that empowers households to resume their capacity in leading the solution to their concerns rather than being considered as a mere recipient of support. NRC respects confidentiality and safeguards personal and other sensitive information and apply tight protocol when sharing beneficiary’s information or during the referral of protection cases to the related cluster.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NRC identified the most vulnerable households based on a socioeconomic household vulnerability assessment. The factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability includes socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household especially female/widow/elderly/PWD, income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets. The scoring system is designed to prioritize female/elderly/disabled headed households, heads of households with high dependency rates, large family size and overcrowded housing. During the design phase of the project, male and female households of vulnerable and affected communities especially youth and elderly as well as PWDs were consulted to reflect Shelter & NFI needs including gender, age, protection, and special needs. At the start of the project, essential project information including selection criteria and access to NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism is communicated to the target communities. NRC engages male and female beneficiaries of all ages in designing the most suitable response to each renter households’ specific gender/age/protection concern. Integrate gender and age analysis in project monitoring and reporting. NFI support considers gender/age specific needs and concerns. NRC will provide substantial technical support to beneficiaries including 1) advice on the prioritization of shelter needs raised by male and female household members 2) advice on how to best respond to the identified personal concerns of the household members including protection concerns by suggesting adequate durable shelter solutions. The supported populations will be empowered to request information, raise concerns, and provide feedback related to project activities, which will strengthen the capacity of affected populations to claim their rights.
