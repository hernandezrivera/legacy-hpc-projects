Project Summary

While there are only sporadic areas of active conflict left in the Darfur states, the entire region is still suffering the consequences of decades of neglect and war. The prolonged conflict resulted in a massive movement of people and the destruction of sources of livelihoods. The deterioration of natural resources exacerbated the communities' inability to rebuild. North Darfur is even more adversely affected by this conflict because it is already a drought-prone zone. The conflict has had drastic impact on the entire socio-economic life of the affected people. One of these impacts is the massive displacement of rural populations. Consequently, there has been a transformation of socio-economic patterns into subsistence. This intervention aims to build the resilience of IDPs, returnees and host communities in North Darfur through establishing capacity enhancing FSL projects and various WASH activities. It also will conduct Nutrition activities in tandem with the FSL and WASH ones.
In Central Darfur, this intervention will focus on promoting the provision of legitimate land tenure rights using VGGT.

Needs

Sahari aims to address food security, WASH and Nutrition needs through its activities through multidimensional projects. Sahari plans to address the physical and economic access, availability and utilization of food, as well as and resilience building food, agricultural and livelihood interventions.Firstly, through enhancing the self-reliance and resilience of the most vulnerable communities to cope with current and future shocks and stresses. Small scale farmers will be targeted for improved agricultural productivity and efficiency across the food value chain. Agricultural production will be improved through the provision of climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary inputs and support to services. Similarly, small scale farmers will be targeted for reduced food losses and strengthened post-harvest techniques through improved food and crop storage and processing facilities. On and off farm livelihood assistance will be diversified and managed and coordinated to respond to food chain threats including plant pests and diseases; and trans-boundary animal diseases. In addition, communities will be involved in building, restoring and maintaining productive assets, such as water points. Female headed households will also be supported with vocational training to promote income-generation, self-reliance and equitable access to resources and decision-making. Malnutrition prevalence remains high in Mellit, Um Keddada and Al Laiet, in North Darfur due to continued food insecurity, poor dietary diversity, a high disease burden among children under five, and low awareness among caregivers in effective feeding and caring practices of infants and young children. Therefore, Sahari plans on implementing an Outpatient Therapeutic Programme for severely malnourished children and inpatient Therapeutic Programme for severely malnourished children, as well as a Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) for moderately malnourished children, in addition to complete the community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) treatment package over the year to prevent increased SAM burden and reduce the mortality rate of children under five. The programme will consist of a community mobilization as well as Safe Access to Fuel & Alternative Energy (SAFE) projects, in order to encourage longer-term food security for targeted households.

Activities

Upkeep of Nutrition centers will be able to address the malnutrition load in the localities of Mellit (11 centres), Um Kedada (8 centres) and Al Laiet (5 centres). Provision of SAFE activities will encourage long term food security needs of targeted households. This will be done through cash modality, targeting 2100 vulnerable women through small stipends allocated for cash-for-training for the FES, handicraft, food processing and adult literacy programmes. In the budget allocated for the SAFE activities, there is a market survey planned; after which the transfer value will be determined. It is a one time, conditional assistance, that will be provided as the beneficiaries have complete their relevant trainings. The overall SAFE project is one year long and aims to be complete by the end of 2019. It will be conducted in Mellit, North Darfur.Training on land management and the promotion of legitimate land tenure rights using VGGT in the localities of Bindisi and Um Dukhun, Nertiti, Azum in Central Darfur. PHL training to reduce the losses that occur during storage of crops for 800 beneficiaries in Mellit, North Darfur.Provision of improved seeds, tools and trainings to boost productivity and crop value.Construction of 200 latrines as well as hygiene promotion, awareness raising and training.Drilling of 3 wells to increase the accessibility of the target communities to natural resources and water sources. All activities are aimed at IDPs and host communities.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The planned FSL, WASH and SAFE activities focus primarily on environmentally friendly alternatives to energy and construction, such and soil stabilized blocks (SSB) to reduce the use wood for fuel and construction.Other projects include the reforestation of community forests and reseeding of rangelands, as well as the construction and rehabilitation of water sources to improve the access of beneficiaries to natural resources. Some projects include digging latrines using cement slabs as opposed to wood, which contributes to the WASH goal of creating ODF areas, which is both hygienic and environmentally friendly. With regards to the digging of wells, their locations are strategically chosen to avoid conflict among water users taking in account the needs of women and the marginalized elements of the community. Additionally, these water sources are operated using solar pumps and will all have water resource management committees to ensure the operation and maintenance of the wells. A minimum of 30% of these committees will be women; as per Sahari's mandate. Another built in element of sustainability, will be the technical trainings coupled with environmental messages provided to the youth on the operation of these facilities, in order to support sustainable use and maintenance of WASH facilities. Broad community level WASH cleaning and awareness raising campaigns on basic hygiene with specifically designed messages targeted at different sectors of the community, such as children and women, this helps guarantee the proper use of facilities, and reduce environmental risks due to standing water in connection to hand-washing and open defecation
