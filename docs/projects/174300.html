Project Summary

Tdh plans to implement a 12-month project which focusses on the provision of life-saving WASH support to vulnerable populations most in need in Mafa and Kala Balge local governorates. Tdh plans to support a total of 39,333 individuals. This project will build upon Tdh’s experience providing WASH services in the targeted areas and contribute to the overall objective of the Nigeria Humanitarian response strategy plan to Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and protection interventions to the most vulnerable, IDPs, returnees and host communities. The project will strive to increase and improve access to safe and equitable water, sanitation and hygiene thereby contribute to save lives, improve health, safety and dignity of the affected population. The action will also boost the cholera outbreak preparedness in the targeted areas. The first and second outputs of the proposed project will focus on improving the standards of water and sanitation facilities through installation of emergency water and sanitation infrastructures to new and emerging caseloads, and by ensuring the operation and maintenances of water and sanitation infrastructures (installation of water points, repair and or upgrade of water points, distribution of water treatment tabs, Household latrine and bathing rooms construction and decommissioning of sanitation infrastructures). The Third output focuses on hygiene practices improvement through community awareness raising sessions and distribution of hygiene kits to practice all or some of promoted messages.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project prioritizes the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries throughout all stages of the project cycle while being in line with the Do No Harm principle, protecting people from further deprivation and preventing them from adopting negative coping strategies exposing them to future risks. The programme include estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. The design of latrines, integrating components such as gender segregated sanitation location, safety, appropriateness and convenience of facilities will be decided in consultation with the users, particularly women, adolescent girls and any persons with disabilities, to ensure the protection of affected people. All sanitation facilities, including latrines, will be constructed near residential units to ensure the privacy and security of women and children and facilitate the access for elderly or disabled persons. Project will ensure that separate lockable, well-lit household latrines are constructed for women and children and the criteria and details of activities will be explained in the community for better understanding. Project will ensure women's participation while selecting the sites for sanitation facilities, water points and timing of hygiene promotion sessions and WASH committee meetings. When planning the hygiene kits distribution, the accessibility and safety of distribution points and times will consider the differing needs of women, men, and persons with disabilities. Distributions will be done in batches throughout the day to ensure beneficiaries do not face long waiting times or crowds at the distribution point, and to avoid conflict with other scheduled activities the day. All community volunteers will benefit from safety equipment, specifically productive gloves, and, prior to beginning the construction works, will receive an introduction and briefing on safety. In summary Tdh will implement the five minimum commitments under the global WASH cluster, throughout the project life of this action: • Consult separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities, to ensure that the project is designed to provide equitable access and reduce incidences of violence;• Ensure that girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities have access to appropriate and safe WASH services;• Ensure that girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities, have access to feedback and complaint mechanisms so that corrective actions can address their specific protection and assistance needs;• Monitor and evaluate safe and equitable access and use of WASH services in this project, including through safety audits and community feedback mechanisms;• Give priority to the participation of girls, particularly adolescents, and women in consultation processes.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

All Tdh programming is rooted in a community-based approach and accountable to the affected population (AAP). Tdh adheres to the five Inter-Standing Agency Committee (ISAC) commitments to AAP and will involve beneficiaries in all stages of this project.More particularly, the proposed project will continue to be implemented in partnership with the affected population in the targeted areas; their participation will be solicited at every stage of the project, from the design of the facilities to the evaluation, through the encouragement of feedbacks. Therefore, concerns of all affected people are factored into the programme, ensuring that the proposed activities are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. Tdh will ensure that all people are aware of their rights in this process and can participate and influence the decisions affecting their community. AAP is assured in all leadership areas highlighted by ISAC, including Country Strategy, Proposals, Monitoring & Evaluation, Recruitment, Staff Inductions, Training and Performance Management, Partnership Agreements & Reporting. During recruitment, accountability to the affected population will be presented to all candidates and, to identify staff members committed to the highest professional standards, the Nigeria mission prefers to hire local staff as much as possible, and limit the recruitment of the international staff. The Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) is rooted in Tdh’s commitment to Accountability to Affect Populations as well as the Humanitarian Code of Conduct, and international humanitarian standards, as it allows beneficiaries to provide their feedback and complaints, confidentially via a hotline, complaint box or verbally to Tdh’s staff.
