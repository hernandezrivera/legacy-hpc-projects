Project Summary

The project is designed to cover vegetable crops farmers during spring summer and fall season2023, as well as to enable them to face climate changes, especially the drought, the approach will repeat in 2023 in the same groups and same inputs but with different beneficiaries. the main activities will be divided into two groups:
 G1 potato farmers: The intervention will support potatoes crop for the Autumn seasons in Zardana community – Idleb Governorate. So, 200 beneficiaries will receive potato inputs and a drip irrigation kit. As well as our technical team will provide 3 training sessions. The training will be conducted according to the implemented activities and knowledge needs of the beneficiaries at different stages of the crop growing season.
 G2 vegetable farmers: here we will support 300 farmers (2 donums per beneficiary) in Ram Hamdan and Kafrehmoul communities. 20% of them at least are women, and they will be provided with seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, cost irrigation (voucher 100$), drip irrigation kit, and agricultural tools. On the other hand, agricultural training will be provided at all stages of the project to educate the farmers with correct scientific methods.
The proposed action will contribute to the strengthening of the resilience of the targeted crisis-affected people and their host communities. Through the primary surveys and consultations made with experts in agriculture inside Syria.
Beneficiaries Selection Criteria: The beneficiary must be 90% Host Community (HC), 10% IDPs, In total Minimum 20% women, 10% People with Disabilities (PWD).
The beneficiaries will be selected according to specific criteria.
This project has not been funded yet.

3 - Needs

Syria continues to suffer from multiple and complex socioeconomic difficulties, the protracted crisis, now in its 12th year, is exacerbating food security and livelihood needs for many families across the country, further eroding the resilience and recovery prospects. The According to HNO2023, People in Need (PIN) has increased from 14.6 million in 2022 to 15.3 million in 2023, including 5.3 million IDPs, 9.9 million residents, and 52,700 returnees. According to the intersectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning and are classified as catastrophic needs in three sub-districts in two governorates.The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has also been severely impacted, with half of the national grain requirements met through imports – where once the country was producing enough to meet its own requirements and even exporting before the crisis. High cost and limited availability of agriculture inputs, the damage to the irrigation infrastructure across the country, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, the regional economic and banking crisis affecting trade in Syrian produce, direct and knock-on effects of COVID-19 on food security and the agriculture sector, the devastating wildfires and other climate-induced shocks such as floods and erratic rainfall, have all contributed to the worsening situation in Syria.

4 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities will implement as following: conducting advocacy and community mobilization for project in target areas, Hiring project staff, Open beneficiary application process through public advertisements in target communities, Beneficiary registration, verification and selection, through KOBO application. The project will include: G1 potato farmers: 200 beneficiaries will receive potatoes seeds(tuber), fertilizers, pesticides, irrigation costs (fuel voucher 100$), drip irrigation kit and agricultural tools (spade, hoe, black plastic mulch and manual sprayer) which will be enough to cultivate 5 dunums of potatoes. As well as our technical team will provide 3 training sessions. G2 vegetable farmers: 300 farmers (2 donums per beneficiary) in Ram Hamdan and Kafrehmoul communities. 20% of them at least are women, where they will be provided with seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, cost irrigation (voucher 100$), drip irrigation kit and agricultural tools (spade, hoe, planting trays, black plastic mulsh and manual sprayer) for (tomato, pepper, cucumber and zucchini crops ). the MASRRAT agricultural team will supervise and follow up the crop case during the whole season. On the other hand, agricultural training. The project will not include using of vouchers.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks were analyzed throughout the project life-cycle. According to the project activities, the following protection risks and mitigating measures are presented: 1): Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Mitigating measures: - Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. - Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. 2) Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low-quality Mitigating measures: - Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context -. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision. - Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. - Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. - Child Protection messaging to non-Protection actors 3) Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, and favoritism.
