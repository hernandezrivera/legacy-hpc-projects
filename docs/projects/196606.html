Project Summary

This project aims to provide protection services, that is targeted to children and women, to people who are vulnerable to danger in different category; kids of poor families, out of care kids, orphans, juvenile kids, survivals of violence, labor kids, survivals of armed conflicts. As it targets women who are survival of violence or vulnerable to sexual abuse or body abuse. The project also aims to provide services of protection from mine and explosive things.This project will provide positive parenting services to 800 adults, socio psychological support to 1000 kids, life skills to 450 young, educational services to 400 kids, and case management to 450 kids and 75 women.
Moreover, 20 youth initiatives will be performed. These initiatives serve protection issues with the participation of the local community, and make awareness campaign on protection issues and the dangers of mines and explosives to 1,000 people (in 2022, and the same number will be targeted for each service mentioned in 2023).
Rural Damascus will be the target in this project; Kafar Batna township (the city of Kafar Batna, Ein Tarma, Saqba, Hamoria, Gisreen, Aftrees, Beit Sawa, Al Ahaary, Almohammadia) Daraya, and Qudsaya through the centers in each one such as: out of care kids center that provides care to kids from the age of 5 to the 17th, and Al Ghazali reformatory which provides protection to juvenile kids from the age of 10th to the 15th.
CRS, the Child Right Society, has had a good experience in the fields of protection since 2006. It belongs to the protection sector. It is also an affective member and attends the regular meetings that are held in the UNICEF .It worked with different categories of children(survivals of disarmament and crisis kids, out of care kids, juvenile, children of separate parents, early childhood kids). CRS has also a community center provides protection services in Eastern Ghouta, and supervises the centers for juveniles, out of care children, and Al-Ghazali reformatory in Qudsaya. It participated in health and awareness campaigns throughout the Damascus countryside, including the city of Daraya.
These centers will be used to serve the objects of this project, and the protection programs, that the CRS is working on, will be invested in coordination with the protection sector in Syria. The most needed category will be targeted for protection services.

3 - Needs

According to the preliminary estimates, 14.2 million people are in need of protectionhumanitarian assistance, including over 4 million PLWD and over 6.2 children. Thedeterioration of the economy is forcing families to adopt negative and harmful copingmechanisms. Children continue to be exposed to a variety of risks and forms of violenceand their prevalence is reported at increasing levels in all governorates. Child labor wasreported as occurring in 84% of the assessed communities, family separation in 36% andpsychosocial distress was reported by 26% of the surveyed households. Girls are more atrisk of early marriage (reported in 71% of the assessed communities, against 62% in2021), harassment, sexual abuse and movement restrictions; boys are more exposed toheavy child labor, negligence and harmful discipline methods, recruitment, detention,family separation and engagement in illegal activities. Women and girls aredisproportionately affected by GBV and despite under-reporting (due to stigma, social andtraditional norms, lack of awareness and support services),CRS and noticed many of family violence phenomena and school violence in more than 80% of families and 85% of schools in the targeted areasIt is also noticed the existence of wrong educational methods which harms the child and affects negatively on society in Syria since it generates many cases of homelessness, beggary, employment and patients with mental disorders.

4 - Implementation of activities

1. 1000 children will be selected and targeted for psychosocial support activities from twoage groups (6-8 and 9-12). Every child attends 10 psychosocial support sessions. Eachsession lasts for 60 to 75 minutes. Each session receives about 20-25 children. 2. Properparenting sessions (positive parenting) will be held for about 800 caregivers to raise theirawareness of the importance of raising children in a protective environment that optimallydevelops them up to the age of puberty. 3. 450 teenagers from 13 to 17 years old will betargeted with life skills activities. Each teen attends 10 life skills training sessionscovering 10 essential life skills. 4. 1000 person from all ages will be selected and targetedin an awareness raising activities. 5. 135 participants will be selected to participate in 9community initiatives concerned with issues of child protection, mine protection and theprevention of gender-based violence. 6. Identifying 400 children from those who faceobstacles that prevent them from following the progress of their classmates to participatein 5-10 educational therapy sessions aimed at enabling them to finish their school year atthe best level. 7. Identify 450 children of those who are identified as cases of violence;suffer from health problems, lack care or juveniles, and work to provide them withspecialized services. 8.Identifying 75 women exposed to gender-based violence andworking to provide them with specialized services.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Lack of/low quality of services and referrals expose survivors to further harm,discrimination, stigmatization. Mitigation measures include: training and supervisionof case managers; service mapping including only providers implementing ensuringsurvivor centered approach; protected and confidential information systems. 2.Resistance from the community on GBV/CP messaging and activities, resulting in lowparticipation, stigmatization or harm to target groups. Mitigation measures:community and leaders’ engagement on all phases of the project cycle; adaptingawareness and information messages to the specific context; only female staffworking in WGSS; engagement of volunteers from the target communities. Risksrelated to the location of service provision (safety of movements for beneficiary,contamination from remnants of war, proximity with sensitive buildings). Mitigationmeasures: choice of locations done in close consultation and agreement with thecommunity, conduction of safety audits. COVID 19: Not adherence to preventive andIPC related measures can increase the risk of transmission to beneficiaries. Mitigationmeasures include: monitoring of adherence to preventive measures; reduction ofbeneficiaries numbers for group activities; provision of PPE; trainings/refreshers;suspension of service provision if increase in the number of staff withconfirmed/suspected infection occurs. PSEA: please refer to the dedicated section.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA related risks include abuse and exploitation both towards staff and beneficiariesseeking services at CRS facilities, either in exchange for services/assistance or not. Atorganizational level, all acts falling under the definition of SEA are prohibited andconsidered a gross violation of CRS Code of Conduct, which all CRS employees mustsign and commit to adhere to. The Code is supported by CRS Safeguarding andWhistleblower Policy. All employees and volunteers are required to complete amandatory training on the Code of Conduct & Ethics and Safeguarding Policy, and onreporting mechanisms, with refreshers provided on annual basis. Awareness andinformation sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism arealso provided, together with information on how to safely report any incident.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Delayed/lack of approvals. Mitigation: Regular follow up with relevant authorities,advocacy for uninterrupted access. 2: Resistance and non-acceptance of CRSactivities from the community. Mitigation: Community engagement and awarenessthrough culturally and context adapted tools and communication strategies, especiallyfor new areas of intervention. 3: Delays in international bank transfers. Mitigation:Maintain more than one bank account; advocate for dedicated channels forhumanitarian transfers; info sharing with partners and donors. 4: COVID 19 limitcapacity to deliver services either because of lockdowns or infection among staff.Mitigation: waivers to allow movement of staff and mobile facilities; adherence topreventive and isolation measures; temporary suspension of services to protectbeneficiaries from transmission, remote case management services. 5: Deterioration ofthe security situation. Mitigation: monitoring and info sharing with relevantstakeholders and temporary closure of facilities if risks raise; security/surveillance atfacilities, premises
