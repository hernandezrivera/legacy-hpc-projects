Project Summary

Due to the dire impact of losing WASH services and supplies on human life, Bonyan has put providing water and sanitation facilities among the top priorities of its interventions in crisis. In corresponding with the strategic objectives of both the HRP and WASH Cluster, Bonyan has designed its project guided by an analysis of the severity of needs in accordance to assessment findings and key indicators. Therefore, Bonyan 2019 WASH program aims to restore or keep Water and Sanitation infrastructure at the minimum level of operation and emergency WASH interventions such as provision of water, and sanitation.). The program consisted of three main components targeting ~100,000 people (48,250 male / 51,750 female, 79,450 are IDPs – 50% are children under 18 years old
*** Support to water systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria targeting 20,000 individuals (9,650Males /10,350 Females)
Large IDPs influxes in NW Syria combined with host communities. Increased the pressure on excited damaged water systems, this component will rehabilitate 10 drinking water wells (maintenance of water pipelines and pumps; or replacing broken parts.) to ensure sustained improvement in access to water Bonyan will operate the wells for 90 days, during this period a tariff-based system management will be Developed in cooperation with relative LC.
*** Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behaviour and practices targeting 80,000 of most vulnerable people (38,600Males/41,400 Females)
In crisis, people follow survival instinct which mean give high priority to avoid tangible risks with less care to intangible risk such as dangerous impact of incorrect hygiene practices. Six Hygiene promotion campaigns will be conducted to promote the basic hygiene practices along with distributing of essential WASH NFIs on 54,000 of most vulnerable people (26,055Males/27,945 Females). The kits will be customized to ensure the need of all gender and age segments (including tooth paste and tooth brush for children, and sanitary pads, Feminine disinfectant ..etc)
With no credible solution looms in the horizon for the conflict in Syria, Bonyan have set up an emergency plan for lifesaving intervention to provide water and sanitation facilities to potentially new displaced people through
* Provision of one elevated cement water tank with capacity of 150 cubic meters to improve access to clean water for 20,000 people near overcrowded and HTR IDPs settlements
* Provision of small water tanks (2 cubic meters) and needed latrine blocks including toilets and baths, as well as, water trafficking for 6,000 people throughout 90 days.
During implementation of project’s activities, Bonyan will ensure that the accountability will take its place by applying a mechanism to monitor and evaluate activities; also, to receive feedback from beneficiaries and stakeholders.

Needs

Based on HNO 2019 - WASH assessments there are an estimated 5,142,178 people who require WASH assistance in Aleppo and Idleb governorate (people who are at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement), 2,115,728 of whom are considered in acute need (displaced people in camps, informal settlements and host communities).Idleb Governorate and northern Aleppo, as well as, surrounding NSAG controlled-areas have become extremely crowded amidst the significant displacement to and within those areas. Increased pressure on already overstretched WASH services has resulted in increased dependency on alternative and often unsafe water sources that significantly increase the risk of waterborne diseases. Access to sufficient quantities of safe water remains limited, with increased water quality assurances and support to water systems rehabilitation, operation and maintenance needed to ensure the population has continuous access to safe water. Up to 55 per cent of surveyed households are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. Average water consumption during 2018 amounted to an estimated 71 liters per capita per day. However, communities purchasing water exclusively from commercial water trucks received on average only 57 liters per capita per day, with at least 10 per cent of them receiving less than 30 liters per capita per day. Areas well served by free water from networks use far more than the average of 71 liters per day. Families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 10 per cent of their income on water, with 3 per cent of the population spending more than 20 per cent of their income to purchase water. The situation is even more dire for people living in informal settlements, where families can spend more than 50 per cent of their incomes on water. Reduced incomes and purchasing power of families in Syria could lead to adoption of harmful coping strategies, with potentially significant implications on health and hygiene behavior/practices. Regarding sanitation, at least 70 per cent of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are either not functional or only partially functional leading to significant community-level health risks. Dysfunctionality of existing wastewater treatment plants and sewage networks contributing to the discharge of raw sewage, will continue to have a significant impact on water sources, soil and the overall water resource management. Amongst people in needs there are groups with specific WASH needs including children under 2 years (7 per cent), women of a reproductive age (33 per cent) and people with disabilities living in camps. Particular groups also face specific WASH challenges. IDP and returnee female-headed households have more difficulties to afford a majority of hygiene items in comparison with female-headed households in host communities. For instance, affordability of baby diapers for female-headed households is an issue for 17 per cent of returnees and 8 per cent of IDPs, compared to less than 1 per cent of host communities. The major barrier to accessing hygiene items is therefore the severely reduced purchasing power of families Assessment results in some of the IDP sites in northern Syria confirmed that protection issues including the lack of door locks and adequate lighting, lack of gender segregated facilities, long distances to the facilities as well as the lack of privacy and the fear of harassment on the way to WASH facilities are considerable concerns for women and girls. Facilities adapted for children and for persons with disabilities are often lacking, therefore those groups are most likely to be affected by lack of safety. In addition, IDP and returnee female-headed households are more vulnerable in terms of access/affordability of WASH items, including sanitary napkins and other hygiene items, and services.

Implementation of activities

Bonyan will coordinate with Cluster, LCs, and active actors in the targeted communities to initiate WASH program activities, Bonyan will conduct RNA to update IDP, and host community data and needs. Beneficiaries will be selected and provided with the services. If the intervention is targeting limited number of people Bonyan will announce the selection criteria through several ways to ensure availability of distribution information for all age and gender groups. And share final beneficiary list publicly. MEAL team will follow up the Program and conduct PDM. CLWASH/CA1. Repair and rehabilitation of water systems targeting 20,000 individuals (9,650Males /10,350 Females)Bonyan will be responsible for: • Assessing water needs in targeted communities to improve access to water• Preparing BoQ of materials of repairing and rehabilitation of water system such as; rehabilitation of water pipes and wells; or maintaining water pump station or electricity generator. • Delivering materials then implementing repairing and rehabilitation of 10 drinking water wells• Operate the rehabilitated well for 90 days then hand out the well to LCs• Build LC capacity on tariff-based system management. CLWASH/CA4. Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotionConducting 6 Hygiene promotions with the distribution of WASH NFI targeting 54,000 of most vulnerable people (26,055Males/27,945 Females).• Assessing WASH NFI needs in the targeted community • designing and publishing posters and leaflets of hygiene promotion key messages based on assessment of problems with focus on women and children.• mobilizing targeted communities to mainstream healthy hygiene behaviors and practices • Procurement of WASH NFI kits considering needs of children, elderly and women in reproductive age• Distributing the kits CLWASH/CA5. Improved access to humanitarian lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services Targeting 6,000 individual who are newly IDPs and settled in informal locations (1920 Boys, 1980 Girls, 1125 Women, 975 Men) Bonyan will be responsible for: • Planning for lifesaving and emergency response to improve access to clean water.• Distributing of 30 water tanks (2 cubic meter) as a lifesaving response targeting 6,000 people in accessible points for women and girls, considering; SPHERE standard for the distance between tanks and shelters (less than 50 meters).• Water trafficking of 10,800 cubic meters of water by throughout 90 days. considering SPHERE standard (15 liters/person/day)• Installing 30 prefabricated double latrines (one latrine for men/ the other for women). Each latrine will be; prepared with four toilets, two bathrooms, along with all the required accessories Locations of latrine will be selected to be accessible for women, girls and people with disabilities. In emergency, Bonyan will conduct rapid assessment to identify sites which are in critical need for sanitation facilities for 6,000 people. As a response, Bonyan will prepare BOQs of materials and labor to build up 30 Establishing one cement elevated water tank (150 cubic meter) as an emergency response for water needs of extremely crowded population in NW Syria targeting 20,000 people (9,650Males/10,350 Females) near overcrowded and HTR IDPs settlements o Prepare engineering and construction layouts by technical expertise o Setting up BoQs of materials and CfW workers o Implementing and supervising the building processes.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk and Assumptions: • Understanding that Syria remains a war prone zone, Bonyan is not able to mitigate risks around shelling, airstrikes, etc. Bonyan will advise appropriately to protect its staff and ensure assistance to people in acute humanitarian needs.• Temporary closure of border for transshipments at Turkey or within Syrian, may disrupt humanitarian response. There may be diversion of supplies especially in locations under the control of armed groups. Transport and handling of supplies may lead to damage of items.• In case there is a military operation and a resultant escalation of conflict, there may be large displacement of people needing WASH assistance.• Risk of air-to-ground and ground-to-ground bombardment, or other forms of attacks may affect response. • Changes in the areas of control between: Turkey or Government of Syria or opposition groups.• There are incidences, beneficiaries have sold some items from hygiene kits to secure cash.Mitigation Measures:• Bonyan will be carrying out continuous security assessment. Bonyan continues to remain alert over key information sources for the security situation.• Bonyan will train field staff to improve their security management on daily basis and improve their knowledge to assist people in risk.• Bonyan will prepare plans for hibernation, restriction of movements or suspension of activities in short term. • Bonyan will be directed on supplies handling and replacement in case of damage. Damages and diversions of supplies will be monitored through Bonyan at warehouses and distribution points.• Bonyan will ensure relevant local authorities are aware of response and commit to ensuring basic safety/access. • Bonyan will provide a monitoring plans that consider specific operational context. • Bonyan will closely monitor utilization of supplies and keep a track of what is being sold out in local market. An analysis will be carried out that will advise future procurement and/or selection of beneficiaries.
