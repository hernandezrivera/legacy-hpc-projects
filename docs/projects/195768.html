Project Summary

Meet the lifesaving food requirements of people in South Sudan who are facing food security crises, including those living in (ex)-POCs and IDP and those otherwise affected by conflict, natural disaster and/or seasonal food insecurity. WFP will provide general food distributions (GFD) to the most food insecure people in South Sudan. At the field level, food assistance will be overseen by the WFP Field Offices and implemented by NGO partners or provided through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). WFP provides food assistance and common services through a comprehensive and collaborative humanitarian intervention by deploying mobile teams, supported by cooperating partners to reach populations facing the most urgent needs in the most inaccessible areas of the South Sudan, subject to the availability of resources.
Complementary to food distributions, WFP will seek to offer value-added solutions (light resilience- building activities, peace/conflict resolution training also called GFD+), in response to needs and priorities identified by the community. Through GFD+, WFP together with its cooperating partners and other stakeholders will provide unconditional opportunities, identified through a community-based, people-centric participatory approach to targeted beneficiaries. GFD+ adds value to emergency food assistance with a focus on solutions that address the root causes of food and nutrition insecurity and better equip beneficiaries to fight hunger. The GFD+ activity type, duration, frequency, and participation will be catered to specific local sites and may range from COVID-19 awareness, training, or sensitisation on peaceful resolution of conflicts, mitigation measures to nutrition education, dyke rehabilitation and communal asset management, income-generating activities which lead to women's economic empowerment and have positive impact on household food security, or other solutions. WFP will also implement resilience building activities including Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) to support communities to improve their food security, restore their livelihoods, strengthen their resilience to shocks and build self-reliance, while contributing to peace.
Mitigation measures to reduce and prevent the potential the spread of COVID-19, including new variants, and other potential viral outbreaks have been incorporated into standard operating procedures for all interactions with beneficiaries, including training of partners and stakeholders, increasing general awareness among partners and beneficiaries, enforcing social distancing, promoting handwashing, disinfecting sites, reducing the frequency of distribution cycles, and mandating the utilization of personal protective equipment (PPE). These measures are mainstreamed into all WFP activities.

1 - Needs

The dire situation in South Sudan is driven by unresolved conflicts at national and subnational level, recurrent natural disasters, compounded by climate change and poor disaster management capacity. In 2022, the protracted crisis in South Sudan was exacerbated by increased subnational conflict, a fourth consecutive year of unprecedented flooding, drought in Kapoeta and an economic crisis. In 2023, 7.76 million people (67% of the population) will need some form of humanitarian assistance, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase classification, with 7.72 million in need of food assistance and 43,000 projected to be facing 'Catastrophe Likely' conditions. By November, an estimated 781,720 people were affected by severe flooding particularly in Unity State, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap. Violence continues to be one of the main drivers of severe food insecurity in South Sudan. Despite the signature of the Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in September 2018 which ended the unrestricted war, the political situation and conflict dynamics are fragile. Early 2022 already saw a spike in subnational conflict that has resulted in displacement, for example, from Agok to Abyei and Twic requiring rapid onset emergency response. August - October saw a significant upsurge in violence and conflict along the Tonga-Canal river strip which led to widespread displacement in the wider upper Nile area. In South Sudan, protection issues are particularly significant. Vulnerable people, especially older persons, women, boys and girls and persons with disability are especially at risk. Men and boys are at risk of forced recruitment by armed actors/street gangs, targeted detention, ill-treatment, and killings with high occurrences of physical assaults, theft and robberies. Girls and women are at risk of abduction, injury and killings in addition to all forms of gender-based violence (GBV) which remains common, including sexual violence in homes and public spaces. Conflict and violence continue to contribute to a rise in GBV incidents through rape, forced prostitution and other forms of sexual violence. Cultural and social perceptions lead to the social stigmatization of women and reinforce patriarchal practices within the household and community. In addition to a fragile legal framework and enforcement mechanisms, fear of social repercussions compels women into tacit approval of violations and infringement on rights.Economic shocks, high food, firewood and charcoal prices, and local currency depreciation continue to impact food availability and household access to food, thereby increasing the need for humanitarian food assistance. The South Sudanese Pound (SSP) has continued to fluctuate against the US dollar. The humanitarian situation is expected to further deteriorate in 2023 with some 8.6 million people, including refugees and internally displaced populations (in POCs and recent flood/conflict-affected) in South Sudan expected to need humanitarian and protection assistance, almost an 11 percent decrease from 2022. 8.6 million people including refugees are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the height of the lean season (April to July). Based on IPC analysis, 77 analysed counties are expected to experience extreme or severe food insecurity at the height of the lean season in 2023. It is worth noting that three states of Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity contribute 66% of total counties classified in IPC 4 (Emergency). It is critical that WFP continue providing life-saving food and cash assistance to support these vulnerable populations in meeting their basic food and nutrition needs.

2 - Project activities

As per its mandate, WFP concentrates its efforts and resources on addressing critical food and nutrition insecurity and provides nutrition-sensitive food assistance to crisis-affected populations comprising of refugees, IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities. It will provide food or CBTs to crisis affected men, women, boys and girls in counties facing high levels of food insecurity. Rations will be seasonal or year-round depending on the severity of food insecurity. This is complemented by the current WFP Country Office vision which seeks to address entrenched inequity and isolation, with the goal of “leaving no-one behind” and fostering unified, interconnected and peaceful communities. Alongside its crisis response efforts, WFP seeks to build the resilience of the most vulnerable people and facilitate self-reliance by promoting value-added solutions (light resilience promoting activities and peace training called GFD+) that gradually reduce dependence and need for protracted humanitarian interventions by transferring knowledge, skills, and tools. GFD+ operationalizes programming along the humanitarian-peace-development nexus, which aligns with national and international priorities for South Sudan and further promotes the pathway towards Sustainable Development Goal 2 to end hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture. In remote areas, WFP and partners deployed mobile teams to deliver food, nutrition and other basic services through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM).

3 - Project indicators

As of June 2022, 29 percent of the targeted households had an acceptable food consumption score (FCS) against a target of 47%. In addition, due to inaccessibility caused by insecurity/flooding and high market households received a reduced ration due to funding constraints. This is evident by the higher coping strategy of 17 against a target of less than 10 implying that households adopted more negative coping strategies to meet their household food needs. Results are generally worse compared to the same time last year (June 2021). FCS measures dietary diversity, energy, and macro and micro value of the food consumed at the household level. FCS score will be calculated according to WFP methodology and definition of thresholds. WFP uses PDM and survey/FSNMS for the source and method of data collection.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The needs assessment process includes activities to analyze household food and nutrition security as per WFP standard as well as an in-depth Market Functionality Index assessment. Through this process, basic information on geographic and household targeting, estimation of beneficiary numbers and their profile is captured as well as a market analysis that looks at 9 key market components in each area where WFP does cash or voucher operations. The needs assessments also contain a market assessment component, which evaluates the functioning and trends of food and labour markets, including their regional integration, supply chains, capacity to respond to an increase in demand, the availability and quality of the food accessible at reasonable and stable prices, and the opportunities for beneficiaries to access them safely. During 2022, 18 MFIs were conducted in various locations to support WFP’s decision to intervene in any given context to address identified needs and determine the suitability of a market-based response option. More than half of the MFIs recommended direct cash distributions to 333,000 beneficiaries out of the planned 783,500 beneficiaries. The remainder received business support (B2B), B2B/cash distributions, hybrid cash and in-kind assistance or remained with in-kind entitlements. All the above, in complementarity with the sectoral assessments (cooperating partners and financial capacities, retailers logistics assessments, security risk, ICT, among others) provide the necessary inputs to determine which is the best modality of assistance for each particular context while identifying related operational risks and associated mitigating measures.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Since 2008 WFP declared an institutional shift from a model of food aid to one of food assistance to improve nutrition and reduce hunger in low and middle income countries. Moreover, WFP strongly supports the Grand Bargain and will continue to play a strategic and constructive role in the implementation of the commitments, in particular through the co-leadership of the cash workstream. Therefore, for WFP, cash transfers are a cost-efficient and effective way of accelerating progress towards the Sustainable Development Goal of Zero Hunger by 2030. Cash transfers are effective where food is available in the markets but beyond people’s financial reach, whereas physical food deliveries help respond better in emergency situations where basic market infrastructure is no longer functioning or cannot be revitalized using proactive market support tools. For that purpose, WFP conducts in-depth analyses to determine the most effective way, given the specificity of the situation, to achieve its humanitarian objectives. That includes assessments of local market conditions, financial service providers, nutrition requirements, beneficiaries preference, among others. WFP takes an active approach to investing in and improving markets and has a range of tools that it can adopt to help strengthen markets and increase the outreach of cash and voucher programming. The situation is closely and continuously monitored locally to be able to switch modalities if required. Cash transfers enable the people in need, especially women, to decide what is best for themselves, thus empowering them to make the key decisions in their life. They have also proven to be effective in building and strengthening markets and increase competition, availability and price stability within markets. If deployed in the right context, they can improve access to basic goods, contribute to a more diversified diet, improve nutrition as well as reduce negative coping strategies such as selling production assets or taking children out of school to cover essential needs. Furthermore, cash transfers are known to have a multiplier effect on the local economy. By enabling people to purchase essential goods locally, they can help strengthen local markets, encourage small-holder farmers to be more productive and build national capacities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Together with partners, WFP will strengthen communication with most vulnerable members through the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) which includes the help desk and Project Management Committees (PMCs), community consultation meetings, and community-outreach activities. WFP will also ensure that the most vulnerable and marginalized women, men, boys and girls participate and have a voice in the selection and implementation of the programme. In some locations, WFP has created "safe spaces" for women and their young children to wait during distributions. This creates some opportunity for messaging (nutrition, gender and protection and other feedback).

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

WFP focuses on strengthening two-way beneficiary feedback and engagement in South Sudan. Specifically, it comprises of a formal complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) comprised of community outreach, complaint and feedback helpdesks and a helpline. In addition, there are informal channels in which WFP received and shares feedback: face to face complaints where beneficiaries can raise their concerns to WFP and cooperating partners and through the Project Management Committees which are trained to receive, respond and relay feedback to WFP. The implementation of a customer relationship management software enables secure management and analysis of protected data gathered through the feedback systems. WFP has been improving the consistency and quality of information shared with beneficiaries on the targeting process, their entitlements, assistance and on the available complaints and feedback channels. As the primary recipients of WFP’s assistance, women are the main users of the CFM.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WFP will continue with direct engagements with communities, staff and partners, including Government partners. PSEA will be integrated into existing community consultation approaches as a trust building and prevention measure. This includes the sharing of PSEA principles and codes of conduct (including what will be done in response to any complaint) in accessible formats (simplified texts, picture messages, audio recordings, graphics or videos) and languages, and dissemination through multiple channels. WFP will disseminate this information directly as well as with and through its cooperating partners and using the Project Management Committees which are existing at every food distribution site.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Complaints predominantly related to information on distribution dates, ration sizes and change in modalities. The feedback prompts WFP to design an action plan to guide field offices and cooperating partners to strengthen implementation of the CFM, and to put in place periodic reviews to ensure compliance. Exclusion errors in verification and registration processes are often raised, which has led WFP to strengthen its authentication process by ensuring 100biometric registration of beneficiaries and their alternate food collectors.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

WFP contributes to building the resilience of communities affected by floods by building and/or rehabilitating dykes, roads, and other community assets that protect arable land, promote crop diversification and therefore improve their household food security and livelihoods. The rehabilitation of feeder roads and flood control dykes build and strengthen community resilience and improve access to markets and other social services, whereas maintenance of trunk roads facilitate humanitarian operations while boosting commercial activities.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

WFP coordinates with other UN agencies, partners, and relevant clusters to address, refer and resolve issues reported through the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism, including GBV, AAP, and protection concerns. WFP conducts training on gender, protection, and AAP for communities, partners, and stakeholders, to increase awareness. In 2023, WFP will continue with joint gender and protection technical support to the most remote locations with its RRM missions.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

In 2023, an estimated 8.1 million people (including refugees), are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May-July) as shocks appear to be intensifying. WFP plans to prioritize the most vulnerable communities with available resources. Addressing root causes of food and nutrition insecurity, WFP seeks to address entrenched inequity and isolation, with the goal of “leaving no-one behind”. WFP will employ an agile response, aligned with the continuum of the humanitarian development-peace nexus, through a combination of unconditional resource transfers and resilience building activities to respond to the diverse needs of the population.
