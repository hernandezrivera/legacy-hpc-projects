Project Summary

AMAN (Association for Mass Advancement Network) has developed the Shelter/ NFI for JRP 2023 with objectives to Provide safe and decent living conditions to Rohingya refugees and host community (living around the camps) to reduce exposure to physical and protection-related risks (SO3, SO4, SO5) by ensuring that, Shelter/NFI activities are inclusive and responsive to community feedback, promote peaceful coexistence between refugees and host communities, and enhance resilience.
AMAN will concentrate to construct 700 New MTS for 3,360 individual Rohingya Refugee and NFI Activities among 18,840 Rohingya Refugees in camp 17 (block A, B and C) as well as Host Community 1,120 individual and 900 MTS Repiar and maintenance at Camp 7 (block C & D) camp 16 (block A) camp 17 (block B &C) by co-ordination with sector, CiC and other stake holder; Provide NFI for HC, porter support through in kind gift to 1,120 individual host community at Ukhiya and 18,840 Rohingya Refugees at camp 17 (block A, B & C) for the protection from winter including item blanket, hoodie, shawl, palazzo and skirts; New Mid Term Shelters (MTS) construction based on the size of the family with HH level Site Improvement (SI) estimated target 700 HHs including EVHHs (3,360 individual) for Rohingya Refugee in Camp 17 (block A, B and C); Repairs of 900 Mid Term Shelters previously built by AMAN, labour and porter support 900 ( 4,320 individual) HHs including EVHHs and HH level Site Improvement (SI) at camp 7 (block C and D), camp 16 (block A) and camp 17 (block B and C); New Solar and porter support among 700 EVHHS (3,360 individual) at camp 17 (block A, B & C). Winter cloths will be distributing among the refugees- involving women, older people, people with disabilities and other forms of gender diverse population. Separate data sheet will be used through questionnaire and need assessments also. To reach all of these targets, AMAN’s estimated budget is $1,135,920 for Rohingya Refugee (22,391 individuals) and Host Community (1,063 individuals) in the year 2023.
Gender responsive and peoples with disability with elderly people inclusion will be considered when and where applicable. All SNFI activities will include porter and construction labor support for extremely vulnerable households (EVHHs). EVHHs will be identified based on systematic door-to-door assessments in coordination with SM focal points in the camp. AMAN will ensure that shelters are accessible and appropriate for persons with disabilities and older persons by making modifications to individual shelters where possible following the Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheet prescribed by SNFI sector.
AMAN always emphasizes on capacity building training and its concerned staff has already attended in different training by the initiatives of Shelter/NFI, other sectors focal and stakeholders. Moreover, AMAN’s field staff will be trained in Safeguard/PSEA to ensure proper protection mainstreaming. Ensure that, shelters are accessible and appropriate for persons with disabilities and older persons. Before intervention any projects, AMAN will always collect and analyze and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with women, girls and marginalized groups, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As per report of ACAPS - NPM analysis hub, 30 August 2022- In 2021, 71% households reported shelter issues where in 2020 it was 59%, likely attributable to a large reduction in maintenance: 30% did not upgrade their houses even when they reported the need to do so. 58% of respondents reporting not receiving enough shelter material items compared to 11% in the last PDM. [Post Distribution Monitoring shelter/NFI items- September 2021 by UNHCR]; As per report of ACAPS - NPM analysis hub, 30 August 2022- Some households had to pay for transportation as they did not qualify for free porter services (ACAPS/IOM 28/04/2021); As per report of ACAPS - NPM analysis hub, 30 August 2022- In 2020, 58% of households reported issues regarding lack of light (J-MSNA Rohingya 2019 and 2020). This proportion increased to 66% in 2021, when one of the most reported needs among NFIs was solar lamps (ISCG 30/09/2019 a); 22% of respondents paid or asked for favors to porters to collect and transport their assistances from the distribution points to their shelters which is an increase compared to last year PDM where 20% of respondents were paying porters to collect and transport their assistances. [Post Distribution Monitoring shelter/NFI items- September 2021 by UNHCR]; Around 4% of respondents reported having problems with the items received during and after the distribution of which 40% made a complaint. [Post Distribution Monitoring shelter/NFI items- September 2021 by UNHCR]Based on the above information AMAN would like to construct 700 new MTS and HH level Site Improvement (SI) including solar lamp for 3,360 individual Rohingya Refugees; NFI support for 1,120 individual host community and among 18,840 individual Rohingya Refugees with porter support including EVHHs; Repairs of Mid Term Shelters 900 HHs including EVHHs at Camp 7 (block C and D), camp 16 (block A) and camp 17 (block B and C) including labour and porter support.New MTS in camp 17 will be built based on the Shelter/NFI Sector assessment to target only completely damaged shelters and if it may not reach the planned target and in this case AMAN will coordinate with the Sector to include other camp based on needs and discussion with the Focal Point organizations to reach the target.NFI likes blankets, shawl, solar lights, winter cloths will be distributed as in kind gift. Gender diverse & inclusion of people with disabilities: The normative framework that governs the lives of Rohingya Refugees unequally affects women and girls by limiting their mobility, their ability to make decisions about their lives and access lifesaving services, and deprioritizes their needs and demands compared to those of men. Women are excluded from decision-making systems in the community, and their lack of access to reliable information, consultations and feedback mechanisms limits their ability to influence both prevention and response plans.To make the project right-based strategies, inclusive and effective AMAN will conduct with woman leaders and stakeholders, make a preplan for perfect implementation with sectors, site management and also volunteers. Through the proposed project, AMAN will provide culturally and gender appropriate NFIs, including for those people with disabilities and other forms of gender diverse population. As per gender age group adolescent and women along with elderly will be provided training on GBV issues and above all social cohesion thus they will be aware of their rights and their roles in the Communities in regards of achieving Shelters and NFI objectives.

2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

AMAN will focus on the rationalization principle to adopt an integrated approach aims to improve intersectoral camp-based coordination, service delivery to refugees and host communities, and relation with the CiC, sector and other stake holder. AMAN will ensure stability in the refugee operation, and improve relations with refugees, maji, the Government, sector and other partners in the camps. AMAN has planned to implement its activities for Rohingya Refugees at camp 17 (block A, B and C) for New MTS construction and NFI Support and for HC at Palong Khali in Ukhiya in 2023; Repair and maintenance will be existing constructed shelter at camp 7 (block C and D), camp 16 (block A) and camp 17 (block B and C) aiming multi-year planning till 2025, Moreover, AMAN is an active member of PSEA network and its staff will be trained further to ensure proper protection mainstreaming following safeguarding policy and PSEA maintaining necessary coordination with Shelter/NFI sector and respective CiCs and above all, following the sector prescribed guideline and principles. This project will support for extremely vulnerable households (EVHHs) ensuring that, planned services are accessible and appropriate for persons with disabilities and older persons. Protection Mainstreaming (safety/security, meaningful access, participation and accountability to affected populations) will be adapted to all SNFI activities before intervention any projects. AMAN will collect, analyze and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with men, women, boys, girls and marginalized groups, including during assessment, design, implementation, and in the overall response monitoring.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

AMAN follows the Safeguard and PSEA policy during the implementation of project. Regarding mainstreaming protection, 4 principles will be considered in all project activities: Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm; Meaningful Access; Accountability; Participation and Empowerment. Mainstream Protection into all stages of programs, in relation to refugees, implementing partners and staff. AMAN will conduct consultations with the community to ensure shelter activities are inclusive to different needs of vulnerable households. EVHH selection will be following SNFI Sector criteria: Single female-headed household, Person with disability, Person with a chronic illness, Older persons, Child-headed household with no able-bodied male member (18-60 years old) within the household. Protection cases to be considered as vulnerable HHs. Protection Mainstreaming will be adapted to all SNFI activities before intervention any projects and in all stages of activities.

3.2 - Gender

AMAN will collect, analyze and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with women, girls and marginalized groups, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring. AMAN will apply clearly defined risk management processes and ensure that project design and implementation are aligned with, and clearly mainstream, age, gender and diversity, and to ensure that all refugee groups or host community are taken into account and that protection concerns of persons with specific needs are duly addressed with PSEA Policy. AMAN will follow the policy of gender-based violence towards children, women with disabilities and older women in the camps during the implementation of project. The activities will be performed through involvement of site management team, proper communication with CiC, sector focal and other stake holder who are related to implement the project.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

AMAN will implement the project, with or come into contact with children by following its safeguarding policies and procedures to ensure that every child, regardless of their age, disability, gender reassignment, race, religion or belief, sex, or sexual orientation, has a right to equal protection from harm. Setting up and following safeguarding and PSEA policies and procedures by smooth cooperation with site management, CiC, sector focal and other stake holder for children are safe from adults and other children who might pose a risk. This includes voluntary and community organizations, faith groups, private sector providers, as well as schools and hospitals s. AMAN Safeguarding and PSEA policy is the action that is taken to promote the welfare of children and protect them from harm. Below 18 Years children are considered minor and cannot be hired as labor and porter by AMAN.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity program will have followed by AMAN during project implementation period which involves three major steps: (1) Building a corporate culture that values diversity. (2) Changing structures, policies, and systems to support diversity. (3) Provide diversity awareness training to beneficiaries to enhance and understanding of and sensitivity to cultural and other difference by building awareness and understanding. AMAN will follow transparent, fair and equitable hiring practices, establish pay equity, provide support that helps each team member succeed and give all team members a voice. AMAN will follow true diversity—be it of gender, age, race, experience, sexual orientation, thought, nationality or culture during implementation of the project. The activities will be performed through involvement of site management team, proper communication with CiC, sector focal and other stakeholders who are related to implement the project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations is an active commitment by AMAN to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the people they seek to assist. AMAN will consider following suggestions while designing its humanitarian projects for JRP 2023 and beyond: Information, Communication, and Transparency; Participation, Engagement, and Inclusion; Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism; Learning and Adaptation. Information should be routinely provided to the community by both the organization and its partners (Supplier, Vendor) and on the organization’s vision, mission, mandate, accountability commitments, code of conduct, feedback, complaints procedure, and relevant contact details. Beneficiaries will be involved during all phases- needs assessment and program planning, program implementation, distribution and service delivery, monitoring and evaluation. The Do No Harm framework or equivalent approach will be employed to all concerned.
