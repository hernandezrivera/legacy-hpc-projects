Project Summary

IOM will continue to support refugees and host communities aiming at reaching 96,684 households living in 18 camps of IOM’s Area of Responsibility (AoR) and 11,936 host community households with Shelter, NFI and LPG interventions.
IOM will continue providing emergency shelter, distribution of tie-down kits, response to cyclone with the emergency shelter kits for cyclone response in all 18 camps directly, through pipeline partners and in coordination with other shelter partners. IOM will also aim at having dedicated personnel for 6 catchment areas where IOM is in charge to ensure the coordination to respond the needs. Support will be also provided to those at home care/quarantine with NFI package and LPG delivery.
IOM will implement TSA2 programme which includes trainings on DRR and construction techniques and distribution of shelter materials, construction of 2-storey steel frame shelters, mid-term shelters (where/when it allowed), community shelter repair and maintenance, provision of NFIs through voucher approach, household solar lights and distribution of LPG refills and stoves. LPG activities will be implemented in host communities in addition to camps to improve environmental management. IOM will ensure involvement of women, older persons, persons with disability in our activities so that they can take part in the appropriate activities.
In all Shelter/NFI and LPG activities, IOM will ensure protection mainstreaming in line with IOM’s Gender-Based Violence in Crisis Framework and its concurrent Action. Community feedback mechanisms with complaints desks, community mobilizations, regular project monitoring and post distribution monitoring will be deployed to ensure the complaints and feedback received are responded and changes are incorporated for the on-going future programming. All data collected during assessment and monitoring activities will be sex and age desegregated and particular information regarding vulnerabilities such as female and child headed households, persons with disabilities will be collected to provide additional support during the implementation.

1 - Identified Needs

The MSNA 2020 results indicate that lack of shelter assistance and financial capacity to repair shelter themselves. The monsoon season in 2020 and COVID-19 containment measures negatively affected shelter conditions which resulted in 100% increase of damages in May-July 2020 compared to the same period in 2019. According to MSNA 2020, 64% of households surveyed reported that shelter materials are priority needs and highest need among others. Many of shelter materials such as bamboos, tarpaulins and ropes require replacement, considering the limited lifespans and difficult weather conditions with monsoons and cyclones. Improvement and maintenance of existing shelters are required also to increase privacy, security and ventilation. In addition to the robust shelter intervention with TSA 2 covering IOM AoR camps, the emergency shelter response need to continue to address the lifesaving shelter needs affected by monsoon, cyclone and/or other shocks within the short timeframe, considering the vulnerable shelter conditions across camps. Need for repair and maintenance of community shelters such as mosques, madrasas, women friendly spaces and child friendly space continues to ensure that it could serve as the evacuation centre for families in case shelters got significant damages due to natural disasters. In 2021, with the approval of the Government, the construction of 2-storey steel frame shelter is proposed to allow to expand the size of shelters to the minimum Sphere standards while reducing the footprint of the shelters therefore decongesting the current environment. In the Camp 20 and 20ext, the need for new shelters is evident for the internal and external relocation in the open plots prepared with site planning and site development. Through the coordination with Government, SMSD actors, the new construction will be planned at identified locations either with 2-storey steel frame shelters and/or other type of shelters such as MTS to address the need for new shelter constructions. As per the MSNA 2020, in host community the ability to make repairs are limited due to a loss of income and less households could afford to repairs.For the lack of lights, kitchen, bedding items and LPG are identified as needs and reported through key informants that commonly families are borrowing money as a means to meet their NFI needs according to the MSNA 2020. It further described that 58% of households surveyed reported not having enough lights. Several studies have indicated that provision of LPG has positively contributed to the improved social economic status of the Rohingya and host communities. In camps, while 88% of refugee HH surveyed have reported using LPG, 12% HHs are reported still using the firewood. The need for refilling and replacing stoves continues to be evident as the data shows in the host community where the LPG supports are limited, the percentage of HH using firewood remains high with 45%.On top of existing difficulties in the camps across the Rohingya refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar, the COVID-19 outbreak further impacted the lives of vulnerable groups including girls and women. Lack of space and privacy in shelters of Rohingya refugees may lead to increasing protection-related risks, such as GBV and child abuse. Specific actions to mitigate the risk of GBV will be incorporated in all Shelter/NFI and LPG activities including female and protection staff presence in all activities, prioritization of vulnerable individuals in distributions, staff (refresher) trainings on good distribution, clarifying referral pathways when encountered with survivors. PSEA training had been conducted to all Shelter/NFI and LPG staff including field staff in 2020. The refresher training on PSEA and training for new staff, as well as awareness on PSEA with beneficiaries will continue throughout the 2021.
