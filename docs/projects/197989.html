Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to promote the participation and protection of women and girls, especially in communities of Jere and Kala Balge LGA of Borno State and Geidam and Gujiba, LGA of Yobe State. The project will initiate positive thinking and reflection among community members regarding the prevention of GBV, while engaging men is an essential part of preventing violence against women and girls (VAWG). Engaging Men through Accountability Practice (EMAP) helps men become allies in the movement to end violence against women and girls to be safe and achieve their full potential. Men can only become allies if they hold themselves accountable for their actions and the actions of other men in their communities.
Engaging Men through Accountable Practices (EMAP) group will be established in target locations and their knowledge and capacity on women’s rights increased to further enable preventing violence against women and girls/GBV to able them to provide their feedback about interventions with men in their communities. Without regular input from women and girls, our work with men can’t be fully accountable to them, Using the Engaging Men through Accountability Practice approach will build a tailored capacity for Men and Women to be accountable and recognized when they act harmful to women.
There shall be a series of gender discussion groups (GDs) that aligns with women’s rights, prevention of gender-based violence, gender inequality, abuse of power, and other protection issues such as child protection, female gentile mutilation, early marriage, and establishment of community-based protection mechanisms. The intervention will target 5,000 beneficiaries with messages on GBV prevention and responses, and 5000 beneficiaries will be provided with timely age-appropriate case management service for Gender-Base Violence survivors (Women and Girls) 5000 Beneficiary will be provided a referral case management service for Gender-Base Violence survivors (Women and Girls), IEC material shall be made available and various community structures will be empowered to sensitize and create awareness in their local languages.
Project Objectives
1. Conduct GBV Needs Assessment and GBV Safety Audits.
2. Provide timely age-appropriate case management service for those who are Gender Base Violence survivors (Women and Girls) in jere and Kala Balge LGA of Borno State and Geidam and Gujiba, LGA of Yobe State
3. Increased knowledge of 5,000 community members in the prevention of GBV through the Engaging Men through Accountability Practice approach.
4. Distribution of (5000) Dignity Kit to the Gender Base Violence survivors (Women and Girls) in Jere and Kala Balge LGA of Borno State and Geidam and Gujiba, LGA of Yobe State

1 - Needs

Violence against women and girls (VAWG) impacts individuals, communities, and societies across the globe. Recent Data indicates that 35% of women worldwide have experienced VAWG in their lifetime, either through intimate partner violence or non-partner sexual violence, according to the Global Risk Index INFORM (Risk Index, 2020), Nigeria was amongst the countries with the highest overall projected conflict risk index and increased risk in socio-economic vulnerability, inequality, and food insecurity in 2019. The thirteenth-year conflict in North-East Nigeria has contributed greatly to this and resulted in deepened humanitarian needs in the region. Violence by armed groups and military operations has left 7.9 million people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in the worst-affected states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (HNO, 2020) . The needs are particularly high in the urban areas surrounding Maiduguri, which hosts large numbers of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees as well as vulnerable host communities. The insurgency by Boko Haram has most significantly deteriorated governance, rule of law, and the civil and human rights of marginalized populations in North-Eastern Nigeria. Killings, displacement, insecurity, violation of human rights and violence against women are pervasive. The group has subjected many abducted women and girls to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), including forced marriages, rape, and sexual slavery. At the same time, a report by Human Rights Watch in October 2016 documented cases of rape and sexual exploitation, and abuse of IDP women and girls committed by government officials and other authorities. (Risk Index, 2020) Because of the worsening socio-economic conditions in the camps and communities, there has been a growing demand, for IDPs to return to their places of origin and restart their means of livelihood. Unfortunately, many IDPs have returned to find their houses and farms burnt down, public infrastructures and facilities destroyed, and the majority of their productive and financial assets lost leaving them with no sustainable means of livelihood. Also, the incessant attacks on returnees in communities have resulted in many of them returning to their various camps and host communities. Findings from UNDP surveys in the three most affected states indicate that 86% of households spend more than they earn; 30%of households are economically inactive whilst typically, one economically active member sustains 2.3 non-active members . Most households do not have a sufficient food supply , and household income insecurity is compounding protection risks and prolonging vulnerable children’s suffering. Many households are headed by women and girls who most times resort to negative coping strategies including survival sex as alternative means of livelihood.

Visits shall be carried out in all the project communities, targeting community leaders, community members, and exiting women groups. Activity 3: Construction of women and girls safe and friendly Space.Multi Aid and Charity Initiative will establish Women and girls safe space to offer integrated SRH/GBV services in the selected LGAs of Borno and Yobe State.Objective 3: Increased knowledge of 5,000 community members in the prevention of GBV through the Engaging Men through Accountability Practice (EMAP) approach Activity 1: Train Project Staff Multi Aid and Charity Initiative will train staff and volunteers.Activity 2: Formation and Facilitation of Women’s and Men’s group (single-sex group)3 women groups of 30 beneficiaries will be formed and shall participate in 8 weeks’ sessions, during these sessions staff will document concerns raised by the female participants, which will be used to adapt the men’s curriculum. Following the completion of women's sessions, the formation of 3 men's groups with 30 beneficiaries will participate in 16 weeks’ sessions.Activity 3: Weekly meeting with Facilitators and SupervisorsWeekly meetings shall be observed with Multi Aid and Charity Initiative facilitators and supervisors, these meetings shall enable them to share feedback and work through challenges that may arise with the groups and review key points raised in Women’s groups and integrate them into the Men’s curricula. Activity 4: Training of Community Volunteers on Outreach/Community Mobilization and Awareness creation. Activity 6: Commemoration of key International daysThe Multi Aid and Charity Initiative team shall commemorate key international days which include 16 days of activism.Activity 7: Development and printing of IEC materials.Activity 8 – Establishment & running of complaint and response mechanisms (CRMs)

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Establishment & running of complaint and response mechanisms (CRMs) To increase accountability and community engagement and improve response to Gender-Based and human rights incidents, the project shall establish/strengthen complaint response mechanisms in the target communities. A dedicated CRM officer will manage complaints desks weekly in each community. Experience has shown that the complaints desks, which provide beneficiaries with the opportunity to provide feedback face-to-face and to receive an immediate response, are the most popular and effective. Besides, complaint boxes will be placed in each community. All complaints and responses will be collected in a dedicated tracker, including referrals made.
