Project Summary

The intervention aims at sensitizing host communities, displaced persons and returnees on risk mitigation practices and basic services available. This will be done through the implementation of several channels of communication and exchange with the communities. The project will target the most vulnerable neighborhoods in Benghazi and Ejadabiya. The main risks and concerns in each targeted area will be identified through the conduction participatory needs assessments.

Needs

The situation of IDPs, returnees and host communities alike have been slowly but steadily degrading in the East of Libya. As of September 2018, the 31% of IDPs were identified in the Eastern region, according to Round 21 of International Organization for Migration’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM DTM), with 26,865 individuals in Benghazi and 13, 550 in Ejdabyia. Moreover, the 51% of returnees were identified in the same region (189 000 in Benghazi ).In the Eastern region currently (particularly in Benghazi and Ejababiya there are widespread needs related to the presence of unexploded ordnances (UXOs), improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and mines, collateral damage to basic services infrastructure, environmental pollution from weapon residue, human remains, and refuse, as well high rates of psychological trauma as result of the violence and conflict in combination with a weak psychosocial support infrastructure. Specific population groups (women, men, youth elders etc.) have different protection needs, for instance women are more at risk of being victims gender based violence, while youth suffer for the lack of recreational spaces. Conflict affected population lack access to information on risk-mitigating best practices and services available for Health, Security, Sanitation, Shelter and Household Water. As part of the UNHCR project implemented throughout 2018 in the Benghazi region, ACTED carried out a Participatory Need Assessment published in September 2018, covering essential services such as health, water and sanitation, security and shelter or education. This study was conducted by Libyan consultants who would have strong contextual knowledge with the support of communities including IDPs, returnees, community leaders and host communities in the Greater Benghazi region including post conflict neighborhoods. Based on the results of this research, it was possible to identify the most urgent problems faced by IDPs, returnees and host communities and to design tailored need-based awareness campaigns. Among the priority needs identified we shall highlight the problem of access to drinkable water, associated with the high contamination of households wells and some public tanks which in turn results in the spread of diseases. Similarly, there are some severe environmental pollution issues, mainly due to the absence of a proper waste management system which result in the adoption of negative coping strategies by the communities and here again in the spread of diseases and the contamination of groundwater. The damage and needs observed in the assessment represent considerable protection risks and barriers to meet basic needs for the affected populations, highlighting the need for a sensitization campaign in order to enable communities to identify the basic services to which they can have access, as well as disseminate key messages on good practices to be adopted according to the context they are facing. Activities

Throughout 2019, ACTED plans to further raise awareness on risks and protection services available for the most vulnerable and in needs of protection in the East. A needs assessment exercise will be conducted to assess the mains concerns of the local population on 5 essential themes: health, water and sanitation, security and shelter. In Benghazi and Ejadabyia where previous assessments were conducted in 2018, Key informant interviews (KIIs) will be organized to confirm that the needs identified in 2018 are still the most relevant. In addition ACTED aims at extending the needs assessment exercise to further areas in East to identify a range of potential protection risks related to health, disability, legal assistance, psychosocial assistance, gender-based violence (GBV) and access to basic services. ACTED will organize focus group discussions to better assess the needs of specific population groups, FDGs on specific topics will target a particular population group (women, man, elders, youth etc.) in order to allow a more detailed discussion on specific needs. The results of the assessments will allow to design need-based awareness raising campaigns. The identified topics of the campaigns will be agreed upon with relevant sectors and conducted at the inter-agency level. Awareness-raising activities will be organized in collaboration with local CSOs, and will take a variety of forms such as information sessions, radio broadcasts, the creation and publication of videos, resources published on the Internet and particularly on social networks, or the creation of advertising products and poster information. ACTED also wishes to explore the feasibility and work on the diffusion of a mobile application in the region. The partner CSOs will be selected on the basis pre-established criteria that will include: previous experiences, legal status, commitment, feasibility, relevance and efficiency of the intervention proposed. ACTED will also provide specific training to selected CSOs members on protection approaches, norms, and humanitarian and human rights laws.Six awareness campaigns will target neighborhoods of Benghazi, Ejdabiya, and one additional location in the East (selected on the basis on the need assessment and the possibility to access the area) with high concentrations of IDPs and IDP returnees with the objective of raising awareness of risk-mitigating best practices and available basic services (protection, GBV, child protection, mine action, health, security, household water, sanitation and shelter). This is a critical step towards ensuring host communities, IDPs and IDP returnees have access to life-saving assistance and helping IDPs take well-informed decisions regarding return to their homes.This initiative will complement, the protection monitoring and referral activities implemented by ACTED in the East of Libya. Indeed the information gathered through the protection monitoring will contribute to assess the recurrent needs. Moreover, information on the services offered by the established network of service providers that collaborates with ACTED in the region will be included in the awareness raising tools produced.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

The research component to identify needs and assess level of knowledge of risk-mitigating best practices and services available will be done directly with the population of concern via focus group discussions (including women), incorporating their views into the design of the program. Implementation of the project will be done with local CSOs who also represent the communities for which these activities will be targeted. Furthermore, project monitoring will involve home visits to a sample of beneficiaries, further enabling beneficiaries to express their feedback. An ad hoc survey will be also designed and conducted to assess the satisfaction and the improvements of beneficiaries of training sessions.Finally, through ACTED’s C CRM (complaints response mechanisms) beneficiaries will also have the opportunity to submit their complaints through hotlines, sms, Viber and email. According to ACTED procedures the complaints will be addressed with a maximum delay of 15 days. ACTED adopts a zero tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse. ACTED is committed to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse both within the organization and within the framework of ACTED programmes and ACTED beneficiary populations.In this context, the aim of ACTED’s PSAE policy is to define expressly sexual exploitation and abuse, to set up a reporting process in the case of a violation of the rules and a specific disciplinary sanction procedure based on the seriousness of the acts committed.
