Project Summary

In 2021, DRC aims to strengthen the resilience of Rohingya refugees and host community towards the effects of climatic change, environmental degradation, and natural disasters through livelihood and food security which falls under the protection services. For the upcoming year, DRC will focus on improving the livelihoods of the Rohingya in Camps 4, 5, 7 and 8E and the host communities in Palongkhali and Rajapalong Unions in Ukhiya sub-district. The proposed intervention will be carried out through knowledge and skills development on local technologies, climate change and zero waste management and use of natural resources in targeted communities especially for the vulnerable Rohingya women. DRC will focus on improving shelter structures through lattice production. Waste management systems will be improved through implementation of regenerative circular economy solutions using 4R concept (Redesign, Reuse, Reduce and Recycle). Through this intervention, livelihoods will address water scarcity to promote dry-season homestead gardening which will contribute to primary level production, income/employment generation through inclusive waste management while some of this will be cash based. The project will be implemented through the coordination with government - Camp in Charges and Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner office, Deputy Commissioner’s Office - Cox’s Bazar district, Upazila Nirbahi Officer as well as other actors implementing Shelter, Livelihood, and Site Management Support interventions. As part of its localization strategy, DRC plan to work with local partners/NGOs in 2021 and the modalities will focus on partnership development with local government and NGOs, and capacity development of local volunteers and community-based organizations and will be equipped to provide back stopping services as part of the exit and sustainability plan. It is expected that through implementing this project, more than 4,150 beneficiaries (Rohingya - 2,350, Host- 1,800) will receive services leading to an improvement in their livelihoods.

1 - Identified Needs

The crisis in Myanmar has resulted into the displacement of over 900,000 (UNHCR Population data – October 2020) Rohingya people to date further exacerbating the humanitarian situation of Cox’s Bazar in Bangladesh. Malnutrition, health status and food insecurity are at crisis levels, and the poverty rate is already well above Bangladesh’s national average. The host community and the refugee community both are largely dependent on daily labour as a main source of income while many of the settlements are in rural with makeshift camps, villages, and agricultural plots interspersed with government forest, plantations, and Ecologically Critical Areas (ECAs). The food security and nutrition situation as well as the local economy have been affected by introducing a labour surplus which has driven the labour wages down and increase in the price of basic food and non-food items. The COVID-19 impact of refugees and host communities in Ukhiya (WFP - May 2020) report shows that nearly seven out of ten families in refugee community and four out of ten families in host community had an acceptable diet and losing households income between BDT 1,000 - 3,000 in a week. The shelter structures are also fragile, and the sufferings doubled during monsoon while 54% respondent asking for NFI items (Need Assessment Working Group Bangladesh - April 2020). The water scarcity in dry season for irrigation and domestic uses is also a burning issue in Cox’s Bazar particular in Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-district (FAO irrigation and Water assessment 2018) while the climate change issue has led to the deterioration of the scenarios. In addition, waste is also a burden and reportedly increasing the waste (solid, bio and non -perishable) in the camps and the host communities. The current needs to reduce the vulnerability, generate income, develop the skill for self-reliance, innovative ideas, and environmental protection and maintain safety and security to protect the COVID-19 scenarios. Though many UN actors: WFP, UNHCR, IOM and INGOs: World Vision, Save the Children, ACF, NRC, DCA, DRC and others have been implementing numerous projects to address above mentioned crisis and improve the situation; there are still unmet needs that need to be addressed. Thus, DRC is proposing an innovative and environment-friendly labour-intensive women-led livelihood intervention (lattice project: objective 1) and environment friendly innovative intervention (livelihood promotions through zero waste management and rain water harvesting to address dry season home gardening: objective 2) through which more than 4,150 beneficiaries households (Rohingya women - 1,300, Rohingya male - 1,050; Host Female - 1,050 and host, Male -750) will be directly beneficiated. A group of skilled lattice makers/service providers will be in place through capacity building support by the implementation of objective 1. Through the implementation of objective 2, formation of Zero waste management committee (ZoWMC) will be formed to manage the waste at community level, water user groups (WUG) will be formed for manage the dry season gardening and irrigation (dam and plastic tank installation along with some scaling up of DRC’s current initiatives . A group of volunteers and community leaders, beneficiaries will be trained on livelihoods intervention and management and leadership skill by this project. These skill development training will provide them with an opportunity to be engaged in livelihoods activities and improved their food security. DRC is currently implementing livelihood interventions in the proposed locations and will aim to sustain the activities in 2021 in order to build on the gains made in 2020 as well as mitigate against additional livelihood shocks as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Employment generation through CFW (Zero waste management, by road repair and land stabilization through plantation).Activity 2: Partnership development and capacity building (financial, management and governance) of the partners.Activity 3: Livelihood awareness raising and training (Bio-char, waste management, home gardening and compost, skill development, plantation and land stabilization).Activity 4: Livelihood assessment and initiate starts up for the youths (Circular waste economy and impact assessment).Activity 5: Community-led livelihood interventions (group activity and private sector development).Activity 6: Lattice production by women through CfW in Camps.Activity 7: Cash-for-work (CfW) installation for localized technology- plastic tanks and construct earthen Dams and formation of Water User Groups (WUG).Activity 8: Formation of two Zero Waste Village Committees (ZWV Committees) and inclusive waste management through employment creation.
