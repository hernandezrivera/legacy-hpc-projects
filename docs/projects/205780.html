Project Summary

This project aims to provide integrated WASH services to the communities in Wau, Raga and Jur river counties of Western Bahr El Ghazel State, Duk and Twic East counties of Jonglei state and Baliet county of Upper Nile State through provision of adequate safe and clean water, sanitation facilities and improved hygiene practices among the vulnerable population in order to curb and reduce food insecurity associated with malnutrition and other waterborne diseases while also reducing incidences of child related morbidity and mortality attributed to water related diseases that exacerbate malnutrition caused by gaps in food consumption. The states of Western Bahr El Ghazel, Jonglei and Upper Nile have significant number of people in IPC 3, 4 & 5 according to IPC analysis while displaying significant gaps in water supply and quality, sanitation and hygiene.
Food Agriculture and Disaster Management (FADM) working in these states of Western Bahr El Ghazel, Jonglei and Upper Nile proposes to implement an integrated WASH project to address short- to-midterm WASH concerns targeting 13,333 vulnerable host communities, IDPs and returnees. The project aims at increasing access to clean and safe drinking water at the community, and nutrition facilities through rehabilitation of boreholes, construction of temporary latrines and rehabilitation of existing household and communal latrines, distribution of WASH NFIs to the most vulnerable community members including MHM Kits to vulnerable girls and women. Furthermore, FADM will increase access to good hygiene practice messages through hygiene promotion awareness at the community level and nutrition sites.

1. Needs

Limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage such as water points and latrines, poses multi-sectoral impacts. Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as evidenced by the high prevalence of malnutrition in the country. It is estimated that the standard of living and well-being of about 6.1 million people will be affected as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation in South Sudan in 2023 with Jonglei and Upper Nile sates being among the states badly affected. (South Sudan HNO 2023). This situation is likely to slide to 2024 or even worsen if the WASH needs and gaps are not addressed in time. Increasing levels of insecurity, both ethnic and politically motivated conflict, localized violence and disputes over land are the underlying drivers of WASH conditions in almost all states in South Sudan, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services and restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure. Conflict has caused families to retreat to remote areas with limited WASH infrastructure, increasing pressure on the existing WASH services in the host communities and contributing to elevated inter-community tension. Insecurity has also disrupted livelihoods, trade and supply chains, further perpetuating the economic challenges and reducing the household income for WASH goods and services. Conflict- and/or climate-driven displacement has also led to the formation of new IDP sites that require comprehensive WASH services to ensure sufficient access to water and operation of WASH facilities. The influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan and Ethiopia has put pressure on already constrained WASH resources in the counties where they are currently residing including counties in Western Bahr El Ghazel, Jonglei and Upper Nile States that are experiencing limited water supply and sanitation facilities. Most of these returnee’s population are using untreated surface water while practicing widespread open defecation. This is of serious concern as this creates a substantial public health risk, especially during rainy season. Limited access to water is also compounded by lack of WASH NFIs such as jerry can and bucket to safely store water and water treatment chemicals (PUR and Aqua tabs). In the Sudan crisis, as of 20th July 2023, 183,854 individuals from 42,103 households had arrived in South Sudan (OCHA situation Report No.5.). With the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) predicting that El Niño will cause above average levels of rainfall, there is high likelihood of flooding and flash flooding. Displacement is expected (especially for those living in flood-prone areas) as well as crop and livestock destruction and damage to infrastructure thus increasing the WASH, health, and mental health needs of individuals affected by these events. Lack of adequate WASH services in these counties have contributed to food insecurity and malnutrition, especially amongst children, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), the elderly and persons with special needs. In the IPC report between, July 2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five years have suffered from acute malnutrition based on analysis and estimations from the FSNMS and program admission trends. Approximately 345,893 will need SAM treatment while another 1,057,703 will need MAM treatment. FADM therefore proposes an integrated WASH program targeting Wau, Raga and Jur river counties of Western Bahr El Ghazel State, Duk and Twic East counties of Jonglei state and Baliet county of Upper Nile State that will rehabilitate boreholes, construct and rehabilitate and carry out hygiene promotion in order to increase access to WASH services among the targeted populations.

2. Project activities

• Borehole rehabilitation. FADM will target to rehabilitate boreholes in both nutrition centers and the community to provide increased access to clean and safe drinking water. In flood prone locations the boreholes’ rehabilitation will ensure that the boreholes are made to be flood resistant by raising their platforms based on the WASH cluster standards. FADM will also carry out water quality tests for the rehabilitated boreholes before they are commissioned. FADM will also carry out safety audits at the targeted boreholes to ensure that they are safe for both women and girls. • Training of water management committees. To ensure that the rehabilitated boreholes are properly managed, FADM will train water management committees based on the WASH cluster’s set guidelines. The committees’ formation will ensure that women are adequately represented.• Hygiene promotion. FADM will select and train Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) who will carry out hygiene promotion both at the community and at the nutrition centers. At the community FADM will carry out door to door hygiene promotions and hygiene promotions in public places. FADM will also carry out hygiene promotion at the nutrition facilities with the hygiene promoters visiting the facilities to teach caregivers on good hygiene practices. The project will also use mother to mother support groups to ensure that the good hygiene practices shared are practiced by the caregivers as the support groups will be used to support the members to ensure that the good hygiene practices are practiced by the mothers who are group members. • Rehabilitation of latrines. FADM will rehabilitate existing latrines that will require rehabilitation at the nutrition centers in the targeted locations to increase access to sanitation services. • Construction of latrines. FADM will construct temporary latrines at the nutrition centers and in the community households that lack latrines to increase access to sanitation services. • Distribution of WASH NFIs. FADM will distribute standard WASH kits to both caregivers at the nutrition facilities and the vulnerable community members in the targeted locations. The WASH NFIs will be acquired from the WASH core pipeline supply
