Project Summary

The goal of the this project is to contribute to the achievement of SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) by 2030 by ensuring crisis-affected populations, including , internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host populations, have safe access to adequate and nutritious food during and after crises in targeted communities in the NW/SW region of Cameroon.
The project will support crisis-affected in priority areas with emergency agriculture assistance and income-generating activities, aiming to improve livelihoods, strengthen self-reliance, and facilitate early recovery. This will be done through building capacity of targeted families and supporting them with CASH for startup and farm inputs. Support will focus on small ruminant, local poultry, table birds, piggery, fishery and agriculture.
This assistance will lead to improved food security, strengthened livelihoods, enhanced nutrition-related practices, and reduced acute malnutrition among vulnerable families in the NW/SW region in a sustainable way. The project is expected to reach 1000HH and 6000 beneficiaries before the end of 2025

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since 2016, the English-speaking North West (NW) and South West (SW) regions of Cameroon have experienced a spike in social instability erupting into violence displacing several hundred thousands of people and disrupting the livelihoods of millions. Since the beginning of the crisis in 2016, at least 6000 civilians have been killed by both government forces and separatist fighters. Civilians and Member of Parliament have been abducted by armed separatists who allegedly request some ransom for their liberation. (Human Right Watch). This has left a negative impact on the socio-economic tissue and has increase drastically the vulnerability and susceptibility of the population to hunger. This escalation of tension have also seriously affected the civilian population and contributed to the displacement of many people within these regions and in surrounding regions such as the Littoral and the West as well as neighboring Nigeria. The situation in the NWSW regions of Cameroon remains tensed, impeding access to health and education.Humanitarian access remains difficult, mainly due to the unpredictable security situation, poor road conditions, restrictions on movement in some villages and demonstration in ghost towns during which socio-economic activities are suspended Frequent attacks resulted in further displacement of people across the regions and beyond, while non-state armed groups (NSAGs) increased the use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs) with increasing numbers of civilian casualties. The deteriorating security situation has resulted in multiple population displacements across the NWSW. These movements are often temporary and the displaced return to their homes once the security situation allows.Approximately 581,112 people are internally displaced within or from the NW/SW, but also 459,605 returnees, and a total of 2,2 million of affected people of which 1,8 million are in need of assistance (OCHA 2024). As the crisis persists more households have been pushed into food insecurity, especially internally displaced people (IDP) and farming populations, who are impacted by the disruption of their livelihoods. The continuing COVID-19 pandemic further impacted the most vulnerable households’ food security, but also a recent Cholera outbreak in October 2021, in addition to the Ukrainian crisis which is now endangering global supply chains.
