Project Summary

The proposed project will provide integrated nutritional services (nutritional assessment, need-based counselling, diet management and referral services) through existing 11 nutrition corners in BRAC health facilities in 10 camps. As per ISCG rationalisation guidance, we expect that all facilities will be operational until the funding periods completes. After that, number of health post will be reduced to five due to the ISCG rationalisation process. Community health volunteers will be deployed in host communities (4 unions) of Ukhiya and Teknaf. At BRAC health facilities, clients from the Rohingya and host community will be screened by anthropometric measurement tools to ensure early detection of malnourished children (aged 0-59 months), pregnant and lactating women (PLW). If the nutritional status is not satisfactory, we will provide them with need-based nutritional counselling. However, in case of malnourished children under five years and PLW, the project will refer them to the dedicated integrated nutrition facility in camps and host communities, ensuring their enrollment in the programme for the treatment of malnutrition. Moreover, children in the 0-36 month age group will be provided with age-specific Early Years Stimulation services from the BRAC nutrition corners for optimum physical and mental development and improved nutritional status. Furthermore, the project will focus on enhancing the nutritional status of PLW by providing essential nutrition-related counselling at the time of their visit to the BRAC health facilities for ANC and PNC. Moreover, BRAC will provide them with Iron Folic Acid supplement. At the same time, adolescent girls will be targeted for counselling on nutritional practices and recommended dietary intake; also counselling will be provided regarding their menstrual hygiene management and to improve their nutritional status. The project will significantly contribute to improving the nutritional status of beneficiaries from targeted age and gender groups with a special focus on the most vulnerable, i.e. women and girls, children, LGBTQI and persons with disabilities. BRAC will provide them with nutrition and dietary management counselling through sessions in the BRAC health facilities. Gender-segregated and disable-friendly WASH facilities will be established in the facilities. Moreover, each facility will have a breastfeeding corner for lactating mothers. There will be a feedback and complaint system in the nutrition corner, and the issues will be considered as a priority and addressed accordingly. In addition, the project will carry out capacity-building training for the nutrition volunteers, community leaders (i.e majhee and imam) and project staff (i.e, MIS staff for nutrition information system) to ensure the quality of service delivery. The training receivers are expected to facilitate and scale-up the nutrition interventions. The nutrition corners in the BRAC health facilities are durable and disaster resilient and will provide uninterrupted service delivery during monsoons and other natural disasters. Promoting gender equality through nutrition-related activities will be the key focus of this project. Therefore, women, girls and differently-abled people will be at the centre of the services regardless of the interventions designed. All the nutrition facilities will take an inclusive approach to reach women, adolescents girls, persons with disabilities and gender-diversified individuals. Project staff will also be trained on safeguarding, gender equality, inclusion and protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The level of acute and chronic malnutrition in Rohingya refugee camps has reduced significantly since the influx in 2017 through coordinated efforts of various development stakeholders. However, it remains unacceptably high and needs to be worked upon with utmost importance. As per Standardised Expanded Nutrition Survey conducted by AAH in Rohingya camps of Cox’s Bazar in Oct- Nov 2021, the prevalence of combined GAM rate (WHZ and MUAC) among children of 6-59 months was between 13.7%- 14.1%, which falls under the public health threshold category of “HIGH” according to the new 2018 WHO/UNICEF classification for wasting. The prevalence of Combined GAM was 11.8%, with a Combined SAM of 1.4% among children of 6-59 months in makeshift settlements. The rate is still under the public health threshold category of “HIGH” according to the new 2018 WHO/UNICEF classification for wasting. A recent SMART survey conducted by UNHCR/WHO/UNICEF (2022) among the Rohingya community shows that children under the age of 5 have a high prevalence rate of 14.9% for acute malnutrition. In this category, the high anaemia rate is more than 40% (45.7%), the low Vitamin-A coverage rate is 69%, and the early initiation of breastfeeding coverage is 67.7%). The prevalence of Diarrhoea among children of 6-59 months as per a two-week recall period is 14.9%, where World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 50% of cases of child under-nutrition are due to repeated Diarrhoea and intestinal infections caused by poor sanitation and hygiene practices. Hand washing with soap is a critical determinant for achieving and maintaining good nutrition. This healthy behaviour is essential in preventing micronutrient deficiencies, stunting, wasting, and deaths. Though the camp's exact number of patients with chronic diseases is unknown, they need special support regarding lifestyle modification and diet management to maintain a good nutritional status and physical well-being. In February 2021, AAH Bangladesh conducted an integrated SMART Nutrition survey in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas. The survey shows the under-nutrition prevalence among host community children aged 6-59 months, with a breakdown of- wasting- 11.4%, underweight- 27.2%, and stunting- 33.1%. To address the above critical malnutrition situation in the camps and host community, BRAC proposes to deliver preventive and lifesaving nutrition services through 11 Nutrition corners in 10 Rohingya camps. Though there will be no dedicated nutrition corner in the host community, but they can receive services from these Nutrition corners. Community volunteers will disseminate nutritional messages in the host community and will suggest them [children (0-59 months), adolescent girls, and PLW] seek nutritional services from the adjacent BRAC nutrition corners.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

BRAC has adopted an integrated approach to service delivery in the camps and host communities. BRAC's Nutrition sector will work in an integrated manner with Health, Education, Child Protection, and WASH to ensure holistic support. BRAC is planning to provide nutrition support in 10 Camps, including 1E, 7, 8E, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 22, and host communities of Ratna Palong and Palong Khali under Ukhiya Upazila and Nhilla and Baharchhara under Teknaf Upazila. BRAC has also developed a good infrastructure, including Primary Healthcare Centres (PHC) and health posts, and developed the prerequisite relationships with other camp actors, host communities, refugees, community groups/committees, and the government, particularly the DC, UNO, Chairmen, RRRC and the CiCs. BRAC has been ensuring stability in the refugee operation for the last five years and improving relations with refugees, the Government, and other partners in the camps. As a result, BRAC is now considering multi-year funding, especially for critical activities that may require sustained longer-term engagement in nutrition, particularly preventive and curative measures. In addition, BRAC is committed to serving in the nutrition sector in the same camps for several years, which is mentioned in its 05 years humanitarian action plan that will support strengthening relations with government agencies, the refugees, and other implementing partners at the camp level. All activities are aligned with the JRP following current Sectoral/Working Group objectives and strategy. We will also actively participate in the relevant Sector and/or Working Group meetings and will ensure active participation in Cox’s Bazar PSEA Network. Planned activities may require some approval from government authorities. BRAC has a strong government liaison team to obtain the relevant approvals promptly.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

BRAC will ensure that all stakeholders are safe while providing nutrition services. As a PSEA network member, BRAC has policies on complaint mechanism prioritising protection and safeguarding with an emphasis on 'Do No Harm' to the programme participants. Beneficiaries and volunteers can provide feedback through different channels that allow us to prevent/mitigate adverse situations. BRAC has a comprehensive safeguarding policy to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation, and child abuse for all stakeholders. The organisation ensures prompt response when any offence is reported. It demonstrates a zero-tolerance approach to sexual harassment and other safeguarding concerns. Protection and anti-harassment policies are strictly followed in BRAC. All nutrition programme staff and volunteers will be trained on protection policies. This programme will also be supported by BRAC's Child Protection and Protection sector which will help mainstream the protection issues in the nutrition sector.

3.2 - Gender

BRAC is committed to ensure that the special needs of all gender groups are identified and taken into consideration. Following its Gender Policy, BRAC will emphasise empowerment and inclusivity of vulnerable groups, irrespective of gender. The nutrition sector will conduct a needs assessment with specific questions to understand different nutritional needs of different gender groups. Based on the findings, this programme will ensure providing nutrition-specific services for vulnerable groups, especially boys and girls under 5 years, adolescent girls and women. This programme will also engage community leaders and male members of the household to increase gender sensitivity in the community. The programme will set different indicators to measure age and gender segregated data. BRAC will ensure its all staff(s) are well trained on gender mainstreaming and all facilities are gender friendly.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

BRAC has a safeguarding policy which will be followed throughout this project, which includes avoiding child labour, child harassment and exploitation. Since BRAC is a member of PSEA and the Child Safeguarding Network, all of our health and Nutrition staff, including community workers and volunteers, are aware of the policy on safeguarding and child protection. Through Focus Group Discussions (FGD) and Key Informant Interviews (KII), specific needs of different age groups will be identified and reflected in programme design, development and implementation. Nutrition interventions will aim to address the needs of different age groups through counselling and providing age specific nutritional support.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity refers to the differences based on gender, age, disability, cultural background, sexual orientation, social and economic background, education, work experiences and organisational role. BRAC always promotes equity and equality at every stage of project design, and encourages to provide services irrespective of gender, age, race, culture to uphold the rights and dignity of the affected people. BRAC’s nutrition programme will acknowledge diversity through gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, and disabilities inclusion while selecting target beneficiary as well as the project staff. BRAC has a Gender Response Team (GRT) which will monitor and ensure the inclusion of gender-diversified people in the nutrition programme. Gender equality and diversity practices will make sure that the nutrition services are accessible to people of all gender and age groups, cultural backgrounds and sexual orientations of the Rohingya and host community.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

While designing humanitarian projects, BRAC considers UNHCR’s AAP framework- i) participation and inclusion; ii) communication and transparency; iii) feedback and response; and iv) organisational learning and adaptation. In the designing phase, BRAC will ensure community participation through consultations. Community health workers will conduct home visits to share information regarding services and the feedback mechanism using Rohingya dialect. Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) related information in the facilities will also be displayed in Burmese language. The Nutrition sector collects and analyses gender, age, and disability-disaggregated data to understand various needs related to nutrition for the affected population. BRAC regularly analyses the feedback/trends/unresolved issues and links with respective organisations/sectors for feedback referral to ensure AAP. Based on the community needs, interventions, planning and priority of the programme can be modified.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. During any man-made or natural hazards or disaster, the Nutrition and DRR sector of BRAC will work jointly in the affected areas by conducting primary assessments and forming temporary nutrition corners including breast-feeding corner and providing emergency nutrition support. The nutrition corners in the BRAC health facilities are durable and disaster resilient and will provide uninterrupted service delivery during monsoons and other natural disasters. BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF), which applies a science-based precautionary approach to project design and implementation. BRAC’s all interventions anticipate and address any potential harm before it happens. Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, circumstances of not having scientific certainty will not be used as a reason for avoiding cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation in BRAC’s project implementation mechanism.
