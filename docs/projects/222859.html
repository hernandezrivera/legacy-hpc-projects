Project Summary

South Sudan has experienced a worsening humanitarian situation since the outbreak of Sudan war in April 2023 that triggered the arrival of 880,348 returnees and refugees in the country. This situation highly increased the pressure on an already very fragile context where humanitarian needs are dire due to the combined effect of displacement related to inter-communal violence, successive flooding episodes and economic and political instability leaving 9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024—a figure anticipated to remain consistent with the 2025 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) projections.
This project aims to support the most vulnerable people located in the priority area where SI has operations namely in Upper Nile, Unity and Northern Bahr El Ghazal States.
The lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices, lack of shelter, food insecurity and livelihood insecurities puts at risk the most vulnerable.

Since 2006, Solidarités International has become a key humanitarian actor in South Sudan, implementing emergency
lifesaving responses across the country and providing integrated WASH and FSL services to crisis-affected populations in areas of static presence in Upper Nile and Unity States. SI plans to broaden its operations in Northern Bahr El Ghazal in 2025 to strengthen its assistance in response to the Sudan crisis at the border crossing sites. Since 2012, SI has played a key role in the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) strategy in South Sudan, implementing multi-sectoral assessments and delivering emergency responses to crisis-
affected populations all over the country. Since 2022, SI is a member of the ERRM consortium and was able to deploy emergency response teams countrywide following conflicts, displacements or epidemic alerts.
In 2025, SI will continue to provide emergency live-saving WASH, FSL and Shelter responses across South Sudan to restore basic access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation while ensuring
equitable access, safety, and accountability to supported populations. Through its Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism team, SI will deploy to emergency-affected locations to provide first phase and second phase responses. It will include WaSH,
Shelter and as well FSL quick response under cash or in-kind distribution to tackle emergency food insecurity situations.
Fishing and agricultural support will be as well provided in second phase response to improve coping mechanism as well as livelihood economic opportunities for long term recovery for the population.
At the same time, SI is reinforcing its presence in Upper Nile and Unity as well as starting activities and Northern Bar El Gazal to provide basic WASH and FSL services that target the most vulnerable, including those suffering malnutrition, food insecurity, disease outbreak and protection threats.
Static programs will focus on providing community-based solutions for second phase response with the assistance of the population in agricultural and fishing sector with specific designed kits and trainings to allow durable response and economic activities that can be maintained independently by communities. Engagement and joint programming with

1. Needs

In Unity and Upper Nile, where SI has a static presence, flooding and conflict result in displacement of thousands of households, it reduces their access to basic WASH services, food access as well as increased protection risks. Forced displacement of population induces increased risk of food insecurity depriving the communities from their livelihood activities.This situation in addition of the increased pressure over resources (e.g. water, land, etc.) since April 2023 in link with the influx of migrants from the Sudan crisis will further impact the already affected population. For instance, Unity State, one the county that received the greatest number of refugees/returnees, flooding has caused huge loss in land for farming and livestock. In November 2024, 47% of the overall population are facing high levels of acute food insecurity. Among children, 2.1 million are likely to suffer from acute malnutrition situation through June 2025. IPC projections for the first part of 2025 expect to have 7.69 million people facing acute food insecurity with a peak of vulnerability for the population during the lean season. On top of the needs identified, epidemics have been also reported in SI area of intervention, notably the recent cholera outbreak in Upper Nile, currently spilling out from the state, or the last hepatitis outbreak in Abyei, where SI intervened within its ERRM project. This lack of access to safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene as well of food production and/or livelihood economic activities and continuing shocks has increased the risk to vulnerable populations of water- borne diseases in turn influencing rates of malnutrition (affecting especially children under 5and pregnant and lactating women).People with specific vulnerabilities, such as People with Disability, also face many additional challenges to accessing basicServices.

2. Project activities

SI’s 2025 strategy regarding FSL will focus on priority 1 and 2 areas, Malakal and Fashoda counties in Upper Nile State, Rubkona county in Unity State and Aweil East in Northern Bar El Gazal State. 1) Providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5) (CO1.1 and CO1.2). 2) Strengthening and diversifying livelihood opportunities for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5) (CO1.1 and CO1.2).- Assessment and support of local markets to supply FSL items and materials for activities. - Provision of agricultural inputs and technical training to farmers to restore local food production.- Support to kitchen gardening and fishing activities, including distributing seeds, gardening tools, fishing kits and capacity building on - theoretical and practical climate-smart farming practices- Value chain development by supporting income generating activities such as fish drying and processing to increase incomes and reduce waste. 3) Development and strengthening of markets for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5) (CO1.1 and CO1.2).- Distribution of transportation means, including bicycles and canoes, will be distributed to improve market and resource access for both farmers and fisher folks.- Rehabilitating markets, roads, and working with trade unions to improve the availability of essential supplies, thereby strengthening local economies.Transversal to all activities: Incorporate flood resilient designs. Safety audits and AAP consultations will be conducted before all interventions to implement inclusive activities that contribute to GBV mitigation.
