Project Summary

According to the IRNA report (2019), the main driver of the current crisis has been the armed conflict (between the Government and opposition forces) from 2015 - 2018 which led to multiple displacements of civilian populations resulting in a total loss of livelihoods.
Furthermore, the competition for natural resources is the main cause of violent intra/inter communal conflicts in the some states. Pastoralist communities compete for access to grazing land, water, fishing areas and these drivers are exacerbated by civilians having access to guns, erosion of moral authority of local and traditional leaders, weak states institutions to protect the population and a culture of cattle rustling. The majority of the youths that participate in conflicts have less education, are not employed and have disengaged with the traditional authorities. This makes it easy for the youths to be easy targets for recruitment into the army or local militia groups.
Local conflicts and militarization are linked to increasing poverty, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), psychological abuse, denial of education and economic opportunity, have a profound effect on women and girls. It is currently estimated that 50 % of girls in South Sudan are married off before 18 years and 7% marry below ages of 15. Girls are usually considered as a means for transfer of wealth (dowries). Adolescent birth rates in South Sudan are estimated at 300/1000 females aged 15-19. SGBV is promoted by a culture of silence and stigma, linked to masculine identity tied to cattle raiding, bride price, no access to legal recourse, and customary practices that promote crimes like rape. The ineffective justice system limits access to justice for victims and prosecution of perpetrators of violence.
The proposed project will help enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population on peace building and peaceful co-existence. World Vision will conduct awareness raising activities and trainings on conflict resolution and peacebuilding with women, men, girls and boys in the former states of Warrap, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria. World Vision will work on reducing barriers to engagement in resilience building, food security and income generating activities, especially for women and youth; increasing anticipatory, absorptive, and adaptive capacity with respect to disruptive events including natural disasters and conflict as well as create cohesion among community members in the target counties so that conflicts will be reduced.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity is closely inter-related with conflict; farmers facing constant insecurity due to conflict have a difficult choice to make between protecting their lives or put their lives on line to fight armed cattle herders. Deliberate targeting of livelihoods assets (cattle raiding, but also destruction of grazing areas and agricultural implements) is common. Hunger itself is one of the major drivers of conflict because people are forced to fight to earn food for their survival.Women’s primary role in food production has been compounded by this situation, as many adult men have been conscripted, killed, or gone elsewhere for work. However, women are still more subject to violence during conflict and insecurity. Traditional gender roles mean they have less access to inputs, training and finance, and little formal support for this role through e.g. land tenure. Traditional gender roles mean they have less access to inputs, training and finance, and little formal support for this role through e.g. land tenure. South Sudan’s youth population is large and underserved – poor availability of education and jobs has resulted in their disengagement with traditional authority figures and increased participation in cattle raiding and intercommunal violence. As communities’ coping capacity (resilience) is eroded by years of repeated shocks and stresses, negative coping strategies – in particular in relation to the natural environment, e.g. charcoal burning – become more common. Combined with the ongoing effects of climate change, this has produced a noticeable level of environmental degradation in just the past few decades. Women in particular are deeply affected by this, given the dependence of their traditional roles and tasks on natural resources (gathering water and wood, growing and gathering food). Traditional governance systems to manage communal use of these natural resources and buffer against climactic shocks and stresses are stretched to their limit as their severity and unpredictability continue to increase.In March 2019, WVSS conducted a Peacebuilding Needs Assessment in Western Equatoria, Greater Bhar el Ghazal and Central Equatoria. Some of the key findings and recommendations are as follows:* Power struggle among political leaders and ethnic conflicts: There is a need to create awareness on the implementation of Chapter 1 of RARCSS 2018 which has provided a framework for addressing this issue during Transition Period of 3 years from May during which power has been allocated to the Parties. * High rate of youth unemployment: There is a need for further support and creation of awareness on implementation of Chapter 4.15.1.4 of RARCSS 2018 for Youth Enterprise Development Fund with the goal of bringing more youth into economic growth and development. Promote and support vocational training for youth to acquire skills for self-employment in agriculture, technical skills and small business.* High bride wealth and impregnating of girls: There is a need to assist the youth to lobby for review of customary laws on marriage and possible reduction of bride wealth to a minimum between 11-50 cows only. This will enable young people to get married in a normal way and thus avoid breaking customary laws of marriage by impregnating girls as it is the case now.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will contribute to the Sub Cluster's objective of enabling vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas. In particular, World Vision proposes the following:OUTCOME 1: Individuals, particularly children and women are facing less violence at the community and local level.- Coordinate with UNMISS to conduct patrols and peace rallies;- Integrate protection considerations including location selection, beneficiary selection, and training into all activities; OUTCOME 2: Communities have effective mechanisms in place that meaningfully include women and youth to resolve conflicts peacefully.- Conduct awareness raising and sensitisation campaigns on the contents of the Tri-State Peace Agreement and the (revised) Marial Bai Agreement, Small arms Act and dissemination of peacebuilding messages - Conduct radio talk shows on issues of conflict mitigation, cattle migration and peacebuilding.- Conduct sports and cultural events to provide opportunities for youth from competing communities to meet in forums that promote social cohesion. - Establish, train and operationalize Peace Committees, including Joint Border Peace Committees (13 members per county) to encourage communities and authorities to mitigate intercommunal conflict, cattle raiding and revenge; - Strengthen Council of Traditional Leaders and Authorities (COTAL) in the states;- Organize and conduct conflict management workshops for county executives, cattle herders, traditional authorities, influential personalities (spiritual leaders), civil society, women and youth;

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed action has been designed to address many of the immediate, underlying and basic causes of low resilience and conflicts in the targeted counties taking into consideration the institutional and policy context of South Sudan, as well as the partners’ experiences implementing similar initiatives and interventions in South Sudan and across the East African region. It aligns with the Government of South Sudan’s Comprehensive Agricultural Development Master Plan (CAMP), which sets out the country’s framework for agricultural development under four themes: Reconstruction and Recovery, Food and Nutrition Security, Economic Growth and Livelihood Security and Institutional Development. The project interventions contribute to the CAMP goals by supporting households in the project locations to recover from the shocks experienced due to violent conflict, improve their food production and strengthen their livelihoods. The project will also link with government such as the County Agriculture Department executing its mandate to provide extension services to the community, as well as with traditional structures in their dispute resolution and natural resource management capacity. The National Agriculture and Livestock Extension Policy’s call to address the lack of agricultural extension by working with and strengthening both public and private sector providers has also influenced the design. Finally, coordination and non-duplication of efforts will be ensured by ensuring that activities implemented with support from key factors such as FAO and WFP will be given high priority, particularly in the areas of cash for asset (CFA) and systems development.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The project will directly target youth, women and men with peace-building activities is crucial to both strengthening overall safety in project locations, and encouraging youth, women and men themselves to participate in other project resilience and income activities. Youth will be targeted through school structures, and directly through activities in cattle camps, in order to establish peaceful role models, establish pro-peace recreational activities, and solidify empathy and hopes for the future.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The problem analysis has been informed by recent reports from field assessment missions and consultations with communities and local authorities from different actors such as UNMISS field missions reports, WFP Community Based Participatory Planning and the Interagency Rapid Needs Assessment. In addition, some interventions respond to gaps and recommendations of different conferences that included the local communities and other actors. In addition to promoting ownership by local communities where projects will be implemented, sustainability will be ensured with capacity building and training embedded in the strategy and the regular engagement of existing structures that have a conflict prevention and/or peace building mandate; and by championing locally owned, grassroots solutions that are long term in nature.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

In case, women and girls might not be included in decision-making process, World Vision will ensure that separated focus group discussions be conducted for girls and women to specifically address their protection needs. World Vision also will broaden the participation of women, youths and children in peace-building activities through the local faith actors in the target communities. Especially, World Vision will lay ground for active youths involvement in the peace process through youth-led campaigns under the theme of peace and child protection. Through the child-led campaigns and awareness raising sessions, messages to prevent sexual and gender-based violence will be clearly raised along with the support for the faith based organizations to actively engage in strengthening community protection structures in the communities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

World Vision will use participatory tools and exercises to engage women, girls, men and boys, with specific use of child friendly age-appropriate tools to prioritize feedback from girls and boys. Information will be provided to adult and child beneficiaries in age appropriate methods at the project start, through community engagement & routine monitoring, and at project end to discuss outcomes. Child Help Desks, Information Boards at Child Friendly Spaces (CFS)/schools/health facilities, community mobilization through local faith partners, and distribution of IEC materials will also be done. While Key Informant Interviews and feedback is collected from local authorities, community leaders and faith leaders, the majority of influencing data and feedback is sourced from women, men, girls and boys within the community broadly. Regular learning sessions will be done with partner to reflect on the progress and challenges of the project. Fragile Context Programming Approach will be applied to be responsive to the changing needs of South Sudan’s fragile context.
