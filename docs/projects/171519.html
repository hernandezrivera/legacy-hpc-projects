Project Summary

MRF will Conduct 6 community dialogue and awareness meetings project locations to introduce project activities, outputs, stakeholder’s roles and responsibilities as an AAP strategy. MRF proposes to build the capacity of 120 (60 female) community social services provider including local authorities and community leaders (Teachers, social workers, religious leaders, etc.) and strengthen their ability to protect, prevent and respond to the core rights violations of children especially those affected by the conflict and support their participation in peace building activities and community-based referral pathways. About (6) CFS's will be established and equipped with child learning and recreational materials.
MRF will provide comprehensive case management for 350 most vulnerable children
MRF resolves to conduct family tracing and reunification of 250 UASC. During the implementation of the project, MRF propose to train caregivers (750 female and 250 male) on proper child care.
Through coordination MRF will establish and strengthen referrals of children and also provide direct psychosocial support to 8,400 children (including disabled children) affected by displacements.
In a bid to strengthen the reporting mechanism, MRF will Conduct 6 CP monitoring and contribute information to CPiE and share reports with relevant clusters
Through awareness raising sessions, MRF will carry out 6 CP campaigns to support girls and boys in vulnerable situations through Case management and available specialized referral services, in addition 6 other awareness dialogue sessions on prevention of recruitment of children into the armed forces and groups targeting children, their families and communities through displacement villages, Mosques and schools will also be conducted.

In doing so we shall increase the volunteers in the community to activate our response speed to solving the problems of the most vulnerable in community. 3. 8 Assessment and monitoring and conducting social distancing in interviews, phone interviews to reinforce eviction monitoring and alert, including through the creation of an eviction hotline and legal support to deconges¬tion. 4. Mitigation Measures

MRF will participatory implement this project in a conflict sensitive manner, ensuring that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries at all stages of this project cycle. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner and frequent consultations of key beneficiary groups in decision making particularly women and girls. MRF will spend at least two weeks in the field to engage and work closely with beneficiaries to set up a project implementation committee that will last throughout the project, nominated by the target beneficiaries and are composed of the beneficiaries. This committee will share with all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, the elderly, those who are illiterate, women and girls issues agreed upon during their meetings such as types of assistance needed, safety audits, meeting and distribution locations and sites.MRF will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor any distribution process, and to provide feedback to MRF on issues concerning protection such as SEA, MRF issues, livelihoods, the distribution process and the whole intervention process from the beginning of the project. MRF will also use the presence of beneficiaries to pass messages such as health, livelihoods, SGBV, MRF among others through messaging and awareness creation. The feedback team will be composed of the project implementation committee representatives, representatives of various constituencies of beneficiaries and MRF’s protection advisor. Conducting both pre and post distribution awareness and monitoring respectively will provide feedback from the various groups of beneficiaries. Reports developed by MRF will be available for community, partners and even government authorities to access for references. This action will address key lifesaving threats identified.The dignity of beneficiaries is measure through feedback by beneficiaries as all staff are train to uphold the dignity of beneficiaries irrespective of their social, physical and psychological status. MRF will ensure that all children have the right to adequate nutrition, sanitation, health care, housing, and education and to be treated with respect, dignity, and courtesy. Staff members will promote the human rights of all people and no one will be discriminated on the basis of an individual’s race, color, ethnicity, national origin, religion, age, political affiliation, gender, sexual orientation, marital status, pregnancy, or disability The indicators are designed to capture the safety of beneficiaries in the monitoring of activities. All the activities will be utilizing the "Do no harm" approach for the local communities. Our beneficiaries will learn about the Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) strategies as part of the life-skills component so they are equipped to overcome spikes and shocks. A community safety audit will be conducted to ensure safety of children while accessing CP activities including the CFS's Access to assistance is one of the main pillars in the design and implementation of the project. Quality measures will be use to quantify and track access to quality humanitarian assessment. Measurement of access will provide information on beneficiaries registered timely and having appropriate access to assistance. This may include: access to several components of humanitarian assistance not limited to structural barriers impacting access of children to humanitarian. MRF is committed to ensuring that children have unhindered access to case management, FTR, MHPSS, PFA and community reintegration services
