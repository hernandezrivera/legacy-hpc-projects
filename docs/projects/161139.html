Project Summary

Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement South Sudan still faces one of the world’s direst humanitarian crises as a result of the 5 years’ brutal war that has left 6.5 million severely food insecure and over 2.3 million displaced. GLI’s proposed intervention is intended to focus primarily on life saving WASH activities that will include rehabilitation of emergency existing water facilities (boreholes, motorized boreholes, and emergency water treatment units), distribution of hygiene/sanitary kits and construction of emergency sanitation facilities and reinforce with emergency targeted hygiene promotion to reduce the risks of increased WASH related morbidity and mortality.
This project is going be implemented in selected payams of Kapoeta South, Kajokeji and Northern Tonj Counties. GLI is currently implementing other WASH programs and already has an established presence with well qualified local staff to ensure timely delivery of services with good standard.
The proposed project will target women, children, boys, girls and men including IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host populations. Inadequate WASH services in these four counties contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased malnutrition. There is lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices specifically for those communities displaced by current conflicts into Kajokeji and the host communities. To respond to the negative WASH impact in the above mentioned areas GLI has currently set up static and mobile teams that are responding to the WASH needs in the targeted areas. This proposed intervention will reduce the vulnerability of those communities who do not have access to safe water supplies and proper sanitation and hygiene in Kapoeta South, Northern Tonj, Pibor and Kajokeji counties through provision of improved water, sanitation and hygiene services and increased knowledge and coping capacity of affected communities to prevent outbreak of WASH related diseases such as cholera and diarrohea.
In addition, this proposed intervention is designed to improve the quality and access to services especially through rehabilitation of existing water points and household emergency water treatment, construction of sanitation facilities, promote good hygiene practices through training and capacity building community based hygiene promoters. GLI’s proposed project will integrate with the nutrition and food security clusters through referrals to specialized partners with OTP centres since it is widely noted that nutritional status is greatly impacted by diarroheal and other WASH related diseases/ illnesses which largely affect children under 5 years of age.
GLI will encourage active participation of the local community including women and children in the implementation of the proposed project. This will be done through Consultation of local authorities at all levels, women, men, boys and girls in the selection of the project payams within the county based on the identified needs. The local authorities, host community and IDPs men, women, girls and boys will be involved in the design of the community and institutional sanitation facilities putting into consideration the need for privacy for females for the case of latrines and bath room’s construction.

1 - Needs

The August 2019 IPC report records that 65% (62,421) of the population in South Sudan fall under IPC 3,4 and 5. The UNICEF South Sudan Humanitarian Situation Report (Aug 2019) the nutrition data from IPC Acute Malnutrition Aug 2019 (IPC AMN Aug 2019 phase 3) for Kapoeta South County which reported global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels above >15 % prevalence by Weight for Height (WHZ) which is consistent with the IPC Aug 2019 data. The drivers of the child malnutrition are mainly sub-optimal child care and feeding practices driven by food insecurity, high morbidity, poor water and sanitation. Only 13 per cent of children aged 6-23 months received minimum dietary diversity, 23 per cent of them received minimum meal frequency, whereas only 7 per cent of them received minimum acceptable diet. Two out every five children aged 6-59 months reported fever, which is usually associated with malaria. Furthermore, 25 per cent and 22 per cent of the children reported diarrhoea and respiratory infection during the same period. Access to sanitation in the county is also very poor with only 21 per cent of the households across South Sudan reportedly with access to latrines. The overall situation of water supply and sanitation in Kapoeta South, Kajokeji Pibor and Northern Tonj Counties is one of the poorest with 68% not having access to improved sanitation and nearly 45% still using unimproved water sources. Intensifying conflicts, repeated natural disasters and poor operation and maintenance is a huge burden on WASH infrastructure. Unplanned and continued arrival of migrating cattle keepers from the neighbouring counties is putting additional pressure on already limited and dilapidated infrastructure. Majority of the cattle keeping population rely on public hand-pumps (49%) and female headed households have far less access to private hand-pumps (23% by male and 13% by female). A recent inter-agency assessment led by UNOCHA in Kajokeji County clearly show the dilapidated WASH infrastructure with minimal water points or non functional water points. Likewise, much lower percentage of returnees have access to water within the compound (4%) compared to conflict displaced people. This means, both the host cattle keepers and migrating populations face the burden of traveling long distances to fetch clean water. Such conditions are pushing affected population (including host communities) to share their limited water sources and often seek refuse to unimproved water sources and practice risky behaviours like open defecation. Female headed household are more likely to opt for open defecation compared to male headed, presumably because of their inability to build pit latrine. Such conditions are conducive for manifestation of diarrhoea: Attention need to be paid to female headed families, as these families are less likely to have access to the means of hygiene (access to soap by 43% male headed families compared to 35% by female headed) and sanitation. The condition of poor WASH compromise the dignity of women and girls, put them at risk of harassment, and expose people to life-threatening diseases including outbreaks of acute diarrhoea, cholera, ARI and measles, especially to young children and sick and elderly people. GLI’s proposed interventions will be targeting cattle keepers, both male and female headed households without pit latrines, communities without protected bore holes The recent flooding in many areas in South Sudan has caused entire villages to be submerged in water and the WASH facilities have been greatly affected especially in Pibor County. Another survey conducted in Tonj North County, by the WASH Cluster, reported that that ¼ of the population do not have household latrines and do not practice basic hygiene. From interviews from key respondents, it was noted that three children had died due to watery diarrhea. It was also noted that 9 elderly people were bitten by snakes when they went to the bushes to ease themselves.

2 - Activities

• Rehabilitation of existing 30 hand pumps (20 Kajokeji, 10 in Pibor).• Establish sustainable 30 water management committees responsible for the operation and maintenance of water facilities with 50% comprise of women.• Mobilize women and men to participate in the location, design, and maintenance of water points.• Organize distribution of WASH/NFI to 4,000 households. GLI through core pipeline manager UNICEF will access water treatment products and water containers. The beneficiaries will be provided with education on how to use these WASH emergency supplies specially the proper storage of the water containers and application of water purifiers.• Construction of 12 gender segregated communal latrines with consultation of all groups in Kapoeta South, Kajo Keji, North Tonj and Pibor Counties. Design communal latrines in consultation with women and girls to ensure that users have privacy. The selection process of communal latrines sites will be based on the needs of the community eg IDPs locations will be prioritized in this selection process.• Procurement of household latrine materials for Kapoeta South Counties• Carry out concerted hygiene promotion campaigns on safe excreta disposal and hygiene practices.• Mobilization and training of 400 hygiene promoters. Hygiene promoters will sensitize the IDPS about the use of latrines and host communities, water sources, importance of cleaning water containers, solid waste management, the importance of hand washing at critical times and the importance of personal hygiene among the affected community. • Conduct daily hygiene campaign/awareness among the internally displaced persons in Kajo Keji, Pibor, Kapoeta South and Tonj Counties. Ensure all users in particular women and girls participate in identification of risks hygiene practices and coordination.Distribution of hygiene/WASH kits to vulnerable girls and women severly affected by conflict• GLI WASH activities will be integrated with SC/OTP through a referral system to nutrition partners running this centres.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

This project will ensure that the affected populations in the targeted areas have a genuine voice during activity design and decision-making process that inform the proposed project’s priorities and modalities. All community members including women, men, boys and girls will be incorporated from the design of the project to ensure ownership. GLI will ensure that communication and messaging is timely, accurate, appropriate, effective and understood. Project parameters such as site and beneficiary selection will be openly discussed with beneficiaries using a highly transparent methodology that is grounded in community participation. Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, focus group discussions, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used. We will also ensure that a gender-sensitive community feedback mechanism (for consultation, feedback and complaints) is established to inform and change programming, including the establishment of accountability groups based on community-defined vulnerability criteria. Routine monitoring shall be conducted using a mix of participatory appraisal and quantitative methods throughout the duration of the project, engaging primary agents of change, communities, local stakeholders, and project implementing teams. At field level progress toward project objectives will beclosely monitored by the WASH coordinator and MEAL specialist supported by the Head of Programmes. Where possible, beneficiaries and local officials will take the lead on activity monitoring, and report on progress at community learning events. GLI will train its staff on humanitarian accountability principles and protection principles
