Project Summary

The project” Protection and Support of FHHS With Disabilities and Survivors of Cancer Subjected to GBV through Multisectoral Responses” was designed in order to provide multi-sectoral service to FHHS affected of COVID-19 women with disability and women with cancer in the Gaza Strip” The change which can be attributed to the implementation of the project or the project will contribute to ultimate outcome 1000: Alleviated suffering, protected and maintained human dignity, and lives saved for vulnerable FHHs (suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs, women with cancer, and GBV survivors) 18-59 and >59 years, in poorest localities in the north, Gaza, Middle area, Khanyouins, and Rafah governorates of the Gaza Strip in Palestine. That change will be achieved 'only if ' the following intermediate outcomes will be realised:
1100: Increased performance and change behavior among GBV service providers in offering gender-responsive assistance for vulnerable FHHs (suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with cancer, and GBV survivors) 18-59 and >59 years, in poorest localities in Gaza, Middle North Gaza, Khanyouins and Rafah and affected of COVID-19.
1200: Increased use of gender-responsive assistance including structured group psycho-social support, family therapy, awareness-raising workshops, group legal counseling/awareness, referral to receive additional legal support services, dignity kits, temporary shelter assistance, case management (individual psychosocial counseling, psychotherapy, and medication, individual legal counseling, legal representation, economic empowerment, reproductive health, and social services) by vulnerable FHHs (suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with cancer, and GBV survivors) 18-59 and >59 years and their families. These intermediate outcomes will be achieved 'only if ' the following immediate outcomes will be realised:
1110: Increased access to staff ToT training on Hawa Elzahra and case management approach for GBV service providers to promote recovery and resilience of vulnerable FHHs (suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with cancer, GBV survivor) 18-59 and >59 years and their families.
1210: Improved access to gender sensitive structured group psycho-social support, open recreational days, family therapy, awareness-raising workshops, group legal counseling/awareness, dignity kits, temporary shelter assistance, case management (individual psychosocial counseling, psychotherapy and medication, individual legal counseling, legal representation, economic empowerment, reproductive health, social services, and referral to other service providers to receive additional services) conforming to cultural norms of users for vulnerable FHHs with and without disabilities (suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with cancer, GBV survivor) 18-59 and >59 years and their families.
The project will be implemented in partnership between AISHA, Wefaq Society for Women and Child Care and Red Crescent Society for Gaza Strip. The project directly targets 3309 FHHS, 809 men, and indirectly 30,000 community persons. The project will target the most vulnerable FHHS and their families in the localities: Rafah: Rafah, Rafah Camp Gaza: Al Mughraqa (Abu Middein), Ash Shati Camp, Gaza City
Deir Al-Balah:An Nuseirat Camp, Az Zawayda, Deir Al Balah Khan Yoins: Al Mawasi, Khanyouins camp, Khuza'a, North Gaza: Beit Hanun, Beit Lahiya, Izbat Beit Hanun, Jabalya, Jabalya Camp.
Due to the outbreak of COVID-19, Aisha will use remote modality like Zoom, Google meeting, hotline, phones, and Zahr online case management system to respond to the needs through crises.

1 - Needs

The results of the violence survey in the Palestinian society carried out by the Palestinian Statistical Center showed that 38% of woman who are married or previously married in the Gaza Strip have experienced at least to one type of violence (psychological, physical, sexual, economic or verbal) and that 57% of Married women have been subjected to psychological violence by their husbands at least once. UN Gender Alert 2020 identified that female-headed households represent 11.7 percent of the West Bank and 9.2 percent of the Gaza Strip. 31 percent of female-headed households are vulnerable to food security challenges, five percent more than male-headed households in the oPt.. 62 per cent of female-headed households in Gaza are vulnerable to food insecurity. Though female-headed households represent 11 per cent of the oPt, they make up 20 per cent of those suffering from extreme poverty. Female-headed households face increased vulnerability due to COVID-19. A rapid assessment on COVID-19 carried out by the UN Women revealed that domestic violence has increased due to the conditions created by the COVID 19 pandemic, which in most cases resulted in lock down of survivors of violence with their abusers. With families in lockdown, helplines are seeing a surge of cases requesting a multitude of services. The findings of the assessment also showed that security and justice services, including protection, violence against women cases are not considered essential under the state of emergency with the main shelters not taking in new women and courts limiting litigations procedures for cases of the emergency law violations. Additionally, the report concluded that front-liners are working under stressful conditions, putting their emotional and psychological well-being at risk. According to a report carried out by AISHA Association on the impact of COVID-19 crisis on PSS conditions of women, 2 out of 3 respondent women reported experienced violence during home isolation. The report further showed that more than half of women are at risk of violence and have no access to protection services. Another factsheet commissioned by Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children showed that 84% of respondent families reported increased anxiety and 89% reported that their mental health well-being was negatively affected due to COVID-19 crisis. Moreover, 82% of children reported the negative implication of COVID-19 crisis on their MHPSS. In addition to the medical needs, the closure of schools, limitations on access to work places, and the imposition of quarantine and curfew, particularly in overcrowded households, alongside general uncertainty, are expected to increase mental and psychosocial distress, particularly among children, as well gender-based violence. The impact on women, boys and girls is further influenced by the layered socio-economic and political realities which they live under. Pre-existing socio-cultural factors contribute to the formation and implementation of health emergency response. FHHS in the Gaza Strip being vulnerable group had a bad impact of their lives. Aisha through its work with affected people of COVID-19 since March highlighted the damage of family’s entities, with families staying at home, household tension will increase and higher incidence of GBV against women and children was recorded. Fear of the virus and limited households’ resources and capacities will increase psychosocial pressure on all members of the family including women and children. More vulnerable groups such as women and girls with disabilities are also in need for health and protection related risks. As an emerging global humanitarian crisis, the spread of Coronavirus (COVID-19) and subsequent mitigation policies and measures, are expected to affect women, girls, men and boys differently. Consequently, their needs, priorities and interests may differ, as do their resources, capacities and coping strategies. Women are often the first responders to a crisis, and they play a central role in the survival and resilience of families and communities. Understanding the gendered impact of this humanitarian crisis on the population is key to ensuring that the response of humanitarian partners guarantee the protection of all members of the affected population and contribute to addressing the needs and priorities of women, men, girls, and boys equitably and effectively. Aisha conducted a specific needs assessment on 14.10.2020 in the 5 governates affected of Covid-19 through the following methods:Phone calls with 100 FHHS (30 out of them GBV cases with chronic diseases, 20 WWDS) affected with COVID-19 or already recuperative.o Phone calls with 50 adolescent girls and boys 14- 17 yearso Phone calls with 20 men18-59 and>59 years in quarantineo Phone calls with 50 women and girls via AISHA’s helpline calls asking supporto 4 Zoom meetings targeting frontline MHPSS specialists like psychiatrist, case managers, psychologists, psychotherapists, social specialists, lawyers & health workersThe results of the assessment highlighted the needs of different groups disaggregated by gender and age as follow:FHHS showed the needs for:• MHPSS support through easy methods to reach• Case management services and multi-sectorial interventions • Family therapy method and social integrations• Dignity kits• legal services including consultations, awareness and representation • Awareness raising workshops on gender and protection pathways GBV cases with chronic diseases:• MHPSS support• Screening, diagnostic, treatment, home visits and rehabilitation• Physiotherapy, artificial silicon breast in addition to medical breast bra, artificial hair, and primary health care kits containing iodin-burn cream-cotton-alcohol• Family therapy and social integration. Women with disability:• Specialized mental health services• Individual counseling• Awareness raising workshops on Gender and COVID-19

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

AISHA applies PSEA manual and code of ethics and conduct. - AISHA has infrastructure adaptations including fitting ramps and railings to facilities so that all PwDs can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity. - The location of AISHA’s facilities and the routes to them are safe. - AISHA provides WWDs and GWDs assistive devices and signs language interpretation services to ensure full access of direct beneficiaries to services.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

- AISHA advances access approach that is consistent with the humanitarian principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence and humanity). - AISHA follows the guidelines in its "Risk management plan including risk analysis and risk mitigation" and "Security and Safety Manual" to protect the lives of their staff during emergencies. - AISHA provides case management as multi-sectorial services at an adequate time when all women and girls and their families can realistically access them. - AISHA facilitates access to the services for persons with reduced mobility (persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) through making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. - AISHA facilitates access to MHPSS services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g. the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled) through providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations. - AISHA will raise awareness of target women and men about gender, GBV and their rights - AISHA uses their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls in ARAs to GBV services to leave no one behind. - AISHA adapts activities in accordance with hygiene and safety measures. - AISHA involves grassroots CBOs and local communities in these localities.
