Project Summary

Medair’s proposed intervention will provide life-saving and sustainable shelter interventions at household-level to target beneficiaries in areas that are hard to reach, newly accessible, or with recent or sudden population movement. Medair will meet HHs in urgent need for basic shelter-related assistance for vulnerable, conflict affected households. The intervention builds on the experience, expertise, and relationships from Medair’s previous project cycles to reach those in-need, with protection mainstreaming and sustainability at the core of its programme. Medair intends to reach near 1,000 beneﬁciaries (excluding overlap) with this shelter intervention through:

1. Rehabilitating HH-level shelter units of damaged houses via tailored rehabilitation to improve physical safety and security, privacy, and provide more digniﬁed living conditions to a total of up to 220 individuals. This will target the most vulnerable households according to Medair’s vulnerability criteria.
2. Special adaptation will be made for People Living With Mobility Impairment (PLWMI). Based on the result of house to house assessments that Medair has conducted and through previous shelter rehab projects implemented around Syria, it is expected that 12% of HHs are in need of disability adjustments, which will be provided alongside the rehabilitation of the damaged houses. Medair will follow accessibility and universal design principles to complete adaptations for up to 25 households with members who live with mobility impairment, with disabilities, or older people wherever this is possible within the constraints of the building structure.
3. Medair will rehabilitate common areas of apartment buildings within the building plot by providing additional protection upgrades for the 85 targeted buildings (e.g. doors, lights) and adaptation for elderly or disabled people (e.g. ramps, hand rails), targeting around 700 beneﬁciaries (excluding overlap)
4. Distribution and installation of 40 shelter kits aim to address the emergency and ongoing shelter needs in selected areas, Medair will provide shelter kits to vulnerable HHs to improve climatic protection and privacy while providing more digniﬁed living conditions to 200 beneficiaries. The most vulnerable HHs or communities will be prioritized.
Assistance might also be given to extremely vulnerable HHs returnees and host communities who have no capacity to meet growing needs. Medair will remain flexible in terms of location of the intervention and hard-to-reach areas will be prioritized. Medair teams are able to conduct independent house-to-house assessments jointly with SARC, using Medair’s own vulnerability and needs assessment criteria to identify and prioritize HHs. As Medair uses a multi-sectoral approach, needs assessments cover Shelter needs, as well as WASH and health needs and interventions are customized for each community and/or HH. Medair will conduct monthly FGDs wherever possible prior to the interventions to obtain beneﬁciary feedback and continue to recenter beneficiary needs.

4 - Needs

It is estimated that 5.92 million people in Syria (including nearly 50% of returnees and 26% of IDPs) of the overall population of 18.60 million lives in substandard shelter. 80% of returnees state inability to meet their basic needs, along with 30% of the host community households (HNO, 2022). Shelter adequacy issues stem from an inadequate protection from the elements, unaffordability of repairs, and/or an inability to obtain approvals (HNO, 2022). According to HNO (2021 SNFI chapter) shelter needs are concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb, Raqqa, and Rural Damascus governorates. In the fourth quarter of 2021 Medair conducted house to house assessments in the Al-Hamdaniya neighborhood of Deir-ez-Zor city, an area left decimated during the crisis. Results found many people to be living in damaged buildings due to poverty and desperation (e.g. high rent in their previous living situations). Many were forced to return to their homes, which are now damaged and lack essential protection needs such as doors and windows. Intervention by iNGOs in this specific area was limited when compared to the size of the damage as a total of 623 houses were rehabilitated. Needs are further intensified by new displacement in NWS, critical infrastructure gaps, protracted displacement in NES, and limited housing options across the country. Deir-ez-Zor and rural Aleppo have been chronically underserved with limited humanitarian presence. Communities such as these are Medair’s target population.

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair will carry out up to 220 shelter rehabs. Technical assessments deﬁne basic required rehabilitation for each site. Rehabilitation focuses on basic house repairs, protection, security, and WASH needs. Each apartment will be rehabilitated according to Sphere standards. Additional work will be completed to accommodate special needs of elderly or PLWMI. Medair will target within Deir-ez-Zor city and Aleppo. This will be done using a vulnerability assessment criteria framework. The project will incorporate HLP components as per sector guidelines. Based on technical assessments, rehabilitation may include walls, doors, windows, roofs, electrical wiring and chimneys for winter fireplaces. For each apartment, Medair customizes interventions per beneficiary needs, tailoring up to 12% of 220 of targeted households for members with PLWMI and the elderly. Medair will provide and install emergency shelter kits for 40 HHs in areas that are newly accessible based on sector recommendations and previous beneﬁciary feedback. Medair distributions and assessments are in coordination with SARC and municipalities. All Kits are in accordance with sector standards and delivered directly to HHs. Quality assurance teams examine kits prior to distribution and during distribution. PDMs will be undertaken at least one month. All activity will conducted in line with to the Shelter Sector guidelines for household repairs and with approval from MoLAE.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Delays in planned procurement and/or importationMitigation: A procurement plan will be completed at start of project cycle to ensure sufficient lead-time for normal operations. Procurement is planned to maximise use of reliable in-country suppliers, transfer of responsibility for import issues to suppliers as they are more familiar and able to manage the process. Risk: Fluctuations in exchange rates, differences between black market and Central Bank of Syria rates and inflation impact suppliers, in turn affecting procurement and budget management. Monthly budget and inflation monitoring as well as use of forecasting spending.
