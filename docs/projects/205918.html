Project Summary

The Disability inclusive integrated health response will be targeting boys, girls, women and men, persons with disability and elderly from internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East Counties (Eastern Equatoria), Akobo and Uror Counties (Jonglei State) and Nasir County with chronic emergency health needs.
The planned health intervention activities will as well be integrated with nutrition, WASH and protection/GBV prevention interventions by creating synergies and coordination with Health, protection/GBV and WASH, and nutrition clusters through Health, protection/GBV and WASH and nutrition implementing partners so as to prevent and reduce health related morbidity and mortality by reducing the prevalence of common and vaccine preventable diseases among children under five years, vulnerable men and women in Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Akobo, Uror and Nasir Counties.
The project will offer integrated health services by supporting 5 health facilities 2 primary health care center (PHCC) and 3 primary health care unit (PHCU) and 10 mobile clinic in the five counties. TADO will carry out vaccination for 2500 children against vaccine preventable diseases, support 100 community health workers to promote primary health care at community level, support rehabilitation of 25 persons living with disability, provision of mental health and psychosocial services to the 500 beneficiaries, conduct 12,000 consultations and treatment for boys, girls women and men including people with disability and elderly for common diseases, conduct 250 deliveries for pregnant mothers by skilled birth attendants, reporting on weekly EWARS, supporting 25 SGBV survivors with CMR services, conducting 50 community meetings geared towards for behavioural change communication for health seeking behaviours.
TADO will work closely with the county health department and other key stakeholders that will ensure delivery of quality integrated disability inclusive health services. This project will also supplement the provision of immunization services to ZERO DOSE children in all the 5 counties supported by GAVI project.
The project intends to support health needs of 35,000 beneficiaries from most vulnerable (IDPs), returnees and host community beneficiaries of Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Akobo, Nasir and Uror Counties. This project comes at a time when Kapoeta East, Kapoeta South, Uror, Nasir and Akobo Counties are struggling with intermittent flooding, continuous intercommunal conflicts causing internal displacements worsening the vulnerability evidenced by inadequate medical supplies, high prevalence of common disease like malaria and diarrheal diseases and high morbidity and mortality rates.
The situation has and will continue to impact negatively on the already limited basic health, nutrition, WASH and protection services in these locations if no intervention is done. Therefore, this project will address the health needs of the most vulnerable boys, girls, women and men, persons living with disability and elderly in the target counties.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to struggle to meet the health needs of the vulnerable and affected populations due to the chronic challenges associated with poor health systems evidenced by poor health facility functionality, inadequate medical supplies and poor health facility coverage, natural disasters like intermittent floods and drought and intercommunal conflict that has negatively impacted health service delivery due to increasing burden of internal displacements and recently increasing number of returnees. According to Humanitarian needs overview (HNO) report issued November 2022, Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Uror, Akobo and Nasir Counties are experiencing limited functionality of health facilities, capacity and poor coverage, including recent closures of health facilities due to reduced funding, which has made it difficult for the ministry of health to meet the increasing health needs worsened by floods, malnutrition and conflict which has caused vulnerable women, children, the elderly and people living with disability to have limited access to quality lifesaving health care services (HNO Nov 2022).According to IRNA report for Kapoeta East County March 2023, Kapoeta East County is experiencing inadequate health services across different payams most especially Jie and Kauto payams with intermittent shortage of medical supplies, low coverage of nutrition services, inadequate primary health care facilities, poor immunization coverage, protection concerns against women and girls, inadequate clean water and water purification supplies including limited knowledge on hygiene and sanitation and this is not different from the neighboring Kapoeta South County. (Kapoeta East IRNA March 2023).According to UNICEF South Sudan humanitarian situation report NO.9 September 2023, over 30,000 South Sudanese returnees crossed to Nasir and Akobo Counties from the refugee camps in Ethiopia due to humanitarian aid cuts and escalated conflict in the area increasing burden to the already stretched health facilities in these counties due to increased health needs.According to the rapid assessment conducted by REACH March 2023 in Akobo West, internal conflict that took place from December 2022 to March 2023 caused heavy displacements worsening vulnerability to the already vulnerable population leading to more health, WASH, protection and food security needs. This also led to increase in number of patients with Acute watery diarrhoea and other waterborne diseases. Patients were lacking medical supplies and there was urgent need to improve accessibility to reproductive health services. In general, these counties still face inadequate delivery of mental health services, management of non-communicable diseases, delivery of reproductive health services and referral pathways lacking in many parts thus calling for efforts to support scaling up delivery of lifesaving primary health care services in Akobo, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta South, Uror and and Nasir Counties. TADO will utilize its operational and static presence in Akobo, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta South, Uror and Nasir Counties with field offices where it’s a health implementing partner implementing routine vaccination program mainly for ‘ZERO DOSE’ children in 23 health facilities which are majorly not supported by health partners supporting the curative services in these counties. These facilities are majorly in hard-to-reach payams where the vulnerable populations are located. This vast static presence and experience in the 5 counties with many community mobilizers and vaccinators will be utilized to make sure equitable access to integrated quality preventive and curative primary health care services in the 5 counties are offered to the vulnerable population. This synergy will contribute to improve cluster objectives by reducing morbidity and mortality and preventing children and mothers from vaccine preventable disease.

2. Project activities

• Immunization services against vaccine preventable infections• Mental health services and referrals• OPD consultations• ANC and PNC services• Injury and trauma management.• Sexually transmitted infection management• Skilled birth attendant deliveries.• Health Education geared towards behavior change communication and health seeking behaviors.• Nutrition screening for pregnant and lactating mothers and children under five years old• Case management of rape • COVID 19 prevention awareness and vaccination.• Distribution of contraceptives and reproductive health services.• Infection prevention and control in the health facilities.• Coordination meetings at the county health department.• Community engagement meetings geared towards improving health service uptake in the community.
