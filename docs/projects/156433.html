Project Summary

The project will Improve equitable access to quality lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services through systematic identification, referral of acutely malnourished cases for boys and girls under five and PLWs. further the malnourished boys and girls will receive MNP, LNS, and PLWs will receive iron folate tablets. in addition, parents and caregivers will be counselled on appropriate IYCF.
The project will be implemented in close coordination with Nutrition cluster. The geographic areas of implementation include Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. close coordination will be maintained to implement the interventions in the areas ranked from 3 to 5 by the nutrition survey.
The project will contribute to nutrition cluster objective 1 i.e. Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition.
A total of 60,000 children and PLW will receive support through the following activities:
1. Boys and girls and PLW will be screened for malnutrition through community outreach workers.
2. Boys and girls and PLW with moderate and severe acute malnutrition will be referred to the treatment sites.
3. caregivers including the PLW will be counselled on IYCF and use of micro nutrient supplements.
4. Boys and girls will receive MNP and Plumpy Doz -LNS.
5. PLW will receive iron folate tablets.
The nutrition supplies will be received from UNICEF/WFP as in kind.

Needs

As per Humanitarian need overview (HNO 2019) 4.7 million girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of under-nutrition and in need of preventive and curative nutrition services in 2019. 3.13 million girls and boys under 5 years of age require appropriate nourishment for adequate nutrition status while 1.59 million pregnant and lactating women require nutrition services. Around 91,811 girls and boys aged 6-59 months are acutely undernourished and if malnutrition is not prevented additional 146,898 children under the age of five years will become acutely malnourished during 2019. Nutrition survey data shows that across Syria there are 19,263 severely malnourished children who are eleven times more likely to die than well-nourished children. If not treated appropriately half of the severely malnourished children could die due to complications related to severe acute malnutrition. Continued conflict, displacement, shifts in control, and increasing returnees together with depletion of livelihoods, limited financial and physical access to food, unexploded hazard contamination have exacerbated vulnerability of the population in Syria. The limited access to quality water and sanitation, as well as suboptimal hygiene practices, may contribute to a high prevalence of childhood disease and illness. These factors negatively influence the nutrition situation, especially in the context of disrupted health services and coverage that characterizes the situation faced by many people living in hard-to-reach areas, overburdened communities, newly displaced or ‘newly accessible’. Combined, these factors have the potential to further jeopardize the health status of girls and boys under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), increasing the overall risk of under-nutrition as well as the short and long-term consequences impacting nutrition and health status such as stunting, growth faltering, morbidity, and mortality. Overall, nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive interventions as well as financial and human resources are critical to appropriately address the needs of the population in Syria.

Implementation of activities

60,000 boys and girls (6 – 59 months) and PLW will be screened for Malnutrition. 3,000 boys and girls (6 59 months) and PLW with moderate and severe acute malnutrition referred to the treatment sites. 30,000 caregivers (including PLW) counselled on appropriate IYCF. 40,000 boys and girls (6 59 months) who received micro-nutrient supplements (MNP). 20,000 Pregnant who received micro nutrients including iron folate tablets. 10,000 boys and girls aged 6-59 months reached with LNS for four months.

Protection Risk Analysis

Some of the identified protection risks include the following:I. Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Data on attacks against healthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA) during the period of January - September 2018 documented 134 attacks across Syria (47 attacks in Idleb and 10 in Hama), exceeding the incidents reported throughout the year 2017 (122 incidents). Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declined significantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers. II. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. It is important to strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels. III. Provision of nutrition support for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Services: The groups which are identified as the most vulnerable due to their inherent status that can be exacerbated by the ongoing crisis, 1) children under 5 (11%), 2) women of reproductive age (26%), 3) people living with disability PWD (15%), 4) internally displaced personnel IDPs and returnees (36%).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

some of the risks and their mitigation measures identified are as follows: 1. Qualified health staff leaving area.Mitigation Measure: IR will ensure rigorous coordination with the health directorates in order to find alternatives for the health staff. 2. Taxes imposed by non-state armed groups at check points inside Syria or at the border crossing. Mitigation Measure: IR remains vigilant of any such check points. Thereby to avoid these check points IR use alternative routes for the movement of project deliverable. Additionally, while delivering nutrition supplements, IR asks the hospital staff to accompany the trucks moving supplies from IR warehouse to the targeted facility/community to avoid any such check points. 3. Groups under UN security council sanction list may financially benefit from the project supplies passing through Syria-Turkey border crossing.Mitigation Measure: IR is implementing the similar project in same areas. The beneficiary receive support from IR without involvement of any intermediaries. At the project initiation all the relevant stakeholders (Health Directorate, Health facility, community etc) are informed of the project activities. Further IR Syria strong monitoring and project controls and documentation ensure that there are no external influences or diversion of support. IR will apply the mitigation for the protection risks mentioned above include following:1. IR will ensure continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the humanitarian leadership (cluster) in their efforts to highlight protection challenges facing health and nutrition workers. 2. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: Mitigation Measure:  Emplacement of effective complaint and feedback mechanism.  Effective recruitment and performance management of staff.  Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about IR, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the project it is implementing and what it intends to deliver. 3- equitable access: Inclusion of vulnerable groups needs throughout the program cycle (analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring) through gender, age, dis aggregated analysis of beneficiaries with identification of risk factors impacting service provision.  Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups  Encouraging that health/ nutrition service providers ensure accessibility to services by vulnerable groups (physical accessibility, dis-aggregated areas for women and girls, etc.)
