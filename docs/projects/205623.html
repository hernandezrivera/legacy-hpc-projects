Project Summary

With the civil wars in Sudan (Khartoum), many citizens of the 3 counties are returning to their villages. RRC and IRNA reports of August 2023 have indicated a sharp rise in the number of returnees estimated 12,816 individuals (Nasir= 5,188; Ulang=3,890). Fashoda, Nasir, and Ulang counties have continued to receive large numbers of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia, yet these counties have continued to suffer from violence, displacements due to floods, and various forms of shocks with unmentionable levels of loss of properties. The host communities in these 3 counties can hardly accommodate IDPs and returnees as their stocks of food are already bare.
The lack of food in terms of access and availability exacerbates the nutritional, health, WASH, and developmental outcomes of the community. Making them more vulnerable to further shocks that may come. GAM rates in Ulang and Nasir have GAM rate of 26.4% which are all in the crisis level. According to the IPC report of November 2023, Fashoda, Ulang, and Nasir are in IPC 4 for acute food insecurity.
Despite these challenges, these 3 counties have not been adequately prioritized with live saving food security and livelihoods services that would support the communities and improve their livelihoods. There is already a devastating lack of access to food due to scarcity and high prices in all the local markets, lack of agricultural tools and seeds in these Agro-pastoralist communities. Many development partners have not put in the necessary measures to prepare and equipe the community with necessary resilience tools and knowledge to be able to withstand the shocks that they face.
There is therefore great need to support communities in the 3 counties with Food Security and Livelihoods live-saving interventions that adequately address key factors of food insecurity and alleviate their livelihoods at all levels. This project therefore seeks to address such needs.
Activities:
UNKEA will support the provision of FSL interventions that saves lives, alleviates suffering of the communities and improves their coping mechanisms to all forms of shocks that they experience. These shall be done through the implementation of the Cluster prioritized activities that contributes to the set objectives as below:
Obj1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5)
Obj2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock.
Obj3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes
Support provision of food, non-food items to beneficiaries.
Distribution of agricultural inputs, vegetable seeds, and fishing kits to beneficiaries
Vaccination and treatment of livestock
Establishment of kitchen gardens at nutrition sites, schools, and households
Capacity building and training of farmers/beneficiaries on improved livelihood productivity, market development, and emergency actions.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face a political crisis which manifests in violent conflicts and insecurity associated with an economic crisis with 8.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. The food insecurity in the country is primarily triggered by the conflicts, climatic changes, such as prolonged dry spells, flooding that led to displacements of the people, returnees and persistent macroeconomic deterioration which led to high commodity. This has reduced on the households purchasing power as many lost their properties.According to IPC report of November2023, 5.83 million people (46%) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). An estimated 14,000 returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). With the civil wars in Sudan (Khartoum), many citizens of the 3 counties are returning to their villages. RRC and IRNA reports of August 2023 have indicated a sharp rise in the number of returnees estimated 12,816 individuals (Nasir= 5,188; Ulang=3,890). Fashoda, Nasir, and Ulang counties have continued to receive large numbers of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia, yet these counties have continued to suffer from violence, displacements due to floods, and various forms of shocks with unmentionable levels of loss of properties. The host communities in these 3 counties can hardly accommodate IDPs and returnees as their stocks of food are already bare. The lack of food in terms of access and availability exacerbates the nutritional, health, WASH, and developmental outcomes of the community. Making them more vulnerable to further shocks that may come. GAM rates in Ulang and Nasir have GAM rate of 26.4% which are all in the crisis level. According to the IPC report of November 2023, Fashoda, Ulang, and Nasir are in IPC 4 for acute food insecurity. Despite these challenges, these 3 counties have not been adequately prioritized with live saving food security and livelihoods services that would support the communities and improve their livelihoods. There is already a devastating lack of access to food due to scarcity and high prices in all the local markets, lack of agricultural tools and seeds in these Agro-pastoralist communities. Many development partners have not put in the necessary measures to prepare and equipe the community with necessary resilience tools and knowledge to be able to withstand the shocks that they face. There is therefore great need to support communities in the 3 counties with Food Security and Livelihoods live-saving interventions that adequately address key factors of food insecurity and alleviate their livelihoods at all levels. This project therefore seeks to address such needs.
