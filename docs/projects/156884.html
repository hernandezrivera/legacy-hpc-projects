Project Summary

The project aims to empower IDPs living in camps by providing them a source of stable income and encourage them to exit the camps. The project represents a pilot for Syria and it is in accordance with the HRP objectives (alleviate suffering, enhance protection of the most vulnerable and build resilience of communities). It is also subscribing to CCCM priority regarding decongestion of camps which must be seen as temporary and last resorts. The projects seeks to established 3 cooperatives within host communities (HC) close to formal camps, cooperatives which will organize and manage different initiatives related to entrepreneurship. Around 300 jobs will be created and services and products needed within camps and the hosting communities will be delivered.

Needs

The main problem for IDPs is finding a sustainable solution to generate income for their families and thus increasing their self-resilience. Many of the IDPs have no access to education with professions that can’t be exercised in the new context. Depleted of any assets and with their savings exhausted, households become aid dependent or adopt negative cooping strategies. It is the case for HCs also. Although camps are seen as a short time solutions for hosting IDPs, the protracted crisis from Syria demonstrated that people tend to remain stuck in the locations due to the lack of exit strategies to help them target a better status. WATAN proposes as rational of intervention the creation of 3 cooperatives which will host entrepreneurship initiatives providing training, tools and managing grants for people living in camps and HCs to ensure their financial sustainability. The action will decrease the burden placed on donors and NGOs providing aid, and it will swift the accent from emergency to medium term stabilization solution. In addition, will support reconciliation and integration among IDPs and HCs.

As a pre-condition for each cash transfer activity in a given location, WATAN determine if a cash transfer company is present and accessible, and willing to be contracted to ensure payments to beneficiaries. WATAN is aware that cash and asset transfers, and activities that aim at promoting equitable access of vulnerable groups, including PWD/Is, women, etc. can provoke a backlash at the community level and/or changes in household dynamics that increase protection risks, such as, jealousies or increased levels of GBV. Field teams engage with local stakeholders in discussions on necessary actions for creating an enabling environment.
