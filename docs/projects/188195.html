Project Summary

Many women, men, and youth in the targeted communities are looking for employment opportunities to become self-reliant and be able to support themselves and their families, however, with the lack of needed capacity, educational background, or practical experience, it is difficult to succeed in finding a proper employment opportunity while obtaining these mandatory skills and experiences requires time and financial resources.
Small and medium business owners, on the other hand, seek to employ staff members with either related educational backgrounds or practical experience to obtain the highest levels of effectiveness and prevent waste of time and effort to train inexperienced candidates, especially in the current economic situation.
The project is considered as the linking pin between most vulnerable community members and potential employers to integrate them in the labor market system through paid apprenticeships opportunities over four months duration that allows them to obtain tailored technical employability skills that eventually ends with paid recruitment either with the same employer, another or even to establish their own business when relevant.
Apprenticeships are traditionally the way in which the trades have trained their workforce, through on-the-job training in the workplace with qualified workers or master craftsmen as instructors and mentors. Employers, business associations, and joint labor-management organizations, known collectively as apprenticeship sponsors, provide apprentices with instruction that reflects industry needs. Apprenticeship sponsors pay most of the training costs, and apprentices are paid during their training period.
The project considers immediate support to vulnerable groups in the community where they will be directly integrated into the labor market system gaining in-job skills without the need to go through theoretical capacity building programs that may not guarantee employment opportunities, or may not be required by the labor market, or even won’t include practical or on-job training which is the essential part that grants career and experience building.
The approach that this activity is seeking is significant due to the fact that it is directly supporting beneficiaries with the minimum cost possible and in a timely manner in addition to securing income generation solutions.
On the other hand, having an apprenticeship sponsor is a direct benefit for the employer too, with the struggles that SMEs are facing within the uncountable challenges in North-west Syria, one of the main inputs for business growth is to have cost-effective human resources as full or part-time staff to increase the productivity of that business. And by this activity, we can mobilize up to 500 apprentice workers to be engaged in at least 250 Small and Medium enterprise (SME) businesses without having the employer pay any salary during the apprenticeship program.
And in return to the apprentice workers will receive a monthly cash-voucher with an amount equivalent to the minimum known wage in line with the estimation of SMEB cost in Northwest Syria (Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket) based on the recommendation of the Cash Working Group.
Finally, hosting small-medium enterprises that will host the apprentice workers won't only benefit from upscaled human resources within their businesses but will also be targeted with a light rehabilitation component for their facility mainly with enhanced access for people with disabilities.

4 - Needs

Based on the first phase of Emissa’s Apprenticeship Program 2020, a post-evaluation of the project was conducted in early 2021 to check the feedback on the project technical modality and its results, the research included 127 apprentice workers and 49 SMEs in 3 different geographical locations. 39% in Killi, 46% in Batabu, and 15% in Hazano. One of the significant outcomes is that 40% of the targeted apprentice workers secured a job post-intervention, 95% of them were contracted by the same employer that hosted them through the apprenticeship program. 85% of the apprentice workers think that have become more empowered to secure a job opportunity after they finished their apprenticeship program. 68% of SME owners stated that their business has grown more than expected because of the human resources upscale during the program. In addition, an updated assessment was conducted recently targeting 112 businesses in North Idleb and Western Aleppo areas 95% of the respondents believe that upscaling human assets in their businesses would absolutely support their work to enhance, and 87.5% of them are facing financial challenges that prevent them from recruiting new staff. HNO 2022: With a focus on those living in areas hosting large numbers of IDPs and returnees and/or where access to basic services and livelihoods are significantly reduced, as well as those particularly affected by the socio-economic deterioration and growing food insecurity in particular.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Within Emissa’s apprenticeship program, special consideration has been given to effectively targeting GBV survivals using referral pathways from a peer partner (Woman Support Association) and from other humanitarian actors part of the protection cluster specialized in GBV cases, to be part of the planned targeted beneficiaries.In addition, Emissa will coordinate with the protection cluster and especially with another peer partner (SENED) to ensure the inclusion and engagement of persons with disabilities. Our engagement plan is not limited to targeting those beneficiaries but also, we will facilitate other activities under this grant to ensure their safe access to project activities as demonstrated in the project implementation plan under several activities especially rehabilitation of certain infrastructures to remove all barriers that prevent them from accessing the existing local labor market.Steps that will be taken by Emissa to mitigate the challenge of including PWD in business sites:1: SME selection criteria, additional score will be granted to business owners that will accept physical disabled people.2: Only medium businesses will be focused on hosting PWD, those who have the capacity to host around 3 apprentice workers at the same time. 3: Light rehabilitation that is offered to the business owners will include actions related to transforming the business facility to become PWD friendly.4: Awareness raising on the importance of inclusion.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level. The targeted communities and learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures. The organization’s staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. Organizations should ensure a survivor-centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person. The survivor should have a say in the process. Poor handling of allegations of sexual explanation and abuse can put the accuser/survivor, alleged perpetrator and others at risk. If you need support in dealing with PSEA issues, please contact OCHA who has technical PSEA experts.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Security of staff remains of utmost importance and work will be suspended in case of serious threats.2- Evacuation and Security Plan for operations inside Syria is shared among all staff and updated regularly.3- Risk assessments, risk matrix, situation scenarios, and mitigation measures are being reviewed on annual basis.4- The field team is directly engaged in security reports. 5- All security update platforms are monitored by the safety and security committee, through INSO, HAT reports, and other platforms. 6- COVID-19: preventive measures for both Turkey and Syria offices were designed and distributed to all staff with serious consideration towards any violation of any neglection towards the measures that are adapted from the United Nations’ WHO.7- Aid diversion: The two main components of the program (Apprenticeship and CfW) both targets beneficiaries directly through a 3rd party financial service provider to ensure no diversion. 8- Community Tension: Selection criteria will be shared and announced transparently throughout the implementation period. 9- Abuse in the workplace: Complaints hotlines and channels will be available for any feedback from the beneficiaries, in addition to the regular spot checks that will be conducted during the activity period. 10- CVA Risks: the project plan and implementation will refer and take serious consideration to the "CfW Minimum Recommended Actions Guidance for NWS" that was developed jointly by ERL and CWG partners.
