Project Summary

Under this project, NRC will provide non-formal education and psychosocial support through hybrid modalities including paper-based distributions, in-person programming and online learning for children and youth experiencing disrupted learning. Each approach is presented in respect to guidelines implemented to prevent the spread of COVID-19. NRC will train local teachers and community members to facilitate non-formal and first phase education response programming, recreational activities, and psychosocial support through NRC’s Better Learning Programme. Planned interventions will aim to keep children and youth connected to learning, maintain the habit of learning, as well as ensure they are protected while learning.

1 - Needs

Despite a delayed school opening in December 2019, the 2019-2020 academic school year was later suspended in mid-March 2020 for 1.3 million school-aged children in an effort to prevent the spread of COVID-19. All children were sent home with an uncertain plan for reopening or return to learning. The lack of basic services such as internet and electricity prevent more than 81% of children, from participating in formal learning opportunities offered by the MoE. According to household surveys conducted by the October 2020 MSNA, roughly 32% of children did not attend the 2019-2020 school year, higher than the 3% reported the previous year. This disruption in education has the potential to severely impact the academic and social emotional development of children and youth. Students will need remedial support as they re-enroll or are at an increased risk of dropping out. Teacher strikes over low pay delayed the start of the 2019-2020 academic school year and the early closure as a result of COVID-19 has severely disrupted children and youth’s access to and continuity of learning. Teachers will require capacity building to meet the academic and social-emotional needs of children and youth matriculated to the next grade with little preparation when students return for the 2020-2021 academic school year. Teachers will also need ongoing support for their own well-being given increased anxieties, strengthening existing teaching strategies and applying new techniques to support the potential for continued distance, or remote learning. Additionally, the most recent data from IOM shows around 25 schools being used as shelters across the country, with the majority in the greater Tripoli area; moreover, 43 schools were reported as destroyed and 209 schools as partially damaged nationwide. Across the country, shifting students from one school to another as displacement occurs has contributed to classroom overcrowding and will further inhibit the social distancing needed to safely return to school after the onset of COVID-19.

2 - Activities

(1) NRC will provide non-formal education and psychosocial support through multiple channels available, whether through in-person, online, or paper-based distributions to those unable to access or are not enrolled in formal education opportunities. Academic programming will be tailored from the formal Libyan curriculum and NRC basic literacy and numeracy materials to meet the needs of beneficiaries. Psychosocial support will be mainstreamed through all activities and intervention modalities using NRC’s Better Learning Programme in order to improve learning capacity and stimulate the children and youth’s resilience and natural recovery process. NRC will also work with existing Child Protection actors to refer cases of clinical trauma or severe psychosocial distress to partner agencies active in implementation areas. (2) NRC will provide training for local teachers and community members on INEE’s Teacher in Crisis Contexts (TiCC) training pack with emphasis on well-being and child-centered pedagogy, and the Better Learning Programme. In preparation for continued movement restrictions and social distancing in response to COVID-19, NRC will also provide additional sessions on distance learning relevant to the context.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.4 Project results in increased exposure to C-19 transmissionNRC anticipates implementation of programming such as non-formal education and teacher training through remote modalities such as online channels popular among the target beneficiaries or paper-based distributions in respect to social distancing guidelines. Beneficiaries participating in any in-person programming or recipients of paper-based programming will be expected to respect social distancing guidelines as well as used PPE supplied by NRC. Online modalities, when access is possible, will reduce the risk completely as beneficiaries are able to participate from the safety of their homes. 4.22 Lack of access to/awareness of referral servicesNRC has established referral pathways with existing child protection actors in the areas in which NRC operates. While NRC provides psyschosocial support through the Better Learning Programme and teaching staff are trained on identifying cases of concerns, given that programming is anticipated to be held remotely, parents and caregivers will be provided a brief induction at registration of additional services to support their children that can be made available.
