Project Summary

IOM CCCM activities plan to contribute to all the four CCCM Sector objectives for 2021 and to improve the coordination of humanitarian lifesaving, multi-sectoral assistance.
The project aims to address the Objective 1 through:
• Maintaining and managing three Reception Centres (RCs);
• Maintaining and managing eleven Planned Camps (PCs);
• Implementing small scale care and maintenance activities in 25 informal sites; and
• Identifying and collecting information about IDP sites in Idleb and Rural Aleppo, including multi-sectoral gap analysis and referral to IOM Cross-Border Unit for multi-sectoral response and assistance.
The project plans to meet the Objective 2 through:
• Enhancing camp management capacity of camp management in informal settlements in Idleb and Rural Aleppo under the Site Management Support (SMS) project; and
• Enhancing camp management capacity of NGOs through the provision of remote CWC and community engagement trainings for humanitarian workers based in Syria.
The project would contribute to the Objective 3 through:
• Enhancing communal capacity of informal settlements in Idleb and Rural Aleppo to address fire risk through the provision of fire safety measures and training under the SMS project.
Finally, the project aims to meet the objective 4 through:
• Collecting information on IDP movements and displacements in Idleb and Rural Aleppo and reporting to Cluster members.
The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with CCCM Cluster Coordinator, by IOM Turkey cross border team based in Gaziantep. IOM will work in partnership with well experienced local NGOs in each specific activity in camps and camp-like settings as implementing partners, selected from active members of the CCCM Cluster.
In case of underfunding, IOM CCCM will prioritize the support of the affected community in the 11 planned camps by ensuring the continuation of multi-sectoral response and including strengthening the community governance, care and maintenance, fire safety and camp management capacity. Currently, the 11 camps are hosting highly vulnerable beneficiaries selected during the project designing and still in need of humanitarian support.
The continuation of CM activities in receptions centres will be highly impacted by the reduction of funding. In that case, IOM CCCM will work closely with both partners and beneficiaries to identify better shelter solutions for the affected community hosted in RCs. This will require assessment on beneficiaries’ intentions and ‘needs along with the identification of viable shelter solutions before closing the centres.
Additionally, the reduction of CCCM activities in informal sites will likely increase their vulnerability- having already been suffering from the lack of or limited humanitarian assistance- IOM will be unable to continue the coordination of an integrated response in case of a fire or flood incident, including referral to protection or health partners. This will also affect IOM's ability to provide training in first aid and site risk reduction, including distribution of fire and first aid equipment, to the affected community living in informal and self-settled sites.

4 - Needs

There are 1.6 million IDPs living in last resort camps, informal settlements, and transit and collective centres, the majority of whom are in Idleb Governorate (CCCM Cluster, Feb 2021). In NWS, 64% of people displaced in March 2021 moved to camps, while the remainder were displaced to non-camp settings. Furthermore, 90% of the 1,379 active IDP sites in NWS are self-settled and lack organized camp management systems. IOM’s 11 planned camps currently host a total of 29,200 vulnerable IDPs. RCs have hosted 66,740 IDPs (monthly cumulative sum) since their establishments in late 2017 until the end of 2019. Since the beginning of 2020 RCs have stopped receiving new arrivals following COVID-19 mitigation measures. Only very vulnerable cases were accepted in Azaz and Al Bab, including those affected by recent airstrikes, floods and fire incidents. In 2021, an objective for IOM is to find better shelter solutions, particularly for IDPs who have been living inside RCs for more than 9 months. Compared to formal camps, collective centres, and RCs, informal settlements have intermittent or non-existent basic service provision and poorer standards of sanitation and housing infrastructure. This can be attributed to people’s needs not being regularly captured or referred to partners and clusters for a sustainable response, causing deterioration of protection and increased vulnerability amongst the most affected communities. Many protection risks are impacted by Syrians’ direct exposure to hostilities, limited reach of humanitarian actors to informal IDP sites, and the low awareness of community actors on IASC standards, IHL, humanitarian principles, protection and the role and responsibilities of camp management among duty bearers.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM will work with experienced NGOs selected from active members of the CCCM Cluster. Based on feedback from the affected population the project will be modified as much as possible to better address the needs of camp residents. Gender and age are key vulnerability criteria for beneficiary selection in some activities. IOM will continue implementing capacity-building targeting IPs’ staff and site committees to improve AAP and mainstream protection in all camp management activities. IOM will focus on strengthening community engagement to enable camp populations to participate in the decision-making process as well as to play an active role in camp management, including their inclusion in fire safety activities, hygiene promotion sessions, and COVID-19 preparedness and response. IOM will also launch an Area Based Approach targeting the most vulnerable self-settled sites. IOM will assess the needs of the most affected communities, engage with multiple and diverse stakeholders, provide small-scale care and maintenance activities, coordinate the provision of multi-sectoral support, and support training to mitigate risks. IOM will work closely with the IOM Protection and Access Units to target programme duty bearers and community leaders across NWS with ad hoc capacity building to raise awareness on International Humanitarian Law (IHL), International Human Rights Law, IASC standards, humanitarian principles, humanitarian access, protection, CCCM, and PSEA. Teams will also conduct structured site-based assessments with data collection on adherence to IASC standards which will be regularly reported to relevant Clusters. Finally, IOM will conduct consultations with local authorities to enable policy and institutional change.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Continued operations of planned camps, reception centers, and any possible coordinated relocation assistance will create negative pull and push factors for IDPs. 2. Needs assessments or intentions surveys will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs living in camps and contribute to assessment fatigue. 3. COVID-19 breaks out in NWS and spreads among the population in Planned camps and RCs without the ability to manage a huge caseload of cases. 4. The management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities. 5: Equipping and training emergency responders and IDP committees in IDP sites will let individual residents over-rely on the trained responders and committees for responding to disasters and shirk individual responsibility. Mitigating measures 1. Maintain the CCCM guidance and position on camp expansion and establishment and camp support ensuring that camps are the last resort. 2. Ensure that the intention surveys and needs assessments are as light as possible and inform operational guidance. 3. Several measures for COVID-19 risk mitigation are being applied and will be enhanced throughout the project period (including social distancing measures, coordination with health and WASH partner, disinfection, and more). 4. Ensure that any management structure is representative of the entire community with strong selection criteria. 5. Incorporate general awareness-raising focused on the individual capacities of men, women, and children; ensure that partners work in line with humanitarian principles and follow the principles of Do No Harm; and apply the IOM AAP frameworks and work to build the capacity of project partners in AAP throughout the project.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

For all activities, the risk of SEA by IPs and their sub-contractors exists during the physical implementation phase. For this, key mitigation measures include having all IPs ensure that their staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct, have taken part in PSEA training, have a dedicated complaint mechanism in place, and that TPM teams monitor to ensure activities are carried out in a principled manner.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Staff mobility and access restrictions to affected population: Active fighting, shifting of the conflict lines and potential closure of the border might prevent delivery of humanitarian assistance. IPs are encouraged to report any security and access-related issues and to develop and update their evacuation/contingency plans. An assumption is that COVID-19 cases are promptly isolated to avoid the risk of widespread outbreak in PCs and RCs. Several measures for COVID-19 risk mitigation are being applied and will be enhanced throughout the project period based on the CCCM Cluster COVID-19 guidance. There is an assumption that local stakeholders respect humanitarian facilities in line with the land agreement signed with the related Local Councils which guarantees the utilization and access of the sites. Potential risks are that local stakeholders disregard their commitment of non-interference in camp management activities and/or take control of the site. IOM through the IP will regularly engage with the local council to mitigate this risk. Potential access issues will be shared and followed up in coordination with OCHA Access WG. Contingency areas in other locations will be pre-identified with the CCCM cluster during project design if possible. ‘Duty of care’ strategy in place for local partners. IPs are encouraged to report any incidents of aid diversion and interference incidents by the armed groups. IOM utilizes third-party monitoring mechanisms to conduct warehouse verification, distribution, and post-monitoring distribution activities to mitigate the risk. Recruitment of female staff will be promoted. HLP Due Diligence Guidelines has been/will be used to minimize the risks associated with land ownership.
