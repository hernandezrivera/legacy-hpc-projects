Project Summary

The program is a continuation of existing IMC activities through static clinics and medical mobile teams.
- Masakeh Barzeh static clinic is operational since 2008, Jaramana and Sanamayn clinics since 2013. Barzeh was established to respond to the needs of the Iraqi refugees, while Jaramana and Sanamayn targeted conflict affected population.
- Mobile clinics are deployed in different areas based on assessed needs; current areas of intervention are 8 villages in Nashabiye (since October 2018); Babella, Yalda and Beit Sahem (since January 2019); Medira, Misraba and Beit Sawa (since May 2019); shelters and open communities in Dummar, Nahr Eshe and Al Zahera (since June 2019). Additional 2 mobiles are planned to be deployed in Zamalka and Sabqa based on needs assessments conducted during the last quarter of 2019 (pending approval).
IMC plans for 2020 are to continue provision of comprehensive PHC services to most affected population including hosting communities, displaced and returnees through its static clinics (existing) and mobile medical teams (5 operational, 2 planned), while also expand its work by providing support to PHC public facilities through capacity building, provision of supplies and equipment. While the specific support to public facilities will be determined based on findings of needs assessments, IMC will seek to prioritize newly accessible areas and areas assessed with highest gaps in primary healthcare service provision and will coordinate with other humanitarian actors (either bilaterally or through the cluster) in order to complement and avoid overlapping.
Although specific services vary based on the facility (either static or mobile), generally IMC provides the following services - related to preventive and curative healthcare for both communicable and non-communicable diseases: internal medicine, pediatrics, gynecology, ENT, ophthalmology, dental care, provision of medications, basic lab services, health education and malnutrition screening. Services such as gynecology, pediatrics and screening for malnutrition target specifically women and children. Some of the facilities (2 static and 3 mobiles) also provide case management for MH and GBV related cases, with available advanced support as needed.
Through private service providers, IMC financially support referrals to advanced care (consultations, surgeries, specialized diagnostic services) for most vulnerable population and provides mobility/disability NFIs (including hearing aids, wheelchairs, crutches, diapers, medical consumables) to people in need, based on internal SOPs and available budget. Selection criteria for the referrals include: socio-economic vulnerability; necessity and duration of the suggested treatment; concomitant diseases and age; feasibility and evidence base of the treatment plan; prognosis. For provision of NFIs, criteria vary based on the item to be provided, but all require IMC doctor to examine the patient.
All services at IMC clinics, both static and mobiles, are provided free of charge to all population. On average among all facilities, female represent 60 to 70% of the total beneficiaries served; children below 5 years of age are approximately 15% and beneficiaries below 18 years of age about 30%. Consultations to people with disability amount to approximately 2% of total consultations. Based on previous trends, approximately 65% of the consultations are related to NCDs.
IMC is part of the health cluster as well as the MHPSS sub-group and shares regularly 4Ws regarding implemented activities both to Health and Nutrition clusters as well as IMCI monthly reports. IMC also coordinates with the MoH regarding the Early Warning and Response System (EWARS), aimed at strengthening the national surveillance system and early detect epidemic threats.
The proposed response is in line with the sector priorities for 2020, overall focusing on sustaining and expanding quality healthcare services at primary, secondary and tertiary levels.

4 - Needs

As per preliminary findings of the assessments conducted to inform the HNO 2020, the main gaps in the health sector continue to be the result of a combination of factors which overall hinder availability and quality of the services provided: partial or total damages of the health facilities, severe shortages of staff, medical supplies, equipment and pharmaceuticals. In addition, a large majority of the population faces financial challenges in accessing private services and can hardly afford medications, including those needed to ensure continuity of care in the case of chronic diseases. Constraints related to distance, cost of transportation and lack of documentation represent further barriers to access basic healthcare services. Urban areas (including Damascus) have a high severity rate also because they host most of the internally displaced population. EWARS/EWARN reports indicate that influenza-like illness, acute diarrhea, cutaneous leishmaniosis and suspected hepatitis are the most common morbidities. Quality education, prevention and treatment services offered at PHC level are thus essential to ensure continuity of care; PHC health facilities also constitute a crucial entry point for advanced/secondary health care, reducing the risk of further deterioration of the health conditions and ultimately contributing in decreasing morbidity, mortality and permanent disabilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

1) IMC will continue providing comprehensive PHC services in its existing 3 static clinics and 5 medical mobile teams, as well as support for advanced health care services (surgeries, specialized consultations and lab services). 2) MH/GBV Case management will continue to be available in Barzeh and Jaramana (with specialized MH support through psychologists and psychiatrists) and 3 mobile teams. 3) Provision of disability/mobility consumable and devices (diapers, crutches, wheelchairs, walkers, hearing aids, etc.).4) Trainings and supportive supervisions to IMC staff and, as requested, to MoH staff. Topics include among others: chronic diseases in children, mhGAP, IPC, CMR, Rational Use of Drugs, medical triage. 5) Support to public facilities will be based on discussions between IMC and MOH on priority locations and findings of needs assessments. Once the locations of interventions are selected, IMC will proceed with procurement and dispatch of supplies and equipment, and will coordinate with relevant stakeholders for other type of support (such as supportive supervision, capacity building) that can be provided.6) For the 2 additional medical mobile teams, once approvals are obtained IMC will proceed with recruitment of staff and all needed logistic arrangements.7) Aside from regular supportive supervision, the following monitoring exercises are conducted as feasible (some do not apply for mobile teams):- Quarterly monitoring checks of diagnoses and treatments;- Monthly monitoring of medication consumption through IMC Pharmacy Information System (PIS);- Joint (health and MEAL) quarterly monitoring visits, using a customized monitoring tool to evaluate performance in key areas of operation. - Patient satisfaction surveys are conducted by IMC MEAL team through visits or by phone, in addition to PDM for beneficiaries receiving disability/mobility related consumable and/or devices and referral services.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1. Lack of quality services expose beneficiaries to harm and deterioration of their health conditionsLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) Regular supportive supervision and monitoring on quality of health care provided in IMC facilities; 2) capacity building activities; 3) Effective feedback and complaints mechanisms in place. Risk 2: Violence against health careLikelihood: Low/Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) close collaboration and information sharing with UNDSS and other NGOs security focal points; 2) temporary suspension of services in case of increased likelihood of security related incidents Risk 3: PSEA and safeguarding related risksLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) IMC policies and procedures in place, including effective, safe and confidential reporting mechanisms; 2) All staff provided with training and refresher training on PSEA and safeguarding issues. Risk 4: inequitable access to health care, including for PWDsLikelihood: low. Impact: Medium/High Mitigation measures: 1) facilities open to everyone and services provided free of charge; 2) removal of physical barriers for PWDs to access healthcare; 3) deployment of mobile teams to reach population unable to travel to static health facilities; 4) gender balanced staffing operating in static and mobile clinics.
