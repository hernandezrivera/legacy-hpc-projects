Project Summary

SADO’s proposed response is designed in alignment with the Food Security priorities and objectives focusing on livelihood support and food assistance using food vouchers targeting support to both urban centers of displaced persons and preventing displacement in rural areas in Galgaduud and Gedo region. SADO will provide extremely vulnerable HHs with unconditional food vouchers to redeem the food items from food vendors in functioning markets. SADO will build on its successful experience implementing similar SHF funded project in Gedo and Galgaduud regions in Somalia. Each selected household will receive monthly food voucher equivalent to 100% MEB for 3 months. The MEB is based on Cash Working Group (CWG) recommendations. The targeted beneficiaries are the weakest and most vulnerable families that are not able to meet the MEB or are not able to meet the minimum living threshold of 2100Kcal per person per day. As well, appropriate farm inputs (seed and farm tools and equipment) will be provided to riverine farmers recently affected by floods in Gedo to restore and protect their livelihoods. For households who are food insecure but can participate in labour work, the project will engage them in CFW activities of rehabilitating community assets. SADO’s response will help stabilize crisis affected communities and prevent populations from migrating to urban centres in search of food and other services.

Needs

FEWSNET/FSNAU Sept. report indicates 82,000 people are in IPC 3&4 in Galgaduud region. The latest assessment by SADO and partners through FGDs done separately to ensure that the views of women & men were captured in 3 districts in the region, indicate that households (HHs) level food insecurity continues to deepen despite the above average rainfall received, and most rural and urban HHs are in borderline or poor food consumption categories (FCS) respectively. The IDP HHs (some recently displaced from Dacdher) are the most affected - 60% having poor or borderline FCS. HHs continues to survive on less diversified meals of less than 5 food groups’ further undermining HHs nutritional status. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence is at 14% and 1.6% respectively. Rural and urban HHs respectively have low & medium dietary diversity and high CSI. The existing FCS; limited dietary diversity of less than 5 food groups indicate existing food gaps which is characteristic of population in IPC 3. In Gedo region, 40,000 people are in IPC 3&4. Following the floods in Bardera, assessment conducted by ACTED and SADO (May 2018), found out that flood affected close to 2,000HHs, many of whom are IDPs. The food MEB for Bardera increased by 20% between March &May as a result of rising food prices in the local markets. Farms were covered by flood water; and water pumps swept away causing heavy loss of crop and farm products that would have been harvested at the end of May. The situation is expected to worsen given the massive loss of crop and thus little or no harvest is expected for most households that depend on agriculture for livelihoods.

Activities

1. Conduct livelihoods and food security needs assessment in targeted areas to determine the gaps and needs. FGDs will be done separately to ensure that the views of women and men were captured. 2. Unconditional food vouchers to 2000 households (12000 persons) who will be receiving a monthly food voucher valued at 100% MEB for a period of 3 months.3. Conditional cash transfers to beneficiaries who will be participating in CFW activities - A total of 1200 households (7200 persons) will participate in CFW activities for rehabilitation of 12 water catchments and benefit from 30 days of CFW opportunities at a daily value of USD 5 or the daily labour rates of the area. 4. Provision of agricultural seeds and farming hand tools to 1000 riverine and agro-pastoral farmers (6000 persons) and provision of irrigation pumps and start up fuels to 50 riverine farmers (240 persons) in Bardera districts in Gedo Region giving specific consideration to farm families headed by women-led families.5. Provision of capacity building, training, knowledge transfer on disaster risk management, and utilization of natural resources - 12 Water Users’ Committees will be trained to enable them manage the water resources effectively.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

SADO ceased operating in areas under the control of the non-state controlled militia groups. In the targeted project areas under the control of government in Galgaduud and Gedo regions. SADO has been having unrestricted access and has not faced any security challenges that may hinder resilience / durable solutions in the project locations. SADO will monitor the security situation on daily basis and if possible adjust outreach schedules as needed while consulting with government institutions and local communities through Village Relief Committees. During project launch and design, SADO will engage the local actors and stakeholders to discuss the long term interventions in the project areas. As such, the livelihood component of the project is intended to bring back the livelihoods of the farmers who lost pumps and tools during the flooding. The provision of farm tools is therefore considered a long term solution for improving food security and ensuring resilience. The beneficiaries will also be informed of their entitlement during project sensitization and mobilization sessions. The VRCs will inform the beneficiaries about the entitlements and a complain and feedback mechanisms will be established. As well, MOU's will signed with the community for asset rehabilitation. SADO will ensure a proper clan balance in the VRC as well as a gender balance.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

SADO mainstreams protection principles into all programs by incorporating protection concerns from assessment to implementation of the interventions. The Do No Harm approach utilized by SADO creates harmony between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. This is achieved through awareness raising and joint targeting and selection of the most vulnerable HHs to participate in the project. SADO maintains consistent and accurate messaging about selection criteria, resource availability, prioritization of assistance, and recognition of the need at the community level to reduce misunderstandings and to maintain stability in the project implementation area.Protection measures will be implemented throughout the project ensuring the needs of men and women, the elderly, children, and the disabled are taken into account. These measures include, among others:• Sex and age disaggregated data as well as conditions of vulnerability;• Sensitizing the CoC regarding Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (SEA) to project staff and RCs.• Beneficiaries will be sensitized to the nature of the assistance & an accountability system will be established for feedback and reporting;• The timing and location of beneficiary registration will be organized to accommodate women’s roles as caregivers and the needs of vulnerable groups;• All project monitors will be trained on protection issues.Furthermore, SADO has adopted CoC and therefore will ensure that all its staff have been sensitized on and have signed CoC, which is consistent with the six core principles of the IASC Task Force on PSEA including implications for violations. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the CoC and will benefit from sexual exploitation and abuse prevention messaging, and will receive information on report violation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

SADO will ensured that the protection environment is maintained by utilizing the checklist developed by the Somalia Food Security Cluster (FSC) members. Activities will be implemented in a non-discriminatory and impartial manner that promotes the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance. This will be supported by the establishment of the local village relief committees. SADO will ensure that targeting focuses on vulnerable populations, including persons with disabilities and older people who are particularly susceptible to food insecurity, through unconditional food assistance using vouchers.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

SADO has adequate information on the proposed project areas and that there are no concerns of security. However, SADO security officer always gathers information on any threat that may hamper the operations of the organization. Besides, any cash payments to the beneficiaries will be conducted through money transfer companies and hence the risk is transferred to the company and there will be no risk of SADO moving around with cash that can cause some sort of risk. And importantly, all SADO staff are sensitized and trained on the organizations risk and security systems and mechanisms of reporting any imminent threat.SADO ceased operating in areas under the control of the non-state controlled militia groups. However, in specific locations under the control of the government in Galgaduud and Gedo regions, SADO has been having unrestricted access and has not faced any security challenges. In addition, SADO has worked in Abudwak, Balambale and Adaado districts in the same region responding to the 2011 famine and understands the dynamics of the locals as well as ethnicity. SADO will monitor the security situation on daily basis and adjust outreach schedules as needed. While daily work schedules may be adjusted during the project period, SADO is confident that all activities in the proposed intervention will be accomplished on time. In particularly high risk environment, SADO brings its skills in risk management to the project.
