Project Summary

World Relief Sudan (WRS) is currently providing integrated humanitarian emergency response, recovery and development programs in health, nutrition, education, protection, peace building, WASH and FSL activities funded bilateral and multilateral donors including SHF, OFDA, DFID, SIDA, UNICEF, WFP, MoFA of Netherlands and private donors.
This Food security and livelihoods (FSL) project is designed with the objective of saving lives and building resilience of the most vulnerable HHs and reducing acute food insecurity and malnutrition through ensuring availability of nutritious food to 88,854 HHs in nine localities in the three states including El Kurmuk, Geissan Baw localities of Blue Nile, El Geniena, Beida and Kreinik in West Darfur and Nirtiti, RoKero and Gollo of Central Darfur. In alignment with the FSL sector objectives, the project also focuses on two major areas. The first focus area is that ensuring conflict and other disaster affected people in the three states including IPDs, returnees, host communities and nomads receive timely, adequate and required assistance during and in the aftermath of a shock. The other focus area is improving availability and accessibility to sufficient, quality and nutritious food to individuals impacted by protracted crisis. To achieve these objectives, World Relief (WR) conducted a comprehensive assessment in Blue Nile. The assessment includes community level discussion, field visit, discussion with government line ministries, UN agenies, NGOs operating the area and review of various data. In West and Central Darfur WR conducted community based participatory planning and project plan has been prepared with active involvement of the community and activities are prioritized for implementation based on community priorities and triangulated with data from different sources. IDPs, returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities are the central focus of the project. Provision of agricultural inputs, improving livestock veterinary services & water schemes, income generating activities, various capacity buildings, saving and loan schemes, restocking, promotion of FFA activities and gender & environment are the main focus areas.
Even though women are heads for almost half of the households, they are often hindered by many traditional norms from making real progress which make it difficult for them to access new agricultural technologies as well as improved livelihood opportunities. This project will mainly target women in order to build their capacities thus allowing them to sufficiently increase their productivity beyond consumption.
The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups such as women, girls and minority groups. While implementing this project, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights.
All interventions will be done in partnership with government line ministries, community-based committees, local authorities, community leaders and beneficiaries themselves. As described further below, interventions are designed to meet immediate emergency needs of the beneficiaries mainly women, girls and children while employing approaches that increase community ownership and thus sustainability.

1 - Needs

According to Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC 2021), an estimated 7.1 million people face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and need urgent action to save lives. 51% are women and 49 % men. The figure, the highest ever recorded in the history of the IPC analysis in the country, represents an increase of 22 per cent if compared with the same period last year. At least 1.3 million people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 4), while around 5.8million people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 3). Another 17.4million people are estimated to be under stress (IPC 2) and any additional shock could push them to severe levels of hunger. Some localities in areas affected by flooding have shifted to higher levels of food insecurity compared to June 2020 projection; five localities shifted from minimal IPC phase 1 to phase 2, while 10 localities shifted from stress (IPC Phase 2) to crisis levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3). Few localities reported that 5-10 percent of the affected population has shifted to emergency levels of food insecurity (phase 4). Based on various food security assessments, the most vulnerable groups are IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities. The highest severity of acute food insecurity is recorded in the 61 localities classified as IPC phase 3 with one locality under phase 4, North Jebel Marra in Central Darfur. Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in the targeted nine localities of Blue Nile (Kurmuk, Damazine and Bau), Central Darfur (Nirtiti, RoKero and Gollo) and West Darfur (El Geniena, Beida and Kreinik) due to increased and protracted displacement, economic decline, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high inflation rates. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population. Based on the IPC, the three localities in West Darfur, three localities in Blue Nile and two localities of Central Darfur (Gareb and Wasat Jebel Marra) face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3). The community of Shamal Jebel Marra are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (Phase 4). In 2020, WR conducted a rapid assessment in Blue Nile, West Darfur and Central Darfur states in collaboration with local communities, local implementing partners and stakeholders. The findings of the assessments and other sources of information reveal the following problems that contributed to food insecurity: • Trends show unpredictability of rainfall patterns in the localities with adverse effect on crop production, pasture development for animals, and market prices which undermine efforts of vulnerable people (women, children and other people with special needs); • Poor production of crops due to lack of productive seeds, , agricultural technologies • Lack of food/nutritional diversification due to the major use of Sorghum/millet as staple food, instead of diversifying with protein and vitamin sources (legumes, vegetable, milk/meat, etc) • Poor post-harvest • Inadequate agricultural/livestock extension services .Project beneficiaries per age group:• West Darfur 154,465 (38618 children, 23169 Youth, 61785 Women and 30893 Men).• Central Darfur 165,015 (41754 children, 25052 Youth, 66806 Women and 33403 Men).• Blue Nile 122,788 (30697 children, 18418 Youth, 49115 Women and 24558 Men).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project provides maximum care not to cause pollute the environment. For activities having an impact on the environment, the project design required mitigation measures, which will be implemented parallel to project activities implementation. Environmental markers guideline was reviewed to ensure that the project is designed sensitive to the environment and necessary mitigation measures will be to reduce the risks. The project will not encourage the use of toxic chemicals and commercial fertilizers, rather the communities will be trained and supported to apply biological and cultural pest control and organic farming. To ensure environmental compliance and adhere to no-do-harm principles WR will provide training on integrated water resources management and create awareness to the community on environment. The irrigation facilities and the hand dug wells for livestock will be accompanied with drainage ditches to avoid water stagnation. The project works together with the MOA so that the different types of inputs provided to the community pass through quality check based government standards and other quality check measures. In addition, the seeds and related inputs will be inspected for presence of pests and weed seeds not to introduce to the localities. In this regard, the project will not import or introduce planting materials out of the Ecological areas. Through other programs, WR is supporting several tree nurseries in the project proposed localities and the seedlings produced in the nurseries will be distributed to individual households, planed in public places, and provided to different institutions including health facilities, schools and social centers. Moreover, there will be environmental protection campaign at villages’ level.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WR will ensure that all activities to contribute to improving the wellbeing of women and children and contribute towards improving women access to and control over assets created by the project. Women and girls are overburdened in domestic and agricultural activities and are sole responsible for child care in the household. Besides, women have almost no right to make a decision on the utilization and transfer of the major assets. This project has analyzed all the constraints that affect women and girls and develop strategies that ensure their active participation and benefits. Women and women headed households will get priority to address their needs. More than 60% of the beneficiary households will be women. Improved technologies that reduces the burden of women are included in the project so that women get enough time for domestic and other reproductive activities. WR recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups minority groups. While implementing this project, WRS will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights including SGBV problems. Mobile veterinary services will be conducted, reaching out to populations who are unable to access the static facilities, thus ensuring that individuals afraid or unable to move to the static facilities are not denied assistance or services. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances to project sites. The organization will also be sure to promote conflict sensitivity by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations will be mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. In addition, the M&E system segregates data by gender and age groups to measure and analyze how the project is affecting different groups. The organization will focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding of the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity. In addition, the organization ensure that all staff, volunteers and contractors working with the organization sign code of conduct.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The actions proposed by WR are prepared based on the needs and priorities of the communities, which has been collected and compiled through needs assessment, field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. In addition, the intended communities have also confirmed their commitment to fully participate through the entire project life cycle. Overall the community will be involved in the project as depicted below. • Through their representatives the community had been involved in needs assessment and formulation of this project; • WR established three layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community-level volunteers in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-level development committees and extension workers. These committees established to oversee the work of WR in their respective areas or locality. In addition, CAHWs and extension workers, who contribute to the strengthening of livestock and agriculture-based livelihoods of the communities, and provide feedback through various reporting and communication mechanisms. • The community level engagement materialized through conducting a regular meeting with community-level committees and volunteers. Sector officers and area coordinators conduct monthly meeting at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. WRS displays hotline reporting mechanisms so that the community members can report their level of satisfaction on the services provided by WRS and partners to relevant staff in the organization. Furthermore, WR will continue using the existing suggestion box to get the feedback from beneficiary communities. The beneficiary community will be informed to put their feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WR coordinators will collect information from the suggestion box on a quarterly basis and discuss with all concerned including higher management. Also, the actions taken to the community findings will be shared back to the community.
