Project Summary

The impact of the humanitarian crisis in the conflict-affected state presents one of the most significant vulnerabilities to the spread of COVID-19 in Nigeria. According to the Borno state COVID-19 Preparedness and Response Plan, IDPs are a high-risk category for the spread of COVID-19. The infection prevention and control system of the health facilities are also very limited; they have no trained human resources, limited supply of personal protective equipment, the triage system is not properly functional to identify suspected cases and properly isolate before contamination of the health care workers and other patients in the facility.
In response to the situation, ACF has decided to come up with a countrywide coordination mechanism specifically in its operational states namely Borno and Yobe. ACF strategy is aligned with the WHO and states’ COVID strategic response. Among those core pillars of strategic response ACF is providing support on State-level coordination, risk communication and community engagement, infection prevention and control, surveillance and logistic. Considering this focus areas, ACF will address specific needs and considerations required in humanitarian situations, including camps and camp-like settings and the surrounding host communities, in scaling-up readiness and response operations for the COVID-19 outbreak through effective multi-sectoral partnership and active engagement with other organizations.
Considering this the following activities will be emphasized on identification and mapping of health and isolation facilities available for IDPs and host communities. Health facilities will be assessed for readiness, response capacity, policy and protocols using the WHO facility readiness checklist for COVID-19. Health facilities and critical infrastructure will be safe, prepared, well maintained and resilient to avoid the disruption of basic services. Suspected or confirmed COVID-19 infection in the intervention area will be screened, triaged tested and provided with care. Active case finding, contact tracing and monitoring help to identify contacts that may need quarantine and patients who need to isolate in line with WHO guidance and the national guidelines. The project will also support the provision of medical care for IDPs and host communities with COVID-19. High-risk groups with underlying conditions such as HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, chronic health conditions and disabilities should be identified and paid particular attention. Essential services should be maintained to ensure continuity of care for these conditions as well as for COVID-19.
In addition, the project will focus on training of health staff in case management and infection prevention and control measures at health facilities. Provide Accurate and timely evidence based information on the possible impact of COVID-19 outbreaks in IDPs and host communities. This information should seek to dispel fears and misperceptions among host populations regarding IDPs and COVID-19 outbreaks.
Provide culturally and linguistically appropriate, accurate, timely and user-friendly information in accessible formats on the health facilities available for COVID-19 care. Information on how people can seek testing, care and treatment could be provided through local telephone hotlines as well as visual and other forms of educational materials. Such information should cover the prevention and triage of people with respiratory symptoms, as well as home care and individual and community hygiene.

1 - Needs

IDP camps and most of the host communities have limited WaSH services, with poor access to health facilities, irregular access to food, and experiencing high levels of stress due to years of instability has likely weakened the immune systems of many individuals. These challenges are compounded by a weak health care system in the NE offering limited infrastructure, few skilled health care providers, and inadequate supplies. Furthermore, high numbers of individuals living in a shared residence, both in IDP camps and host communities, crowded humanitarian facilities including water points and food distribution sites, present conditions that facilitate the rapid spread of COVID19. Individuals with disabilities and the elderly are particularly vulnerable, as they will have difficulties accessing sanitation and health facilities.
