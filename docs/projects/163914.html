Project Summary

IMC plans to establish a new “Women and Girls and Safe Space” in Babella sub district (exact location to be determined based on assessment) in Eastern Ghouta, Rural Damascus. The center will work as entry point for identification of GBV related concerns, representing a safe space for beneficiaries to access confidential support through case management, psychosocial and referrals services. At the same time, the center will be offering a range of structured and non-structured evidence based activities focusing on GBV prevention and awareness.
Women and girls will be able to express and exchange concerns and experiences with their peers and qualified counselors, which will be crucial in enhancing prevention mechanisms while ensuring confidential and appropriate responses are provided when needed. Beneficiaries will be able to determine their interests in terms of PSS activities that the center will offer, which in turn will ensure their active participation.
Women and girls and the community overall will be consulted in an effort to better understand the main risks as well as the types of community support systems existing before the crisis and how they have changed in the past years. In conservative communities such as the one pre-selected for this intervention, having a space only to women and girls will enable them to participate in activities in which they are not usually allowed to participate; create a safe space for them to discuss about subjects of their interest; raise awareness on topics that are not usually talked over; reduce stigma for survivors in reporting and obtaining life-saving assistance, while contributing in strengthening prevention and community support mechanisms.
Please note that the location for this intervention although pre-selected based on needs observed by IMC teams during 2019, is subject to approval from relevant authorities. IMC will be working for GBV project and a separate protection project in mobile teams in the same area. The two projects will be connected through internal referrals. The WGSS will be open to women and girls of all ages, while the mobile team will be working with boys and men within the community.

4 - Needs

Preliminary findings informing HNO 2020 confirm that GBV in Syria continue to affect disproportionally women and girls. The socio-economic situation and increased poverty among the large majority of Syrians are further contributing to other underlying causes of GBV and thus exposing women and girls to higher risks of violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse, child/forced marriage, domestic violence, denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse. Women have been increasingly engaged in livelihood and economic activities in Syria and domestic violence is reported to have increased because of the existing patriarchal culture that defines what is culturally acceptable and enforces compliance by violence, coercion, pressure and rejection. Female beneficiaries have often provided examples of men as perpetrators of violence because allegedly they feel the pressure of these norms that want them to be able to provide for their families.Observation and feedback from beneficiaries receiving IMC services provided through one Protection mobile team and one medical mobile team (which also provides case management services) in different areas of Eastern Ghouta, confirm the above findings. Women have reported to face severe restrictions in their movements and in accessing humanitarian assistance. Cases of forced and/or early marriage have increased throughout the crisis, often ending in divorce, leaving adolescent girls and women alone with their children and being stigmatized by families and communities, increasing the risk for sexual exploitation. In IMC areas of intervention, adolescent boys also report being particularly vulnerable to violence, exploitation and child labour.

5 - Implementation of activities

Provide life-saving and specialized GBV services including case management, psychosocial support.Strengthening of internal and external referral pathways to provide comprehensive assistance to GBV survivors.Structured and non-structured psychosocial support activities including:PSS activities for women and girls.Life skills and empowerment program for adolescent girls which will enable them to become active members of positive change in their community, providing them with opportunities to exchange personal challenges and experiences with their peers.Recreational and educational activities. Early Childhood Development program. Enhance strategies to empower women and girls. Given the specifics of the proposed area of intervention,IMC qualified staff will design context specific awareness and sensitization activities to support most at risk groups like adolescent girls and female head of household, divorced and widowed women in better understanding GBV concerns and ultimately strengthen prevention. This will overall contribute in empowering families to protect children and enhancing the resilience of the entire community. Training of humanitarian actors and community members on GBV core concepts, GBV mainstreaming, referral pathways, PFA. During the project focus group discussions will be conducted,feedback mechanism will be establish to measure the beneficiaries satisfaction on the service overall and how this service needs to be improved to meet their changing needs.IMC will include community volunteers in project, who will be trained on basics of PSS and GBV,through them IMC will be able to identify new needs within the community.IMC aims to monitor the quality of its activities by systematically tracking project outputs measuring the effectiveness of the program and building an evidence-based intervention by modifying the implementation based on findings of its MEAL activities which supports synthesizing data collected from multiple sources.
