Project Summary

The interventions planned with the project will provide urgent minimum initial services package of sexual reproductive health services (MISP) for the affected population during cyclone, monsoon and other natural disasters. During natural disasters and displaced population with disruption of health facilities and infrastructure the need for sexual reproductive health services is expected to increase
Of the 1.3 million people targeted in the JRP, this project will focus on those 406,108 refugees and host communities which includes women of reproductive age that are facing critical life threatening situations in the Teknaf and Ukhiya sub-districts of Cox‟s Bazar. The prioritized refugees and host communities are the most at risk of recurrent incidents such as landslides and floods, notably due to the monsoon and cyclones, the topography and soil properties of the site. These women of reproductive age are at constant and immediate-risk of pregnancy related complications and will need urgent and immediate care.
• To achieve the above main goal, interventions will focus on
• Strengthening and supporting the referral facilities to provide management of obstetric, gynaecological complications and new-born care
• Ensure timely evacuation and referral of obstetric emergency cases and safe deliveries during natural disasters and emergencies
• Ensuring GBV survivors, including women, girls, boys and men’s access to quality life-saving healthcare services, with an emphasis on clinical management of rape.
• Reinforce, repair, and maintain vital sexual and reproductive health structures
• Distribute reproductive health kits, including post-rape treatment supplies and other clinical commodities to viable health centres, mobile clinics and health actors. Distribution of clean delivery kits to visibly pregnant mothers during displacement and emergency response

Needs

Bangladesh is ranked 13th in the world in risk for natural disasters with flooding and cyclones ranking the highest. Less than one year ago, this same area of Bangladesh, including Cox’s Bazar and Chittagong, was ravaged by Cyclone Mora bringing widespread flooding and damaging winds affecting over 3 million people. The priority sexual and reproductive Health include ensuring the minimum initial service package (MISP) for reproductive health in crisis for the affected population. During natural disasters and displaced population with disruption of health facilities and infrastructure the need for sexual reproductive health services is expected to increases. As part of the contingency planning support to strengthen the referral facilities to provide 24/7 management of emergency obstetric, new-born care and gynaecological complications is needed. Cyclone and emergency preparedness that includes strengthening of facilities structures and repositioning of emergency reproductive health commodities needs to be in place. Due to social instability and displacement loss of support from social fabric affected population are at also at a high risk of transmission of sexually transmitted infections (STI) and HIV. Care provision for pregnant mothers with other complications which may increase the risk of obstetric emergencies need to be strengthened. Control measures need to be put in place to prevent infectious diseases of outbreak potential such as Hepatitis E. Life-saving services to safeguard women’s health and address gender-based violence are as essential as food, water and shelter and needs to be prioritized in humanitarian action. GBV is significantly exacerbated in conflict and disaster contexts, where the “normal” risks of violence are compounded by displacement, breakdowns in certain social norms and limited access to services or formal systems of protection and justice. Addressing the physical and emotional consequences of gender-based violence, including providing psychosocial assistance and medical treatment to survivors is essential. GBV survivors, including women, girls, boys and men’s access to quality life-saving healthcare services, with an emphasis on clinical management of rape is critical in emergencies. To reduce avoidable maternal and neonatal mortality, morbidity and disability it is critical to ensure the delivery of essential minimum initial service package (MISP) of sexual reproductive health in crisis and meet the health needs of the most vulnerable including adolescents and teenage pregnancies.
