Project Summary

This project is aim to end Open defecation, train and equip community representative on good hygiene practices, on hand washing techniques, door to door sensitization of the community,This programme will target boys, girls, women and men across all genders.
This programme goal is to end open defecation and improve the health and social economic well-being of communities by reducing the diarrhea-related aliments through providing sustainable proper sanitation and good hygiene practices including capacity building.

1 - Needs

Water and Sanitation related diseases cause significant deaths and sickness in the north East Nigeria. Even without the disruption of an emergency, diarrhoea kills over 30,000 children per week worldwide. During conflict in particular, simple diarrhoea diseases can often kill more people than the conflict itself. the main focus of this programme is to help people to understand and develop good hygiene practices, so as to prevent disease and promote positive attitudes towards cleanliness. And also for the reduction of diarrhoea, and other water related dieases.Access to hygiene and sanitation for estimated 2.8 million is hindred as the households have no latrine and practice open defecation and share a single latrine with more than 20 others, with limited access to handwashing with soap and water and no personal hygiene materials.The feacal sludge management is poor, with low infrastructure to support liquid waste from desluging activities, which is considered as one of the reasons for yearly outbreak of cholera. Additionally flooding is a major concern as most of the feacal sludge worsens with households facing exposure of untreated waste.The high need for WASH services amongst IDPs and returnees has had an increase from last year, mainly due to continuous displacement and the high volume of expected returns. New reported acute diarrhoea cases, with average bi-weekly data of 1,500-2,000 individuals amongst populations further increases the needs category. While there is high vulnerability across all groups, available data shows that majority of women and children (over 51%) have reported severe needs for water, hygiene and sanitation.

2 - Activities

SANITATIONS 1. Construction of accessible and gender friendly latrines 2. Refuse pit constructions 3. Solid waste management training Hygiene Promotion and Capacity Building PHAST (Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation) will be used. The methodology will focuses on participatory learning and will aim to empower communities to manage their water supply and to control sanitation related diseases by promoting health awareness and understanding. The approach includes basic steps that helps the community identify their issues themselves and find solutions by themselves using local knowledge and wisdom. 1. Sensitization on hand hand washing benefits 2. sensitization on the dangers, health implications and environmental consequences of Open defecation 3. Implementation of Community-Led Total Sanitation training for community representative and leaders. 4. Selection and Training WASH committee members water system management 5. Organizing a Training the Trainers (ToT) on the community hygiene promoters, hygiene promotion sessions with a focus on the prevention, transmission and treatment of diarrhea, especially focusing on children under five. 6. Training of artisan on how to construct latrine and other WASH structures. 7. Provision of Hygiene kits

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A feedback mechanism will be established and operationalised through stakeholder engagement meetings, anchored by project staff and community leaders, to provide and receive necessary feedback about the impact of the project, including aspects of the project that are going well, challenges, delivery methodology, context-appropriate solutions to problems, and opportunities to drive community capacity and ownership of the activities. Through the engagement of an external project evaluation expert, the aim is to ensure that objective feedback is received from support communities and beneficiaries about the quality, target and impact of the intervention on addressing the goals of the project. The feedback received at intervals during the implementation of the project will, along with the stakeholders’ meetings, influence any changes to the implementation of the project to ensure that it meets the needs of target beneficiaries.
