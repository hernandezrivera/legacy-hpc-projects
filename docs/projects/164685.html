Project Summary

GOPA – DERD places the protection of affected populations at the center of its response. we will prioritize protection responses, ranging from status and protection of individuals, prevention of and response to violence (including GBV and torture), assistance to specific vulnerable groups, including Persons with Disabilities (PwDs) and the elderly, Child Protection, humanitarian mine action, access to documentation and information management.
Interventions designed to reduce and mitigate the protection risks of man-made violence, coercion, deprivation and abuse of persons inside Syria could be supported in the form of stand-alone or integrated programs aimed at achieving protection outcomes through other programme activities and protection-sensitive targeting. It is important to highlight that considering the limited coverage of specialized protection services in Syria, GOPA will support the roll out or strengthening of specialized protection services, including those to address complex cases.
The socio-economic impact of the crisis remains a main cause for families to continue to be highly dependent on humanitarian assistance and to resort to harmful coping strategies, which affects women, adolescent girls and children in particular. Such conditions affect multiple groups within communities, including the displaced, returnees, hosting communities and Palestine refugees. In addition, the strain and poverty generated by the protracted crisis may also cause marginalization and the neglect of groups with specific needs such as the elderly and persons with disabilities
Based on HNO protection 2020 :44 per cent of the assessed communities respondents reported presence of at least one of the assessed seven protection issues as very commonly occurring and in 52 per cent of the communities respondents have reported presence of five or more out of the assessed seven protection issues3 .There are 13.6 Million person in need where Female 50% male 50%. In addition to 5.7 million children in need, Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. In the first nine months of 2019, the UN verified 1,792 such violations against children9 in 12 of 14 governorates of Syria, illustrating persistent trends of violence though not the full scope of protection concerns affecting children10
Project Goal: To reduce the vulnerability and mitigate the suffering of war-affected Syrians through providing lifesaving assistance by improving access to protection services through implementing 10 MHPSS family groups Services and establishing two Family Safe Space (FSS) Centers in the project catchment areas; Daraa (Daraa city), Homs (Al Wa'er neighbourhood).
Project Objectives: Supporting and protecting Trauma survivors and their families to enhance their wellbeing ,and assist vulnerable population in the project catchment areas to become more resilient to Trauma and reconnect with society through effective coping mechanisms, to prevent and reduce risk of GBV and improve MH of target beneficiaries, and improve economic situation of vulnerable population. The proposed project period is 9 months; including three-month preparation period of the proposed two Family Safe Space (FSS) Centers: Daraa (Daraa city), Homs (Al Wa'er neighborhood)

4 - Needs

GOPA DERD will work towards meeting the needs addressed in the protection 2020 HRP as follows: Protection sector and its Areas of Responsibility (AoRs) will maintain a community-based approach in its interventions and will continue to tailor the response to the evolving situation and the emerging needs. The sector will maintain capacity to respond to emergency situations, particularly in the context of north-western and north- eastern Syria. This will be largely carried out through rapid deployment of mobile resources addressing urgent protection needs arising from renewed hostilities or sudden displacement and providing initial measures to ensure adequate protection response and mitigation of protection risks (e.g. presence along routes and crossing points, at reception sites, in emergency collective accommodations; psychological ﬁrst aid, prevention of family separation, communication on available services, immediate support to and referral of persons with speciﬁc needs). In parallel, capitalizing on the achievements of the previous years, the sector will maintain and possibly expand its regular coverage to areas of severe needs, characterized by protracted displacement, shifts in control, return movements, and the presence of overburdened communities. This part of the response strategy will continue to rely on the provision of integrated protection services through community-based facilities such as Community Centers, aimed at responding to protection needs, promoting inclusion and community participation and providing a safe space for community interaction.

5 - Implementation of activities

MHPSS family group Services 1.PSS Sessions: Selected families will take part in a four-day session, for six hours per day. PSS sessions are held in “safe spaces” such as social centers and community halls that are utilized exclusively for the project activities, fostering a safe and stress-free environment for the participants. A memorandum of understanding (MoU) is signed with the administrators of the identified spaces. Participants are transported to the venue and provided room and board; all costs are covered by the program. Over the lifetime of the project, staff will facilitate 25 PSS sessions. 2.One-on-one Counseling and Referral: During the four-day sessions, GOPA will conduct individual consultations to help individuals in need of specialized services. Sessions related to life-skills and self-esteem will also be provided when needed. If PSS specialists and the cases manager uncover major psychological issues and symptoms during the pre-test screening discussion with survivors, they will form a focus group. GOPA specialists will identify difficult cases in need of additional PSS support, such as severe depression, psychosomatic diseases, behavior disorders, etc. and divide them into focus groups according to their common symptoms. The four to five participants in the focus group will ideally come to understand the nature of the specific symptoms they exhibit and whether their behavior is anti-social. The specialist and case manager do not require any details, but provide the opportunity for the participant to understand that s/he has a problem that needs to be addressed individually. Family Safe Space (FSS) Centers: GOPA will respond to GBV & MHPSS cases through the 2 centers in: Daraa and Homs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

GOPA-DERD Protection staff will prioritize the safety, dignity and “do no harm” ethic during the implementation of all activities; including the provision of specialized services throw implementing “GOPA-DERD protection mainstreaming policy”. A suggestion and complaint reporting mechanism will be set for beneﬁciaries to channel their concerns. Financial, cultural and security barriers are lifted through partially covering the provision of transportation, recruitment of staff from the local community, and the active engagement of community leaders. GBV& PSS Teams will schedule visits well in advance so that potential beneﬁciaries are able to plan and take advantage of services offered.
