Project Summary

IOM will undertake a wide variety of activities to create an enabling environment for the Rohingya refugees towards eventual sustainable voluntary return and reintegration in Myanmar in the future when conditions permit. Such activities will help Rohingya beneficiaries increase self-reliance skills. The livelihoods activities will also focus on the host communities affected by the influx by integrating income-generating livelihoods through support outside the agricultural sector Off-farm activities.
IOM will implement skills development projects to promote the economic resilience of Rohingya refugees in IOM AOR camps. This technical (1,500 individuals)/ vocational (600 individuals) training on Community Health Worker, Plumbing, Small Engine Mechanic, jute-bag products, shoe making, etc will enhance their capacity and help them utilize their skills in the camps in Bangladesh as self-reliance opportunities and can be used as transferrable skills by them upon return to Myanmar. Rohingya beneficiaries will receive skills training related outside the agricultural sector Off-farm activities that are applicable to the humanitarian sector (e.g. health, wash, site development-construction, etc.) in the camps. IOM will facilitate in-kind asset support materials to Rohingya beneficiaries working outside the agricultural sector “Off farm activities” who are willing to gain basic skills or competencies but lack the physical assets to do so. 200 beneficiaries receiving formal vocational training will be referred to the relevant sectors for volunteer engagement. Besides livelihood opportunities will be created for 1,000 beneficiaries.
IOM will provide host communities with transferable and life skills training (e.g. communication, negotiation, psychosocial) and vocational skills training including skills related to green businesses and green financing such as plumbing, catering to disaster resilient outside the agricultural sector, Off-farming tools and methods, waste recycling, electronic refurbishment, related to resilient investments and planning, etc. The trades will be identified by skills and needs assessment. The productive asset support will be given to beneficiaries who have technical skills but lack inputs/opportunities/assets and are willing to earn a modest income. IOM will provide small business grants through the Self-help groups (SHG) to help beneficiaries expand and strengthen their businesses for outside the agricultural sector “Off Farm activities”.
To enhance the private sector engagement and promote market linkages, wage subsidy opportunities for beneficiaries will be implemented and to ensure sustainable income generation, IOM will organise activities like product fairs in the host community to sell and promote their products to individuals and wholesalers.
The selection criteria of beneficiaries will be based on eligibility criteria such as female-headed households, income below the poverty line, widow, etc. IOM will also focus on disability throughout the selection criteria and assist livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities. One of the major challenges for vocational training is the literacy and numeracy level of the communities. IOM will address the challenge by incorporating Accelerated adult learning (3,500 individuals) into the program to improve the literacy and numeracy level of the beneficiaries. Women empowerment, leadership, and decision-making will be promoted by incorporating income opportunities and entrepreneurship opportunities, business development training, financial literacy, etc., for women in the host community and self-reliance opportunities for women in the camps to ensure sustainable development and integration into the society. The budget for vocational training has been calculated as the standard cost per beneficiary shared by the sector. All activity costs include non-operational costs and are presented as a total of operational and non-operational costs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The socioeconomic condition of Bangladesh withstood COVID-19 pandemic shock through its diversified agricultural and skills-based income generation activities (Tackling the Covid-19 Social and Economic Crisis in Bangladesh, UNICEF, 2021). In 2022, overall household-level expenditure escalated due to global economic crisis and its extensive impact on the overall market of Bangladesh (WFP-REVA, 2023). Access to foods, shelters materials/upgrade, and income generation activities remain three main priorities of host community and Rohingya households (J-MSNA 2023).Despite many humanitarian and development activities in Teknaf and Ukhiya, the resilience and capacity index of these two Upazilas remain low compared to Cox’s Bazar Sadar and Ramu (FAO Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2022).Bangladesh has a long history of sheltering Rohingya refugees including the 960,539 from Myanmar who were forced to seek refuge in Bangladesh due to the brutal ethnic violence (GoB-UNHCR Factsheet 2023). This additional populations has a social, natural, environmental, legal, and financial impact on Bangladesh. Skills development support for the Rohingyas is mostly dependent on the humanitarian assistance provided by national and international donors with the aim to make them self-reliant upon their return to Myanmar. Economic vulnerability among Host Community households has increased to 35% in 2022 compared to 32% in 2021 (WFP-REVA 2023). Bangladesh is facing high inflation of food market reducing the purchasing power of both communities increasing their vulnerabilities (WFP-VAM, 2023). Food insecurity is currently a global challenge (WFP-VAM, 2023) and continues to constitute the highest share of expenditures incurred for both the communities (WFP-REVA 6, 2023). One of the main barriers for both host and Rohingya households was identified as lack of income opportunities. Therefore, access to skills, training, in-kind support, and entrepreneurship opportunities, will give them more accessibility to affordability living conditions (MSNA 2023).Women have the lowest resilience capacity index compared to men in both the host and Rohingya communities (FAO-Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2023). To ensure equal opportunity for women and increase their empowerment, IOM will implement a wide range of activities focusing on women both in the camps and host communities including Persons with disabilities and third genders.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IOM will implement and plan the activities in close coordination with the relevant sectors and other implementing partners to reduce duplication and overlapping of activities. This will help to harmonize the activities for the maximum benefit of the vulnerable people. The project implementation information (location, achievement, target, funding status) will be regularly shared with the sectors in order to contribute to a consolidated information management system. The project locations will be identified in consultation with the sectors so that IOM activities are not duplicated by other implementing partners. IOM and its partners will strictly adhere to the local standards and policies implemented by the government and the sectors. IOM will ensure the partners have required policies related to PSEA, AAP, and Gender engagement before identifying the partners.While designing the project, IOM will prioritize the sustainability of the activities, taking into account the possibility of a decrease in funds in humanitarian support, increasing a necessity of self-reliance support. Only the sustainable activities will be identified through the needs assessment and secondary analysis for the community populations to be able to acquire and enhance the knowledge and skills that can be utilized on their own, even after the completion of the project. IOM will also ensure the continuation of the project through a regular funding channel beyond the year 2023 so that the activities are not suddenly stopped creating a void in the community needs.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. “Do no harm” is the guiding principle for implementing the activities for the vulnerable people. Privacy of the beneficiaries and confidentiality of the shared content will be maintained while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness-raising sessions to protect them from any unwanted incidents. However, IOM will follow referral pathways if any incident occurs.IOM will deliver services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, that are culturally appropriate, and that avoid any unintended negative consequences. IOM will provide assistance and services according to needs and not on the basis of age, sex, gender, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance,etc. Services and assistance are provided on an adequate scale, within safe and easy reach, are known by the affected individuals, and accessible by all groups including medical cases, Persons with Disability, discriminated groups and the like.

3.3 - Age & Disability

IOMs' beneficiaries are mostly aged 18+ years and above and does not include child beneficiaries. IOM plans to include youth and the adult community as the direct beneficiary of the self-reliance and livelihoods activities. However, the activities will indirectly benefit all the age groups of the targeted households. The interventions will be designed in a manner that takes into account the safeguarding of the children of the female-headed households beneficiaries. The training centers will be equipped with breastfeeding corners, and child-friendly spaces so that mothers can take the training courses while the children can safely stay with them. All the activities planned for the year will consider people from different races, ethnicity, persons with special needs/ disabilities and elderly population. The activities aim to benefit all the beneficiaries in need. IOM will especially consider minority groups in the community to mainstream them into society. The activities will be tailored to their specific needs and skills based on the outcome of the needs assessment.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability remains a vital and integral component of this proposed project. During the needs assessment, the affected populations in all the targeted locations will be consulted for their needs and priorities. They will also be given the opportunity to contribute to the design and planning of trade selection, opportunities, market analysis, with support from IOM. The affected people will take a lead in making decisions on their roles, local resources mobilization, and livelihoods/self-reliance skills considering safety, security, and gender dynamics. Affected women and girls will be consulted, in addition to and separately from men and boys, about trades and skills. In addition to that, persons with disabilities and the elderly will get access to participate in the information sharing and data collection sessions. Specific project activities will be planned based on their needs, priorities, and context. No one will be left behind/out of skill development opportunities. IOM and the partners will establish a complaint feedback mechanism at the project sites to receive regular feedback from the beneficiaries regarding the activities. The privacy of the beneficiaries and confidentiality of the content will be ensured and the response to their feedback will be delivered regularly with diligence

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM is a member of the PSEA network and the partners are also aware of the protection of sexual exploitation and abuse. IOM ensures the capacity building of the partners before implementing of the project. The partners must comply with PSEA standards to be awarded IOM funds. In addition the staff of the implementing partners receive PSEA related trainings from IOM which they disseminate during various trainings in the field. The beneficiaries are also aware of PSEA and can report if necessary to the proper channels.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will include extensive work on DRR activities through renovation of cyclone shelters, capacity building of local communities on DRR to protect their life and resources, and increasing awareness on early warning systems. The project will also undertake watershed management activities in the host community to protect the natural water flow and reduce flood risk as mentioned in the project summary.
