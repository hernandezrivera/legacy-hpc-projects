Project Summary

The general protection status defined by the more than decade-old crisis continues to be characterized by complex and interconnected protection concerns. The civilian population in parts of the country remains vulnerable to ongoing and new hostilities, leading to civilian casualties and forced displacement as people search for safety. The deterioration of the economy, the spread of poverty, the shortage and loss of livelihoods, the destruction and loss of housing and property, long and multiple cycles of displacement, poor living conditions (even for people in areas of relative stability), the disintegration of the family, and the disintegration of the family or society that has depleted the adaptive capacities of individuals and societies and generated levels of High psychological distress. As a result, people resort to harmful coping mechanisms. Protection issues disproportionately affect groups such as women and children, especially adolescents, adolescents, the elderly, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable, marginalized or socially excluded persons and groups. They also face additional barriers to accessing the limited services available. Against this backdrop, the COVID-19 pandemic has brought a new set of protection risks and consequences to the affected population while multiplying existing vulnerabilities. Restrictions on movement disrupted ongoing protection assistance, prevented protection actors from expanding their reach, and restricted people further in situations of violence and vulnerability, adding to their distress and vulnerability, especially women and children, to violence within the family. Disruptions to assistance, particularly specialized protection services, increase distress, increase risks of GBV and child protection, cancel progress, and can reset or exacerbate the severity of accompanying needs.
Based on HNO 2021 Protection, there are 13,125,292 people in need of protection services in Syria; (29.6%) are women, (4.2%) are elderly over 60 years old.
The elderly, (24.7%) are disabled. We are interested in intervening in Aleppo, Homs and Rural Damascus, which are among the cities in greatest need of protection. The total needs in these governorates are Homs (507,122), Rural Damascus Zabadani (70,925), and Aleppo (823,422), Hama (683,475) ALQunaitra ( 46,383)
Based on HRP 2021 Protection Strategy; GOPA-DERD protection Staff will help vulnerable people to improve the emotional and physical wellbeing of vulnerable individuals who were exposed to the traumas of this prolonged war, through the provision of a comprehensive MHPSS intervention from a community-based approach, which engages each member of the family, including children and adolescents, in age- and gender-specific activities, GOPA/DERD protection staff” physiatrist and psychologist” will implement 10 Group sessions that aim to raise the awareness of the vulnerable beneficiaries of the different GBV and MHPSS topics and issues. Beneficiaries will be intro and duced to/educated on the different types of GBV and how to deal with each type to mitigate negative effects. The sessions will also address types of MHPSS issues; psychological stress, loss and grief, fear, PTSD, and other cases, how to deal with shocking incidents and will learn strategies for combating psychological stress. Also GOPA-DERD will help vulnerable people to improve economic situation of vulnerable population through two proposed activities: Family Safe Space Centers (FSS) and MHPSS family group activities.

5 - Implementation of activities

MHPSS family group Services: 1.PSS Sessions: Selected families will take part in a four-day session, for six hours per day. PSS sessions are held in “safe spaces” such as social centers and community halls that are utilized exclusively for the project activities, fostering a safe and stress-free environment for the participants. A memorandum of understanding (MoU) is signed with the administrators of the identified spaces. Participants are transported to the venue and provided room and board; all costs are covered by the program. 2.One-on-one Counseling and Referral: GOPA will conduct individual consultations to help individuals in need of specialized services, through identify difficult cases, such as severe depression, psychosomatic diseases, behavior disorders, etc. The participants in the focus group will ideally come to understand the nature of the specific symptoms they exhibit and whether their behavior is anti-social. Family Safe Space (FSS) Centers:GOPA will utilize the three Centers to conduct, awareness sessions, group-based, structured psychosocial activities, one-day GBV sessions, one-on-one counseling, PSS & GBV case management and the provision of specialized psychological and health services for survivors of GBV through an established referral pathway. GOPA MHPSS & GBV Teams will replicate the same support services from HtR areas. The program is adapted to the cultural background of the population in the communities. GOPA Protection approach is to pro-actively raise the awareness of women, girls, men and boys to GBV and thereby mitigate risks and improve the well-being of beneficiaries. Community Mobilizers and Case Managers will engage with the local community through FGD.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The locations where the GBV & MHPSS activities take place are safe and do not present any risks to the concerned populations. Financial, cultural and security barriers are lifted through partially covering the provision of transportation, recruitment of staff from the local community, and the active engagement of community leaders. The program will also target PWDs, especially those suffering from medium to severe mental disabilities. PWD can benefit from the awareness raising sessions, as the topics addressed will help protect them from physical violence and abuse. GBV&MHPSS Teams will schedule visits well in advance so that potential beneficiaries are able to plan and take advantage of services offered, In addition to the application of case management for cases of gender-based violence and cases that need specialized services in the field of mental health, which contributes to reducing what contributes to reducing the risks related to mental health and gender-based violence for the most vulnerable groups in addition to improving the level of psychosocial well-being.
