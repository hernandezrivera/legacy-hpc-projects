Project Summary

The Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) presents a comprehensive proposal aimed at overcoming critical barriers to quality basic and vocational education for vulnerable school-aged children, including girls, boys, and children with disabilities (CwDs), aged 3 to 17, in conflict-affected regions within Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. The project’s scope encompasses seven targeted local government areas (LGAs), with an objective to directly benefit 970 individuals from each LGA, culminating in a total of 5,820 beneficiaries across the BAY states. These children, encompassing conflict-affected displaced, returnees, and members of host communities, will be engaged through both formal and non-formal educational frameworks.
CNEF's initiative is structured to enhance and broaden the educational knowledge of all direct beneficiaries. The project prioritizes scaling up timely and equitable access to quality basic and vocational educational empowerment. This will be achieved through focused community mobilization, sensitization, advocacy, and capacity building efforts.
A key aspect of our strategy is the adoption of a participatory approach. This involves collaboration with not only the beneficiaries but also key educational stakeholders such as the State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB), School-Based Management Committees (SBMC), Islamiyah School Associations, and other relevant entities within the target states.
The project is meticulously designed for a 12-month intervention period, with an allocated budget of $426,507. This time frame and budget are strategically allocated to maximize impact and ensure comprehensive coverage of the project’s objectives. CNEF is committed to facilitating a transformative educational experience for these children, fostering an environment where they can thrive academically and develop essential life skills in a safe and inclusive setting.

1 - Needs

The prolonged conflict in Northeast Nigeria has significantly undermined the already fragile educational system, particularly in the North East region. This has resulted in the existing infrastructure being overwhelmed and incapable of accommodating the large influx of displaced children seeking education. The situation is characterized by a high number of out-of-school children, suboptimal learning outcomes, and increased drop-out rates. An estimated 1,385,082 conflict-affected children across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states are in dire need of educational services. The crisis has led to widespread displacement within Nigeria and across its borders, resulting in a substantial number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. This displaced population faces considerable challenges in accessing education, a situation that is particularly acute for those residing in formal or informal camps. These individuals often live at a considerable distance from government schools and have limited financial resources to cover educational expenses. Among the displaced population, girls are notably less likely to engage in organized educational activities. This disparity is largely due to increased domestic responsibilities, concerns about safety and interaction with unrelated males, and prevailing perceptions about the necessity of education for girls. While children in host communities may have better access to educational facilities, they often face suboptimal learning environments marked by overcrowding, inadequate supplies, and a shortage of qualified teachers. Enrollment rates in the BAY states were notably low in the 2018-2019 academic year, attributed to limited learning spaces and scarce resources. In Yobe State, only 24% of schools have adequate learning materials, with even lower proportions in Borno (14%) and Adamawa (10%). Furthermore, the availability of school furniture, such as chairs, desks, and mats, is limited: 33% in Borno, 24% in Yobe, and 20% in Adamawa, according to JENA 2019. It is also observed that school attendance, especially among girls, increases with the provision of complete uniforms, including sandals and socks. This highlights the need for a gender-responsive approach in educational programme planning. The Education in Emergencies Working Group (EiEWG) has identified primary needs that encompass both alternative and formal education support. These needs include the development of alternative learning opportunities for children and youth currently out of the education system, supported by the construction and rehabilitation of temporary learning centers, renovation of classrooms, provision of furniture and WASH facilities, teacher training, and the supply of teaching materials, student kits, and school uniforms. To enhance the quality of education, there is a need for adapting lesson delivery methods, including facilitating lessons in local languages and expanding curriculum content, to ensure the effectiveness of educational initiatives such as radio education. Addressing these challenges requires a multi-faceted approach that not only focuses on physical infrastructure and material provision but also embraces inclusive and innovative educational strategies to cater to the diverse needs of conflict-affected children in the region.

2 - Activities

This proposal by the Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) outlines a comprehensive approach to providing Education in Emergencies support across selected Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa states. The project is designed to assist a total of 5,820 children, with a focus on gender equity, ensuring that 58% of beneficiaries are female. Key components of the project include: 1. Target Population Identification: Careful selection of schools and beneficiaries within the chosen LGAs to ensure the most effective reach of our interventions. 2. Infrastructure Development: Where existing structures are insufficient, we will construct semi-permanent Temporary Learning Centers (TLCs) conforming to best practices that prioritize the safety and security of children. This includes considerations for safe locations, perimeter fencing, first aid facilities, WASH services, and adherence to COVID-19 guidelines. Special attention will be given to making these facilities accessible to Children with Disabilities (CwDs). 3. Capacity Building: Training sessions will be organized for community members, School-Based Management Committees (SBMCs), social workers, and government entities (including the Ministry of Education and SUBEB) focusing on educational infrastructure management and child protection issues, psychosocial needs, and referral systems. 4. Community Sensitization: Implementing “back-to-school” campaigns to address parental hesitations regarding school attendance, particularly for girls. 5. Financial Support: Provision of conditional cash transfers to households to assist with school fees and the procurement of learning materials. 6. Teacher Training: Professional development for teachers in enhanced teaching methods, risk education, and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). 7. Educational Programs: Offering both formal and alternative basic education programs, including integrated Qur’anic Education and accelerated learning models. 8. Educational Services Package: Distribution of Early Childhood Development (ECD) kits and supplies for scholars and teachers. 9. Material and Equipment Supply: Provision of essential school materials, equipment, and furniture, such as chairs, desks, and registers. 10. Community Resilience: Engaging communities in school development and risk reduction planning to strengthen resilience. 11. School-based Committees: Creation and/or reactivation of School-based Management Committees (SBMCs) to foster community involvement in school governance. 13. COVID-19 Response Mechanism: Establishing a comprehensive community/school COVID-19 Referral System with clear protocols and procedures. Through these multifaceted efforts, CNEF aims to create a safe, inclusive, and effective learning environment for children affected by emergencies in the BAY states, fostering resilience and continuity in education amidst challenging circumstances.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) is committed to implementing a robust protection mainstreaming framework in its operations. This commitment will be actualized through a comprehensive approach, ensuring the highest standards of safety and ethical conduct in our programs. Key strategies include: 1. Regular Community Consultations: CNEF will engage in ongoing, structured dialogues with the target communities. These consultations are vital for understanding community needs and ensuring that our interventions are responsive and tailored to the specific contexts and challenges faced by community members. 2. Staff Training and Capacity Building: A significant focus will be placed on enhancing the capabilities of our staff, particularly in the areas of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). This includes training on effective reporting channels and case management modalities, ensuring that our team is well-equipped to handle sensitive issues with professionalism and efficiency. 3. Promotion of Reporting Mechanisms: In all community meetings, CNEF will consistently promote and encourage the use of existing Voluntary Service Overseas (VSO) hotlines and email channels. This is to facilitate the reporting of any cases of protection concerns, fraud, abuse, and to provide guidance on how primary actors can seek redress. This measure is pivotal in maintaining transparency and accountability, fostering a safe environment for all stakeholders. 4. Awareness and Accessibility: Ensuring that these reporting mechanisms are visible and accessible to all community members will be a priority. We recognize the importance of making these resources known and easily reachable to enhance the protection of all individuals involved in our programs. CNEF’s approach to protection mainstreaming is multi-layered, integrating community engagement, staff development, and clear reporting procedures. This approach not only ensures the effectiveness of our programs but also upholds our commitment to the safety, dignity, and rights of all individuals in the communities we serve.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) is dedicated to maintaining high standards of accountability and transparency throughout the implementation of our projects. To achieve this, CNEF will employ proven engagement mechanisms that actively involve community members and stakeholders. This approach is designed to foster a sense of ownership and participation among the communities we serve, ensuring that our initiatives are responsive to their needs and expectations. As part of our commitment to accountability, CNEF will establish functional and contextually appropriate feedback and complaint mechanisms. These systems are designed to empower beneficiaries, providing them with safe and confidential channels to express their concerns, dissatisfaction, or suggestions regarding the project implementation. This will be achieved through various means, including strategically placed suggestion boxes within the affected communities, the establishment of a complaint help desk, and a toll-free line for comfortable and secure communication of grievances. These feedback mechanisms are integral to our project strategy. They are not only intended to collect input from beneficiaries but also to provide timely and appropriate responses, adhering strictly to the principle of confidentiality. By implementing these measures, CNEF ensures that beneficiary voices are heard and respected, reinforcing the effectiveness and impact of our programs while upholding our core values of accountability and transparency.
