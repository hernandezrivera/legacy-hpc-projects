Project Summary

The humanitarian situation, particularly in the Idleb de-escalation area, continues to deteriorate as a result of the hostilities and economic situation. 1.8 million people living in camps and are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, with an increase of 470,000 people facing acute food insecurity in 2022 compared to 2021. The designed project is aligned with sector objective 1: Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with a high severity of need.
Through this project, IR Syria plans to reach vulnerable families who are newly internally displaced in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. Through this project, IR Syria plans to reach 22,100 families (110,500 individuals) who are newly IDP in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The targeted families will be assisted with:
 1.5 kg of baked bread daily to 2,100 households,
 Monthly food parcels to the most vulnerable groups,
 Emergency food parcels in a time of emergency,
 Provision of livestock,
 Provision of agriculture kits, and
 Veterinary clinic and vaccination.
The project will be implemented at IDPs camps and villages in Idleb and Aleppo governorates that have been identified in close coordination with the local councils, camp management, and other humanitarian organizations working in the area. The targeted areas host a large number of new IDP families and there are gaps in the food assistance provided. Therefore, the project will support extremely vulnerable families to meet their basic food needs.
This project aims to reach vulnerable individuals with food support. The overall project objective of this financially secured project is to reduce suffering, increase and maintain human dignity, and save the lives of vulnerable IDP in Harim and Jebel Saman Districts of Syria.

4 - Needs

Syria continues to suffer from multiple and complex socioeconomic difficulties. According to HNO, the food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 14.2 million to 14.6 million, with 12.8 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity. Further on that HNO states that the protracted crisis in Syria has intensified needs over the past year through a complex and deepening socio-economic crisis, degradation of livelihoods, knock-on effects of COVID-19, the ongoing conflict in the North of the country and major losses in the food production sector. Additionally, HNO reveals that in areas affected by conflict in the north-east, north-west, and parts of southern Syria, agricultural production will be further affected. Islamic Relief Syria team conducted a detailed need assessment in the targeted locations. The study targeted these areas due to the existing large scale of agricultural and livestock breeding activities. The assessment revealed that in Northern Rural of Aleppo, %84 of the farmers is owning the land that they are producing, however, %68 of farmers has challenges accessing to enough irrigation water. They are majorly using the rainfalls in the rainfed agricultural activities in a large scale (%69). The majority (57%) of the farmers in N. rural of Aleppo tend to purchase the seeds. The average prices of the different types of livestock have increased dramatically.

5 - Implementation of activities

In close coordination with the FSL Cluster, local council, camp management, local community and other humanitarian organizations active in the field, the geographic areas where a large number of new IDP families arrived will be mapped. The newly displaced families who are residing in the camps, unfinished buildings, informal settlements and have not received baked bread will be provided with daily baked bread. As recommended by the FSL cluster, after the initial weeks of displacement the families need monthly food parcel support to sustain minimum food security requirements without adopting harmful negative coping strategies. Each of the targeted families will receive one food parcel monthly for a period of four months. IR will conduct a detailed household-level need assessment to identify the most vulnerable families to provide agricultural vouchers including pesticides, fungicide and support with the agricultural kit. These families will be provided with agricultural training to improve their knowledge and practices to get the best yields. The families will further be supported through links with the markets so that the crops can be marketed well and ultimately the sale of which can increase the household level of income. Furthermore, IR will support targeted households with feed, Veterinary kit, the breeders need these vet materials continuously to treat and control some pests which attack their livestock.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk of Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection. IR will ensure to inform the beneficiary selection criteria clearly to all the stakeholders. Local councils and community committees will be engaged in beneficiary selection. Targeting for IDPs versus Host communities. The risk includes inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions and sexual exploitation of IDP female. This will be mitigated through local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities. Clear/precise guidance to the staff on the beneficiary selection process and criteria. Effective communication as regards the guidelines include communication materials in local language. Targeting for IDPs/Returnees for livelihood projects. The risks include access to land/assets. Risk to secondary rights holders e.g. female headed households. This will be mitigated through engaging with local authorities to verify status and through work alongside other complementary programs on HLP. In provision of general food assistance there is a risk of violence from the beneﬁciaries among themselves as well as the humanitarian staff. Also there is a risk of SEA by humanitarian workers male dominated, gate keepers and potential other beneﬁciaries. This risk will be mitigated through establishing a robust complaint and feedback mechanism so that any such cases are appropriately reported and followed up.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. IR’s CFM uses multiple channels to collect beneficiary complaints and feedback including a complaint box, hotlines, during-distribution and post-distribution monitoring, community committees, casual enumerators and program staff. In addition, during action activities, random interviews, spot checks and dialogue will allow beneficiaries to report concerns. The received complaints and feedbacks will be classified and handled as per IR policy for CFM and high-risk complaints, such as those relating to PSEA, child protection, or corruption, will be prioritized and promptly managed. Confidentiality and data protection are ensured as per Islamic Relief’s data protection policy. IR’s commitment towards Safeguarding against misconduct will be upheld, and options for safe access to IR’s feedback mechanism, especially to the most marginalized groups such as women, children, persons with disability, and those who cannot read and write, will be sought through WhatsApp, telegram, Skype and SMS.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Potential conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. This is a medium level risk. Islamic Relief, in cooperation with the Community Representative will choose the beneficiaries according to specific criteria to serve this community and will adopt a clear and transparent policy in project implementation. 2. Takeover of trucks moving the agriculture and livestock inputs. This is a medium level risk. IR will make continuous assessment on accessible safe routes prior to transportation. Appropriate time of truck movement will be assessed and chosen. The movement will take place only on the main and frequently used roads during daylight and journeys will be planned beforehand and based on the latest security and accessibility information. 3. The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, which may limit the access to the target area. This is a medium level risk. IR will coordinate with local partners and local committees in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. All required documentation will accompany all shipments of goods along with experienced staff who are familiar with the situation. 4. Medium level risk of Spread of COVID-19 in Northwest Syria: IR staff will take the following measures during implementation and monitoring: organize an awareness session for staff, use hand sanitizer that contains at least 60% alcohol, avoid close contact (distance of 6 feet), wear facemask, avoid crowded distribution.
