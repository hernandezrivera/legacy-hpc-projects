Project Summary

Under this grant, SI will support the most vulnerable households to improve their livelihood and enhance their resilience. This will be accomplished through the provision of multi-purpose cash grants (MPCG), implemented through vouchers. SI FSL team will also impart training for the beneficiaries to earn a living, thereby contributing to beneficiary households' resilience. Projects will be implemented in MMC/Jere, Monguno, Ngala and Dikwa LGAs.
Under the proposed project, SI will also respond to the vulnerable populations' emergency food needs through food vouchers for food assistance, in coordination with the Food Security Sector recommendation. To support early recovery, SI proposes distributing small livestock to the most vulnerable and women-headed families to enhance their income and improve the nutrition for the family members, especially children under 5 years.
Host community members/returnees and IDPs staying with host communities will be targeted in priority for these interventions, as per the FSL sector guidelines. Through the proposed projects in the areas, SI will ensure that adequate Do No Harm analyses are being conducted and that participatory methods in all aspects of the project cycle are implemented. All services to be provided will be designed to ensure safe and easy access for all vulnerable persons with specific needs (women, children, elderly, etc.).
SI has developed monitoring tools that include protection mainstreaming indicators and has included questions related to protection mainstreaming in all its surveys to measure that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner.

1 - Needs

Since 2015, North-East Nigeria has been confronted by a protracted humanitarian emergency characterized by armed conflict, forced displacement, and grave violations of civilians’ human rights and dignity. This situation continued to deteriorate due to resurgent violence, substantially reversing the humanitarian gains of previous years. In addition to other structural factors, conflict is declining the livelihoods of the population, increasing food insecurity and poverty, worsening lack of access to development opportunities, and limiting access to essential services. In 2019, out of the total population of 13.4 million people living across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) States, 7.1 million people were estimated to require humanitarian assistance (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2019). Additionally, nearly 1.8 million people remain internally displaced, and from those in need (7.1 million), an estimated 80 percent are women and children (USAID, Lake Chad Basin - complex emergency, 2020). According to the Global Risk Index Nigeria, today is amongst the countries with the highest overall projected conflict risk index and increased risk in socio-economic vulnerability, inequality, and food insecurity in 2019. Following the projections of the Cadre Harmonisé from June to August 2020, about 3.6m individual food and nutrition situation will deteriorate during the lean season, especially in Borno State. There will be around 3 million of the population moving into the emergency phase (Phase 4). Acute food insecurity in the Northeast of Nigeria is mainly due to lack of access to farmlands, protracted conflicts leading to an increase in the number of IDPs, disruption of market supplies and livelihoods, and limited access to food. This confirms the need to sustain the ongoing humanitarian assistance and complement it with recovery and resilience interventions in order to promote self-reliance and prevent further deterioration of the food and nutrition security situation. All LGAs currently targeted by the project are now all in the Crisis Phase (IPC3). Market functionality remains constrained by poor transport infrastructure and insecurity. Women are specifically highly vulnerable to food insecurity, as they have globally less access to resources and assets, less access to education. They also are poorer with less access to cash and less access to food. Women are more exposed to violence, especially sexual violence and are more involved in petty trade, begging and sale of natural resources. Vulnerable HH is particularly at risk of food insecurity and will require a specific focus with continuous inclusion and tailored activities. The negative coping mechanisms are affecting more women. SI is currently implementing food security and livelihood project funded by EU; packages include food distribution to vulnerable households during the lean season, distribution of agricultural inputs (seeds and tools), livestock recapitalization and support household income source through the provision of Multipurpose Cash Grats through vouchers. SI will capitalize on this current experience to analyze lesson learn related to the implementation of these activities to increase their impact and efficiency.

2 - Activities

Data collection will be disaggregated by gender and age. Community participation will be a key principle in assessment and project implementation. Following are the key activities under the proposed project:1) Support the most vulnerable Household with food assistance. (1,000HHs) SI will provide monthly food voucher support with a transfer value of 25,000NGN to 1,000 vulnerable HH; 2) Multipurpose cash grants (MPCG) to 500 most vulnerable HHs; 3) Provision of small ruminants 1200 HHs, Each HH will receive 3 small ruminants, based on their preferences and choice of production: 2 female goats/sheep and one male for HH who chooses reproduction, while 3 male sheep for HH who chooses to fatten, together with feed to cover half production cycle to prevent early destocking of livestock.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In accordance with SI’s core policies, assistance is inclusive and does not discriminate based on ethnicity, age, religion, gender, or sexual orientation. SI will implement these interventions focusing on the cross-cutting principles of gender sensitivity, participatory conflict analysis, community-based planning and Do No Harm. SI regard gender equality as a fundamental principle of their work, recognizing that it is a social, humanitarian concern rather than merely a women’s issue.SI are aware of the importance of including gender equality to increase humanitarian assistance efficiency and recognize that men, boys, girls and women have specific needs and constraints. Ignoring these specificities can have disastrous implications in terms of protection and survival for the victims of humanitarian crises. Participatory methods are used to access and incorporate gender equality and age perspectives in all aspects of the project cycle, focusing on gender during the design phases, with community discussion and assessments mandatorily conducted with groups of men and groups of women. Gender-related issues, including women’s, men’s, boys’ and girls’ role and specific needs, were included in the assessment.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SI will collect the need of the community since the assessment phase through community consultation and participatory approaches. This would be done by using the mechanism to accurately collect the different community groups' specific needs, considering gender and age specificity, and other specific vulnerabilities. SI will establish through community participation, community-based complaint management committees that members will comprise of elder statement and women of integrity whom are known and well respected by the community members. These people are usually the point of call as traditional means of settling the conflict in the community. SI has already established feedback and complaint response mechanism (FCRM) and feedback, so that community can provide feedback about SI intervention through different channels, i.e., CRM boxes, Hotline numbers, feedback collected by MEAL team on a regular basis. All Solidarites staff have been trained on protection issues by Protection actors and can identify and refer all cases of GBV, child abuse/etc. if needed. SI employees have been trained about PSEA risks. SI is a member of PSEA network in Nigeria. The needs analysis and assessments conducted by SI capture the differences between men and women, and both groups are consulted. Although all the beneficiaries will benefit from the proposed intervention equally, women will be involved in identifying the locations for Water and Sanitation services. Gender and age disaggregated data on programme coverage and impact will be collected, analysed and routinely reported on and programme activities monitored for improvement in self-reliance as well as beneficiary satisfaction for both women and men, girls and boys. SI will use at least 1% of this budget towards protection mainstreaming activities.
