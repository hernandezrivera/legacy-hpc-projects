Needs

The Darfur crisis is not only protracted but also a forgotten one. Funding has decreased over the years while critical humanitarian and recovery needs remain. External shocks (conflict, natural disasters) as well as poverty, eroded livelihoods and lack of access to essential resources and basic services means that basic living conditions of disaster affected communities is worsening. While longer term development approaches are needed to tackle this situation, excessive mortality linked to such a situation is also to be addressed through live-saving and targeted interventions focusing on most at risk population: The 2019 HNO highlighted that many IDPs, returnees and host communities remain extremely vulnerable to the impact of both natural disasters and conflict-related displacement. Darfur remains the most affected area in the country, and the two states, Central and South Darfur, have large numbers of people in need of humanitarian assistance, 443,311 and 710,775 respectively (HNO2019). Access to Safe Water: The WASH assessments in 2018 show large gaps in service delivery for both SS refugees and IDPs eg. in Bilel access to water is reported by households at 7 and 12 litres per person per day (lppd). Infrastructure assessments showed gaps in reliable access with breaks in system operations. In the case of Al Radom, the community depends on a single water system, which is insufficient. NCA’s KAP survey in the area reports that people access only 2 lppd of safe drinking water, well below the sector’s agreed target. Without ongoing humanitarian support, even this access would rapidly disappear. Improved Sanitation and Hygiene: The assessments show that, much in line with access to safe drinking water, access to sanitation as well as the ability to practice good hygiene measures are lacking. While access to HH toilets in some of the project locations is quite good (largely due to earlier ECHO and other donor engagement), Al Radom still suffers from too few toilets for too many people – 75% of HHs report that they defecate in the open, and of the 25% who report access to some kind of toilet, 40% of them share communal facilities. Assessments in Rokero show a similar situation. The situation in school sanitation is worse, and our assessments show student to toilet ratios of 1:390, and that the majority of schools do not have functional toilets at all.

Activities

Water:• Construct water points in IDPs, host community, returnees, and pastoralists communities and villages of return.• Rehabilitate existing water sources at camps and host/Nomadic communities and villages of return.• monitor Water quality on daily and bi weekly bases.• Maintain adequate water supply in the IDP’s camps.Sanitation:• construct and rehabilitate latrines in IDP camps, host community and schools in programme areas.• Hand washing facilities installed in all schools supported.• conduct Vector control exercises in all communities and camps of programme areas.• Community waste management approaches enhanced to all programme coverage areas.Hygiene Promotion:• Hygiene promotion campaigns and workshops conducted in target communities using PRA and PHAST approaches.• Women groups formed and supported in dissemination of safe and good hygiene behaviours.• Community message billboards, talking compounds and IEC materials distributed in programme areas.• Conduct household village competitions. • Partner hygiene promotion in 2 villages, with community led initiatives.Capacity building:• Water committees trained on water tariff and cost recovery mechanisms.• National staff and partners trained on CHAST, BCC and CLTS approaches and IGAs.• Leadership and sustainability trainings conducted for targeted IDP and returnee communities• Training workshops on solar system operation, trouble shooting and minor repairs conducted.

Impact on environment/natural resources

NCA will ensure that mitigation measures are in place. All the means put in place to avoid or mitigate negative impacts of usual WASH activities on the environment will be considered in the project. Different level of environmental care will be considered according to level of priority. This particular type of measures will be put in place at the earliest stages of the programming phase even in an emergency context. An effective way for preserving environment in emergencies will be the inclusion of environmental consideration in contingency planning of local and other external stakeholders. NCA for example will prioritize solar systems instead of fuel based water systems at all the operational areas.
