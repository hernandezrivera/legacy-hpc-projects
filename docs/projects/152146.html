Project Summary

The crisis in northeast Nigeria is a protection and food security crisis, with an impending threat of famine that requires scaling up of life-saving humanitarian assistance whilst working with the Government and other partners to ensure that assistance also contributes to the resilience and durable solutions of the displaced population and host communities. NRC food security response for 2019 will target 10,000 households in Borno state and has two key objectives. The first objective of the of the response strategy for 2019 will focus on the provision of immediate food assistance to address both acute and chronic food and nutrition challenges faced by IDPs, returnees, non-displaced and host community households in accessible but hard to reach areas in Borno state. The emergency food assistance component will target 2600 households and directly contributes to preventing starvation and acute malnutrition among children living in the targeted very poor households. Without assistance, the targeted very poor households would face a 70% monthly food deficit from May to October 2019. Very poor households will be supported with monthly e-voucher of EUR59.85 to cover 70% of the agreed and validated WFP, Food Sector Working Group (FSWG) and the Cash Working Group (CWG) food minimum expenditure basket (FMEB) of NGN 3,532.52 /person/day based on the prices of local rice and maize meals at 2100 KCal/p/day. Food assistance is targeting the lean season during which very poor households are most vulnerable due to high food prizes and reduced farm labour income opportunities. The second objective is to support access to reliable livelihoods through capacity building and provision of agricultural input support for both rain fed and dry season crop production. NRC plans to support 7,400 households with agricultural inputs. Whilst the agriculture input kit will vary depending on location, NRC will ensure the input kits in specific locations are harmonized with other partners within the FSWG. Beneficiaries will also be targeted for supplementary activities that include awareness sessions on basic hygiene, preparation and consumption of balanced diet. In order to minimize or mitigate the risks of domestic or gender based violence for the targeted women, NRC will mobilize and engage community leaders such as the Bulamas and Lawans and communities at large to sensitize program objectives, target groups, and targeting criteria. NRC will ensure protection mainstreaming throughout the implementation of the projects; insuring the most vulnerable people are targeted for assistance. Assistance modalities will be designed to ensure all groups of vulnerable groups have access to assistance. The design and scheduling of activities will ensure that women are able to participate in the project through ensuring the timing of activities allow them to do other household duties according to culture and context.

Needs

The humanitarian crisis in north-east Nigeria continues as hostilities between Nigerian security forces and non-state armed groups enter their ninth year. Civilians still bear the brunt of the conflict that has resulted in widespread displacement, lack of protection, destroyed infrastructure and collapsed basic services. The food and nutrition crisis is of massive proportions with an estimated 7.7 million people in the three most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe now depend on humanitarian assistance for their survival (2018 HRP) and it is feared that a slide back into conflict in these accessible areas could cut populations off from current assistance. Without assistance, the number of people food insecure will rise dramatically. They will pass the approximately 3.7 million people projected to be in crisis or emergency phases of food and nutrition security during the 2018 lean season (Cadre Harmonisé phases 3 to 5), figures that were already not inclusive of populations in the Guzamala, Marte, Kala/Balge and Abadam LGAs where data could not be gathered due to access constraints. Food security remains extremely fragile with large populations in northeast Nigeria projected to continue to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes from October 2018 to January 2019. Some hard-to-reach areas which have poorly functioning markets and a lack of livelihood opportunities are projected to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes from October 2018 to January 2019. Whilst IDPs are the most likely to have food security and livelihoods needs, non-displaced populations are also now included among those in need . Most of the IDPs living in host communities have limited access to food assistance and for those receiving assistance, this is irregular. NRC targeting data indicate that 90 percent of IDPs have poor food consumption scores (FCS) while those with acceptable FCS have a high coping strategy index (CPI).Malnutrition as a result of food insecurity remains a major challenge in the northeast. An estimated 940,000 children between 6–59 months in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe are acutely malnourished. Some 440,000 have severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 500,000 have moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). Since mid-July 2018, more than 25% of children arriving from inaccessible areas in Bama town, Borno, are screening positive for SAM and 48% are screening positive for global acute malnutrition (GAM).

Activities

1. Targeting and selection: A consultative process facilitated by NRC will be set up to undertake the geographical selection of targeted communities at LGA level. In each community, the most vulnerable and food insecure households will be registered for food assistance as well as agriculture assistance (for those with land). The selection of the 10,000 household beneficiaries will be made on the basis of household vulnerability to severe food insecurity using a combination of food consumption score and the livelihood coping strategies to assess food security status of households. NRC will use door to door registration approach to reduce the risks associated with gathering large crowds. NRC will sensitize communities and advocate for women to register as direct beneficiaries to receive the assistance.2. Selection of Food and Inputs Vendors: Food assistance will be provided through a network of both local and Maiduguri based pre-selected food vendors who are selected through a rapid market mapping and competitive bidding process. The winning bidders then sign contracts with NRC and receive training on humanitarian principles, NRC code of conduct, and the e-voucher system. In order to do no harm, prior to the signing of the contracts, the beneficiaries will vet the selected vendors.3. Printing, distribution and Activation of Smartcards: NRC in Nigeria has a partnership with renowned e-transfer service provider Red Rose company. NRC shall be responsible for the printing and distribution of smart cards and for providing food vendors and agro dealers with the electronic e-transfer technology devices (Android and Bluetooth printers) to be used for processing the food and input purchases. Each beneficiary will receive a smart card and a system generated unique Personal Identification Number (PIN) that will be distributed at the beginning of the project after a thorough beneficiary verification process and finalization of the database. Beneficiaries will only be able to use smartcards at selected participating food vendors or agro-dealers that will display NRC visibility including their entitlements.4. Redemption of vouchers for food and agriculture inputs: NRC staff will carry out intensive sensitization activities to ensure that all targeted beneficiaries are able to safely access the food vendors and agro dealers and redeem their vouchers to purchase food and agriculture inputs. NRC will use a variety of sensitization methods including use of NRC staff, community leaders, committees, mobile calling, texting, flyers, banners and door to door sensitization and mobilization of project beneficiaries ensuring 100% of targeted beneficiaries have full knowledge of how the smartcards work, how and where to find vendors. NRC will upload the equivalent in Naira each beneficiary smart card each month. In cases where beneficiaries are elderly and/or chronically ill and are unable to physically collect their food or inputs, a verified family member who is endorsed by the community leader may collect on behalf of the beneficiary. NRC food security staff will closely monitor all such cases and the disbursement can only be authorized by an NRC field staff member. 5. M&E: M&E activities will be undertaken by the food security team during the implementation of the project. Monitoring will take place before assistance to determine baseline values for outcome indicators, during implementation to assess progress and at the end to measure change. To address broader issues potentially related to food security programming such as corruption and sexual abuse, NRC will add other complaints/feedback accountability mechanism (e.g. complaints hotline and complains boxes) that will be available to the target population to enable beneficiaries to provide feedback. Throughout the implementation, NRC will ensure protection is mainstreamed to reduce the negative impact of the project.
