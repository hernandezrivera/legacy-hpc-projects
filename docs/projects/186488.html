Project Summary

Objectives
The general objective of the project is to improve the access of women of reproductive age, adolescents and young people to quality RH / GBV services in the six (6) humanitarian regions. Specifically, it will be: (i) Improve the SSR service offer in the six priority regions of the humanitarian response; (ii) Strengthen the capacities of structures to ensure holistic care for survivors of GBV; (iii) Strengthen community engagement on SRH and GBV issues by developing innovative advocacy actions involving men through the strategy of positive masculinity and (iv) Ensure the coordination and capitalization of project interventions.
●Short summary.
Obstacles to access and continuity of SRH care and services caused by the closure of health centers and the displacement caused are sources of excess morbidity (unwanted pregnancies, abortions, STIs / HIV / AIDS, difficulties in taking in charge of childbirth complications, obstetric fistula, sexual violence, survival sex and other forms of GBV) and excess maternal and neonatal mortality in localities which already have the lowest sexual and reproductive health indicators in the country. To address these priorities, there is an urgent need to improve the supply of SRH services including GBV in priority regions of the humanitarian response. As such, UNFPA is proposing this project to improve access and continuity to care and services for Sexual and Reproductive Health and Based Violence. This project will (i) strengthen the availability of services that save lives for women and girls according to minimum standards in humanitarian situations, (ii) mitigate the multiple risks to which women and girls are exposed, (iii) ensure continuity SRH / GBV services in areas of difficult access and, (iv) ensure effective coordination of areas of SR / GBV responsibility. Better still, this project will ensure the sustainability of UNFPA interventions by strengthening community engagement on RH / GBV issues.
●Justifications
(Urgence, Obligation, Unpredictability, Non-Substitutability)
The worsening security situation deeply affects the access of vulnerable populations, in particular women and girls, to emergency obstetric and neonatal care and to clinical rape treatment services. It also limits the ability of affected people (especially women and young adolescents who remain sexually active) to access services that help reduce HIV transmission, prevent unwanted pregnancies and clandestine abortions. According to the health cluster bulletin dated April 2021, 6% of health facilities in Burkina Faso are closed and 19.3% are functioning at a minimum. This situation affects approximately, 809,608 people who are deprived of care and / or who have a problem of continuous access to the provision of sexual and reproductive health.
The current response in the context of preventing and combating GBV does not cover the needs in terms of multisectoral care. The system clearly lacks adequate structures for holistic care of survivors. Indeed, the socio-economic reintegration component of survivors of GBV is inoperative in all humanitarian regions. As for the medical care, it sometimes gives rise to costs which are not always within the reach of the survivors.
In view of the above, the major challenges remain: (i) the lack of integrated care centers for survivors of GBV; (ii) the difficulties for the actors to assist some survivors linked to the reduction of humanitarian space; (iii) the low availability of basic social services (health, education) due to the security situation; (iv) the low coverage of the six humanitarian regions in services for the prevention and care of GBV. To address these challenges, project interventions will focus on a comprehensive approach that takes into account structural elements and social identities that fully understand and integrate the indivisibility of human rights.
