Project Summary

According to the OCHA Situation Update release on 29th November 2024, over 1.4 million people remain affected by flooding across 44 counties and three Administrative Areas. More than half of the affected people are in Jonglei and Northern Bahr El Ghazal states. Over 379,000 people are displaced across 22 counties couple with a month into the cholera outbreak, suspected cases continue to be recorded in the flood-hit locations. On 18 November 2024, the three UN agencies Joint release stated that almost 2.1 million children are at-risk of malnutrition, up from 1.65 million. Children are returning to nutrition centres multiple times throughout the year as they continue to suffer from poor access to safe drinking water and sanitation. Illness is a major contributing factor to their malnutrition and almost half all children included in the data collected had been ill.
The humanitarian situation for children and their families in South Sudan remains critical due to ongoing conflicts, floods, and other disasters, with 3.07 million children and their families urgently needing lifesaving child protection services in 2025. Key challenges include family separations, recruitment into armed groups, and violence, particularly affecting adolescent boys and girls. Vulnerable urban children may turn to gangs, while children with disabilities and survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) face increased risks and limited service access. Flooding has worsened family separations, GBV against children, including child marriage, child labor, and sexual exploitation, with limited WASH and privacy facilities for children and caregivers. Many children continue to be abducted, recruited into armed groups, or exploited.
Acute food insecurity is widespread due to the destruction of livelihoods from floods and conflict. The 2025 (CP AoR) strategy emphasizes working with communities and partners to provide child-focused responses. This includes tracking service delivery, updating referral pathways, and training community leaders in Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) to ensure effective mobilization and adaptability to changing needs. The most vulnerable children include unaccompanied or separated children, children with disabilities, those with chronic or acute illnesses, child-headed households, child-mothers, and children of elderly or disabled caregivers. Without proper care and family-based support, these children may struggle to engage in everyday activities like play, learning, or social interaction.
CINA will provide lifesaving child protection services to 9,500 children and their families, including 475 children with disabilities, in conflict and disaster-affected counties. CINA will respond with child-focused interventions, including case management, psychosocial support, and material assistance, while also working to strengthen local child protection systems in hard-to-reach areas targeting 9,500 beneficiaries; 300 adults (156 women, 144 men) 9,200 children (4,416 boys, 4,784 girls) as mentioned below Jonglei, Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Western Bahr El Ghazal, Unity, and Lakes states. Services will include case management for separated and unaccompanied children, children associated with armed groups, GBV survivors, and children with severe psychosocial distress. CINA will also support caregivers and caseworkers with psychosocial support services and will conduct child protection awareness campaigns, especially around flood risks. The interventions will align with the Protection Cluster HRP Objectives for 2025, as well as key child protection standards and guidelines. CINA will work closely with partners and non-CP actors to enhance coordination and build synergies across the humanitarian, peace, and development sectors, ensuring a comprehensive, age- and gender-sensitive response.

1. Needs

According to the OCHA Situation Update release on 29th November 2024, over 1.4 million people remain affected by flooding across 44 counties and three Administrative Areas. More than half of the affected people are in Jonglei and Northern Bahr El Ghazal states. It is estimated that 3.07 million South Sudanese children and their families (83% children and 17% caregivers) will need child protection services in 2025. Ongoing emergencies driven by floods, conflict, displacement, economic crisis and poverty continue to expose boys and girls to increased risks and vulnerabilities. This challenging situation has weakened the protective environment for children and severely impacted families' ability to care for them, leaving them at risk of violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Many South Sudanese families live below the poverty line, and the destruction of livelihoods due to floods and conflict has left them in dire financial distress, leaving them feeling helpless and without hope. CINA will prioritize vulnerable children, adolescents, and young women, particularly in the conflict-affected regions such as Upper Nile and Jonglei. Efforts will focus on those facing family separation and GBV, with ongoing assessments and community feedback to ensure timely, needs-based responses throughout the implementation period. Approximately 2.2 million children in host communities, 610,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs), and 320,000 returnees, including a significant number of children, are in need of child protection services. Floods have destroyed shelters, crops, and drinking water sources, making it difficult for children to access child-friendly spaces, schools, health facilities, and markets, while also hindering humanitarian access to these areas. In 2025, South Sudan is expected to face a severe child protection crisis, particularly in conflict-affected areas. Major issues include family separations, recruitment into armed groups, and violence, with adolescent boys and girls being especially vulnerable. Vulnerable urban children may turn to gangs, while children with disabilities and survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) face increased risks and limited access to services. CINA will respond with child-focused interventions, including case management, psychosocial support, and material assistance, while also working to strengthen local child protection systems in hard-to-reach areas targeting 9,500 beneficiaries; 300 adults (156 women, 144 men) 9,200 children (4,416 boys, 4,784 girls) as mentioned below Jonglei, Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Western Bahr El Ghazal, Unity, and Lakes states. In these crisis conditions, children face trauma, loss, fear, and anxiety, and lack psychosocial support, recreational activities, and educational opportunities. States like Jonglei, Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Western Bahr El Ghazal, Unity, and Lakes have among the highest rates of documented child protection issues, including unaccompanied and separated children, missing children, child labor, children with disabilities, GBV, recruitment by armed groups, child abduction, child marriage, and other harmful cultural practices. This situation is exacerbated by a lack of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, as well as a shortage of core child protection services like family tracing and reunification, case management, and reintegration for children affected by armed groups. Many children remain untreated and unable to access the services they urgently need. The CP AoR's 2025 strategy emphasizes collaboration with communities and partners to ensure effective, child-focused responses. Key actions will include tracking service delivery, updating referral pathways, and training community leaders in Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) to promote efficient mobilization and adaptability to changing needs.

2. Project activities

CINA prioritizes vulnerable children, adolescents, and young women in conflict-affected areas like Upper Nile, Jonglei, GPAA, Eastern Equatoria, Western Equatoria, Lakes, Unity and Western Bahr El Ghazal States. This project will be Targeting 9,500 beneficiaries; 300 adults (156 women, 144 men) 9,200 children (4,784 girls, 4,416 boys) including 475 children with disabilities. The following interventions shall be implemented during the project period: 1. In line with objective 1 and protection cluster strategy for 2025, CINA will provide case management services to unaccompanied and separated children and other highly vulnerable children. Using the interagency case management SOPs, CINA proposes to provide comprehensive sets of protection packages and services to children separated from their families and other highly vulnerable children such as orphans, children living/working on the streets, children with disabilities, children and youth gangs, children formerly associated with armed groups, etc. The services, to be provided by trained case workers that will be drawn from the affected communities, and who must be youth and adolescents themselves, will include family tracing and reunification, reintegration packages, enrolment in schools and referral to other services in nutrition, livelihood and economic empowerment. Throughout the four prioritized states, CINA will reach at least 1,000 children with comprehensive case management services. This number includes those children to be reached with family tracing and reunification services as well as placement and support in family and institutional (interim care center) based care mobile teams to provide integrated package of child protection assistance including psychosocial support, psychological first aid, case management and capacity building of local actors, 400 with Case management individual cash based assistance. CINA will deploy existing pool of trained social workers/child protection community volunteers to run activities in remote and hard-to-reach locations and supporting 8,000 children with MHPSS interventions. CINA is operating child and adolescent friendly spaces where children management process and referral system. In Jonglei and Upper Nile states, CINA has existing projects (funded until September 2025) where at least 110 case workers have been trained and deployed. This technical capacity will boost project implementation in those locations. 2. In order to address the psychological needs of conflict affected families, CINA will provide psychosocial support services through the community-based systems as well as through static child friendly spaces and mobile interventions programming 3. Through community networks that will be strengthened by social workers and child protection workers. CINA will train 100 child protection and non child protection work force on CPiE, CINA will create awareness on the child protection situation at community levels. A total of 150 sessions (representing 2% of the CP AOR target) in the selected priority counties. Similarly, CINA will participate and facilitate capacity building efforts of community-based networks, social workers, teachers, and caregivers and non-child protection workers in various sectors to help in the preventing and addressing child protection needs in their communities.
