Project Summary

According to the 2024 Syria HNO, there has been a 4% increase in the sector PIN compared to the previous year, with 7.2M school-aged children and teachers needing emergency education services. Moreover, according to the MSNA conducted by WH in Dec 2023 across 15 SDs in NWS:
- Education was reported among the top three priority needs in more than a third of assessed communities.
- Most commonly reported EDUCATION needs of populations were school equipment (18%), school stationery (17%), qualified teachers (15%), additional classrooms (13%), teaching/learning materials (12%), and teacher training (10%).
- Most commonly reported barriers to access education faced by HHs were families needing boys to work/support at home (52%), families needing girls to work/support at home (35%), and parents cannot afford to send children to school (31%).
- Most commonly reported issues with education facilities/services were insufficient teaching/learning supplies (46%), low quality of education (32%), lack of recognized certification (28%), damaged facilities (25%), insufficient number of qualified teachers (23%), and overcrowding (15%).
The proposed intervention's overall objective is to contribute directly to the education sector's 2024 HRP objectives by improving equitable access for school-aged children to safe, inclusive, and quality learning environments to foster their resilience. This will be achieved through implementing a package of education activities for 25 schools in NW
Syria located in underserved and high-needs areas, where 13000 children (6000 boys and 7000 girls 'including 300 CwD ~2.5%', 5-17 years old) are enrolled (70% IDPs).
WH seeks to increase children's enrollment in the targeted schools by supporting a quality learning environment.
Based on 'WoS_PiN_Severity_2024' statistics, 25% of the project's total direct targeted BNFs are within severity scale 5 (Jandairis and Dana sub-districts), and 75% are within severity scale 4 (Raju, Afrin, A'zaz, Aghtrin, Maaret Tamsrin, and Harim).
WH aims to meet the objective of this program through the following interventions:
1. Provision of formal and non-formal education for children. NFE will include ALP, Curriculum B, Remedial classes, Catch-up, and SLP for OOSC more than one academic year based on age & period of dropping out.
2. Small-scale rehabilitation of damaged schools and establishment of other safe temporary/alternative learning spaces through a gender-sensitive and disability-friendly approach.
3. Conduct a BTL campaign at the community level and in the targeted camps.
4. Provide schools' monthly operational costs, including recreational materials, refreshments, cleaning, hygiene materials, stationery, fuel for heating, and providing students' and teachers' kits.
5. All teachers and education personnel (500: 225 men & 275 women) will be provided monthly incentives to increase motivation and ensure school retention.
6. The quality of education will be improved through contextualized and targeted teacher training and further support their professional development within each school by delivering (CSG, TiCC, PSEA, Cholera, and COVID-19) trainings.
7. Provision of schools’ furniture and students’ desks.
8. Providing weekly PSS sessions to overcome the harsh experience of war conditions and to motivate them to continue their studies at schools.
To ensure safe and easy access for CWD (boys & girls), WH will provide schools with tools and equipment for CWD such as ramps, Iron stair handrails, special toilets, not connected desks and chairs with adjustable height functions in addition to providing transportation for CWD.
The project will make sure that children engaging in child labor or recruited for military purposes, or living with older or disabled caregivers or adolescent girls at risk of early or forced marriage are given special attention and have their own space to participate in the project activities and are properly referred as per Protection cluster guidelines.

Protection Risk Analysis:Risk1: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff, or related entities.MS: Learning facilities should have CSG policies and systems in place by the WH should be regularly monitored and corrective action is taken when needed. The goal of child safeguarding is to create and maintain a safe learning environment through preventive actions that protect children from actions, or inactions that may lead to harm. Risk2: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. MS: All teachers will sign a code of conduct once hired, the code of conduct will be explained carefully. Principals/headmasters, teachers, and caregivers will be made aware of positive discipline methods. Teachers and school personnel will be trained on the prevention and reporting of violence, physical punishment, and bullying. Risk3: Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe. MS: Learning facilities will be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected including stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electricity, water, etc. All works will be supervised and monitored by WH technical staff.Risk4: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women & girls. L:H/I:H. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts & Address any SEA complaints. A child safeguarding risk assessment, gender, GBV audit, and conflict sensitivity analysis are conducted for each program. These identify gaps, inform the project design, and mitigate protection risks for children and their families while accessing WH facilities and using WH services. In addition, WH has prioritized more sustained capacity-building for staff related to disability and disability inclusion, as well as on gender mainstreaming and GBV.
