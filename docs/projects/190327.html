Project Summary

According to the latest DTM dataset, there are 2,124,983 internally displaced people (IDPs) living in 1,577IDP sites across the country as result of conflict, drought, and seasonal and flash floods. Of these, 106,440, 391,260, and 871,176 live in Afar, Amhara, and Somali Regions respectively.
The conflict in the Tigray region and intercommunal and trans-boundary conflicts account for the majority of displacement. The significant escalation of violence continues to cause large-scale human suffering and dire humanitarian needs, with loss of lives, grave violations of human rights and mass forced displacement. Prolonged drought as well as flash-floods continue to affect millions of people and displace thousands every year. Lastly the covid-19 pandemic has further disrupted the social and economic order.
IDP communities living in IDP sites across these Regions continue to face multi-sectoral critical gaps in services. IDP site living conditions are characterized by overcrowding, critically affecting the ability to practice key COVID-19 mitigation behavioral strategies, such as social distancing, while simultaneously lacking access to adequate and safe water sources, and basic health services.
In response to the service provision gap, ACTED aims to improve the living conditions and protection of 256,459 individuals (comprised of 221,552 IDPs and a further 34,907 host community individuals) living in 54 IDP sites across the 4 targeted regions through the following CCCM activities:
- coordination of humanitarian response and information sharing;
- empowerment of site residents through establishment of governance structures;
- site improvements for increased protection or residents;
- communication channels’ establishments/strengthening for most vulnerable individuals.
Due to the mixed nature of target IDP sites, which typically include IDPs and host communities, ACTED will formalize the inclusion of the latter as beneficiaries through their representation in Site Maintenance Committees (SMCs) and Camp Management Committees (CMCs). More broadly, host community members will also benefit from CCCM activities under this Action.
Moreover, the proposed Action takes seriously the responsibility of addressing all forms of marginalization, exclusion and inequalities in access while upholding the principles of gender equality and non-discrimination. ACTED will address the susceptibility to marginalization in participatory processes of vulnerable groups by prioritizing inclusive policies in program design and implementation. Women, men, girls and boys, among other groups, often experience crises very differently due to their different roles within the family and society, as well as unequal power dynamics that exist between and among them. Specific attention has therefore been allocated in the design of the present intervention to prevent and reduce those vulnerabilities.

1. - Projects activities

ACTED proposes to expand its CCCM standard programming to a total 54 IDP sites across Ethiopia. Please note ACTED proposed 2022 HRP also contains currently funded CCCM activities (for a total funded of 1.3M). The core activities currently being undertaken by ACTED and to be further replicated are proposed as follows. CREATION OF SITE MAINTENANCE COMMITTEES (SMCs) AND CONDUCT OF SITE IMPROVEMENT ACTIVITIESACTED will establish one Site Maintenance Committees (SMCs) and its members will undergo a one-day training, covering topics such as site maintenance standards, site planning and fire safety to enable SMCs to identify emerging site maintenance needs and conduct site maintenance and site improvement activities through an incentivized labour scheme. ACTED will also provide technical support and site maintenance toolkits, materials and fire safety equipment. ESTABLISHMENT AND/OR STRENGTHENING OF SITE LEVEL GOVERNANCE STRUCTURESACTED will establish site level governance structures such as Camp Management Committees (CMCs) and Women Committees (WCs). ACTED will ensure the committees are age, gender and diversity balanced, with a strong focus on ensuring meaningful female participation, as well as those from underrepresented groups such as the elderly or those living with a disability. ESTABLISHMENT OF COMMUNICATION CHANNELS FOR COLLECTION AND DISSEMINATION OF INFORMATION TO COMMUNITIES Community participation and Communication with Communities (CwC) is central to ACTED and intervention, by providing affected populations with reliable information on their rights, services available and how to access them, as well as relevant information to enhance their safety and protection. OPERATION OF COMPLAINTS, RESPONSE AND FEEDBACK MECHANISM (CRFM)ACTED will provide Complaints, Response and Feedback Mechanisms (CRFM) in each target site. The CRFM include dedicated 24/7 CRFM hotline, complaint boxes and text messages/WhatsApp. Consolidated CRFM reports will be developed to ensure a holistic overview of beneficiary feedback and consistency in response. MONITORING AND ADDRESSING RISK AND VULNERABILITIES THROUGH SAFETY AUDITSSafety audits will be conducted in collaboration with local community members and protection partners/cluster in order to identify key protection risks, gaps and needs present in the target IDP sites. Safety audit reports produced by ACTED and its partners will specifically aim to understand protection risks faced by different marginalized and vulnerable groups, inclusive of risks faced by women, girls, boys, and persons living with disabilities and recommended mitigation actions will be directly integrated in the site improvement activity to be undertaken in this project. ENHANCE COORDINATIN OF HUMANITARIAN SERVICES AT SITE LEVEL THROUGH REGULAR COORDINATION MEETINGSACTED and will establish coordination mechanism at regional, Woreda and site levels and convene bi-weekly meetings with all the service providers to discuss among others progress, challenges and possible solutions. ENHANCE INFORMATION MANAGEMENT THROUGH DEVELOPMENT OF SERVICE MAPIING, 4WS, SITREPS ACTED will carry out consistent analysis of needs, gaps, and trends through service mapping, 4Ws report, service monitoring, and sitreps and will regularly share these information management products with the National CCCM Cluster. CAPACITY BUILDING FOR HUMANITARIAN AND LOCAL STAKEHOLDERS TO STRENGTHEN CCCMACTED will organize one training per Woreda for including humanitarian partners and local authorities. Participants will be trained on key modules such as: Accountability to Affected Populations, Humanitarian principles, Roles & Responsibilities, Settlement Design and Standards, Coordination, Information Management, Community Participation, Care and Maintenance and Protection mainstreaming.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

ACTED works closely with the government entity in charge of IDP affairs and namely National Disaster Risk Management Commission (NDRC) and especially their counterparts at Regional, Zonal and Woreda level. ACTED also works closely with the CCCM and other relevant Clusters at National and Regional levels. Similarly, ACTED works with different working groups such as the durable solution working group (DS WG), durable solution technical advisory group (DR TAG) etc. In line, with the Nationalization strategy, ACTED works with National NGOs such as UNISOD and RACIDA. ACTED also works directly with different community committees such as Camp Management Committees (CMCs), Women Committee (WCs), Youth Committees (YCs) etc. With respect to stakeholders engagement, ACTED is a member of the Somali Regional Durable Solutions Working Group (DSWG), the Technical Advisory Group (TAG) of the Somali Regional DSWG. ACTED is also the CCCM Sub-National Cluster Coordinator for Somali Region and will use all these platforms including the regional ICCG, Disaster Risk Management Bureau (DRMB) to advocate for durable solutions for IDPs. ACTED developed a CCCM Mobile Response Strategy for Afar region, which was endorsed by the National CCCM cluster. ACTED will adopt this approach to reach out to IDPs in out of camps in its target locations. As a large component of beneficiaries' displacement is caused by climate, ACTED will ensure flood alerts and early warning messages are reaching communities promptly for them to take all the necessary precautions measures. ACTED will through the cluster coordination meetings remind government sectors and partners to work together to minimize flood-related health risks such as cholera and waterborne disease through preparedness and early action.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

ACTED will establish/maintain Complaints, Response and Feedback Mechanisms (CRFM) in each target site. The CRFM will utilize a range of communication modalities determined in consultation with communities at project onset. This may include among others direct engagement with ACTED Accountability staff, dedicated 24/7 CRFM hotline, physical complaint boxes and text messages/WhatsApp. Consolidated CRFM reports will be developed to ensure a holistic overview of beneficiary feedback and consistency in the response. The feedback will be classified on a monthly basis and categorized according to sensitivity as well as directly shared with country coordination and HQ investigation unit. ACTED applies a five-tier scoring system for complaints received via the CRFM, enabling ranking of feedback, with each level from one to five having pre-determined procedures for due course of action. Complaints are also assessed with respect to ACTED’s Code of Conduct, which includes the aim of upholding ACTED’s accountability to those it serves and prevent any forms of abuse or exploitation. For the most urgent and/or serious complaints (sensitivity levels 4 and 5), the response pathway will be launched the same day. This system ensures consistent handling of complaints in line with accountability standards and enables appropriate action to be taken as required. Additionally, the CRFM builds on a gender-sensitive framework and will detect any allegations of GBV, fraud, conflict of interest and diversion of aid. Where possible ACTED will support the referral of GBV cases or reported cases to ad-hoc agencies for handling of such complaints. Overall, the CRFM will ensure a direct, confidential and accessible feedback mechanism for all beneficiaries and wider stakeholders to register feedback whilst underpinning accountability to affected populations. As such, the CRFM emphasizes the inclusive participation of the communities, building trust and enabling them to influence decisions for themselves. Given the extremely fluid operational context, ACTED will undertake the following mitigation measures: Regularly attend the various field level meetings in Semera such as the access working group, Regional ICCG, security signal groups cluster coordination, etc. to be well updated on the new developments in the region and to plan accordingly ahead of time. Preposition all project supplies and deliver to IDP sites as they open up Apply the CCCM Mobile Approach in target locations in Afar region.  Maintain a CCCM Mobile team in Semera who are deployable in short time as first line responders to new displacements.  In accordance with the CCCM Mobile Approach, carry out rapid needs assessments to timely identify the needs of IDPs and advocate for multi-sectoral response using the existing cluster system in the region.
