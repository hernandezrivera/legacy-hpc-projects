Project Summary

This education Project is a multisectoral intervention in selected municipalities affected by various crisis, to enhance the access to quality inclusive education for school aged boys and girls. The project aims at creating and showing successful and replicable models to enhance – through education and protection - the human capital in the selected communities.
The program focuses on improving the equitable access to safe learning, by acting on situations where infrastructures are insufficient or need rehabilitation or adaptation towards inclusiveness and accessibility. Thanks to the progress made on digital learning before and during the COVID 19 crisis, access to learning in Cameroon, is meant beyond the classroom, as the digital learning platforms are available for out of school children as well as to complement the teaching in the classroom. In all communities’ children with disabilities who already access formal learning will be supported by the program to stay in school, excel in their study and be motivated to productive members of the communities. Also, those children who are still out of the reach, and out of school, will be proactively identified through this project support, and will be given access to relevant education and training opportunities. Beyond the field-based interventions on inclusive education, the program will influence and support the finalization of national policies towards inclusive education.
Additionally, the program aims at improving the learning outcomes in basic subjects, as well as the acquisition of life-skills by boys and girls in pre-primary, primary and secondary school age. In the formal education structures teachers will receive capacity building, teaching learning and vocational training materials will be distributed, the protective learning environment will be promoted, and a special action will be targeting girls, to send them and maintain them in school, and to finally empower them. The impact of improved learning outcomes will be measured in formal and non-formal settings, to generate knowledge on successful packages of interventions in target municipalities, and for the scaling up of successful models elsewhere.
Thirdly the project aims at influencing and supporting the creation of a protective environment around the children targeted by the project. In selected municipalities holistic interventions will be implemented using the school as a platform, as well as identifying with key stakeholders the protection needs of the communities surrounding the targeted schools. The main bottlenecks which obstacle access to education and learning, (birth registration, violence against children, early marriage, early pregnancies) will be addressed by convergent programming in communities.
A specific mental health psychosocial support will be provided to heal the class of teachers from NWSW who are displaced and those who are working under very difficult circumstances. The project will support the implementation of the sexual exploitation and abuse guidance, and the implementation of the Safe Schools Education endorsed by Cameroon government in 2018.
The project strategically opts for the communal approach, to develop and give visibility to ‘model municipalities where access to formal and vocational education, learning, protection, and education governance are enhanced through a multi-sectoral convergent approach. All categories of children in selected communities will be reached by the interventions that overall offer a protective environment to in and out of school boys and girls. The project aims at building the resilience of the Education and Vocational sector in Cameroon by acting on the wellbeing of students, teachers, and parents in communities, and by addressing the important emergency related bottlenecks as well as the structural challenges that negatively impact the access to quality education for the most vulnerable children in Cameroon.

1 - Besoins / Needs

A diverse set of bottlenecks hinder access to equitable and inclusive quality education for boys and girls in Cameroon, among which protection related challenges require particular attention. While systemic structural challenges - i.e. the lack of birth certificates for enrolled pupils - persist in the most vulnerable zones of the country, the ongoing crises have exacerbated protection risks for school aged girls and boys, hindering their access to basic services. As families continue to suffer from displacement and poverty and host community carry the burden of the influx of the displaced populations, risks are increased of sexual exploitation, and violence, against young Cameroonian girls particularly. The teaching force of the North West and South West is greatly impacted due to the displacement, and the continuous threats against schools. Reference to reports from participatory assessments, monitoring and studies conducted in IDP hosting areas (Yaoundé and Douala ) by UNHCR Cameroon and partners, access to education remains hindered for school aged children, especially for adolescents and youth. In the regions of East-Adamaoua-North of Cameroon, quarterly reports of the last school year 2020-2021, indicates that only 3% (1,485/386 girls) of children at secondary level were enrolled. As a result, most refugee youths and adolescents are still exposed to numerous protections risks and engaged in negative coping mechanisms in order to address their immediate needs. Despite ongoing efforts by UNHCR and partners, and according to the humanitarian needs overview and humanitarian Response Plan, the needs have increased due to continuous displacement of the population, the reduction of services and the COVID 19 pandemic. The focus for this project is tackling the impact of vulnerability on children and youth’s ability in Cameroon to access learning, in line with the guiding principles of SDG4 and the increasing complexity of the Cameroonian context. The problem that the AMEF seeks to address can be summarized as follows: A growing proportion of children living in Cameroon is vulnerable to losing or never realizing their right to access education. Meanwhile, for those who are enrolled in schools, vocational centers or alternative educational pathways, the right to learn is compromised by the impact of their vulnerabilities, the limited capacity of the system to absorb additional learners, as well as violence and threats against education institutions and effects of displacement. In line with the SDG4 renewed global commitment to early childhood inclusion, preschool access to education in Cameroon has almost doubled by 2020 reaching 38.83% (compared to 2008), however this level remains underserved by partners. For primary school children (6-11 years old), the net enrolment rate has reached 85.4% in 2020 (gross enrolment rate 114.7%) however, the completion rate remains a major concern, as children’s access to education has been greatly impacted by the crises in the country: only 71% of children at primary level has completed their school year by 2020, a decline following the growth the country has experienced between 2010 (73%) and 2016 (76.7%). For youth, the Government, in coordination with the Ministry of Youth and Education has set up initiatives to promote the socio-economic integration, inclusion, and citizen participation of youth, through socio educational and literacy programs for young people who do not have access to formal education. However, the reach remains limited to date. Schools and universities also experienced additional closures as of March 2020 that were announced by the government to reduce the spread of COVID-19, thus affecting the schooling of 7.2 million students enrolled in public and private institutions.
