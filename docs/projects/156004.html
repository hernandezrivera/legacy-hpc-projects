Project Summary

The intervention aims at supporting vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities through the provision of basic NFIs kits, including mattresses, blankets and winter clothing. A total of 37,500 women, men, boys and girls will be supported with 1) provision of core and essential NFIs and 2) provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs.

Needs

While market access appears to be gradually improving and NFI items are available in markets, many essential, humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining basic items continue to be unaffordable for many people, including clothing. Populations in areas which experience cold conditions during winter continue to need seasonal support (winter kits). The need for clothing, hygiene items and heating fuel are among the most reported NFI needs across the country. Communities reported different NFI needs among different groups based on gender and age. For instance, boys and girls were reported as being in greater need of clothing and shoes; men as in need of heating fuel, light sources, and water containers; women and girls as in need of sanitary pads; women as needing kitchen-related items and; the elderly as requiring blankets, energy and light sources. These findings demonstrate the wide range of items that households continue to struggle to access.

Implementation of activities

AHH will support vulnerable conflict-affected populations selected based on vulnerability criteria, with priority to most vulnerable families such as women-headed HHs, households with PLWs and/or children under 5, elderly, a member chronically ills and/or with physical/mental disability, hosting IDPs for more than 3 months, and/or with more than 7 members. AHH will procure and distribute winter kits (thermal pajamas & underwear) complemented by distributions of mattresses and thermal blankets. The kits will help alleviate the suffering of IDPs, returnees and host communities during the harsh winter weather. The kits will include thermal underwear and pajamas (for both number and size based on needs identified), mattresses and thermal blankets and will be design in accordance to sector standards and to address the different needs of women, men, girls and boys. The distributions will be done through SARC teams of volunteers in most cases and AAH role should be complementary, making sure that the distributions are timely. Moreover, efforts will be out in place to ensure accessibility for all target groups, as well as reduce any risk for the population during the distribution. Priority will be given to female headed families, households with large number of children, elderly and disables and in general AAH will ensure that relevant age/sex groups are considered with all data sex disaggregated throughout the project cycle from assessment and implementation to monitoring and evaluation. AAH and its partners have capacity and willingness to intervene in different areas, therefore several sub-districts are selected as potential targets. The definitive areas of intervention will be defined on a later stage based on needs assessments, according to which the support will target the most vulnerable.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Risk of increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between host community, IDPs and returnees.Likelihood: High, Impact: HighMitigation: AAH regularly participates to sector meetings and coordination with partners operating in the same area to ensure consistency of assistance package. Ensure changes to the package are thoroughly assessed and validated. Identify item/s particularly sensitive to the extent of increasing the risk of tension, and exclude them. AAH and its partners ensure beneficiary selection criteria are clearly communicated widely within targeted communities and selection process is transparent; whenever feasible, local committees will be established to support during the process of selection. AAH and partners engage with community leaders taking into consideration gender balance in the committees to be selected to support in disseminating information in the communities. Field staff (both AAH and partners) and community field monitors will be trained (from both genders) on conflict mitigation techniques.Protection risk: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety)Likelihood: medium, Impact: highMitigation: AAH and its partners ensure distribution points are organized to avoid overcrowding and long waiting period. Pre-consultation with communities is regularly done to agree on best distribution modality to ensure that all different target groups, including those with specific needs, have equal and easy access. Staff involved (both AAH and partners staff) is trained in organizing and implementing distribution, including the monitoring during the distributions and PDM to identify best practices and constraints to be avoided in the future. Protection risk: selection criteria is influence by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, tension in the community and risk of most vulnerable being left out and/or abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion)Likelihood: high Impact: mediumMitigation: the distribution of NFIs specifically targets children in the ages 2 12 (thermal underwear and pajamas), thus AAH and partners will ensure beneficiary selection criteria are clearly communicated within targeted communities and selection process is transparent. Assistance package is in line with sector standards and guidelines. AAH engages with community people (both men and women), to support in disseminating information in the communities. AAH and partners create feedback and complaint mechanism at community level taking into consideration sensitivities in the communities and allowing women to express their complaints in safe manner. AAH and partners staff is trained on conflict mitigation techniques, awareness of relevance of social structures for aid programs, neutral acceptance of AAH programs and red lines when outreaching social structures.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The security situation in targeted areas is unstable and AAH has no access to implement the activities; there are additional unforeseen administrative procedures imposed, and restrictions on access to beneficiaries. Likelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: AAH works and coordinates with authorities and Ministries to maintain access to target areas and beneficiaries, and the capacity to implement activities. AAH will reach out to local authorities and line ministries with trust building activities to further develop relationships and capacity to avoid or mitigate restrictions in AAH capacity to operate in Northern and Southern Syria. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community and humanitarian diplomacy activities on the local level are also considered to contribute to this mitigation measure. Risk 2: Substantive devaluation of the EURO or USD against the Syrian pound; International bank transfers to Syria are fluid and timely.Likelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, timely communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk 3: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruptionLikelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Risk 4: Information gathering activities hampered by suspicion or misunderstandingLikelihood: low. Impact: mediumMitigation: Transparency, trust-building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers
