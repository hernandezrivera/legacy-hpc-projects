Project Summary

The project "Youth for Tomorrow: Building Peace and Green Futures in Cox’s Bazar." aims to empower Youth and adult in refugee camps and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, directly benefiting 3,040 (age18 and above) people and indirectly impacting over 13,992 people. Special focus will be on vulnerable youth and adult with special needs, those who are unskilled, unemployed. Additionally, support will be extended to vulnerable women, families of persons with disabilities, and separated/unaccompanied youth and adult as well as women -headed households. The project’s central focus is to enhance adaptive sustainable economic resilience against the impact of climate change, improve disaster preparedness, promote social cohesion, fostering long-term stability and Self-Reliance and Resilience for these vulnerable groups.
Component 1: Economic Stability and Climate Resilience
Objective: Increase economic stability and climate resilience among adolescence and youth through sustainable practices and Green/Circular Economic Skills.
Key activities:
To foster self-reliance and resilience among refugees and host community, the project will deliver hands on competency-based vocational training on climate-smart gardening techniques, such as vertical, homestead, rooftop, and multi-layer gardening, complemented by the distribution of eco-friendly tools, including organic seeds, vermicompost, bamboo materials, and biodegradable sack-bags. Specialized training will focus on Green and Circular Economic skills, including solar repairing, eco-friendly handicraft production using bamboo, and band pigeon/chicken rearing. Additionally, technical training in electric maintenance, barbering, and June product (handicrafts) creation will be offered. Refugees will receive input support and mentorship to establish green ventures, along with digital literacy training to enhance employability and access to markets. These activities aim to build sustainable livelihoods, reduce dependency on aid, and strengthen their capacity to adapt to economic and environmental challenges. These will be designed in accordance with the needs and interests of the youth and adult and focused on harnessing the potential for employment opportunities which would minimize dependence on humanitarian aid. Moreover, elevate community engagement in peacebuilding and foster social cohesion through a diversified vocational skills development and livelihood.
Component 2: Disaster Risk Reduction and Climate Change Adaptation (CCA)
Objective: Enhance the capacity of adolescents, youth, and adults to manage climate risks and disasters through targeted training and community engagement.
Key activities:
Training on gender-responsive Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Climate Change Adaptation (CCA), integrating mental health and psychological first aid. Conduct Community Risk Assessments (CRAs) to pinpoint vulnerabilities and hazards, followed by the development of actionable Risk Reduction Action Plans (RRAP)s – these assessments will also incorporate mental health indicators to understand the psychological impact of climate risks. Empower youth to develop and implement climate action strategies. Facilitate youth-led climate action initiatives and raise awareness on protection, DRR, and CCA through events and social media, mobilizing community action. These initiatives will highlight youth leadership in tackling climate challenges.
By focusing on climate resilience and economic self-sufficiency, the project will lay the foundation for a more stable, peaceful, and resilient community in Cox's Bazar. The project’s integration of protection mechanisms and MHPSS ensures that participants not only build skills but also cultivate emotional strength, enabling them to navigate future challenges with greater resilience. Ultimately, the project contributes to long-term development goals aligned with the SDGs, fostering an environment where both refugees and host communities can thrive together.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to ISNA findings, 34% of the Rohingya population in Bangladesh is between the ages of 18 and above. These young people lack formal livelihood opportunity and education, as a result, face severe security and safety related risk with related to livelihood. They cannot access formal secondary or higher education, have limited employment prospects. This disenfranchisement significantly impacts their mental health, with many young people increasingly driven to seek alternatives in crime or gangs. Women and girls are particularly vulnerable, often risking dangerous journeys abroad where they are forced into marriage. Gender-based violence is widespread due to high levels of frustration within the camps.According to Educo’s Multi-sectors Need assessment Report 2023 SPRIT Consortium MSNA), exposes limited livelihood options, with the Refugees and host community members primarily engaged in agriculture (14%) and daily labor (29%) in the Refugees camps. Women play a key role in tailoring (45% in camps, 48% in hosts) and poultry farming (15% in hosts), yet face barriers to resources and market access. Only 36% of camp and 30% of host respondents accessed training. Tailoring, livestock rearing, poultry farming, and small businesses are identified as priority areas to enhance self-reliance and gender-inclusive resilience.The study by the COAST Foundation in July 2022 found that only 7% of Rohingya women are involved in economic activities inside the camps, when the figure is 57.46% for the overall Rohingya population. Almost half of the Rohingyas (42.54%) are involved in different economic activities inside the camps. In economic pursuits, 19.48% are engaged in vegetable cultivation, 15.19% in running grocery stores, 43.09% have the opportunity to engage in skill labor like building shelters or homes, repair of solar panels, maintenance work, and vegetable cultivation, and 36.46% to run grocery stores. 46.41% wanted seeds, 56.35% wanted chickens, and 40.33% sought poultry sheds. Almost all (95.58%) viewed these products as having great potential for sale inside the camps. As for other livelihood skills, 32.60% can make bamboo products, while 47.51% are in need of needles, yarn, and frames for designing tapestries on clothes.The camps are facing growing challenges. According to recent ISNA statistics, over 4% of the population are persons with disabilities. They face heightened risks and lack tailored support services, leaving them marginalized and without sufficient livelihood opportunity or inclusion in community Income generating activities. Additionally, the crowded and resource-scarce environment unemployment tensions, impacting both refugees and the local community in Cox's Bazar, who sometimes perceive the Rohingya as a source of competition for jobs and resources.Further compounding these challenges, food aid was reduced by one-third in 2023 due to declining international support. This reduction has placed immense pressure on families already struggling to meet basic needs and has increased malnutrition risks across the camps. The ISNA notes that more than 75% of shelters are constructed with basic materials, making them vulnerable to extreme weather, and the camps' exposure to climate change threats, such as landslides and floods, is exacerbating these risks.If these challenges go unaddressed, the employment and livelihood security impacts will be severe, including escalating violence and destabilization of both refugee and host communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Educo is a child , adolescent and Youth rights organization dedicated to supporting children, families, and communities through a rights-based approach, addressing cross-cutting issues such as climate/environment, gender equality, disability inclusion, and conflict sensitivity. In Cox’s Bazar, Educo’s expertise extends to supporting displaced young people through gender-responsive Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Climate Change Adaptation (CCA), with specific attention to disability inclusion. We are active in child and youth mobilization on climate issues, employing nature-based solutions and climate-smart technologies to enhance community resilience, particularly aligning with SDG 2, SDG 3, SDG 13 (Climate Action) and SDG 16.2 (ending violence against children). Educo integrates protection principles in all livelihood-focused interventions, prioritizing safety, inclusivity, and accountability, with an emphasis on active participation and equitable access. Our livelihood activities aim to build resilience and foster sustainable income generation, reducing dependency on aid and mitigating harmful coping mechanisms. Staff will undergo protection training to ensure an understanding of key risks—such as exploitation, gender-based violence, child abuse, and health complications—which disproportionately affect women and girls with restricted mobility. By focusing on education, employment opportunities, and tailored livelihood support, Educo’s project will empower refugees and host community members to develop resilient livelihoods, ultimately improving reintegration prospects and fostering contributions to the local economy.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project aims to directly support 3160 people. This livelihood-centered intervention will prioritize vulnerable adolescents and youth with special needs, including those who are unskilled, unemployed, or at increased risk of abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation. Support will also extend to vulnerable women, families of persons with disabilities, separated or unaccompanied youth, and women- and child-headed households to strengthen their economic stability and independence.Project participants will include adolescents, youth and adult from both refugee camps and host communities, the Peace Promotion Group (PPG), local craft practitioners, community members, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services. Approximately 200 community members—including caregivers, teachers, and religious and community leaders—will be engaged in promoting sustainable livelihood practices and inclusive economic participation.The project, led by partner management, will promote disability inclusion, ensuring that project staff are sensitized to the rights and entitlements of persons with disabilities. An indicator has been added to the results matrix to monitor age- and disability-inclusive livelihood activities, supporting the engagement of individuals with disabilities in income-generating opportunities. Additionally, tailored, door-to-door support will be available for women and girls with disabilities to enhance their livelihood participation. Educo and its partners will actively involve persons with disabilities in all economic empowerment initiatives, coordinating closely with other NGOs, UN agencies, and partner programs. Aligned with LSDS sector goals and the JRP 2025, the project will aim to include around 12% of persons with disabilities in each targeted livelihood activity, supporting their path to economic self-reliance and community integration.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project’s monitoring approach emphasizes both learning and accountability, tailored to the complex operational environment. Throughout implementation, Educo-Bangladesh and project staff will actively engage the target population, conducting formal and informal consultations and data collection with adolescent girls and boys, women, and men. This will be done using a gender-integrated, child-friendly approach, with a strong emphasis on safeguarding.An Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) plan will be developed, outlining expected results, indicators, and activities in line with the project’s Performance Measurement Framework (PMF) align with AAP. The Educo MEAL team, supported by Educo HQ, will lead the design, oversight, and management of the AAP system, collaborating closely with stakeholders. Tools and procedures will be created to ensure timely, accurate, and consolidated data collection, quality control, analysis, and use in alignment with project objectives. All project staff will receive training to effectively use AAP tools and understand their roles within the system.To enhance accountability, a monthly progress tracking system will be implemented. A structured feedback mechanism—including a complaint box, help desk, and quarterly satisfaction surveys and focus group discussions (FGDs)—will allow for continuous community input, managed by both MEAL units. Monthly MEAL team visits will capture project progress, community feedback, and insights for best practices. A dedicated space will enable participants to express opinions on the project’s activities and impact, allowing Educo to make iterative improvements. The national Suggestions, Complaints, and Compliments (SCC) committee at the country office will introduce a formal SCC mechanism to receive and respond to feedback consistently.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Educo has a strict PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) policy to ensure staff are responsible and uphold the code of conduct, particularly in interactions with affected FDMN and Host communities to prevent any form of sexual abuse or exploitation. The project will provide PSEA training for staff and all levels of volunteers, along with community awareness sessions on PSEA and its reporting mechanisms. To support this, Educo has established the Suggestions, Complaints, and Commendations (SCC) system to receive feedback and address all PSEA cases in line with its policies. A designated and trained PSEA focal point will manage, refer, and report SEA cases within the camps.For sustainability, the project will empower community groups, especially adolescent girls and youth, to build their capacity in managing protection concerns and accessing positive alternatives to reduce negative coping mechanisms in both host and camp communities. Educo is also an active member of the PSEA network and upholds a zero-tolerance policy on sexual harassment, gender-based violence, and abuse of authority within the organization

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Educo and its partners are committed to protecting children from disasters and engaging in activities that mainstream Climate Change Adaptation (CCA) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). To mitigate the risks of landslides, cyclones, and flash-floods, especially in areas where FDMN cannot evacuate to safe zones, concrete and innovative protective and preparedness interventions are being implemented. This project integrated the seasonal contingency planning for its implementing tenure, which is essential to mitigate risks associated with monsoons, flash-floods and cyclones, ensuring a rapid response. This includes establishing stand-by and roster systems for staff during weekends, holidays, and critical periods, ensuring preparedness during monsoon and cyclone seasons. Multi-purpose centers (MPCs) serve as child-friendly spaces and emergency response shelter for supporting children and their caregivers needs and collaborating with like-minded service providers and the Child Protection Sub-Sector to provide comprehensive support in post-disaster scenarios. Tree plantation, solid waste collection campaign etc. increase sense of responsibility of the children and adolescents about environmental degradation and help to natural restoration. Overall, this project incorporates climate change adaptation and mitigation by addressing DRR, DRM, anticipatory actions, and environmental issues.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project has taken Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to enhance resilience and ensure effective response during emergencies. The project allocated contingency funds are integrated into the project’s financial structure, enabling immediate action for urgent needs arising from emergencies such as cyclones, flash floods, or fires. This funding provides the flexibility required to respond promptly to sudden protection needs resulting from crises, whether they are natural disasters or significant social disruptions. The project also emphasizes community capacity building by equipping community-based child protection structures with the necessary skills and resources for effective emergency preparedness and response. Training for child protection actors, community volunteers, and caregivers on disaster risk reduction (DRR) and response readiness is a core component, complemented by awareness-raising sessions focused on building protective capacities within the community to anticipate and manage risks effectively. By embedding these EPR measures, the project aims to enhance the overall resilience of Rohingya refugee communities and their caregivers, ensuring they are better prepared to respond to emergencies.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Youth engagement (ages 12-34) is a central component of this livelihood-focused project, designed to enhance child protection and build resilience within Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. Through vocational training in areas such as sewing and dressmaking, solar repair, Car Driving and handicraft-making, as well as leadership opportunities and participatory activities, the project empowers youth to become economic contributors and change agents in their communities. These skill-building efforts aim to equip young people with practical, marketable skills, fostering economic self-reliance and reducing reliance on external aid.Youth also receive structured psychosocial support through individual or group sessions that focus on coping strategies and stress management, promoting emotional resilience and contributing to a stable community environment that supports livelihoods. They actively participate in courtyard sessions, community campaigns, and awareness days, promoting child protection, gender equality, and violence prevention alongside their economic empowerment. Training on child protection also covers safe working practices and equips youth with the knowledge to recognize and respond to risks, including those that impact livelihood activities, such as disaster-related hazards and workplace safety.In community-based protection mechanisms, youth assume leadership roles, directly contributing to project implementation and refining their decision-making abilities, essential for entrepreneurship and livelihood activities. They are engaged in project monitoring, providing feedback and participating in consultations, ensuring that livelihood interventions are youth-centered, gender-sensitive, and culturally relevant. By shaping project adaptations, youth help create a more inclusive and supportive environment for sustainable economic activities and long-term community

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes: The project promotes self-reliance and resilience among Rohingya refugees by equipping Youth and adult with vocational skills, financial literacy, and livelihood opportunities aligned with market needs. Families are supported with conditional cash grants and livelihood diversification, reducing economic pressures and enabling investments in education. Mental health and psychosocial support services help youth cope with trauma and build emotional resilience, while inclusive approaches ensure access for people with disabilities. These initiatives empower refugees to achieve economic independence, enhance coping capacities, and contribute to more resilient communities.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes: The project activities in the host community are closely linked to development initiatives and align with Cox’s Bazar District's development and growth priorities, including the District Development Plan and other planning instruments. By promoting vocational training, livelihood diversification, and entrepreneurship, the project supports economic empowerment, a key district priority. It also addresses resilience through disaster risk reduction training and mental health services, aligning with strategies to enhance community well-being. Collaboration with local authorities and stakeholders ensures coherence with district-level development goals, fostering sustainable outcomes for host communities.
