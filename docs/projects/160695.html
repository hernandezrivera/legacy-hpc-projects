Project Summary

This project is targeting 2 counties of Jur River and Raga in Western Bhar el Ghazal, the project will reach total of 7,446 pupils (4,381 boys and 3,065 girls) as primary beneficiaries and estimated cost of $476,408.
The implementation approach of this project will promote basic access for boys and girls and adolescents to education, building the capacity of Teachers and PTAs/SMCs in life skills and peace education and good governance, support school with teaching and learning materials, establishment and renovation of school roofs, doors and windows to promote conducive learning environment.
The following cluster objectives shall be achieved; CO1: Improve access to safe and protective education services for 7,446 emergency/crisis affected IDPs and host community children and youth (girls and boys) through formal and non-formal education programme, CO2: Improve the quality of education services to all children, including children with disabilities and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and CO3: Strengthen the education system response capacity of the community and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergency on children and youth by 2020
Our intervention approach is guided by the state education strategy and existing conditions of the schools in both Jur River and Raga counties. The plan is to integrate immediate and long-term interventions with key clusters like Nutrition, WASH and Food security and livelihood that will support continuity and sustainability of education in the state.
AFOD being a strong NGO, is well versed and placed to implement owing to the presence of experienced and qualified local staff that are familiar with the counties targeted for these interventions. The project shall focus on teachers, School management committees, PTAs and School WASH clubs as indirect beneficiaries.
Key stakeholders shall be engaged in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including establishment of feedback mechanism, school and community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback.

1 - Needs

The effect of protracted conflict, displacement and lack of basic social services continue to impact negatively on the communities with an estimated 7.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, about 5 million women, children (boys and girls) and men require urgent assistance and protection (OCHA July 2019). 6.35 million people face ‘crisis’ food insecurity (IPC phase 3) of whom 1.7 million people are in emergency (IPC phase 4) even in the presence of humanitarian food assistance (IPC, August 2019). In South Sudan The seven (7) years political conflict since December 2013 has significantly affected the security and the livelihood of people in Western Bahr el Ghazal particularly those in rural areas. Within Bhar el gazel, further humanitarian crisis and displacements are deepened by the protracted conflict between cattle keepers, poverty, and livelihood insecurity which forms the key drivers leading to interruption in education. Traditional gender norms, harmful traditional practices are present in Western bahr el Ghazal mainly early and forced marriage, abduction of girls for household chores, polygamy and denial of education for girls due to societal expectations. WASH coverage in schools is very low with basic water supply and sanitation is a big challenge and below Ssphere Sstandard, the population still practicing open defecation, it estimated that at least 2.2 million school-aged girls and boys are out of school, with thousands more at risk of dropping out. The returning of IDPs to their original residential areas is translated into increase in the number of enrolment exacting pressure on the education sector. The prolonged economic and social crises have also affected teachers directly through lack of payment of incentives and most of the schools are being managed by untrained volunteers’ teachers which likely compromise the quality of education in the state (Education sector report 2019) as observed by AFOD. Justification: The peace conference between Tong and Wau authorities including the revitalised signed South Sudan peace agreement is holding water at the grass root prompting voluntarily returnees of the population in Jur River County, especially, the respect to the agreement between Tong and Wau authorities has great impact on the civilians, hence prompting returns. Returnees are believed to be coming from Wau POC, IDPs collective sites, Tombora, Uganda, the Sudan, Central African republic and other places, this might likely increase burden on already scarce resources couple with limited agricultural activity, poor harvest, depletion of livelihood activities, poor school structures and broken WASH facilities which are not conducive to quality inclusive learning and education for the children leading to increase in school dropout rates of particularly girls. There is need to establish and rehabilitate WASH facilities that are gender segregated, to ensure effective learning, the schools will be equipped with teaching and learning materials including basic furniture. AFOD South Sudan, for the last 4 years has been implementing school meal program with the support from World Food Program, a total of 18 schools 6 are currently closed due to insecurity in Jur River and 5 schools in Raga in WBeG, with total enrolment targeting 7,446 pupils with gender ratio of approximately 2 boys to 1 girl. Therefore, through this project AFOD will increase basic access for quality inclusive education for boys, girls and adolescents in 6 schools in Raga and 12 schools in Jur River County. Teachers and PTAs/SMCs will also be trained in life skills and peace education and good governance, distribution of teaching and learning materials, establishment and rehabilitation of roofs blown by wind, doors and windows in order to motivate children to learning as conducive learning environment reduces the rate of school drop outs and absenteeism.

2 - Activities

• Enroll children in Primary learning centers• Establish and rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms• Establish and rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion• Provide emergency-affected children with quality education and recreational material • Provide life-skills training to youth and adolescents• Equip teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience for emergency-affected children.• Training of teaching personnel on psychosocial support• Training for community members (SMC/PTA) and education authorities on psychosocial support• Equip SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance• Provide emergency-affected children with life-skills and basic vocational training• Conduct Nutrition sensitive sensitization in schools• Establish and promote school gardens
