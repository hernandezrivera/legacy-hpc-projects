Project Summary

In 2022 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors reaching a total of 185,027 beneficiaries. ZOA was able to secure $4,185,282 in funding, of which $1,313,293 was SHF funding. ZOA has to date secured $ 2,085,141 for 2023. The cost per beneficiary for the proposal is estimated to be $1508.
ZOA proposes to increase livelihood opportunities for communities most in need in Aleppo, Quneitra & Rural Damascus, through the provision of small business (SB) start-up & business recovery grants. 300 vulnerable beneficiaries falling under set criteria will be targeted in the proposed target locations. A needs-based approach will be followed. The proposed average size of each grant is $2500. Beneficiaries will receive basic trainings on how to start up and run successful business with ongoing support from ZOA during implementation. In summary, all selected 300 beneficiaries of SB will benefit from sector activities 15.1, 15.2, & 19.2. The 300 potential beneficiaries are unique beneficiaries, who will be involved in more than 1 activity. The project targets IDPs, returnees and people from host communities according to needs and the market.
An additional apprenticeship activity with another 300 beneficiaries is proposed for this project, which will be linked to each business grant disbursed. The aim of this activity is to encourage on-job trainings for skilled beneficiaries. Since IDPs are considered more vulnerable population, 60% of the beneficiaries of this activity will be IDPs, to mitigate social rupture. Each apprenticeship will last 3 months and each beneficiary will get conditional MPC for 440,000 SYP per month. The condition to receive the MPC is to work with the assigned small business grant beneficiary at least 120 hours per month. The MPC is based on the recommend amount of 440,000 SYP by the Cash Working Group (November 2022) and the additional 75,000 SYP will cover transportation costs. Each beneficiary accepted for the small business grant will only receive the funding under the condition of employing one beneficiary from the apprenticeship activity for 3 months without being responsible for the MPC allocation mentioned above.
ZOA will prioritize communities with high severity of needs, and beneficiaries will be selected based on a set criteria. ZOA’s vulnerability criteria also incorporates protection concerns. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project and implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach and impactful support to households. Beneficiaries are selected after presenting all the needed documentation and a project proposal. Project committees will be set up in all locations to have their input in selecting the beneficiaries.
For all activities, families with members with disabilities will be targeted, and training as well as job opportunities will be explored for people living with disabilities to gain an income independently. Also, women will be encouraged and given opportunities to equally benefit from the project.
The proposed intervention is in response to the HNO 2022-23 ERL Chapter, which emphasizes the importance of livelihood opportunities stating, “Socio-economic deterioration is greatly straining the social fabric and increasing destabilizing, negative coping mechanisms like crime and participation in non-state armed groups, especially among male youth.” The project is aimed at generating employment and sustainable livelihoods for the most vulnerable households in target locations, providing a catalyzing effect on the local market.
The selected locations are accessible by ZOA as well as its implementing partners. Targeted sub-districts fall under minimum severity score 3 based on the ERL HNO 2022 ERL PiN severity score document.
ZOA will also do rehabilitation of schools, sewage networks, water stations and agricultural infrastructures. Detailed projects have been submitted to individual sectors (Education, WASH & FSA).

3 - Needs

The 2022-23 HNO ERL Chapter states an increase of 19% in the number of PiN of early recovery & livelihoods interventions (11.6m in 2021 to 13.8m in 2022). The increase has largely been due to HHs depleting their savings & assets & struggling to find livelihood opportunities to meet their basic needs as the crisis prolongs. Massive depletion of livelihoods infrastructure, high levels of unemployment & poverty, coupled with high food prices, sanctions & inflation have all contributed to increased humanitarian needs across Syria. While 2 years planning can be encouraging to have long term sustainable interventions addressing the root causes, as so far emergency responses were the main direction. In this sense, an intervention that enables people to rebuild their lives by creating income security will improve not only effectiveness but also dignity and durability. “Vulnerable Syrians, especially those in areas no longer experiencing high levels of instability, request assistance that empowers them to meet their basic needs autonomously, thereby respecting their dignity & sense of self-worth. A continued failure to deliver this kind of assistance, could significantly damage the credibility of humanitarian actors in Syria, which could ultimately hurt trust in aid actors” (HNO ERL). Per the sectors’ advice ZOA is taking into consideration the most vulnerable groups (IDPs & families with PWDs) & is working in areas that have interventions under other sectors for sustainable results.

4 - Implementation of activities

ZOA will work closely with relevant local authorities, the sector and local communities to ensure that assistance reaches the most vulnerable. The steps undertaken to achieve results are: General Activities: 1 Market assessment; 2 Establishing Community Committees and provision of required trainings; 3 Training & capacity building of project staff and volunteers, which will include topics on accountability to beneficiaries, protection and gender mainstreaming, safety and security and COVID-19 awareness; 4 Consultations and coordination with affected communities; 5 PDM - Follow up with small grants and apprenticeship beneficiaries; 6 Reporting and M&E. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logframe, indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and to demonstrate impact. Small Business Activities: 1 Receive proposals; 2 Selection of 6000 applicants based on feasibility studies & eligibility according to set criteria; 3 Agreement to provide onsite training to 1 apprenticeship beneficiary; 4 Basic training to successfully set up and run a business; 5 Allocation of funds. Apprenticeship Activities: 1 Getting applications; 2 Selection of successful 600 beneficiaries, based on set criteria; 3 Connecting the apprenticeship applicants with SB beneficiaries; 4 Onsite training for 3 months, receiving 515,000 SYP each month.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Tension in community - for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: 1 Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the ERL sector, such as UNDP beneficiary targeting tool; 2 Consult with actors on the ground to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible; 3 Consistent M&E and feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/changes. R2: Selection criteria, influenced leading to project not accessing most vulnerable. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1 Clear complaint and feedback mechanisms; 2 Ongoing engagement with community leaders and clear community communication; 3 Humanitarian standards, accountability and PSEA training for staff. R3: Physical safety of beneficiaries and risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 1 Liaison with communities and local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2 Project coordination, sharing and seeking lessons learned with other actors; 3 Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age and gender; 4 Regular staff training; 5 Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4. Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-distribution monitoring.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Government entities require agencies to adhere to new regulations or additional approval levels are required to be able to implement activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measure: 1. Coordinate within the sector, to be aware of how other actors deal with the challenges; 2. Engage closely with authorities; 3. Build capacities of local actors & continue to implement through them. R2: Market & exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2. Transfer funds into Syrian bank according to the immediate needs. 3. Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency. R3: Movement restrictions due to COVID & Cholera. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Carry out activities as soon as possible through existing infrastructure before more restrictions & lockdowns are enforced; 2. Explore remote alternatives for field monitoring. R4: Fraud & corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements using multiple channels; 2. ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy in its code of conduct; 3. Procurement, Logistics, Fraud & Corruption Policies in place for the mitigation of these risks; 4. All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption & abuse; 5. All financial transactions are documented and filed; 6. Regular finance spot-checks & unannounced audits are carried out.
