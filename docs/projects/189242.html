Project Summary

The nutrition situation in South Sudan remains serious as food insecurity is expected to worsen. Currently the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels in South Sudan are at 12.6%, a 2.4% short of 15% emergency thresh hold (FSNMS r25). In Upper Nile GAM, SAM and MAM rates are even reported highest at 20.5% and 17.1% in Baliet and Panyikang respectively, these have shown increasing levels of malnutrition in the country. The Consolidated Approach to Reporting Indicators of Food Security revealed that 69.2% of households were food insecure by December 2021, and FSL cluster sectoral analysis projects by May - July 2022, 31% and 60% of the counties will be in Phase 3 and 4 of food insecurity and is expected to detoriate even further and affect the levels of malnutrition given that the aggravating factors such as flooding, internal conflicts, communicable diseases, outbreaks and economic crisis still exist.
The Objective of this project is to decrease prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under the age of 5 years and PLW in priority areas by end of 2023.
The above objective will be achieved specifically through:
1. Increasing equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2022.
2. Increasing equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2022.
3. Enhancing nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2022.
4. Increasing equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2022.
Health Link south Sudan intends to reach 107,294 beneficiaries, 71,393 children<5yrs and 35,901 above 5yrs in Eight (7) Counties of Baliet, Panyikang, Ikotos, Juba, Lainya, Yei and Morobo in south sudan and this accounts for 15% of nutrition cluster targets for these locations. The project targets total direct beneficiaries: 5401 SAM, 12,727 MAM, Vitamin A and Deworming 53,264 (among Children aged 6 to 59 months) and 9190 MAM (among Pregnant and Lactating Mothers).
The modalities to achieve the above objectives will be delivered through Out Patient therapeutic program, and Targeted supplementary feeding programme.
The major activities among others will include:
-Treatment of SAM and MAM among children aged 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating mothers. Routine nutritional screening at facility entry points and community.
-Establishment of OTP and TSFP service sites at all the 8 functional health facilities of Tonga PHCC, Owech PHCU, Bungu PHCU, wondoruba PHCC, Tore PHCC, Kimba PHCU, Geri PHCU, Yukube PHCU and establish outreach teams in each county to provide nutrition services to select hard to reach sites within the counties.
-Training of health and Nutrition workers on CMAM and MIYCN.
-Establishment of Mother support groups and conduct Individual and group IYCF counselling.
-Mass screening and Family Muac Screenings, case detections and referral
- Micronutrient supplementation and deworming.
-Conducting community outreaches and engagement meetings.
-Conducting Smart survey in collaboration with MOH and nutrition cluster.
This proposed nutrition project will be integrated into the already existing health, nutrition and protection programmes implemented by Health Link in these locations. The integration will better address the nutritional needs of children, pregnant and lactating mothers. HLSS will implement this project in collaboration with other stakeholders such as the county health department and other clusters to ensure a holistic approach in service provision.

1 - Needs

More than 11million people are in urgent need of humanitarian lifesaving nutrition assistance in south Sudan (HNO2022). The malnutrition situation in South Sudan remains Serious with Overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels at 12.6%, a 2.4% points short of the 15% emergency threshold. (FSNM r25) In Central equatorial, GAM rates are estimated at 10.3% and 4.8% in Juba and lianya counties respectively which have improved from 9.6% and 15.3% reported in 2020. (FSNMS R24 & R25) Juba has remained fairly security wise except mangalla Payam facing a complex emergency as vulnerable populations are forced to seek refuge following persistent flooding in Jongole state and later conflict rose between their supposed host communities. Although implementation of revitalized peace agreement is in full swing, multidimensional conflicts continue in some counties like; Panyikang, Lianya, Yei and Morobbo among others. Lainya currently hosts IDPs from neighbouring Kenyi, kupera &Wuji payams that are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection due to ongoing conflicts. (21st Jan2021 Assessment Report). In EES, Proxy GAM rates are not any better, reported at 10.77% in Ikwoto with malnutrition levels still high at 15.45 and 16.5% in Budi and Kapoeta East counties respectively worst the eastern equatorial regions (Nutrition Cluster Feb 2022) classified at critical levels. Ikwoto county, the area anciently known as the countrys’ food basket changed following 2013 insecurity as communities fled refuge to neighbouring countries. Likewise in Upper Nile, statistics reported extremely critical GAM rates in the country rising from 11.25% and 10.5% in Dec 2019 to 17.1% and 20.5% in Panyikang and Baliet respectively, this calls for immediate interventions. Based on the Consolidated Approach to Reporting Indicators of Food Security, 69.2% of households were food insecure by December 2021, and FSL cluster sectoral analysis projects by May - July 2022, 31% and 60% of the counties will be in Phase 3 and 4 of food insecurity and is expected to detoriate even further. High levels of food insecurity are driven by continued insecurity caused by population displacements and livelihood disruptions. The floods greatly affected Lafon and Baliet in 2021.To date, Baliet County remains flooded, thousands of homes, health facilities submerged, and livelihoods devastated leading to crop destruction, reduced planting impeding households access to food sources. Furthermore, high prices of food commodities and sustained depreciation of South Sudanese pound against US dollar has reduced purchasing power of vulnerable households. This suffocated livelihoods in families and children under5 are hit hardest by this catastrophic situation. Populations suffer constraints accessing health services with under5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as Immunization coverage still remains low. Recurrent measles outbreaks, upsurge of communicable diseases, compounded by flooding and food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to malnutrition and related mortality, with health care system is over stretched with increasing morbidities(MoH, Updates, February 2022). To bridge the gap, HLSS intends to roll out a strategic nutrition response by establishing mobile teams to deliver nutrition services in 7 counties, targeting locations of: -wonduruba, Bungu and Ganji in Juba County, - Tore PHCC in tore payam- Yei county, Kimba PHCU, Lokube PHCC and Geri PHCU in Morobo county, -Romula, Mohina, Madwany and Ifere in ikwoto county, -Ryangnum, Benythiang, Baychan, Gelacel in Baliet County, Pakwa and nyibol in Panyikang County, - Kupera, mukaya and Lainya town in Lanya County where beneficiaries walk over 2 hours to seek services. This proposed nutrition project is meant integrate into ongoing Health, nutrition, Protection and ICCM projects run by health link in these locations.

2 - Project activities

The proposed nutrition project will be integrated in to the already existing Health link south sudans’ health, nutrition, protection and CCCM projects implemented in this locations. The following activities make up the project: • Community sensitization and awareness creation focussing on prevention and causes of malnutrition.• Mass MUAC screening for all children under 5 including pregnant and lactating mothers in the community• Strengthening Family MUAC screening and formation of mother to mother support groups.• Identification and referral of children with malnutrition to OTP/TSFP sites.• Establishment of OTP/TSFP sites that will be used for management of malnourished children.• Treatment of Severe and Moderate acute malnutrition among children 5-59months and Pregnant and Lactating mother.• Conduct integrated outreach sessions to hard-to-reach areas and to displaced populations in Baliet County.• Training of health and nutrition workers on MIYCN, CMAM and IMAM.• Conducting MIYCN/IYCF individual and group counselling sessions at facility and community level.• Screening and testing for malarial among all children with severe acute Malnutrition and those with medical complications admitted at the Stabilisation Centre.• Support existing facilities in establishment of kitchen gardens and demonstration gardens with support of the community nutrition volunteers and mother support groups.• Conduct cooking demonstration sessions to beneficiaries both at facility and community level.• Screening , identification, referral and linkage of GBV survivor to GBV services• Training of health and Nutrition workers on GBV case detection and referral• Testing and treatment of malaria• Training of nutrition and health workers on Malaria testing and management.• Support the national nutrition SMART or FSNMS country surveys in the locations of implementation.• Establish a community complaint feedback mechanism in the nutrition sites by placing suggestion boxes, establishing a help desk at each site, initiating community focus group discussions with the community members.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback that HLSS will receive from beneficiaries during the project implementation shall be adapted during indicator reviews and as well factored during implementation at all service delivery points. Feed backs are from beneficiaries are gathered through engagement meetings, help desks, suggestions boxes and review meetings. HLSS analyses the feedbacks shares their responses with the beneficiaries, and adaptations for the project are made as follows. - For whatever has worked well and is appreciated by the beneficiaries, HLSs, will strengthen the strategy/activity, and keep track for future planning for proposals and replication to other locations. - For any strategy that does not go well, discussions are made during the meetings and consultations are made to identify what is workable within for the community without compromising on the quality of services. Some strategies or activities may not be applicable in some locations due to context and cultural difference which will need consistence sensitization and reinforcement. - Best practices shared by beneficiaries are also replicated and shared in cluster meetings. - Awareness and sensitizations and routinely conducted and incorporated in to the health education packages to clear misinformation and miscommunications. - For any feedback that are not within the mandate of HLSS to address or adopt in to the project implementation, this will be share with the necessary departments or stakeholders for their response will be given to the beneficiaries as soon as available. Such adaptations based on beneficiary feedbacks have helped HLSS in meeting the needs and right of the people, improved relationships with the community and instils the spirit of project ownership.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HLSS will ensure that PWDs are always considered with 5% of all services to be provided and thus separate feedback meetings will be conducted with them and responses acted accordingly throughout the implementation period. Further HLSS will ensure service delivery sites support the vulnerable women and girls and their feedback captured and incorporated in all the activities. HLSS will ensure that the project design will factor all categories of beneficiaries even those that are not literate and they will be engaged in other hand activities. To ease communication, groupings will be made based on gender and disability and special translators will be used to pass information/feed back to such vulnerable groups. More than one language will be used in meeting to ensure inclusion of all beneficiaries. Posters and images will be printed and displayed on the walls communicating different messages for those who are not literate to get information. Reports will be easy to read tailored and presented in form of picture, graphs and charts among others. Existing community structures such as Boma Health Initiative, mother support groups, Community nutrition volunteers and community mobilizers will be used to share feedback as they already have constant communication mechanism with the beneficiaries.Awareness messaging will be ongoing through out the project period through various media such as radio talk shows and/or jingles , public address and health education talks

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HLSS has PSEA Policy and reporting mechanism in place and implemented at all her project locations. Which include hot line for reporting, email address for ethics and integrity. Core protection activities are described clearly in the logical framework. Additionally, in cross-sectoral activities and implementation, protection mainstreaming will be applied as follows: - During distributions OTP/TSFP days, the nutrition personnel with protection team will routinely conduct the safety audit survey which will advise the programme team on necessary adjustments. - HLSS staff members and temporary workers in the field (such as nutrition promoters and volunteers) understand and sign the Code of Conduct, PSEA policy and Child Protection. These policies are made available for the beneficiaries in a widely understood form such as images and posters. - Entitlements to beneficiaries are clearly visible at the distribution points and these are communicated to the community. The static and outreach sites will be selected closer to beneficiaries to minimize the risk of exposure of violence because of a distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. - HLSS will include women, men, boys and girls for community engagement activities to minimize intercommunity conflict over access to limited livelihood activities. - The protection officer will be responsible for the verification/establishment of the referral pathway and to refer cases to the nearest active service providers. - The beneficiaries will be informed and trained on the case reporting mechanism, provided information on the access to or awareness of referral services for survivors of GVBS, Child protection incidents. Advocacy on HLP and supporting the facilitation of discussions with key stakeholders and community. - Well-established, understandable and widely known complaint & feedback mechanism will be part of all activities. HLSS will further ensure that the poster containing the hotline and email address for reporting will be put in all the service points and educate the beneficiaries on the use. - The M&E team will measure whether beneficiaries feel safe accessing the services before, during, and after implementation. Throughout the intervention, HLSS commits to the collection of age, gender and diversity disaggregated data. - HLSS will further ensure that the poster containing the hotline and email address for reporting will be put in all the service points and educate the beneficiaries on the use. - HLSS further conducts PSEA training to all staffs and this is conducted bi-yearly every year and every staff is to adhere to the polices and there is zero tolerance of PSEA for all staffs at work place and as well during service provision for the beneficiaries. - HLSS further has embedded PSEA in all staff contracts and code of contacts which all staffs sign from the time of joining the organization and as well whenever staff’s contract is being renewed. Therefore during service delivery HLSS will ensure to conduct awareness to the beneficiaries making sure that all the services are provided at no cost, and they are never attached in return of any goods from them in order to receive the said services, secondly HLSS will ensure to aware the beneficiaries on the importance of reporting any staffs that has asked them in return of the services they are receiving or attached conditions of sexual in nature for services to be received and letting them know what ever is reported will be treated with confidentiality and managed in safe manner by investigation team.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HLSS with its current projects have a strong AAP mechanisms established in all the field locations and this has proved effective with the implementations in line of engaging the communities and their leaders in the project activities right from the inception of the project. For every project that is approved for implementation, HLSS conducts an orientation meeting with the stalk holders at different levels informing them of the new project in place, duration, the key deliverables, and the role of the community in relation to the project. Posters are pinned on the wall at the service delivery points with images relating to the project so as to cater for those unable to read. And at all service delivery points, HLSS has established a help desk and suggestion boxes for beneficiaries to raise any concerns /complaints and to be able to seek clarity and guidance in regards to the project. Feedback meetings conducted with beneficiaries (women, girls and boys including men) at service points, client satisfactory survey as well conducted with beneficiaries to ensure they are engaged on the various services they receive and well HLSS factors their feedback during this sessions. Exit feedbacks are always been conducted with the beneficiaries when they report to receive supplies. These post distribution feedback meetings with beneficiaries help in keeping the community engaged and feedbacks collected to improve on the service delivery. Monthly AAP feedback meetings conducted by HLSS with the community which basically looks in to the concerns and responses of the feedback received from the community in the course of the month. For any concerns that are urgent, the organisation makes gets in touch with the affected beneficiaries immediately. HLSS will ensure to have all these rolled out in the intended proposed locations for this project to keep the community engaged throughout the project implementation period.
