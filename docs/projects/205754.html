Project Summary

2500 people will be reached with community outreach GBV preventive messages through awareness raising session.
• 100 caregivers, Community based volunteers and teachers will provide everyday psychosocial care and support to vulnerable and traumatized returnees children , women, men, youth In Fangak and Budi Counties
• WAO will facilitate the provision of group counselling sessions in eight schools each reaching 30 persons through outreach program and 30 in women and girls’ friendly space (WGFS) in Old Fangak
• 50 GBV marginalized survivors will be provided with other services through referrals including legal assistance for 5 months in Budi and Fangak . The menstrual Hygiene Training and distribution of dignity kits to 200 Women and Adolescent girls in WGFS to improve their (MHM),shall be conducted and also share essential information on Sexual reproductive health rights (SHR Health) and engage them in micro finance saving and loan programs for three months to improve their social and economic wellbeing. EMAP Program will be conducted to initiate discussions with women, men, and youth to be agents of change for behavior change in the Communities of Fangak and Budi Counties
• WAO shall Conduct the livelihoods training in the two locations of Fangak and Budi building the capacity of women and girls on Soap, shampoo and sanitizer production, hair dressing , computer applications and Tailoring targeting 500 beneficiaries for 6 months
• Conduct advocacy on 16Days of Activism against GBV through press release and panel discussions, screening of SGBV in Schools and Radio –Talk –show, IEC-materials developed, grand-Marching of Women and girls with stickers in Fangak and Budi Counties.
• Participants chose the dates November 25, International Day against Violence against Women, and December 10, International Human Rights Day, in order to symbolically link violence against women and human rights and to emphasize that such violence is a human rights violation. The 16 days of Activism offers an opportunity to raise awareness and scale up advocacy for effective initiatives to prevent and respond to GBV together with all the stakeholders and Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare

1. Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is deeply entrenched in South Sudan, where women and girls continue to be at high risk both at public and private spaces. Gender based-violence is not only manifested in the physical and sexual violence, but also lack of equal opportunities for women and girls to lead a fulfilling life, high rate of illiteracy amongst girls and women, high rate of child marriage, high maternal mortality due to preventable causes, limited access to livelihood opportunities, are some of the indicators that highlight the dire need to address the gender inequality as the root causes to end gender-based violence South Sudan’s population faces pressure from various patterns of migration, including external migration, internal displacement, and the return of previously displaced populations. New internal displacements have intensified in recent years, with more than 550,000 of the 2 million internally displaced persons in 2021 alone (OCHA, 2021). Early marriage is widespread in the country, with 2010 estimates citing that 52% of South Sudanese girls between the ages of 15 and 18 were married, with some marrying as young as 12 (Ministry of Health, 2010). This contributes to the high adolescent birth rate also observed in the country, with one-third of women aged 15−19 already having begun childbearing in 2018 (World Health Organization, 2019). Early marriage and childbearing contribute to girls’ dropout from school, which in turn contributes to overall lower levels of education. There are cases of Child compensation and grooming with the highest rates of child marriage, with more than half of girls marrying under age 18 both in Fangak and `Budi Counties. This is reinforced by other harmful traditional practices such as very high bride wealth that is believed to be behind the cattle raids, forced and child marriages. Many women and girls have experienced gender-based violence in Budi and Fangak Counties, because their vulnerability concerns have not been addressed and therefore, required immediate responses. 20% of returnees in Fangak County reported to their respective locations., assessment has been conducted on their arrival and on transit to camp by UNIMISS and IOM. Budi , Malakal , and Fangak Counties have been prioritized by humanitarian Actors with the massive influx of returnees and other refugees. Therefore, Women Advancement Organization would love to intervene with the provision of preventive response services to GBV survivors by linking them with referral mechanism to access the PSS services including individual and group counseling, shelter, food security and livelihoods, legal and protection services in Fangak County. Fangak and Budi Counties Experiences High cases of early child and, forced marriage, physical assault to women and girls. Particularly in Budi County where there is Inadequate trained law enforcement, lack of GBV laws and absence of security forces including police and military interventions which enables the armed actors to commit GBV crimes. This similar situation is making women and girls refrain from engaging in Domestic activities to avoid being targeted by armed groups. Traditional and local authorities are doing less on GBV cases even if the cases are brought to them.
