Project Summary

The health status of the population in need remains fragile. Health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations per person per year (Sphere standards), however critical gaps remain in access to 24/7 services with each functional 24/7 primary health care centers serving on average 54,000 refugees (standard: 1:20,000). The temporary nature of facility infrastructure poses a challenge to longer-term continuity of services, as the infrastructures degrade. Marginalized and vulnerable groups (such as the elderly, people living with disabilities and chronic conditions, survivors of gender based violence, sex workers etc.) are also not accessing health services. Adherence to minimum standards for services and staffing and quality of care in health facilities is a concern and availability of Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) treatment, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services (including inpatient/psychiatric care) and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs) remain limited, as identified in the health sector external review. Community outreach activities need to be improved, harmonized and monitored.
The Sector remains seriously under-funded. While this does not affect the total number of health facilities, it does affect the quality of services provided. Indeed, adherence to the validated minimum package of primary health services remains inconsistent. Programming for non-communicable diseases, malaria, TB, and HIV/AIDS remains insufficient. Similarly, partners are struggling to scale up 24/7 service provision which is critical for emergencies including obstetric emergencies.
Through the project 53,000 beneficiaries will get quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary health services in Camp 1E, Camp 8E (Refugee in Camps) and Ukhia - Palong Khali (Bangladeshi in Host Communities).

Needs

The health status of the population in need remains fragile. Health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations per person per year (Sphere standards), however critical gaps remain in access to 24/7 services with each functional 24/7 primary health care centers serving on average 54,000 refugees (standard: 1:20,000). The temporary nature of facility infrastructure poses a challenge to longer-term continuity of services, as the infrastructures degrade. Marginalized and vulnerable groups (such as the elderly, people living with disabilities and chronic conditions, survivors of gender based violence, sex workers etc.) are also not accessing health services. Adherence to minimum standards for services and staffing and quality of care in health facilities is a concern and availability of Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) treatment, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services (including inpatient/psychiatric care) and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs) remain limited, as identified in the health sector external review. Community outreach activities need to be improved, harmonized and monitored. The Sector remains seriously under-funded. While this does not affect the total number of health facilities, it does affect the quality of services provided. Indeed, adherence to the validated minimum package of primary health services remains inconsistent. Programming for non-communicable diseases, malaria, TB, and HIV/AIDS remains insufficient. Similarly, partners are struggling to scale up 24/7 service provision which is critical for emergencies including obstetric emergencies. The following critical gaps continue exist in health service provision: surgical capacity; 24/7 health service provision; psychiatric and psychological specialists. Gaps in surgical capacities could pose a serious barrier to saving lives in the event of a cyclone as only two out of six field hospitals will remain open in the event of a Category 2 cyclone. Only one field hospital will remain open in the event of a Category 3 cyclone. One of the main field hospitals will close in 2018—which will result in a gap in surgical services. The Sector is urgently reviewing this situation to identify means of mitigating the risks. While the overall target of number of health facilities for the population in need is met, gap analysis reveals considerable inequitable distribution of health facilities across the camps. These can be difficult to fill in camps with high population densities where there is limited available land to allow construction of new facilities—such as in Camp 2E where there are zero health facilities. One key information gap is specific data on the number of host community members reached with health services. The Sector does not currently have a system in place to specify these services. According to most recent family counting data, approximately 52% of the Rohingya refugee population are women and girls with the largest gender discrepancy being among the population of working age (age 18-59) where 23% are female. Overall 80 per cent of the Rohingya population are women and children. Key vulnerable groups have been identified as single mothers (16%), person with serious medical condition (5%), older persons at risk (2%), persons with disabilities (4%), older person with child (2%), separated children (2%), unaccompanied children (1%), single male parent with infant (1%)_UNHCR Bangladesh Population Fact Sheet as of 31st October 2018. In the above context, Qatar Charity will continue its health intervention and support Global One to continue quality health services and scale up operations for Rohingya and Host community to support the women, men, boys and girls including eye care and individuals with serious medical conditions.

Activities or outputs

# Provide quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary health services through 2 health post to support 53,000 Beneficiaries Activities are following: Emergency medical care through outdoor service; Treatment of trauma and injuries (minor/medium); Treatment for communicable disease; Ante-natal and post-natal care; new born baby and child care; adolescent health; Immunization; Mental Health and psychosocial services; Emergency referral services through ambulance; Eye camp; Support individuals with serious medical conditions; up-gradation of health facilities; In kind support to other health post; Maintain Supply Chain; Staff training on primary health care, MhGAP, nutrition services, childhood blindness, RTI, STI, IYCF, clinical management, waste disposal management, SPHERE, CHS, Protection mainstreaming Principles and CRM; Establish help desk and complaint response mechanism; Ensure safety and dignity of beneficiaries and principals of do no harm in all level of project implementation; Equal an impartial access to assistance and service to all vulnerable groups; Maintain database with disaggregated information (Sex, age, disability and context specific vulnerability); Conduct satisfactory survey; Regular reporting to health sector and concern departments.
