Project Summary

The project seeks to ensure timely protection assistance for IDPs children in Central Darfur State (Nertiti ang Rokoro). The project will respond to urgent needs by providing life-saving support to extremely vulnerable IDPs & host communities through establishment of Child protection community base network, psychosocial support, awareness raising, establishment of referral mechanisms, provision of cash assistance, monitoring of child rights grave violations, prevention & reunification kits for UASCs. The project will strengthen resilience’s efforts through supporting capacity of community structures & local authorities in response to CP concerns. Rapid profiling/registration/documentation of persons with specific needs/vulnerable groups including UASCs will be contacted prior to any activity. The project will improve the capacity of targeted communities through establishment & training of 2 CBNs in Nertiti and Rokoro to respond to CP concerns. CBNs will be trained in provision of crucial information; direct support to child protection prevention mechanisms; FTR services; emergency referrals of vulnerable populations to other sectors & services providers; psycho-social support to traumatized population affected by armed conflict; prevention, monitoring, & response against grave violations of children’s rights. 2 CFSs will be established with environmentally friendly materials & furnished.
The project will provide community awareness program through conducting 10 public campaigns on CP issues, threads, needs etc. The project will consider accountability to affected population through community participation on conducting needs assessments & implementation of activities.
100 active community members will be trained on CP, through this activity, CP awareness & information will be widely disseminated and become part of social awareness. To improve the environment, the team will conduct 20 environmental awareness sessions (10 in each locality)
The project will consider AAP through the community participation on designing, developing and implementing the project activities, this will be done through consultation meetings and focus group discussion. Labena has adequate experience in involving beneficiaries in implementation of the project. Labena’s promotion of community self-management through intensive strengthening of their capacity and engaged them with clear responsibility during the implementation. This will contribute to boost-up their ownership towards the project and hence ensure its sustainability in the long run. Community members and members of Community based committees will be entrusted with monitoring and reporting progress. The project will stablish Feedback and Complaint Mechanism, As Labena has already present and currently implementing different intervention in the project locations, Labena's team will consult communities on establishing a viable feedback /complaint mechanism to ensure the community perspective. Complaints will be validated through initial screening processes; an investigation will take place in case of valid and serious cases. Labena will follow principles of ‘Do no harm’ prior to and during the intervention of the project. The project will highly consider the best interest of women and children when handling, sharing the data and information about the identified cases. The gender will be mainstreamed throughout the project phases to enhance gender equality. The project will make gender analysis to identify different needs of the women, girls, boys and men.

Needs

Violence against children in Sudan is widespread but often unreported. Physical punishment is frequently viewed as a culturally acceptable form of discipline by parents. Violence and sexual abuse of children, including gender-based violence, is also a concern. The biggest threat to children realizing their basic rights in Sudan is poverty. A father may decide, for example, that it’s better to keep his son in school and marry off his daughter.Protracted conflict in Central Darfur has led to breakdown of traditional social structures & long-established adapting mechanisms. The disruption of social networks &post conflict trauma has increased intra-family violence & other protection risks, which require psychosocial support & additional social welfare services. Trauma cases have been predominant among all groups, particularly children, more than 6,000 cases according to community leaders in Nertiti & more than 2500 in Rokoro. 90% of the IDPs in Nertiti and Rokoro are women & children. Approximately 80% women HH, they are caring for large numbers of children & experienced domestic violence frequently. Sexual & gender- based violence (SGBV), domestic violence and confronts cultural barriers limited their access to education, life-skill training and livelihood opportunities. Early marriage & FGM are widely practiced in the area, with 60% of girls aged 14-16 years getting married & 95% have been exposed to FGM/C. CP issues are a concern & alarming in project areas there are major gaps in terms of access to CFSs, CP monitoring, reporting & community-based protection.

Activities

 Identification UASC and most vulnerable children in Nertiti and Rokoro (Central Darfur state). Establishment of 2 registration points one in each project area. Registration of separated children and reunification of separated children in each project area.  Provision of assistance kits for 500 UASCS for most vulnerable children, 250 boys and 250 girls. Sensitization of community based alternative care arrangements.  Provision of psychosocial support to 100 UASCS (50 boys & 50 girls) through provision of 2 fenced child friendly spaces one in each project area.  Establish /strengthen Family Tracing & Reunification System (FTR) services, in Nertiti and Rokoro in collaboration with Child Protection sub-cluster working group, State council for child Welfare (NCCW), State Ministry of Social Welfare, FTR networks and child protection community based and committees/networks.  Establishment of 2 community-based child protection mechanisms, (50% of the members are women) to assess, monitor and address child protection issues one in each project area. Train 100 active community members (50% Women) on child rights and protection issues. Conduct 20 environmental awareness sessions (10 in each locality). Organize technical meetings for development, testing and verifying of SOPs for child protection intervention. Conduct awareness raising sessions on environment issues and concerns.
