Project Summary

FH will provide unconditional food assistance to 10,000 new IDPs affected by the ongoing violent conflict in Metuge district. The project will also support 2,920 IDP households (14,600 people) in resettlement sites, with access to land will be provided with agricultural inputs such as seeds and tools to enhance food production and improve thier short-term livelihood recovery and resilience. Agricutural inputs will include certified cereal seeds, vegetable seeds, hoe, matchete and watering cans. Beneficiaries will be provided with technical support on conservative agriculture and pest management for increased production. The combination of FSL support will address both food security and livelihoods challenges among IDPs and host communities. Farmers will be organized into groups of 15-20 members who will be supported by trained farmer leaders. FH/M will implement the Farmer Field Schools approach for knowledge sharing and demonstrations. The project will also establish VSLAs in the target communities. Experience from recent emergency response projects in the area have proven that VSLA groups are effective in enhancing livelihoods and access to loans by community members the majority of targeted IDPs have shown more interest in village savings activities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The ongoing armed conflict, insecurity and climatic shocks have resulted in thousands of households losing their properties, disrupting their agricultural activities and affecting their sources of income. IDPs are forced to depend on humanitarian food assistance and have no livelihoods activities. At resettlement centers, the government is allocating land for agricultural activities to IDPs to facilitate their quick recovery and reduce dependence of humanitarian food assistance. The 2021/2022 agricultural season has been characterized by delays in the onset of rainfall, above-average temperatures, and prolonged dry spells across and cumulative rainfall is less than 45 percent of the long-term average. The late start to the agricultural season resulted in a below-average to average crop harvest in 2022. Although some IDPs have started to return to their homes to prepare for the next agricultural season particularly in Quissanga and Macomia districts, the number of returning IDPs is outnumbered by the number of IDPs from the continued insecurity. In response to the humanitarian crisis resulting from the displacement of people due to the ongoing terrorist attacks in Cabo Delgado, FH/M will implement life-saving and early recovery humanitarian response activities that ensures safe, equitable, gender-sensitive and inclusive assistance to IDPs and host communities directly impacted by the violent conflict and insecurity situations in the provinces of Cabo Delgado and Nampula. The project will support 25,500 people in critical need.FH/M will provide unconditional food assistance through commodity vouchers to 2,000 IDP households (10,000 people) in Metuge district for at least 11 months (February to December 2023). Food Voucher Transfer (FVT) will be preferred over the in-kind modality because of its logistically light and simple to handle as well as it give self esteem and dignity to beneficiaries.The project will target 15,500 IDPs in resettlement sites and host communities with access to land will be provided with agricultural inputs such as vegetable seeds, watering cans and a hoe for home gardens. Beneficiaries will be provided with technical support on conservative agriculture and pest management for increased production. Engaging IDPs in agricultural and livelihoods activities will help them to recover from the disrupted livelihoods and reduce dependents on emergency humanitarian assistance. The combination of FSL support will address both food security and livelihoods challenges among IDPs and host communities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Consultations will be held with affected populations, including discussions with specific target groups such as the elderly, disabled and women and girls on access and provision food assistance and agriculture based livelihood services and supplies. All voucher distributions will comply with the FSL cluster starndards. The placement farmer field schools and kitchen gardens will include consultations with the elderly, the disabled and women and girls to ensure that these groups feel safe in accessing and using the sites.Continuous feedback will be sought from the IDPs through different feedback channels including regular community meetings with the IDPs, help desks, linha verde, and community committees. The project will also conduct a gender analysis during the first quarter of its implementation and results from this analysis will be used to improve on the implementation of the project.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

FH/M will ensure that gender associated risks are assessed and adequately addressed in the project design. FH/M has ensured women participate in the project through purposeful targeting of at least 50% women headed HHs, having 50% representation by women in project steering committees and help-desk committees. The committees will not only be trained in management but also on the vital role of women in HH and community development. Gender awareness will be extended to the entire community at every point of interaction with the project. An in-depth gender analysis will be conducted by FH/M to guide project implementation and ensure sustainable empowerment of women beyond the life of the project. FH/M will promote shared power between men and women - including control of resources and decision-making - and support for women’s empowerment as a pathway to reducing gender inequalities.Women with babies will be prioritized during food voucher distributions and voucher redemptions to allow them ample time to travel back to their homes to feed their children and perform their other roles (reproductive, productive, and community managing activities). FH/M will engage both men and women as part of the project steering committees for meaningful involvement in decision making by both sexes. In this project, FH/M will reinforce gender empowerment and transformation by engaging women who will comprise at least 50% of beneficiaries and project committee members respectively. FH/M will ensure that distances from homes to voucher distribution points or vendor shops are within walking distances (less than three kilometers) of the beneficiaries. To ensure protection and inclusiveness, FH/M will ensure that voucher distribution is conducted during daylight and will provide space for nursing mothers. FH/M will take advantage of pre and post distribution speeches to convey key messages on issues such as gender and protection.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

FH will create or revitalize disability management committees to ensure identification and effective involvement of PWD and other vulnerable people in the project. Personalized support particularly in transportation of entitlements after distributions will be provided through community based management committees. The project will seek to strengthen the local social-networks to ensure continued support to PWD. Distribution sites will be set in consideration of the inclusion requirements.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This project will have minimal negative environmental impact and FH/M will put in place measures to mitigate any potential negative environmental impact. These measures include avoiding crowds during distribution by serving project beneficiaries in small groups at a time (15 people at a time). Agricultural activities will include training on climate smart agricultural practices, avoiding burning the fields, intergrated pest management. FH/M field staff will ensure garbage is collected after distributions and properly disposed of. Following the redemption of vouchers, the vendors will attach all the vouchers to the invoice and send them to FH/M for payment. Therefore, the paper vouchers will not be disposed of by the vendor, which might result in poor disposal and thus impacting the local environment. An FH/M staff assigned to monitor voucher redemption will also monitor compliance to environmental protection by stakeholders.
