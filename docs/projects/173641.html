Project Summary

The NWSW regions of Cameroon continue to have hundreds of thousands of individuals affected by crisis and in need of humanitarian assistance. DRC is proposing a multi-sector humanitarian response to meet the most urgent needs of affected populations, including a response to protection, shelter, WASH, NFI and food security needs.
Through emergency WASH, shelter and NFI support, vulnerabilities will be mitigated in the immediate term for those permanently or chronically displaced by conflict in highly vulnerable, hard to reach areas. The proposed action aims to address some of the most critical gaps in assistance through the provision of cluster standard shelter, NFI and WASH assistance to 6,500 households.
The crisis is threatening to produce a lost generation of children who have been highly exposed to violence and conflict, without any form of education, and are caught in an environment where community and family structure degradation is pervasive, leading to high needs for psychosocial support. DRC will expand on the demonstrated success of its current child protection programming to continue to meet the social and emotional needs of children affected by conflict through community-based child protection programming, and individual child protection assistance. This will produce an improved protection environment for over 10,000 children in the immediate and medium term.
The crisis in the southwest has created diverse and widespread protection needs that are in critical need of being chronicled and addressed. DRC has had dedicated protection monitoring and response capacity in the southwest since January, 2019. The proposed action will allow DRC to expand coverage of monitoring and response, building the capacity for evidence based advocacy, and improving the protection environment for individuals and communities in the longer term. Protection needs will be addressed through psychosocial support including psychological first aid, with immediate protection cases being supported through individual protection assistance, as well as community based protection assistance through Community Based Protection Committees and community protection assistance. Protection monitoring and reporting will allow DRC to continue to gather important information on the protection environment in highly vulnerable communities informing both advocacy and response.
To respond to the urgent food security needs in NWSW regions, DRC will provide food security assistance through paper and e-vouchers to the 2,600 most vulnerable households.
As a result of the proposed intervention, crisis affected households will have better capacity to meet their basic needs, and the protection environment for both children and adults will be improved in the longer term.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon is currently struggling to cope with three competing crises that have put millions of people into humanitarian need. In the northwest and southwest regions, 3 million people are affected by the crisis, over 700,000 people have been displaced since the start of the conflict, and needs continue to climb as the conflict enters its fifth year without a resolution in sight. Despite this, the crisis remains critically underfunded (less than 50% in 2020), which compromises the capacity of responding agencies to provide a timely and comprehensive response. OCHA and cluster reports indicate that over 791,000 people are in need of NFI/shelter. This in addition to the 690,000 people in need of WASH support in the south west and north west region, meaning that the need to provide a response at scale has never been greater. The protection environment in the north west and south west regions also continues to deteriorate, with an increase of reported incidents in 2020. 97% of DRC Key Informants (KIs) report threats against schools, and 29% against medical facilities. Insecurity, prolonged lockdowns imposed by NSAGs and roadblocks and clashes between NSAGs and governmental forces constrain humanitarian access and impact assistance delivery . DRC has been collecting primary data on emergency needs since January, 2019. Evidence overwhelmingly supports the need for improved multi-sector response. Protection monitoring is ongoing and has highlighted the multitude of risks that crisis affected individuals and households face including arbitrary arrest, extrajudicial killings, destruction/burning of homes, movement restrictions and more. Protection data across 24 months of protection monitoring demonstrates that protection risks and violations are not localized and time-bound, but extend throughout the southwest and are creating widespread individual and community level protection needs, as evidenced by hundreds of key informants. DRC has also been conducting focus group discussions with over 600 children, adolescents and caregivers to understand the needs of children as a result of the crisis, as this information is largely absent in the response so far. These indicate that children have been drastically affected by the conflict in the southwest region, and are in need of significant psychosocial support. Exposure to conflict between state and non-state actors, significant incidence of sexual violence and abuse against children as young as 8 years old, children as young as 9 forming associations with non-state armed groups, and widespread family separation are a few of the trends found throughout DRC’s FGDs in highly affected communities. Years without access to education have robbed children of a healthy environment for cognitive development, and unless the crisis reaches a resolution soon, the risk of losing a generation to the impacts of conflict will rise.
