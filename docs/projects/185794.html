Project Summary

The overall aim of this project is to provide vulnerable populations with quality and equitable, safe water supplies, latrines and bathing facilities, including community engagement on key hygiene behaviours to reduce public health risks.
Improved access to safe water supply: The water component of the project will focus on water quality improvements and monitoring at source and point of use. The project will support the underground water resources monitoring and assessment of the water network coverage to inform future water provision options. DPHE will be supported to expand the ground water resources monitoring in other parts of Ukhia and Teknaf Upazilla of Cox Bazar District. In the camps, 6 small scaled water networks will be installed in critical areas to ensure equitable distribution of safe water supply to vulnerable populations. UNICEF will support routine repair, operation and maintenance of existing water networks and hand pump wells to ensure functionality of faciliites to meet the needs of the population. In addtion, areas not covered by networks will be identified and assessed for options for the continued provision of safe drinking water services.
Safely managed sanitation: For sanitation, the project focuses on operations, maintenance and improvement of existing latrines and bathing spaces to ensure functionality, quality, safety, privacy and address the needs of women, girls, PwD, the elderly and other vulnerable people of the community. UNICEF will construct WASH blocks for inclusive access to sanitation services. WASH infrastructure assessment will be done to identify the needs and address gaps. The operation and maintenance of Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) will be ensured while further assessments of the efficiency of FSM will be supported to improve the quality of treatment or decommissioning of any technology. In the camps, 5 new FSM will be constructed in critical areas. UNICEF will continue to support an integrated solid waste management in line with the WASH sector strategy/guidelines. UNICEF will support the expansion, operation and maintenance of SWM sites for composting and segregation units. UNICEF will support the provision of waste collection bins and materials for household and communal use.
Hygiene promotion and community engagement : The hygiene promotion will focus on community engagement changing identified high-risk behaviors through the participatory approaches such as CCCPLTH (Clean Camp Campaign-People Let Total Hygiene) and RANAS (Risk, Attitudes, Norms, Abilities and Self-Regulation) approaches as well as supporting participatory community monitoring and feedback mechanisms to contribute to AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations). Gender, GVB and Disability inclusion will be mainstreamed in all interventions.
Life-saving WASH supplies: The project will continue to support the provision of WASH supplies such as soap, Aquatab and MHM materials. UNICEF will procure emergency WASH supplies for prepositioning and/or replenishment of emergency stocks of supplies for natural disasters and other emergency situations. The target populations are 250,000 Rohingya refugees living in eight refugee camps and 150,000 Bangladeshi people living in Teknaf and Uhkia affected by the refugee influx.
WASH in host communities: In the host community, the project will support the provision of safe water supply, development of water safety plans WSP), promotion of good hygiene practices and promotion of MHM among reproductive age of women, and improvement of WASH services in institutions. In addition, the project will also support the creation of open defecation free (ODF) communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf by installing sanitary latrines."

1 - Identified Needs

Although 66% of the population reported using piped networks for drinking, there is a need to improve equitable access to piped water supply. The JMSNA, 2021 indicated that 19% of the Rohingya Refugees reported having used negative coping mechanisms for water needs, while 32% of the population reported not being satisfied with water supply (EIMS,2nd Quarter Report, 2021). Handpumps are still a major source of water for drinking -33.5%. Access to water in camp 22 in Teknaf remains a major challenge during the dry season, with water trucking required again in 2021. UNICEF needs to provide an additional 13 water networks as per Water Master Plan. There is a need to improve the operation & maintenance system, including community engagement for the operation and maintenance of water supply facilities. At household water quality surveillance, 30% were found to be below sector standards (23% with zero FRC).In general, the person to latrine ratio is high at 1:17 across most UNICEF WASH camps. However, at the block level of camps 6, 7, 8E and 8W, the person to latrine user ratio is as high as 1:23 compared to the minimum sector standard of 1:20 (due to high population density). Access to safe/functional latrines ranked as the 6th top priority needs with 19% households reporting this as a need for 2022.There is a need to improve operation & maintenance, including community engagement for cleaning & safe operation. The current functionality latrines is estimated at 87%. Facilitating safe and dignified access to WASH facilities to PWD is a priority. 59% of persons with disabilities, elderly and vulnerable population experience challenges accessing latrines. Women and girls face overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night, they risk harassment and violence. There is a need to ensure inclusive, safe and dignified use of all latrines by women, children, people living with disabilities, and the elderly. Access to bathing spaces is a gap for women and girls. A key concern is the coverage and quality of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements or scale-up. Coverage of FSM sites needs to be increased ensure all latrines are being emptied regularly and the sewage is safely treated and disposed of. Solid Waste Management (SWM) remains a challenge, with 57% of households disposing of their domestic waste in communal bins. The remaining 43% are still disposed of in open spaces, water bodies, or drains. Disposal of MHM materials remains a challenge (30% of women are using open spaces and pit latrines as final disposal), support is also needed towards safe disposal of facemasks.Hygiene knowledge and soap availability remains high, but behavior change remains an issue. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females including girls. MHM awareness and mobilization need to be strengthened, covering all reproductive-age women and girls, including men and boys. MHM materials need to be provided regularly. Cox’s Bazar District has some of the worst water and sanitation indicators. The district is regularly affected by cyclones and floods. Approximately 23% of the host community’s population was affected by floods, affecting water and sanitation facilities. An estimated 23% of households reported not having enough water for their domestic need. In addition, 20% of the population reported using an unimproved sanitation facility. Women also reported difficulties accessing MHM materials and conduct appropriate menstrual hygiene practices. Promotion of knowledge on disaster and climate change resilience amongst the Rohingya Refugees particularly the boys, girls, youths and adolescents, to improve their awareness and engagement in child centred disaster risk reduction are inevitably critical. Involvement of adolescents as change agents in promoting correct and appropriate Infection prevention control measures to contain Covid-19 is critical in WASH programming.
