Project Summary

In 2023 6.64 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance as they are categorized in IPC 3 or worse across the country. This is lower compared to 8.9 in 2022 and 8.3 million in 2021 (HNO 2023). According to the HNO (2022), climate change continues to impact different sectors, particularly agriculture, which is one of the most climate-sensitive economic sectors. Humanitarian needs are expected to remain the same or worse with limited funding and widespread disasters including flooding and conflict aggravated by macroeconomic situation.
The project will be implemented in Aweil East of Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Twic East, Duk, and Bor South of Jonglei as the proposed locations with a target of 45,000 people. The project proposes to target 15,551 in Aweil East, 6796 in Twic East, 10,267 in Duk, and 12,395 in Bor South, of which (boys: 12,680, girls: 12,613, men:9,104 and women: 11,054). The project will target the most vulnerable households in these locations through the distribution of emergency food production kits (vegetable, crop kits, and fishing kits), livestock vaccination and training of CAHWs, cash transfers, assets creation, and unconditional food assistance) and capacity building to boost coping strategies by scaling up ongoing or planned interventions. The project seeks a budget of 300,000 USD to provide the integrated services in 2023.

1 - Needs

In 2023 6.64 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance as they are categorized in IPC 3 or worse across the country. This is lower compared to 8.9 in 2022 and 8.3 million in 2021 (HNO 2023). In 2023, though the population in crisis or worse has reduced compared to the past two years, the humanitarian needs are expected to remain the same or worse with limited funding and widespread disasters including flooding and conflict aggravated by macroeconomic situation. Humanitarian needs remained high across Jonglei State in the period between December and March 2022 particularly Pibor, Canal-Pigi, and Fangak Counties, which were severely affected by flooding that resulted in widespread displacement and food insecurity and a pocket of population in catastrophe. While Jonglei state as a whole is projected at 63.5 % and 77.2% through December - March and April - July respectively. The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, the HNO added. Individuals displaced due to the flooding continue to gather in informal displacement sites or seek shelter in churches and schools in higher ground areas; many of those displaced by the flooding had already been uprooted by sub-national violence or by previous floods in 2020 (USAID November 2021).GBI is a static partner currently implementing emergency food production to 5825 HHs with a total of 35,950 people in Duk county. With previous experience implementing emergency projects in FSL and WASH, ready capacity to scale up in locations of high needs. GBI through SSHF implemented Integrated WASH and Nutrition project targeting 1,600 SAM children and over 17,000 individuals in communities with safe water and sanitation in 2019/2020 and Emergency WASH flood response in Twic East County supported through Rapid Response Fund (RRF). In 2019/2020, GBI reached 12,623 households with emergency food production in Aweil East through the provision of crop kits, tools, fishing, and vegetable kits while providing capacity building to selected farmers in groups and kitchen gardening in nutrition centers. In line with the call for proposals and 2022 HRP FSL Cluster objectives, GBI plans to scale up in greater Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, where we have an operational presence. Activities integrating FSL, WASH, Health, Protection, and Nutrition programming at households, institutions, and community level while mainstreaming GBV and COVID-19 risk mitigation, prioritizing the immediate provision of lifesaving livelihoods and nutrition services to the most food insecure populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5.

Training will be delivered jointly to male–and female–household members, as a means of promoting joint decision-making over household budgets.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Cash and voucher assistance is only possible where there are functional markets. The market functionality map will be studied for the design and implementation of CVA in those locations. GBI will use monthly published market information from JMMI-TWG. In an effort to inform cash-based interventions and better understand market dynamics, the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) was created by the South Sudan Cash Working Group (CWG) in August 2019, led by REACH and supported by the CWG members. The JMMI aims to track the Multi-Sectoral Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB) across all assessed locations on a monthly basis. Functional markets are available in most county headquarters and though beneficiaries exist in the outskirts of these centres, access can still be created by sub contracting the suppliers of the vouchers to bring the services closer to the people. Suppliers of cash programming will be recruited and selected based on their competence and capacities to provide the needed assistance according to the agreement.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

South Sudan has been affected by widespread violent conflict and flooding for four consecutive years since 2019 impeding humanitarian access by road. This normally affects the delivery of sufficient aid materials to remote parts of the country.Livelihoods materials are normally bulky constraining their transportation. Where a functional market exists, beneficiaries can use the cash and vouchers for accessing their immediate needs. As a cross-cutting activity, market-based programming should be integrated into FSL programming to promote a multiplier effect in local markets and the intervention area. The project will recognize and seek to identify ways in which the local market can contribute to meeting the needs of the community. According to Reliefweb report (Jan 2022), decreased level of access to resources, logistical constraints due to rainy season road blockage, limited access, and the uncertainty in the availability and prices of key items in the local markets has impacted the ability of humanitarian agencies to respond to humanitarian needs in an efficient, effective and timely way. Cash vouchers are always easy to implement and lifesaving as logistical bottlenecks can be skipped and rapid response provided to the affected households as long as the markets are functional. Cash vouchers also provide hope in the local market by building the local economy and encourage local production such as seed multiplication and it can also apply very well for projects such as food for work or DRR activities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will conduct entry meetings with the local authority, RRC, beneficiaries as part of community mobilization and with different groups, for example, PWDs, women groups, youth representatives, chiefs, local authorities, and beneficiaries’ representatives at the project inception. The aim of the meetings is to orient and mobilize different stakeholders and disseminate the project’s information (project duration, target beneficiaries and selection criteria, distribution site selections, donors, and the role of the community in the selection of beneficiaries and volunteers) and the beneficiaries have the chance to speak in such meetings to have their voices heard. The project will prioritize the needs of vulnerable people such as men, women, boys, girls, PWDs, PLW, elderly, etc. and this information is communicated through community mobilization and inception meetings in presence of local authority. The project will consult people with disability and collect disability disaggregated data which allows them to identify specific needs/vulnerabilities, risks, and barriers that prevent people with disability from accessing FSL services on an equal basis with other people.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Individuals accessing humanitarian assistance are the primary stakeholders of any humanitarian action. This means that they must be able to receive communications in a form they can understand, provide feedback on the delivery of humanitarian assistance, and be included in decisions that affect their lives. During community meetings, beneficiaries share their say or beliefs with the agency. Before distribution, beneficiaries are informed about their entitlements, rights, and contribution to the project. The AAP committees formed are used as channels through which beneficiaries who can't present their own complaints can speak to the organization. During distribution suggestion boxes are provided or Rapid Post Distribution Monitoring (RPDM), FGDs, or exit interviews are conducted to collect views from the beneficiaries on their perception, satisfaction, or dissatisfaction about the project. Feedback from beneficiaries is reviewed from time to time for adjusting or guiding the intervention. If the feedback is within the project scope, the agency will respond positively. Rapid Post Distribution Monitoring (RPDM), end-user surveys, exit interviews are conducted for beneficiaries to express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the project inputs and through one on one or face-to-face discussion with beneficiaries. Beneficiaries are also provided with suggestion boxes through which they can share their complaints or feedback i.e. for those who know how to read and write. Gender issues are also addressed through AAP committees by ensuring gender equality in the selection of members.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

GBI has a policy on PSEA which is served to all project staff for their understanding. Every time a new project is being launched, the staff is oriented on PSEA, as well as vendors and beneficiaries. GBI works with protection partners to handle protection issues including SEA cases. In case there are SEA cases, GBI through referral pathways available refers the cases to the protection partner to investigate and help the survivors. GBV focal point or officers are consulted to identify safe, confidential, and appropriate systems of care (i.e. referral pathways) for survivors, FSL staff have the basic skills to provide survivors with information on where they can obtain support, GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) are incorporated into project messaging and other FSL related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback from the beneficiaries is used to guide programming and incorporate the inputs into activities. The feedback helps in addressing shortfalls in future interventions. Feedback from beneficiaries is reviewed from time to time for adjusting or guiding the intervention. If the feedback is within the project scope, the agency will respond positively and revise the projects to address the needs of the beneficiaries and the most vulnerable. The project will conduct entry meetings with the local authority, RRC, beneficiaries as part of community mobilization and with different groups, for example, PWDs, women groups, youth representatives, chiefs, local authorities, and beneficiaries’ representatives at the project inception. The aim of the meetings is to orient and mobilize different stakeholders about the project’s project duration, target beneficiaries and selection criteria, distribution site selections, donors, and the role of the community in the selection of beneficiaries and volunteers. The project will prioritize the needs of vulnerable people such as men, women, boys, girls, PWDs, PLW, elderly, etc. and this information is communicated through communication and inception meetings in presence of local authority. The project will consult people with disability and collect disability disaggregated data which allows them to identify specific needs/vulnerabilities, risks, and barriers that prevent people with disability from accessing services on an equal basis with other people.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Male and female, women and men or boys and girls are differently affected by humanitarian crises, situations, or disasters requiring an intervention. Community mobilization brings together all people of different sex, age group, and status to decide for themselves as far as humanitarian assistance is concerned. The project will prioritize the needs of women and girls by involving them in key decision-making processes and by being part of AAP. 70% of beneficiaries of the target population are women and 30% men, 45% are children (31.5% girls and 13.5% boys). Special support will be given to women groups in form of cash transfers, training, and loan and assets ownership. Forced taxation of Humanitarian Assistance and access restrictions is mitigated by seeking a letter of clearance from the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Juba for humanitarian items moving from Juba to field locations. The risk is also transferred to a third party by giving transportation work to logistics companies that take responsibility for the safe delivery of items. While delivering within the locality security reports or situations that might arise to forced taxation are analyzed before authorizing delivery. Movement of staff is planned to take place between 9:00 am – 4 pm when the security of guaranteed – no voyages allowed. Normally, the local authority and local leaders try to corrupt the process of identification of beneficiaries by bringing their close relatives instead of vulnerable people in need of assistance or even having themselves registered. To mitigate targeting of wrong beneficiaries GBI conducts community mobilization before identification of beneficiaries to inform them about the selection criteria. We also work with Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Committees established at the Boma level to help in the identification of beneficiaries. GBI also works in close coordination with relevant government ministries who are aware of the humanitarian work.
