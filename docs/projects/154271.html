Project Summary

Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) will provide multipurpose cash assistance to the most vulnerable 2000 families in conflict-affected areas of Northern Iraq (Erbil, Dohuk, Ninewa and Anbar Governorates), apart from that PAH will also refer identified protection needs and provide linkages with Iraq Government social safety net.
PAH project will contribute to following CWG objectives:
Obj.1: Provide MPCA to extremely vulnerable households affected by armed conflict and to vulnerable households affected by a shock generating humanitarian needs that cannot be covered by existing national services,
Obj.2: Facilitate vulnerable households access complementary and critical humanitarian and national services including non-contributory social protection programs

Needs

Even one year after the end of military operations in Iraq there are still 5 900 000 vulnerable persons (internally displaced persons -IDP's, returnees, host community), according to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2019. In 2018 while the number of returnees was steadily increasing the population of IDPs remained significant with many of them postponing the decision to return to their original areas due to protection and livelihood needs. Initial data for multipurpose cash assistance for Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019 shows that despite ongoing increase in recovery activities there are still 1 900 000 persons (700 000 IDPs, 1 000 000 returnees and 200 000 host community) in need of cash assistance. Out of this group cash assistance should reach 740 000 persons (400 000 IDPs, 300 000 returnees and 40 000 host community). Current priority locations focus on ten Governorates including, among others, Ninewa (Telafar, Sinjar, Mosul), Anbar (Falluja) and also Erbil and Dohuk (Sumel, Zakho). Based on 2018 house to house assessment conducted by PAH in four locations in Sumel and Zakho District in Dohuk Governorate high needs were identified among 21% of households, while this proportion for female headed household reached 85% level (for men headed household - 16%). 31% of IPDs families were identified as vulnerable, for host community high vlnerability level was identified in 15% of households. Age analysis identified following division of heads of households: less then 29 years old (18%), 30-59 (65%), 60 and more 18%. Main three negative copying strategies that were followed by assessed families: reduce of spending on healthcare or medication due to lack of money (93%), relying on donations of food or basic goods from relatives, community, or the mosque (85%) and limiting portion size at mealtime (69%). On average main expenditures categories were: food (35%), healthcare (18%), rent (9,4%) with clothing (9,3%) slightly behind. Assessment provided also information about main vulnerability criteria for specific households such as: head of household disability and illness (28%), toilet shearing (20%) and missing legal documents (9%). Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA) conducted by Reach in September 2018 provide additional insight into current needs of IDPs in Iraq. 40% of IDPs families require protection assistance including 8% of persons who are in need of legal assistance. Negative copying strategies are visible in child labor and forced marriages area with 6% of child working and 2% of households having at least one married child between 12-17 years old. 28% of households were identified as in need of health assistance, with 58% of them claiming that cost of services are the main barrier accessing healthcare. 37% of households confirmed that they are in need of education assistance, with 46% mentioned that the main reason of not attending to school is related to education related cost. 12% of assessed families are food insecure and additional 51% is vulnerable to food insecurity. Main negative copying strategies that were identified in relation to lack of food were: shifting toward cheaper / lower quality food (73%), consuming less food during meals (47%), reducing the number of daily meals (41%). Main livelihood negative copying strategies were: buying food on credit or borrowed money (62%), spending savings (60%) and selling household property (44%). 62% of households reported a total monthly income less than their monthly expenditure. There are 32% households (out of which 12% are female headed households) with at least one unemployed adult actively seeking work. 12% of households has a monthly income from employment and pension less than 480,000 IQD (400 USD) while 51% of households own debt valued at more than 505,000 IQD (420 USD). Main reasons of taking a debt were mentioned as rent and utilities for shelter 53%, food 21% and healthcare 15%. In 2019 according to CWG data the number of returnees will reach 4 000 000 apart from 1 500 000 IDPs in need. When compered with HRP 2018 data from December 2018 (3 200 000 returnees and 2 600 000 IDPs) it can be noticed that less then 50% of IDPs decided to return to their homes during 2018. IOM research provides main reason of IDPs reluctance to return to their place of origin: house destruction, property occupation and legal disputes, no livelihood and lack of basic services in a place of origin, community tension, population change and perceived insecurity in a place of origin. Since those reasons are related often to long term problems it can be expected that the population of IDPs will remain significant in 2019. Data sources:- PAH own data (house to house assessment)- Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA) Septeber 2018 by REACH- Reasons to Remain: Categorizing Protracted Displacement in Iraq by IOM

Activities

1. Identification of project locations - project locations will be identified through data analysis of UNOPS IDP Referal Centre in cooperation with Cash Working Group (CWG) as well as through needs assessment of specific locations in Dohuk, Erbil, Ninewa and Anbar Governorates.2. Selection of cash service provider - based on appropriate logistic procedure PAH will select a private company partner who will be responsible for direct provision of cash assistance to selected families.3. Cooperation with local authorities - before initiating needs assessment PAH will share a schedule of activities with local authorities to inform them about project and technical aspects of implementation. Specific agreement form will be signed with local authorities that will list PAH and local authorities obligations.4. Communication with community - PAH team will organize a meeting for community leaders to explain project activities, intervention steps, how to provide feedback and complains, etc.4. Household level needs assessment - PAH team will assess needs in each specific household on geographically limited area to identify vulnerable families. PAH MEAL team will be responsible for monitoring of assessment activities. Beneficiary selection criteria will be based on MPCA Vulnerability Model version approved by CWG.5. Based on acquired data PAH will select the most vulnerable families eligible to receive multipurpose cash assistance based on CWG tools. HHs will be targeted and assisted based on socio-economic vulnerability using standard CWG tools, depending on situation cash assistance might be provided for one, two or three months. Based on CWG data it can be expected that about 44,4% of identified vulnerable persons will be eligible for one month assistance (480 000 IQD), 31,8% for two months assistance (960 000 IQD) and finally about 23,8% should be eligible for three months assistance (1 440 000 IQD).6. Families with missing legal documents identified during household level needs assessment will be refereed to protection-specialized organizations. Standard CWG house to house assessment tool already include question about legal needs and in case of confirmation of such needs assessed person may agree to share personal data with external protection-specialized organization. Once such case will be identified PAH will send information of such case to external organization who will be responsible to conduct more detailed protection assessment and provide assistance. PAH has existing cooperation in this area with Mercy Hands for International Aid (MH) since 2018 (MH and PAH jointly implement cash activities and MH is responsible for refereed legal cases), in 2019 PAH will initiate cooperation with new organizations to expand possible options for referral cases. PAH will also verify, as a part of PDM if referral was successful and whether legal problem was solved. In 2019 if CWG will decide to initiate referral cases for other sectors then protection cluster PAH will expand it's partners portfolio to initiate other types of referral cases.7. In 2019 PAH will initiate cooperation with MoLSA under CWG network related to referral cases for Iraq social safety net (a part of exit strategy for Iraq). Depending on developments in this area in 2019 this cooperation may include joint meetings, advocacy for development of socially acceptable criteria for selection of beneficiaries, sharing PAH experience and in case of establishment of Iraq social safety net - referral of identified vulnerable families within system that ensures data protection and safety of PAH beneficiaries. 8. Selected families list will be verified with other organizations providing cash to avoid overlapping of the assistance.9. Depending on type of cash service provider that will be selected selected households may receive sim cards to access e-wallets.10. Selected households will receive cash assistance. Transfer value will be calculated based on minimum expenditure basket. Mobile points will be established to allow beneficiaries for easy access for cash withdraw. PAH MEAL team will be responsible for monitoring of distribution process, exit interviews and complains and feedback collection through multiple tools.11. One month after the end of distribution cycle PAH MEAL team will conduct post distribution monitoring to verify results of intervention.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Presence of armed groups in project implementation area.2. Difficulties in cooperation with local authorities (local authorities requesting for a review of beneficiary list, local authorities asking to add specific families for selected household list, local authorities questioning status of selected families or they actual presence in project implementation area, local authorities actively trying to disturb distribution process by prohibiting entrance to distribution area).3. Distortion of local market functionality as a result of cash intervention.4. Changes in Iraqi law which may affect private company ability to provide cash services.5. Increase of tensions between host community and IDPs and/or selected and unselected families.6. Risks related to corruption, bribery and abuse of vulnerable households.

Mitigation Measures

1. PAH will conduct security assessment before entering new project operational area to verify security level and presence/possible interference of any external actors2. PAH will organize a meeting with local authorities to discuss major cooperation points and to sign MoU which will list obligations and rights of each party to ensure independence of PAH actions. PAH will also use support of major governmental coordination units such as JCMC and JCCC to ensure that PAH team will have all necessary permissions to operate within selected areas. PAH will not share beneficiary data with any actors with whom PAH did not sign a specific data protection agreement. In 2018 PAH signed such agreements with cash service provider, local implementing partner (MH) that is also used for referral of legal cases and UNHCR (to minimize risk of doubling assistance to the same group of beneficiaries). PAH is also in the process of signing agreement with WFP that will also allow PAH to start using SCOPE system which will enable to control beneficiary data in a more secure way. Starting from 2018 PAH has implemented data protection policy in line with EU GDPR/RODO regulations.3. PAH will conduct a market assessment for those operational areas where such research was not yet conducted. Apart from that PAH team will conduct price monitoring during intervention to analyze cash intervention influence over local markets.4. In order to mitigate possible threats related to distribution of cash service provision PAH will closely cooperate with selected private company to identify possible challenges in advance. Transfer of funds to private company account should be done in advance. Specific messages to beneficiaries (such as timeline of distribution) will be done only once funds will be available on cash service provider account.5. PAH in order not to increase tensions between different society groups will conduct blanket needs assessments to ensure local community that all households within certain geographical areas are assessed, without preferring one group over another. To mitigate community tensions between those who have received cash and those who have not, PAH will implement key messages to ensure that beneficiary selection criteria are understood by the local community. Number of complaints from not eligible beneficiaries can be lowered thanks to calling unselected beneficiaries with information about the reason of not being selected to receive assistance through standard messages.6. PAH team will follow standard policies (anti corruption policy, code of conduct, protection against sexual abuse). PAH MEAL team will also be responsible to monitor staff performance and behavior towards the beneficiaries.

How is your project accountable to affected population

PAH cooperates with local authorities and community leaders (PAH organizes community meetings to inform local population about PAH activities, explain beneficiary selection criteria and timeline of activities). PAH uses complains, feedback and accountability system that uses multiply tools to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback to PAH (complain box, exit interviews, telephone hotline). Based on the current experience the biggest group of received complains (52%) are related to persons who were not eligible to receive assistance, live outside of selected are of operation or has already received cash assistance recently through UNHCR. About 8% of complains turned out to be eligible beneficiaries who were either not present at home at the time of registration or were accidentally skipped by the field team during house to house needs assessment. The most popular way of feedback providing are complain boxes (70% of total complains) with telephone hotline covering remaining 30% of complains. PAH team has regular project review meetings to analyze quality of response and analyze beneficiaries feedback. Finally PAH team organizes lessons learned meeting to identify weak points in project activities and how to improve them.
