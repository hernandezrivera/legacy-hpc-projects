Project Summary

In alignment with the 2022 HRP the 2022 WASH Cluster Strategy, Objective 2 “Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians”, and Objective 3: “Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians”. NRC in partnership with Improvement and Development for Communities (IDCO) aims to deliver water, wastewater and storm water management activities to ensure that access, physical health and assets of most vulnerable communities and families are ensured and protected. The project will also respond to the CMWU appeal (October 2022) to support their capacity to react to routine and sudden needs as an essential and urgent necessity, including the preparedness for winter to avoid flooding, shortage of critical equipment, materials and spare parts.

The proposed project responds to the humanitarian needs and the severe consequences on the public health in a vulnerable community located in Beit Lahia Municipality, Aslan neighborhood with inadequate, inappropriate or non-existent water, drainage and sewage network. The project is included in the WASH Cluster Vulnerability mapping for Gaza that was conducted in 2022 and is approved by the PWA and is also part of the Municipality of Beit Lahia master plan which is being coordinated with the CMWU. Through this project, 320 HHs living in a flood prone area will be supported with the construction of a drainage network, the installation/ rehabilitation of the wastewater collection network, and the upgrade of the water network ensuring that HHs have a proper wastewater system, are protected from flood events and the overflow of sewage in the streets and in their homes, and finally a water network protected from contamination is ensured.
Furthermore this project supports WASH responders in all Gaza Strip governorates with preparedness and response measures to protect the vulnerable communities living in high flood prone areas from the risk of displacement, damage of property, lack of access to public services, and public health impact through 1) small scale storm water drainage interventions, which include installing storm water pipes, manholes, rehabilitating storm water drainage systems, and disconnecting storm water networks from the existing sewage system. 2) preparedness of water and wastewater facilities for the winter, maintenance of wastewater treatment plant and sewage pump stations. Finally, the project targets are the most vulnerable communities based on a detailed flooding risk matrix, shared by the WASH Cluster, CMWU and the PWA.

During the implementation of this project the communities’ needs and concerns will be acknowledged and reflected before the implementation phase with the support of IDCO. This collaboration will ensure active community engagement by sharing project information, involving key community members in the design/implementation of the project and supporting the active presence of women representatives. Moreover, NRC through IDCO will also provide hygiene NFI and awareness material to sensitize the local community, improve hygiene practices and finally communities will be informed about the NRC complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) and how it can be accessed.
This project will also complement NRC shelter interventions supporting vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather living in flood prone areas in addition to flood mitigation activities involving installation of storm water infiltration borehole in the public school at the target area.

1 - Needs

In Gaza, the blockade, recurrent outbreaks of hostilities and the internal Palestinian political division have contributed to a serious deterioration in quality and quantity of basic services and facilities. Entry restrictions on materials and equipment have delayed rehabilitation of already damaged infrastructure and recovery of facilities and services and delay the implementation of direly needed longer term projects. The COVID-19 outbreak in the last years and the recent war in Ukraine further exacerbated an already dire situation, severely affecting the coping capacity of vulnerable households and their access to basic, safe and sufficient services. As already mentioned in the summary, the proposed project responds to the humanitarian needs of the vulnerable community living in Aslan neighborhood and is part of the WASH Cluster Vulnerability mapping for Gaza. It is also part of the Municipality of Beit Lahia masterplan which is being coordinated with Coastal Municipality Water Utility (CMWU). According to the WASH Cluster Vulnerability Mapping that was conducted in 2022, 1051 HHs (8913 people) are living in the selected area of the intervention. According to the assessment 42% of the population is unemployed, leading to 55% of the residents being below the poverty line. 13 % of the population is suffering from chronic diseases or are considered people with disabilities and finally there is a 45% aid dependency rate. Out of the 1051 HHs, 320 HHs are not connected to the sewage network and the area experiences frequent flood events with severe impact to private and public assets loss. Due to the flooding, overflow of sewage in the streets and houses is also witnessed putting in danger the public health of the community. The proposed intervention will target 320 HHs most vulnerable and flood affected households and will contribute to the Municipality of Beit Lahia masterplan. In October 2022, the Coastal Municipal Water Utility (CMWU) has conducted an assessment across all 25 Gaza Municipalities to update public infrastructure needs. This data collected registers all the water facilities, wastewater pumping stations and treatment plants and storm water drainage networks as part of their preparedness plan for the upcoming winter season. In addition, the assessment specifies the reason of requested maintenance linking with flooding events and affected communities. The assessment was designed to list all required technical information for operation and maintenance activities including estimated costs. According to the assessment, 39 water facilities and 28 wastewater pumping stations and plants were identified as being in critical condition requiring urgent support to ensure proper operation during winter and to avoid negative effects and loss of assets from the supported community. Priority works identified include general maintenance and replacement of the hydraulic accessories, maintenance of electromechanical equipment, rehabilitation and repair of the facilities infrastructure. Similar for wastewater treatment plants and pumping stations supply and installation of sluice gates, pump maintenance, rehabilitation of bar screens and finally the supply of diesel engine for the wastewater pumping unit due to unreliability of main electricity provision network are the most urgent works identified.

2 - Activities

As identified in the needs assessment, the proposed intervention will respond to identified health risks due to the non-existing or inappropriate wastewater management system, the loss of assets due to flooding events and finally to ensure that adequate and safe water is provided to the vulnerable community. The proposed project will complement NRC Shelter programming to ensure that vulnerable HHs living in substandard housings units or are previously affected or exposed to harsh weather risks are supported. Due to the complexity and the needs of the project, NRC will closely collaborate with CMWU and Technical Department of Municipality of Beit Lahia to develop and receive technical designs and documents for the proposed works. Proposed works will include:-Excavating and properly backfilling all trenches following all necessary safety measures-Backfilling the existing septic tanks, to reduce the physical /public health/environmental risks, such as the physical risks related to collapse of the deteriorated septic tanks roofs and the environmental risks related to the mixing of wastewater/storm water during flooding.-Installation of piping network with required fittings.-Installation of manholes to ensure access and monitoring of the network -Installation of new drainage pipe network -Rehabilitation and upgrade of current pipe water distribution network. Under close supervision of NRC field technical staff, a certified and experienced construction provider will be selected to implement the project. NRC local partner IDCO will make sure that residents are informed about the construction activities taking place in the area, monitor that all safety measures are in place and communicated accordingly and that all concerns and challenges of the affected population are addressed. Furthermore, this intervention is designed to respond to the urgent preparedness needs of the WASH infrastructure mitigating winter related and flooding risks, considering and prioritizing the impact on vulnerable communities, especially those that lack the resilience to cope with natural crisis. The target communities in this project are among the most vulnerable and marginalized, living in depression areas, in substandard housing units. Specifically, NRC will be looking to support: Wastewater Pumping Stations -Supply and installation of sluice gate in Block O in Rafah Municipality , -Rehabilitation of Bar Screen in Main Pumping Station in Al Nussairat Municipality,- Maintenance of pumps in Al hassaina Pumping Station in Al Nussairat Municipality.- Maintenace of pumps and sluice gate in Al Bassa Pumping in Dair Al Balah Municipality.- Maintenace of storm water drainage network on the school street in Abasan Al Kabira Municipality .Water Facilities:-Supply and install new chlorine dosing pumping unit in K21 Al Berkah water well in Deir Al Balah Municipality.- Various maintenance activities and electromechanical works in STLV 6,000 DP in Deir Al Balah Municipality which also serves Khayounis and Rafah Municipalities.-Maintenance of the existing well pump and Construction new well room instead of the deteriorated steel container, Maintenance of the existing electrical board and supply and install new manifold 4” in Alsatar 2 Well in Khanyounis Municipality.-Supply and install new manifold 6” and a new electrical board in Al Najar water well in Bani Suhaila Municipality.-Supply and install new cyclone 6” and pressure gauges, pressure switches in Al Tahliya water well in Bani Suhaila Municipality.-Supply and install chlorination dosing pumps for 10 wells in various in need locations in Gaza Strip.-Supply various stock materials required for desalination plants operation, maintenance and emergency response such as (cartridge filters, BWRO membranes 8"& 4", media, chemicals, end caps and accessories) for the CMWU. Winterization activities-Supply and install circular cast iron manholes cover 60 cm for wastewater manholes,-Supply and install rectangular cover with bearing capacity of 25 ton for rain gutter manholes. The project improves the WASH responders’ preparedness and response capacity to provide timely and effective response during winter-floods, aiming to mitigate the potential effects of winter-flooding on the most vulnerable people living in unserved communities. Where the impact of storm water flooding and its mix with wastewater due to operation failure of water or wastewater facilities and networks cause temporary displacement, loss of livelihood, damage to property and assets, public health risks, and barriers such as blocked roads to access public services like schools, hospitals, closure of business. . The infrastructural and maintenance support in this project will provide durable solutions to ensure a long-lasting impact. The community engagement activities will ensure that the target people are well informed about project activities, and consulted in planning/ implementing a storm water and flood prevention measures, aiming to empower the local community roles including the women, in monitoring service providers and local authorities activities, in which will enhance the feeling of ownership towards community facilities and support the role of local community in preserving the infrastructure facilities from damages and misusing, and increase the awareness about the environmental impact of climate change and its negative weather effects. All NRC staff and local partners staff receive an information on code of conduct, PSEA and CFM. NRC will also include binding conditions in all procurement documents and procedures with suppliers to ensure their commitment. In addition, NRC protection team will develop and disseminate PSEA awareness raising material.

3 - Indicators

The monitoring and evaluation system ensures, through specific procedures, the tracking of the project objectives and indicators. Reporting mechanisms are in place to ensure regular reporting on defined project indicators. Furthermore, M&E will monitor the progress of implementation, and reporting any feedback received from beneficiaries or programme team, especially when using field visits to follow up closely on the progress of the project. In addition to that, monthly project reports feed into the NRC Global Output Reporting System used by all of NRC’s field offices to report on project progress. The project technical monitoring and reporting plan will include the following activities: •Detailed database for interventions, including assessment data, BoQs, implementation status reports and completion report• Monitoring visits during implementation • Communication of NRC's beneficiary complaints mechanism and follow up of complaints. The WASH technical staff is responsible for collecting data and information. The M+E department is responsible for analysing the data and outcome reporting. M&E will conduct sample visits to the households to ensure the progress of implementation and report on any feedbacks received during the visit to the programme team. At the end of the project, M&E and programme team will conduct post assessment to provide critical feedback to shelter program staff about the quality of the intervention and the process and ensure outcome reporting. Progress review meetings will be organized with the participation of NRC WASH team, and local partner staff, NRC programme management, M&E, finance and logistics team to review progress of targets versus actuals, discuss any challenges/risks that may hinder progress and mitigation measures and review budget versus actuals. The monitoring tools include an indicator matrix with definition and project M&E plan, post-implementation interviews with targeted beneficiaries, which will provide quantitative and qualitative data. Lessons learnt will be captured in the grants closure meetings and used for informing NRC's following year strategy. in addition to that, The NRC WASH team will ensure that the WASH Cluster is regularly updated on the progress reached. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NRC will communicate and receive feedback from the technical department of CMWU, the Municipality of Beit Lahia and affected communities throughout the project implementation. At the designing phase, the targeted community will be informed about the objective and scope of the project. This will give them the opportunity to raise concerns, share information and inform NRC’s design. This will also be done through coordination with the Municipality and the CMWU. The affected population and beneficiaries will be provided with an Information, Complaint, and Feedback mechanism throughout the project duration to be able to quickly respond to any concerns during the implementation process. At the end of the project, NRC will carry out a Post Distribution Monitoring to analyse the results of the intervention, the level of appreciation from beneficiaries and collect any comments/suggestions/complaints. Field staffs are sufficiently trained in identifying protection concerns of vulnerable households. As part of the baseline/endline assessments, target households will be asked protection-oriented questions to assess their feeling regarding safety and dignity improvement. The self-assessment of the level of safety and dignity is not only asked in relation to WASH as a product but also to WASH as a process e.g. the service provide implementing activities in the area. A confidential referral process is in place to ensure support to identified cases encountering protection risks. The strong community involvement and the feedback mechanism in the project will prevent harm in the first place and will allow project management to quickly react in case of concerns of beneficiaries throughout the project duration. Moreover, they will receive induction about the suitable safety measurements to avoid accidents during floods and during construction activities. The school of the area will also be targeted and direct meetings with students and teachers will take place to develop and disseminate safety and hygiene awareness messages .NRC respects confidentiality and safeguards personal and other sensitive information and apply tight protocol when sharing beneficiary’s information or during the referral of protection cases to the related cluster.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will focus on the marginalized/economic vulnerable community of Aslan neighborhood of Municipality of Beit Lahia in Northern Gaza, a flood prone area, that do not have access to wastewater collection network and/or sufficient resources/capacities to prevent/respond to effects of the winter season. The project is addressing population needs at a community level however NRC also identifies the most vulnerable households based on a socioeconomic household vulnerability assessment. The factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability includes socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household especially female/widow/elderly/PWD, income and dependency ratio . The scoring system is designed to prioritize female/elderly/disabled headed households, heads of households with high dependency rates, large family size . During the implementation phase, the target communities will be provided with clear and effective information, complaint, and feedback mechanism about the project, that will be link to the related municipality, in addition, sharing project information and discussing the pre-identified site-location and works separately through women’s committees will provide a protected space for women to have a strong involvement in infrastructure-decisions related to their communities.
