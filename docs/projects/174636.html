Project Summary

The influx of Central African refugees in the East Cameroon region has accentuated the vulnerability of these host populations in terms of precarious living conditions, access to socio-health infrastructure and means of subsistence. This situation has created pressure on the available natural resources and on the services offered, which are already largely insufficient. Localities such as Mandjou and Boulembe are experiencing strong demographic growth and are facing enormous difficulties in resilience, however preventing them from finding lasting solutions. Among these, we can cite: very severe cassava rots which compromise the yields of family farms, an upsurge in agro-pastoral conflicts between breeders and farmers, uncertainty of the savings resulting from market gardening production due to bad weather, To this must be added the growing problems of under-education of young people and the delay in the evolution of mentalities both among nationals and among refugees which cloud the prospects for development and social cohesion. The most exposed vulnerable populations are women (53.5% of the refugee population) and children aged 0 to 11 who constitute 44% of the refugee population. Almost 34.4% of refugees and 15.5% of host populations are in a situation of food insecurity (severe and moderate). The significant increase in the availability and diversity of local food productions, then the establishment of means of preventive control against white flies and cassava rots and finally the reframing of the agricultural calendar according to the climatic and environmental specificities of the villages in order to ensure market gardening activities throughout the year are the main challenges in our intervention areas. Considering the massive and sudden arrival of refugees from the Central African crisis in 2004, 45% of food consumed by households in these villages has been imported. Local productions (cassava, corn, vegetables) are not yet significant in meeting local household food demands.
According to the assessment made by WFP, humanitarian solidarity partners (UNHCR, International Solidarity, FAO, PLAN CAMEROUN ...) and sectoral (MINADER, MINEPIA, MINPROF, MINEPAT, MINFOPRA, MINEPDED ...) accompanied by a Participatory Community Plan held in 2018 and 2019 showed that in these villages on the vulnerability of populations, 30% of the population is very vulnerable; 50% have a medium vulnerability level and 20% a moderate vulnerability.
The main activity of the populations noted during exchanges with local communities, held throughout our coverage area, is the cultivation of cassava, to which is grafted that of corn, peanuts, market gardening, petty trade, fishing, breeding.
In view of the situation mentioned above in terms of food imbalance which has generated rampant insecurity and a shortage of food availability in line with the ever-continuing demographic growth within communities which, at the end of the consultations, one major solutions had been to enable them to meet their own nutritional needs through the establishment of agro-pastoral activities; the provision of small agricultural equipment; quality seeds and openness to small trade, which justifies a need for support on the technical itineraries recorded. Hence the project will support the increase in agro-pastoral production in the East.
This intervention is in line with the consolidation of the assets carried out between 2018 and 2019 on the one hand and with a view to building resilience capacities within the beneficiary populations: food security, nutrition and social cohesion of somewhere else.
These activities, effectively put together, will constitute a march towards an integrated agriculture that can contribute to the achievement of the Zero Hunger Objective by 2030 according to the agenda that Cameroon and the other UN member countries have adopted and which constitutes the mission of the World Food Program.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Overall objectiveThe project aims to increase the availability and improve the diversification of consumption and accessibility to nutritious foods for 47,500 beneficiaries in the North, Adamawa and East regions for a period of 10 months.Specific objectivesOs 1: Increase agro-pastoral production of 9,500 households despite the COVID-19 context in order to contribute to the availability of food and economic empowerment.Key Activities :• Distribution of food to 47500 beneficiaries as they create productive assets;• Development of 300 ha of land in North, Adamawa and East regions to produce bio-fortified cassava, sorghum, cowpea (for an increase in production and nutritional aspects), market gardening, and corn;• Continuity in training and follow-up in agricultural technical itinerary.• Monitoring of individual plots to ensure the replication of new agricultural techniques learned.• Ensure the health protection of beneficiaries and staff by respecting preventive measures against the new coronavirus.• Construction of 15 fishponds of 1000 m² each in the sitesOs 2: Improve the nutritional practices of 9500 households based on local food products.Key Activities:• Culinary demonstration, training on recipes based on local products • Sensitization and technical monitoring on optimal infant and young child feeding practices • Sensitization on hand washing Os3: Strengthen the capacities of vulnerable households on post-harvest management in order to reduce post-harvest losses.Key activities:• Training and support for beneficiaries on the transformation of commonly grown crops.• Support the communities to improve market access. • Construction of 10 dryers of 5m * 5m and 6 warehouses.Implementation strategyWFP through its cooperating partners will offers a multitude of complementary advice, technical, financial and material aid in order to achieve the objective of the project. In addition, the local communities who host the project will make the land available and take care of settling in advance all questions related to land ownership in order to guarantee and secure the assets of the beneficiaries during and after the implementation of the said project. Considering the different stakeholders and beneficiaries, we will adopt the participatory management method which requires the involvement and participation of all beneficiaries in all stages of implementation. The main beneficiaries of this project are the Central African refugees (70%) and the host population (30%) from some selected villages in North, Adamawa and East regions depending on the WFP targeting.The activities will be done in teams and each team will be under the supervision of Animators who will be responsible for organizing activities in the field, technically monitoring beneficiaries during production, giving daily tasks to beneficiaries and ensuring that they are respected, as well as establishing daily attendance lists on each site. The Animators will report to the project assistant (supervisor) who will compile the information collected. He will also ensure that labor standards are respected in the various sites, 6 hours of work per day from Monday to Friday (days chosen by the communities). The community relays will accompany the animators on the sites and will be chosen from the group and by the members of the community. Each beneficiary receives food according to the number of working days (20 days per month) and according to the daily rations set by WFP. The beneficiaries of food assistance will be identified after discussions between the local populations and partner based on criteria pre-established by the communities and guided by WFP. They will be informed about the food rations per day, per person and on the number of days per month (20 days).
