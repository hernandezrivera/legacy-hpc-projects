Project Summary

The project is located in 6sub-districts with different severity scale ranking (Albab 4 , Daeetaza 4, Dana 4, Afrin 4, Ras Alain 4 an and Tell Abiad 3 ) targeting 6000 individuals (3000 M+3000 F), 4500 school aged children from 6-17 years(2250 M+2250F). 600 teachers and 600 teachers(300M+300F) and consists of two key component areas: preparing currently out of school children from boys and girls for returning to formal learning, and securing equitable access to education provision for children and youth in these areas
A ‘Back to Learning’ campaign will be given alongside details of the upcoming rehabilitation works, ongoing support available to children and resources provided to families. This work also forms part of the recruitment strategy for the emerging Parent/Teacher Associations,to secure ongoing community support and participation in the provision of the stable education services.
For children not currently in school, non-formal education provision will be made in each of the five sub-districts. An initial cycle of ALP from Jan-September 2023 will target those children deemed most likely (based on assessments and time out of school) to be able to transition into formal provision for the start of the academic year 2023-2024.
Additional ongoing support will be provided throughout the academic year once children have transferred to formal schools; this will support their retention in this provision through remedial classes and homework support as appropriate. Children will be identified according to needs (difficulties with learning, access to learning spaces outside of school, at risk of dropping out etc
All NFE programs will integrate Child Protection activities. In the formal component, a program of rehabilitation will be undertaken in 12 schools (10 primary and 2secondary) across the 6 sub-districts. This will include appropriate remedial works to the fabric of the buildings (doors, windows, lights, toilet facilities etc). the rehabelitated schools will also targeted by additional building works in order to ensure the sites are appropriate and accessible for children with disabilities. Essential materials will be supplied to the schools, including whiteboards, classroom furniture, visual aids and other teaching materials. First aid kits and fuel during winter months will be supplied; security and safety plans will be established and resourced at each location. Stationery and textbooks will be provided for children boys and girls, CWD and staff females and males. Professional development and capacity building activities will enhance the performance and quality of the staff.
The rehabilitation work will be designed to target the WASH facilities to be accessible and safe for boys, girls and CwD.
The project also includes provision of transportation for needed children and incentives for the volunteer teachers building on the truth that all targeted schools are not supported by any organaization and all education personnel there working as a volunteers .Through all these components, the project will therefore improve the access to and the quality of formal and non-formal education in NWS which is align with the same objectives for HRP 2022 .
The project also contains provision of PSS sessions in addition to referral mechanism for Child Protection.
The project activities have been designed in accordance to responce to the main findings reported in the HNO 2023.
Till now RW has secured 15% of the planned fund for this project .
Finally all the COVID 19 preventation measures shall be taken into acount while implementing the project activities.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Harassment among the project staff on the one hand, as well as possible incidents of sexual exploitation between teachers and their male and female students. RW will respond to such potential action by following the strong PSEA strategy: No offer of employment is made before background checks Engaging local populations and having community support is vital to protect against sexual exploitation and abuse, this involves clearly communicating with local populations about their rights and entitlements. Signing a code of conduct is obligatory for the project staff RW also works to identify and reduce risks of sexual exploitation and abuse in all of our operations through various training courses All personnel is required to complete two mandatory online courses on the prevention of sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. RW induction and other leadership training courses also include a focus on our Ethics and how to live up to our fundamental principles. RW will review internal reporting mechanism PSEA, There is evidence of policy/policies implementation through regular monitoring and reporting to the project manager as well a mechanism for monitoring and review of the complaint mechanism and reporting on all allegations of SH, including actions taken, and timeliness of response, ensures that they are made publicmessaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries, through posters paneers Finally RW will assign a trained staff to be PSEA focal point
