Project Summary

The constant threat of eviction in IDP sites destabilizes the ability for long-term community development preventing viable durable solutions to occur. As of August 2021, 92,042 individuals have been evicted from their settlements stemming from a total of 2,304 incidences of forceful eviction perpetrated by landowners (NRC, Evictions Information Portal 2021). Over 56% of all evictions occurred within the Kahda and Daynile districts in Banadir which accommodate some 1,808 IDP sites or 848,760 IDPs (Kahda and Daynile Site Verification; CCCM Cluster, 2021). Furthermore, 11% of IDPs or 17% of Banadir IDPs believe that they are currently at risk of being evicted from their shelters (REACH, JMCNA 2021). Inaccessibility to land tenure security continues to function as a primary obstacle preventing IDPs from establishing permanent settlements.
The situation in most of Somalia is highly variable. Women and young girls are still exposed to continued protection risks or other forms of exploitation and abuse. Due to several social and cultural factors exacerbated by displacement and lack of resources, generalized insecurity and limited access to support networks, in particular, lack of specialized service providers addressing HLP issues and lack of information regarding available services prevent even the most basic support for vulnerable IDPs and the host population. Moreover, there are still gaps in preventing and responding to HLP issues along with the limited availability of national skilled human resources with the required knowledge and background. Access to areas where populations are known to be in severe distress is highly restricted. In this scenario, the role of national NGOs like HIWA, who has the broadest and deepest reach and can access difficult areas, is crucial.
Protection, monitoring and response mechanisms with regards to land, housing and property is lacking in most of the protection programming. Most often legal Aid services, land, houses and property rights are not prioritized hence leaving big gaps in the protection response mechanism. There is poor coordination among protection and response service providers, especially on land, housing, and property rights. Furthermore, the protection response mechanisms have limited or no provision of free legal aid services to IDPs, survivors of GBV and HLP.
Therefore, this project seeks to improve and strengthen coordination among service providers and to enhance protection and response mechanism for IDPs on land, housing and property rights. These will be achieved through eviction monitoring, prevention and response services, improved referral pathways, on spot monitoring and response to assist IDPs to reclaim housing, land and properties, awareness-raising, free legal aid, and innovative and sustainable alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. The project will ensure individuals affected by displacement receive support assistance to improve their tenure security situation and address post-eviction complications including enhanced capacity to Prevention and response to evictions.
Cognizant to promote equity, HIWA shall endeavor to provide equal chance, inclusiveness and participation of minority members of the community as well as disabled groups in the locations and entrench transparency in recruitment of beneficiaries, capacity building, empowerment and resource sharing to achieve a comprehensive community engagement. Active participation of individuals with special needs will be pursued to identify, support and strengthen the coping mechanisms that beneficiaries often create to protect themselves against threats to their safety and dignity, and also foster beneficiaries’ self-esteem and development of skills. Case worker’s selection and orientations will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity are addressed as well as offer services as per the community acceptable practices.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1.1: Advocacy and awareness campaigns on HLP issues in target locations to mitigate eviction risks through information sharing targeting 22,150 individuals. Provision of information services will be designed to assist eviction victims access services, navigate complex procedures, and utilize existing remedies in areas of displacement to enhance their own protection. Activity 1.1.2: Establishment of mobile legal aid clinics for counseling and legal assistant services: Through Lawyers and paralegals HIWA will establish one legal aid clinic in each of the project locations for the provision of legal advice, counseling, and litigation for cases related to tenure insecurity: The paralegals will facilitate negotiations on tenure security since evictions are highly due to tenancy agreements are either not in place or are not properly negotiated. HIWA will assist and provide legal services to about 2500 IDPs and other affected persons through mobile legal clinics. Activity 1.1.3: HLP Prevention and Response Services through protection of IDPs against forced eviction with a focus on sites at high risk of evictions in target sites and beyond. In target locations, small-scale evictions occur on a daily basis and IDPs constitute the largest and most vulnerable of those affected. Prevention interventions will include: negotiating with landowners, gatekeepers or local authorities for the continuation of tenure by the IDPs, with the support of District Commissioners, negotiation of extended notice, consultation on alternatives, alternative accommodations and non-discrimination. Activity 1.1.4: Assessment, monitoring, documentation and reporting threats of eviction and forced evictions by landlords, authorities and settlement managers, the assessment will also cover land tenure use, the status of rental agreements, and forced evictions, assessment to be conducted by trained HLP monitors. This activity involves undertaking a rapid assessment of the current ownership and tenure rights of IDP communities in target locations, and a detailed study of property state, legal documentation, and tenure rights in the case and/or to motivate a return to areas of origin once the conflict has ceased.Activity 1.1.5: Training of community leaders, camp leaders, local authorities and protection monitors on Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) issues such as security of land tenure, forced evictions, displacement, and durable solutions. This will target 150 participants from each of the targeted locations. 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance - Mitigation measures include the following; 1- Design assessments to enhance participation - include representatives of diverse groups as key informants (e.g. women, older persons, persons with disabilities, single-headed households, minorities, and other marginalized groups) 2- Aim to have a proportionate balance of male and female enumerators to interview both male and female respondents and respect cultural practices. 3- When engaging with children, obtain parental consent; use age-appropriate questions; and ensure enumerators are trained in interviewing children 4- Use local knowledge to determine the cultural appropriateness of the questions; 5- Collect Sex, age and diversity disaggregated data whenever appropriate. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas - Mitigation measures include the following; 1. Establish a referral process for legal disputes involving IDPs residents and landowners. 2. Map HLP stakeholders in the selected regions. 3. Establish referral mechanisms and, if possible, legal assistance for disputes arising from the tenure agreement between landowner and shelter beneficiary, to ensure that disputes are taken to the proper venue. 4. IEC materials on HLP rights and distribute to communities; these can be translated into local Somali language 5. provision of alternative land (private/government) for relocation.
