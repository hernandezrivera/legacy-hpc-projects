Project Summary

The project will aim to improve the access and availability of improved water, sanitation, and hygiene service to IDP, and the most vulnerable host community in El-Fasher, al-Malha, Al-Tewisha, Kebkebiya, Kalemando, Mellit, Saraf Omera, Umkadada, Tawila localities in North Darfur; Abu Jabra, Elferdous, Bahr Al Arab localities in East Darfur, and Bau, Geisen, Roseries, and Damazine localities in Blue Nile States. To address the challenge of extreme water scarcity in these areas, the WASH intervention under this action will focus on expanding access and availability of WASH services through water yards rehabilitation, handpump rehabilitation, upgrading of handpumps to mini-water yards, integrated hygiene promotion; construction of household and institutional latrines, and essential WASH upgrades to health facilities; water quality and groundwater level monitoring; installation of handwashing facilities and the distribution of hygiene kits. The proposed WASH activities focus on communities in El-Fasher, al-Malha, Al-Tewisha, Kebkebiya, Kalemando, Mellit, Saraf Omera, Umkadada, Tawila localities in North Darfur; Abu Jabra, Elferdous, Bahr Al Arab localities in East Darfur, and, Bau, Geisen, Roseries, Damazine localities in Blue Nile.

1 - Needs

The 2018 simple spatial survey method (S3M-II6) showed an increase in access to water by six percent over the 2014 multiple index cluster survey (MICS) figure of 68 percent. Still, around twelve million people (or around one-third of the population) do not have access to safe drinking water and are at risk of disease. Proper water management through reliable groundwater monitoring, construction, and maintenance of safe water points and skilled technical expertise remain the priority need. The outbreak of waterborne disease, including an outbreak of (AWD) in 2016 and 2017, has shown the need for strong water and sanitation systems and safe hygiene practices. UNICEF's 2019 annual report also shows that sanitation coverage has stagnated, and nearly a third of children and their families are practicing open defecation, depriving the majority of the population of their basic rights. Around twelve million people do not have access to toilets and as a consequence have to relieve themselves in the bushes – often after dark – which is especially dangerous for girls and women as it increases their risks of becoming the victim of harassment, sexual violence, and abuse violence.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In the targeted areas, IDPs and host community members are combatting extreme water scarcity. The project’s contributions to safer water access revolve around rehabilitation and upgrading of water points as well as water quality testing and groundwater monitoring. . In total, RI will rehabilitate 3 water points in El-Fasher locality in North Darfur benefiting 15,000 people; 1water points in Al-Malha reaching 5,000 people;4 water points in Al Tawisha reaching 20,000 people, 4 water points in Kalemando reaching 20000people,6 water points at Kabkebia reaching 12,000people, 6 water point at Saraf Omra reaching 12000people, 6water points at Tawila reaching 30,000people, 2 water points in Umkadada benefiting 10, 000people and 3 in Baw, 4 in Geisan, 1in Damazine and 4in Rosaries localities reaching 21,000 people, and 2 in Abujabra and Bahr Al Arab localities reaching 10,000 people. Additionally, to improve the water availability in RI supported facilities, RI will carry out facility water tracking in El-Fasher, Mellit, Al-Malha, Al Tawisha localities in North Darfur; and Damazine and Roseries in Blue Nile; and Abujabra and Bahr Al Arab in East Darfur reaching a total of 104,832 people.To improve the access to sanitation services, RI will also intervene in the same localities with the construction of household and facility latrines. Accordingly, RI will undertake the construction of 1,210 household latrines reaching 21,780 people; and 2 facility latrines reaching 4, 400 beneficiaries in El-Fasher locality. RI will construct four facility latrines at Al Tawisha, Tawila, Saraf Omera, and Um Kadida benefiting 8,800 people. Similarly, in Damazine and Rosaries localities, RI will construct 1000 household latrines reaching 18,000 people; and 10 facility latrines reaching 22,000 people visiting the facility for health and nutrition services. Under the hygiene promotion intervention in target localities, RI will undertake the Installation/ construction of 434-foot press hand washing facilities in communal setups (communal toilets in camps, schools, markets, public places, and health facilities) in El-Fasher Almalha, Mellit, Al Tawisha, Tawila, Saraf Omra and Um Kadida localities in North Darfur reaching 6,365 people; 46 facilities in Damazine to reach estimated 736 people. As part of the hygiene promotion intervention, RI will conduct 362 campaigns in El-Fasher Almalha, Mellit, Al Tawisha, Tawila, Saraf Omra, and Um Kadida localities in ND reaching 70,446 people; and Damazine 90 reaching 23,700 people. Integrated with the hygiene promotion messaging, RI in El-Fasher will do WASH supplies to 5,120 households reaching 30,720 people; and in Damazin will distribute 1,500 supplies to 9,000 people.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI will mainstream minimum protection standards to ensure equitable and safe access to WASH programs and facilities. In addition to setting locations of community water points based on hydro-geologic conditions consideration, socio-political considerations will be factored in to ensure the location is open to the full community and that the WUC is representative. Safety and dignity are key considerations for sanitation activities, and a household will be involved in the construction of their IHHL to account for the unique needs of its members, and the hygiene kits will incorporate MHM items. The hygiene promoters, CHWs, and CNVs will receive hygiene promotion training that considers how to tailor messaging to diverse groups.RI will ensure that protection is mainstreamed throughout the project’s WASH activities. Priority will be given to the elderly, the disabled, female-headed households, and beneficiaries with cases of acute malnutrition. When selecting beneficiaries for latrine construction, beneficiary households meeting the vulnerability criteria will be prioritized. In the selection of sites for the rehabilitation of water yards and hand pumps, priority will be given to locations with high percentages of vulnerable households. In the training of WUCs, RI will ensure that there is representation from vulnerable community groups and that the WUCs develop processes to ensure that vulnerable community members can access water easily. Throughout the project planning and implementation phases, RI will continue to monitor the communities to ensure that vulnerable households are benefiting from WASH programming.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries, including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy.Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system putting a mechanism which enables coordination with the inter-agency to facilitate learning on best practices, complementarity in complaint responses such as referrals of GBV cases; and strengthening capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedbacks to inform project designing, improve service delivery, and, enhance program recipients participation in each state of the project management cycle, etc. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program designing and adjustment, budget allocations, and programming in the different sectors.
