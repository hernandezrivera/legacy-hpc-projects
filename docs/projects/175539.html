Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to address the wellbeing as well as improve access to basic services of 33,252 people through an integrated WASH response with Nutrition and Health aiming at reducing food insecurity and malnutrition prevalence as well as the disease morbidity and mortality whilst providing access to quality emergency WASH services in Jonglei (Pigi/Canal, Bor) and Unity (Rubkona and Guit).
In providing integrated response with nutrition showing high GAM prevalence, 24,000 people Canal/Pigi, Rubkona and Guit Counties will be targeted for water supply with semi-permanent or permanent solutions through reparation of existing water points, installation of new or additional water points in locations showing low coverage, protection of water points from contamination and establishment of water management committees for all the targeted water points to enhance self-reliance for the water points. Additionally, hygiene promotion in the community will be carried out with emphasis on disease prevention (WASH related diseases and COVID-19), safe excreta disposal management and handwashing with soap where the use of "tippy taps" will be promoted in the community.
To reduce prevalence of WASH -related diseases and risk of outbreak of transmission, 18,000 people in Rubkona, Guit and Pigi/Canal will be targeted. These targeted people will be provided with water supply through rehabilitation of existing water points, installation of additional water points in locations with low coverage, protection of existing water points from contamination and water management committees will be established for all the water points to enhance self-sustainability. Where sanitation is a public health concern, communal latrines in crowded settings will be constructed with GBV mitigation measures implemented and community maintenance of the structures as well as use of local materials will be emphasized and promoted. In addition, hygiene promotion will be carried out in the communities with emphasis on disease prevention (WASH-related diseases and COVID), safe excreta management, and handwashing with soap where the use of "tippy taps" will be promoted at household level. Furthermore, targeted distribution of "famine prevention" minimum package minimum for 3 months period based on vulnerability and availability of the items.
To address the gaps with access to basic WASH services, Cordaid will reach 33,252 beneficiaries in Bor South, Rubkona, Canal/Pigi and Guit Counties' with its enhanced emergency capacity across the four locations through both temporary (delivering rapid assistance with temporary services) and more durable solutions (Semi-permanent solutions if applicable). These will be done through emergency rehabilitation of water points, installation of additional water points, water quality monitoring, installation of EmSWAT system, distribution of HHWTS, construction of temporary or semi-permanent communal latrines with GBV mitigation measures implemented if justified by public health concerns, hygiene promotion at community level emphasizing on disease prevention (WASH-related diseases and COVID), safe excreta management, handwashing and finally distribution of minimum WASH NFI package for 3 months period.
Cordaid has been responding to the increasing humanitarian needs in all 4 areas and is fully operational in the 4 areas with offices in Malakal, Bor and Bentiu. It has an EmSWAT at its disposal, mechanical drilling rig at Bentiu, enhanced water quality monitoring and testing capacity for all water quality parameters, manual drilling rig at Malakal. in 2020, Cordaid drilled 4 boreholes in Koch and Guit and responded to flood displaced people in Bor South with WASH NFIs. Cordaid also conducted needs assessment in Guit, Rubkona, Canal/Pigi and PDM in Bor South to inform its 2021 programming and the report shared with state focal points.

1 - Needs

The 2021 South Sudan HNO estimates that 8.3 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance this year. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. In addition, 60% of the country’s population will be facing Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3+) levels of food insecurity from April–July 2021 (IPC results). From October–November 2020 Pigi/Canal, Bor South, Rubkona and Guit Counties were all in IPC4 and in WASH vulnerability classification 3/4/5. Cordaid conducted assessments in all 4 the locations in December, 2020 and the results are in line with the HNO, IPC results and the WASH severity classification mapping. According to Cordaid’s assessment, HH are demonstrating multiple and high levels of vulnerabilities increasing their protection risks: more than three-quarters of the HH is headed by a female, who is in charge of an average HH size of 10+ people. The majority of the HH includes an elderly person and most of the HH have a PLW and children under the age of 2. In Rubkona 5% of the HH also includes a disabled person and in Guit and Pigi this is much higher, 12% and 15% respectively. On average there are more females than males (52% female, 48% males) and more than 60% are children under 18yrs. In Rubkona and Guit most of the assessed population is host community and most of them (85%) had not received any support in the past months. In Pigi 54% are IDPs and most of the respondents to the PDM in Bor were IDPs. In Pigi 50% had not received assistance in the past few months. People in all the proposed areas are acutely food insecure: 70% to 81% is facing moderate hunger (Hunger Scale). This is affecting most people equally although HH are trying to provide more meals for the smaller children. 75% of HHs in Rubkona have a FCS ‘borderline’ and 23% have ‘poor’. In Guit this is 25% and 2% and in Pigi 43% have borderline and 15% have poor FCS. 42% in Rubkona, 90% in Guit and 77% in Pigi have a high rCSI and most people have incurred debts to meet their food needs. People are also livelihood insecure with more than half to 70% of the people in LCS ‘emergency’ using coping strategies such as selling one’s land, and therefore also affecting future productivity and more difficult to reverse or more dramatic in nature. Most people live off what they produce at home. The high level of food insecurity is affecting PLW, elderly/ chronically ill and children under 5 disproportionately, causing health risks especially among children under 5 and may increase the risks on GBV. High WASH needs exist in all areas but the situation is worse in Rubkona and Guit where 90% of respondents indicated not to have sufficient access to water for drinking, cooking and bathing. People in Pigi and Bor do have access to sufficient water but it is mainly from an unprotected source (river) and not treated. Most people need to travel at least 30% and waiting time varies with Guit having the longest waiting time 30 min – 2 hrs on average. As female adults are normally tasked with collecting water, the distance to and waiting time at the water point is a concern and increasing risks and burden on females. 90% in Rubkona, 77% in Guit and 85% in Pigi practice open defecation and in Bor this is in between 61 – 80% of the people. This is resulting in protection risks for women and girls as they have no safe place to defecate. There are very few handwashing facilities at HH level and hand washing is practiced the least in Rubkona. Limited water, high percentage of ODF is indicating low hygiene practices. There’s also a high need for WASH related NFIs, such as water storage containers, water handling and soap. The lack of it is affecting women and girls, and children under the age of 5 disproportionately. The lack of safe water sources/ use of treated water and ODF is a public health risk, affecting the most vulnerable including PLW, children under the age 5.

2 - Project activities

In line with the HRP and WASH cluster strategic and specific objectives, the project will reach people with WASH vulnerability 3/4/5 as well as addressing IPC3+ inadequacies in WASH services by; 1. Rapid assessment and response analysis including rapid inventory of water sources and high risk practices with aid of Wash'Em rapid tool. 2. Installation of new or additional water points. Cordaid has its own mechanical rig in Bentiu and this will enable new water points to be drilled to increase safe water access to nutrition facilities with insufficient water as well as locations with reported WASH related diseases. Four new boreholes will be drilled in locations with reported WASH related diseases and high prevalence of GAM lacking sufficient safe water. Covid-19 will be mainstreamed by ensuring that a clearly earmarked safe distance of 1-1.5 meters at water sources/water collection points is maintained, and through the Water Management Committees, communities will be sensitized/trained to stand on those earmarked places. 3. rehabilitation of existing water points and extension of water points, including protection of water points from contamination – at community level. Water points with sufficient yield will be prioritized and 15 existing water points will be rehabilitated in a more sustainable manner by overhauling all the underground components. 4. Installation of EmSWAT system. Emergency surface water treatment system will be installed when there is no existing water point alternatives. One EmSWAT will be on standby ready for installation where there is emergency need for safe water provision. A complete set of SWAT system is in Malakal store and the team is well trained for rapid installation and functioning with capacity to provide safe water to 1,500 people. All high-risk water sources and supplies will be treated and water at system level preferably, or otherwise at household level will be chlorinated. Basic parameters of water quality (bacteriological and physical, chemical only if warranted) and residual chlorine will be controlled. 5. Establishment of self-sufficient water committees. Nineteen Water management committees comprising seven members with three quarters of the members female will be established with the female encourage to take key decision-making position. This is due to the fact that women are more associated with water collection and related issues. The committee members will also be trained to pass and disseminate key hygiene messages at the water points and sources. 6. WASH NFI kit (Famine prevention minimum package) distribution. This will be targeted distribution with standard kits of "famine prevention" minimum package with demonstration on usage of the items done. Distributions will take Covid-19 SOPs and protection and GBV mainstreaming in the process. 7. Hygiene promotion. This will be done and awareness will emphasize on disease prevention (WASH-related disease and COVID), safe excreta management, handwashing with soap with promotion of use of "tippy taps" at household level. 54,000 people will be reached and enabled to practice good hygiene practices. 8. Construction of communal latrines. Where lack sanitation facilities will pose a public health risk, communal latrines in crowded settings will put up. The local practices and designs will be considered, and the facilities will be constructed away from water sources but close enough to dwellings. The basic issues of privacy and security, especially for women and girls and people with special needs will be considered. Latrines will be sex-separated and doors will be lockable from the inside. The proper use and maintenance of the facilities will be promoted. The need for sanitation NFI or locally available construction materials will be assessed and where possible, the facilities will be built mostly out of locally available materials. 6,000 people will be reached with 10 blocks of 5 stances emergency latrines. 9. Post distribution monitoring.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

At proposal stage a project risk matrix will be completed identifying operational, political, programmatic, reputational, financial and organizational risks. At the start and during the implementation the risk matrix will be reviewed and risks closely monitored. The below identified risks are part of Cordaid programmatic risks:8.1 Risk of beneficiary exposure to violence during registration/ distribution: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will ensure that all registrations and distribution sites are safe, nearby people’s dwellings and easy accessible for all. People will be hired for crowd control and when desirable, separate male and female distribution sites will be organised. Sites will be selection jointly with the community. In addition, Cordaid is using the PMWG checklist for safe distribution to identify potential risks.8.2 Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will inform communities about policies and procedures in place such as code of conduct and PSEA. All Cordaid staff are trained in these procedures and policies. This will explain expected behaviour of Cordaid staff and how to report misconduct. To prevent sexual violence by community members distribution sites will be safe and nearby people’s dwellings. Distribution times will also be convenient to make sure that people are home during day time. Community leaders and communities will be asked to protect their women, girls and boys as well. 8.3 Risk of insufficient internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid as part of the Caritas network has child safeguarding policies in place and staff are trained on these procedures. In addition, in the event children are on their own, Cordaid will make sure they are connected with local leaders and make sure communities look after them.8.4 Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral service: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will map other and protection actors in the area and establish a referral with these organisations. This information will also be shared with communities and community leaders, raising awareness on available services and referrals.8.5 Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: As part of the assessments, Cordaid organized FGDs about and with people with specific needs but also different age groups, to ensure that their needs were raised and incorporated in the assistance. Cordaid will continue to do this during the project preparations, implementation and also PDM.8.6 Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. Mitigation measures: Through the project starting at the first community visits and consultations until the PDM, Cordaid will share its complaints and feedback mechanism and how to access it with communities. Information on the CRM and its importance will be shared verbally and in writing and is based on communities’ preferred ways of communication (informed by assessments).8.7 Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. 8.8 Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. 8.9 Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. 8.10 Risk of explosive hazard: Likelihood is low - Impact is high
