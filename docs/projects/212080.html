Project Summary

After 13 years, the proacted conflict continues to generate humanitarian needs, impacting the physical and mental health of the affected population. Escalating armed conflicts, deteriorating socio-economic conditions, poor infrastructure/WASH conditions, power shortage, food insecurity, malnutrition, and water crises have all also affected and worsened the health sector situation and further contributed to disease outbreaks. More recently, the earthquakes that hit South Turkey and NWS in February 2023 have further affected parts of Aleppo & Idlib governorates and, to a lesser extent, Lattakia, Tartous, and Hama governorates. According to HNO 2024, the number of people who need humanitarian assistance increased from 15.3M to 16.7M between 2023 and 2024, and 14.9 million need health support across the country.
A joint MSNA prepared by HARVEST For Development and Consultation for the Dutch Relief Alliance in September 2023, showed that in light of the economic deterioration, living conditions, inflation, and increase in fuel prices, caused a rise in medicine prices, people face several barriers to getting available services, mainly lack of medicine and medical items (30%), cost of the services (24%) and lack of privacy (10%). As per the 2024 HNO, almost 40% of primary and secondary care health facilities - serving over 4.8 million people in need of life-saving health services in the WoS- are either partially functioning or not functional.
Moreover, Health data in Jebel Saman and Menbij districts shows a high prevalence of WASH-related diseases such as Leishmaniasis, diarrheal diseases, head lice, Hepatitis A, and typhoid (specifically in Maskana). Aleppo governorate accounts for 50% of Leishmaniasis cases and 13% of the diarrhea cases in Syria since January 2022. Also in 2022, Aleppo suffered from a severe Cholera outbreak which affected many people and locations, causing the Ministry of Health in Syria to announce an emergency and ask UN agencies to support it. The root causes include but are not limited to, lack of access to quality water, insufficient water quantities, poor Solid Waste Management (SWM), unfunctional sewage systems, lack of basic services, in addition to lack of access to hygiene items because of the soaring prices of materials, such as soap and sanitary pads.. The health sector in Syria in general and specifically in Aleppo is suffering from a lack of medical personnel due to the continuous immigration of health staff and a severe lack of fuel to operate health facilities, medical supplies, and medicines.
Hama governorate is located in the middle of Syria with an estimated total population of 1,485,590. Hama has been affected by the earthquake and the economic situation in Syria. According to the HNO 2023, 81% of the population’s ability to meet their basic needs is insufficient. The number of humanitarian actors providing humanitarian services is relatively low in Hama, while the SJR joint needs assessments show an increase in the needs of people caused by the lack of basic services. The needs assessment has highlighted the following.: In Hama, 80% of the assessed households is in the ‘high’ category of the reduced livelihoods coping strategy index, indicating that a large majority of the households is often resorting to high-risk coping strategies to meet their basic needs without being able to save any money.99% of households surveyed in Hama stated that they do not currently have a stable source of income, but instead rely on temporary jobs / seasonal activities mostly agriculture. For health, there is a lack of medical facilities, particularly in rural areas, medical staff, and supplies. Combined with increases in transportation costs, this is negatively affecting the ability of people, including elderly people and children, to access medical services. For Idleb, the estimated total population is 2,927,392, 78% of the population is unable to meet needs based on the HNO2023.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In summary, Syria is confronted with: - lack of qualified health human resources, - lack of medicines and equipment,- lack of economic inputs for Health Facilities (HF), staff, - lack of health protocols and guidelines Dorcas will partially address the mentioned needs by increasing the availability of medicines and medical items and reaching areas where health services are almost nonexistent. This could be achieved through various interventions, such as providing medical supplies and medicines to health facilities and partnering with local organizations to provide primary healthcare services free of charge in hard-to-reach areas. Based on its experience in the health sector since 2016, Dorcas will implement several activities aiming to support vulnerable populations and health systems in Aleppo, Hama, and Idleb. This will include supporting 7 PHCCs, 1 hospital, 1 referral center, and 1 center for special needs in Aleppo, 1 PHC in Idleb, and operating 3 medical mobile units in Aleppo/rural Aleppo, rural Hama, and rural Idleb as medical mobile units are essential to overcome access barriers such as transportation costs, geographical distances and to serve the marginalized population in rural areas. In rural Idleb, rural Hama, and rural Aleppo, communities live in rural areas where health services are nearly absent, transportation is not available, nor water, connection, and electricity, to be able to reach the nearest health facilities, people have to wait days before identifying potential transportation means and have to pay a large amount of money that they cannot afford.The support will include light rehabilitation, medication, medical equipment and supplies, and assistive devices, with a focus on increasing the availability of medicines and medical items and providing healthcare services by a qualified health team for the affected population in hard-to-reach areas (rural areas) where health services are almost inexistent. Moreover, Dorcas will facilitate health education sessions at Aleppo's Hanano and Hellok community centers, increasing communities' health knowledge and promoting well-being. These sessions will also serve as referral points to identify medical cases, guiding attendees to nearby Primary Health Care Centers (PHCs) for examination, follow-ups, and treatment as needed.
