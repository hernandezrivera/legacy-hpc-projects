Project Summary

The nutrition situation in target district remain poor over a period of time. The situation in this districts, with parts inaccessible due to militia control, is poor. Despite slight improvement many still face acute food insecurity, rampant disease, poor WASH factors and poor care practices for pregnant and lactating women as well as children under 5 years still drive high rates of acute malnutrition in these districts. This underscores the need for continued humanitarian assistance and protection through 2021. More than half the population is expected to experience deterioration of their food security state from Stressed (IPC Phase 2) to Crisis IPC Phase 3, mainly due poor access to food due to control of highways by insurgents and the predicted La Nina in February 2021. WASH status and care practices remain poor due to poor access to water which also impedes on the sanitation situation. These two factors then act as fuel for rampant disease outbreak coupled with poor primary prevention of disease, further compounding the nutrition situation.
According to the nutritional assessment from FSNAU 2020 the nutrition situation in Elwak among children under 5 is critical while that of PLW in emergency state.
The district also continues to have one of the worst infant and young child feeding and micro-nutrient indicators in the country (HARD 2019; IPC 2019). Disease outbreak including AWD/Cholera and measles are commonly reported (Health cluster ,2020). In addition, the number of protracted IDPs in the two districts continue to increase due to insecurity caused by presence of insurgents in part of the districts and in neighboring districts. IDPs represent a huge growing number of the people in acute livelihood and food crisis in these districts. The most vulnerable in these districts, including children under 5(boys and girls) ,pregnant and lactating women and the disabled need urgent intervention.
The proposed interventions will align with cluster 2021 HRP objectives, HRP strategic objectives and Government and strategies to providing lifesaving interventions and protect lives in the target districts, especially among the most vulnerable.. Interventions will focus on providing adequate nutritional support to bring up and avoid further deterioration of already existing situation. To achieve this and realize sustainability HARD will provide both treatment and prevention interventions. HARD will work closely with government entities, both at local and federal level including MOH at all levels. The organisation will also intensify community action in order to include affected population in interventions affecting them. Interventions will also integrate with those of health WASH, Food security and protection. All nutrition interventions will integrate with health interventions and provided through health facilities. Serious protection issues observed in these districts will be addressed working with the protection cluster and any protection risks likely to emanate from projects will be mitigated. All this will be done through 1 fixed sites and 1 mobile team.
Priority will be given to the disabled in all treatment sites. Staff will be trained to work with the disabled. Physically disabled who qualify will be employed. Community members will be encouraged to bring out disabled children and PLW to access services. Those will be followed up by CHW.
HARD is developing a PSEA policy which will guide staff. All staff will be trained on PSEA.
Access to services will be improved by use of mobile teams that will go to rural areas and hard to reach areas.
HARD will also work in coordination with other cluster partners.

Mitigation Measures

HARD has gender policy that ensure that when hiring staff that women are given 50%consideration in different positions. In addition, the project target populations aresegregated into men, women, boys and girls hence giving emphasis to all gendergroups in the community. During the screening and admission of children into nutritionprograms, boys and girls will be given equal chances. Gender considerations will betaken into account throughout the assessment, planning and design of theinterventions and significant consideration will be given to pregnant women, mothers,and women of child-bearing age. In the provision of health and nutrition promotioncampaigns, messaging will be targeted for specific gender and age groups and asappropriate the events will be organized separately. Equitable and gender-sensitiveparticipation and decision-making approaches will be adopted throughout the project,including women’s participation in the community nutrition committees and access togender-specific health and nutrition information. Community consultations throughfocus-group discussions will take place during the project implementation, andstakeholders will be involved in decision-making processes to tailor the project to theneeds of different gender and age groups. Furthermore, data will be dis-aggregated byage and gender in all recording and reporting.
