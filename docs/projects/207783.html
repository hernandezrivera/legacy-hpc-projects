Project Summary

Sector wide, agencies are supporting the scale up of the Myanmar Curriculum program, and IRC will be expanding its focus on supporting learners to continue the MC, in addition to targeting teachers with additional capacity building and Sector-members with knowledge sharing opportunities. Equity and technical gaps persist in access for learners, with strong disparities in learning seen between boys and girls in IRC's 2022 Pop Up Research. In 2024, IRC will focus on narrowing this equity gap, working with children (4860), caregivers (14490), teachers/facilitators (206) and sector members to expand access as well as evidence-based programming in the Rohingya education response. The project is aligned with the Education Sector's 3 Objectives: Access, Quality and Community Engagement. The project will reach its goal through continuing and establishing learning centers for the expansion of the MC under the ECW MYRP consortia, through reaching caregivers to strengthen Early Childhood Development (ECD) activities through LEGO funded ECD programming, through expanding the teacher supply in the host community and through continued work leading the ERICC Education Research in EiE project. In addition to that, IRC will also focus on the older adolescents specially girls below 18 years old who are not eligible for livelihood activities and are not being able to mainstream in MC due to age and competency issues, will be supported with Alternative learning opportunities. Thus, the overall project will include, center based ECD intervention in host (20 CBLF, 600 learners) and camp (42 CBLFs, 1280 learners), MC in camps (12 LCs, 28 CBLFs, 1540 learners) and ALP for adolescents in camps (48 CBLFs, 1440 learners). In addition to that, teachers’ capacity building and parents/caregiver sensitization activities will be included for the parents of all targeted learners in both camp and host community.
The roll out of MC also created high need of Burmese language skills among the Rohingya teachers, thus IRC will work on Language skill development through technology supported Language lab, training, and use of supplementary materials. The project will follow ISCG and education sector’s standard and guidelines during implementation and will share all required data/information agreed with sector as sector’s partner as well as ensure compliance with government law/policy and will work closely CiC, site management and education focal at camp level. The project will identify the safe learning facilities considering the risk prone mapping developed by ISCG. Orientation of relevant stakeholders on DRR and emergency preparedness plan by Emergency Preparedness Team of IRC and safety materials will be provided at facilities. To focus on the community members, there will be capacity building and sensitization activity/messaging’s which will focus on gender issues like girls’ education and drop out, early marriage prevention messages, parenting, social cohesion between two communities. Even while designing projects, IRC will focus on the exact need of the two communities and will divide the activities accordingly to reduce tension between the two communities. IRC in 2024 is also planning to support teachers from the host community particularly on the areas of emergency responsive teaching learning for the host community, teacher and student wellbeing and sensitization.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Since August 2017, an estimated 745,000 Rohingya refugees fled to Cox’s Bazar, increasing the total number of Rohingya refugees to more than 860,000 (ISCG situation report, June 2020). Schools and learning centers were closed for more than 18 months due to COVID_19, finally reopening in late 2021 after some of the longest closures globally. These closures have had a still unknown impact on children's learning and retention in schools. Disruptions to education have left up to 70% of 10-year-old unable to read or understand a simple text, up from 53% before the pandemic. IRC's 2022 report, "The State of Learning for Rohingya Refugee Children Affected by Displacement and Crisis in Bangladesh" notes the following barriers hampering quality education provision: 1) a lack of qualified teachers, with specific challenges in identifying qualified female Rohingya educators; 2) complex language issues, with divergent language of instruction and home (oral) language; 3) curriculum restrictions and changes, with the shift from the LCFA to the MC; and 4) COVID-19, with extended disruptions to education and a lack of learning continuity. IRC's assessments showed that 78% of children are non-readers, with only 22% showing emergent English literacy skills; over 32% of children have not developed any math skills, 67% are gaining foundational math skills, and only 20% of children being able to conduct simple subtraction problems correctly. Overall, the achievement gender gap was stark, with boys outperforming girls in all learning measures. For example, in literacy, the differences were statistically significant across all EGRA subtasks, with boys scoring, for example, 20 more correct letter sounds per minute than girls. In addition, children with disabilities are behind in literacy tasks, as well as in assessed social emotional learning dimensions. The roll out of Myanmar curriculum entered its second year in 2023 with all the grades from KG – Grade 10. It is also identified that due to the roll out of Myanmar curriculum many adolescents particularly girls are being out of the mainstream education a part of them have been supported by Accelerated Learning Program or Adolescents program. The MSNA 2023 draft report shows that only 7% are getting support from Accelerated learning Program while 4% are from Adolescent education program. The roll out of MC also created need for addition learning facilities which increase the level of space challenge in camps. The enrollment of adolescent girls also continues to be a challenge as at the secondary level only 4009 girls are currently enrolled in the academic year 2023-2024 which is only 24.2 % of the existing enrolled learners in secondary level. In addition to that teaching MC needs a good command on Burmese language which is also a big challenge currently at the camp level where efforts needed to make. The MSNA report also included that not being able to afford the educational cost, attending Madrasha education, not seeing education as priority and involvement in financial activities are the major challenges for the children for not being able to continue regular education. Particularly for the adolescents, boys (23%) and girls (21%) do not see education as priority while boys (20%) and girls (19%) are busy with the household level support. It also includes that early education is not a focus for both communities where 78% 4 years old children in host community and 46% of 3-4 years old children in camp do not attend early education.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IRC will seek to align with Principle #3; adopt an integrated approach, using IRC’s other sectoral intervention where possible following the camps where education and other sectors of IRC have programming. This mainly targets IRC’s Women Protection and Empowerment program which also targets adolescents’ girls and have WGSS where possible integration of educational activities is on process. This integration will help to reach more girls despite the space scarcity in the camps. IRC’s ECD program has done integration of health and nutrition into its programming following the nurturing care framework and had plan to provide ECD supports in the PHCCs operated by IRC. In addition to that, IRC’s current activity will focus on remaining in the existing camps where IRC has been working for over three years to avoid duplication of effort with other organizations. Even for the new activities, it will try to be in the same camps where other activities are ongoing based on the gaps and needs identified. Close coordination with the education sector, CiCs and camp focal will be maintained to avoid overlapping.IRC will seek to align with Principle #4; through ensuring stability in refugee-focused programming and improving relationships between key stakeholders and partners in the camps. IRC will contribute further through its leadership of the new ERICC Education in Emergencies Research Project, in which buy-in from key government and sector stakeholders will contribute to the development of a shared research agenda, reflecting the priorities and perceived gaps with key stakeholder groups involved in the provision of education services in the refugee and hosting community. A specific focus of this research is on educational continuity and the alignment of strategic objectives and goals of stakeholder groups, with which IRC's analysis on gaps and promising practice will seek to contribute to positive change.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IRC is a global leader in Protection and Child Protection programming, and currently implements Protection Rule of Law, Women's Protection and Child Protection programming in Bangladesh. IRC's Education department draws on this expertise to ensure rigorous approaches to child safeguarding, codes of conduct, and improved safety and referrals for children in education programs. IRC will continue to strengthen approaches to ensuring safety and reducing gender-based violence in education programs. This will be accomplished through ongoing research efforts, which include gap analysis on safety and protection in education programs, with a potential focus to fund research into protection-based risks and barriers impacting school aged children. IRC will work with parents and caregivers to raise awareness on codes of conduct, violence at home, and reporting mechanisms for violence in schools and on the way to school which impacts children's learning and development.Particularly for the reporting mechanism for protection issues, IRC has multiple way which includes protection focal person, hot line numbers and email address from the Ethics and Compliance Unit (ECU) where any kind of concerns and cases can be reported, and all the reports were addressed with high confidentiality.

3.3 - Age & Disability

IRC will cover all the age range from 3-18 through its programming in 2024 with ECD, MC and ALP programs. Only in the host community, IRC will focus on age 4-6 to prepare the children for formal schooling. IRC always use the WG questionnaire to assess the disability level of children (if any) and ensure that the children are getting adequate support to continue education including assistive devices as necessary. In addition to that, IRC has a very strong focus on parents and community members to raise awareness and enhance sensitization towards the access and quality education for the children specially for adolescents’ girls. IRC seeks to provide equitable services based on need to all client populations. IRC's staff is 99% Bangladeshi, and facilitators are recruited from the refugee and hosting community. IRC's ensures that all program targeting is needs based, and this begins with understanding the challenges and barriers facing learners and communities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to client populations is the cornerstone of all IRC's programming. IRC seeks to ensure all programs are based on participatory needs assessments, so that project design reflects the inputs of communities, including children. Ongoing monitoring seeks to improve engagement with children, whose voices are often overlooked in improving accountability to clients. Active mechanisms for engaging children, based on IRC's new Child Participation Guidelines (2022), will be employed so that child-friendly mechanisms are used to obtain diverse perspectives on design and implementation, and that safety risks facing children are understood and handled in an active and not passive manner. Not only children, but also parents also/caregivers and community members will be engaged in multiple ways throughout the project design and implementation through community consultations, testing of contents developed and agreeing on a delivery mechanism which best suits the needs of the community. In addition to that capacity building for the education committees, sensitization and parenting session with the parents/community members will be ongoing.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IRC has its policy called IRC Way which clearly guides staffs regarding the protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. It is mandatory for any IRC staffs to follow IRC Way and any violation of the policy directly results to official actions. Regular orientation and refreshers were provided to mainstream the policy to prevent any kind of PSEA issues. All the implementing partners working with IRC also receive training and orientation on this.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Under ECW-funded programming, IRC will conduct training for teachers and community members on DRM. The goal of these DRM trainings will be to understand risks, local capacities, and mitigation measures which can be utilized to reduce risks facing communities. Considering context-specific natural hazards and previous disaster impacts, DRM has been incorporated to reduce the risk of negative effects and adverse impacts on life and the environment; the project has established an information management system to ensure early warning dissemination, multi-hazard preparedness messages to increase awareness of target population, enhance knowledge and skills of frontline staffs and volunteers to implement disaster risk reduction interventions effectively and identify the risk of natural hazard through community risk assessment periodically.
