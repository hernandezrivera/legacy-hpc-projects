Project Summary

The multiple crises impacting Lebanon and its institutions have left thousands of children without the basic right to education and are faced with major protection risks threatening their immediate/long-term wellbeing and opportunities. As an emergency response, the proposed project will promote equitable access to inclusive educational opportunities for socio-economically vulnerable Lebanese children impacted by the crisis in Beirut, Mount Lebanon and Baalbeck/Hermel Governorates.
Recognizing the link between child protection and educational outcomes, the project will fully integrate CP in Education to support children and families severely affected by the crisis cope with the stressors of the situation through protective, safe, and inclusive environments. Building upon TDH IT’s long-lasting experience in preventing and responding to protection threats faced in various communities, this education project will make use of the technical expertise, guidelines and tools developed by TDH IT in collaboration with UNICEF and the CP WG. CP and Inclusion specialists will be part of the implementation of this project, providing training, guidance and tools to be used by Education staff to ensure that children with disabilities are not excluded from education/CP services provided, and are provided equal opportunities to benefit from the project. This approach focuses on enhancing the skills, knowledge and tools available for staff to better assist each child and family, while enhancing families and community awareness of children’s rights and how the crisis is affecting them.
Community Support [SO1] For Lebanese communities, in response to the limited data available on the educational status and barriers faced by Lebanese children as a result of the crisis, needs assessment will be conducted considering both in-school and out-of-school Lebanese children in specific locations where TDH IT has years of experience implementing Formal/NFE/CP programs, as well as strong connections with the communities. Community-based retention activities will be implemented, complemented by PSS support, awareness raising sessions and in-kind support (food and educational materials needed for learning). TDH IT’s CP-Education integrated approach will allow the identification of households’ educational gaps and the child protection risks families are exposed to.
Engagement of key stakeholders and duty-bearers at community and governance levels will take place to increase their accountability in upholding children’s rights and foster inclusion during this emergency response.
The project will be implemented in line with Sphere Protection Standards and CHS, and National Sectors technical guidelines and SOPs.
This intervention will complement pre-existing response funded by other institutional donors.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Community Support [SO1]Remote learning support (retention activities) will be provided, focusing on an individualized family engagement and support approach to address the unmet educational needs of most vulnerable children identified and their caregivers. Caregivers will be provided with tools to develop their resilience through socio-emotional learning approaches and practical material assistance such as food and items needed for distance learning. Accordingly, caregivers will be enabled to play an active role in their child’s learning path, while local stakeholders will be engaged to ensure access to essential services for families and to promote social stability. Children with disabilities will be provided with inclusive educational opportunities, assistive devices and access to specialized therapies. Whenever possible, families will be supported through multiple activities and will be followed-up on individually by TDH IT M&E and program teams to ensure their needs are met and their engagement in educational activities is maintained.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The intervention will respond to protection risks faced by vulnerable Lebanese individuals, incl. children at risk of exclusion from education, CwDs, and undocumented individuals. All child protection cases identified during outreaching and education activities will be safely identified and referred. For high risk activities (e.g. awareness raising on SGBV or cash assistance), strict guidelines will be followed in line with CP Sector and SGBV Task Team guidelines. Awareness will be raised on CP and SGBV through customized activities on the risks and consequences of SGBV using age-appropriate language and material to reach children and caregivers. All staff, partners and service providers involved will receive trainings on TDH IT Code of Conduct, PSEAH policy, Child Safeguarding policy and Gender. TDH IT will ensure collection and analysis of gender/age/disability/nationality disaggregated data.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

TDH IT has two regional hotlines for CM reporting and Complaints boxes in its centres which are managed according to the TDH IT Whistleblowing Policy. TDH IT uses WhatsApp to capture feedback from beneficiaries on activities, with adaptations made on an ongoing basis. HH visits are also conducted, especially for CP cases or CwDs. Feedback is recorded during distributions with PDMs completed with recipients of distributed items or cash to understand feedback on distribution quality, sufficiency, utilisation and effectiveness. TDH IT will monitor safety, protection and operational challenges at distribution points for cash transfers and selection criteria are clearly communicated (e.g. why specific households have been selected for cash assistance programs). Referrals ensure that HHs receive the services they need, especially for CM and essential services. TDH IT also updates service mappings and coordinates to ensure integration of services and create synergies between sectors.
