Project Summary

Through the proposed responses, International Medical Corps will continue providing integrated lifesaving nutrition responses to conflict affected resident and displaced populations in six locations: Juba PoC, AL Sabah hospital, Malakal town, Malakal PoC, Aburoch and Nyal Panyijar County. The proposed response will focus on life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs through management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), at OTP/TSFP nutrition facilities. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced particularly Mother support groups and community nutrition volunteers will be supported and used as entry point to reach caregivers/mothers. International Medical Corps will continue capacity building of the nutrition team, MoH staff, to equip and respond to nutrition emergency on timely management of SAM and MAM cases. IMC emergency nutrition response will expand on nutrition specific interventions, as well as reinforce integration and linkages with other clusters to address the key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional child care and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. The response attentions on areas where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and internal displacement coexist.
Through the proposed humanitarian response plan projects, International Medical Corps will reach 22,994 children under five and 10,188 PLW beneficiaries with acute malnutrition in Juba PoC, AL Sabah hospital SC, Malakal town, Malakal PoC, Aburoch and Nyal Panyijar County. The project will provide lifesaving nutrition services focusing on the most vulnerable, especially PLW and children under five. IMC will reinforce and promote access to integrated nutrition, health and WASH, FSL responses and will will ensure that most of the nutrition sites are functional at the health facility, and others through outreach activities, as well will work closely with other partners providing WASH, FSL and Health to ensure that mothers/caregivers receive additional services at facility level.
International Medical Corps with already established Nutrition surveillance team, will undertake nutrition assessments that provide robust information on the nutrition situation of a given population, this information will be key in responding effectively to nutrition needs in the project area. FY 2020 survey plan, IMC is one among the potential survey partners requested to conduct SMART surveys in both IMC operational and non- operational areas. 3 SMART Surveys will be conducted within IMC’s supported project intervention sites while 5 SMART surveys will be conducted in priority counties with high information gap, as identified and assigned by the Nutrition Cluster and NIWG.
IMC will engage the affected population to participate fully in the project design and implementation to ensure communities have clarity and have their say on the objectives of the nutrition response, the activities, the location, and their entitlements, through formation of Quality, Accountability of Affected population committees in each site in Juba PoC; AL Sabah hospital SC, Malakal town, Malakal PoC, Aburoch and Nyal Panyijar County. Community consultative sessions will be organized on a quarterly basis by field nutrition managers, site managers and field M&E officers, to receive feedback and draw an action plan on areas to improve program implementation.
IMC has already started the process of 2020 Field level agreement with WFP for TSFP supplies in managing moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases and had signed PCA with UNICEF until 2021 to support severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases covering in Juba PoC, AL Sabah Hospital, Malakal town, Malakal PoC and Nyal Panyijar County.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, the cumulative effect of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods has led to a humanitarian emergency of high proportions. The recently revitalized peace process promises to offer new opportunities in the coming years for South Sudan’s women, men and children. However, Maternal and child malnutrition is a significant public health, social and economic problem faced by communities in South Sudan. According to the FSNMS round 24 results indicates prevalence of GAM of 16.2%, the country has crossed the emergency threshold of 15%, with the highest prevalence observed in Unity and Upper Nile were IMC operates. In July 2019, IMC conducted a SMART survey in Malakal PoC with results indicating GAM rates of 16.9 % (13.2↔21.3 95% C.I) the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition was 3.0 % (1.6↔5.5 95% C.I) and MAM prevalence was 13.9 % (10.9↔17.6) Therefore, the current nutritional status of the county is classified as very high according to the new UNICEF/WHO thresholds. In the same survey of July 2019 data on IYCF shows that exclusive breastfeeding and early initiation of breastfeeding are 91.2% and 92.5%, respectively. Continued breastfeeding at one year for age 12-15 months is 92.7% while continued breastfeeding at 2 years (20-23 months) is 81.4%. Thus, the breastfeeding indicators are commendable in Malakal PoC based on WHO recommendation. However, introduction of solid or semi solid foods is still low (53.6%). hence, the need to continue and scale up both curative and preventative nutrition services is pertinent striving to minimize and alleviate the suffering of under five children in these counties In December 2018, IMC conducted a SMART survey in Greater Panyijar County. Results indicates GAM rates of 15.6% (11.6↔20.7 95% CI) while SAM prevalence was 2.1% (1.0↔4.2 95% CI) based on Weight-for-Height. Compared to December 2018 data, November 2017 GAM prevalence was 13.2% [10.1  17.2, 95% C.I.] and the prevalence of SAM was 2.5% [1.4  4.4, 95% C.I.], which is indicative of Serious nutritional situation based on the WHO standards. The worsening nutrition status and food security situation demonstrates that this is an area of concern requiring continuation of focused humanitarian assistance. In February 2019, the SMART conducted by Concern Worldwide at Juba PoC reveal, Global acute malnutrition (GAM) WHZ as 16.5% (13.7-19.8 95% CI) and SAM WHZ was 3.5% (2.4- 5.1 95% CI) indicating critical level above the emergency threshold. This shows a deteriorating situation of nutritional status compared to the SMART survey conducted in similar season in December 2017. The high rates of malnutrition in the proposed counties are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above), sub-optimal child care and feeding practices, lack of safe water and sanitation, internal displacement and conflict. Poor quality and diversity of food and high prevalence of diseases (up to 40%). Furthermore, poor access to health and nutrition services during the rainy season due to poor road infrastructure constrain service delivery to these areas already afflicted by high malnutrition levels. Through the proposed response plan, IMC’s key nutrition humanitarian priorities in 2020 continue to be the provision of appropriate interventions through implementation of prioritized and integrated multi-sectoral responses (including emergency food rations, support to IYCF-E, micronutrient supplementation, and CMAM interventions) which will be critical and life-saving. The ability to meet these needs will depend on humanitarian assistance. In line with nutrition cluster strategy, IMC nutrition program will mainly focus on treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk under-five children, pregnant and lactating women.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IMC will conduct needs assessment to identify gaps and best possible ways to fill those identified gaps. IMC will consult entire community and local authorities in all stages of the program through beneficiary feedback assessment. IMC will double its efforts towards people-centred approach to mainstream Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), Gender and Age considerations into programming and improve programme responsiveness to evolving and varied needs of the affected population. Through this, IMC will also establish Quality Accountability of the affected population ( QAAP) committees,to ensure complaint mechanism that is confidential, accessible and responsive. IMC will facilitate QAAP committees to undertake quarterly meeting to review complaints from the community, actions taken and way forward. QAAP committees is deemed best approach for illiterate people who can not read and write.IMC will build the capacity of the local local staff and institutions including Ministry of Health staff at national and sub national levels, project staff and community nutrition volunteers. IMC will train all staff, Community Nutrition Volunteers and Mother support groups on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse ( PSEA). IMC will ensure to have a joint monitoring and evaluation system, that reflects how well the project tracked and responded to beneficiaries' complaints
