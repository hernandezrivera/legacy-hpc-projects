Project Summary

Since the beginning of conflict in December 2013, around 1.96 million people in South Sudan continue to remain internally displaced with over 203,000 IDPs in PoCs and around 2.47 million refugees in neighbouring countries with the majority, 54% being women and girls (OCHA, September 2018). There are critical health challenges in South Sudan due to already limited resources of state and county health services, high disease burdens and conflict. NBeG remains relatively stable but suffers the indirect impacts of conflict including displacement from other areas, high food prices and limited flows of food commodities. NBeG remains one of the locations with the highest rates of morbidity of 157/1,000 in children under-5 in South Sudan compared to a national level of 99.2/1,000 . This is due to the limited capacity of the SMoH and CHD to provide basic health services to the population along with poor health, nutrition and hygiene practices. Concern will continue to collaborate with HealthNet who are currently supporting the provision of BPHS at 25 PHCUs and 2 PHCCs in Aweil West and 24 PHCUs and 1 PHCC in Aweil North where Concern has nutrition sites to identify and fill gaps in providing lifesaving primary maternal and child health services, particularly in hard-to-reach areas through mobile health teams and Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM). Concern’s nutrition services will be part of BPHS. Concern will provide support to strengthen health systems and clinical support; prevent communicable diseases; improve maternal and child health and improve health practices.
To improve health systems and clinical support, Concern will train health staff to deliver quality health and nutrition services to provide BPHS. Concern will also provide training on iCCM training of trainers for mobile teams as well as CHD staff. As illness is one of the main causes of malnutrition, health staff have already received training on CMAM. Refresher trainings will be organised based findings of joint-support supervision visits by Concern and the CHDs/SMoH as well as new developments from the MoH and Health and Nutrition Clusters. To improve access to BPHS in remote locations, Concern will continue to support 2 mobile clinic teams in remote locations to provide curative and preventative health services at 8 outreach sites. A functional referral system will be continued for severely sick children to the appropriate SMoH designated referral facility. Concern’s WASH programme will construct latrines and handwashing stations at outreach sites based on an assessment conducted at these sites.
Prevention of communicable diseases will be through EPI and iCCM. With only 20.7% of children in Aweil West and Aweil North vaccinated , communities are prone to vaccine preventable communicable disease outbreaks. Concern will support the CHDs to provide vaccinations at 49 health facilities and 8 outreach sites through logistical support and community mobilisation. WHO and UNICEF provide vaccines and other EPI supplies to the SMoH. Immunisation activities will also be integrated with OTP and TSFP services. Due to high morbidity rates in NBeG it is necessary to ensure correct diagnosis and prompt treatment and enhance health seeking behaviour of the communities. Concern will continue the iCCM approach to support 774 CBDs to provide basic health services focused on treatment of children 2-59 months with common causes of illness such as malaria, diarrhoea and ARTIs to allow for early diagnosis, particularly in remote locations. Due to their sensitivity children under 2 months are referred to health facilities. CBDs will also screen for acute malnutrition and make referrals to OTP and TSFP sites.
Concern will continue supporting maternal health covering ANC, facility delivery and PNC as part of BPHS. Through mobile health teams, Concern will support TBAs to promote awareness of attendance at ANC, delivery at health facilities by skilled personnel and attendance at PNC. Community based workers and volunteers including IYCF Facilitators, CNVs, CBDs, TBAs and MtMSGs will be supported to provide education on areas including good health practices, conducting home visits and referrals to increase use of ANC, deliveries at health facilities and PNC.
Engagement with communities will be essential to improve healthcare seeking behaviours and health, nutrition, hygiene and sanitation practices. Community health activities aim to reduce the factors that contribute to high child morbidity and malnutrition caused by malaria, diarrhoea and ARTIs. Informed by Concern’s KAP survey in 2017 and SMART survey to be conducted in November 2018, community health activities will be implemented by 2 approaches, mass campaigns and targeted community engagement with groups such as MtMSGs, ANC groups and men’s groups.
The main targets of the programme will be children under 5 and women for primary health care and reproductive health care services. As women are the primary caregivers and are particularly vulnerable to illness, they will be the main targets for community health activities. However given men’s roles as decision makers in South Sudan, they will also be targeted to analyse how their roles can impact on their spouses’ and children’s health.

Needs

Since the beginning of conflict in December 2013, around 1.96 million people in South Sudan continue to remain internally displaced with over 203,000 IDPs in PoCs and around 2.47 million refugees in neighbouring countries (OCHA, September 2018). While NBeG remains relatively stable, it has suffered the indirect impacts of conflict including reduced flows of medical supplies, limited resources from the government to provide health services and displacement from other locations. South Sudan faces critical challenges due to limited resources by SMoH, high disease burdens and conflict. The population has limited access to health services due to the direct and indirect effects of conflict and the under-developed health infrastructure. Governance systems at national and state levels are weakened by the ongoing conflict and political instability and at county levels are even weaker. South Sudan has one of the highest maternal and child mortality rates in the world at 789 per 100,000 live births and 93.8 per 1,000 live births respectively (MoH, 2017). Children suffer frequently from preventable common childhood illnesses such as malaria, diarrhoea and pneumonia. Aweil West and Aweil North have limited health infrastructure, with only 44% of people living with in 5km radius of a functional health facility, making it a major challenge to deliver health services (MoH, 2015). Only 49% of pregnant women in Aweil West and 57% in Aweil North have attended four ANC visits (8 ANC visits are recommended by WHO guidelines). Only 20.7% of children in Aweil West and Aweil North are vaccinated and this low immunisation coverage makes the counties in NBeG prone to communicable disease outbreaks. From February to March 2018, there was a measles outbreak in Aweil Centre. High rates of illness are caused by poor hygiene practices such as open defecation, indiscriminate solid waste dumping coupled with lack of clean water and bath shelters. 75% of the population in Aweil West and Aweil North practice open defecation, only 34% of households in Aweil West and 15% in Aweil North have access to an adequate toilet facility within 50m and only 40.3% of households in Aweil West and 15% in Aweil North have a place for handwashing (Concern KAP survey, 2017). Infectious diseases and poor health result in a continuing cycle of illness and malnutrition.

Activities

Specific Objective 1: Improved provision of life-saving primary and reproductive health servicesActivity 1.1: Capacity building of health staff: Concern will provide training to health facility staff on provision of BHPS based on findings of joint-supportive supervision visits by Concern, the SMoH and CHD.Activity 1.2: Outreach mobile services: Concern will support 2 mobile teams to provide weekly outreach services at 8 mobile clinic locations to increase primary health care services to remote communities. Focus will maternal and child primary health care services as well as preventative services. Those with severe sickness will be referred to appropriate SMoH facilities. Activity 1.3: WASH integration with health services: Based on Concern’s assessment of mobile sites, some sites still lack adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities. Concern will rehabilitate latrines and install handwashing stations and provide clean water containers to prevent the spread of waterborne diseases.Activity 1.4: EPI: Concern will collaborate with the CHD to scale up EPI at 49 nutrition sites and 8 mobile sites through logistical support and community mobilisation. Concern will coordinate with the CHD and SMoH to ensure vaccines are delivered in a timely manner to health facilities. WHO and UNICEF will provide vaccines and EPI supplies to the SMoH and Concern will support volunteers to conduct vaccinations and community sensitisations. Concern will integrate immunisation activities with nutrition services. Concern will also support National Immunisation Days with vitamin A and deworming campaigns and MUAC screenings incorporated. Activity 1.5: iCCM: With high child morbidity rates in NBeG, it is necessary to ensure early diagnosis and treatment of the common illnesses such as malaria, diarrhoea and ARTIs as well as malnutrition particularly in remote communities. 774 CBDs will be supported to provide basic treatment of malaria, diarrhoea and ARTIs for children 2-59 months (children under 2 months will be referred to health facilities). CBDs will also screen and make referrals for acute malnutrition. CBDs will be selected from communities based on criteria such as having good health knowledge and able to train others. CBDs will also be supported to provide education on early recognition and treatment of diseases, exclusive breastfeeding, hygiene, childcare practices and immunisation.Activity 1.6: Reproductive health: Due to poor maternal health and limited capacity of CHDs to provide such services, Concern will support provision of reproductive health services. This will be through mobile teams supporting TBAs to make referrals for ANC, delivery at health facilities by skilled personnel and PNC. TBAs, CBDs, CNVs and MtMSGs will be supported to provide awareness on good reproductive health practices to increase use of ANC, delivery of health facilities by skilled personnel and PNC; family planning, iCCM and HIV/AIDs and integration with Concern’s Nutrition Programme where MtMSGs will target women in ANC and PNC with MIYCN sessions. Objective 2: Improved health and nutrition behaviours and care practices at household and community levelActivity 2.1: Community behaviour change: The poor health situation in NBeG requires community health interventions specifically targeting PLW and children who are the most impacted by preventable diseases. Informed by KAP surveys, education will be provided through targeted engagements with community groups and volunteers such as MtMSGs, CNVs and CBDs. Men will also need to be targeted considering their role as decision makers to support them to analyse how their roles impact on their spouses’ and children’s health. The activity will be integrated with Nutrition and WASH to promote hygiene and nutrition practices. Activity 2.2: Integrated community awareness campaigns: Concern will provide support to communities provide education on public health, maternal and child nutrition to reduce preventable diseases.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

For treatment for malaria, diarrhoea and ARTIs by CBDs and at health facilities as well as referrals for malnutrition, Concern will support CBDs to sensitise communities to diagnosis criteria based on symptoms using visual information due to high illiteracy levels. CBDs will conduct follow-up visits to ensure caregivers administer medications correctly to ensure recovery of children. Information will be provided to communities on details of community health activities. Beneficiaries will also be informed of the Concern’s Complaints Response Mechanism process and the monitoring process as an opportunity for them to provide feedback on the programme.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Given high levels of illiteracy, information will be visual, such as using IEC cards with pictures to illustrate admission criteria to PHCUs or PHCCS and treatment by CBDs. Emphasis will be where skills and knowledge are available, will be on recruitment of local staff, know the local languages and social and cultural norm and thus are better able to communicate with beneficiaries. Given the different roles of women and men in child rearing and decision making, activities, particularly community health activities will be specific for female and male members of the communities. Women and men will be targeted separately for groups so they can be freer to discuss the issues and identify solutions regarding health of their spouses and children. Other groups from Concern’s Nutrition Programme such as MtMSGs provide a safe space for women to discuss openly primary health and reproductive health topics. Men’s groups allow men to analyse their role in the household and how impacts on their spouses’ and children’s health.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern needs to consider protection given that although NBeG is relatively stable, it remains fragile and there is a high risk of GBV in the area. Research on GBV in South Sudan conducted in 2017 found that 65% of women and girls had experienced physical or sexual violence in their lifetime. Protection of beneficiaries will be essential during the programme. Women especially are at risk of GBV especially when travelling to health facilities and mobile sites. Concern has a Programme Participant Protection Policy, known as the P4, and Code of Conduct along with a Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-Trafficking in Persons Policy which all staff and those involved in programmes must sign. Included in these policies in mandatory reporting of beneficiary sexual exploitation and abuse. Concern’s health staff will work closely with protection partners for a referral pathway in the event of identification of protection cases such as through the CRM. Beneficiaries will be sensitised on measures that they can take to reduce protection risks such as travelling in mixed groups to health facilities and mobile sites and only during daylight hours.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

As well as collaborating with Health partners, SMoH, CHDs and community leaders, Concern consults with community members to inform the design of health interventions such as CBDs, and caregivers attending PHCUs, PHCCs and mobile sites. Concern conducted two evaluations of recently completed programmes in the proposed locations that involved with obtaining feedback from beneficiaries of health interventions that will allow continuing what works well and make improvements in this proposed programme. Although one of the evaluations was specifically for an emergency nutrition intervention, impacts of nutrition programmes on communities to be considered as the high rates of morbidity are one of the main causes of malnutrition and the involvement of groups such as MtMSGs in promoting health practices.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

In Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern will have a CRM where beneficiaries can submit feedback and/or complaints through approaching a Concern staff, in writing or through telephone. Complaints are assessed by an CRM committee that is independent of the programme and decisions on accepting or rejecting the complaint are made by the committee and recommendations to the Programme Managers.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Monitoring activities allow communities to provide feedback. Caregivers attending health facilities and mobile sites will be asked to provide feedback on the quality of the service. Beneficiaries such as CBDs, TBAs as well as those from Concern’s Nutrition and WASH Programmes including MtMSG members, lead mothers and Male Change Agents will provide feedback on quality of health trainings. Surveys such as KAP surveys to understand communities’ knowledge and practice of key health practices as well as early healthcare seeking behaviours. Evaluations will be community focused to assess the impact of health interventions and provide an opportunity for beneficiaries to provide feedback on the programme.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

South Sudan ranks 187/189 countries in the 2017 HDI and the country loses 36.3% when the value is discounted for inequality (HDI reduced from 0.388 to 0.247 . This statistic reflects the strong patriarchal society and culture; and traditional gender roles subject women to play secondary roles in the community. This place them at a disadvantage with household decision-making and this disempowerment exposes women and girls to several risks including early/forced marriage and sexual and domestic violence. While the laws in South Sudan provide for the protection of women and girls against discrimination, abuse and GBV, they are often misinterpreted or ignored. In 2017, the Protection Cluster reported 3,585 GBV cases (95% female, 19% children); an increase of 35% compared to 2016 . While NBeG has escaped much of the direct impacts of conflict there continues to be a high risk of GBV. This can impact on adopting health practices such as early healthcare-seeking behaviour. In remote locations in Aweil West and Aweil North, there can be considerable protection risks for caregivers if the they are located far from the health facilities and mobile sites.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In South Sudan, women as per social and cultural norms have limited decision-making roles where decisions are made by men. This can include on whether a woman may bring her child to the health facility or mobile site. In Aweil West and Aweil North, there are high levels of male migration to Sudan or other locations in South Sudan to seek employment. This can increase workload burdens on women and reduce time for early healthcare seeking and attendance at health education sessions. Women with disability are excluded from decision-making processes, 48% of women with disabilities reported never having access to decision-making process through community participation and engagement mechanisms. In rainy season, access to health facilities becomes restricted due to poor road conditions which can impact on healthcare seeking behaviour.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With considerable inequalities faced by women, and considering women as primary caregivers of children are most impacted by nutrition crises in South Sudan, gender equality will be mainstreamed during the programme. Health facilities have standard operating times limited to daylight hours, reducing the need for health staff and beneficiaries to travel at night and reducing protection risks. Prior to setting up mobile sites, the locations will be assessed and agreed with communities to ensure locations are safe and secure and will not put populations accessing sites at any additional risk. Mobile sites will be located in remote areas reducing time for beneficiaries to travel to health facilities. Provision of health services at community level through iCCM will reduce the need for women, children, elderly and disabled to travel long distances. Involvement of both women and men in community health activities will enable them to analyse their roles and impact on health of their households. Mobile clinics and iCCM in remote communities will mean more accessibility during rainy season and early treatment of common illnesses. Concern will make referrals identified protection cases to Protection partners. Concern will support IYCF Facilitators to report protection incidents and make referrals to appropriate services such as the Red Cross. Community volunteers involved in the programme such as CBDs, TBAs and MtMSGs will be trained on the importance of early healthcare seeking behaviour for those impacted by GBV and protection cases. A referral mechanism will be in place where for survivors of GBV can access services such as psychological care. Mobile teams will provide treatment and make referrals for survivors of sexual and GBV violence and make referrals in the event of cases presenting. Protection cases will also be identified through the CRM.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern will engage with state and local authorities to inform them about Concern, its programme and operations in line with the humanitarian principles to ensure acceptance. Women of childbearing age will be encouraged to join MtMSGs and other groups and supported through individual and group counselling sessions. Trainings will be scheduled so they do not interfere with childcare responsibilities. As men have dominant roles in decision-making, they will be targeted for community health interventions, both community leaders but also partners of the women involved in the project. Men will be supported to share health knowledge with other men to understand how their role can impact on their spouses’ and children’s health.All Concern’s staff working in the mobile sites as well as those involved in the programme (such as partners, suppliers and consultants) will be inducted and required to sign the Code of Conduct, Programme Participant Protection Policy, Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti Trafficking in Persons Policies. Included is mandatory reporting of abuse and exploitation. For remote locations, mobile clinic sites and iCCM will reduce travel times for caregivers. Concern will conduct security assessments prior to setting up mobile sites to ensure locations are safe and secure. Mobile sites and PCHUs and PHCCs will only operate during daylight hours and caregivers will be sensitised on measures to reduce risks such as moving in mixed groups and only in daylight hours. During mobile clinic days, cases will be triaged where PSNs such as elderly and disabled receive attention first. Protection cases identified at mobile clinics or the CRM will be referred to Protection partners.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Concern collects gender-disaggregated data during monitoring activities on the impacts of the programme on girls, boys, women and men. This allows for ensuring that the programme equally impacts girls, boys, women and men. Concern conducts annual KAP surveys which measures key health behaviours as well as nutrition and WASH practices in the programme locations. Included in the survey is collecting qualitative information from caregivers on barriers to accessing health services. Concern conducts monthly monitoring of mobile clinic and iCCM caseloads and submits to the SMoH and Health Cluster. This allows for Concern to monitor data such as malaria, diarrhoea and ARTI trends and further investigations if these rates are high and take actions such as increasing follow-up visits from CBDs.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Concern collects confidential information on protection cases such as through the CRM and shares this information with the Protection Cluster. Concern collaborates with the Protection Cluster to have up-to-date information on protection incidents in the programme areas and to take measures to reduce protection risks to beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Concern’s monitoring tools will collect information on issues such as protection and satisfaction levels of the programme. Beneficiaries will be sensitised on the use of the CRM as a way of providing feedback on the programme and how it affects dignity of beneficiaries.
