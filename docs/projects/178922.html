Project Summary

The overall objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to quality and inclusive formal and non-formal education in a safer and more secure environment for recently displaced children (girls, boys, IDPs, and HC aged between 6 and 17 years old – 2% children with disability). the Project intends to enhance access to formal and non-formal education services and promoting child safeguarding in learning facilities by supporting 4 schools in Al-Atareb by providing minor rehabilitation and operation cost over the project duration. During the recent crisis, salaries for teachers have generally not stable and decreased. Salaries will be secured for teachers, to ensuring that they are sufficiently compensated to ensure the livelihoods of their own families. the project will support 4 formal schools, as operational costs and Incentives for teachers. Minor rehabilitation for 4 Schools to expand enhance education infrastructure to ensure safe and conducive learning environments through inclusive and integrated light repairs. Recreational area and sanitary facilities for the schools to be suitable for girls and boys in the school. 2 blocks of 2 gender-sensitive and disability-friendly toilets will be constructed in each school.
the proposed project M&E activities will include the collection of project data including students and teachers lists, project detailed assessment to identify the specified needs. also it will include monitoring and documentation of project activities including on-site monitoring and post activities monitoring, and lastly, project final evaluation. during the M&E activities implementation, the following tools will be used; baseline and end-line surveys to measure the level of knowledge and needs at the start and end of the project, interviews with the beneficiaries, focus group discussion sessions, and satisfaction surveys for a random sample of beneficiaries. the M&E team will prepare a detailed logical framework at the beginning of the project including output and outcome indicators and will use a monthly tracker to measure the activities progress toward objectives. monthly monitoring and evaluation report will be prepared by the M&E team to inform findings and recommendations, and a project final M&E report will be produced by the end of the project including the lessons learned and best practices.
by the time of preparing this project, MISK was not able to secure any funds for the proposed activities, all requested amount should be considered as a gap.

4 - Needs

According to the United Nations International Children's Emergency Fund (UNICEF), more than 2 million children were deprived of education in Syria within the 10 years of conflict, 2.4 million children are out of school, 40% of them are girls. UNICEF has also declared that the situation is deteriorating, in its statement dated 24/01/2021, they mentioned that the COVID-19 pandemic has taken its toll on the education process and sector, increasing the number of out-of-school children and exacerbating the education situation in Syria.In relatively safer locations, some students were able to continue their education, nevertheless, they require support to fill the educational gap that happened through the years, as many are showing academic weakness and are not able to keep up with their peers. 2.1 million children in Syria are out of school, and a further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. Displaced communities remain significantly underserved, with most IDP camps having insufficient or non-existent education services. More than one in three schools are damaged or destroyed, while others are used for purposes not related to education such as shelter for displaced persons. the proposed project will respond to the needs of 3975 students and their parents ( 2346 girls, 1629 boys) out of which 80 CwDs (47 girls, 33 boys) in addition to 120 teachers ( 76 female, 44 male). the needs identified in the targeted schools included rehabilitation of school buildings, lack of school books and education materials, stationary, teachers, and hygiene items. also, schools are not equipped to receive CwDs.

5 - Implementation of activities

the proposed project will provide Minor rehabilitation 4 Schools in Atareb sub-district to expand enhanced education infrastructure to ensure safe and conducive learning environments through inclusive and integrated light repairs. Recreational area and sanitary facilities for the school to be suitable for girls and boys in the school. 2 blocks of 2 gender-sensitive and disability-friendly toilets will be constructed in each school.the proposed project will provide the following activities: -provide Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for children and Children with Disability (CWD) aged between (6-17) (grades 1-9).-provide (Catch-up Education) for students from the age of 6 to 17 years.- provide language courses (English, Turkish) for students from (6-17)years.- Run recreational, psychosocial, and resilience activities in centres and schools.- support schools and centres with hygiene supplies to support the prevention of COVID-19 infection.- provide Incentives for 120 teachers.- provide Minor rehabilitation 4 Schools - provide transportation services for girls so that parents can feel safe to send their female children to education.the project target approximately 3975 students and 120 teachers ( 2346 girls, 1629 boys) out of which 80 CwDs (47 girls, 33 boys) in addition to 120 teachers ( 76 female, 44 male).The overall objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to quality and inclusive formal and non-formal education in a safer and more secure environment for recently displaced children (girls, boys, IDPs and HC aged between 6 and 17 years old – 2% children with disability) which is in line with the sector objectives, and HRP strategic objective.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Children (Boys/Girls/IDPs/HC, including Children with Disability) aged 8 to 16 are not interested in the program because they are affected by child labour, early marriage, and other exploitation forms.Mitigation 1: The outreach team will conduct awareness sessions to communities about the value of education.Risk 2: Girls aged 11 to 16 cannot attend due to the social norms of the communities.Mitigation 2:Providing transportation for girls to be able to come to schools.Risk 3: sexual exploitation/harassment toward students. Mitigation 3: Ensure that staff has read and signed their Code of Conduct. Risk 4: Learning facilities could be attacked. include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunfire, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school. Mitigating Measures 4: To reduce the risk of a military related attack learning facilities can be deconflicted using the deconfliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA.Risk 5: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. Mitigating Measures5 : Principals/headmasters, teachers and caregivers should be made aware of positive discipline methods. The application of learning by teachers, school personnel should be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support. Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct.Sufficient safe and clean bathroom facilities. provision of regular psychosocial support services including self-care and coping support.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Loss of cash or assets (operational): with low likelihood and medium impact - Mitigation measures: daily inventory, control the treasury better, involve many people in this process to extend the circle of suspicion.2- Unforeseen changes in the conflict: with medium likelihood and high impact. Mitigation measures: “Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues. 3- "Kidnapping / Abduction - with low likelihood and high impact. Mitigation measures: travelling in the daytime - transportation in convoys - high coordination on movement tracking - deterrence through local authorities. 4- Direct targeting of educational centres is an external risk caused by the Syrian regime and pro-forces, with low likelihood and high impact. The mitigation measures are raising awareness and guidance among children and parents to deal with emergency situations, develop evacuation plans and train those concerned, and providing all schools with fire extinguishers and first aid kits. 5- Risk: Lack of qualified teachers (females/males) for non-formal education. Mitigation: Training teachers (females/males) on non-formal education modality and curricula 6-Risk: Closing the border gates between Syria and Turkey hindered the education supplies to be delivered in a timely manner. Mitigation: Procure the supplies and print textbooks and brochures in Syria.7- The risks of spreading the Covid 19 virus.Prevention measures: providing sterilization in schools (sterilization + masks) and providing instructions brochures inside the schools, and awareness sessions.
