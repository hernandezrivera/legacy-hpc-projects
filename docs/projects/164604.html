Project Summary

The project aims to provide quality primary health care services through preexisting eight fixed and six mobile centers, for people affected by crisis in North Syria. The fixed centers will be in following sub-districts: Dana, Armanaz, and Kafartakharim in Idleb governorate, Azaz, Souran, Al Ra'ee and Raju in Aleppo governorate, in addition to Tal Abiad in North Raqqa governorate. As will the project supported mobile centers which will be providing services mainly for internal displaced people in the villages and camps of Atma, Maarat Al-Ikhwan, Kelly, Afrin, Azaz and Albab. The project will target people in need for health care with more focus on most vulnerable groups including pregnant and lactating women, neonates and young children, older people, people with disabilities, people with chronic diseases, and people with mental health problems. All services will be afforded according to the agreed essential service package and SEMA will continue update the protocols and improve the quality of services based on cluster recommendations and guidelines. All centers supported by the project are provided with skilled staff, and each facility is linked with communities through community health workers and psycho-social workers outreach teams to improve coverage and accessibility. During 2020, the project will contribute to improve accessibility of beneficiaries to primary health care services and raise the awareness of communities through delivering health messages.
Each fixed center will be able to reach an average of 3000 beneficiaries per month (totally 288,000 people), and 1500 beneficiaries for mobile clinic per month (totally, 108,000 people). Each mobile centre is linked to fixed health facility.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In traditional communities, social mores expect women to maintain modesty and chastity, and anything perceived to threaten these values is regarded as a threat. This has resulted in a gender gap that has been exacerbated by the conflict. Women and girls now face different layers of vulnerabilities ranging from the heightened risk of physical, sexual, and gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, early marriage, restricted mobility that limits access to services, and other forms of negative expressions of masculinity resulting from displacement and insecurity. SEMA, in general, responds to such issues by upholding individual rights in accordance with the humanitarian principals. In that regard SEMA implements all of its activities, taking into consideration doing no harm to the target communities, and providing all services and activities to beneficiaries regardless of their gender, religion, sect, ethnic group, political or regional affiliation. In addition, SEMA works to improve gender equality in term of recruitment, considers protection mainstreaming in all of its projects, provides PSEA training for all staff, provides self-care sessions for staff in order to help them feel more comfortable when dealing with beneficiaries, and introduces staff on how to refer CP and GBV cases to active protection programmes whether implemented by SEMA or other active organisations. Specifically to this project, SEMA initiated a GBV case management team in each of the three supported facilities to receive referral cases from the healthcare providers where detecting such cases is rather easy. SEMA will hire localized and gender-balanced staff, the staff will be trained on GBV core concepts and referral mechanism to refer any GBV survivor upon disclosure.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

 Risk 1: - Temporary cutting of cross border accessMitigation: - SEMA will ensure the availability of supplies and buffer stocks in field warehouses, and will be able to supervise the service provision through field offices and teams. Risk 2:- Displacement waves from or to areas of implementation and other war related risks (, air bombardment, shelling, bombed vehicles, theft, kidnapping, fire, etc):Mitigation:SEMA through a network of facility based Liaison and humanitarian access focal points, will monitor the situation on the ground and send immediate feedback regarding security alerts and beneficiaries movement, and programs teams will work through mobile team approach to reach the new commers and displaced people in site. Safety equipment for facilities and warehouse, guards, personal safety training and evacuation plan orientation will be in place especially for facilities staff working in higher grade if risks. Risk 3:- Outbreaks of communicable diseases, due to poor shelter conditions, increasing needs for medications:Mitigation:SEMA will ensure the commitment of health facilities to report to EWARN for any suspected reportable communicable disease, will deliver preventive health messages through community health workers, and will coordinate with other clusters for more comprehensive multisectoral interventions.
