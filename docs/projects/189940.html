Project Summary

Building on it’s protection by presence approach, integrated Protection-MHPSS strategy, a solid coverage of several worst affected districts and strong linkages to CCCM, shelter, and health, IOM will work towards improving provision of and access to services for IDPs at site and community levels, improved protection data collection and analysis.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project’s “protection by presence” approach is strongly community-based and integrates strengthening and expanding existing actions and supporting government-led protection and MHPSS services. The project aims to support the most vulnerable with integrated MHPSS-Protection activities. IOM’s direct interventions under this project cover all layers of the IASC MHPSS pyramid, with some referrals to specialized services. The project focuses on strengthening the protection environment by cooperating with the partners and government in addition to the prevention and response activities such as awareness-raising, counselling and community-based MHPSS activities, and referrals to the protection services (e.g., victim assistance, police, social services, civil documentation etc.). Based on the needs and in coordination with the partners, IOM will target IDPs living in sites (relocation, temporary, host community extension, government) and within host communities particularly support the most vulnerable, such as elderly, women and child-headed households, victims of violence and GBV, human trafficking and people with disabilities. Host families and communities whose coping capacities and resilience are affected by the presence of IDPs will also be targeted. Recipients of direct assistance, including victims of trafficking in persons, will be selected jointly with the social services based on established vulnerability criteria and their individual assistance plan developed with their and the beneficiaries’ active participation. In addition to that, IOM will increase the protection and mental health capacities of other IOM programmes such as health, CCCM, and shelter.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Deployment of integrated Protection/MHPSS teams with strong community-based support, which raises awareness and facilitates access to protection/MHPSS information, services and referrals for specialized care and assistance by IOM, government and NGOs. The teams’ coverage will be expanded to districts with high needs. 2. Provision of in-kind assistance to the most vulnerable individuals, including persons with disabilities, survivors of GBV and victims of trafficking. 3. Provision of focused psychosocial support services (e.g., individual and family counselling and support groups) and identification and referral to the specialized mental health services 4. Mainstreaming protection into CCCM, Shelter, and Health and strengthening protection/MHPSS services at site level through safety audits; capacity building, including on protection in CCCM, PSEA and psychological first aid (PFA); support to protection desks, deployment of site-based community activists able to provide information, PFA and referrals, support to government service provision 5. Data collection: support to multi-sectoral protection assessments and safety audits, strengthening DTM based protection data collection and analysis 6. Support to government protection/MHPSS institutions with infrastructural, material, equipment, technical or logistical assistance to re-establish operations and access hard to reach communities 7. Building the capacity of government and non-governmental protection actors on human trafficking in emergencies; protection in camp management, community-based MHPSS and PSEA Above mentioned activities will be implemented in the following provinces and districts:Cabo Delgado: Ancuabe, Balama, Chiure, Pemba, Ibo, Metuge, MontepuezNampula: Meconta, Memba, Nampula City Niassa: Lichinga, Marrupa

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM employs an integrated protection-MHPSS approach which ensures intersectional considerations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with health programming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM identifies individual and structural-level protection risks through outreach to IDPs through its activists and by conducting regular protection/safety audits in the districts. In the provision of referrals and assistance.IOM’s community-based activists are trained to identify vulnerable groups, including children and adolescents and the elderly, refer them to available services, advocate and assist them in gaining equal access to available humanitarian aid and protection services. IOM follows strict data protection rules and assistance standards in order to avoid causing further harm and empower individuals and communities assisted. IOM’s Protection-MHPSS Programme ensures awareness raising and capacity building on PSEA at the field level. All of the community workers and team members are trained on PSEA by IOM PSEA focal points and/or members of the PSEA Network. In line with that, all IOM programmes are targeted with PSEA training and refresher sessions conducted by IOM PSEA Focal Points. In addition, IOM Protection-MHPSS Programme conducts regular PSEA awareness-raising sessions not only for the communities but also for government counterparts at the district and provincial levels. IOM will continue increasing its and partners’ capacity in PSEA while ensuring communities’ access to the reporting and complaint mechanisms. IOM has a global mechanism called "We are all in." which allows all IOM workers to report SEA allegations.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

This project aims to increase access of IDPs to the protection and MHPSS services. IOM has a multi-disciplinary core team and community field workers working at the site/district levels. The teams identify protection risks and needs and address them either through direct service provision or referral to the more specialized services. Male and female community workers conduct door-to-door outreach to reach individuals at the risk of marginalization due to limited access to the services (e.g., elderly, persons with physical disabilities, persons with chronic diseases) and the individuals who live in distant locations. Moreover, all community workers were selected based on their language capacities in cooperation with the social services to ensure that IDPs can express their needs, concerns, and requests in their native language. In addition, IOM cooperates with and supports the government entities to reduce the barriers in access. For example, IOM conducts joint mobile brigades with government health and social services to target individuals with limited or no access to the facilities. In order to implement these activities, IOM works very closely with the community leaders and members in the sites and host community settings. The activities at site level are designed in cooperation with the communities to ensure that their needs are addressed in culturally appropriate manner.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams and community-based protection activists identify and refer people with special needs, including individuals with physical and mental disabilities, to adequate services. IOM’s trained MHPSS professionals are able to provide counselling and mitigate some mental health impacts of displacement. IOM pays special attention to ensuring that persons with disabilities, incl. albinism, are protected from human trafficking, abuse and exploitation under the integrated health-protection-MHPSS activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project does not include any construction work or other activities with significant environmental impact. In IOM’s community-based MHPSS activities strive to use environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for the environment is included in some awareness-raising sessions with communities.
