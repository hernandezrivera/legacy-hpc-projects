Project Summary

with the objective of "Vulnerable boys and girls, including children with disability, have improved access to safe, inclusive and dignified education opportunities", the proposed project corresponds with the 3 indicated cluster education objectives. In response to the educational needs for 38 targeted schools that are located in Area C in the West Bank- Jordan Valley, Salfit, Nablus, Qalqilieh, Tubas, Jenin and south hebron-, and upon an assessment done by World Vision and in coordinate with MoE and verified by the cluster. The project will target 5224 male students, 3718 female students, 620 male and female school education staff and school counsellors. To build the resilience of the vulnerable students and teachers within the targeted areas, and as those schools are highly affected by the protracted conflict, the recent escalation in the political situation as well as their geopolitical location, the project is designed to mitigate, prepare for and provide immediate response to emergencies directly affecting the 38 schools.
within the proposed response, the project will target the 38 schools in 4 set of activities reflecting their urgent needs and in alignment with the cluster’s 3 objectives. To contribute to the protection of students and education staff, a training on implementing PSS extra-curricular activities using psychodrama and art therapy will be implemented for the assigned counsellors and social studies teachers in each of the targeted schools the extra-curricular activities will be integrated within school plans to ensure that all students are exposed to such activities at least once on daily bases.
To increase the preparedness of the schools, 2 levels of activities will be implemented as follows; a preparedness assessment focusing on infrastructure, tools and capacity of teachers and students to respond to disasters, where results will be translated into a development/updating the school’s contingency plans. plans will be then developed/updated by the school-based committees. Following the recommendations and results of the contingency plans, an intervention plan will be developed for each school which will include infrastructure work, the provision of DRR materials and supplies, informational materials as well as others. plans will reflect the gender and CWD needs and all activities will be designed and implemented in accordance to their needs.

1 - Needs

According to a recent World Vision assessment conducted September 2022 and MSNA results published on July 2022 and other data, Areas B and C in the West Bank is considered most fragile and vulnerable area due to its geopolitical location. Following MSNA results, the rates of school attendance for girls is higher than boys especially at the secondary school level, where 92.7% of school girls aged 16-17 years old attending schools comparing to 70.6% of school aged boys. 19% of those children dropped out of school during the scholastic year of 2021-2022 with 19.8% with 19.8% for boys and 13.5% for girls dropping out. Digging deep into that indicator, MSNA report states that 11.3% of households with school-aged children not attending schools. The result of this indicator is contributed to many variables that were also highlighted by the report, where 32.8% of the children lack interest in education, 9.6% of children are working to support their households, 7.1% of children didn’t attend because schools were closed due to closures/ stopped functioning. The variables also highlighted the needs of CWD, where 7% of the children with disability stopped going to school as schools are inaccessible for children with disability, while 6.1% of the schools cannot accommodate children with disability. According to a UNICEF Humanitarian situation report published on August 2022; more than 2.1 million people, including 934,000 children are increasingly vulnerable due to the protracted protection crisis in the State of Palestine. Between January and June 2022, 15 children were killed, and at least 517 children were reported injured in conflict-related violence, with nearly all violence occurring in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. At least 360 children were arrested in East Jerusalem, 491 people, including 232 children, were displaced due to the demolition of 383 Palestinian-owned structures in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Access to basic services or livelihoods of another 15,512 people, including 6,901 children, mostly in Area C, was affected.13 A further 1,144 Palestinians, including 569 children, are at imminent risk of forcible transfer in Masafer Yatta and Area C. The results of World Vision assessment had reiterated the humanitarian needs as follows:- IHL Violations: The realization of the right of children to education and protection remains severely compromised in Area C; the main violations are attacks on schools, movement restrictions, insufficient and/or inadequate safe school infrastructure. 8226 children, in more than one third of Area C communities lack access to primary schools Exposure to intimidation: FGDs with parents confirmed harassment and intimidation, especially on boys. The ISF conduct regular patrols and sometimes attacks on schools; occasionally boys face the risk of being arrested while at school. As a result, children are traumatized, and the intimidation has negative effects on their ability to learn and attend school. - Unsafe learning environment: Learning environment especially in Areas B and C is unsafe, as students from Area C walk to their schools located in Area B. Approximately one third of schools in Area C do not meet the minimum standards for safe learning environment, both in terms of structure and facilities. This is in line with the oPt Education Cluster strategy which points out that schools in Area C are below the minimum standards for safe learning environments. The COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated the prevalence of CP concerns and GBV (including domestic violence, sexual abuse and forced marriage). - Limited/lack of Psychosocial Well-being: The MoE developed a school-based PSS programme aimed to help students cope with the daily stress they face accessing schools and the effects of the pandemic, as well as to reorient students to re-enter the education system; the system entails reaching out to communities for better social cohesion and inclusion. However, major capacity gaps in it resulted in extra burden on teachers - who are often untrained to deliver PSS-, and in an increased risk of long-term mental health disorders among affected children. Teachers and counsellors reported during FGDs that PSS and counselling services were either absent or inadequate to meet the needs in most school of Area C. Due to the disequilibrium between demand and supply of counselling services, families in West Bank opine that the service in schools is increasingly becoming discriminatory especially for girls.Referral System: Referral system could be more effective if its operational framework was survivor- centered; continued training and deployment of more school-based counsellors and case managers on survivor-centered approaches would enhance the effectiveness of the system. Despite the existence of an elaborate referral pathway, cases of GBV and other violations involving family members are rarely reported, for which community awareness raising is very much needed. The escalation of violence in May 2021 on Gaza placed further pressure on the overstretched and insufficient protection services and resources in the area.Status of School Level Preparedness to Disasters: Response programming has largely neglected DRR at community and school levels given the nature of these communities and geopolitical context. Strengthening education systems to prepare for and respond to disasters is central to reducing disruption to education. Yet basic interventions facilitating emergency preparedness in schools and communities in Area B and C are absent, and include: supply first aid kits for schools; lifesaving and life skills training for students (components of DRR, exercises, drills, etc.); training for school staff and community members on how to conduct inclusive school risk assessments and develop inclusive Emergency Response Plans and School DRR Plans; and inclusive DRR advocacy and awareness raising for communities and local authorities

2 - Activities

In response to the immediate needs of the vulnerable children and teachers in the targeted 38 school, World Vision and in coordination with the relevant stakeholders from Ministry of Education, Ministry of social Development and the Palestinian Red Crescent, will implement a holistic approach focusing on protection and education. To better design the response in accordance to the needs, while ensuring a participatory approach among stakeholders and beneficiaries, World Vision and in coordination with the MoE and cluster will do a thorough need assessment, where results will be translated in the contingency plans of each of the schools. the contingency plan will be comprehensive to ensure covering all aspects that might affect the lives of the children and students. The plan will highlight the different needs of the students, teachers including PSS needs and the infrastructure of the schools and that is to consider schools as a safe haven for students and teachers. following the development of the contingency plans, an implementation plan will be developed with the participation of the school-based committees representing students and teachers. the committee members will prioritize interventions in alignment with the main objective of the project. based on the above, the proposed activities are: 1- Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by the education-related violations and pandemic. World Vision and in coordination with the MoE will assess the capacity of selected teachers and counsellors in terms of their knowledge about conducting PSS related extra-curricular activities, detect of stress symptoms among children and refer cases when needed. PSS extra-curricular activities will be done at schools using psychodrama and art, where the activities will be integrated as part of the school plans and be used by the teachers on regular bases to ease the stress among children and support them in building their confidence, be safe and enjoy school. through this activity; 50 counselors and teachers will be targeted in a 6 days training distributed among 2 groups. Gender issues and CWD needs will be given much attention in the training and that is to mitigate any challenge that might face both of these vulnerable groups. 2- Provision of essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that schools environment is inclusive and safe for students and teachers. The basic emergency rehabilitation will be done in alignment with the results of the contingency plan and the implementation plan. The priorities will be selected as per the needs of the school to be considered safe for students and teachers. To ensure better implementation of this activity, a supervising engineering company will be hired to assess the infrastructure of the targeted schools and a plan will be presented to be endorsed by the committee and MoE. Gender and CWD needs will be fully covered in the plans and reflected in the actual work at the school level, including adapting any infrastructure work to the needs of CWD. 3- Support the most vulnerable schools in oPt in responding to emergencies through structured preparedness packages and post-emergency response. Following the results of the assessment and the development of the contingency plans and the implementation plans, there will be specific structures DRR interventions that will take place at the schools including capacity building for the committees. In partnership with the civil defence and the red crescent society, several capacity building sessions will take place at the schools to build the preparedness capacity to respond when a disaster take place related to either the conflict or natural. Awareness raising sessions for all students and teachers on DRR and response mechanisms will take place including drilling activities, use of fire extinguishers and others. 4- Provision of emergency education supplies for vulnerable and unserved students and teachers. supplies will be selected in reference to the needs of each school as presented in the contingency plans and in coordination with the MoE. Type of tools and specifications will be selected jointly which will include DRR tools, PSS kits to be used for the extra-curricular activities, hygiene and cleaning materials and others based on needs.

3 - Indicators

The project is designed to measure 4 outcomes indicators and 7 output indicators as indicated in the education cluster monitoring framework. The output indicators correspond with the set of activities designed under the response, where the monitoring tools and documentation of data will be fully managed by the M&E department of World Vision. As for the outcome indicators under the 3 strategic objectives, a tool will be developed to and will be used before the intervention to measure the baseline data, and will be used after the end of the intervention to measure enldine and thus have a clear impact results of the response. A solid Detailed Monitoring and evaluation ( M&E) plan will be developed based on the set indicators andtargets, and details all monitoring tools and time-frame. Pre- Post information will be collected to measure the level of impact and information will be inserted with the M&E plan. Monitoring tools will be developed taking into consideration age and gender and CWD aspects and will be child friendly, where tools will include pre-post tests to measure the level of knowledge, observation reports, focus groups, surveys, PRA, interviews with key stakeholders including children and children with disability, alongside with the monitoring reports that would be submitted from each of the targeted schools stating progress, impact and successes. The project M&E approach will follow key principals as follows: 1- Joint monitoring practices through collaboration with the MoE. WV has strong relationships with the MoE, thus creating a foundation for shared responsibilities and accountability in M&E tasks. 2- World Vision field team will be oriented on using the monitoring tools .3- Progress reports will be regularly collected on bi-weekly basis and shared with key stakeholders 4- End of project report and evaluation. The project team will ensure Community participation and follow up on activities as part of the M&E.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

World Vision uses the "Do No Harm" Principle (also known as "Local Capacities for peace" or LCP analysis tool) to integrate conflict-sensitivity wherever it is relevant. Do No Harm (DNH) is now used widely by many aid organizations, including governments and multilateral organizations. World Vision is an active organization globally using the "Do No Harm" principle. Therefore, it is mainstreamed throughout the project design and will continue to be used throughout the implementation with all relevant parties WV will follow a robust conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. WV will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits, cluster consultation, and local stakeholder information. WV will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. WV will continuously monitor the development of violence escalations and the fragile context and risks. It will analyze the potential interaction between the planned activity, the changing context and potential implications for the relations between stakeholders and communities. WV`s Social Tensions Tracking and Mapping system will locate geographic areas with communities vulnerable to social tensions. WV`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. WV will strictly enforce its Child and Adults Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed. Employing the survivors-centered approach in responding to any human rights violations.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Ensuring equitable access and gender sensitivity was taken into consideration regarding the selected schools, as two schools are girls’ schools and two boys while the rest are co-ed. Moreover, impartiality was considered in prioritizing the schools with high need due to location and # of violations and activities designed gender specific c and attending also to CWDs. Each group will be exposed to a set of activities that are fit for their specific needs. this is clearly shown in the gender-specific activities that are part of the project, including but not limited to, PSS support for females and females specifically, and hygiene awareness information for males and females.To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project’s stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks. WV considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project will prioritize for example PwDs with means to enhance their access to project services. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to boys, girls, women and men according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create women only working groups to facilitate their participation. WV, together with project partners, will check during the project visits the problems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse by the family members. Both Men and women will be included in sessions with different interests and fields related mainly to enhancing their protection and building positive resilience and coping strategies. The Gender Advisor at JWG will ensure gender mainstreaming in the interventions. WV’s Field Officers will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by WV at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of WV policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities in communities.
