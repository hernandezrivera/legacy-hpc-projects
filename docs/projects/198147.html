Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to food and contribute to improving social cohesion and supporting early recovery and building resilience of vulnerable and food-insecure communities affected by the Boko Haram crisis and violence in the Far North region, the influx of refugees in the eastern regions, Adamawa and the North linked to the Central African crisis, the internal socio-political crisis in the North-West and South-West regions. This project will address the immediate food needs of 116,000 vulnerable people, including refugees, internally displaced persons, returnees, and local populations who are severely affected by the 03 crises mentioned above, disrupting economic, agricultural, and pastoral activities in these regions. The 116,000 beneficiaries will be distributed as follows: 86,000 people to be assisted under the FFA for early recovery and 30,000 people by the FFA for Resilience. The Resilience FFA will be implemented only in the regions (04) affected by the Boko-Haram crisis and the CAR crisis while the Early Recovery FFA covers all regions (06) affected by the three crises. The implementation of livelihood activities, community participatory planning (CBPP) sessions are conducted in collaboration with local community members (women and men) to identify the real needs and priorities of local communities that will help affected populations develop their own response strategies and build resilience. This community-centred approach ensures the provision of effective solutions with beneficiaries and reduces reliance on relief.
Assistance through for FFA, will be implemented using cash modality and in-kind Food for four months under the FFA-early recovery, and eight months for the FFA-Resilience. Therefore, the cash transfer value is aligned with the in-kind food ration. It considers beneficiaries' food preferences for the commodities and prices on the local market. The transfer value is calculated based on the identified food gap and is designed to assist beneficiaries to reach the recommended minimum calories intake of 2,100 Kcal per person per day. The use of cash-based transfers is expected to improve the cost-effectiveness of WFP's operations, while seizing the economic opportunities of local markets, with particular consideration for the beneficiaries’ choice and dignity.
WFP will collaborate closely with Government technical ministries, cooperating partners and communities to ensure project implementation and sustainability. Nutrition, Gender and protection issues are taken into consideration at all the stages of the project elaboration, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Gender and protection risk analysis will be carried out and the most vulnerable will be targeted, including female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities.
Project sites will be in secure areas and at convenient distance from the homes of the beneficiaries.
To support smooth project implementation and ensure sustainability, capacity strengthening sessions will be organized for all stakeholders on gender and protection policy, project management, monitoring and nutrition.

1 - Besoins / Needs

WFP Cameroon under the framework of the Country Strategic Plan (CSP) 2022 - 2026 will implementing a series of activities with the support of partners, to deliver food assistance to refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and local vulnerable population as well as to improve the resilience of vulnerable communities. Early recovery and resilience activities are geared towards the implementation of strategic objective (SO) 1 & 3 of the CSP which ultimately aims to achieve the Sustainable Development Goal 2 of ‘Zero Hunger’. The goal of these activities is to address immediate food needs of vulnerable individuals and households including refugees, returnees and local communities, while helping them to reinforce their livelihoods, to reduce the risk to natural disaster and to build resilience to present and future shocks. All these activities include the provision of food assistance to vulnerable persons where general food distributions have considerably reduced or stopped. FFA-Early Recovery consists of providing food assistance for asset creation in targeted communities during an emergency to support early recovery. It aims at:• Reconstructing and/or restoring lost or destroyed assets.• Establishing livelihoods to facilitate the transition from emergency to development after a crisis.• Shortening the emergency phase while boosting efforts to strengthen peace, reconciliation, community stability and social cohesion.• Establishment of backyard/home gardens for dietary diversification • Construction of dry fences or vegetation fences to control grazing and agricultural fields• Support for the establishment of tree nurseries (of socio-economic and environmental importance)• Support for the development of income-generating activities (IGAs), both agriculture-related and off-farm activities (i.e., Micro, small, and medium enterprises of food transformation, petty food trade, garage, hairdressing, transport services, blacksmithing) • Promotion of gender and nutrition-sensitive activities (i.e., nutrition sensitive messaging and training on infant and young child feeding, SBCC, vocational training for the youth, and introduction to CSA practices)The resilience FFA activity of the CSP consists of providing food assistance for the creation of productive assets and market infrastructure, and environmental protection and adaptation. It contributes to: • Support to vulnerable communities to build/restore productive assets for climate change adaptation (i.e., support for water harvesting, soil enhancement, moisture retention, reforestation, watershed protection, construction of protective dams and flood preparedness) • Support the rehabilitation of facilities or Create community market infrastructures • (i.e., like warehouses or storage facilities, rehabilitation of feeder roads, shelter mills, etc.) • Enhance the resilience of production systems (i.e like improved seed production units, soil preservation, fertility, strengthening irrigation systems, capacity strengthening on modern agricultural-farming techniques, seed protection etc) • Support acess and acquisition of land for farming and other resilience activities (i.e. landplots granted to displaced communities)• Develop income generating activities.
