1 - Activities

WFP will continue prioritizing monthly food assistance to vulnerable populations through its LSA (rural) and Urban Food Security programmes. During the peak of the 2020-21 lean season (January-April 2021), the LSA programme will scale up to reach a total of 2,332,559 people in 57 rural districts with monthly in-kind food assistance, which will cover 100% of the IPC 4 population and roughly 60% of IPC 3. On top of this, WFP will continue its humanitarian food assistance programme reaching 1,167,441 beneficiaries in 22 districts for the entire duration the lean season (January-April). Overall, WFP will provide food assistance to 3,500,000 people, while supporting them to optimize the current growing season in collaboration with FAO and through linkages with WFP’s resilience building activities. WFP will also explore (extrabudgetary) opportunities to maximize the nutritional value of the food basket by fortifying cereals and/or including fortified products to counteract challenges experienced in diet diversity of the most vulnerable populations. The Urban programme will provide cash-based transfers via e-voucher and remittance modalities to approximately 550,000 people in 27 urban domains. Key operational activities include beneficiary registration and verification, retailer contracting, remittance service provider identification, beneficiary sensitization, regular markets analysis, as well as post-distribution monitoring. SAMS activities include the procurement and distribution of input packages (fertilizer and seeds); promotion of conservation agriculture techniques through trainings for agricultural extension workers and smallholder farmers in 30 rural districts. FFA seeks to target 108,675 vulnerable people (21,735 households) in 15 districts whose food security and livelihoods are affected by recurrent shocks and stressors. Targeted households will receive trainings and will work to build both community and household level assets, in exchange for food assistance (50kg cereal, 10kg pulses, 3.75kg oil per person per month) over the course of 6 months (May-December).

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

WFP diligently employs a variety of complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM) during the implementation of all programmes to ensure operational transparency, protection and improve overall service delivery. CFM channels provide a safe space for both beneficiaries and non-recipients to voice their concerns or seek answers to queries, etc. WFP uses community-based participatory planning, in coordination with relevant stakeholders, to determine beneficiaries of assistance based on need. Risks related to transactional sex, access, safety, dignity and access to assistance by vulnerable groups, particularly women, children, people with disabilities, chronic illnesses, and the elderly are monitered and dealt with the highest level of priority. WFP employs enhanced processes and mechanisms to prevent acts of sexual exploitation and abuse from occurring, including through the regular training of field personnel, sensitisation of affected populations, as well as the continued strengthening of feedback mechanisms. In FFA programmes, WFP adopts a Community-Based Participatory Planning approach to ensure that local communities are involved at every stage of the planning process. WFP employs a variety of communication methods in order to reach affected communities to ensure that information is shared widely and reaches the most vulnerable.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

A key component of the SAMS programme is the promotion of conservation agricultural (CA) practices which improves soil structure and protects the soil against erosion and nutrient losses by maintaining a permanent soil cover and minimizing soil disturbance. Furthermore, conservation agriculture practices enhance soil organic matter levels and nutrient availability by utilizing the previous crop residues and keeping these residues as a surface mulch rather than burning. Thus, arable land under conservation agriculture is more productive for much longer periods of time. CA requires significantly less water use due to increased infiltration and enhanced water holding capacity from crop residues left on the soil surface. Mulches also protect the soil surface from extreme temperatures and greatly reduce surface evaporation. WFP aligns its Food Assistance for Assets programming with the government's and WFP’s environmental social safeguards requirements that all assets to be created for a positive environmental impact. All planned assets will be assessed for impact and cleared to proceed. During implementation, Environmental Management Agency (EMA) officers inspect sites continually, ensuring expected standards are respected. Mitigation measures are identified and adopted, such as limiting the number of trees felled when preparing sites and educating communities about soil erosion, including training on good agriculture practice and watershed management.
