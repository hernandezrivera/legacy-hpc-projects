Project Summary

Pulka is one of these garrison towns and hosts more than 60,000 IDPs living both in camps settings and host communities. This locality is characterized by a chronic water scarcity (less than 7,5l/p/d) obliging humanitarian actors, including NCA, to truck water (around 210 m3 daily) throughout the year as most of the existing water points are not productive either because they are not deep enough or not well designed/constructed. This pushed the communities to replace all solar components of most of the solar boreholes with motorized pumps which need fuel to be operated. This further increases the vulnerability of conflict-affected populations as water is now commercialized to get money for fuel and engine oil necessary for the O&M.
Many actors tried to drill new boreholes equipped with solar powered pumps but due to lack of clear information about the underground water resources and poor designing of the solar systems most of them are also not functional or even affected by the drawdown. Consequently, the communities are more and more reluctant to use solar water schemes which makes it difficult to upgrade or to retrofit any existing water point with solar powered pumps which normally, are the best solution to guarantee access to water for all, including the most vulnerable who cannot afford to pay before fetching water.
To overcome this challenge and prevent health risks due to this water scarcity, NCA Nigeria is considering, the construction of well designed, deep, and productive solar powered borehole in Pulka. This is part of NCA’s phasing out strategy from water trucking in that location. The idea is to conduct a detailed geophysical survey, through the proton magnetic resonance method, more effective and reliable than the electrical methods (VES) which have been largely used for underground water exploration in Pulka with few good results. NCA Nigeria already has a budget for the survey aspect of the activity and will recruit a qualified consultant to carry out this geophysical survey. After identifying the areas with highest aquifers potential, the organization will contract a heavy drilling machine that can get to deeper aquifers with high productivity

2 - Activities

NCA has conducted geophysical studies involving several methods not previously used in the area. Productive points were determined and two test boreholes were drilled by the consultant. The idea is therefore to set up a water supply system for small towns by exploiting the productive boreholes. This project therefore aims to build large productive boreholes connected to the existing water supply network and to extend the distribution to areas that were not previously covered. Photovoltaic pumping will be used exclusively to reduce the operating costs of water production.This project, once implemented, will reduce the crucial drinking water problem in the town of Pulka and consequently the risks incurred by women and children when fetching water.It will also improve children's education, family economy in the sense that the people concerned will have enough time to do other activities.In addition, throughout the project, awareness-raising will be carried out to call for behavioural changes and community acceptance of the importance of solar pumping. Hygiene promotion activities and also sanitation activities will be developed for a complete WASH response.For hygiene, NCA will develop a behaviour change approach through the implementation of a strategy based on community engagement.For sanitation, Nca intends to set up a totally community-driven sanitation project with a small grant to the poor to take charge of the construction of latrines and showers themselves.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Sensitivity to cross-cutting issues is fundamental in NCA programmes and gender and equity issues is a strategic commitment.NCA's intention is to look at protection as a cross-cutting issue with the aim of "Do No Harm". When women and children seek help to meet their needs, they are susceptible to certain protection risks and therefore NCA will seek to mitigate those risks. Therefore, to ensure that proposed activities do not create a protection risk for certain groups such as women, youth and children, NCA will take special precautions. For example, by ensuring that sensitization sessions are conducted in a way that does not increase the risks, and by adapting the time, place and staff to these vulnerable groups. And we will seek to limit their travel to health centres and/or their reception on site in order to limit unnecessary exposures. Sensitivity to cross-cutting issues is also reflected in the integration of women into community management structures and the adoption of practices such as the separation of male and female latrines in public places. The policy is firmly rooted in the promotion of women to key local governance positions enabling them to influence and guide decisions to address the concerns of the most disadvantaged. The situational analysis within communities identifies priority needs, while prioritising vulnerability criteria to ensure interventions that benefit the disadvantaged (i.e. dimensioning of facilities appropriate to the needs of children and people living with reduced mobility).Priority will be given to the establishment of an accountability framework for the programme. This will bring together all local leaders, rights holders and other stakeholders to ensure regular information sharing, strengthen self-assessment involving the role/responsibility of each actor and the establishment of appropriate governance systems. The participatory approach will build the capacity of local actors and structures (masons, artisanal repairers, management committee), under a governance system for water services and water safety under the leadership of the accountability framework through the community improvement plan. To promote the local market, local contractors will be strongly involved in the local labour force and as local suppliers of the materials they are able to produce, including cement blocks.
