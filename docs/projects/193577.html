Project Summary

The proposed project is in line with the EIEWG framework objectives 1 and 2,and HRP SO 1 and 2, with the MOE protection and recovery plan 2020, the cluster objectives related to “increase equitable safe access to inclusive ,quality educational services for the most vulnerable children”, and the protection objective related to provision of school based PSS to school staff and students affected by the education related to violation and C-19 and legal aid in WB. The project aims to provide protection of vulnerable children affected by conflict and other situations of violence, particularly those at risk of denied access to educational services and individual and mass forcible transfer in the WB. NRC will work with the MoE and partners to identify new methodologies to reach the most vulnerable children and promote more inclusive education practices, in-person and the blended approach and distance education programs. NRC will promote the identification and coordinate inclusive education activities with CBOs in Area C and EJ. NRC will promote parental engagement in education, but also include a legal and counselling component at the school level and with teachers and non-teaching staff. The project targets 70 most vulnerable MoE and MoEHE schools supporting children access to a protective and safe learning environment for a total of 17,710 (9,342M and 8,268F):1) 17,710 (9,342 M and 8,268 F)students will be provided with PSS through training of teachers on how to manage a classroom with children who have gone through stressful and traumatic events, how to better support under-achievers and training of counsellors to support students with chronic nightmares that severely affect their school functioning thought the NRC flagship BLP; 2) 17,710 (9,342 M and 8,268 F) will benefit from emergency school kits to support crisis-disaster risk reduction in tandem with capacity building; 3) 17,710 (9,342 M and 8,268 F) will benefit from delivery of legal information sessions through awareness raising for students, school staff and parents about legal rights as well as response to incidents/violations through documentation; 4) advocacy pursuing enhanced protection from violations of the right to education. 5) 800 (400 M and 400 F) children will be involved in remedial education and catch-up classes to contribute to mitigation to the learning loss and children drop out of school due to C-19 restrictions. 6) 800 (400 M and 400 F) children support distance learning by providing tablets to most vulnerable children; printing self-learning materials; rehabilitate ICT infrastructure in schools, and trainings on digital literacy. Throughout the intervention a gender-responsive approach will be adopted to ensure girls and boys participation and needs assessment conducted to identify gender barriers that could affect access to education.Informative sessions will be conducted with parents and teachers on the importance of education for both boys and girls, reducing risks to access learning opportunities, with provision of psychosocial support in order to ensure children’s ability to freely express themselves in a protected environment. During the project, NRC will partner with Humanity and Inclusion to address challenges and barberries in accessing education for children with disabilities, promoting recommendations for schools on adapting or adding facilities for children with disabilities and promoting schooling for those children. Moreover, NRC will adopt Washington Group questions to ensure a disaggregation of identified disabilities. PYALARA, NRC’s local partner, is mainly devoted to transforming the role of education in Palestine to become a socially responsible education. Their objectives are to alleviate the suffering of adolescents and build the capacity of teachers and counsellors .

1 - Needs

The coercive environment in the West Bank, presenting itself as the systematic denial of rights, structural barriers to development, conflict and lack of accountability for violations of international law, has led to an ongoing threat of forcible transfer and created a cycle of chronic humanitarian need and aid dependence, which presents a major obstacle to the development effort. In vulnerable communities in the West Bank, the rate of demolitions of Palestinian-owned structures and settler violence remain high, and many Palestinians, particularly in Area C, East Jerusalem, and Hebron city(H2), continue to face the risk of forcible transfer. These areas have witnessed multiple cycles of violence and increased on Education related violation include attacks on schools, military use of schools, threats of attacks against school staff and students and interferences with access to education. Children continue to face violence and other challenges in accessing schools and the realization of their right to education remains severely compromised due to violence and armed search operations in schools; harassment, intimidation, and violence towards students and teachers on the way to and from school; poor education infrastructure; demolition and stop-work orders on school buildings. Additionally, children suffer from loss of school time due to firing tear gas into the vicinity or inside schools, delays at checkpoints and continuous Israeli military presence and operations around schools. In area C in particular, children have to walk long distances to reach the nearest school, often having to cross one or two checkpoints. Teachers and school personnel also report frequent harassment, searches and humiliation at checkpoints. In East Jerusalem, a discriminatory, restrictive planning and zoning regime has allocated 2.6 per cent of the land for the development of public buildings for Palestinians, resulting in more than 2,557 classroom shortage for Palestinian students within the public-school system, while in terms of infrastructure, many schools are in rented premises. The rented schools/classrooms are normally private residential properties, not adequate to meet education requirements. In March 2021, NRC, in partnership with the University of Auckland and with the support of ECHO, finalised a research to explore the impact of COVID 19 on children’s wellbeing, and on education. The study indicated that in West Bank, the most vulnerable schools presented more risk factors than the less vulnerable schools. For students attending schools in communities and locales that were already more educationally vulnerable prior to COVID, there is a real risk that educational inequalities will worsen in COVID’s aftermath, particularly as learners in the more vulnerable communities miss out on learning, or experience sub-optimal e-learning experiences, compared to other students.(Ritesh, Zhao and Choo, 2021). According to MSNA 2022, in West Bank, rates of school attendance for basic and secondary education were slightly higher for school-aged girls than for school-aged boys, although the difference between girls and boys was most pronounced at the secondary school level, where 92.7% of school aged girls 16-17 years old attending in school compared to 70.6% of school aged boys 16-17 years old. 19.0% of those children attending school, dropped out of school during the current school year (2021-2022), with 19.8% of all school-aged boys and 13.6% of school-aged girls reportedly dropping out. The main reasons why children reportedly dropped out were protection risks of a political character while traveling to and from school (32.8%), including violence or harassment from Israeli soldiers, forces, or settlers, and school closures (26.9%). Schools being demolished or under threat of demolition was provided as a reason for children by 2.8% of households with at least one child that had reportedly dropped out of school. Lack of interest of children in education was frequently provided as a reason for non-enrollment, non-attendance, or dropping out. 72.6% of refugee households and 69.3% of non-refugee households reported a need for catch-up learning due to school closures due to COVID-19 or other reasons, Of the 11.3% of households reporting school-aged children not attending school have reported these as main reasons: Lack of interest (of children) in education, children working to support household, school stopped functioning/school closures, school inaccessible for children with disability, school cannot accommodate children with disability. From the interviewed households 19 % have reported school-aged children (5 - 17 years) dropped out of school during the 2021 - 2022 school year and as main reasons were mentioned: political protection risks, school closures, lack of interest, medical issue, cannot afford. As per the research conducted by NRC: “Learning on the margins” (April 2021), the main reasons of inequality for girls to attend school are rooted in: Major gender equality and development issues in education include: narrow range of female specialization. In higher education, girls primarily enrol in the humanities and in “soft” commerce and business tracks rather than technical fields such as engineering or computer science. Gender differences in dropout rates: While the gender gap in both enrolment and dropout rate is minimal in the lower grades, dropout rates increase, and the gender gap grows as students move from one grade to another in the secondary cycle, dropout rates increase, and the gender gap grows. High demands of meeting basic needs (towards gender equality in Palestinian Territory)

2 - Activities

NRC has signed a MoU with the MoE for collaboration and engagement across the West Bank. The activities are clustered in the following main areas: 1)Provision of Psychosocial support at all levels, by teachers and counsellors at school level: a. assessment to select and phase out from formal schools. b. informative sessions with school personnel, directorates and MoE. c. training package on BLP 1, BLP 2 and BLP 3. d. training for supervisors and key staff in the MoE, to ensure the continuation of the programme. e. Support supervisors, teaching and non-teaching staff to implement BLP 1, BLP 2 and BLP 3. 2)Promoting psychosocial wellbeing of students and teaching and non-teaching staff will contribute to promote a better-quality basic education, which will be enhanced through provision of safe and protective learning environments. MoE staff, parents, teachers, community leaders, religious leaders and women’s committees and associations will be part of awareness sessions focused on children’s wellbeing. Community groups and children’s clubs will be established or where existing strengthened, and receive trainings on PSS and child safe guarding, in order to ensure that children and communities will be empowered to identify, mitigate and respond to risk factors.3)Ensure access for children (aged 4-17) to quality basic education in a safe, inclusive and protective environment focusing on drop-out, risk of drop- out and out of school children a. provide direct technical support, b. Identify the specific needs at school level (pedagogical gaps and needs), c. support training on teachers in crisis context package, e. Develop a community engagement on the importance of education f. Support the adoption of blended plans at school level, ensuring in-person and distance education. 4)Children at risk of dropping out will be provided with services aimed at enhancing the empowerment and skills of vulnerable children, developing their capacities and resilience and enabling them to play their full and effective role in society by prioritising skills application through exploring opportunities for livelihood initiatives and by adopting a complete youth skills development approach targeting youth in secondary schools. Early identification of drop out or at high risk of drop out children, to support their learning improvement. Drop-out students will receive a learning service provided by recruited support teachers. The activity will require salaries for the support teachers, training sessions to ensure that support teachers might be able to continue their teaching under emergency and able to help children with PSS. Remedial classes will part of school set up in formal schools with NRC’s provision of school material, recreational material and learning kits for students. The activity will aim to guide children to regain attention during the lessons, and catch-up with the curriculum as per their peers which will contribute to their personal growth. D) comprehensive situation analysis of out-of-school children and identification of mitigation measures. Out of school children will be part of a bridging programme to formal education and will get the opportunity for a fresh start. Support teachers will be selected to lead catch-up classes for out of school children, using public venues or where needed coordinating with CSOs, local children clubs etc 5)Throughout the project implementation, advocacy efforts using field and desk research will take place to produce materials, briefings and submissions to the international community in oPt, including EU and UN bodies and diplomatic missions, as well as EU and UN bodies based in Brussels, Geneva, and New York, and the U.S. Congress. Development of focused evidence-based content, with rights based legal analysis, on education-related violations and interferences impacting Palestinian children’s right to education. All NRC staff and local partners staff receive an information on code of conduct, PSEA and CFM. NRC will also include binding conditions in all procurement documents and procedures with suppliers to ensure their commitment. In addition, NRC protection team will develop and disseminate PSEA awareness raising material. Part of NRC policy is to adhere and follow the IASC guidelines when providing PSS services to children, teachers, and care givers. It is NRC’s goal to enhance the wellbeing of children in respectful and dignified manner, promote gender and age equality, support care givers and parents, and do no harm. NRC acknowledges that girls, boys, women, and men are exposed to different risks, cope differently, and have different needs that are better addressed from a gender-specific perspective and in a culture-specific manner, therefore the action will seek a balanced gender representation of beneficiaries at all levels while remaining sensitive to local norms and constraints in regard to female participation in the project activities.There will be safe and appropriate training for female participation, including the provision of separate training where necessary, for example when physical activity is a core component, female students may feel more comfortable in a female-only training environment. Parents will be encouraged to participate in parent activities, as they are often the primary caregivers, in order to foster sustained involvement in the Action and to enhance their involvement in their child's education, which is often considered primarily the responsibility of the mother, However, The project aims for male parents to attend the awareness raising sessions. A capacity building strategy will take into account the specific needs of teaching and non-teaching staff (males and females).Psychosocial approaches tailored to address gender discrimination will be implemented in order to facilitate equitable relationships between males and females

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Education programing in NRC is grounded in and based on NRC longstanding experience of supporting the psychosocial/learning development of children and young people. The service modality focuses on tailor the specific intervention on the existing needs of each school, Building the capacity of schools and empowering the teaching and non-teaching staff in delivering protection, PSS/SEL services to children and communities.in-kind modality fits to the education intervention specifically when the delivery is directly targeting schools rather than a community. This modality considered as a part of the organization management regulation when dealing at school level. Additionally, this modality selection has taken into consideration target schools, MoE and educational directorates preferences; needs, and risks. Finally, the situational analysis conducted in target schools validated the selection modality. In the event where a teacher detects a child in need of additional support from specialized services, the teacher will refer the child to the Ministry of Education through the school's referral system.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Through the Complaints and Feedback mechanism, NRC is committed to strict standards of transparency and accountability across all core competencies where everyone has the right to file a complaint and receive a response. NRC’s CFM focal points in West bank and Gaza respond to all requests/complaints directly or within 14 days as per SOP. The complaints and feedback received by NRC is confidential and closely monitored to be used to improve our working methods making our programs more responsive, effective, and appropriate to the needs of the affected populations. Across core competencies, the CFM team, based on the feedback or complaint received, have the responsibility to inform program teams to respond and act to bring positive change, and continuously inform the community on the appropriate actions taken to respond to the feedback or complaint. NRC CFM contributes to organizational learning, and continuous improvement of accountability and quality of NRC’s work to be gender-sensitive, safe, and confidential, avoiding any risk, harm, or retaliation to our affected population. CFM and M&E units compile, and analyze data collected through various learning exercises and then share the learning and findings with program management. This includes findings and instances of learning from all M&E exercises, field staff observations, outcome surveys, recommendations from evaluations, and recommendations from complaints or feedback received. Program management are accountable to ensure that the learning takes place, and recommendations and findings are incorporated into projects as much as possible.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NRC recognises that safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults engaged in their programmes is a fundamental responsibility. All staff must sign and are trained on NRC’s Code of Conduct, and are required to sign and be trained on the organisation’s Child Safeguarding Policy. The training for Education staff and staff with regular contact with children includes content on child rights, understanding child abuse and sexual abuse, reviews behaviour and conduct required from staff and how to receive disclosures and report and refer cases. BLP content is also designed to promote safe and inclusive learning environments for children and should support staff and teachers’ understanding of child protection. NRC Action trusts working on incorporating protection principles and prompting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in the proposed humanitarian aid. At the very beginning of the action in the needs assessment phase, the NRC will identify the people at risk, kind of risks they face and expected to face, and why they face such kind of risks, as well as activating a structured referral system who people who seek more help and support. The NRC action follows the participatory approach, where beneficiaries will be included from the early beginning of the action, participating in the needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation phase, and in identifying the area of improvement. Moreover, all beneficiaries will have the same chance of receiving the service (e.g. school children will receive the service at a voluntary manner and with a respected informed consent from their parents). Both teachers, and counselors as a main beneficiaries in NRC action will participate in a voluntary manner, and due to their interest. A follow up mechanism will be shared with all NRC suggested beneficiaries, where open channels of communication will be offered by NRC (e.g. Generic emails, field visits etc) with all beneficiaries that guarantee the confidentiality.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The NRC action focuses on three dimensions of intervention: i) a general classroom-based intervention approach targeted 17,710 (9,342 boys and 8,268 girls) children in target schools, its suitable for all children in any context, as its designed to reach larger numbers of children through classroom delivery. This dimension is designed to ensure and guarantee the equal chances of access for all children both boys and girls and their specific needs promoting inclusive education through parents ‘engagement, male teachers positive role and including age-appropriate vocabulary to encourage children to think about values. ii) The second dimension offers a special kind of support to boys and girls who need additional support, to support resilience among them for the early recovery or protracted displacement when conditions for learning have stabilized. iii) The third dimension is a specialised, clinical one designed to address nightmares, which many children experience as a chronic symptom of traumatic stress. Equal chances of both males and females, boys and girls will be promoted at all level and partnering with specialized organizations, cases that will not go under NRC expertise will be referred externally. As well as without any kind of discrepancies among the beneficiaries either they are children or adult. The NRC action welcomes all children being part of the service delivered regardless of the differences among them based on inclusion approach.
