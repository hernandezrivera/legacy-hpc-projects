Project Summary

Syria is one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities, triggering one of the worst displacement crises in modern history, resulting in widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, including limited access to WASH facilities leading to WASH insecurities
As reported by HNO 2021, over 7 million people are highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in IDP last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive.
Given the fact that most IDPs need support and are not in a position to sustain even simple hand washing without support it is imperative to have a sustainable intervention, which at a minimum will provide basic water facilities. Building upon the previous program, the knowledge and practice, we have planned integrated programs for 2022 – 23 as follows:
Water Station Support: During the past years, Big heart was one of the organizations implementing WASH activities in Aleppo/Afrin. Big Heart identified the water stations by conducting RNA in Afrin and close coordination with the LCs and other organizations working in that same locations, in addition to reviewing the information provided by ACU for the water stations in Afrin. Therefore, we have identified 12 Water Stations (Sheikh Alhadid, Maydan, Kosan, Karababa, Masikan, Tobal Mahmood, Afrin, Raju, Suran, Marae'e, Al-Bab, Ar-Rae). While designing the programs, we focused on integration and sustainability. The plan is to solarize all 12 stations to reduce dependence of public power. This is a critical factor in sustainability as Cost Recovery Mechanisms are rudimentary and at time nonexistent making it difficult to operate the water stations. Further complexing the situation is outdated equipment and major/minor repairs required to water infrastructure, including pumps and generators. The planned programming will address al needs, including installation of solar power, replace non-functioning pumps, civil works to water reservoirs and tanks and rehabilitation of network where required. During first quarter, we have acquired funding from SCHF for 5 water stations (Maydan, Kosan, Karababa, Masikan, Tobal Mahmood and Afrin), reaching out to approximately 200,000 beneficiaries leaving a funding requirement for 500,000 beneficiaries under 7 remaining water stations.
Hygiene Promotion: The planned programming including funded part has integrated hygiene programs built into it. The programming will include provision of hygiene kits, awareness sessions and monitoring of hygiene practices at the community level.
Cost Recovery Mechanisms: All of our WASH programming including currently funded programs have CRM as an integral part built into it. All possible economic factors have been taken into consideration while designing the CRM. A dedicated team has been established to design and commission the CRM, enabling water stations to operate without external assistance.
Solid Waste Management: All the communities will receive solid waste management, to include repair of existing system and where required providing new systems such as garbage cans and repair of garbage pickup trucks and related facilities.
Modality – The planned activities will be implemented without any sub partners and will be through direct contracting. The programming will also support the stipends for water stations staff and regular supply of fuel and other material required for optimal operations of each water station. The target is to provide water at 35 ltr/person/day. We aim to achieve 90% satisfaction levels.
Data Collection and Monitoring - The monitoring will be through our well-defined M&E systems. Monitoring will be diligently executed, starting from Verifications all the way to Satisfaction Surveys. A robust system of lodging complaints is already in place through communications tools such as WhatsApp, SMS, email, and via hard copy and drop in the complaints boxes.

3 - Needs

As reported by HNO 2021 over 7 million people are highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in IDP last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive. The water stations currently are unable to provide required services due to several factors, including lack of resources to have any sustainable operations. This is primarily due to outdated equipment, lack of cost recovery, leading to insufficient resources, ultimately resulting in an in-operational water station.Based on HRP 2022/23, Up to 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water to meet or complement their water needs, and at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated. At least half of the sewerage systems not functional, and over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency. Further to this the COVID outbreak has complexed the situation where there is a dire need to have a sustainable solution with less reliance on external assistance. There is an urgent need to program on alternate energy sources, strengthening cost recovery mechanisms, upgrade of water station equipment and hygiene promotion, including hygiene kits and awareness. The current programs only provide sections of assistance required and what is required is a holistic assistance. In absence of a holistic assistance, the water stations will remain dependent on external assistance thus impacting resilience.

4 - Implementation of activities

The proposed action will deliver an evidence‑based program of assistance to targeted water systems to immediately increase access to safe water, while also safeguarding WASH service provision beyond the implementation period thru working in coordination with LCs and water units to establish cost recovery mechanisms. To further support sustainability of service provision, planned activities include installation of innovative hybrid power solutions to reduce long‑term costs associated with service delivery. Complementary works to improve sanitation systems and water networks were assessed and costed as part of 2022-2023 planning. Hygiene promotion activities will continue to be delivered in catchment areas, with particular focus on COVID‑19 prevention and response.Targeted Locations are below: ‑Rajo and Bulbul : (Aleppo-Afrin City). 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible. Accessibility measures will be built into assistance delivery, including the option for home delivery to beneficiaries unable to safely access distribution points due to mobility or other restrictions. To mitigate against protection risks, we work closely with LCs and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries served. Refresher training on NGO PSEA and Child Protection policies were delivered during 2020 to ensure all staff understand how to identify and correctly report any SEA or CP concerns


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

We have strict protocols re to PSEA and Child Protection (CP) specifically with refresher trainings completed regularly with all staff on our PSEA and CP Policies & staff responsibilities as these relate to conduct, monitoring &reporting. Gender balance in our FSL & M&E teams will continue to ensure that activities are responsive to the particular needs of men, women, boys& girls with female staff present at all distribution points and responsible for all M&E activities completed with female BNFs. Our Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) will continue to be widely promoted, through information displayed prominently in public places, at all distribution points, printed on in‑kind food baskets / food vouchers and through face‑to‑face interactions with project BNFs & key stakeholders. The recent RNA completed by us found no protection risks associated with accessing markets in targeted areas. Monitoring by our staff will continue to assess for any protection concerns related to food voucher redemption. Targeting criteria will continue to mitigate protection concerns by actively targeting the most at risk groups, including female‑, child‑ and PWD‑headed HHs. Monitoring and routine PDM, completed by male and female staff with are presentative sample of both male and female BNFs will also continue to monitor for any SEA risks or concerns. The design of LLH activities places particular focus on gender‑appropriate and sex‑disaggregated LLH activities plus add'l mitigation measures.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real‑time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary.The following assumptions have been applied to program design: • LCs and local authorities (including AFAD) remain supportive of the NGOs activities and continue to cooperate with established Standard Operating Procedures designed to ensure targeted and transparent delivery of assistance on the basis of need alone• 100% house-to-house beneficiary verification reduces the risk of inclusion or exclusion criteria while close coordination with other FSL actors reduces the risk of duplication• Established guidelines for COVID-19 prevention measures for all field operations continue to mitigate against the risk of transmission • Security context in targeted areas allows ongoing access by NGO staff. • Female beneficiaries continue to be willing to engage in culturally appropriate livelihood activities.We are continually updating contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in Syria, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Should breaks be experienced due to border closures, contingency plans are to be in place to adapt to the changing context in NWS. These plans detail various scenarios and required actions to mitigate risks both operational and programmatic.
