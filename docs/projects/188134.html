Project Summary

The project aims to provide Life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on underserved areas with high severity of needs and in response to the demonstrated needs and gaps in health service provision for the conflict- affected population within Harim district, Human Appeal (HA) proposes to support and sustain the functionality of three healthcare facilities in the district for a duration of 12 months, namely:
• “Iman” maternity hospital in Sarmada/ Dana sub-district/ Harim district/ Idleb governorate (HeRAMS identifier code: G0703086).
• Human Appeal Mobile Clinic in Sarmada IDPs camps/ Dana sub-district/ Harim district/ Idleb governorate (HeRAMS identifier code: B0200196).
The above facilities will continue providing primary and secondary healthcare services for people in need within their individual catchment areas as follows:
• RH outpatient consultations in all three facilities.
• Pediatric outpatient consultations in all three facilities.
• General medicine outpatient consultations in HA MC
• Nutrition care services for children and pregnant women in Iman Hospital.
• Community health awareness services
• Provision of essential medicines
• Referral capacity (“Iman” hospital and HA MC).
• CEmONC services, laboratory services, inpatient healthcare for the women of reproductive age and newborns in Iman hospital
Besides referring to the health cluster/ Turkey hub dashboard health facilities map, and for the purpose of ensuring coordinated and non-duplicating intervention, HA, during the design phase, was very keen to coordinate and communicate with the partners and coordination bodies relevant to project including Idleb health directorate, SRH TWG, and Sham foundation as being operating a pediatric specialized hospital in Sarmada, the same community where “Iman” hospital is based
In addition to the above service package, and to ensure integration of COVID-19 related preparedness and response measure, HA proposes to operate COVID-19 triage stations at the entry of Iman hospital for the whole project period, particularly confirming any case of COVID-19 may enter the hospital. This will help the Health facilities to mitigate the risk of COVID-19 transmission and infection among the staff and the beneficiaries who are visiting the facilities.
In order to support directing the use of the allocation- matched funds for the purpose of directly serving the targeted population, It’s imperative to mention that HA will support the provision of all medicines, medical and lab supplies required at all facilities, rehabilitation costs of “Iman” hospital, and provision of the majority of project support staff remuneration costs needed for ensuring the smooth implementation of the proposed activities and provision of the planned healthcare services.
With the above details alongside the planned indicator targets, beneficiaries to be reached and the consideration of the total costs proposed to be matched to this allocation, the cost per medical procedure matched to this application will be around 8.33 USD, this value is considered to be highly efficient against the average cost of around 10-12 USD across NW- Syria.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, The fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, Relatedly, with the UN Security Council resolution extending the authorization for Bab al-Hawa until 10 July 2022, health actors in Northwest Syria are heavily focused on increasing operational supply chain capacity, At the same time, increases in poverty across the country which have already resulted in economically-driven displacement may also increase and worsen determinants of health thereby negatively impacting health outcomes.Iman hospital current caseload records, around 93% of beneficiaries served through Iman hospital are IDPs. Also, the facility provides monthly averages of 3,500 procedures for the presented women of reproductive age and children that are in need, out of which are 3500 outpatient consultations, 1500 ANC counselling sessions, 200 PNC sessions, 120 admissions, 75 C-section deliveries, 400 normal deliveries, and 175 RH minor and major surgeries respectively.HA mobile clinic is the only healthcare facility currently serving some 22,860 individuals within 13 IDPs camps in Sarmada in coordination with the health partners. The aforementioned details highly inform the need to provide the support required to ensure the functionality of the proposed facilities to cope with the healthcare needs of the targeted population within their catchment areas.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

HAT will maintain the strong relationship with the protection cluster and the referral networks to get support when needed. Protection aspects will be integrated within all programming. HAT staff are trained on child safeguard codes of conduct and other emergency programming standards such as SPHERE. Conflict sensitivity tools such as Do No Harm will be used to assess proposed project activities, identify potentially harmful impacts on beneficiaries and adapt accordingly. In doing so, a cross section of project beneficiaries, hosts, the field team and other relevant actors will be engaged to gain more understanding of the operational contexts, concerns, interests and social dynamics as part of such analysis. 1. Planning Phase Map and analyze the stakeholders, their interrelationships, roles, influence and their potential impact on the intervention. Set communications and participatory mechanisms to engage or not to engage these actors in the program stages. Mapping of the potential external and internal sources of violence, danger, coercion and take preventative and responsive measures to address, mitigate or reduce these threats Ensured that the plan addresses the specific needs of the most vulnerable groups and individuals, gender issues are critical needs that must be given a special consideration at the planning stage. 2. Implementation stage Ensure that the equal access of the most vulnerable groups to services is tangible and implemented.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

HA is an active member ins the PSEA network, and fully aware of the technical aspects related to the PSEA, This project is designed in a manner to refelect the different needs of women, men and children throughout the project cycle i.e. design, implementation and monitoring. The distinct health care needs of women, men, girls, boys, elderly and disabled are well analyzed. HAT will make sure that the health facilities have female staff to address the needs of women and promote gender equality. HAT will identify the facilities which has specialized services to provide medical assistance to GBV survivors and psychosocial support. In case if HAT supported facility received any such case this will be referred to the specialized service provider. HAT has already built the capacity of some of targeted health facility staff to appropriately record, report and follow up such cases. PSEA: For protection from PSEA the following mitigation measures will be used: Emplacement of effective complaint and feedback mechanism. Effective recruitment and performance management of staff. Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about HA, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the project it is implementing and what it intends to deliver. Do no harm will be further mainstreamed as follows: The identification of the targeted facilities was dependent upon the needs and gap analysis and no particular political or social affiliations.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Targeted centers remain accessible. - Security situation allows centers to operate.- Turkish borders remain open to transport the project deliverable.- Health professionals to continue operating at centers inside Syria. Risks and their mitigation measures (MM) identiﬁed are as follows: 1. Qualiﬁed health staff leaving area. (MM): HAT will ensure rigorous coordination with the health directorates in order to ﬁnd alternatives for the health staff. 2. Disease outbreak. MM: HAT will keep regular coordination with the targeted health facilities, health directorates and other organizations involved in the prevention of infectious outbreaks. 3. Taxes imposed by non-state armed groups. MM: HAT remains vigilant of any such check points. Thereby to avoid these check points HAT will use alternative routes for the movement of project deliverable. 4. Groups under UN security council sanction list may ﬁnancially beneﬁt from the project supplies. MM: HAT The health facilities will receive support directly from HAT without involvement of any intermediaries. Plan B for the above mentioned risks include the following: HAT will deal only with screen suppliers and will hire only screened staff.If the disease outbreak is not detected early, HAT will ensure to quarantine of affected staff in order to reduce the propagation of the disease.Communication with the local councils and community in order to ensure the goods move tax free.
