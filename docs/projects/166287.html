1 - Needs

Communities in northeast Nigeria continued to experience volatile and unpredictable security situations and displacement. According to Humanitarian information product 2020, indicates that communities continue to experience challenges in accessing basic needs and services, and face a variety of, and often inter-linked protection risks. Also, the GBV focused analysis of FGDs, as well as the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), identified this to be a common issue, mainly affecting women and girls who are trying to access aid. Adolescence, in particular, is a time when exposure to new forms of violence increases. Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, detained and recruited by parties to the conflict or to be involved in child labor, while girls are increasingly at risk of being recruited and used by parties to the conflict in some locations. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms of gender-based violence, including sexual violence. As is the case globally, women and girls are disproportionately affected by all forms of gender-based violence in Nigeria, in both the public and private spheres. In addition, women who have experienced divorce and widowhood and women and girls with disabilities continue to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination, as well as forced marriages. Men are particularly affected by restrictions of freedom of movement and forms of violence directly associated to the conduct of hostilities. Qualitative sources indicate that during situations of hostilities and displacement, persons with a disability are at increased risk of separation from their family

2 - Activities

(1)This project will mitigate the risk of infection through ensuring adherence to IPC protocols in service (2) provision facilities; harmonized COVID-19 and GBV awareness creation and innovative PSS.(3) Provide with GBV case management and specialized services (medical - including psychiatric services, psychosocial services, access to justice, safety options and security mechanisms). Adopt modalities for transitioning to remote service provision to GBV Survivors in the context of COVID 19. (4) Preposition and distribute dignity kits and other critical material support for women and girls; Scale up of the local production and assembly of standardized dignity kits and locally made PPE materials.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

For all projects, Taimako-CDI foresees an interim monitoring system, which includes verification on the achievements and on the status of the activities’ implementation every three months. Keeping these deadlines in mind, the accountability time frame must allow Taimako-CDI staff to collect and analyze information, and to disclose it to beneficiaries respecting the principle of “taking into account”. Another key point for a functional accountability system is the awareness of Taimako-CDI staff of the fact that the monitoring process is a continuum: since the planning phase, through all the implementation process, the gathering of information and feedback has to be continuous and carried out both in structured, specific moments, and in more informal contexts that can be found through the projects’ development. Regarding the opportunities and right times for collecting and sharing feedback, information and changes, Taimako-CDI staff have to be aware that the moments for doing this are not only formal and agreed (meetings, reunions with representatives, interviews, etc.), but can happen during other activities, even those that are not foreseen or implemented by the organization, but of which it has to be made the most.
