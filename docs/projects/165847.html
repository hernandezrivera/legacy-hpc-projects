Project Summary

1: Reinforcing the management of acute malnutrition
UNICEF has been supporting the MoH to revise the current protocol for the management of acute malnutrition to integrate the mobile outpatient treatment at community level. This approach will enable to cover hard to reach children suffering from acute malnutrition and living in vulnerable geographic areas. To do so, the departmental capacity will be supported to deliver life-saving nutrition services to children under five suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). The integrated package and provided at health facilities and community level using mobile Outpatient treatment with the support of Community health agents comprises three pillars: identification of malnutrition at community level through active screening, outpatient care with ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) and routine medicines for SAM cases without medical complications, and inpatient care with therapeutic milk and specific drugs for SAM cases with medical complications. Therapeutic milk and medicines will be procured offshore using UNICEF resources while RUTF will be procured locally at MFK (Meds and Foods for Kids) factory located in Haiti. Management of MAM will be carried out at the same outpatients’ sites with the same health providers. Thus, it is planned to treat a total of 82,961 children aged 6-59 months (33,109 SAM and 49,861 MAM) however focused will be put on children aged 6 to 23 months suffering from acute malnutrition. Based on the results of SMART survey, an estimated of 46,607 children aged 6-23 months will be suffering from acute malnutrition (22,033 SAM and 24,574 MAM). To date, in the three departments there are 109 nutrition facilities (13 in-patient’s facilities, 96 outpatient centers).
2: Improving access to integrated package of preventive interventions
The grant will contribute to promote and sustain recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices in emergency context, promotion of Hygiene and support the provision of multiple micronutrients to children aged 6-23 months. Mothers and or care givers will receive messages on best nutritional practices such as optimal breastfeeding, complementary feeding practices, how to use multiple micronutrients and hygiene practices, that are essential actions to prevent the deterioration of children’s nutritional status into acute malnutrition. Though this will be done at various points of contact at facility and community level, the main platform for delivering this integrated package will be through community agents using IYCF counselling material. Customized multiple micronutrient powders will be procured offshore. Decentralization will help overcoming the issue of inadequate staffing and high workload at health facility level and inadequate quality of counselling of care takers.
3: Nutrition surveillance, program coordination, management and monitoring are reinforced
One sentinel site will be established within North and North-Est departments while 2 will be put in place in metropolitain area and the remaining of the West department..Monthly coordination meetings have proved to be instrumental in enhancing information sharing, identifying gaps and partners, avoiding duplication of efforts, and disseminating technical standards and best practices. Support will also be provided to Departmental Health Offices to analyze nutrition data and statistics to monitor program performance and quality, identify low performing sites/areas, and develop action plans for quality improvement. These desk reviews will be completed by regular field visits to supervise implementation of activities, assess compliance with technical protocols and standards, assess beneficiaries’ satisfaction and complains, and monitor progress. Also, regular meetings will be held with MOH.

1 - Needs

Since 2018, Haiti has been facing a multi-faceted humanitarian crisis with deteriorating political and economic situation. This situation has taken a turn for the worse in 2019, severely reducing the average income and purchasing power of Haitian households, especially the most vulnerable. Civil unrest continued throughout 2019, with increasing levels of violence. These recurrent civil unrest episodes have paralyzed daily life and economic activities nationwide growing further the humanitarian needs.In January 2020 under UNICEF and ECHO funding, the MoH conducted a nutritional survey using SMART methodology. The prelimanry results released in February shows 6 % of Global acute Malnutrition (GAM), an increase from 4 percent (2016-2017 Demography and health survey/ EMMUS VI). Severe acute malnutrition has also increased from 0.8% in 2017 to 2.1%, slightly over the 2% emergency threshold. Global acute malnutrition is on the rise in four out of the 11 departments in Haiti including Port-au-Prince Metropolitan Area. Based on these results, an estimated 134,000 children under 5 will suffer from acute malnutrition amongst them 41,000 from its severe form. In addition, IPC analysis conducted in October 2019 at the national level, for the period from October 2019 to February 2020, 10% of the population analysed (1,046,000 people) is in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 25% (2,627,000 people) is in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis), representing approximately 35% of the population analysed or 3.67 million in need of urgent action. For the projected period, from March to June 2020, 12% (1,203,000 people) of the analyzed population is in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 28% (2,898,000 people) is in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis), representing approximately 40% of the analyzed population or 4.1 million who will need urgent action.
