Project Summary

The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 1.9 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 percent food insecure. Livestock production is vital in Syria to sustain food availability, food diversity/nutrition and income generation among vulnerable women and men affected by the drought-like conditions and prevailing economic hardships. The sector is facing many challenges that are forcing small-scale vulnerable households to adopt negative copying strategies which includes distress selling-off of their assets, subsequently losing a considerable source of income.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 212,500 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 1,275,000 people) in Syria through protecting and restoring their main production assets (livestock).
The objective of the proposed action is to protect the livestock assets of targeted vulnerable herders through the provision of vaccinations and treatment as well as feed for their herds. The expected project outputs are:
Output 1: Livestock productivity for 200,000 vulnerable crisis-affected breeders is maintained and improved.
This output aims at improving the availability of animal feed for the purpose of asset protection for 50,000 vulnerable households (250 kg of feed per household, in line with sector recommendations) as well as boosting milk availability through enhanced livestock production. Output 1 also seeks to provide veterinary services (vaccines and/or treatment) for 150,000 herders throughout the country.
Output 2. Improving animal reproduction for about 12,500 beneficiaries through artificial insemination and capacity building, contributing to increased livestock production.. This output aims at restoring livestock production through distribution of animals to small scale vulnerable households who lost their animals due to the drought-like conditions and prevailing economic crisis.
All outputs would include capacity building to enhance the technical skills of herders and technicians and to enable them to resume production in a viable, efficient and productive way and and better cope with the challenging conditions.

4 - Needs

The Food Security Sector provided record levels of assistance in Syria in 2021, the outlook for the year ahead remains dire since well over 50% of Syrians remain food insecure. Significant challenges continue to impact local food production in Syria and this pillar remains under-funded. Scaling up local food production, improved food systems and agriculture-based livelihoods, especially in rural areas, will be fundamental to sustainably reduce the humanitarian caseload and high food insecurity in Syria. The water crisis and economic difficulties have had a particularly devastating impact on the South and Central governorates as well as Northwest Syria and Northeast Syria. Furthermore, the harvesting season there is still a significant gap between available wheat and the national consumption needs. The macroeconomic conditions show no signs of improvement and it is likely that high food and agricultural input prices will persist. This will drive more people into food insecurity in the coming months. Based on the FAO Special Report published by FAO in December 2021, the livestock sector faces two principal challenges, namely animal production and animal health. The protection of the remaining livestock assets is essential for poor herders, particularly women headed households, who have partially or totally lost their herds. The provision of feed will help to preserve surviving herds particularly during the lean season from January to April, which is the most critical period.

Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (examples: IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth based their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees).2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establish a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring an effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation measures: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do not harm principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for the project.
