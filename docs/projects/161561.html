Project Summary

This project is to be implemented in Rann Town, Kala Balge LGA, Borno State targeting 1,095 households (HHs) (6,570 beneficiaries: 1310/girls, 989/boys, 2434/women, 1839/men) amongst IDPs and host population. The project focuses on rebuilding livelihoods to address resilience and increase capacity of the affected population to withstand future shocks.
Borno, and Kala Balge in particular, is impacted by large-scale displacement and re-displacement, killings, abductions, and rape This is alongside significant destruction and breakdown of social services, infrastructure including roads, markets, livelihoods and access to justice. The area also has a high level of poverty, low level of literacy and high mortality due to disease and conflict.
According to June 2019 Cadre Harmonise, most conflict affected HHs lost their assets and have limited income generating opportunities, which undermines their productivity due to a high reliance on asset depleting coping strategies like selling off their livestock to buy food. Displacement has meant that target communities lost access to economic assets and livelihoods and are therefore solely dependent on insufficient humanitarian assistance. In addition to the hardship the IDPs and host communities face, a lack of civilian authority in the LGA affects critical decision making, coordination and access to basic social services such as health, education, sanitation among others.
The porous border shared with Cameroon makes Kala Balge vulnerable to cross border population movement of conflict-affected persons and criminals. The LGA has seen the killing of hundreds of civilians, abduction of women and girls, destruction of villages, and large scale forced displacement of populations. Despite government forces regaining control of the LGA, its control is largely limited to Rann Town, and Boko Haram still has a substantial capacity to cause insecurity and inflict damage. During the rainy season, Rann Town is completely cut-off due to flooding between Ngala/Kala Balge LGAs, and only accessible by UNHAS flights. Freedom of movement is restricted due to curfew and security perimeter. Nonetheless, civilians do access markets in Cameroon (8km away). New vulnerable and/or displaced populations continue to arrive in Rann on a regular basis from insecure areas.
Tearfund will implement this project in conjunction with a resident local National NGO partner, COCIN Community Development Programme (CCDP). The project will consult beneficiaries appropriately in determining their priority needs in the recovery process. Based on current needs assessments conducted by Tearfund in August / September 2019, the project plans to provide requisite skills/business training and start-up grants to the target beneficiaries (qualified women, men and youth). In addition, beneficiaries will receive skills on the production of energy saving stoves/briquettes, and be provided with kitchen gardening skills/tools, seed capital and training on the management of VLSA to ensure sustainability.
Both partners will actively participate in coordination meetings through Kala Balge Local Coordination Group to review emerging issues and needs of conflict-affected beneficiaries. Tearfund is well known for localization and strengthening the capacity of local NGO partners, strengthening resilience, re-building/strengthening capacity, and contributing to durable solutions.
5% of this project budget is set aside to mainstream gender and protection pursuing the principles of accountability to affected people through interactive engagement with the target community. 600 vulnerable girls(250), boys(130) and women(250) will be referred to other agencies through inter-cluster referral system to benefit from other protection services.
Tearfund will put in place appropriate feedback mechanisms by giving beneficiaries a ‘voice’ through suggestion boxes, audio recording, telephone and dedicated staff contact staff on the ground.
