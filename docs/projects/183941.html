Project Summary

Kuwaiti patients Helping Fund (KPHF) proposes to assist vulnerable population affected by conflict, displaced and malnutrition by supporting 128 OTPs and TSFPs integrated including FBMAM & HF and 3 SCs distributed as follows: 31 in South Darfur State ( at the following localities: 2 in Al Radoum, 2 in As Sunta, 2 in Beliel, 3 in Ed El Fursan, 3 in Kubum, 2 in Mershing, 2 in Nyala Janoub , 3 in Nyala Shimal, 3 in Sharg Aj Jabal, 3 in Shattaya, 3 in Tulus and 3 in Um Dafoug), 20 in North Darfur State (at the following localities: 3 in El Fasher , 4 in Dar As Salam , 2 in Kutum , 3 in Saraf Omra, 5 in Tawila and 3 in Um Baru) ,25 in Kassala State (at the following localities: 5 in Reifi Hamashkureib, 5 in Reifi Kassala, 5 in Reifi Shamal Ad Delta, 5 in Reifi Telkok and 5 in Reifi Wad Elhilaiw), 16 in Red See State (at the following localities: 5 in Al Ganab, 5 in Dordieb, 3 in Haya and 3 in Sinkat ), 15 in Gedaref State (at the following localities: 3 in Al Fashaga, 5 in Al Galabat Al Gharbyah – Kassab, 5 in Al Mafaza and 2 in Basundah), 15 in Sennar State (at the following localities: 5 in Abu Hujar, 2 in Ad Dinde, 3 in As Suki and 5 in Sharg Sennar ) and 6 in Khartoum State (at the following localities: 2 in Jebel Awlia, 2 in Karrari and 2 in Sharg An Neel). The project proposed to treat 50,000 children <5 of age from SAM cases at OTPs and 7,500 children <5 of age from SAM with medical complications at SC. In addition, the project will treat 220,000 children <5 of age and 45,000 PLW from MAM cases. Moreover the project will prevent 70,000 children aged from 6-23 months and 30,000 Pregnant and lactating women from MAM. Beside that the project will fortify 25,000 children 6-59 of age who are not acutely malnourished through Home Fortification (HF) intervention service. Finally, the project will provide 90,000 caregiver with IYCF counselling. Malnutrition remains a critical problem affecting the North Darfur, South Darfur, Kassala, Red See, Sinnar, Gedaref and Khartoum States. KPHF will provide health services through SCs and nutrition services through OTPs and TSFPs. Nutrition services to cover SAM and MAM cases for U5 and PLW which include screening at admission, medical treatment of severely acutely malnourished (SAM) children with and without medical complication, dietary management and individual child follow up, among others. It will also cover the administrative (support) cost of case management of moderate acute malnourished (MAM) girls and boys of 6-59months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Furthermore, nutrition education and counseling will be provided for mothers/caregivers in Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) practices and hygiene promotion as well as MSGs will built to facilitate of nutrition education and contribution in MUAC screening activities. Selected trainings will be provided on SC case management, CMAM, NiE, identification of medical complications at OTPs and TSFPs level, community screening and follow up. In addition to, women club, women cornea and community awareness session campaigns will be conducted under nutrition program.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels with overall GAM levels of 13.6 percent. The situation is more critical in 64 localities that are classified with very high prevalence of acute malnutrition of 15% and above as per WHO emergency levels classification of which 9 localities fall into catastrophically levels i.e GAM 30% and above. The projected worsening of drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, deterioration in provision of Health and WASH services in 2022; increasing inflation while no significant improvement is projected on caring and feeding practices for under-five children. All these drivers will contribute to further deterioration of nutrition situation. Apart from acute malnutrition, the sector analysis revealed very high prevalence with over 36% stunting and micronutrient malnutrition affecting over 48% of under-five suffering from anaemia. Given the high risks of mortality and morbidity associated with acute malnutrition in normal and humanitarian situation, the sector has developed three objectives, interventions and targets as summarized below guided by envisaged response capacity. Approximately 522,000 children under 5 require treatment for SAM, and approximately 2.2 million children require treatment for moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). The prevalence of MAM among pregnant and lactating women is 6.3 per cent. The prevalence of underweight children in Sudan is among the highest in the world 12 under nutrition exacerbates morbidity and mortality, and contributes to underdevelopment and a weak immune system. Additionally, in the case of maternal nutrition approximately 883,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) suffer from acute malnutrition annually in Sudan. Also, one million mothers need appropriate infant and young child nutrition (IYCN) counselling services. In North Darfur, South Darfur, Kassala, Red See, Sinnar, Gedaref and Khartoum States the nutrition needs continue to persist in on the background of low coverage and access of nutrition services and inadequate response capacities. HNO 2021 showed that North and South Darfur States are the highest malnutrition rates with GAM rates ranging between 16 – 19 per cent and SAM between 3.7 and 4.9 per cent and Kassala has stunting rates above 30 per cent. The S3M 2018 showed that that the wasting measured by mid-upper-arm circumference (MUAC) was highest in Red Sea state its 29.6 % also severe wasting measured by weight-for-height Z-score, was highest prevalence rate in Red Sea state its 10.95 %. In addition, Red Sea state had a significantly higher prevalence rate of SAM, at 8.5%. Meanwhile, the high rates of obesity were also found in some areas in Red Sea, Sennar, Darfur states. Prevalence of fever was highest prevalence in South Darfur state it was 23.13%. Also the prevalence of GAM (HAZ 23.85% & WHZ 16.43%) in South Darfur is classified as critical, which is above 15% as per the WHO threshold for assessing severity of malnutrition. IYCF counseling’s massage coverage only 48.06% in Ed El Fursan and 33.96% in Nyala North, Exclusive breast feeding only 87.5% in Ed El Fursan and 73.33% in Nyala North locality. This is aims to reduction of mortality and morbidity associated with SAM and MAM among children and PLW. This aim will be done by treatment of SAM and MAM cases into proposed OTPs and TSFPs as well as SCs for SAM with medial complication. Moreover the project also aims to prevent the children aged from 6-23 months and PLWs from MAM through FBPM intervention also fortify children 6-59 of age who are not acutely malnourished through Home Fortification (HF) intervention service.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

KPHF recognizes the importance of mainstreaming protection in its projects in order to make sure that its efforts would ensure full respect for the rights of individuals and groups. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of IDPs, returnees and all groups especially the most vulnerable groups such women, girls and minority groups. While implementing all projects, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations which expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights. Through the Community Committees, partner and staff members, PHF work to foster trust and mutual understanding between various groups and to minimize conflict and reduce or stop violence. Also PHF focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding on the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity, gender and protection mainstreaming to ensure that vulnerable beneficiaries (women, girls, men and boys and minorities) are respected and feel protected.
