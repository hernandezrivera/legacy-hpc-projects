Project Summary

DRC proposes to undertake a life-saving and sustainable assistance to Disaster and Conflict Affected Communities (DACs) through provision of emergency and sustainable WASH interventions (water, sanitation and hygiene). Areas that are mostly affected by droughts, famine and floods will be targeted in the emergency interventions, some of the key areas to be targeted include the following Mogadishu, Baidoa, Baletweyne and mudug regions. proposed key activities to be implemented include water trucking through water voucher, construction of emergency latrines and other related WASH activities. DRC defines DACs as people who are internally displaced and are affected by drought and other factors and returnees seeking for reintegration. Therefore, DRC targets IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities. DRC understands that the primary displacements in Somalia is as a result of natural disasters and mana-made conflict. However, secondary displacements are now a common reality due to either military offensive or forced evictions by government. These displacements are the key driver of humanitarian WASH needs in Somalia. IDPs are currently residing in urban and peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to WASH services that raise protection and health concerns. Therefore, DRC will ensure that, where IDPs settle they get immediate assistance of life-saving interventions. These will include water trucking where there is no safe water source, emergency latrine, sanitation , hygiene kits and rapid hygiene promotion. On the other hand, DRC targets Host Communities - to ensure peaceful coexistence between the local population and target groups and also because many of them are equally vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. Returnees are also targeted with durable solutions in which case DRC will focus on governance and overall management of water and sanitation facilities. In this case, DRC will ensure there is a robust market system in place for continued service provision. For the case of water, DRC will encourage existing water source owners – both private and government, to extend water services to the settlements of DACs. For the case of sanitation, DRC will encourage a market-based approach – looking at creating demand for latrine construction and building capacity of the supply side to ensure enterprise-creation and therefore sustainable services beyond the project period
DRC has developed standard designs for water and sanitation facilities with detailed description scope of work. DRC also has a team of engineers and WASH staff across areas of operation. This provides a good basis for strong monitoring of project quality. The quality of program delivery will be monitored intermittently by the different staff frequently including the Area Managers of the respective areas, wash technical manager, National wash coordinator, Field Officers and dedicated M&E staff, the above mentioned team will develop master plan to guide the implementation, a pre-KAP survey will be initiated with the project to inform the current water, sanitation, and hygiene behaviors. Prior to any implementation DRC will organize project familiarization meeting with all stakeholders, and bilaterally with project beneficiaries to discuss project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, DRC feedback and complaint system, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles.
Furthermore, DRC wash technical team will assess the needs technically with close consultation of local authority, community elders and concerned cluster, together will develop outstanding Designs and BOQ for the needs, which includes but not limited to construction/rehabilitation of strategic water sources, sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion activities then procurement process will start to get eligible contractor who filled the selection criteria under continuous monitoring and supervision of dedicated wash team, after work completed as per technical designs/specification ,the village water committee will sign off on progress and completion of the project, witnessed (co-signed) by Village Chairman and local authority.
Furthermore, DRC will organize two joint monitoring visits with key stakeholders including Federal government, Local Authority, OCHA, other cluster members and independent monitoring agency to assess the project progress and beneficiary satisfaction on the project activities. The joint team will be provided all project documents and telephone contacts for community members benefiting from the project (especially numbers of the most vulnerable), such as CHPs, water management committee members, vendors, contractors and authorities and those that did not benefit from the project. As well they will be provided all direct beneficiaries contact details to allow them independent monitoring and easier contact. In addition, DRC will facilitate any Independent third monitoring required by OCHA and WASH Cluster.
In principle, all DRC staff are obliged to collect the telephone contact information of all beneficiaries as per DRC compliance policies, the same will be applied for this project. DRC to address the concerns of the beneficiaries has set up a comprehensive complain/feedback platform free of charge, where all beneficiaries can raise their concerns/complaints confidentially.
The reporting arrangements will be done in accordance with grant agreement, additionally DRC is committed to update the 4W matrix on monthly basis. Any other information’s requested by the donor and cluster will be provided on timely manner.
DRC will ensure that activities implemented with an approach of building back better (BBB), where the infrastructure rehabilitation or construction are done in such a manner that they have in inbuilt disaster risk reduction (DRR).

Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the most complex and longstanding emergencies in the world. In 2018, an estimated 6.2 million people, half of the population, will continue to need humanitarian assistance and protection, of whom 3.3 million will require urgent life-saving assistance. More than one-third of those in need are internally displaced persons (IDPs). Nationwide, access to an improved water source remains below 40%, with large variations from one region to another (e.g. Bay, Gedo and Bakool below 25% each). Most of IDP settlements drink un safe water due contamination by poorly design water supply and sanitation system.More than half of the population lack of access to adequate sanitation. The situation is particularly critical in Lower Shabelle, Lower Juba, Gedo and Bakool (below 25% each) and in IDP settlements. Latrine safety is worryingly high in all parts of Somalia, resulting in all categories of users being at high risk of violence when using facilities. Hand washing with soap at critical moment is practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability to a functional handwashing point is limited . the areas that most affected AWD/cholera are Banadir and Hirshabelle. In drought affected regions, water scarcity and poor quality are leading causes of population displacement and conflict, particularly in Puntland and Somaliland. In Sool and Sanag regions, respectively 69 and 35% of household need more than 30 minutes to reach their main drinking water points. Heavy rainfalls affected more than 700,000 people in 2018. WASH infrastructures suffered from important damages in more than 25 districts. Most of these infrastructures have not been repaired yet. Because of the floods and the chronical lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, Somalia continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2018 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots (Banadir, Hiraan, Middle Shabelle, Lower Juba and Bay).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

DRC is cognizant of the fact that, there are four (4) categories of people in need of either humanitarian assistance or durable solutions depending on their situation. In the context of Somalia, DRC targets Refugees, Returnees, IDPs and host communities. The targeting of latter group is done on two fronts - one is to ensure peaceful coexistence between the local population and target groups; and two is that many of the host communities are as vulnerable as the displaced population. However,DRC and other implementing partners in the area together with the local authority are in agreement to provide land to the IDPS. DRC is also keen to partner with other WASH agencies like UNICEF to improve Water and sanitation sustainability. DRC seeks to implies a sufficient clean water through (rehabilitation/construction of available strategic water sources like boreholes, shallow wells, berkad), appropriate sanitation facilities through (construction VIP latrines, sanitation tools and garbage system), Hygiene promotion campaign (maintenance of health, and prevent spread of diseases including hand washing, menstrual hygiene management and food hygiene) which is suitable for use by men, women, girls and boys of all ages including people living with disabilities. DRC seeks to engage in promoting public private partnership (PPP) in provision of services to ensure sustainability. For instance, for water provision, DRC will ensure that they support existing water utility companies to continue providing services and at a reasonable tariff, to enable returnees, host community and institutions pay include Gaalkacyo, Baidoa, Bosasso, Kismayu , Lughaya and sections on Mogadishu. These locations will therefore be given priority in ensuring sustainable access to WASH services.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The IDP status is often strongly correlated with extreme economic vulnerabilities and insufficient social network, where the extended family members and relatives are unable to provide sufficient support. Majority of IDPs residing in spontaneous settlements come from marginalized clans with unequal access to resources. Weak clan protection and lack of resources to support positive coping strategies, makes them vulnerable to secondary exclusion, abuse and extortion from power-holders with interest to commercialize aid. DRC will consult DACs during both project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation, through existing, well-functioning community structure, where needed improving their inclusivity, to give voice also to traditionally marginalized groups, women, members of marginalized clans, youth. DRC will ensure all beneficiaries are aware of DRC feedback/complain mechanism. DRC will adopt an area-based approach, ensuring the intervention benefits the whole community, regardless of displacement status and duration, and supports social cohesion, rather than further divisions. During mobilization sessions DRC field staff with sound understanding of the community dynamics will provide detailed information on vulnerability-based selection criteria. DRC has strong framework for advocacy opportunities, on the issue of evictions from IDP settlements and collective centres; and promotion of land tenure security for IDPs at the onset of an emergency. To ensure that provision of WASH services do not lead to conflict among DACs, DRC will ensure that there is a fair representation of all affected community members in management committees. DRC will ensure that the selection criteria of the beneficiaries of WASH services is agreed upon by affected communities and respective authorities to ensure that the most vulnerable households are given the first priority.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

DRC will ensure proper protection mainstreaming through the activities proposed in the response, aligned with other protection activities as well as the Action Plans between the FGS and UN to prevent and respond to grave child rights violations; FGS Gender Policy.DRC will ensure that specific needs of women, children and elderly are included when designing and implementing the WASH activities. WASH kit content will be tailored to those needs, supporting dignity, based on consultation and will include solar powered torches, to mitigate risks related to movement after sunset. Latrines will be equipped with lockable doors and enabling access to people who use mobility devices will be considered during their erection. Distributions plans for WASH kits (hygiene and sanitation) will include mitigation of most common risks, making sure the distribution sites are accessible and safe, risk of looting is minimized. Beneficiaries will be informed about their entitlements prior to the distribution and pictorial user instructions will be provided to ensure knowledge on use the safe use of distributed items. DRC will also reinforce activities that focus on women and their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes in conflict affected areas by leveraging women’s roles and leadership in expanding the humanitarian space, ultimately promoting community resilience, and furthering protection of civilians and civilian assets. DRC understands that, WASH activities to be effective, there is need for integrated approach. For durable solutions, land tittle deeds agreements will be priotrized by giving consideration to women, DRC will engage women in shelter, CCCM, WASH and protection activities recognizing that they (women) are key stakeholders in implementation of the project activities. Where cash for work related to delivery of WASH will be implemented, activities will be designed to allow participation of both men and women, as well as PLD.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

DRC is aware that, abuses against civilians, including wide spread sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), recruitment and use of children, indiscriminate and disproportionate attacks on civilian areas and infrastructure, explosive hazards and forced displacement, remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in Somalia. Additionally, natural disasters further affect the already fragile coping capacities. Most affected groups include women, children, youth and marginalized communities, especially the most vulnerable (older people, persons with disabilities, female-headed households etc.) Through the Protection Cluster DRC is spearheading the protection monitoring initiative – establishing a Somali-wide PM system that will monitor key protection concerns across Somalia to identify trends and patterns of protection risks. Based on this, DRC will further engage in joint advocacy with other protection actors, as well as the international community to address these protection concerns. DRC will support survivors of abuses especially GBV through case referral and case management and provision of psycho-social support. Child Friendly Spaces will be established to provide a safe space to children affected by conflict. DRC will also support community-based protection mechanism and leverage existing positive coping strategies in terms of protection. For the case of WASH facilities, DRC will ensure that siting is done with consultation with the camp committees as well as with inputs from women to ensure that these facilities do not end up becoming sites for rape.
