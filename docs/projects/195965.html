Project Summary

This project is to set up an innovation eco-system in Cox’s Bazaar to equip youth with design training, enabling them to develop solutions to the problems caused by environmental degradation and DRR/DRM including Nbs, upcycling plastic waste.
This would include work with youth in the refugee camp and youth from host communities. The innovation eco-system would:
-Provide foundation Humanitarian principle training and focus on mainstreaming GBV, disability, and CP trainings for all participants.
-Provide youth with the skills and knowledge they need to design solutions to a series of challenges
-Give youth the support to develop, test, and refine solutions
-Build a network of relationships between the youth and other actors to expand the impact of the solutions,
-Include co-creation as a means of joint innovation between youth and NGOs.
The aim would be to bring diverse stakeholders into the ecosystem to develop an integrated approach that empowers youth to develop solutions across different sectors.
Design challenges will be conducted in a cross-sectorial manner, to include challenges in which youth could apply design skills to develop innovative solutions. (ex, waste management, WASH, plantations and vegetation, sanitation, energy, upcycling plastic waste to building materials etc).
Components of a local innovation eco-system
Provide youth with the skills & knowledge they need to design solutions to a series of challenges.
D-lab’s design training curriculum is called Creative Capacity Building, CCB, MIT D-Lab. Creative capacity building (CCB) training are immersive, workshops that teach participants how to create low-cost practical solutions to problems in the community.

The participants learn and practice the CCB design process by identifying a challenge or opportunity in the community and then building a working prototype to solve that challenge or take advantage of that opportunity.
In the process, they learn basic woodworking and metalworking skills, how to gather information, try different approaches to a problem, give and take feedback as well as new strategies for working in teams.
CCB aims to build the capacity to innovate by taking participants through the design process to develop a practical concrete solution in teams. After which people have a week of refining their solution and then building their final prototype. Different CCB workshops focus on different problems.
MIT D Lab will work with a group of NGOs, refugees & host communities in Cox’s Bazaar to contextualize the CCB curriculum and train an initial group of CCB trainers. These trainers would be based in the innovation centers and run a regular series of CCB design training and refining sessions.
Give youth the support to develop, test & refine solutions
Innovation centers will be coordinated in a harmonized manner: physical spaces with tools and materials, staffed by 2 trainers and an innovation manager. The CCBs and refining take place in the innovation centers. The staff provides mentoring and ongoing support to the participants as they continue to work on their projects. Youth can test their solutions, get feedback and refine them.
Build a network of relationships between the youth & other actors to expand the impact of the solutions
The youth from different innovation centers will have a program of exchanges, between the refugee-based centers and if possible, the host community center. Youth working on the same projects can learn from each other to improve or work together. After each workshop and refining, youth can demonstrate their solutions in a community showcase.

Include co-creation as a means of joint innovation between youth & NGOs
Create a coordinated mechanism to bring together various stakeholders, sectors, and private and public sector participants to help create employment opportunities and also help support and scale ideas.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Bangladesh continues to host Rohingya refugees fleeing persecution in Myanmar. The largest influx occurred in August 2017, when an estimated 726,0001 Rohingyas arrived in Cox’s Bazar and took shelter in refugee camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts, joining other groups of Rohingyas who had arrived since the 1970s. The COVID-19 pandemic has aggravated a situation that was beginning to stabilize and take on a protracted nature placing stress on income and livelihood opportunities.Some of the many challenges have been around the following issues, when it comes to capacity building of youth around technical skills that can be marketable in Myanmar and also globally, focussing on DRR and Green skills. Overall vulnerability levels have remained alarmingly high since 2020 among Rohingya households. The latest findings showed that 95 %t of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance, similar to 2020 (96 %). These results reflect the slow economic recovery of an already fragile population with no income sources or livelihood opportunities. (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-5) – Summary Report) Self-reliance and livelihood activities ought to rely on better targeting criteria and consider the diversity of the camp and host community populations in terms of their different capabilities. Such programmes should have a lens on the childcare needs of single parents and disabled persons whose participation incurs a higher opportunity cost relative to non-disabled participants; and the effects of the trauma experienced by most people. All these are exogenous factors that could impede effective participation in self-reliance and livelihood programmes. (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-5) – Summary Report) Host communities and social cohesion Greater advocacy is recommended for livelihoods and self-reliance activities which strengthen linkages between the refugee and host communities. Evidence building on social cohesion between and within the two populations can assist in this regard. Incorporating conflict sensitivity and a social cohesion lens into every stage of the WFP response is recommended to introduce a social cohesion perspective across the programme cycle. (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-5) – Summary Report) Protection, gender, and accountability mechanisms An increase in theft and robbery has arisen from people’s lack of economic capacity to meet dire needs. Including community members in decision-making processes should continue or increase/be stepped up. Leveraging the existing community engagement systems in place, an agile community feedback and response mechanism should be developed to consolidate information from diverse feedback channels, act on the issues raised and close feedback loops effectively. (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-5) – Summary Report) . While the introduction of the Learning Competencies Framework and Approach and the Myanmar curriculum are likely to improve education quality for all children regardless of age and prepare them for future employment, opportunities for them to work and improve their quality of life remain severely restricted (J-MSNA Rohingya 2020). The biggest challenge raised by most participants, regardless of demographic group, was that there are not enough IGAs available. This was commonly discussed in relation to a desire for increased self-reliance. Those who had engaged in IGAs in the past said these opportunities are often short-term and ad hoc, with individuals working only a couple of days a month or less, especially for unskilled labour. This makes it very difficult for households to plan and make financial decisions such as improving their shelters or saving money for essential needs, such as food or healthcare. (Añárar Báfana Our Thoughts: 2021)

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection training is provided to all participants and volunteers. These trainings are core foundation part of the cirriclum to help maintsteam protection. Awareness activites and Design thinking challenges will focus around building community based protection solutions and content via youth particiapnts. During beneficiary selection priority will be given to the most vulnerable households such as persons with disability, vulnerable women, people with disabilities and the elderly, and the LGBTI community. Proper consent will be taken before data collection and confidentiality will be maintained as per implementing partner's policies. All participants and staff must take Protection mainstreaming, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, meaningful participation, community engagement, and accountability to most affected populations training, assessments, and performance review will be conducted around those criteria.

Youth will be engaged in strengthening and improving already existing referral pathways, with a focus on providing community geed back to sectors. Community engagement in every phase of implementation and monitoring of the project. HAP's own safeguarding policy & PSEA policy (sector approved) will be translated into every local language and sign language and disseminated among the project participants and every staff will be oriented as per the policy. A hotline number & complaint box will be disseminated and established respectively in project areas to address and deal with the complaints and feedback by the community people.
