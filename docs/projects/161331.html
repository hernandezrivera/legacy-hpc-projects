Project Summary

The role of women in peace-building as well as in conflict prevention efforts cannot be overemphasized. Their very important role in peace processes underscores the UN Security Council adoption of Resolution 1325 on 31 October 2000 and the development of National Action Plan by the government of Nigeria ensuring active and direct participation of women in conflict prevention and peace-building as well as post-conflict efforts, leading to the assigning of Gender Desk Police Officers across Police Stations.
Establishing mechanisms for consultation and collaboration with these groups can help strengthen a positive relationship that can play an important role in the fight against insurgency in North East Nigeria with specific contributions in understanding changes and challenges in the communities, strengthening police knowledge and capacity to address gender-based violence, gathering critical local intelligence on insurgent activities, and women’s active involvement in security matters.
This activity seeks to facilitate and strengthen dialogue platforms between women and Gender Desk Officers in Gwoza, Mobbar and Monguno communities in Borno State to discuss and share information on the vulnerabilities they face in their communities, incidences and early warning signs of violence.
The strategy adopted to improve engagement and dialogue between the police and women in the communities will include the following:
1. Consultative Meetings with Local Authorities: Meetings will be held with local authorities including District Heads and the police for introduction of the project’s goal, solicitation of their endorsement, as part of advocacy for women’s active involvement in security and peace-building initiatives at the community level.
2. Community Police Dialogue Meetings (CPDM): Two (2) separate dialogue meetings will be held, pre-assessment and post-assessment dialogue involving community women and Gender Desk Police Officers in Gwoza, Mobbar and Monguno communities. The dialogues aim to enable women share information on the vulnerabilities they face in their communities, their fears and perceptions about the police, as well as incidences and early warning signs of violence in their communities. The police on the other hand, will develop better working relationships with women groups in the communities and build confidence and trust for better information sharing and overall cooperation.
3. Formation of Women Security Committees (WSC): The dialogue will promote the formation of Women Security Committees in the communities who will include experienced women leaders from different community structures, including women experts, social workers as well as the police. The specific mandate of the Committees will be to liaise with traditional rulers, community stakeholders and the police on ways to reduce violence against women and to encourage women to report early warnings of conflict to the appropriate local authorities.
4. Encourage the Police to Develop Toll-Free Helpline for Intelligence Gathering: The dialogue will also encourage the police to develop toll-free helpline which will facilitate prompt reporting of early warning incidents of violence in the communities by community members for early response by the police.

GRA will leverage on its team of highly experienced gender, peace building and conflict management experts who have facilitated various similar dialogues on behalf of the organization to also facilitate this dialogue process. The dialogue will adopt and promote the Community Oriented Policing Ideology which supports collaborative partnerships between law enforcement agencies and the community members they serve, in order to proactively address the immediate conditions that give rise to public safety issues such as crime, social disorder, and fear of crime, and further increase trust in the police. The ideology will be contextualized to foster a cohesive relationship between women and the police in the target communities that will address perceptions, concerns and create women cooperation as it is necessary for the counter insurgency efforts in the North East Nigeria. The activity will use a combination of approaches to manage access by prioritizing regular local consultations with both the police and traditional rulers, as well as by localizing operations with staff drawn from the target communities, while maintaining a low-profile stance. The measures will enhance acceptance and familiarity within the local community and also forestall cultural and language barriers by way of local staffing.Based on the past experiences GRA learned from similar dialogues it facilitated, the above methodology will achieve the overall goal of the project.
