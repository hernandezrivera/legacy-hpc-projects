Project Summary

IOM will contribute to community revitalization and resilience through supporting local economic recovery, improving access to basic/social services and promoting community engagement and social cohesion. The project will strengthen access to livelihoods by creating income-generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery. This comes in line with both Early Recovery and Livelihood Sector 2021 objectives.
IOM will contribute to the rehabilitation and restoration of disrupted livelihoods by providing skills, income opportunities, and access to markets in camps and/or host communities for beneficiaries in NWS. IOM will improve access to markets by establishing new market areas in four Planned Camps. IOM will also provide financial or in-kind support to IDPs for entrepreneurial activities, particularly small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and community groups, to establish their business in the newly built markets. Additionally, IOM will provide vocational training and SME support business grants to vulnerable IDPs and host community members. Furthermore, IOM will provide income generating activity through Cash for Work (CFW) programmes. Lastly, IOM will improve the livelihood opportunities and community engagement for IDPs and vulnerable communities through the continuation of the Community Enhancement Center (CEC) and establishment of Community Engagement Teams (CETs) in Afrin, A’zaz, Harim, and Idleb sub-districts.

4 - Needs

Ten years of crisis in Syria have eroded the socio-economic foundations of the Syrian people. Simultaneously, the rapid depreciation and dramatic inflation of the Syrian currency, and new trade restrictions in and outside of Syria have exacerbated the economic situation in northwest Syria (HNO advanced copy 2021). According to the HNO advanced copy 2021, ERL needs remain considerable in scale, severity, and complexity across the country, with an estimated 11.6 million people needing dignified early recovery and livelihood support, which is an increase of 17.2% compared with the needs in 2020 (2021 Needs and Response Summary). The overall poverty rate is estimated at almost 90%, and the extreme poverty rate is estimated between 55% and 65% (2021 Needs and Response Summary), which highlights the acute needs of the Syrian people. According to the Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme’s (HNAP) Socio-Economic Overview Nationwide, 82% of IDP households reported that their monthly income was insufficient to cover their needs. This proportion is even more staggering among those displaced for less than 6 months, none of whom reported income sufficiency. The number of displacements also has an impact, with those who have been displaced 2 to 3 times at a greater risk of income insufficiency. Income insufficiency is also higher than average among female-headed households (87 percent) and very vulnerable households (86%). Moreover, the HNAP Mobility and Needs Monitoring report for January 2021 showed that 65% of IDP and 62% of returnees in NWS reported livelihood as their highest priority.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM’s ERL activities will include:• Conduct a comprehensive assessment to identify the market sectoral gaps and potential sectors for livelihood activities.• Expand / establish Community Enhancement Centers (CECs) and Community Engagement Teams (CETs).• Register, verify, and select IDP and HC beneficiaries for IOM’s direct livelihood activities (VT, SME and CFW). • Refer vulnerable households nearby the CECs to external livelihood opportunities through its Information Counselling and Referral Services (ICRS) methodology.• Conduct vocational training for selected beneficiaries matching labour market characteristics as identified by the market assessment, accompanied by business grants. • Conduct financial or in-kind support for entrepreneurial activities, particularly for micro, small, home-based enterprises and community groups, for selected beneficiaries matching labour market characteristics as identified by the market assessment, accompanied by business grants.• Provide CFW opportunities to selected beneficiaries and related to community public services and/or initiatives.• Implementation of Community Support Projects (CSPs) to improve access to basic/social services and markets in the communities through light construction/rehabilitation of infrastructures and/or provision of specialized equipment.• Establish Community Engagement Teams (CETs) and train the CET members on how to conduct volunteer work, improve their self-reliance, draft community initiatives concept notes, and engage with the community.• Support community initiatives to promote social cohesion in the selected communities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

IOM will ensure beneficiary inclusion and participation at all project stages, involving all gender and age groups, as well as community members with specific vulnerabilities such as PWDs. IOM will ensure that its staff are trained in and will mainstream protection principles in all components of the project, ensuring that all beneficiaries from all gender and age groups and PWD have meaningful access to services prioritizing their safety and dignity. IOM will ensure the principles of non-discrimination and 'do no harm' are followed and that there is meaningful engagement of affected populations to ensure that their input and feedback closely inform project design and implementation. IOM will ensure that the IP uses the proper distribution modalities for beneficiary preferences and dignified modality for individuals with disabilities and can mitigate the risk of diversion. IOM has robust mechanisms in place to ensure compliance with its AAP policy, including ensuring that beneficiaries have and are made aware of multiple channels of communication as part of IOM’s complaint and feedback mechanisms, which will be shared and distributed throughout the targeted communities to ensure the access of all community members to complaints mechanisms. The IP will use reachable and safe distribution points for all types of modalities ensuring separate lines between men and women during the distribution. It is the IP’s responsibility to inform all beneficiaries of the time and location of the distribution in a timely manner. IOM IPs will conduct sensitization sessions for all beneficiaries to share all required information about the project activities, their rights as beneficiaries, PSEA, and AAP principles.

Risks:1.Security situation in NWS does not allow IOM response activities to be undertaken.2.Local authorities are not in favor of IOM intervention.3.Local authorities influence on beneficiary selection/aid diversion 4.In kind support packages are not in line with beneficiaries' requirements, or support packages are sold to gain immediate cash assistance.5.Vendors lack the capacity requested to provide required items.6.Disengagement of communities during the assessment, planning and implementation stages.7.Social tension between the different groups resulted from the partial inclusion of community members. Mitigation measures:1.Establish a security and accessibility plan for the targeted areas, including a contingency plan to target alternative locations.2.IPs ensure local authorities are engaged from initial stage.8.Wider and transparent call for beneficiary registration by IPs. 20% beneficiary verification by IOM TPM monitors.
