Project Summary

Increased access to integrated emergency lifesaving healthcare services
focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality for conflict-affected and vulnerable IDP, host
community and returnee households in Eastern Equatoria, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Unity State . The interventions will include:
1. Provision of essential primary health care services targeting the most vulnerable populations through various clinical packages (Hospital, PHCCs, PHCUs, mobile clinics and ICCM)
2. Implementation of outbreak investigation and response for epidemic prone diseases in locations where CARE works.
3. Support to static Health facilities in Eastern Equitoria, and Unity States particularly on essential medical supplies and capacity building in line with the strategies and priorities of the health cluster
4. Mainstream accountability to the affected populations.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains unstable and volatile. Food insecurity, weak health services and hyperinflation are the results of this conflict with massive humanitarian needs unfolding. The recent signed revitalization piece agreement on 12th September 2018 brought hopes of ending the conflict that has devastated the country since 2013. South Sudan has one of the world’s highest maternal mortality rates of 789 per 100,000 live births according to the WHO health observatory (2017). There is a high burden of infectious diseases such as malaria, acute respiratory infections and measles, compounded by the presence of Ebola in neighboring DRC which has been declared by WHO as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (WHO, 2019). The risk for South Sudan has also been elevated from “high” to “very high” with 87 alerts having been reported to date. Although insecurity leading to destruction of health facilities and attack of health workers in Unity and Eastern Equatoria has reduced, frequent disease outbreaks have exposed the fragile health system existent not just in these two locations but also in other parts of South Sudan. With only 28.6% of the population living within 5 km of a health facility (Macharia et al, 2017), there are continuing efforts from both the Government, the UN, Donors and NGOs to improve access to health services. Drivers of the low access to health services include limited resources especially in rural areas, inadequately staffed facilities and low level of education. These factors contribute to delays in accessing lifesaving health interventions such as skilled assistance for delivery contributing to the high morbidity and mortality An IPPF baseline assessment in South Sudan (2018) established the following:- only 4.7% of married women or those in unions use contraceptive, with the unmet need of contraception being 26 for every 100 married or in-union women. - only 8% of people living with HIV have access to counseling and testing services and estimated PMTCT coverage is 11%. STI prevalence is very high at 13% for men and 19% for women while only 43% of health facilities provide STI services. - ANC syphilis positivity rate is also 11%. - Gender based discrimination and violence against women and girls is rampant with was found to be rampant with 7.3% of the girls are married before the age of 15 and 45.2% are married before they are 18 years old. Lack of resources across the country puts many at a disadvantage when it comes to access to health services, especially women. This is compounded by the poor state of transport infrastructure in many parts of the country making it difficult for patients to move from their location to health facilities. Distance to the health facilities coupled with transport challenges prevents many patients from accessing healthcare In addition to the above, an acute human resource shortage has had a huge impact on the delivery of health services in South Sudan. Many qualified health personnel have turned down offers to take up jobs in areas of conflict where they are most needed, thus depriving communities of health services. Because of the high staff turn over, there has also been need to ensure that training/capacity building is a continuing process. With this is also the need to ensure availability of medical supplies to all health facilities up to the last mile. This has been a challenge particularly during the rainy season when most parts of the country are cut off due to flooding and impassable roads. Some health facilities either do not provide key services due to lack of proper facilities or provide them under trees with no protection from natural elements such as the sun and rain. Consequently, patients can only be kept for a few hours usually for dehydration but without being admitted

2 - Activities

CARE will train staff on emergency preparedness and response and provide the required medical supplies and commodities including RH kits, vaccines and lab reagents. This will enable quick response in case of emergencies. CARE will also preposition supplies to reduce the amount of time required to mount an effective response. CARE will also improve vaccination in the locations where it works to reduce vaccine preventable diseases. To ensure the supplies are utilized well, CARE will train staff on rationale use of medical supplies and drugs. CARE will support Health facilities in the above locations as follows: - Pariang County: 1 Hospital, 4 PHCCs and 3 PHCUs and in Abiemnhom County, 1 PHCC, 3 PHCUs. The hospital will be the main referral health facility for Ruweng and neighbouring states. The Hospital provides comprehensive package of primary and secondary health services including Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal 24 hours of care and child health in addition to surgical services. The hospital has an inpatient department composed of a stabilization canter, pediatric ward, and adult wards. Furthermore, the hospital provides CMR and MHPSS services. The other 6 Health facilities refer patients to the hospital, in addition to 3 PHCCs within the refugee camp. In Abiemnhom, the 3 PHCUs refer patients to the Abiemnhom PHCC, which acts as the referral center within the county, providing a comprehensive range of obstetric, child health and curative services. In Wau, CARE will support a total of 9 Health Facilities (6 PHCCs and 3 PHCUs). In 2020, CARE plans to establish 1 mobile unit to cover the areas of Wau that have been hitherto inaccessible including Bazia, Beselia and Bagari. These areas are not currently covered by other partners and yet, as the security has improved, the populations have started coming back. In Eastern Equitoria, CARE will provide integrated mobile health services using 4 mobile teams that will be supported by 200 community health workers. The teams will work in 14 pre-identified locations within the former state and provide services to populations that live more than 5 km from health facilities. This will be in addition to acting Rapid Responder cases of emergencies within the state. In the above health facilities, CARE will respond to any disease outbreaks and provide scale up as per the need, improve WASH facilities and provide management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications. CARE will also strengthen referral pathways from the community to the health facilities. This will be particularly so in cases of GBV, pregnant women and malnourished children with medical complications. In addition to the provision of emergency services in the above locations, CARE will provide support in new emergencies within the former Unity State, Wau and the former Eastern Equitoria State when needed. In order to respond to the new emergencies, CARE will put aside a certain amount of money as Crisis Modifier to be able to respond quickly to the emergencies. The teams will be deployed to the various sites within the states depending on need and pre-determined gaps, and in consultation with the Health Cluster at the National and State levels. Each of the teams will be stationed in each site for between 2-3 weeks, before moving to the next site. The teams will provide health services as per the mobile clinic package and will focus on case management of selected diseases, immunization and capacity building of staff from the existing partners to enable smooth hand over. In addition, CARE will use the Community Health workers for the provision of Community Health Services including treatment of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea at community level and referral of the complicated cases. The community health workers will also be trained on emergency response and, in locations without health facilities, will provide alerts and communicate on any new diseases in the community.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

CARE, builds feedback, complaints and response mechanisms (FCRM) into allits interventions across all regions, sectors and projects. This is mainly topromptly get positive and negative feedbacks from the bene􀂡ciaries that CAREseeks to serve. Therefore, CARE instructs all the staff to respond or refer allcomplaints to the relevant focal persons promptly so that the issues areaddressed timely. A number of context based systems have been in place,including suggestion boxes, and management committees to accommodatethose who are illiterate. While the positive feedback are documented andreported for possible replication in future, complaints are categorized, actedupon and feedback on CARE would have addressed them provided to thecommunity. This system will be implemented in this project. furthermoreCARE will involve affected communities directly in planning, implementationand monitoring and evaluation of this project. CARE will deliberately facilitateformation of project management committees composed men and womenfrom the targeted communities. The committees will support CARE in planning,implementation and monitoring of all the project activities meant to serveaffected communities. CARE is highly gender sensitive when it comes toformation of these committees. Does the project include communityengagement in its monitoring and evaluation? The project is going to includecommunity members in its monitoring and evaluation through the projectcommittees. CARE will facilitate the establishment of project managementcommittees which will be trained on their roles and how they can support othermembers with monitoring and evaluation. In addition, the project will facilitateperiod review meetings with all beneficiaries to get feedback on the operationsand benefits of the project.
