Project Summary

World Vision Sudan intends to provide lifesaving shelter and Non-food items assistance for an estimated 8500 male and female people affected by conflict in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan State and Blue Nile states
Through this project, World Vision Sudan will respond by mitigating the impact of hazards through preparedness. Provision of timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance to Female and Male affected by crisis to reduce mortality and morbidity , mitigate health threats, and improving living conditions ,to support the transition to recovery by improving vulnerable people’s shelter solutions targeting male and female especially children, elderly , disable persons and pregnant and lactating women ,ill people , COVID affected people and etc.
The Intervention of aligned to the three strategic objectives of the shelter and Non-food items assistance and shall provide interventions to ensure that the 3 outcomes are achieved in the four targeted localities
Regarding SO1: To mitigate the negative impact of hazards, and ensure preparedness to people affected by crisis
World vision will respond by Sensitization of community member on best practices to mitigate the impact of natural hazards, conduct trainings with community members on construction techniques to mitigate the impact of natural hazards, prepositioning of life-saving NFIs in key locations for timely emergency response, conduct market assessments to inform planning of Shelter solutions and NFI.
For SO 2: Provision of timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance to people affected by crisis to reduce mortality and morbidity, mitigate health threats, and improve living
WV will support by construction and/or rehabilitation of collective shelters for crisis affected households in the targeted areas, Distribution of life-saving non-food items to crisis affected households, Distribution of life-saving emergency shelter to crisis affected households in the four targeted areas
Alignment to the SO 3: To support the transition to recovery by improving vulnerable people’s access to shelter solutions.
World Vision will distribute transitional shelter to protracted vulnerable households ,distribution of Cash for Shelter to protracted vulnerable households, distribute transitional shelter to vulnerable protracted households, distribute Cash for Shelter for vulnerable integrated and returnees households, training of beneficiaries on shelter material construction as a source of income generating activity, replenishment of life-saving non-food items to vulnerable households, Provision of Cash or NFI to vulnerable households
The project will be implemented directly by World Vision Sudan, but in partnership with WV local partners and donors. The intervention will be implemented over a period of one year.

1 - Needs

According to 2021 HNO, 1.9 million People in Need of Shelter solutions and life-saving Non-Food Items (NFI) assistance in in some 135 localities across Sudan. These localities include protracted and newly displaced people, returnees, and vulnerable host communities. The sector will target about 1.3 million people in 47 key localities. primarily in the South and West Kordofan, Blue Nile, East Sudan of Kassala, Red Sea and Darfur states. World Vision will target South, East, localities that are affected by natural hazard, such as annual cycles of flooding to support newly IDPs, protracted IDPs, returnees and other hazard affected populations to restore their dignity and reduce exposure to protection and health risks. Among all vulnerable populations, pregnant and lactating women, chronically ill female and male people, Female and Male elderly, unaccompanied minors, the elderly and people with disabilities are highly vulnerable without shelter to protect them from the elements and avoid exposing them to further health risks especially given the outbreak of COVID19 pandemicShelter and NFI assistance is required for Internally Displaced People (IDPs) – new, protracted, and integrated and newly accesses, refugees and returnees. Pregnant and lactating women, chronically ill people, elderly, unaccompanied minors, and physically disabled are particularly vulnerable amongst all affected populations who, in addition to their NFI needs, are highly vulnerable without shelter to protect them from the elements and remain highly exposed to further health risks ( SNFI Sector HNO 2021) Shelter solutions and NFIs - plastic sheeting, plastic mats, jerry cans, blankets and cooking sets. These items help restore a minimal sense of dignity and protection against exposure to the elements, mitigate health risks and provide some privacy and security to those in need. More sustainable shelter solutions for returnees and protracted IDPs affected by not only recent conflict but also natural hazards need to be addressed. World Vision will address the below needs through its activities in the targeted areas Shelter and NFI needs have increased in 2020, despite the decline in armed conflict since 2015, and corresponding reduction in new displacement. Estimate of the number of IDPs was revised upward from 1.6 million in 2019 to 2.55 million in 2020. World Vison will support male, female, boys and girls by providing them environmentally friendly, durable and locally sourced shelterOngoing economic crisis continues to be a key driver of deteriorating humanitarian needs, which have been exacerbated by COVID-19. Control and containment measures increase the cost and time taken to delivering shelter and NFI aid. World Vision will insure that COVID-19 mitigation measures are adhered to all activities as per the Sector guidance note 2020 to both male and female. Flooding is a seasonal occurrence in Sudan and contributes to shelter and NFI needs. This year was particularly bad as floods affected over 830,000 people across the country, with the highest water levels recorded in the Blue Nile in a century. World Vision will work in collaboration with other sectors and line ministries to mitigate the impact of floods and fireThere is a need for durable solutions (return and urban integration) for IDPs and returnees alike including a need to provide environmentally friendly transitional shelters, together with community training on building techniques and livelihood opportunities to promote self-reliance and resilience. World Vision will support by providing income generating and livelihood activities such stabilized soil bricks, woven grass mats, and sleeping mats and train the community on building. Based on finding from the 2020 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA), shelter needs - particularly roofing and shelters walls - are a major gap and WV will fill this gab in collaboration with UNHCR and partners

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WV mainstreams protection activities throughout its programming in several ways.1) WV applies the “Do No Harm” program analysis framework in each of our programs, ensuring that project activities do not exacerbate tensions and conflicts in the proposed program areas. Staff from all different sectors have been or will shortly be trained in this approach.2) When conducting routine community assessments and monitoring, WV also includes an analysis of protection issues to better understand the community’s special protection needs and concerns. For example, factors which might increase the exposure of women and young girls to violence when collecting water and firewood or using latrines and bathing facilities are identified in order to minimize risks in designing program activities. Special focus group discussions with women and other vulnerable groups are also routinely undertaken. 3) The results of these assessments are then used by the different program sectors to identify the most appropriate way of implementing activities in ways that enhance protection and minimize risk. For example, in the S/NFIs sector, bathing shelters are only constructed in central locations where the safety and privacy of women can be assured. 4) A proactive monitoring and reporting system exists to monitor, document and report on any protection violations such as threats, denial of access or violence upon users. These monitoring systems are put in place through community committees, which have been formed and trained by WV. Any violations of human rights are documented and reported to the appropriate authorities or monitoring institutions.5)Protection will be mainstreamed at both levels of the project cycle. The selection of the targeted villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNHCR. The project will ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. WV will utilize its current standard operating procedures for safety and security of personnel and beneficiaries to ensure staff and beneficiary security. All activities will be designed to be accessible to people with disabilities, the elderly women and children.. Each of staff, vendor, contractor, volunteers and other third parties in business with WV will have to sign Child Protection protocol to ensure adherence to WV Safeguarding (Child Protection) policy. Each staff is oriented on WV Code of Conduct as well as Red Cross Code of Conduct.WV will that COVID-19 mitigation measures are adhered to all activities as per the Sector guidance note 2020.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

World Vision (WV) recognizes that the essence of accountability is to respect the needs, concerns, capacities and disposition of those with whom we work with and to answer for our actions and decisions. Our intent is to contribute to changes that result in the improved well-being of children and communities. WV works with the most vulnerable to identify critical needs and seeks to ensure their right to receive assistance and protection on the basis of their informed consent. Globally, World Vision follows the 2010, Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines for accountability to affected populations and the organizations LEAP for rapid onset emergencies. In practice this focuses on 5 key commitments for how World Vision works with affected populations. Transparency: Provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected populations over information provision. Feedback and complaints: Actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breach in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. Participation: Enable affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence.Design, monitoring and evaluation: Design, monitor and evaluate the goals and objectives of programmes with the involvement of affected populations, feeding learning back into the organization on an ongoing basis and reporting on the results of the process Leadership/Governance: Demonstrate ommitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into country strategies, programme proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, partnership agreements, and highlighted in reporting. We also understand that working to implement these commitments can be particular challenging when humanitarian access is limited or absent and with the current COVID 19 pandemic where social distancing and other protocols to minimize infection will limit accountability activities, for example, communication and information flow can be intermittent and difficulties can emerge in ensuring participation or representation; Basic elements of an accountable response. Therefor we will work closely with other partners in the affected areas to ensure that a harmonized approach to the key aspects of HAP guidelines can be adapted.
