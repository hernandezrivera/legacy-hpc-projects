Project Summary

Sudan is among the top four countries globally with the highest prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) estimated at 13.6 percent. Following the conflict in May 2023, the acute malnutrition People in Need (PIN) increased by over 13% from 4.0 M in January to over 4.5M in May. The nutrition situation is projected to continue worsening in 2024 aggravated by the conflict, deterioration in food security, health and WASH services and the continuing displacement. As a result, the 2024 PIN is estimated to increase by between 10% and 30% in non-IDP and frontline localities respectively; with an overall additional 4.1% increase from 2023 PIN levels reaching over 4.7M in 2024, the highest PIN ever estimated in Sudan.
About, 76% acutely malnourished under-five children, pregnant and Lactating women in host and IDP settings live in 103 localities (54% of the total localities in Sudan). These localities are most affected, classified with severity scale 3-5 for WASH, Health, and Food Security; above national average acute malnutrition PIN and very high GAM prevalence of 15% and above- the WHO emergency threshold. Micronutrient malnutrition among children is also very high with 48% of under-five children suffering from anaemia most of which is due to iron deficiency. Minimum dietary diversity among under-five is one of the lowest trailing at 25% which is projected have deteriorated further following the ongoing massive displacement that has disrupted feeding and caring practices.
The proposed nutrition project aim is to improve equitable access to quality lifesaving curative and preventive services through the systematic provision of nutrition services that includes; Preventive nutrition interventions focusing on Maternal and Young child Nutrition (MYCN) & Infant and young child feeding practices, detection, referral and treatment of severe acute malnutrition with and without medical complications as well as supplementation of moderate acute malnutrition among under-fives, Pregnant and lactating women and girls , Management of Small and Nutritionally at risk infants under-six months and their mothers (MAMI) and Nutrition Impact and Positive Practice (NIPP).The action will implemented in South Kordofan, Central Darfur, North Darfur, Sennar, Blue Nile, River Nile and Al Gezira states in which 25 localities with severity scale 3-5 will be covered. Vulnerable children under-five and PLWs are the main target of the project. Through SC -Nutrition interventions, a total of 323,298beneficiaries will benefit from the services provided, among them (71,287boys, 87,129girls, 163,233 women, 1,649 men. To reach the estimated targets and planned response scale up, the nutrition sector will deploy integrated response modalities with health sector, WASH, FSL, protection, education at facility and community levels. SC will deliver nutrition services through existing health facilities and outreach teams and at community such as mother-to mother support groups activities including IDPs and hard to reach areas, use of cash based interventions in one prioritized location in SK to increase access and utilization of nutrition services through FSL will be used for most cases in need. In term of emergency response, rapid response mechanisms for timely delivery of nutrition interventions in the onset emergencies will applied. Specific nutrition response strategies will include scaling up the minimum preventive and protection lifesaving intervention package for prevention of malnutrition, treatment, and geographical coverage of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among the under-fives, strengthening referrals for children suffering from SAM with medical complication through provision of transportation costs to and from the SC, nutrition system capacity strengthening (capacity and infrastructure) will be done to facilitate provision of quality and people centered life-saving nutrition responses, timely delivery of core nutrition supplies.
