Project Summary

The project aims to reduce public health risks related to WASH by enhancing access for approximately 350,000 refugees living in 11 UNHCR-managed camps and nearby host communities. The WASH response will align with three key sector objectives to ensure: 1) consistent, sufficient, and equitable access to safe drinking water and water for domestic use; 2) adequate sanitation facilities that enable safe solid and human waste management; and 3) standardized distribution of hygiene items, targeting health-risk behaviors through community engagement, with a focus on preventing contagious diseases.
UNHCR and partners will ensure continuity of essential WASH services continue through combination of operation and maintenance of water supply networks and tube wells, monitored regularly to secure uninterrupted access to safe drinking water. In addition, UNHCR will continue to support the operation and maintenance of facilities providing sustainable sanitation infrastructure and facilities. Through integrated settlement panning and integration with shelter at household levels, access to sanitation such as household latrine and bathing facilities, especially for women and children will be enhanced to meet UNHCR and sector standards. Following the operationalization of the third large-scale treatment plant in Teknaf, the faecal sludge transfer network will extend to camps 24 and 25 in 2025. To address the anticipated gap in operational partners following BRAC’s cessation of activities in Camp 25 from July 2025, UNHCR has initiated discussions with ANANDO and Save the Children. Both organizations have expressed their capacity to address WASH-related gaps. Furthermore, during the first quarter of 2025, UNHCR plans to undertake several activities to upgrade the existing water supply system, ensuring rationalized water access and improved water and sanitation infrastructure. These proactive measures aim to facilitate a smooth transition of responsibilities under UNHCR’s coordination as the Area Focal Agency.
In solid waste management, efforts will focus on establishing a household collection system with source segregation to minimize waste in household vicinity and drainage areas. As such, UNHCR commits to improving waste collection, transport, segregation, and treatment through an integrated planning and community engagement activities fostering a sense of responsibility within the refugee population. UNHCR will also develop a data-driven waste management strategy to optimize sustainable practices, adopting a multi-sectoral approach and enhancing community involvement to foster responsibility within the refugee population. UNHCR and partners will employ innovative community engagement strategies to promote hygiene practices, providing a basic package of WASH non-food items, including soap and menstrual hygiene kits. Other priorities include water quality monitoring, WASH services in learning centers, health facilities, child-friendly spaces, and other public areas within the camps. Additionally, the project will emphasize emergency preparedness and response to disease outbreaks (e.g., acute watery diarrhea, cholera, dengue) and climate-induced events like monsoons and cyclones and fire outbreaks during dry seasons. UNHCR will also advocate for long-term solutions that address both refugee and host community needs. Throughout, the WASH response will prioritize gender, environmental considerations, and the inclusion of age and disability needs, ensuring equitable access for individuals with special needs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Significant efforts by UNHCR, the Government of Bangladesh, and WASH partners have enabled 89% of Rohingya refugees/forcibly displaced Myanmar nationals (FDMNs) to report adequate water access for their household needs. However, primary water sources varies by location, with Teknaf relying on surface water collected in temporary reservoirs, treated, and distributed via tap-stand networks. Despite these systems, seasonal water shortages continue to impact both refugees and host communities, underscoring the need for substantial investments in water infrastructure with support from government and development partners such as ADB and World Bank. In Kutupalong, groundwater from hand pumps and solar-powered chlorinated networks established between 2019 and 2021 serves as the main water source. While the latest UNHCR Knowledge, Attitudes and Practice (KAP) survey indicates that refugees in UNHCR-managed camps receive an average of over 20 liters of potable water per person daily, additional upgrades are essential to sustain solar-powered networks beyond their four-year operational life. Challenges remain in areas such as hygiene, with high supply costs and insufficient hygiene practices, as well as in solid waste management. In 2024, a household solid waste collection system was implemented, covering approximately 80% of the camps, with plans to reach 100% coverage by 2025. The WASH needs of persons with disabilities (PWDs) have been only partially met, leaving critical gaps in accessibility and usability. Communal latrines, shared by an average of 20 individuals across the 14 UNHCR-managed camps, meet the post-emergency target. However, overcrowding in bathing facilities exceeds recommended levels, particularly affecting women and girls who report limited access. This has led to makeshift bathing solutions inside shelters, increasing contamination risks and structural damage. Moreover, the large numbers of latrines require frequent desludging, posing health risks and driving up costs due to the need for sludge pumping, transport, and treatment. In 2025, UNHCR will focus on promoting environmental sustainability through climate sensitive actions, including alternatives to plastic materials, ensuring the long-term sustainability of groundwater resources, and maintaining water quality, ensuring adequate water supply, desludging of wastewater, solid waste management, access to WASH/NFI, and promoting hygiene behavior change. UNHCR will work closely with relevant institutions to improve the operation and maintenance of WASH facilities, aiming for lasting solutions that support resilience of both refugee and host communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming will be central to WASH intervention, ensuring that the WASH response promotes life-saving measures, safety, dignity, and security for all, with particular attention to women, girls, boys, men, and marginalized groups such as older adults, people with disabilities, and minority communities. UNHCR and its partners will conduct gender-sensitive quantitative analyses to monitor beneficiaries' satisfaction, participation, acceptance, knowledge, and practices. The WASH response will prioritize the most vulnerable groups impacted by protection challenges to ensure a comprehensive and inclusive approach. As in previous years, all WASH and partner staff will complete the annual training on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), facilitated by the UNHCR protection team. WASH GBV safety audit will be conducted to re assess the potential risk and implement GBV mitigation measures. PSEA assessment for all UNHCR partners has already been conducted, with satisfactory results emphasizing the commitment to protection standards throughout WASH interventions.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This project is fully aligned with the JRP Guidance Note on age and disability by prioritizing accessibility, resilience, inclusive WASH services, and community engagement throughout all interventions. Throughout the project cycle, age and disability considerations will be carefully integrated to ensure that all individuals, especially children, elderly persons, and people with disabilities, have safe and equal access to water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Recognizing the unique needs of children, UNHCR will focus on the safe siting and proper maintenance of WASH facilities—such as latrines and water points—to mitigate the risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, particularly in densely populated camp environments. During repair and maintenance activities, strict health and safety protocols will be implemented to safeguard children, ensuring they are not involved in labor or porter activities. Community engagement is a central component of the project’s design, providing persons with disabilities a platform to actively influence the planning process. This inclusive approach enhances resilience and ensures that the needs of persons with disabilities are fully integrated into all project outcomes, fostering a safe, accessible, and supportive environment for all camp residents.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This proposal is aligned with the WASH service needs identified by Rohingya refugees in the 2024 Inter-Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA), regular project monitoring feedback, and findings from the Knowledge, Attitudes, and Practices (KAP) surveys conducted by UNHCR across the camps. UNHCR will continue to uphold AAP commitments, emphasizing participation, partnership, and information-sharing, with a strong focus on feedback and action. Community consultations will take place throughout the planning, implementation, and post-project phases, through house visits, focus group discussions (FGDs), and key informant interviews (KIIs). Beneficiaries of all programs will receive training to clarify eligibility criteria, explain processes, and equip them with the knowledge needed to ensure the proper utilization of non-food items (NFIs). Household-level post-distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted to assess distribution effectiveness. Additionally, the project will promote information sharing and community feedback through the Common Feedback and Response Mechanism, providing the community with a platform to voice concerns and contribute input.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR continues to implement its activities with a strong focus on addressing existing climatic and environmental risks, as the refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar are highly vulnerable to climate-related disasters. In line with its strategic priorities, UNHCR is exploring programs and initiatives that contribute to climate change mitigation and the restoration of the fragile environment. The design and construction of WASH facilities take into account these climatic and environmental vulnerabilities. To date, UNHCR has taken several measures to enhance the climate resilience of water infrastructure in Cox's Bazar, including: Conducting preliminary topographic studies to avoid establishing WASH infrastructure in flood-prone areas.Installing concrete aprons around water points, such as tap stands and hand pumps, to limit erosion, stagnation, and flood damage.Burying water supply and sanitation pipes at sufficient depths to reduce erosion caused by water runoff.Performing long-duration pumping tests on boreholes to ensure sustainable aquifer use, preventing depletion during drought periods.Solarizing pump stations, where possible, to increase operational efficiency, reduce vulnerability to fuel price fluctuations, and minimize the carbon footprint.Implementing proper waste source segregation, especially of organic materials, to reduce methane emissions from landfills and promote recycling efforts.Raising awareness about the importance of not littering in drains to prevent clogging, reducing the risk of flooding due to waste accumulation.These mitigation measures will continue in 2025 as part of UNHCR’s long-term commitment to ensuring safe and sustainable WASH services in the region.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Given the high vulnerability of the refugee camps in Cox's Bazar to climate-related disasters, UNHCR has incorporated emergency planning to address potential crises such as flooding, fires, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), cholera outbreaks, dengue, and the WASH needs of potential new arrivals.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR, in collaboration with its partners, actively engages youth through thematic groups. These youth members undergo training to develop their WASH skills, empowering them to contribute to regular WASH operations as volunteers, as well as to emergency preparedness and response activities. Additionally, they participate in neighborhood meetings, assessments, and focus group discussions (FGDs), which serve as platforms for raising awareness and addressing issues within the camps.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, UNHCR's interventions aim to empower the refugee community to sustain water and sanitation services independently. The implementation of shared toilets, as part of the site planning for a limited group of families, fosters a sense of ownership and encourages self-maintenance of the facilities. Additionally, technical solutions, such as community management teams responsible for the operation and maintenance of WASH facilities—such as water supply, solid waste, and fecal sludge management—are nature-based and simple. These solutions are primarily operated by refugee volunteers with minimal supervision.
