Project Summary

This integrated education project by a national partner in South Sudan Education in hard-to-reach areas aims to ensure that children and youth (boys and girls) have access to quality education and those already in schools are retained in Nasir County (Upper Nile State); Fangak and Pig/Canal (Jonglei State). The following interventions are envisaged: establishing safe learning and recreational facilities in 12 locations – 4 in each County; teacher training to enhance the teaching/learning function supported in schools; providing monthly stipends to 120 volunteer teachers - 40 teachers per County; campaign for education, encouraging children, especially girls to return to school; and provision of school feeding programs to enhance attendance and retention at school. Response priorities will include gender-segregated WASH facilities, provision of learning materials, capacity building of School Management Committees (SMCs) and Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs), life-skills and recreational opportunities to support the recovery of children and youth to promote their overall well-being. Equal access to education will be prioritized regardless of gender, disability or ethnic background. Through introduction of school feeding programme, the project will seek to address one of the top causes of school drop-out. School feeding will help increase and maintain school enrollment and attendance, and reduce risks of death, disease and cognitive underdevelopment. School feeding will also improve protection of in-school and out of school children by keeping them away from potential recruitment into armed groups, child labour, early marriage, exploitation, sexual violence and abduction while on their way to school and back home. The project will seek to support cholera prevention through rehabilitation/construction of hand-washing facilities and latrines, provision of soap, hygiene messaging on cholera symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention to children and their parents. This will help to reduce morbidity and absenteeism, support retention in schools and children to help sensitize their communities. Crisis-affected youths and Host communities will be engaged in basic education and recreational activities in order to create an enabling environment for their critical development and positive engagement with the community. Youths will catch up on primary education through accelerated learning programmes (ALP) to improve their literacy and numeracy, technical and vocational training, and life skills. The project will seek to include physically and mentally challenged children; girls and boys vulnerable to recruitment into armed groups, and child labour; youths; adolescent heads of households; young mothers; and children from different ethnic groups. Teachers, SMCsand PTAs will be trained on life skills and referral pathways to enhance the protective aspect of education in emergency (EiE) intervention. The project will include early childhood development program across schools in the project sites. The projects will also targets host community children who will be enrolled in schools and some of the youths will attend ALP program, others as volunteer teachers. PTAs and SMC will be formed from host communities. Education services mapping (in partners, activities, locations, target groups, added value, breadth and depth etc.) will be done through clusters coordination meetings in targeted counties and states were ADA operate to ensure that there is synergy, cooperation, amongst partners and that duplication is avoided in all locations. The project targets at least 255 children (m=127; f= 128) in each of the 12 schools in the three projects locations.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian context analysisNasir County in Upper State is worst hit by South Sudan crisis which broke out in Dec, 2013. Most of its populations are living as IDPs in the settlements along Sobat and Gilo rivers, areas currently affected by floodinga week from now. Nasir is one of the thirteenth counties of former Upper Nile State with an estimated population of 307,887 based on South Sudan National Bureau for Statistics’ updated figures of 2008 population census.On the 7th – 10th Oct, 2019; many villages along Gilo and Sobat Rivers were flooded due to outburst of the rivers coupled with heavy rain falls. The populations were either wholly or partly displaced within the same village or to the other nearby villages. In total, six Payam, 31 villages,22,507individuals, 4,503 HHswere affected by flooding out of 54,186catchment population. Population figures gotten from recent general food distribution registration and South Sudan National Bureau for Statistics, 2008 population census updated. Among the affected populations, 4,501, are believed to be children under five years of age. Since there is no well documented IDPs and Returnees figures by Payam, it is estimated on average for both IDPs and Returnees that 15% off the affected population are IDPS and Returnees.The most affected villages which were visited during the IRNAwereMakak, Ballok, Jikmir, Nyakang, Benytik and Maker, some of these villages completely submerged under water and some partly submerged. In response to the flooding emergency, the Humanitarian partners in close collaboration with local authorities headed by ROSS State and County coordinated an Initial Rapid Needs assessment which was conducted in the most affected villages mentioned above on 11thOct, 2019.The agencies involved in the exercise were:ADRA, UNKEA, ADA, CHADO, NILE HOPE, ROSS, RI1. Education In many areas affected, schools were flooded children displaced, in Makak alone 453 children because they were displaced and their school is submerged in flood water. In other locations, such as Jikmir, schools become shelters for displaced persons in the community. When asked of community plan to open schools for children to resume learning, the school teacher and community member“right now we have nothing to do because everybody is struggling to find food and shelter for their families, and yet we don’t know when the water level will recede for our children to start learning again”. Unless there assistance from outside which can help establish Temporary Learning Spaces and Child Friendly Spaces in the displacement settlements.According to reach report Fangak county population is 110.134 and Pigi County is 99.068 population figure got from south sudan national Bureau for statistic, 2008 population census update. The recent may assessment figure shown New Fangak host number of 75,000 returnees from Sudan camp, Juba Poc and Neighboring states who are seeking available resource and also some are to settle near cantonment site in Door for SPLM/A IO which is bordering Panyikang and Pigi Counties.

2 - Activities

Activities Activity 1: • Establish 12 Temporary learning structures in 12 schools - 4 schools each in Nasir, Fangak and Pigi counties.• ADA will prioritize establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities and provision of school learning material. • In addition, recreational opportunities will be established and implemented in the 3 project target locations to support the recovery of children and youth to promote their overall well-being. Activity 2: • Provide a monthly stipend to 120 volunteer teachers providing education service to conflict affected IDPs, Host community and returnee children. All volunteer teachers will be provided with monthly stipend as a way of motivation.Activity 3: • Train teachers in life skills curriculum and materials, referral pathways, content of and basic pedagogy for primary education curriculum and inter cluster integration of protection, Health, WASH, nutrition and Food Security Above training will be done for teachers in Nasir, Fangak and Pigi county in identified venues especially during vacation when schools are closed. Activity 4: • campaign messages will be printed on banners, T-shirts, shawls and caps; and awareness will be created in churches, market places and through meetings and procession.• Encourage school enrollment and retention through a “Back to Learning” mobilization campaign, with focused messaging to promote the enrollment of girls Awareness • Provide young children with ECD kits • Establish and rehabilitate temporary learning space and classrooms.Activity 5• Carry out health, nutrition and protection referrals from cases identified in learning spaces to partners in the area of operation and ADA department concern.• Enhance screening for malnutrition status of boys and girls in supported schools in coordination with nutrition partners.• Establishment of registries for emergency school feeding including issuing of meal cards to children and school aged youths attending learning spaces. • Provision of Emergency school feeding to male and female children and school aged youths attending learning spaces managed by ADA in Nasir, Fangak and Pigi Counties

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ADA will carry out activities that aim at reducing and mitigating protection risks including the cases related to GBV, family separations and recruitment of children into armed groups. ADA will continue promoting an environment where protection and assistant are delivered in safety and with dignity. Risk, Vulnerability and Capacity Analysis: ADA will create a context-specific protection risk and benefit analysis. ADA will analyze relative importance (likelihood and impact) and manageability (prevention or mitigation) for different groups and individuals. Desk Review and Needs Assessment: Gather situational protection information on the major risks for the affected population (what and for whom), sources of risks, and any community-based or self-protection mitigation mechanisms. This information could come from school assessments, and feedback mechanisms. ADA will build a monitoring system and an accountability framework on the basis of identified protection risks and benefits. ADA will continue to ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all of its Education activities. land disputes will be refereed to (SSULA) south Sudan land alliance and authority to resolve the issue amicably to avoid violent. ADA is a protection implementer and will remain as protection actor to continually monitor the protection needs in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as youths, adolescents, child headed households especially female headed ones, separated and unaccompanied children, children engaged in worst forms of child labor, exploitation and abuse; and other most vulnerable children. In line with protection referral systems. ADA will continue to conduct comprehensive case management and PSS activities for especially vulnerable children. ADA will also continue to coordinate with Mobile response to take the available service nearer to the people in need and ADA will promote strong measures for data management.
