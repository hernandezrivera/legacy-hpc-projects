Project Summary

The project main activities include the rehabilitation of 21 drainage systems, construction of 70 latrines, and distribution of essential hygiene kits to most vulnerable people in Idleb governorate.
For the drainage systems, WATAN through this project will rehabilitate 21 drainage systems in the area of Atmah. This activity will ensure proper and safe disposal of the flooded water in 80 camps in Atmah, thereby this activity will secure safe access to those camps for any humanitarian aid such as water trucking, hygiene kits distribution and others as most of the aid workers are facing a great challenge in terms of logistics to reach those camps.
In terms of the latrines’ construction, this project will cover the construction of 70 latrines in the camps of Deir Hassan, those latrines are designed in doubles, one for males’ and one for females’ beneficiaries. Moreover, those latrines will ensure 1,000 IDPs have access to safe sanitation services and thereby promote good practices in terms of self-hygiene.
To enhance the resilience, WATAN through this project will distribute 1,000 hygiene kits to the same camps of Deir Hassan. Coming in parallel with the latrines’ construction, the hygiene kits will ensure safe usage of latrines and will promote self-hygiene as well.
The project subscribes to WATAN strategy of alleviating the suffering of people in need from Syria in order to maintain a healthy life and reduce the transmission of water-borne diseases.

Needs

On 26 December 2018, exceptionally heavy rainfall caused severe flash flooding in north-west Syria in Idleb and Aleppo governorates in North-west Syria. This area has a high proportion of displaced people and concentration of camps and sites, making it a region with a notably large vulnerable population. Hundreds of tents were reportedly swept away and concrete houses in camps collapsed in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. As a result of the flooding, thousands of people have been impacted.The results of this assessment showed a critical need for repair and rehabilitation of infrastructure and social services impacted by the flood. 74% of KIs reported partial damage to roads/streets as a result of the flood, and a majority of KIs (78%) reported a high priority need for repair/rehabilitation of roads/streets. The flooding impact has exacerbated pre-existing conditions and needs (INTER-SECTOR RAPID NEEDS ASSESSMENT - FLOOD IMPACT).WATAN field staff conducted a field visits to the camps in Atman and Deir Hassan. The needs identified are as follow:- Malfunction of existing drainage systems due to poor maintenance.- The flooding or destruction of latrines.- Some camps lack access to hygiene NFIs

Protection Risk Analysis

The project aims to target people living in the camps and identified as in need. The project will promote the gender and protection mainstreaming approaches through two key factors. First, in terms of the construction latrines, WATAN’s design for emergency latrines includes one room for males’ and one for females’ beneficiaries, this will ensure safe access to sanitation services for both men and women IDPs living in those camps. Moreover, the hygiene kits’ contents will meet the families’ needs, this includes men women and children as the hygiene kit design is developed specially to address those needs with a great background experience WATAN holds.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Septic tanks not being cleaned:- Coordination with the camp management for dislodging activities.Contamination of ground water with the wastewater from the septic tanks:- The design of the septic tanks includes cement, WATAN will ensure proper quality control for the installed septic tanks this includes permeability test.
