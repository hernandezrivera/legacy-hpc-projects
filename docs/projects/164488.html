Project Summary

According to the latest humanitarian need assessments in Syria, the number of food insecure people has increased approximately 20 percent from last year which reflects the deteriorated situation in NW-Syria as a result of the ongoing war. While the fact in the ground is harsher as the households in the conflict areas flee from their homes daily from seeking security in the safer areas which is mainly crowded by the IDPs. Also, inflation and currency depreciation pose a major threat to food security and livelihoods in Syria as well as the unemployment rates are believed to be at 50 percent, with only 16 percent of women working in the labour force.
The project aims to meet the food needs as an urgent humanitarian response in Aleppo province- Albab subdistrict through distributing 600 food baskets each month for 600 households for eight months sequentially. The beneficiaries in the intervention will be mainly from IDPs with a percentage of the host community of the most in need households in the target areas including the newly displaced in order to contribute to strengthening their ability to tolerate the situation through providing food assistance that meets their basic needs. The food baskets will provide the families with 2100 Calories which is equal to 100% per person per day as Sphere standards. The project location will be as the following:
Aleppo- Albab- Albab.
This intervention will target the families which meet the selection criteria and at the same time we are going to focus on the families that have not received any food assistant during the last three months. The food basket’s weight is approximately 83 Kg. Thus, the food baskets will be distributed to the IDPs to their living places. In addition, the food basket will be procured inside Syria in order to support the local markets.
This project has not been funded before.

4 - Needs

According to the latest HNO 2020, there are an estimated 7.9 million people are thought to be food Insecure with an additional 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity - Physical and Mental Wellbeing with 570.000 people are estimated to be severely food insecure. Also, Persistent and soaring levels of inflation and currency depreciation pose a major threat to food security and livelihoods in Syria (Living standards/physical and mental well-being). Wherein an estimated 70 percent of Syrians are spending over 65 percent of their expenditure on food. New displacements have been most frequent in northwest Syria to the Turkish borders.Albab sub-district in Aleppo governorate was received the highest numbers of newly IDPs in the last period. Wherein, the total population is more than 167366 persons with 25% of them are IDPs and the returnees reached 13.4%. The number of PIN is approximately 17301 persons. This puts the vulnerable population in the target area at an increased risk of food insecurity at the time where approximately all the fled IDPs are facing acute food insecurity and in need of immediate food assistance to be able to survive. MASRRAT conducted many visits and needs assessment in both sub-districts. Wherein, the results showed that the IDPs suffer from the general shortage of basic food items. Also, all the surveyed people assured that food is the biggest need for them next to the shelter.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will start implementation on 01 March 2020, The project duration will be 10 months wherein, the first month will include preparations, procurement and contracting then ten months will include distributions and one month for the closure and conducting the end-line assessment. The staff will be recruited directly to carry out project implementation. The project will provide assistance for 600 vulnerable families monthly for 8 months. The food basket will provide the family with 2100 Calories which is equal to 100% per day as per Sphere standards. The targeted locations will focus on the crowdy IDPs wherein Priority to these locations have been given because they have not been assisted by any organization during the last three months under the same intervention. At the beginning of the implementation of the project, MASRRAT's team will sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MOUs) with the local councils in the area. The process of engaging the Local Councils will start with advertising about the project in the communities of intervention, explaining the mechanism of selection, and the number of potential beneficiaries under this project. Advertisements of the activity will be published and will include selection priorities. The application form will include questions about basic personal information of the family. Data collection of the beneficiaries will be conducted at the beginning of the project (in the preparation month), and this process usually has no risks or any threats to beneficiaries. The data will be verified based on the criteria of selection. Delivery of food baskets will be according to paper voucher and according to distribution lists. Beneficiaries will sign on the distribution lists, and they will be verified through their IDs. The project will follow the procurement policy of MASRRAT, as the food items will be procured from local markets.

Mitigating measures: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. 2)- Protection risk: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality Mitigating measures: 1. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk management process designed to include all of project life. MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions according to the following Potential risks: Assumptions: • There will be permanent access to the project implementation area and the targeted beneficiaries. • There will be Cooperation with the local councils and other humanitarian agencies in the implementation of the project through sharing the data andselection of beneficiaries and others. • collaboration of the targeted families and stakeholders with the team and their participation in the implementation of the project. Risks and mitigations:• Increase in the needs: a new influx of IDPs into the targeted locations (mitigation: collaborate with donors/INGOs to cover any additional needs) • Lack of food materials in the region (Mitigation: purchase the materials from other nearby areas) • Increase in food prices during implementation the project (Mitigation: fix the prices with suppliers during the project period) • Deterioration of the security situation in the region (Mitigation: Postponing the distributions or changing distribution locations)
