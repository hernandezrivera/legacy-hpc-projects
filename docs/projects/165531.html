Project Summary

Burundi is estimated to have an annual cholera incidence rate of 4 per 1000, experiencing 19,943 cases with 758 deaths (Case Fatality Rate 3.8%) annually and around 4.9 million people living in areas at risk of cholera (Ali et al, 2015). Outbreaks have occurred every year since 1978, with the worst outbreaks reported in 2011 and 2013. The outbreaks recorded in 2017/2018/2019 have been primarily reported in the provinces of Cibitoke, Bubanza, Rumonge, Bujumbura and Makamba.
Burundi recorded the first case of cholera in 1978. Since then and every year, it has been reported resurgences of cases in the provinces along the Lake Tanganyika (also called the Imbo plain). As of July 2019, Burundi has already declared two cholera outbreaks with the latest still on-going and occurring during the dry season. This outbreak is a critical concern since it is not contained yet and new hotspots are still being identified.
The main objective of the project is to prevent and respond to outbreaks of infectious diseases through the provision of health and WASH services complemented with activities related to risk communication and community engagement through hygiene promotion. It proposes to build community resilience and address these issues through a coordinated and holistic response to affected communities including health facilities and schools.
