Project Summary


According to HNO (2021) Sudan, 4.7 million people are need of protection support across the country. This includes 1.5 million IDPs, 369,000 returnees, 1.1 million refugees and 1.6 million vulnerable Sudanese. Years of conflict and protracted displacement, lack of basic services and the deteriorating economic situation, have all had profound consequences on conflict-affected persons’ immediate physical and mental well-being, living standards, and long-term resilience. Vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities and chronic illnesses, face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, compounded by their displacement where familial/community support networks have broken down or are overstretched.
Despite Sudan having a transition government, the Darfur region still remains a post-conflict setting, characterized by fragile public institutions, challenged by criminality as well as sexual and gender-based violence/conflict related violence (SGBV- CRSV), a protracted humanitarian crisis and a lack of development. Land, natural resources and tribal differences remain at the heart of intercommunal conflict and constitute the key to Durable Solutions for internally displaced persons. However, land tenure and the management of natural resources are compounded by differing customary, statutory and religious legal systems of ownership and significant IDP displacements. Both make it difficult for the displaced to prove ownership and, consequently, are unable to receive compensation.
Political unrest and increased tensions cannot be separated from the underlying causes of conflict in the region, which includes competition over land and natural resources, ethnic and tribal rivalries, and political and economic marginalization. These conflict dynamics manifest themselves mostly at the local level that requires a peacebuilding approach that addresses drivers of insecurity and conflict at the sub-national and local level. Critically, women and girls, as the primary providers of water, food and firewood at the household and community levels, are generally highly dependent on natural resources for their livelihoods and are therefore particularly vulnerable to SGBV, localized tensions. Resulting changes in the availability of these resources, consequences to the women and girls, and to their family and community, can be significant in terms of physical, emotional and mental wellbeing and security. All these factors impact the justice process, and the stigma involved in cases of rape means that sexual violence and abuse are commonly unreported.
Furthermore, Sudan is amongst the countries hosting the largest number of refugees on the globe. Currently Sudan has been hosting South Sudanese, Central African Republic refugees, CHAD, Eritrean and now Ethiopian refugees. According to HNO 2021, Sudan has 1.1 million refugees. The Sudan Country Refugee Response Plan (2021) further confirms that refugees in Sudan need multi-sectoral interventions to address their specific vulnerabilities, bolster their self-reliance over the long term, and maintain and fulfil their rights as refugees. Therefore, investments in local infrastructure and strengthening gender sensitivity across all sectors, including protection, is needed to ensure that local service systems have the capacity to absorb the increasing needs of refugees and host communities, and further strengthen social cohesion and peaceful coexistence so both communities can thrive.
To respond to these needs, ARC (Alight) will support community-based approaches that enhance protection for all Persons of Concern and strengthen community-based protection mechanisms through community structures that identify protection concerns, raise awareness on protection-related issues, respond to issues at the community level where appropriate, and refer to relevant partners based on need.
