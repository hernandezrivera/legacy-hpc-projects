Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining WASH assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb - Across both Northeast and Northwest Syria.
Informed by a multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in September in 2023 on WASH needs at WoS level, MC will work on improving access to basic Water and sanitation services through the delivery of water trucking, SWM, Desludging and the rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure in IDP locations and areas with a high number of IDPs and returnees, and areas we anticipate significant inward displacement (Ar-Raqqa and DEZ, Dana, Daret Azza and Atareb in Idleb and north Aleppo). In NW, MC will continue providing a full WASH package camps and IDP sites, which includes Cholera related interventions, Water Quality monitoring and treatment and hygiene education. Hygiene education, using carefully tailored messages, and the distribution of hygiene kits will complement the other WASH interventions. Through ongoing Coordination with WASH cluster, working groups, local councils and other agencies, MC will be coordinating its response in relation to the gaps identified and associated needs. In 2024, a special focus will be given by MC to seek solutions for operation and maintenance (O&M), through support to local committees and water authorities directly after the completion of water supply rehabilitation. MC will work actively to identify and implement water trucking sustainable solutions and cost efficient sanitation management in the targeted IDP locations. Internal water supply networks, water tanks and toilets will complement more comprehensive HH shelter activities, providing safe and dignified accommodation standards for displaced and for those who have chosen to return. MC will also distribute multi-purpose cash assistance to conflict-affected households based on regional SMEB values, which include hygiene items.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

PSEA strategy: Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where MC and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programs. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures: (1) Increased inflation. MC will conduct market assessments in order to monitor for price increases. MC will coordinate with UN clusters/peer agencies and follow modalities agreed upon by clusters. (2) Key local stakeholders do not have the interest and/or capacity to fully engage in the project. MC will conduct a stakeholder mapping at the beginning to identify local stakeholders, who have the capacity and willingness to support interventions designed to promote resilience.
