Project Summary

The COVID-19 situation is already impacting some of the most vulnerable groups in the country including Cox's Bazar district. Further, loss of income for these vulnerable groups has led to the use of negative coping mechanisms risking long-term implications. These vulnerabilities (gender, disability, age, ethnicity) are anticipated to increase with time. To reduce the vulnerabilities of COVID-19, UNHCR, in collaboration with partners, is going to support 16,000 households across Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts surrounding the refugee camps. This 16,000 households will receive one-shot unconditional cash grant of BDT 4,500 which is equivalent to Food Assistance Package developed by the Food Security Cluster based on SPHERE STANDARDS. In addition, UNHCR, in collaboration with partners, is producing non-surgical cloth face mask in two registered refugee camps. Under this initiative, 100,000 masks will be produced by 100 skilled women.

Needs

The COVID-19 response is already impacting some of the most vulnerable groups in the country. Further, loss of income for these vulnerable groups has led to the use of negative coping mechanisms risking long-term implications. These vulnerabilities (gender, disability, age, ethnicity) are anticipated to increase with time. Cox’s Bazar is in lagged development. 377,411 people are under the poverty line, and 161,201 people receive the Social Safety net. The District Commissioner addressing humanitarian organizations stated that 700,000 Bangladeshis are likely to be affected by COVID-19 through the loss of livelihoods opportunities, increased prices and market and supply disruption. The livelihood projects beneficiaries are impacted by the movement restrictions and reduced economic activities. UNHCR, in collaboration with the partners, is supporting 6,000 vulnerable host community households through different livelihoods activities. The designed livelihoods activities are halted due to the lockdown. Therefore, people have lost their income and are at risk of productive asset loss. In addition, UNHCR is supporting other vulnerable host communities around the camps by providing LPG as cooking fuel and other material distribution, covering around 10,000 households, who are also impacted by COVID-19. Following risk and mitigation measures, most self-reliance activities in the camps have been suspended. While movement in the camps is limited, food distribution and health related activities requiring minimal social interactions are ongoing - highlighting a need for additional disease prevention initiatives. UNHCR, in collaboration with partners, is producing cloth masks in support of the health response - thus limiting the risk of disease spread while also providing continued access to self-reliance activities when many beneficiaries have experienced a reduction in household earnings.

Activities or outputs

Activity-1: Distribution of unconditional cash grant to 16,000 extreme poor and vulnerable host community households UNHCR, in collaboration with partners, is providing livelihood support to 6,000 households across Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts. In addition, around 10,000 host community households around the refugee camps are receiving LPG and other kind support from UNHCR. These 16,000 households of the host community are currently exposed to the COVID-19 impacts. The livelihoods project beneficiaries and other households surrounding the refugee camps are the extreme poor and vulnerable sections of the community, who are also the primary victims of COVID-19. They have lost their income and are struggling with their food supply. This group of people is also at risk of negative coping mechanisms (potentially at the threat of selling their productive asset). Under the lockdown situation, this vulnerable section of the host community population will receive an one-shot unconditional cash grant of BDT 4,500 equivalent to the Food Assistance Package developed by the Food Security Cluster based on SPHERE STANDARDS . Activity-2: Production of cloth face mask for the volunteers and frontline workers continuing working for the refugee responseUNHCR, in collaboration with TAI, is producing non-surgical cloth face mask in two registered refugee camps following social distancing and other measures. This face mask will be used by the volunteers and frontline workers who are continuing their critical activities in the camps. This intervention will also support the refugee women, who are engaged in producing the mask. At the COVID-19 response, all the self-reliance activities are suspended; hence they lost their cash earning opportunities. This short-term initiative will allow around 100 women to get involved with self-reliance activities.
