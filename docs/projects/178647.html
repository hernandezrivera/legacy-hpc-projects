Project Summary

The proposed activities depend on the need on the ground with strong benefit from Syria Relief’s presence in the same proposed areas, a deep understanding of the reality and need, and more than seven years of experience in WASH services and projects .. Therefore, the proposed activities will directly support the implementation of the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). The permanent water, sanitation and hygiene services for the conflict-affected population in the Idlib and Aleppo regions ranked high in the severity of the water, sanitation and hygiene needs.
This will be achieved through delivery of an integrated and comprehensive response to potable water, Solid Waste Management (SWM) and sanitation needs of approx. 184,535 individuals of which an estimated 76,083 IDP. Areas have been selected based on People in Need (PIN) and Severity of Need as presented in the draft Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2021.
. After ten years of the Syrian conflict and in line with the strategic objectives of HRP 2021, part of the proposed activities includes some sustainability solutions through the application of a hybrid energy system in some water stations or linking water stations to the electricity network in the areas that have electricity access, in addition to starting the application of a cost recovery system Fair and built on community engagement.
Hygiene promotion activities will continue to be offered in clustering areas, with a special focus on preventing and responding to COVID-19.
The intervention will cover the WASH needs as below:
Water Supply:
- Water station rehabilitation including install Solar panel to ensure the targeted beneficiaries have increased access to safe, potable water with 35 liters / person , and with a sustainable solution.
- Water networks fixing :To ensure that water services reach the largest number of beneficiaries and to reduce the negative effects of water leakage within the network or mixing of drinking water with black water ,
- Support of Operation and Maintenance of Waters stations .to ensure the targeted beneficiaries have increased access to safe, potable water with 35 liters / person , the amount per capita depending on the months , so it will be increased to 50 L/P/D during summer months
- Water trucking , This intervention will meet the daily needs (consumption and personal hygiene) of IDPs which living in IDPs sites , Everyone will receive 35 liters per day .
- Water tanks distributing , to address a current shortage, Women will be consulted in selecting the most appropriate location for the water tanks .
- Cost Recovery mechanism ,In line with the cluster recommendations and in line with each targeted area to reach sustainability in the water service
- Solid Waster removal , in communities and in IDPs site ,To ensure the mitigation of the negative and environmental impacts of the accumulation of garbage in the open and in residential areas
- Waste management operation cost , through provide the necessary operational materials for the SWM vehicles
- Sewage and drainage system rehabilitation.
- Toilets and showers constructing and fixing in IDPs sites , to reach the Sphere standards in #of latrines users ,
- Water water treatment plant , to exchange the black and grey water to irrigation water .
- Hygiene kits distribution, In line with the Cluster recommendations regarding the amount of soap and cleaning materials to ensure the continuation of the hygiene practices necessary to prevent Covid-19
-Hygiene awareness , At HHs level, to ensure that the infection is not spread during the sessions
The project will target men, women, boys, and girls from the three governorates, both host communities, and IDPs from within cities in those governorates as well as the surrounding areas or other parts of the city who live in the targeted neighborhoods.

4 - Needs

Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted in mid-2020 to inform the HNO 2021 These assessments included a representative, individual-level household survey that focused specifically on IDP camps and informal settlements in northwest and northeast Syria . The results indicate a slight increase in the overall water share received from the water distribution networks in 2020 compared to previous years. However, the results also indicate that the water chlorination level remains largely insufficient, and that many people are still forced to complement the water they receive from the network with other sources or to solely rely on these alternative sources. , According to HNAP assessment of PIN’s WASH activities in the area in Dec 2020, the PIN rate in Afrin 100% of total population, In A'zaz is 100 % also , While in Idleb is 90 %of total population ,in Apr21,SR conducted full WASH need assessment in Idleb and the key findings are :52% of respondents said that they need 20% to 35% of their monthly water expenditure ,through 35% that they need less than 15% of their monthly water expenditure.-63% of respondents do not have the ability to purchase all cleaning supplies, while 31% are unable to purchase cleaning fluids for the home.-48% of respondents declared that the most common way you disposed of garbage in the last 30 days was private garbage collection paid, while public garbage collection free was 18%, - 11% of the elderly do not have access to a toilet and 29% of the elderly do not have access to a toilet.-the reasons for the inability to access toilets are28% do not have enough facilities,19% inability to reach without assistance,19% has no drainage.

5 - Implementation of activities

Planned proposed activities will improve public health and mitigate against the risk of COVID-19 through ensuring adequate access to improved WASH services. Sustainability of WASH service provision is built into program design through the provision of solar system with the necessary technical support to establish cost recovery mechanism During first months of 2021, Syria Relief rehabilitated and provided support cost fro three water stations in Afrin, ensure access to safe water through water chlorination and training of water station staff on water treatment. When required, essential rehabilitation of water networks will be completed to ensure all households are connected to the main water network. More sustainable solution will be on the ground through install Hybrid system in water stations and through connect the water stations with electricity grid from Turkish side , That will help to reduce the cost of water pumping and reduce the Malfunctions With the electrical items in water stations , all of that will lead to have good chance for a successful cost recovery system.Recent technical infrastructure assessment have also identified the need for rehabilitation of sewage systems in many of the communities targeted, which will be implemented during 2021. Complementary hygiene promotion and sensitization activities will increase awareness and understanding of COVID-19 prevention and response, in addition to other positive hygiene practices. This component will include highly targeted distributions of essential hygiene kits and water storage tanks to the most vulnerable.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The 2021 Protection Risk Analysis for WASH highlights areas of risk for which we have already established effective mitigation measures. In line with the WASH sector,SR will ensure ‘Do No Harm’ principles are applied to identify and mitigate against potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities. We will ensure that SPHEREstandards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of project implementation, especially during emergency sanitation infrastructure implementation phase when the risk of GBV is higher. Measures will also be taken to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female‑headed households) to greater protection risks, and particularly to GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non‑discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. We also ensure that all staff sign code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or beneficiaries, including misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols related to PSEA, with all staff trained on PSEA and Child Protection.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: A member of the SR team uses their position of authority as a method of exploiting vulnerable populations. Their actions cause significant long-term damage to this individual or group. All trust between SR and the affected community is damaged. SR faces severe reputational damage and loss of funding. Mitigation: All staff who join SR Project are going to a thorough and transparent recruitment process. References are obtained for all staff during the recruitment process. The Project Team will sign SR PSEA and CSG Policies SR have developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to all SR staff and project benefciaries.SR PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk and vulnerabilities, etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specifc steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what SR will do when cases of SEA are reported)

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SR will create a risk register, in which it will identify relevant risks, conduct their analysis and their evaluation by identifying mitigation measures. This will allow to have understanding of the risks and agree on the internal controls to be applied and monitored. Overview of the risks and internal controls:The risk of fraud and misappropriation of assets and supplies due to inadequate financial controls, the lack of segregation of duties and personal integrity. This will be mitigated by having robust internal procedures with the adoption of checks and balances allowing the prevention and detection of fraud., monitoring teams from SR is present in the field to report irregularities by monitoring and documenting the delivery of activities and conduct post-distribution monitoring. A thorough stock management process is established with a field capacity to spot-check warehouses and conduct stock counts. A whistleblower hotline is available to all project stakeholders and they are aware of it early in the project to report any suspected act of fraud or corruption.An emphasis will be put on the risk of mishandling Personally Identifiable Information (PII) related to project beneficiaries, staff, contractors, vendors, etc. SR will ensure to adopt data protection and privacy measures and ensure compliance throughout the implementation. A particular attention will be given to data protection in case management due to the sensitive nature of its related activities.COVID-19 may have a negative impact and affects Programme implementation. To prevent the spread of COVID-19, staff are trained on COVID-19 protocols issued by Health, Protection and WASH clusters.
