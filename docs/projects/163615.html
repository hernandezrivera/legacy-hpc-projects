Project Summary

Through this intervention ZOA will effectively and eﬃciently respond to urgent and identiﬁed shelter and NFI needs in Dar’a governorate, and integrate interventions
wherever possible, between ZOA sectors (this project is part of a broader WASH/SHE/NFI programme) and also with other actors working in other sectors in similar areas. ZOA will prioritise communities with high severity of needs and where there is a high
concentration of IDPs and/or returnees. For household interventions ZOA will use vulnerability and selection criteria in line with Sector standards (to align with other actors), that are also discussed with and approved by local authorities and leaders. ZOA vulnerability criteria also incorporates analysis of protection concerns. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project and implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach and impactful support to households.
ZOA respond to ongoing urgent shelter and NFI needs as part of its winterization support, to ensure that households are better prepared to cope with harsh winter conditions. ZOA will provide winter clothing kits to 9,000 individuals. If possible, ZOA will implement its clothing support using an innovative approach that provides beneﬁciaries with choice regarding the items they receive. Further assessment and community engagement will inform the detailed structure, planning and viability of the innovation aspects of this activity. The project will contribute to beneﬁciaries feeling a sense of dignity and also needs being met more effectively.
Following assessment and approval, ZOA will distribute 3000 basic NFI kits to households with highest vulnerability and need for basic NFIs.
As part of its longer-term and resilience-building response, ZOA will also rehabilitate/ upgrade 100 shelter units. Detailed technical assessments will deﬁne the basic required rehabilitation to provide safe living conditions. Interventions will also be designed on a case by case basis based on technical assessment, within the guidelines of Syria Shelter Sector Standards. ZOA will also address key household WASH needs and include adaptations for people with speciﬁc needs, such as handrails and ramps. Where possible, ZOA will target multiple families in single housing blocks in order to limit the impact on other families and also to maintain social cohesion. Repair works will be conducted by local, qualiﬁed contractors/engineers to ensure safe construction practices, with direct oversight and monitoring from ZOA technical staff.
Through assessment and community engagement, which is part of all interventions, ZOA aims to identify speciﬁc needs of different groups, adapting activities as required in order to best meet needs. ZOA also has mechanisms in place to ensure that accountability and learning is captured and acted upon throughout the project cycle. This includes detailed and direct project monitoring, varied feedback mechanisms and project impact evaluation.

4 - Needs

Assessments continue to highlight signiﬁcant Shelter and NFI needs in Dara’a, especially for IDP and returning households. In Dara’a Governorate, 285.699 people have shelter needs, and in Dara’a district, 4 out of 8 subdisctrics are severity level 4 and 5 (HNO 2020). 58,278 people are in need of basic NFIs in the governorate, with a severity ranking of 3.Shelter is the top priority for communities in Dar’a (Assement, 2019) and remains a critical need across Syria (HNO, 2020). Signiﬁcant renovation needs and economic barriers are the main barriers to shelter (Assesmetn, 2019). More than 75% of households paying rent report being unable to afford it (HNAP, 2019). Deteriorating shelter conditions is a signiﬁcant concern for IDPs and the shelter situation in generally worst for IDPs and returnees. The numbers of returnees is expected to increase in 2020 and many returnee households in Dar’a return to shelter that is looted and/or damaged. For people with disabilities and older people, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities, a lack of shelter with adequate insulation, and a lack of appropriate adaptations such as handrails or ramps create dignity issues as well as other health concerns. Similarly, the lack of adequate shelter (particularly locked doors or adequate lighting) potentially poses privacy and safety issues for women and children who are at risk of violence, harassment or abuse. Most needed basic items have been selected through FGDs and past experiences. Beneficiary communities will be selected based on HNO needs, government priorities, and access. NFI support during winter is vital for coping with harsh conditions. Assessments show the varying NFI needs by gender and age, and the need for clothing and shoes has been highlighted as a primary concern for girls and boys (Assesment, 2019).

5 - Implementation of activities

ZOA implements this projecet directly, and targets communities with signiﬁcant IDPs, returnees & vulnerable HH using deﬁned vulnerability & protection criteria. Active participation & accountability within nterventions, promotion of social cohesion, direct M&E, feedback mechanisms and detailed technical review of completed work are core components of ZOA’s programming. ZOA works with relevant authorities to ensure correct approvals and coordination as part of all activities. -Rehabilitation of 100 shelter units: Technical assessments conﬁrm structural safety and inform tailored rehabilitation to meet basic, safe living conditions in line with shelter sector standards, address key WASH needs & adaptations for people with disabilities & older people. Works will completed by qualiﬁed contractors following a tender process, with regular and direct technical oversight and monitoring from ZOA staff. All works require technical sign off from ZOA before handover, and endline monitoring assesses whether repairs have effectively improved living conditions. Secured tenure through owner agreements is part of private building upgrade activities. - Basic Nfi Kits: Provided to 3000 households. The kits consist of 3 mattresses, 4 blankets, 4 pillows, 5 plastic mats, 2 Jerry Cans, 4 electrical LED lights, 2 heavy duty plastic sheets. In coordination with authorities, and following assessment of basic needs, selected households receive kits in line with shelter cluster guidelines and other actors in the area. -Winter clothing kits for 9,000 people: Quality assurance & acceptance of selected items. Family size is taken into consideration to address community sensitivities during distribution. Distribution method considers identiﬁed vulnerabilities and where possible provide choice to beneﬁciaries asa more dignifying approach. PDMs carried out during and after distributions & individual needs inform kit contents.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Market and exchange rate stability: While some products are still difficult to access in South Syria, markets across Dar’a governorate are largely functional. However, there is risk of volatility with the Syrian pound exchange rate which could not only affect prices of goods but also overall implementation as ZOA works through multiple currencies. As this project is heavily reliant on local supplies for rehabilitation and NFIs and on partner implementation within Syria, economic and currency markets will need to continue to be monitored and discussed with all suppliers as part of the procurement process.- Access and security: The proposed activities rely on access to and within South Syria districts. ZOA will adhere to GoS protocols and approval processes while observing core humanitarian principles and maintaining ongoing security and context mapping, security risk analysis and rigid security procedures. ZOA will ensure that all staff receive training on ZOA’s approach to safety and security in the field. ZOA will also maintain positive relationships with communities, local leaders and authorities as part of it’s acceptance strategy and access planning. - Availability of and ability to train staff: ZOA will hire Syrian staff and ensure gender equality wherever possible (in particular, aiming to ensure inclusion of female staff). Trained and qualified staff will also need to be willing and able to travel in South Syria.- Community members and local stakeholders accept and are allowed to participate in project activities: The success of ZOA’s project depends on active participation of community members and local stakeholders, facilitated by the key role of local authorities. ZOA will work within the parameters of GoS protocols in relation to beneficiary engagement through approved bodies such as Syria Trust. ZOA will define beneficiary engagement protocols based on its existing relationships with relevant authorities and it’s experiences.
