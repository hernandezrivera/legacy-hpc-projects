Project Summary

The multifaceted approach of the project will commence by equipping Rohingya refugees with non-formal technical training (120 Hours, 30 Days, each day 4 hour, each batch 15 Person, 150 BDT/Day training incentive) in different trade like dress making, jute and carpenters (wooden/bamboo handy craft) production on the basis of need analysis that includes gender analysis and age group analysis.. In parallel, the project will extend its service to vulnerable host communities by providing non-formal technical training in trades such trades. To create better scopes for inclusion, the project will ensure 12% persons with disabilities in its service provision. At the same time, the project will ensure more than 80% female beneficiaries so that they can be more confident and independent. The project will design and select business options ensuring engagement of community stakeholders and improve quality of the services through receiving feedback from the people regularly. Both in camps and host community, training will be commenced in a strategically located production and training center. Equipment/in-kind support will be provided to graduates of non -formal skill training beneficiaries in Rohingya camps for self-employment within the camp settings. Also, the graduated beneficiaries of the host community will receive in-kind support for producing the products by utilizing the skill gained from the non-formal technical training. In both community, a production cluster for certain products will be established during this phase, and business development training will give them know-how on product marketing within the camp and host community for the respective beneficiaries. Training and business development skills will enable the Rohingya beneficiaries to establish income generating options while they are repatriated in Myanmar. Alsao, the Rohingya beneficiaries will be able to create a source of increasing household resources. Simultaneously, the initiative will ensure that these acquired skills find resonance within the existing camps through diversified self-reliance opportunities. That will foster their potential for sustainable reintegration in the long run. The market linkage mechanism for the host community beneficiaries will use different market platforms, fairs and open markets also. The project will organize market linkage workshops with pertinent markets to promote host community beneficiaries' products in local marketplaces. An online platform will be utilized for the product sale, and the project will advertise the beneficiaries' products through this online platform named “Grassroot” Cox’s Bazar. Integral to the initiative is the incorporation of disaster risk reduction (DRR) and business development training for all participants. This facet goes beyond the immediate focus on technical skills, aiming to fortify resilience and adaptability. The seamless transition into DRR training highlights its pivotal role not only in safeguarding communities from potential risks but also in sustaining the livelihoods that the project endeavors to enhance. Furthermore, the project recognizes the significance of market linkages in sustaining the envisioned livelihoods. Workshops and an online platform developed by PROTTYASHI are poised to connect trained individuals with markets, fostering entrepreneurship and income generation. This dynamic integration of market-oriented strategies directly aligns with the overarching goal of self-reliance, empowering beneficiaries to actively participate in economic activities. Sustainability remains a cornerstone throughout the project, with a forward-looking perspective that includes the forging of public-private partnerships and integration with the formal education system. By interweaving these elements, the initiative aspires to create lasting economic opportunities, fortifying the self-sufficiency and resilience of both refugee and host communities in Cox's Bazar.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Cox's Bazar, an area in southeast Bangladesh struggling to accommodate the largest refugee community in the world, requires a focused strategy to livelihood and skill development. The strained local economy and livelihoods of the host population compound the region's vulnerability to natural disasters and food shortages, with over 925,380 Rohingya refugees living in overcrowded camps (UNHCR, 2022). The camp community is entirely dependent on aid, and the host community is likewise unable to participate in economic prospects because of resource constraints. In March 2023, the World Food Program (WFP) was forced to reduce the monthly amount of its life-saving food vouchers from $12 to $10 per person. On June 1, the WFP was forced to lower the voucher value for a second time, from US$ 10 to US$ 8, due to the continuous financial deficit. The Rohingya are totally dependent on aid from the international world in order to survive after six years of the refugee crisis. Their only source of support for meeting their basic dietary and nutritional needs has been general food assistance (GFA).The situation in the camps is already appalling. Even before the March ration cut, 12% of children were acutely malnourished, and 41% of children suffered from chronic malnutrition. Any additional reduction in the ration is likely to cause acute malnutrition to surge suddenly. It is clear that permanent solutions are needed to give income-generating opportunities for both Rohingya and host community populations to arrange basic family needs by taking into account effective reiteration, especially in light of budget difficulties that are resulting in decreases in life-saving food vouchers. In order to meet the urgent requirements of these communities, trade-based non-formal skill training, wage job opportunities, business development opportunities, and market linkage support for the host community are essential all the components are bringing them towards income generation. Even though Cox's Bazar is linked to many humanitarian actors (ISCG, 2022), the emphasis has to switch to resilience-building skill development. Non-formal technical training, such as handicrafts, can impart practical skills that generate money, promote independence, and lessen reliance on outside assistance. Women in these communities face more challenging circumstances when it comes to earning a living because, without their empowerment, the community as a whole cannot be empowered. Economic inclusion will be improved by addressing vulnerable groups like women and making them priority participants in skill development programs. In this context, their ability to provide wage employment possibilities through skill development training is essential for ensuring economic stability. Initiatives that match the skill sets of the host community and the Rohingya people can be established to create sustainable employment and strengthen the local economy. As the LSDS is focused on long-term economic stability, these programs seek to enable individuals to launch and maintain their businesses. Facilitating access to markets ensures that the skills acquired through training translate into viable economic opportunities, fostering growth and sustainability.To sum up, in order to ensure the resilience and self-sufficiency of the Rohingya and host community populations in Cox's Bazar, a shift towards livelihood and skill development activities is imperative. These activities should include training in handicrafts, creating wage employment, providing business development training, and providing market linkage support through the host community. These programs support long-term stability and prosperity in addition to meeting current needs.

The project will ensure an efficient coordinated response, and the activities will follow a unified approach to fulfill the diverse needs of the POC while maximizing the community's potential. The project has already collaborated with different sectors, particularly the guidance of livelihood and skill development sector (LSDS). To avoid duplication and enhance the impact of interventions, planned to implement similar activities using proven methods and best practices consistently across in both communities by consolidating the services in a geographically contiguous area, example is other partners have initiate to provide hand craft training on camp-15 and this project have planned to provide bamboo craft making training in camp-15 and also there are scope for further collaboration. Another example is for selecting beneficiaries the project will ensure the coverage in such blocks where are presence of similar activities will not be found. PROTTYASHI is aiming to create a holistic support system for refugees and host communities by encompassing its other programs in the same area. Simultaneously, the project is committed to preventing the duplication of service provision for a particular individual through coordinated engagement with various sectors, working groups, and focal organizations. This approach ensures that a maximum number of people have access to humanitarian support without unnecessary repetition of services. The project's interventions will be based on carefully planned efforts, maximizing beneficiaries within limited resources through a cost-effective approach. This structured strategy aims to support the community over a specified period without alterations to the planned services. Additionally, the project will follow fundraising aspects in a structural manner to prevent any interruptions in the implementation of planned activities. This approach aligns with the principles of predictability and continuity outlined in the Rohingya Refugee convention.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will be implemented by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all groups of people involved with the project, including persons with disabilities, and the project supports will be made accessible to all with special provision of the facilities as needed. The protection context will be analyzed to ensure safety, dignity and access throughout the program cycle. The project will assess the potential risks such as physical and psychosocial risks that may affect the safety of the people involved and address those risks immediately to avoid any potential harm. To foster accountability, a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism for the targeted people will be established to provide an immediate response. The project will ensure meaningful excess of the vulnerable people through identifying and addressing specific needs, vulnerabilities, and capacities, tailoring assistance accordingly. Additionally, it will prioritize the inclusion of marginalized groups, such as women, children, and persons with disabilities, to ensure their voices are heard and their needs are met throughout the project implementation. The project will prioritize women's empowerment so that women can raise their voice for their rights and participate in decision-making processes. The project will protect women from the potential of sexual exploitation and abuse, through establishing strict protocols and guidelines for ensuring a safe and secure environment for all participants. This will include background checks for staff and volunteers, as well as conducting regular monitoring, providing training and evaluation to identify any potential risks or vulnerabilities. Furthermore, the project will collaborate with local authorities and organizations to create a support network that can provide immediate assistance and protection in case of any incidents or emergencies. As a response mechanism, the project will use the referral mechanism in Cox's Bazar if required.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project interventions are designed to cover all age groups and persons with disabilities with its services directly or indirectly. The project will identify persons with disabilities including children using Washington Group Questions (WGQ). Strategies will be taken to ensure inclusive and equitable access to project benefits all groups of people. Different training trades will cover different age groups, and this will involve incorporating specific measures to address the unique needs and challenges faced by people with disabilities, as well as considering the varying capacities and requirements of different age groups. By adopting these strategies, the project aims to promote equal opportunities and maximize the impact of its interventions across diverse populations. Additionally, it will provide Sector specific inclusive programming training for staff & volunteers. The project will keep the children away from the labor-intensive interventions and allocate the support proportionately. Livelihood support will be extended to households in such a way that children and elderly people get greater access to food security. This will be reflected in the project planning, beneficiary selection, service provision, and impact evaluation. Apart from that, the project will involve them in disaster risk reduction (DRR) activities and provide them with training and resources to build their resilience against potential hazards. The project will also plan for conducting accessibility audit in facilities and service points

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project was designed and planned after a thorough discussion with the community, utilising tools like FGD and KII, and learning from existing project feedback mechanisms to identify their unique needs. These FGDs and KIIs are being held with different age and gender groups to carefully incorporate all the insights. By incorporating the opinion of the community, the project will ensure the participation of the community in the implementation phase, like selecting the training venue and time of the training, which will be fixed with the discussion of the community. Additionally, PROTTYASHI's dedicated MEAL team will be solely responsible for monitoring and responding to complaints received through the proactive channel (hotline system) and the reactive channel (CFM box). For activities like distribution, the project will engage the community with greater effectiveness in selecting the items and the process of distribution. In a nutshell, mainstreaming AAP will involve ensuring the involvement of impacted people in project design, planning, implementation, and evaluation. Accountability will be put in place by directly seeking client satisfaction and taking measures to improve the level of satisfaction. Through a periodic participatory review and reflection (PRR) toll, the project will prioritise the concerns of the targeted people and initiate modifications to the strategies of the project implementation when needed. The project will strengthen the capacity of the targeted people based on their needs. At the same time, targeted people will also be oriented on the AAP measures.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will implement measures to ensure that all staff and beneficiaries are aware of the risks of sexual exploitation and abuse and know how to prevent and respond to such incidents. Training sessions will be conducted to sensitize staff and beneficiaries on appropriate behavior, reporting mechanisms, and the importance of creating a safe and respectful environment. Comprehensive programs will be implemented to equip staff with the necessary skills and knowledge to handle sensitive situations effectively. Additionally, regular monitoring and evaluation will be carried out to assess the effectiveness of these measures and make any necessary adjustments to further strengthen protection efforts. The project will establish a clear reporting mechanism and guidelines for handling any cases related to PSEA that may arise, and it will maintain the confidentiality of individuals who come forward with complaints or concerns. As a response mechanism, the project will use the referral pathway to connect survivors of abuse or exploitation to appropriate support services, such as counseling, legal aid, or medical assistance. This pathway will ensure that survivors receive the necessary care and assistance they need to recover and rebuild their lives. To increase awareness of PSEA, posters, fliers, etc. will be disseminated throughout the community and in key locations, providing information on how to recognize and report instances of abuse or exploitation. Additionally, the project will raise awareness on the importance of safeguarding and promote a culture of zero tolerance towards abuse and exploitation. These efforts will help create a safe and supportive environment for all individuals involved in the project.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The outlined project is carefully tailored to seamlessly integrate disaster risk reduction (DRR), disaster risk management (DRM), and environmental and climate change mitigation measures. The primary activity is to empower the targeted population with knowledge and strategies for both pre-and post-disaster periods, ensuring the protection of their valuable resources and assets. The project will maintain alignment with the ISCG’s Multi Hazar Response Plan 2022 and National plan of Disaster Management (2021-2025) in its disaster risk mitigation strategies. A significant aspect of the project involves educating the community on the importance of maintaining an undisturbed and environmentally sound habitat, even in the face of development interventions. To actively address environmental pollution, the project will implement mitigation activities. Notably, there will be minimal use of plastic or any non-degradable products during all phases of the project activities. In essence, the project goes beyond theoretical education, incorporating tangible measures to mitigate disaster impact and uphold environmental integrity through a practical and community-engaging solution. In ensuring the assets of the beneficiaries, the project will initiate measures in line with the sector’s EPR and contingency plan. The initiatives will not contradict the got’s standing orders on Disaster 2019.
