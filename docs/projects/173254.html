Project Summary

"The Rohingya are likely to stay for several more years, with no clear perspective of a safe return to Myanmar and no access to sustainable livelihoods in Bangladesh, it is unlikely that the mass of people will return in a reasonable timeframe to Myanmar.
HEKS is operating projects in Bangladesh since 1971 and is registered by the NGO Affairs Bureau. HEKS is Shelter/NFI catchment focal for camp 14, 15 and 16. HEKS implemented TSA (Camp 15 & 8E), emergency response and COVID NFI support on those camps from 2019, HEKS has constructed significant settlement infrastructures in camp 15 and is aware of the areas this infrastructure is still missing. and have very good knowledge of the situation and good working relation with SMS, CiC and other actor working on those camps, for 2021 HEKS aims to expand the working area in camps 14,15, 16 and 8E and host community.
HEKS will improve settlement infrastructure which includes the construction of stairways, Construction of pathways, a mixed approach will be used for implementation contractor and cash for work both, According to SMSD sectors approved site development catalogue V2.
Close coordination will be held with CiC and the Site Management Support Agency for sites selection in the camp and with local authorities in the host community to determine the appropriate implementation locations. HEKS/EPER will prioritize based on the remaining needs all over the four camps and in the host community.
A mixed approach will be used to receive complaints as per CWC Working Group recommendation. Real-time feedback will be collected by the volunteers during household visits. a help and complaint raising desk will installed at HEKS field office. Beneficiary complaints will be recorded systematically and individually followed up by the HEKS/EPER team. All activities will be monitored by independent M&E team. Findings will be used to improve the activities."

1 - Identified Needs

"The camps have been established fast and people had to be hosted with minimal preparation. During the first two years, planned and spontaneous paths were stabilized mostly by sandbags which prevent paths to turn into mud after rains. Stairways were constructed either with sandbags or with bamboo. These solutions are short-term and quickly break due to abrasion through mere utilization, erosion during rain, moisture, and infestation by insects. Many paths and stairways, which were built for the 2018 monsoon, turned back into muddy slopes and paths during the 2019 monsoon, which limits the movement of Rohingya and is a serious hazard. Since mid-2019 lots of progress has been made by constructing more stable infrastructure, According to MSNA 22% Household reported that their infrastructure got worse (roads, pathways, bridges) since the COVID-19 outbreak.In the host community close to the camps infrastructure is not much developed. The host community has been affected strongly by the Rohingya Crisis. Many have lost access to the lands they farmed previously, often government forestland, and many have seen their wages decline or have lost work opportunities to Rohingya refugees. Forest products on which many previously depended are no longer available.’ The current COVID-19 crisis has a significant impact on the host community, as movements are more restricted around the camps than in the rest of the country. Making a living has become even more difficult. Whereas the Rohingya are continued to be supported by food distributions, the host community will face huge difficulties to cover basic food needs from what they earn. The JRP demands to support the host community even without the COVID-19 crisis with ‘cash-for-work for community disaster resilience building - infrastructure development and restoration, forest restoration, and natural resource management.In Rohingya community women are excluded from decision-making systems, and their lack of access to reliable information, consultations and feedback mechanisms limits their ability to influence both prevention and response plans. The inclusion of women leaders, networks and volunteers in the planning, implementation and monitoring of response plans will have a strong impact on making these right-based strategies, inclusive and effective. HEKS will ensure the most vulnerable households, including female-headed households, still have safe access and support to make improvements of their surroundings"
