Project Summary

The IRC will continue to build and consolidate on the gains achieved through OFDA, ECHO, SIDA and NHF/OCHA funding in response to the protracted crises in the North-east which has resulted in influx of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and negatively impacted host communities in the BAY states. The IRC seeks to strengthen provision of comprehensive primary healthcare services including and reproductive healthcare services for internally displaced persons and host community members through direct treatment at IRC supported health facilities both in IDP camps and selected government health facilities in all the IRC targeted LGAs (Maiduguri, Monguno, Akira-uba and Gwoza, Bama, Mafa, Konduga, Magumeri LGAs in Borno state, at Yobe at Damaturu, Gulani and Gujiba LGAs with potential to expand to Adamawa LGAs.)
In addition, the IRC will further strengthen the provision of health care services by improving the referral system to access secondary care and strengthen community based surveillance and optimize community engagement through working with community health workers increate demand for service utilization and increased coverage.
The provision of integrated primary health care services which includes reproductive health service targeting adolescent girls and boys, women of child bearing age, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), family planning, post abortion care, clinical management of rape for all identified cases of gender based violence, (GBV) ante-natal services, post-natal services, and syndromic management of sexually transmitted infection (STIs) services and psychosocial support.
The IRC, in the case of a disease outbreak will be flexible in preparation and response to respond through its existing Country Emergency Team (CET) adapting its programming to effectively respond with support of existing community structures and propositioned medicines and supplies/products.
The IRC hopes to reach a total of 302, 475 individuals disaggregated as follows men 72,594 women 108, 891 boys 48,396 and girls 72,594

1 - Needs

The armed conflict in the North East of Nigeria, and particularly in Borno state, remains active and unpredictable. This situation has arguably been made worse by the new military tactic of Super Camp formations which has left large numbers of communities unprotected. The International Organization of Migration (IOM), Emergency Tracking Tool (November 2019) revealed that conflict, among other factors, resulted in a total of 4,238 people being internally displaced with 13,976 returnees, arrivals were recorded across the 3 BAY states. New arrivals continue to put pressure on the already overstretched infrastructure and available health services in internally displaced person (IDP) camps and host communities. Access to healthcare services continues to be limited. According to the 2018 Health Resources Availability Monitoring Systems (HeRAMs) report, 33% of the assessed health facilities in were partially damaged in Borno state. Physical barriers, distance, lack of finance and inadequate number of qualified healthcare workers also contributed to the limited access to lifesaving health care services for IDPs and the majority of host community members. The most prevalent morbidities identified at these camps are malaria, acute respiratory infection and fever of unknown origin. At all three camps, the IRC continues to be the sole provider of primary and reproductive healthcare services not only to the housed IDPs but also to host community members. Access to secondary/tertiary health services for medical emergencies for IDP population remains a challenge in the proposed IDP camps. Access to secondary/tertiary health services for medical emergencies for IDP population remains a challenge in the proposed IDP camps. There are only three existing hospitals in Borno available to provide secondary or tertiary healthcare to IDPs. Referral systems are weak, this is attributable to a lack of efficient ambulance services, the high cost of medical services at the referral centers and human resources dedicated to support clinical management at the secondary and tertiary centers system in Borno state. Ambulance services are also not readily available to transport patients in need of secondary or tertiary healthcare to referral centers. Currently, the IRC has Memoranda of Understanding (MOU) with the University of Maiduguri Teaching Hospital (UMTH), States Specialist Hospital, Umaru Shehu Hospital and Mamman Shuwa Memorial Hospital for referral of patients with medical emergencies who are in need of secondary and tertiary health services. Due to limited in-patient capacity at UMTH and the large catchment population, the IRC signed MOUs with additional hospital to address the caseload of patients in need of referrals and to reduce the high numbers of in-patient admissions which results to the turning away of referred IDPs due limited bed capacity at UMTH.Heath conditions in the targeted LGAs have also been affected by the rainy season which started in the month of June and peaked between the months of August-September in Borno, which resulted in widespread flooding in Maiduguri and Monguno LGAs, with IDP camps most affected by the floods. With the rains subsiding, and the malaria season starting, it is expected that communicable diseases will peak in the period October- December. The Health Sector Bulletin (September 2019) revealed that a total of 50,148 consultations were made in reporting week 39 of 2019, with malaria being the leading cause of morbidity and mortality. Confirmed Malaria were 9,164 with 45% of reported deaths due to malaria.The IRC conducts regular and continuous desk reviews of assessments by trusted partners such as OCHA, IOM, WHO and REACH, in addition to conducting its own assessments. From the IRC internal assessments carried out; it revealed that the BAY states continue to suffer from Malaria and pregnancy related complications as the commonest morbidities and limited access to healthcare prevalent across the BAY states

2 - Activities

Through the provision of quality primary and reproductive healthcare, the IRC intends to reduce excess morbidity and mortality among children under 5 and women by treating and protecting them from communicable diseases and pregnancy related complications.ACTIVITY 1: Affected IDPs and host community members have access to quality primary and reproductive healthcare services. Primary healthcare consultations with a focus on the common morbidities, such as malaria, acute respiratory tract infection, and acute watery diarrhea, will be provided by skilled clinicians using standardized treatment protocols (Integrated management of childhood illness protocol and MSF clinical guideline) to children under 5 and to adults. Routine immunization will be provided to children under 5, and pregnant women will also be given tetanus toxoid vaccines to protect them from vaccine preventable communicable diseases. Mosquito nets will be provided to individuals who attend the health facilities focusing on mothers of children under 5 and pregnant women as measures to protect them from malaria.ACTIVITY 2: Affected IDP populations have access to quality healthcare services at secondary and tertiary centers. All IDPs and host community members accessing the IRC health facility that present with an acute emergency whether medical or surgical will be first assessed, then given parenteral pre-referral medications and referred to appropriately to a secondary or tertiary referral center. Using the IRC ambulance, as well as rental ambulances, patients will be transported to referral centers accompanied by referral assistants. At the referral centers, IRC referral officer will receive all referred patients providing them with support to ensure that they are admitted and all documentation and logistic regarding to the hospital admissions are handled. Meals will be provided for the admitted patient and care-giver, medicals bills will also be paid for by the IRC. At the end of all hospital admissions, the referral officer will ensure that they are transported back to the referring health facility to ensure that follow up care is continued and referral looped closed outACTIVITY 3: Through antenatal clinics, focused antenatal care services will be provided to all pregnant women who attend the IRC supported health facilities. Basic investigations to rule out complications of pregnancy such as anemia will be carried out for all booked pregnant women, including screening for sexually transmitted infections. Iron supplements, calcium lactate and intermittent prophylactic treatment of malaria including provision of tetanus toxoid vaccines will be provided to all pregnant women attending the ante-natal clinics. Health education sessions will be conducted during antenatal clinics to reinforce the importance of positive dietary habits during pregnancy, and the importance of antenatal visits during pregnancy. Community sensitization will also be carried to educate the community members of the importance of antenatal visits and having facility births.ACTIVITY 4: Women and girls use services that treat physical and mental consequences of gender-based violence (GBV) and prevent further harm. 9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions specifically with women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums to collect beneficiary feedback on quality emergency response actions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, suggestion boxes and community engagement meetings/focus group discussions will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serve as reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews will be conducted to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allow the IRC to make rapid and informed adjustments to the project. Safety audits will also be conducted each month with women and girls in the IDP camp to understand the protection risks. Advocacy strategies will be developed together with other partners, community structures, and female beneficiaries, to respond to identified needs.
