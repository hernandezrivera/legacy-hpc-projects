Project Summary

The project is designed to address the mental health status of the returnees and non-displaced populations of Ninewa, Saladin, and Kirkuk by strengthening the integration of mental health within the package of services of the primary health care centers. The cause of the problem is weakened service delivery through PHCCs because of their destruction and lack of funding to their staff and logistic needs. The project targets 10 PHCCs from Ninewa, Saladin and Kirkuk which will be selected to be the target for the activities. Through the capacity building, service provision support and supervision, the integration of the mental health service will be promoted.

Needs

Iraq is a developing country that went through multiple crises which affected the delivery of mental health services and postponed the development of an efficient system that could meet the emerging needs. Only one national survey had been conducted in Iraq in 2006/2007, The Iraq Mental Health Survey (IMHS) of 2007, with 4332 respondents who showed that anxiety disorders were the most common class of disorders (13.8%) and major depressive disorder (MDD) the most common disorder (7.2%). IMHS also found that 16.5% of Iraqis have suffered from a mental health disorder during their lifetimes. Only 2.2% of IMHS respondents reported receiving treatment for emotional problems in the 12 months before interview, including 23.7% of those with serious, 9.2% with moderate, and 5.3% with mild disorders and 0.9% of other respondents. The survey found that the prevalence of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) was 3.6%, lower than what one would have expected given the ongoing conflict. After 2006 many crises had occurred and in 2014 more than 3 million people have been displaced which might have led to increasing the percentage of psychiatric disorders. In addition, Iraq is facing a great shortage in the number of available psychiatrists and for every 250k Iraqis, there is only one psychiatrist.IHAO had conducted an assessment for the mental health facilities in Mosul which showed that there is a lack in three main categories which limit the provision of the services, and these are: - Capacity building,- Logistics,- Infrastructure.The Mental Health Care Section in the Ministry of Health classifies mental health care units in the PHCCs into three types:Class A: Trained medical doctor, space, and good service.Class B: Trained medical doctor, space, and fair service.Class C: No trained staff or medical doctor, no or available space, no service. (3) In Mosul only and according to the director of mental health care units of Ninewa Directorate of Health, there are 35 functional mental health units and 8 of them is considered class A. In most of the major primary health care centers in Iraq, there is a separated room for mental health service provision and in minor PHCCs the room is usually shared with school health or vaccination and this might decrease the need for building new spaces for mental health service provision. Through the assessment that IHAO implemented, the importance of establishing and strengthening psychosocial support in the PHCC was clearly stated as the largest proportion of people with psychological complaints need social support rather than medications. According to the interviewed psychiatrists, providing the full package of MHPSS is the most helpful approach which means that there should be a trained medical doctor and PSS service in the PHCC.-------References: 1- The prevalence and correlates of DSM-IV disorders in the Iraq Mental Health Survey (IMHS), Al Hassnawi, Monaf Al Jadiry et all, World Psychiatry. 2009 Jun; 8(2): 97–109.2- Healing Iraqis, the challenges of providing mental health care in Iraq, MSF,2013, page:43-Assessment of mental health needs and Plan for action for Ninewa governorate in Iraq, Dr Abdulmunaf Al Jadry, July 2018, Iraq Health Access Organization.

Activities

Based on the assessment that was conducted by IHAO in June 2018 in coordination with the International Health Department in the Ministry of Health and the Ninewa Directorate of Health, IHAO will choose 10 PHCCs in Ninewa, Saladin and Kirkuk. Selected centers will be supported by developing the capacity of the medical staff and providing logistic needs such as printing, gasoline, stationery, and others. Those trained staff will work together in the mental health units of the PHCCs to provide MHPSS service. Step 1 – The trainingsExperienced trainers who had worked in a relevant context and who are experts of MHPSS will be selected to train the staff of the selected PHCCs in two-level trainings after consulting MOH.- The first level training will target the nurses to develop their communication skills, identification of psychiatric disorders and knowledge about the basic psycho-social support which will enable them to work as social workers in the selected PHCCs, especially the minor PHCCs that doesn't have a social worker employed by Ninewa DOH. - The second level training will target medical doctors and university nurses to train them on Mh-GAP and other guidelines that will help them to deal directly with the cases that need a mental health expert.All the trainings will be in coordination with MOH and based on the adopted guidelines of WHO and other UN agencies.The criteria for selecting the trainees:- Nominated by the DoH.- Had worked or is working in a mental health unit in a PHCC.- Shows interest in applying what will be learned during the trainings. Step 2 – The provision of MHPSS service: As the project will focus on enhancing MHPSS services of the primary mental health care units of PHCCs. With respect to the IASC intervention pyramid for MHPSS, IHAO will work on the third layer, Focused and non-specialized support, which will be provided by trained general practitioners and social workers in the mental health care units of the primary health care centers. 1- Mental health consultations: A trained general practitioner on Mh-GAP and other guidelines is to provide the mental health care consultations for the people with mental health disorders that could be treated with medications at the level of PHCC. At each PHCC, selected Psychiatric medications will be available. The cases that don't need medication and need psychosocial support will be managed by the social worker.IHAO will relay on the approved list of medications that is allowed to be available at PHCCs level. Medications will not be procured and it will be provided by the DoH.2- Psychosocial support: In the same unit, a trained social worker will provide PSS for the cases that were identified to have no serious psychiatric complain using the available WHO tools and the tools that will be designed by the consultant and the trainers. Also, the social worker will provide PSS for the cases that were treated by the trained doctor. 3- Awareness-raising campaigns:Rising the awareness of the community is necessary to educate the community about the importance of mental health services and when to seek help. It's also important to decrease the stigma that face psychiatric patients and health workers. Through the consultant and the trainers, Information, Education, and communication ISC materials will be developed to be used in the campaigns. Step 3 -- Support and supervision Supervision through the course of implementation is crucial to maintain the quality of service and the supervision will be done internally by the available capacity in IHAO which was developed by sending staff abroad to be trained by the American University of Cairo on managing and monitoring the mental health project through Leadership in Mental health course, also IHAO have more than three people who were trained on MEAL, MHPSS, and Projects management. The external supervision will be done by the mental health care units, and director of Public health section of each DoH (In each there is a position of the director of primary mental health care which is a section of the public health department) or any relevant supervisor. Supervision forms are to be developed by the mental health consultants and the trainers to be used periodically for the follow up of the project. Quality assurance surveys are also to be done at a point during the project to ensure the provision of good quality service.

Protection Risk Analysis

As the project is dealing with a very stigmatized part of the community, stigma is one of the risks that may render people with psychiatric disorders vulnerable and unable to reach the service or get exploited and humiliated. Staff working on mental health is also exposed to being stigmatized by the community. PSEA is also a risk.

Mitigation Measures

- Awareness campaigns to decrease the stigma and provide a more acceptable environment for mental health service.- Training of the staff on PSEA and it's reporting.- Meeting the officials in the area to provide protection for the vulnerable people.

How is your project accountable to affected population

It's a core concept for IHAO at this project to consider the application of the Inter-Agency standing committee Guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support in emergencies. IHAO always emphasizes on promoting human rights and equity and the Do No Harm principle for the vulnerable population that benefits from the project. Appropriate level of coordination well be kept with other stakeholders to ensure appropriate use of resources. IHAO will also work on establishing firm referral pathways for the beneficiaries to secure the availability of other services that will help in providing the needs of the vulnerable people. IHAO will provide a hotlines in each project and will establish a firm mechanism to collect and respond to complaints. Rapid protections assessments and RNAs will be worked on to keep a very clear understanding of project environment and the needs of the catchment areas. Monitoring missions and creating a firm mechanism for communicating with the communities are also will be utilized.
