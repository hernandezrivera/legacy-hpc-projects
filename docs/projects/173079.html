Project Summary

IDP’s, and returnees in Sudan face significant challenges accessing documentation as well as housing, land and property. Conflict over land and natural resources in Darfur and South Kordofan regions have resulted in massive destruction of property and displacement. Rampant secondary occupation and ongoing disputes over land and natural resources have been identified as a major cause of conflict and hindrance to attaining durable solution in the return context. Inability to access legal identity and civil documents additionally gives rise to various protection concerns including; limited access to essential services, livelihood and employment opportunities.
The project to be implemented by ICLA department (Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance) aims at supporting displacement affected communities to claim their rights through promoting the exercise and enjoyment of housing, land and property rights and facilitating increased access to legal identity and civil documentation. The project will focus on creating awareness on HLP rights aimed at advancing security of tenure and supporting dispute resolution processes and structures. The project will draw on a three-fold approach which aims to 1) ensure that displacement affected communities are better informed about their HLP and legal identity rights through information sessions, information education and communication (IEC) materials, mobile counselling, individualized legal assistance and collaborative dispute resolution 2) enhance understanding amongst authorities, community leaders, and humanitarian actors on HLP rights and the importance of legal identity and civil documentation and 3) promote HLP mainstreaming through adoption of due diligence approach before humanitarian interventions which have an implications on land use and ownership.
To complement the above, ICLA staff will organize mobile group information sessions to increase awareness about HLP rights and the procedures that must be followed to replace documentation that has been lost or destroyed. Following these group information sessions, displacement affected individuals who express the need will receive individualized counselling tailored to their specific circumstances on the procedural and administrative requirements necessary to access HLP and/or legal identity or civil documents. Information and counselling sessions will also touch on the processes involved with accessing legal identity documents as these are frequently linked to HLP rights. NRC will also be available to facilitate referrals for displacement affected individuals who have protection related concerns that are not related to ICLA’s thematic areas of intervention.
The project will undertake legal research and assessments to build on the understanding of housing land and property issues and access to legal identity and civil documentation in-country. ICLA will undertake HLP and collaborative dispute resolution mapping assessment in Darfur and as well as detailed ICLA assessment South Kordofan. These assessments will look into the legal framework governing HLP and legal identity and civil documentation, identify key issues and provide conflict sensitive recommendations for effective programming and policy influence in Sudan.
The project will target vulnerable displacement affected populations including; IDP’s, returnees, host community and conflict affected communities in Darfur and South Kordofan. Access to HLP rights and legal identity documents are gendered issues with regularly recurring instances of gender inequality regarding ownership of housing, land and property and access to legal documentation. This project will target 60% of female beneficiaries to address this gendered gap.

1 - Needs

The conflict over land and natural resources is underpinned by several factors including competing interests over access and use of land/ natural resources, weak land administration, conflicting customary and statutory land governance systems and inadequate formal and informal dispute resolution structures. Mapping of land disputes indicated frequent and intense conflicts over; land occupation by nomads, early free grazing, intercommunal conflict over land, occupation of farm land, use of displaced land by new groups, land occupation by nomads and migrants as well as significant reports of seasonal returns and returnees being denied access to land. Secondary occupation of land abandoned by IDP’s and refugee’s is rampant and has been identified as a cause of conflict and major hindrance to attaining durable solution in the return context. Women claiming HLP rights often face additional barriers due to cultural and gender restrictive practices. These barriers expose women to additional protection risks as they are often unable to obtain adequate housing, exercise their housing, land and property rights, gain access to essential services due to lack of documentation or access fair justice systems for dispute resolution. HLP issues are key in any humanitarian response and if not addressed may exclude the most vulnerable. This project will work closely with displacement affected communities and duty bearers to enhance protection of HLP rights. IDP’s and returnees in Sudan encounter several barriers to accessing legal identity and civil documents as a result of limited access to timely registration procedures. Inability to access legal identity and civil documents gives rise to various protection concerns including; limited access to essential services, livelihood and employment opportunities. IDP’s in urban areas face additional challenges in accessing either legal identity or civil documents. Without these documents they are unable to access labour markets, face movement restrictions resulting in smuggling and lack of viable livelihood opportunities. NRC globally has expertise in resolving HLP disputes and supporting displacement affected communities to access legal identity and civil documents. 2 - Activities/Outputs

Provision of information on access to essential services, housing land and property rights, legal identity and civil documentation - ICLA staff will raise awareness about HLP rights, the importance of legal identity, civil documents, and the procedural requirements associated with securing either of these rights. Information sessions are generally brief and generic touching on the above thematic areas. Contextualized information, education, and communication (IEC) materials related to access to essential services, HLP and legal identity including civil documentation will be developed and distributed during the information sessions. Provision of counselling related to HLP rights, legal identity, civil documentation and access to essential services – Legal counselling is carried out by ICLA staff to explain the more complex requirements needed to; access essential services, exercise ones HLP rights or access legal identity and civil documents. Through the counselling, NRC will empower beneficiaries to pursue these claims on their own however where the beneficiary is particularly vulnerable more comprehensive legal assistance will be provided. As part of its counselling services, ICLA staff will also facilitate referrals to other service providers to help beneficiaries’ access essential services and other protection interventions. Provision of legal assistance - Legal assistance is provided where the beneficiary is unable to pursue a case by themselves. It covers a range of activities depending on the circumstances of each case including; accompaniment to relevant authorities, support with complicated administrative procedures and even financial legal assistance. Integrated capacity building of local authorities, community leaders and humanitarian partners through trainings, workshops and provision of technical and material support on HLP and legal identity including civil documentation. Strengthening collaborative dispute resolution through mapping of dispute resolution mechanisms, training and material support to dispute resolution structures dealing with land issues and supporting access to these structures. The training for dispute resolution structures will aim to improve their understanding of HLP rights, improve their dispute resolution skills, increase access for displacement affected communities and promote women’s HLP rights. Legal research and assessments will be undertaken to build on the understanding of housing land and property issues and access to legal identity and civil documentation in-country.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Before project implementation the project will undertake a gender sensitive assessment that will analyse potential protection risks associated with the programme activities to inform project targeting and implementation strategies. The assessment will consider different needs and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls, and of specific groups that may be vulnerable, marginalised, or discriminated against. The project design prioritises participation of vulnerable groups such as women and persons with disability ensuring that they are able to access services and assistance and removing any barriers that may prevent full and effective participation. The project design ensures that that activities have a ‘protection lens’, intentionally addressing safety, dignity, inclusive participation, diversity, rights, and vulnerabilities. Compliance of the project with protection standards will be monitored. Regular monitoring activities with the affected populations around their perceptions of safety, dignity, and agency practice will be carried out.
