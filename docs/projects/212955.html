Project Summary

According to ERL HNO 2024, 15.4M PiN for ERL. A significant majority in NWS (86%) rely on borrowing and informal credit for essentials, with many struggling to repay debts. Alarmingly, 11% in NWS are selling productive assets, compromising their long-term resilience and recovery prospects. Child labor is another distressing trend, with 9% of households in NWS depending on income from male children. In NWS, 10% of households engage in socially degrading or high-risk jobs for survival.
Recent data highlights a significant escalation in hardships within certain NWS sub-districts following the earthquake, marking a rapid deterioration compared to last year.
Disparities in access to electricity are observed across population groups in NWS. IDPs in camps receive the least electricity, averaging 11.2 hours per day, while out-of-camp IDPs and residents receive 16.8 and 18 hours per day, respectively.
This program aims to support the resilience of the conflict-affected people in NW Syria, particularly in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. WH's proposal is designed to contribute to supporting the transition to the early recovery phase, as it supports the target communities with long-term assistance activities, programs that focus on building/restoring the communities' infrastructure, local economic empowerment, and initiatives that ensure engagement and participation of the community in recovering from the protracted crisis.
The program formulation has been based on needs identified by the ERL cluster, supported by NA conducted by WH in Dec 2023 and first-hand consultations with camps management and local authorities/stakeholders.
WH’s ERL program recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group across the range of ERL interventions.
The plan aims to address multiple Early Recovery sector objectives through the following:
a) Improve access to camps and basic services for IDPs through upgrading/rehabilitation of 15 Km of main roads outside the camps, which are affected by poor weather conditions to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter and in need of rehabilitation (new asphalt layer) with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure. The project was designed to restore sections of the road network which is in poor condition and, at the same time to strengthen the planning, implementation, and management capacity of the local authorities, moreover, to develop further the emerging local road construction and maintenance industry, using mainly labor-based methods and generating job opportunities to assist with poverty alleviation.
b) Implementing sidewalks through the CfW approach next to the newly rehabilitated roads.
c) Rehabilitation of 20 schools.
d) Establishing or expanding Small-Micro Enterprises through the provision of business skills training and financial or in-kind support to 200 selected beneficiaries from IDPs and Host communities. 25 Of them will be PWDs. The grant total will be between 800-1000 USD per beneficiary.
e) Providing vocational and skills training to 400 youth, men, and women applicants (50 of them PWDs) and then supporting successful trainees (Expecting 200 trainees) with small-scale cash grants to enable them to restart/initiate their small enterprises; each grant total will be between 800- 1000 USD per beneficiary.
f) Increase the resilience of IDPs and host community populations by generating new employment for 200 skilled and unskilled workers, including 15 women and 10 PWDs (through supervisory roles) through rehabilitation, maintenance, and cleaning work.
The program will seek feedback from both male & female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access & equal opportunities.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 120,000 individuals (30,690 women, 27530 men, 31,710 girls, and 30,070 boys), including 3,000 PWDs.
