Project Summary

In this project, the provision of protective and quality education services is life-saving, not only equipping children with knowledge and life skills, but also reducing their vulnerability while being displaced. This project will provide a comprehensive Emergency Education package to the targeted communities – classrooms will be rehabilitated, gender-sensitive school WASH facilities will be built, children will have access to drinking water, enrollment of girls and children with disabilities will be increased through community awareness sessions and teachers and PTAs will be trained, continuously supported and mentored on topics such as: teaching cycle one curriculum, core subjects, inclusive education, psychosocial support and positive discipline. Equitable access to quality education can prove an integrating factor of IDP/Refugee/Returnee communities into their host communities and can create a collective sense of ownership to the area of residence.

1 - Needs

Sudan is considered a fragile State, suffering long-lasting internal conflicts, high social and economic disparities and unequal allocation of public resources. The difficult economic conditions in Sudan, besides the continuous influx of refugees and movement of IDPs, put continuous pressure on host communities and their absorption capacity in terms of basic services, natural resources, livelihoods, and economic opportunities. Lack of access to basic services to the population in general and to the refugee/IDP population, in particular, is widely recognized as one of the main push factors of illegal and forced migration. Despite a growing number of children accessing basic education, there are still 3 million children out of school, 52% of them are girls. Even for the ones who entered schools, the probability of dropping out is high where more than 560,000 children are at risk of dropping out before completing primary school, the majority are girls who represent 57% of this number. Other excluded groups include; nomads, children with disabilities, children affected by war, refugees, IDPs, children in rural areas and poor children need in the education sector continue to increase mainly in Central Darfur Zalinge and Jebel Marra and as more areas become accessible, the need for quality education is increasing. Children in displaced communitiesare adding pressure to existing education facilities which cannot accommodate new students due to limited space and resources. In Jebel Marra localities, access to education remains low and enrolment for basic education stands at 40 percent on average.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The program strategy is based on Save the children International quality Framework, the provision of quality education services equitably to all population segments is an initiative that can prove substantially beneficial in giving education and life skills to children reducing their vulnerability while being on the move or hosting children on the move. Equitable access to quality education can prove an integrating factor of IDP/Refugee/returnee communities into their host communities and can create a collective sense of ownership to the area of residence. The quality learning framework approach of Save the children is in line with achieving Sustainable development goal 4, as quality learning framework has the following pillars; emotional and psychosocial protection, physical protection, teaching and Learning, parent and community and lastly school leadership and managementActivities that will address the needs identified; a. The provision of teaching, learning Recreational materials; b. The construction/Rehabilitation and furnishing of semi-permanent classrooms; c. The rehabilitation of permanent classrooms and premises including green spaces; d. The provision of gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools/learning spaces; e. Capacity building for teachers and other education personnel; f. The mobilization and participation of communities and Children in school-based activities.g. Community engagement and interaction with schools through parent-teacher association (PTA) meetings, school management committee meetings.h. Development and Support school improvement plans (SIP) schooling-based disaster risk reduction plans/strategiesi. Teacher training, including Child Safeguarding, Learner-Centered Methodology, and Psychological First Aidj. Establish adult literacy and numeracy classes for parents in the afternoon.k. Organize parent-teacher conferences once in the academic year.l. Organize lectures on the importance of parent-school relationships for the pupil’s academic achievement and school development.

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will mainstream by benefiting the existing child protection staff who will deal with children who are out of school or not attending school activities and should have the necessary information on how children can access education. Messaging around the importance of education can be highlighted in child protection messaging activities or activities in CFS. Orphans and other vulnerable children who are not attending school can be identified in care settings or in extended families by our child protection CP staff. Learning spaces are key locations for identifying child protection concerns and teachers /facilitators should, therefore, have a good understanding of child protection and how to refer children for support. Similarly education staff should be familiar with child protection program activities such as family tracing and reunification and be prepared to support certain activities when requested for support. Schools and CFS should similarly be located in safe areas (walking distance to community, not in close proximity to army/rebel bases).
