Project Summary

The intervention is part of a project aiming at securing the basic needs and providing integrated community-based protection activities in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati. The project is held by Caritas Germany (CS), back-funded by the German Federal Foreign Ministry (GFO) and implemented from 01.04.2019 -30.09.2020 by Caritas Syria (CS). It was designed based on the results of a needs assessment conducted in Karm Jazmati in January 2019. Following a participatory approach, the needs assessment applied a proportional piling method in order to ensure the engagement and thus self-identification of the needs in the community, also taking into consideration i.e. illiterate persons. Throughout the project lifetime, the engagement of all community members is promoted through i.e. monitoring methods following the same approach, and feedback mechanisms ensuring a two-way communication with the community – thus ensuring timely incorporation of community feedback.
After consulting with regional coordination sectors UNOCHA, UNHCR, unicef, WFP as well as with organisations operating in neighbouring areas, CS has opened a community center in Karm Jazmati in September 2019.Since then, CS has provided integrated community-based protection services, to high at risk groups such as women, adolescent girls, children and elderly. .
The 2020 intervention under the proposed project aims at reducing the protection risks for groups prone to protection issues through a community-based approach, implemented in the community center. The center follows an open door policy, ensuring the active engagement of the community, allowing real-time evaluations and timely adjustments of activities as per beneficiaries’ feedback. The intervention will continue to respond to protection needs in Karm Jazmati with four pillars of activities: non-specialized PSS and awareness sessions for women, children and elderly; literacy courses for adolescent girls and women as protection measures against GBV risks; informal education for schoolaged children aiming at reducing child protection risk factors as well as a capacity building components aiming at strengthening the responding capacity of Caritas Syria staff on a national level.

4 - Needs

Multiple displacements, lack of basic infrastructure and services have left a legacy of psychological distress in the community in Karm Jazmati, a district in the Eastern part of Aleppo city. Currently,1.855 HH live in this area (Dec 2019), 87% are returnees and 13% are IDPs. In the beginning of 2019, in collaboration with the regional protection sector in Aleppo and partners, Karm Jazmati has been identied as gap area lacking provision of protection services. In January 2019, CG and CS conducted an initial needs assessment to inform the response. The below draws from the 2019 needs assessment and recent data from regular monitoring excersices as well as the HRP 2019. Increasing levels of poverty has led to marginalization and the neglect of groups with specic needs such as the elderly and people with disabilities. In 12% of HH, there is at least one person 60 years or older. In 5% of HH, there is at least one person with a physical or mental disability. 55% of the interviewed persons stated being illiterate. 21% of HH with one or several daughters 15-17yo reported that one or all of their adolescent girls were illiterate. From all respondents reported being illiterate, 88% stated that it had negative effects on their daily life. Illiteracy does not only deprive people of their ability to know and exercise their rights but also affects the chances of entering the job market and thus contributes to lack of livelihood opportunities. Illiteracy can therefore be considered increasing the protection concerns of already high at risk groups such as adolescent girls and women in multiple ways. Girls and boys continue to endure multiple protection risks on a daily basis; children who dropped-out of school to an even larger extend. In Karm Jazmati, 22% of girls aged 7-14yo do currently not attend school. In 5% of these cases, the reason for non-attendance is child labor. 37% of boys aged 7-14yo do not attend school. The main reason for non-attendance is child labor (45%).

5 - Implementation of activities

Objective 1: Protection risks of vulnerable groups (women, adolescents, children and elderly) in Jazmati are reduced through integrated community based approaches, incl. awareness raising and psychosocial support. Activity 1.1: Provide protection-related awareness sessions to high at risk groups of women and children. Target: 490 persons. Activity 1.2 Provide PSS for women, children, and elderly prone to protection risks. Provide non-specialized PSS for peer groups of women, children and elderly that face high protection risks, strengthening resilience of individuals and support healthy, and functioning community structures. Target: 268 persons. Objective 2: CS staff has increased capacity to respond to protection needs and provide high quality services. Activity 2.1 Conduct CB activities in Aleppo to improve quality provision of protection services, incl. strengthen outreach activities, referrals, protection assessment and analysis through appropriate trainings and supervision. Target: 110 staff members. Objective 3: Risks of GBV for high at risk women and adolescent girls in Jazmati are mitigated. CS staff that has received a ToT by an external service provider in 2019 provide alphabetization courses in the CC, enhancing the empowerment of women in the community: Post monitoring excersises have shown multiplying effects within the community.Target: 80 women and 20 adolescent girls. Objective 4: Protection risks for school-aged girls and boys are decreased. Activity 4.1 Structured and sustained informal education (tutoring) will be provided to school aged children 7-14yo in the CC. With support of a psychologist, children can be referred to other activities or specialized services if needed. The children are attending formal school curriculum A or B in Jazmaty and will be provided tutoring classes in three basic subjects (maths , Arabic, English) throughout the school year, increasing their access to a structured daily routine in a safe space.Target: 160 children.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Different vulnerable groups might not have the same access to services and assistance Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. Follow existing Guidelines ad checklists already available on PSS support, attend and promote discussion in the MHPSS Task Force; make use of existing tools/mapping; 2. Coordinate at local level with other protection actors to ensure balanced coverage responding to needs 3. Contribute to service mapping so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be informed. 4. Ensure information sharing with community members on services available and regular communication to the community about the nature and the target of the services 6. Select staff for all activities (especially outreach volunteers) with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile Protection Risk: Exploitation or abuse of beneficiaries by staff members/external service providers when accessing assistance and services Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. Ensure knowing and understanding of all Caritas Syria project staff of Caritas Internationalis (CI) Code of Conduct (binding for all Caritas Internationalis member organisations such as Caritas Germany and Caritas Syria) 2. Provide trainings and ensure regular recaps of CI Code of Conduct among all CS staff members and volunteers 3. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended 5. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation, ensuring confidentiality of the reporting 6. Ensure standards of data protection 7. Design specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints on a regular basis.
