Project Summary

The project’s dry fodder and livestock distributions, the provision of alternative, drought-resistant fodder sources will improve resiliency to encourage Northeast Syrian (NES) breeders to maintain and increase their flocks. Vocational training and provision of livelihood assets for agribusiness cooperative projects will strengthen livestock value chains and increase family incomes.
Protection Mainstreaming:
SP will prevent, as much as possible, any unintended negative effects of the intervention that might increase people’s vulnerability to physical and psychological risks. For example, SP will ensure that routes to and from any distribution/training or program sites are safe and accessible. Also, SP will prioritize the safety and dignity of participants by respecting the social norms of the target area and ensuring that interventions are culturally appropriate; for example, norms around male and female interaction for the vocational trainings and interactions at distributions. The SP program will coordinate with other non-governmental organizations (NGOs) in the local target areas to ensure no duplication of activities that could be harmful or make program activities redundant in the livelihoods and agriculture sector in terms of fodder training and distribution.
SP will ensure that program staff are capacitated to respond appropriately to protection or prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) concerns or complaints arising, and are trained to follow all policies and guidelines in responding with a survivor-centered approach. An awareness of the unique needs of vulnerable groups will be incorporated into program interventions for IDPs and relevant host community members, in an effort to ensure safety, security, accountability, dignity, and effective access to the program across genders and age groups.
Sensitization will be provided to the community to ensure that women beneficiaries are supported and the wider community understands the value of women being able to earn money through livelihoods/agriculture programming (for example, female headed households). Programming will ensure that sexual violence and exploitation are mitigated through cultural sensitivity and by navigating local gender norms. The program will provide training and education on safe practices for safeguarding and PSEA, as well as provide a safe place for women and girls to receive help and counsel as needed, and a hotline to report incidents.
Meaningful Access: SP will provide people with access to assistance and fodder services based on need and without any barriers such as discrimination. The program will support individuals and groups who are particularly vulnerable or have difficulty in accessing assistance and services. Thus, SP will work to make the program as accessible as possible, in regard to both physical access and ensuring community awareness of the program.
Assessment of Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures
Key risk 1
Description of risk: Beneficiaries sell their livestock before the end of the program
Potential impact: Low
Probability: Medium
Mitigation measures: Fodder distributions will be given in monthly rations to encourage beneficiaries not to sell their livestock. Fodder distributions can also be cancelled if the beneficiary sells most of their sheep.
Key risk 2
Description of risk: Cultural norms make engaging females challenging, limiting their ability to contribute to HH income generation.
Potential impact: Low
Probability: High
Mitigation measures: Reserve spots in the program intended only for women.
-Offer flexibility in their working schedules
-Conduct needs assessment once selected to see if other needs can be addressed to alleviate barriers
Key risk 3
Description of risk: Security situations or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to targeted areas.
Potential impact: Medium
Probability: High
Mitigation measures: Ensure the national team is equipped to manage the program locally.
