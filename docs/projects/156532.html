Project Summary

Rationale:
Based on WASH assessments, estimated 15.6 million people in Syria require WASH assistance, 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need, in particular displaced people in camps or informal settlements, host communities and returnees. Already prior the crisis, only 65 per cent of the population had access to 42 built-up sewage treatment plants; this number has significantly decreased due to the fact that. many of these facilities are no longer operational due to damage, non-availability of critical supplies, and inefficient management, altogether leading to significant community health risks. Garbage collection services are primarily provided by municipalities/local authorities in most parts of the country and usually provide a basic level of service. However, due to the lack of comprehensive disposal strategies and operational challenges, additional efforts are needed in specific rural and urban communities to strengthen and upgrade the quality and regularity of solid waste management.
Objectives:
Based on above, UN-HABITAT is proposing an area-based approach aimed to assist communities in selected areas / neighborhoods through rehabilitation of essential infrastructure and basic services, prioritized on the basis of needs and vulnerabilities. Overall, the project aims to ensure
• Sufficient provision of safe water,
• Increased water quality assurance, through support to water system-rehabilitation, operation and maintenance, in addition to
• Rehabilitation of the sewage systems, including distribution - discharge networks and repair of waste water treatment plants.
The project plans to reach 2,558,250 beneficiaries (direct and indirect) in five governorates (Aleppo 1,6 M beneficiaries, Homs 630,000 beneficiaries, Deir-ez-Zor 152,250 beneficiaries, Rural Damascus 55,000 beneficiaries, and Daraa 176,000 beneficiaries).
UN-HABITAT has significantly increased its targets in 2019 compared to previous years through continuing to support existing water systems in an effort to stabilize and reduce their decline, and to ensure provision of safe water through public water supply networks. Wherever possible comprehensive approach will be promoted to fully restore safe water supply at the sufficient level. Critical activities include basic rehabilitation of infrastructure for life-saving and/or resilience-oriented purposes, in addition to prevent the discharge of raw sewage and contamination of water bodies and soil. In light of very limited wastewater treatment capacities across the country alternative sanitation solutions will be considered. Additionally, capacity building for staff and operation and maintenance of wastewater infrastructure and solid waste (municipal and medical waste) management systems will be supported along with provision of required materials and technology to reduce overall public health risks and environmental pollution caused by the degradation of those systems.
Project Components:
In each location, the project consists of Four main components as follows:
1. Repair, rehabilitation, augmentation of water systems;
2. Support to sewage and solid waste management systems;
3. Distribution of essential WASH NFIs and Hygiene promotion
4. Capacity building: (training, orientations, learning visits/exposure etc.).

Needs

WASH sector assessments underpin that, despite some investment in water supply and wastewater treatment systems, provision of water disinfectants and other interventions, severe WASH needs across Syria remain, especially;•Access to sufficient quantities of safe water•Effectiveness of solid waste management systems•WASH NFIs and Hygiene promotion•WASH related capacity building on all levelsUp to 55% of surveyed households are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet their water needs. General shortage of drinking water also has implications on economy and resilience: families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 10% of their income on water, with 3% of the population spending more than 20%, in extreme cases as much as 40% of their income to purchase water. Regarding sanitation, more than 99% of the surveyed population has access to a functioning toilet. While this figure gives an impression that the sanitation situation in Syria is satisfactory, it is to note that at least 70% of sewage is untreated and that more than half of the sewage systems are either not or only partially functional, leading to only 9% of the population still being served with functional wastewater treatment plants. Dysfunctionality of existing wastewater treatment plants and sewage networks causing discharge of raw sewage, increasingly pollute soil and water sources, therefore causing significant public health risks, as well as negatively impacting overall water resource management. Garbage collection services are primarily provided by municipalities/local authorities in most parts of the country and usually provide a basic level of service. However, due to the lack of comprehensive disposal strategies and operational challenges, additional efforts are needed in specific rural and urban communities to strengthen and upgrade the quality and regularity of solid waste management. In terms of affordability and accessibility of WASH items, WASH assessment data indicates that hygiene supplies are available in most of the local markets, but some small (%) of the population could not afford to purchase them. IDPs and returnees are in a worse situation with up to 10% of respondents indicating being unable to purchase some basic hygiene items. Implementation of activities

In each location, implementation consist of Four main components:1. Repair, rehabilitation, augmentation of water systems:1.1. Quick repair/rehabilitation of water supply networks'1.2. Repair of water treatment plants/replacement of media and other essentials;1.3. Installation of new water supply project;1.4. Installation of water storage tanks/repairs/upgrading.2. Support to sewage and solid waste management systems:2.1. Repair and rehabilitation of sewerage networks;2.2. Quick repair of pumps, pumping stations of sewer system;2.3. Installation of new sewer line and manhole covers;2.4. Procurement of solid waste collection containers;2.5. Collection and disposal of solid waste;2.6. Cleaning campaigns in host communities;2.7. Sludge pumps, additional equipment's and accessories.3. Distribution of essential WASH NFIs and Hygiene promotion:3.1. Awareness campaign to sustain established behaviour,4. Capacity building: (training, orientations, learning visits/exposure etc). The planning and implementation process consist in steps as follows:• Selection of project areas through collaborative deliberations with municipalities, communities, providers for public services, etc.,(list further steps in the process, including handover, O&M and monitoring)• Through MTOS (MUNICIPAL TECHNICAL OFFICE SUPPORT) approach, identify the priority neighborhoods/areas for intervention, as well as type, scope and priority of possible activities• Support local community to form their committee, with representation from Municipality, Mokhtar, other leaders and stakeholders,In coordination with the municipality, water department and cadastral department assess infrastructure, services and their functionality, as well as need and scope of basic rehabilitation work,• Secure required approvals from the respective authorities,• Restoring the infrastructure and basic services will be implemented by private sector through tendering process.• Based on the damage assessment outputs, UN-HABITAT will tender the proposed activities under the supervision of the relevant stakeholders and Municipalities.
