Project Summary

Support skills and capacity building for a durable livelihood.
This is a skills and capacity development project for disabled and elderly people from the Rohingya and host communities. The objective of the project is to strengthen the resilience of the elderly and persons with disabilities men, women, at-risk youth, from both the Rohingya community and Host in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. While the expected outputs of the project are:
(1) Increased skills of targeted Rohingya PWDs and elderly men and women in productive work in targeted trades (270 Rohingya PWDs/ elderly receiving training and seed capital support in trades like textile, beautician, electronics, and bamboo work, disaggregated by sex, age, disability, and trade)
(2) Enhanced capacity in the camps for providing skill learning opportunities for PWDs/ elderly (three training centers rented/renovated (accessible to PWDs), 4 trade specific modules designed to align with the needs and requirements of the camps, and
(3) Increased technical and management skills (non-formal) of targeted Host community PWDs/ elderly men and women in productive work in targeted interventions (270 PWDs/ elderly receive training and seed capital support in IGAs on Off-farm economic activities, disaggregated by sex, age, disability and trade.
To cover the targeted participants, the primary target areas are camp 5 and 2 unions (Sabrang and Baharchara) of Tekanf. If the target number of participants is not covered within these areas, the project has kept provision to go camp 4 extension and Palong Khali Union of Ukhiya.
To achieve outputs 1 and 2, there are 4 components:
Component 1: Skill training for 60 PWDs & elderly on Carpenters (wooden/bamboo, jute products, handy craft) and weaving, knitting, repairing household items.
Technical partner: Bunon Kutir, a Private Sector Social Enterprise specialized in production of bamboo and recycled textile products.
Key Activity
A. This is under non-formal technical training, minimum 120 hrs duration. This training is on production of bamboo products. Target for 40 beneficiaries (in 2 batches). Training topics: product design, making, bamboo treatment, quality control, marketing, etc.
B. 120 hrs training on production of textile products from recycled materials for 20 participants. Topics: pattern development, cutting, matching, colour, machine maintenance, machine operation, weaving techniques etc.
Component 2: Skill training on ELLA Pad production (Sewing Machine Operation for production) on reusable sanitary pads for 60 PWDs/ elderly.
Technical partner: ELLA Pad, a social initiative run by poor working women, specialized in production of reusable feminine products.
Key Activity: This is non-formal technical skills training on reusable sanitary pads.
Component 3: Skill training on Hairdressing/Barbering/Beautification/Beauty Parlor Business for 90 PWDs/ elderly.
Technical partner: Ujjwala, a Private sector Social Enterprise specialized in Beauty and Grooming training
Key Activity: . This is non-formal technical training of minimum 120 hrs on beautician skill Topics will include: bridal make up, hair-cut, hair styling, threading etc.
Component 4: Technical skill training for 60 PWDs/ elderly on Small Engine Mechanic and Electrician (building).
Skill training for 60 PWDs/ elderly on Small Engine Mechanic and Electrician (building)
Technical partner: DAM-Training Centre, a national level development agency
Key Activity: This is under non-formal technical skills development training that is for 156 hrs duration following training package available.
Non-formal raining has provision of incentive and input support for the beneficiary for 6 months.
For output 3, there are two intervention, i.e. Off-farm economic activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Based on our ongoing interventions and to address the growing economic DRP empowerment needs, Pulse BD conducted a needs assessment on livelihood challenges and opportunities amongst DRP in April 2021. Based on this needs assessment and complementary secondary data, it has been observed that the absence of income generating opportunities for self-reliance has become a major concern. Even after months/ years from the influx, the DRP are forced to primarily rely on humanitarian assistance for their basic needs and survival, including access to food and income. This is largely because the current government (GoB) policy restricts them from earning any kind of income, and there is no formal source of livelihood among the camp population, making people fully dependent on external assistance. According to the 2020 and 2021 Joint Response Plan (JRP), all DRP rely on massive food assistance and over 90% of them can only access food through humanitarian actors. Despite the restrictive policy, our needs assessment found that Rohingya men frequently engage in informal income generating activities outside the camp settlements. Prime sources of income are casual labor and selling of humanitarian relief items. Rohingya women are hardly involved in income generating activities. The positive correlation between DRP having a source of income and better food security situation have been highlighted in the Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 2) report. It shows that almost 44% of the refugees have unacceptable food consumption patterns, but at the same time it is presented that 63% of those who have one source of income have acceptable FCS. The report concludes that limited access to an income was proved to be a major driver of poverty for the refugees.To deal with this lack of purchasing power, DRP households engage in different coping mechanisms. The 2019 J-MSNA report observes that certain camps have a significant higher proportion of households engaging in more extreme coping mechanisms. Almost 69% of the surveyed population borrowed money to reduce their level of food insecurity. The REVA 2 report shows that almost 62% of the refugees rely on less preferred less expensive food as the most common food related coping strategy, while 47% borrow food or rely on help from others. In a recent research study published by the Asia Foundation titled, “Navigating at the Margins: Family, Mobility and Livelihoods amongst Rohingya Refugees in Bangladesh”, similar findings emerged. Among the surveyed Rohingya households, it was seen that four households out of five have no cash savings and whatever assets they could bring to Bangladesh while fleeing Myanmar in 2017 or later, all have been dwindled by now. Though the average household income is reported to be BDT 2,648, almost 45% of the surveyed households reported no income at all, completely being dependent on aid. The study found that all groups experienced economic hardship and 91% of the families found it difficult to make ends meet. Regarding the negative coping mechanism adopted by the households, it was found that 83% of the surveyed households have taken out a loan after arriving in Bangladesh and 74% reported being in debt at the time of the survey. Vocational training, asset transfer, saving schemes, and small business training have been successfully combined in income generating activity interventions. Field level monitoring reports, case stories and FDGs have shown that the Rohingya women have positively participated in the skill development training and have started getting more engaged with income earning opportunities using the assets given to them. Women receiving support for small businesses have opened small shops in their houses and their neighbors have become their biggest clients. These small income earning initiatives are greatly supported by the informal economic opportunities available in the camps.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project aligns with the Rationalization Principles, with particular reference to # 3: Adopt an integrated approach, and # 4: Ensure predictability and continuity. In regards to the integrated approach, this has been implemented through a community-led integrated approach in coordination with the CiC, Mazi, government service agencies (DAE), private sectors (Bunon Kutir, Ujjwala , ELLA Pad, DAM-TVET), local government bodies, and the community. This approach includes a community-based target group and a partnership approach. In regards to multiple implementing partners, this community is totally unserved by the non-government development organization. Pulse BD is trying to bring more projects for other services, such as health care, protection, livelihoods. In terms of predictability and continuity, there is one of current project will continue up to February 2025. After that, this project will continue in the second phase based on project performance, i.e., effectiveness. The climate smart agriculture and saline-tolerant high-value crop projects (component for host community) are promising and potential interventions in the development field. There is high predictability of the project's ability to be expended, continued, and replicable. Regarding the other operating principles of the rationalisation, they are met in terms of relevance to the nature of this project, such as the fact that, in the design phase of the project, the community was fully involved, and even the donor visited and met with the community. Before submitting the proposal to the donor, consent has been obtained from the local government and local administration. In terms of transparency and accountability, this project has an M&E framework and reporting systems. That is, regular monitoring and sharing of information about progress and status at a specific interval with the relevant stakeholders

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming is one of the cross-cutting approach of this project. It is primarily focused on addressing the complex child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) risks faced by boys, girls, and young women in both Rohingya camps and the host community. It acknowledges these risks, gender-specific impacts, and access barriers, particularly related to menstrual hygiene management and GBV concerns. This approach entails establishing survivor-centered GBV response services, including individual case management, provider training for women and girls. Additionally, it involves strengthening a protective environment through protection initiatives. Challenges like family separation, limited legal assistance, and high rates of child marriage are recognized, and the project aligns with inter-agency strategies while drawing on past experiences to create a safer environment for vulnerable women, children and youth in Cox's Bazar.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Mainstreaming initiative has been launched to address critical concerns regarding child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) affecting boys, girls, and young women in both Rohingya camps and the host community. These concerns encompass various risks, including gender-specific challenges, particularly related to issues like menstrual hygiene management and GBV. The complex challenges faced by relocated Rohingya individuals emphasize the need for a survivor-centered approach. This strategy prioritizes the establishment of comprehensive GBV response services, focusing on individual case management, provider training, and active engagement with children, adolescents, families, and the community. It also includes the revitalization of safe spaces for women and girls to enhance their skills and resilience. Concurrently, interventions in child protection aim to create a protective environment, emphasizing case management, psychosocial support, life skills training, and family-strengthening initiatives.This project is rooted in the government's unwavering commitment to ensure the secure relocation of FDMNs to the Rohingya and host communities and their sustained presence. Aligned with inter-agency strategies, it places a strong emphasis on addressing the most urgent refugee needs and leveraging the capacities of local organizations in Cox's Bazar. The project will focus on targeted measures to mitigate GBV risks and enhance Child Protection programming, building on past successes and best practices developed by PULSE Bangladesh Society. In essence, the PSEA Mainstreaming approach seeks to create a safer environment and provide comprehensive protection for vulnerable children, adolescents, and young women in Cox's Bazar
