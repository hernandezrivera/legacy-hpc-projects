Project Summary

COVID-19 quickly changed the context in which children live. Quarantine measures such as school closures and restrictions on movements disrupted children's routine and social support while also placed new stress on parents and caregivers who may have to find new childcare options or forgot work. Stigma and discrimination related to COVID-19 may make children more vulnerable to violence and psychosocial distress. Disease control measures that did not consider the gender-specific needs and vulnerabilities of women and girls also increased their protection risks and lead to negative coping mechanisms. Children and families who are already vulnerable due to socio-economic exclusion or those who live in over crowded settings are particularly at risk.
The following issues regarding child protection have been identified.
1. Increased stress levels due to isolation in treatment units or home-based quarantine
2. Children and parents/caregivers with pre-existing mental health conditions are not be able to
access usual supports or treatments
3. Quarantine measures create fear and panic in the community, especially in children, if they do
not understand what is happening.
4. Loss or reduction in household income
5. Opportunity or expectation to work due to school closure.
6. Loss of parents/caregivers due to disease
● Isolation/quarantine of caregiver(s) apart from child(ren)
7. Children sent away by parents to stay with other family in non-affected areas.
8. Closure/inaccessibility of basic services for vulnerable children and/or families
9. Disruption to birth registration processes due to quarantine.
Proposed Strategy:
The project will focus on children and women with and provide equal opportunities to them throughout the project implementation. Furthermore, women headed households and missing, separated and unaccompanied children will be identified and referred for services provisions. During the project implementation, psychosocial support and counselling services will be provided to children and women with psychological distress and hence reduce their distress and related concerns.
The project will also deploy 4 trained Child Protection Mobilizers (2 male and 2 female) who will be responsible for monitoring, reporting and referral of vulnerable cases to available service providers. The Child Protection Mobilizers will further provide support to organize the local communities to form 20 community-based Child Protection Committees (10 men and 10 women) through community mobilization meetings, awareness raising sessions in the community. The community-based Child Protection Committees will be engaged in monitoring, identifying, and referral of vulnerable children and women, including missing, unaccompanied, and separated children, and promoting their access to services through the community-based referral mechanism. Throughout the project implementation, PVDP will develop closed collaboration with other humanitarian organisations, Provincial Disaster Management Authority (PDMA), local Government authorities and departments, Child Protection working group, Protection and GBV sub-working group.

1- Project Activities

1.1. (03) days orientation session for project staff on key components of project, referral, psychosocial first aid and LSBE.To build the capacity of child protection staff about project activities, a three-days orientation session will be conducted with project staff in first month of the project. This orientation session will be about the project key components, , work plan, delivery of services in first aid psychosocial support and LSBE. 1.2. Provide psychosocial support to girls boys and women identified with psychosocial disorders.PVDP will hire two skilled psychosocial councilors (1 male and 1 female) who will be responsible for the provision of psychosocial support to girls, boys and women. Girls, boys and women with psychosocial distress or in need of psychosocial support will be identified directly by the psychologists with the support of child protection mobilizers. Psychologists will also provide psychosocial support through individual, group and family counselling sessions. Psychologists will develop their work plans along with relevant tools/instruments for identifying, diagnosing and reporting of cases1.3. Life skills base education (LSBE) opportunities for adolescent’s girls, boys and women in order to enhance their resilience.LSBE sessions will include lesions, pictorial presentation, stories, dramas, roles plays and example sharing’s on self-awareness and empathy, communication and interpersonal skills, critical thinking and creative thinking, decision making and problem solving, coping with stress and coping with emotions Child protection mobilizers will facilitate and report these sessions. These sessions will be conducted as per COVID-19 SOPs.2.1 Establish and strengthen support mechanism and referral system for addressing the needs and issues of identified vulnerable girls, boys and women.PVDP is working in Kurram Agency since 2011 and has develop a referral mechanism with services available in Kurram. In the proposed project PVDP will revise the map of services available in selected villages and at agency level in Kurram Agency. Project staff will conduct meetings with the providers of available services and will strengthen the established referral mechanism. Project child protection mobilizers along with child protection committees will conduct vulnerability assessments in their respective villages and will develop a plan for addressing the identified vulnerabilities. include separated, unaccompanied, missing girls and boys and other protection issues of girls boys and women like disabilities, orphans, out of school, children involved in labor, children with chronic diseases, street children, GBV survival, psychological disorders, children without birth registration, children using drugs, early child marriages, Child headed household, Female headed household, widows, unmarried, divorced, abused, corporal punishment, in need of legal support etc. The cases identified at the grass root level will refer to relevant stake holders. The cases will be managed and documented at all level of the referral path way. 3.1 Establish and strengthen community-based Child Protection Committees and monthly coordination meetings.The project will establish 20 community-based Child Protection Committees (10 male and 10 female) to support project activities in targeted locations. The CP Mobilizers will build the capacity of these Child Protection Committees. Committee members will develop and follow the action plan regarding their support in project implementation and project activities. 3.2 Deliver messages and information including related to protection concerns of girls, boys and women, keeping children safe, prevention and respond to COVID-19.Due to COVID-19 , PVDP in will arrange awareness rising sessions about child protection, child and women rights in Hujras/Community places, houses. PVDP will develop, print and disseminate 2000 posters and 5000 brochers among stakeholders along 5 radio programs.
