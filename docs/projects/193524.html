Project Summary

The project aligns with the HRP's second and third Country Strategic Objectives: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”, and. Specifically, it is aligned with the FSS SO1: The resilience of vulnerable households is protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced; and FSS SO2: “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.”
This project aims to increase the resilience of vulnerable families, farmers and herders in marginalized communities in the West Bank to restore and protect their livelihoods. Specifically, the project seeks to increase access of vulnerable men and women engaged in agricultural and small livestock activities (855 HHs) living in Area C to employment and income-generating opportunities, agricultural assets and legal aid to support them in meeting their basic needs and protecting their rights (total 4019 individuals, 2037 male, 1982 female). The project will be implemented by Oxfam, in partnership with ESDC and LRC. Oxfam and its partners have a long-standing record and solid experience implementing agricultural activities and providing humanitarian assistance and livelihood support interventions in the West Bank. The project will target localities within the governorates of Jerusalem, Ramallah, Bethlehem and Hebron, particularly Area C, that face various social-economic pressures such as Israeli policies and practices that impede on Palestinian livelihood activities, including demolitions and restriction in access to natural resources.The activities will be implemented in coordination with village councils and Departments of Agriculture (DoA). Initial coordination has taken place with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and the design of activities were carried out in consultation municipal and village councils. Activities include Cash for Work (CfW) to support 360 agricultural workers, multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to 100 HHs, rehabilitation support to 100 poultry farms, income-generating activities (IGAs) to support 45 women engaged in agricultural activities, and 250 HHs will benefit from public awareness on legal protection. The implementation/division of activities will be based on each partner's geographical presence, experience and added value. The project's activities have been designed in consultation with the target groups, through focus group discussions (FGD), the FSS and the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA). The proposed interventions were selected based on the HRP 2023 priority matrix. Oxfam’s leads on integration of cash and voucher assistance in the West Bank, specifically in recent efforts to enhance effectiveness of humanitarian response in coordination and collaboration with cash working group actors and within the cluster specific priorities. Women are at the heart of this response and will be targeted through IGAs and participation in the CfW and public awareness sessions. Oxfam jointly implements cash assistance and livelihood interventions with local partners by providing technical support, strengthening local leadership through the joint implementation of LRC and ESDC, and sharing learning of humanitarian assistance in the evolving context.Oxfam aligns its programs with safe-programming strategies and guidance that continue to steer its project implementation, across all activities in emergency and non-emergency situations, to ensure communities and beneficiaries are safeguarded against sexual exploitation and abuse. Oxfam will ensure mainstreaming protection throughout the project cycle, starting from assessment through the implementation and monitoring. This will be represented through beneficiaries’ selection through tackling protection-base criteria in addition to the livelihood vulnerability

1 - Needs

The context in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains a protracted protection crisis, where many Palestinians struggle to meet their most basic needs and live in dignity. The crisis is characterized by Israeli military occupation, a 16-year blockade of Gaza, the lack of respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities across the West Bank and Gaza. 2022 has witnessed further deterioration of the humanitarian situation due to the restrictive and discriminatory planning regime applied by Israel in Area C and in East Jerusalem, which continues to prevent Palestinians from addressing basic housing, livelihood and basic service needs. Productive capacities and economic opportunities are constrained and limited, contributing to the high rates of unemployment and low household incomes, that intersect with poverty as the main immediate cause of food insecurity in the oPt. In addition, there is an increase in the level of demolitions and seizures of Palestinian structures, coupled with constraints on the delivery of materials needed, and limitations on the implementation of projects that involve building, expanding or rehabilitating infrastructure in the West Bank (Area C, Hebron and East Jerusalem). (HNO 2022)According to the HNO released in September 2022, 34% of the demolished structures in Area C were agricultural-related (reaching over 220 structures), and 36% of demolition incidents affect herding communities. Areas A and B of the West Bank, home to 87% of the West Bank population, faces a different set of constraints according to the HNO report, such as roadblocks, checkpoints, by-pass roads, and the limited accessibility to natural resources – all of which have led to reduced competitiveness of its productive entities, made it harder for businesses to be viable and impeded job creation efforts and initiatives.In coordination with Oxfam, a Rapid Needs Assessment was carried out in September 2022 by ESDC for villages in northwest Ramallah and in northwest Jerusalem, and by LRC in Bethlehem and Hebron. The assessment included key informant interviews and FGDs in the villages, targeting 50 community members (18 of them are women). The results of the assessment indicated that the needs of the communities were primarily focused on protecting their livelihoods and alleviating economic pressures that are reinforced by the overall economic conditions in Area C. In Khalet Al May in Yatta (Hebron), for example, is surrounded by three Israeli settlements and a road that is used by the Israelis in the middle of the village. The population of 9000 people has an unemployment rate of around 30%, where women account for more than 50% of that number. Um el Kheir village (south of Yatta) of 300 residents has received numerous military orders and demolitions, reaching a total of 63 home military orders and 18 demolition incidents in the past few years.In Al Ramadin, the Municipality reported an increase in the number of military orders and unemployment, estimating it to be around 40% of its population. Similarly, for Kharbet Um el Kheir and Der Samet villages, the village council members and community members reported an increase in fear of demolition orders, and many have fallen into poverty due to their inabilities to fully make use of their agricultural lands and respective unemployment. This brings additional pressures to the wider economic uncertainty that prevents the population from achieving their livelihood stability and resilience. This results in having direct impact on food security, protection from external threats and from domestic risks resulting from fragile social cohesion, and from gender norms typically affecting women more negatively than men. Residents of all the targeted villages describe their situations as very harsh, with no support provided from any organization.

2 - Activities

In order to contribute to contribute to increasing the resilience of vulnerable families, farmers and herders in marginalized communities in the West Bank to restore and protect their livelihoods, ESDC, LRC and Oxfam will implement activities targeting 855 HHs.ESDC will implement activities in Jerusalem and Ramallah Area C villages, while LRC will implement activities in Area C in Bethlehem and Hebron. The proposed project will address the increased unemployment and livelihood losses of agricultural workers related to movement restrictions and other coercive socio-economic conditions through the provision of CfW activities and MPCA (Output 1). The project's activities will also support the rehabilitation efforts of vulnerable HHs and farmers through the restoration of their productive capacities in addition to income-generating support for women (Output 2). Moreover, the project will increase the awareness of affected farmers/herders on protection measures and legal actions to support their livelihoods (Output 3).The partners will hold the overall responsibility of implementing the project's activities, in coordination with Oxfam and relevant stakeholders. Oxfam will be responsible for cash delivery, aiming to build the partners’ knowledge and capacity in cash assistance for future interventions, along with overall supervision of joint and harmonized procurement modalities, cash for work, and coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA). All project activities were designed in coordination with the MoA and will be fully coordinated and agreed upon at the directorate level at the start of the project. Oxfam will be responsible for coordinating activities in the field and with the MoA. Oxfam will provide overall oversight and monitoring of activities, as well as guidance and training on safe-programming and safe delivery of implementation. Project community committees (PCCs) will be established at the start of the project and will include representatives of village councils (VC), the MoA/DoA, project partners, women, and youth CBOs. The project activities will include: Output 1: Increased employment and livelihood activities for vulnerable farmers and HHs through the provision of CfW and cash assistance to support their basic daily needs.Activity 1.1: Cash for Work (CfW) activities will be conducted, by ESDC and LRC, targeting unemployed agricultural and cooperative workers in Area C. Specifically, this intervention will target those willing to work for 25 days for a total amount of 2000 ILS (80 ILS per day). Selection of beneficiaries will be conducted through a fair and transparent process, through the project's community committee and public announcements in the village councils. The CfW activity will be carried out in Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, and Jerusalem localities, reaching 360 workers (100 women, and 260 men).Activity 1.2: Multi-purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) will be implemented, by ESDC and LRC, targeting 100 HHs affected by the Israeli demolitions, or those at direct risk of military orders. The selection criteria were set in coordination with Cash Working Group in the West Bank (WBCWG) and will be further refined depending on the groups at risk/affected by the situation on the ground. The transfer value agreed amongst the CWG is 700 ILS, as a temporary best calculation for emergency response.Output2:Increased production capacities and income of vulnerable poultry farmers and women in agricultural work.Activity2.1: Provision of poultry farms rehabilitation, implemented by ESDC and LRC, to small-scale poultry farmers (100 farms) This will include the provision and rehabilitation of animal sheds, including feeders, water tanks and water drinkers to support farmers to cope with climate change impacts and recuperate from demolitions to their productive assets. Selection will be carried out through announcements in the community through the village councils. Parameters for selection will include the level of poverty, protection related concerns, the ability to work and maintain the farm, and finally previous experience in the field.Activity 2.2: Establishment of income generating activities (IGAs), by ESDC and LRC, for 45 women who are engaged in agricultural work in the localities of northwest Jerusalem and northwest Ramallah. Women will be selected through announcements in the community, through CBOs and village councils. An application process will then include parameters on family structure (so female headed HHs will be prioritized), previous experience, lack of stable income or employment, and protection related concerns evident at the household, such as PwD and family , members with chronic diseases. IGAs will planned jointly with project team; modality will be developed after consultation with the community – whether it is cash modality or procurement depends on the nature of the IGA and the type of support needed from the project teamOutput 3: Increased awareness of 250 affected farmers/herders and their HHs on protection measures and legal actions to support their livelihoods. Activity 3.1: Carry out public awareness sessions on legal protection measures, implemented by LRC, to increase affected farmers/herders (Area C) knowledge on ways to handle military orders and to protect their rights in the face of the threats and intimidation. The awareness raising efforts will target 250 community members from at least 10 targeted areas, giving the priority to active women who are more engaged as members of women representative bodies such as community committees.Oxfam will collect disaggregated data by sex, age and disability for all project beneficiaries. Oxfam has provided training on safe programming to its partner staff working in its humanitarian programme, particularly in the field and those responsible for cash and voucher assistance. It will ensure that risks related to access of cash are continuously analyzed and mitigated.

3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common Global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA), the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. CAMSA is an approach based on minimum requirements for MEL and Social Accountability and is aligned to Oxfam’s program quality efforts, providing guidance, tools and methodologies for various topics, such as gender analysis and assessment of gender outcomes. Oxfam and its partners are committed to the EU Data Protection rules represented by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and will enforce Oxfam and partners’ staff members to strictly consider its rules in all data processing and beneficiaries' database, including accountability mechanisms. Oxfam is also committed to participatory MEAL in which partners, service providers, project stakeholders and beneficiaries have active and significant involvement in monitoring and evaluation actions through the continuous and regular consultation in the entire project cycle. All targeted groups, project beneficiaries and stakeholders are considered as influencers to the design, implementation and results of all monitoring actions, including needs assessments FGDs, baseline/end line surveys, project reviews, and evaluations. Monitoring of project actions will be conducted at two levels: Quality Monitoring: Oxfam’s MEAL staff will lead the design of the project's logical framework, theory of change, MEAL plan and monitoring, project internal and external evaluations, and learning. The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive and results-oriented MEAL practices, whilst ensuring accountability towards the affected populations, throughout project implementation.Technical Monitoring: Oxfam FSVL technical team and partners will ensure that the quality of implementation is in line with technical standards, action plan and budget, as well as baseline and end-line data collection. Oxfam and partners will conduct direct implementation of the project's actions with day-to-day follow up by their field teams and experts. Oxfam has designed specific monitoring mechanisms for food security programs which consist of the following monitoring actions:1. Targeting HHs verification: Targeting HHs verification: It aims to ensure that all selected households fit with the selection criteria, updating their contact information through which they will receive the payment related information.2. Baseline Assessment: To identify the pre-intervention status of the project's outputs and indicators. regards to the baseline methodology, the main tool for data collection is the Household Questionnaire, which is a structured questionnaire that seeks for quantitative and qualitative data to measure the following indicators:· The livelihood Coping Strategy Index (LCSI) based on the cash working group (CWG) agreed criteria (None, Stress, Crises, emergency). Baseline: 80% of HHs, either crises or emergency; Target: 40% of the HHs crises or emergency· The Food Consumption Score (FCS) (Poor, Borderline, Acceptable); Baseline: 80% poor and borderline scores, Target: less than 10% poor, and more than 50% Acceptable· % of households who report being able to meet their basic needs, as they define and prioritize them. Baseline:0, endline: 80%· % of households to have improved access to income and effective livelihoods Baseline: 0, endline: 50%· # of vulnerable unemployed farmers and non-farmers to be engaged in CFW activities Baseline:0, Target: 360 (100 Women, 260 men)· # of poultry farms to be rehabilitated by the project end Baseline:0, Target: 100 · # of people to be reached by legal awareness sessions by the project end Baseline:0, Target: 250 3. Payment Verification: The payment verification process aims to ensure that the MPCA targeted HHs received the allocated cash assistance in a safe and dignified way.4. Post Distribution Monitoring: PDM aims to identify the progress value of the project indicators, measured under the baseline, and to provide real time recommendations to the project implementation team. 5. End-line Assessment: the endline assessment aims to identify the post-intervention status of the project's outputs and indicators. The end-line methodology will use the same tools of the baseline, a Household Questionnaire, to collect post-intervention data.6. Learning Event: The final learning event workshop will be conducted to present and disseminate the key findings of the assessment actions, including the baseline, PDM, HH verification, payment verification, and the end-line. The presentations will be “recommendation focused” on both policies and procedures of the social transfer programs and modalities in Gaza. Feedback Mechanism: Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) provides wider access to diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints at project implementation level. This system uses Information Communication Technology (ICT) for receiving feedback, either through an online link, verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels; such as the free helpline, text messages, Missed Calls, and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and Oxfam’s Global Safeguarding Team, and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy. Moreover, media and communication tools will be utilized during public awareness sessions to raise the targeted population’s awareness on safe and responsive feedback tools. However, all received feedback and complaints will be reported and managed by utilizing the ICT tool. Furthermore, minor or major issues will be followed by programmatic action and response to complaints providers.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

In order to adequately respond to the various needs of the target groups, various modalities have been selected for this project: The Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) modality will be used towards those who are affected by shock/sudden change to their livelihoods and basic needs, including those affected by the Israeli demolitions, or those at direct risk of military orders. Therefore, the MPCA will provide them with flexibility and capacity to access their basic needs without restriction or conditions. The transfer will be carried out in three rounds to ensure their needs are met.The Cash for Work (CfW) modality will be used to increase the support the livelihoods of unemployed agricultural and cooperative workers, enhancing their access to flexible cash to meet their basic needs, including food.The In-kind Assistance modality will be used for the rehabilitation of poultry farms and IGAs, specifically animal sheds, as this is considered to be the most effective and preferred modality in these communities, delivered with the high quality and with easier access to beneficiaries through suppliers/contractors hired under the project that were deemed to the most suitable.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam's Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides broader access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive online feedback through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels, such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam's MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud will be referred to Oxfam's Country Director and safeguarding team in line with Oxfam's confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Field supervisors will be handling inquiries and operational complaints from beneficiaries by making their numbers available to all beneficiaries. This mechanism will help resolve issues in the field, such as prices, access to shops, changing contact information, and others. All feedback and complaints will be handled responsibly following Oxfam Responsible Data Policy which ensures its confidentiality to protect the identity of those providing data, unless otherwise outlined and agreed to in the informed consent. Oxfam will ensure that the process of collecting feedback, and the feedback channels, are in an environment where the privacy of the participant (or group of participants in the case of focus group discussions) is upheld. Oxfam and its partners will not discuss or share in any form, information gathered from the feedback channels with any unauthorized (by Oxfam) persons. Oxfam will also ensure to close the loop of the received feedback within a maximum of 2 weeks, while initial action will be taken in 48 hours.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The action, in alignment with Oxfam programming parameters, will be premised on the grounds that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, both direct and indirect, must be protected. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle, to ensure that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to essential protection needs. This is particularly important in the Gaza context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e., on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam, monitored and adjusted if the context changes. These will address and take into account risks like COVID-19, risks related to safe programming, safeguarding, and potential gender specific risks. The Safeguarding focal point will carry out a refresher session for the project team on the overall protection principles, equipping them with the knowledge and tools to conduct a risk analysis and mitigation measures.As Oxfam is committed to following a participatory and an inclusive approach, all activities have been designed in close coordination and full engagement with the target communities. The targeted communities will also be involved in the implementation and monitoring of all activities that aim to respond to vulnerability in all its forms, ensuring its reach to all marginalized people (including children, youth, older people, people with disabilities, ethnic groups and others who are marginalized due to their social status). Specifically, this project will work towards:Participation: We will systematically engage with all affected people and encourage active and meaningful participation of the most marginalized, including people with disabilities, to ensure that their views and capacities are reflected and built into all aspects of the response including assessment, design, delivery and monitoring and evaluation.Data: We will collect, fully disaggregate, and utilize data for different population groups in order to provide robust evidence to design, revise and learn from programming that is reflective of and appropriate for the people and needs identified.Oxfam has already carried out assessments through previous projects, resulting in learning and applying corrective measures during the implementation of the project. Regarding the protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Oxfam has a designated PSEA team, including integrity and safeguarding focal points, as well as confidential counsellors trained on PSEA, that is responsible for ensuring that all field staff comply with Oxfam’s SEA policy. Oxfam’s PSEA focal point is trained as a trainer of trainers by the UN PSEA Network, which is expected to have a greater role in further enhancing awareness on the PSEA and improving current mechanisms and systems in partner organizations.
