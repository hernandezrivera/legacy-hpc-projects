Project Summary

In camp 2E PHC, we will work closely with BDRCS/SRC to deliver comprehensive SRH services (ANC, PNC, FP, MR, STI treatment , CMR and PAC) including normal vaginal delivery (NVD) services. The IRC will dedicate its efforts to strengthening the management of NCDs in its PHCs to address the increasing NCD health service requirements of the population. These facilities will also be complemented by comprehensive diagnostic laboratories, enabling proper diagnosis and reducing the need for unnecessary referrals to secondary or tertiary care facilities. IRC will also pilot of the World Health Organization (WHO) framework for meaningful engagement of people living with NCDs, specifically focusing on diabetes and hypertension. This includes the implementation of self-care management practices in humanitarian settings and the utilization of the Health sector and WHO NCD-facility based monitoring guidance.
The IRC is committed to continuing its community health initiatives, coordinating with the Community Health Working Group (CHWG) within Rohingya camps, with a particular focus on camps 6,11,22, 25 , Kutupalong and Nayapara RC. Our goal is to empower Rohingya individuals by increasing their knowledge of health-seeking behaviors, with a particular emphasis on Communicable diseases,MNCH, SRH, and NCDs.
Also , IRC will support to Emergency patient referrals to tertiary hospital of Coxsbazar and Chittagong which includes patient transport cost, Patient food cost, medicine cost and Investigation cost.
In line with Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) guidelines, the IRC will sustain Five surge Mobile Medical Teams (MMTs) in camps 6, 11, 22, and 25. These MMTs will be ready to respond to natural disasters and artificial hazards prevalent in Cox's Bazar, including fires, monsoon rains, cyclones, floods, and epidemics, through training and simulations. We ensure timely and regular reporting of health services to DHIS2, 4Ws, and the Early Warning, Alert, and Response System (EWARS). We also address gender norms to influence behavior and encourage male engagement in MNCH, family planning (FP), and SRH. We will consider the diverse needs of children, adolescents, youth, and the elderly, incorporating various factors such as gender, age, disability, and ethnicity, and employ Sex, Age, and Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) for program quality improvement. Furthermore, the IRC actively promotes Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) by building the capacity of project staff members, producing community-facing content, and assisting in establishing Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM) while analyzing feedback for program development.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) add to the health burden in the camps, with 32.2% of adults diagnosed as hypertensive and 11% living with diabetes. Factors like smoking, poor diet, and obesity contribute to these high rates, intensifying the demand for healthcare. In 2023, Cox’s Bazar saw 5.55 million outpatient consultations, with each person seeking healthcare on average 3.83 times per year. Despite this high service usage, many refugees and host community members continue to face obstacles in accessing quality care. According to the November 2023 Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), over half of the population (59% of Rohingya and 55% of the host community) reported needing health services, and around 11% of Rohingya and 12% of host community members were unable to access the healthcare they needed.The Cox’s Bazar Health Sector Strategic Plan (2023-2025) prioritizes a strong referral system with a 24/7 standard for Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs). However, limited funding restricts the effective implementation of this system. Noteworthy progress has been made in facility-based deliveries, which rose from 12% in 2018 to 89% in 2023, yet maternal mortality remains high, with a 2024 maternal mortality rate (MMR) of 186 per 100,000 live births. Over 60% of maternal deaths were due to preventable causes, and a significant number of neonatal deaths and stillbirths occurred at home, underlining the need for accessible healthcare facilities and community outreach.By mid-2024, 248 perinatal deaths had been reported, with a high rate of stillbirths indicating delays in seeking care. Addressing these gaps through strengthened healthcare access and maternal health services is essential to improve the well-being of refugees and the host community alike.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The proposed project will focus on integrated protection intervention. The IRC will ensure access to protection mechanisms to increase capacity to stay safe, exercise basic rights, and access essential services. IRC will ensure integration within the protection (CP, GP, and GBV) sector. Particular attention should be dedicated to all mechanisms to protect refugees from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation by from anyone including humanitarian workers. The IRC will ensure staff are trained on humanitarian code of conduct, Child Safeguarding, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH).All part of the health facilities will be cover with adequate lighting with required security guards to make sure all parts of the health facilities are safe for every client and the staff working in these health facilities. There will be separate space (e.g. rooms and screening process) to isolate any client of contagious diseases to reduce the transmission of CD diseases to other clients as pert of IRC’s “No Harm” policy. Health facilities will have adequate medical waste management system (e.g. waste segregation, incinerator) to avoid causing infection and or contaminating the environment including water.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project evaluates client risks and needs with a strong emphasis on age diversity. Services are customized to meet specific requirements, including in-depth assessments for those aged 40 and over, specialized sensitivity for children from 0 to 5, and distinct content and services for adolescent boys and girls, carefully considering cultural factors. Child safeguarding is a primary organizational commitment and is part of a mandatory reporting protocol. Preventive and responsive measures are implemented to promote safeguarding awareness, including thorough sessions for staff and incentive workers who regularly interact with clients. We aim to address the diverse needs of children, adolescents, youth, and elderly individuals, incorporating considerations of gender, age, disability, and ethnicity. To enhance program quality, we will utilize Sex, Age, and Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD). Additionally, IRC will provide orientation on disability for healthcare staff and evaluate health facilities to address any infrastructure requirements for individuals with disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The IRC is deeply committed to placing our clients at the heart of our humanitarian response, ensuring that our actions are guided by their aspirations and feedback. This client-centered approach enhances the effectiveness, relevance, and appropriateness of our services, while fostering accountability to our clients, affected communities, and other key stakeholders. To uphold our Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitment, the IRC will continue to operate Help Desks at Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs) across five camp locations, including camps 2E, 6, 11, 22, 25, as well as in the Kutupalong and Nayapara RC health facilities. To facilitate feedback, we have established Suggestion Boxes and Hotline Numbers, allowing clients to share complaints and feedback directly. Additionally, we will engage with the community through one-on-one discussions, focus group discussions (FGDs), post-distribution monitoring, and "client satisfaction surveys" to assess and enhance community satisfaction with our services.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The IRC is committed to integrating PSEA into the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh by following a comprehensive framework, including the IRC’s safeguarding policy, the Core Humanitarian Standards, and the Minimum Operating Standards for PSEA (MOS-PSEA). The IRC will ensure that all staff and partners are fully informed of, and comply with, the organization’s codes of conduct and safeguarding policies. A rigorous hiring process will include screening for any history of SEA, including self-declarations by candidates. The Senior Management Team (SMT) will lead policy implementation, fostering a zero-tolerance culture with ongoing training on PSEA. Dedicated Safeguarding staff and PSEA Focal Points will work with the project team and PSEA Network to align PSEA. IRC will offer tailored, context-specific PSEA training for staff and partners, especially frontline workers, to build capacity in a “do no harm” approach and confidentiality. Community engagement is key: IRC will develop complaint and feedback mechanisms that are accessible, safe, and clear to beneficiaries at service points. A survivor-centered approach ensures survivors' safety, confidentiality, and access to support services without the obligation to formally report. Feedback mechanisms, including in-person reports, hotlines, and complaint boxes, will allow for reporting across genders and ages. IRC maintains a mandatory reporting policy for SEA involving minors under 18. As the reporting of SEA is a mandatory requirement, IRC is committed to notifying the donor and relevant stakeholders of any SEA concerns. High-quality investigations will be conducted with the intent to support survivors, hold perpetrators accountable, and strengthen prevention. Regular field visits, interviews, and focus groups will monitor implementation, share best practices, and improve program quality.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

To reduce its carbon footprint, the PHCC has implemented the use of solar energy, significantly decreasing reliance on fossil fuel-powered generators. In an effort to minimize plastic waste, the IRC has introduced paper bags for clients to carry their medications, replacing plastic bags. The PHCC has also adopted a comprehensive waste management system, ensuring proper disposal of biological medical waste through methods like incineration and segregated disposal in designated pits, including the Ash Pit, Organic Pit, and Placenta Pit. Medical sharps are safely stored in "Sharp Boxes" and managed by a qualified vendor specializing in their disposal.Additionally, fire safety is a top priority at the facility, with annual fire safety assessments conducted to evaluate the resilience of the building. IRC also provides regular training in fire safety and first aid for staff. As part of its emergency response plan, IRC organizes Mass Casualty Incident Management training for Medical Management Teams (MMTs) to ensure preparedness for any crisis situation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

As part of its Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures, the health facility has a comprehensive Emergency Response Plan in place. In the event of a natural disaster or emergency, Medical Management Teams (MMTs) are always on standby, ready to respond swiftly. The facility also maintains a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for ensuring the continuation of operations during emergencies. Additionally, a stockpile of essential medicines and medical supplies is consistently maintained to support emergency response efforts, ensuring the facility and MMT is well-equipped to handle any crisis.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The IRC places strong emphasis on both clinical care and community awareness among youth. Through our Young Support Group initiative, we aim to empower adolescents aged 10-19, equipping them with the knowledge to make informed decisions about birth spacing, family planning, and facility-based deliveries. This approach promotes their well-being and encourages responsible choices for a healthier future.
