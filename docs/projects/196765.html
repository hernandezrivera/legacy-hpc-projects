Project Summary

The proposed project aims at addressing emergency and more sustainable shelter needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northwest Syria.
Through contractors, ACTED will rehabilitate private off-camp shelter units to 3,258 individuals in Aleppo governorate.
ACTED will take into account the different risks and concerns affecting women, men, girls and boys and people with disabilities when designing this intervention. For the shelter rehabilitation activity, ACTED will ensure to select the households also based on vulnerability, with consideration for income and/or demographics. ACTED will develop BoQs for each shelter based on technical shelter assessments. The assessment of the shelter will take into consideration privacy needs, as well as specific needs for people with disabilities when relevant and particular works will be incorporated as necessary. This will ensure safe and dignified living conditions to the targeted HHs in the area of intervention.

3 - Needs

Across Syria, over 5.5 million persons are expected to be in need of shelter assistance in 2023, thus a 8 per cent increase since 2022, as per the (unreleased) 2023 HNO. In NWS, nearly 1.7 million persons live across over 1,300 displacement sites spread across numerous camp clusters in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, highly vulnerable to adverse weather, including snowstorms, strong winds, and floods. In January 2022, snowstorms and floods affected 287 displacement sites in NWS, resulting in the damage to nearly 10,000 tents and the complete destruction of nearly 1,000 tents.Shelter destruction and limited livelihoods to conduct necessary repairs put already vulnerable populations at risk, especially in winter. IDPs in camp settings in NWS endured an exceptionally harsh 2021-22 winter: in NWS, according to REACH, 63% of assessed communities reported that no IDP households owned their shelter, and that at least one fifth of the IDP population lived in tents. Increasing shelter adequacy issues, such as limited electricity/lighting, poor insulation from heat/cold and lack of privacy is exposing households to harsh weather conditions with a high risk of loss of life, particularly during winter.

4 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will provide shelter rehabilitation support out of camp in order to provide IDPs and vulnerable host communities with safe and dignified places to live: ACTED will provide off-camp private shelter repair works across Aleppo governorate in NWS for 3,258 individuals.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups’ needs during project design/implementation. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach. ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct covering Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV),and ACTED implements Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects. Risk 3: Eviction of beneficiaries from the household upon the completion of the rehabilitation works. Likelihood: Medium – Impact: High. Mitigation Measure: A strong HLP due diligence process and MoUs with the rehabilitated buildings' owners will mitigate eviction risks and related conflicts. Where possible, in order to reinforce sustainability of the agreement. ACTED will also ensure that a Shelter Beneficiary Agreement (SBA) is signed between the involved stakeholders (owner of the house/apartment, ACTED and the contractor). These contracts will contribute to securing tenure for the displaced families for at least twelve months after the SBA signature.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behaviour of the contractor employees towards camp residents and beneficiaries in targeted villages. Information on the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be communicated to camp manager, community leaders, camp residents and targeted individuals in out of camp areas. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. ACTED will ensure that out-of-camp population are particularly aware of the CRM and tools to provide complaints (WhatsApp group, letterbox, desks). Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff in the teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.
