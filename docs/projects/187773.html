Project Summary

The main goal of the project is to deliver immediate humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable IDP people in/off camps and member of host communities (HC) in Northwest Syria (NWS). It is planned to assist nearly 5,000 households (HHs) through food e-voucher assistance to cover their needs in areas of food security. The value of voucher is planned to be $60 per HH per month for min 8 months. The vendors in the targeted area will be assessed and contracted for the redemption process. Considering the price of basic food commodities remains volatile and is subject to speculation, and disruptive events, specifically displacement and COVID-19 have harmful and immediate consequences on the households’ economy and may result in reduced consumption and exclusion, requiring the adoption of a framework of priorities and actions that are focused on livelihoods. Therefore, the activity will help them to increase their food security while reducing the negative coping strategies.. The conceptualization of this project has been with engagement of the targeted communities and relevant stakeholders, following a multi-stakeholder approach that enables ownership, complementarity, sustainability and integration of the project interventions. The project aligns with and contributes to the HNO-2022 by providing safe humanitarian aid responding to the context, minimizing the spread of the coronavirus, and adapting different modalities in the implementation of interventions.

4 - Needs

The following assessments were taken into account for this proposal: 17/12/2021-WHH- Multisectoral Need Assessment (RNA) stated the top 3 priority needs for HHs are Food (87%), Employment/livelihood opportunities (53%) &Medical care(37%). The assessment findings suggest that more households reported using strategies limiting the intake of food (by means of limiting portions or reducing meals per day) than relying on support from others. Almost all households reported shifting towards cheaper and less quality food items in the last month; 28/12/2021 WHH Gender Analysis reported majority of HHs feel that any members of the community were at risk of explosive hazards (76%) followed by the risk of exclusion from humanitarian assistance(30%). Physical violence, sexual violence/assault and verbal harassment risks were all reported at below 5%. When asked which groups were the most affected by these risks, all the HHs reported to be men, and the other (women, girls, and boys) were reported at 81% each; 31/12/2021 WHH-Rapid Market Assessment indicates decreased purchased power, increased fuel prices&C-19 are the main difficulties vendors to continue their business. According to HNO, food insecurity has grown(around 12.8 million people are severely food insecure), Syria ranked amongst the ten most food-insecure countries globally by mid-2021 where access to food became an urgent concern.

5 - Implementation of activities

According to the WHH RNA, the economic vulnerabilities of households impacts their ability to meetbasic needs, such as food. Indeed, households which have already spent their savings or incurred largedebts to meet basic needs such as food potentially risk being unable to absorb economic shocks, such asloss of a livelihood source, or unanticipated expenditures, such as emergency medical needs. In response to above specified multiple needs, the proposed project will seek to provide food e-voucher to IDPs and HC members

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WHH focuses on applying protection mainstreaming (both through the principled approach and in the technical areas of operations), primarily following the guidance of the Global Protection Cluster (GPC). While implementing protection mainstreaming throughout project cycle, WHH is pursuing four elements: prioritizing safety & dignity and avoiding or at least mitigating the effects of (internal or external) physical and psychological threats faced by affected population when accessing humanitarian services, arranging for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e. g. discrimination), setting up appropriate accessible mechanisms, through which affected populations can evaluate the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints in a participatory or involving way and finally supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. WHH adheres to the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), the Sphere Technical Standards, the Code of Conduct of International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and NGOs in Disaster Relief and to other international standards and commitments. The aim is to improve the quality and effectiveness of its humanitarian assistance and to support greater accountability to communities and people affected by crisis. WHH's mandate is to end hunger.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) are unacceptable breaches offundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. The sexual exploitation and abuseof those who depend on WHH for assistance runs counter to all our personal and organisational values.It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassmentof our fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has significantly stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, and taking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UN system, donors, and civil society organizations.Strategy covers both SEA and SH, bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. The Strategy also acknowledges the complexity and limitation to WHH’s authority in relation to SEA perpetrated by implementing partner personnel, including Government personnel. However, it does include measures to address and prevent these violations including supporting partners to fully integrate PSEA into programmes in both development and humanitarian contexts, creating a joined-up accountability culture that abhors, sanctions and prevents SEA and protects victims.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation in Afrin is characterized by a high risk of explosive devices and presence of armed groups. WHH employs a balance of individuals recruited from the target area as well as other parts of Syria. WHH generally works on the basis of the acceptance strategy (acceptance by the local population through high-quality and rapid assistance) and good relations with the target groups. The work of WHH strictly follows the principles of neutrality and impartiality in relation to political, religious, military or other interest groups. In general, a low profile is pursued. The project team coordinates with the regional security advisor and local security officer. They are in constant contact with responsible information services (UN, Turkish border officials, AFAD, camp managers, local councils etc.) and exchange with local staff, Syrian NGOs and community members. The information is communicated promptly to personnel using a predefined communication system. All posted WHH employees are expected to complete security training, COVID-19 training, Code of Conducts training and all personnel are expected to attend first aid course. WHH regularly participates in the INSO security briefings, NGO Forum and the Access Working Group. WHH uses SOPs and chains of communication, and personnel movement in the project area is monitored. WHH has a duty of care policy and C19 contingency plan. Covid-19 stands as a big risk, therefore, necessary COVID-19 precautionary measures.
