Project Summary

In line with strategic objective 3, SO3: Support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks and cluster objectives 2 and 4 : CLO2 food production for the most vulnerable households: Increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through y agricultural seeds and fishing inputs, CLO 4: Dependency reduction on food and agricultural input support: Prevent and reverse the erosion of vulnerable rural & urban households and community assets through livelihood & conditional cash based support for more sustainable livelihoods; RuCAPD seeks to help the populations in Panyikang , Manyo, Fashoda, Baliet in Upper Nile state, Juba, Lainya and Morobo in Central Equatorial as well as Canal Pigi in Jonglei state move through recovery and resilience toward development through own food production livelihood support and livestock health services. This project is designed to effectively contribute to the protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods of the most vulnerable and affected population in Fashoda County, Upper Nile State. The project is a 12 month project targeting 20,000 HH equal to 120,000 vulnerable individuals IDPs -52,800, returnees 45,000, and host community 22,200; of whom 37800 are Men, 58200 are women, 12000 are boys and 12000 are girls. The targeting will be done with gender lens and consideration of incorporation of Accountability to affected population. We expect to train a total of 300 HH on different agronomy practices as well as 140 Community Animal Health Workers (CAWHs) for the livestock aspect of the project. Vouchers will be used in the seed fair project sites. The beneficiaries will use them to get the seeds they want and the traders will then redeem them for cash. The program implementation will ensure that Do No Harm principle is adhered to as transparency and accountability to the affected population is placed right at the center of beneficiary targeting and provision of support. RuCAPD extensive presence and history in Upper Nile, Central Equatorial and Jonglei coupled with an in-depth understanding of the community conflict dynamics and context tailored approaches to specific communities will help identify, analyze and manage risks as well as reduce any potential negative impacts of the project and any protection issues.
The project will receive pipeline supplies from FAO whilst the requested funding from SSHF will be used to provide front line services. The project will use direct distribution to the targeted groups through a transparent process of identification and distribution. In case of security hitches, RUCAPD has extensive network of how to reach the targeted beneficiaries.

Needs

According to Sept 2018 IPC analysis, 6.1M people, about 60% of the total population, are in IPC Phase 3/worse acute food insecurity at the lean season of July – Aug2018 (Sept2018 IPC, Joint FAO/UNICEF/WFP news release). Those who faced acute food insecurity consisted of 21% children <5years, 7% elderly and 7% pregnant and lactating mothers (Sept2018 IPC). The current desperate food shortages is attributed to conflict and insecurity in the country. Conflict makes it impossible for farmers to improve their own resilience and food security situations (Sept2018 IPC). They can only produce food that is sufficient for consumption for 2.9 months (FSNMS21). Renewed and widespread conflicts hinder of humanitarian access thus hunger driven by conflict related displacement, disruption to livelihoods and markets, lack of access to social services and restricted trade due to inaccessible roads during the rainy season (FAO/UNICEF/WFP news release). Protracted economic crisis despite the improved currency against the USD, soaring food prices, a high cereal crop deficit and drought in parts of the country (FSNMS21). Upper Nile continued to face IDP displacements even in 2018 (IOM DTM2018).Panyikang is expected to be in catastrophe /phase 5 (IPCSept 2018). This situation is expected to worsen in areas where they experienced poor harvests, expect influx of populations from within and outside the country and lack of freedom of movement (Sept2018 IPC). Upper Nile is the second state experiencing the highest HH migration at 4.3ppl per HH (FSNMS 21). Acute food insecurity is therefore expected to persist in Panyikang. HH are expected to deplete food stocks from own production early 2019 (Sept2018 IPC).The severe food insecurity, poor diets, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity and poor sanitation and hygiene caused alarming levels of acute malnutrition (Sept2018 IPC). Huge decline in the acceptable food consumption levels in the country (FSNMS21).The crop watch analysis positive results attributed to most places experiencing improved security, better availability of inputs and interest by farmers to produce (FAO operational update, 11Oct2018). Farming in Panyikang 2018 was low due to the uncertainty of the peace process which reduced the interest of the farmers to produce. Further complicated by prolonged drought. The main source of livelihood for people is fishing (RuCAPD, 2018).Despite the great potential for farming in South Sudan but the global the outbreak of Fall Armyworm is a real challenge (FAO news release, Jul24 2018).Livestock is one of the main contributors of food security in rural areas of South Sudan. It faces threat due to lack of veterinary services, challenge of pests and diseases (FSNMS 21). The conflict has continued to disrupt availability of animal health services to the communities. The government has not been able to provide veterinary service to most of the areas. Closure of trade routes has resulted in unavailability of livestock drugs. Normal mobility and migration of livestock has been disrupted, livestock being concentrated in small areas and migrating through previously not used routes. Creating favourable conditions for development and rapid spread of diseases even from endemic to non-endemic Areas. The remoteness calls for RuCAPD veterinarians to intervene. Exposing communities to zoonosis. The livestock of the most of the community members died due to outbreak of diseases and lack of access to veterinary services. RuCAPD has other projects in the area thus will integrate and build on the so much needed uninterrupted multisectoral humanitarian assistance. (Sept 2018 IPC). Multisectoral implementation will improve IPC projections (FAO/UNICEF/WFP news release). Crop, vegetable,fisheries production,livestock treatment and vaccination increase HH diversification and access to food within their communities. They are able to sell the surplus in the local markets (FAO news release, July 11 2018).

Protection Risk Analysis?

Briefly describe how

The identification of risks to food security faced by households and groups, location-specific criteria for differentiating wealth categories, and identification of key leverage points and opportunities to pursue in future interventions. Accordingly, information will be dis-aggregated by income level, gender, age as well as other key factors. In this way, the information gained will facilitate analysis of risk and vulnerability to food insecurity that is contextualized and differentiated according to specific geographic areas and populations sections.The risk analysis will consider : Physiological risks and vulnerabilities for example People living with HIV/AIDS, disabled, chronically ill; Socio-economic risks and vulnerabilities -children, women, men, boys and girls disenfranchised; Political risks and vulnerabilities affecting people based on their ethnicities or political allegiance and affiliations; Physical security – conflict, IDPs ; governance issues- People affected by different shocks. On the same note, the risk analysis will help us understand the potential nature, frequency as well as shocks in our area of intervention. For example: Sudden disasters both natural and slow onset –floods and drought, crop failures, infestations like Fall Army Warm; Emergencies including conflict, cattle raids, large-scale exodus of refugees, IDPs and returnees, genocide; Complex emergencies – unprecedented humanitarian crises, breakdown of government and authority as a result of internal and external conflicts and finally the Long-wave of emergencies also known as silent emergencies for example HIV/AIDS, Cancer, depletion of soil, land ownership and tenure issues, water and other natural resources.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. RuCAPD RuCAPD emphasizes the importance of stakeholders understanding &supporting strategies employed by the at-risk populations to protect themselves and their loved ones from threats to their safety and dignity. This is because they know the internal communication systems very well. The civilian strategies are essential to determining their own safety. Either avoidance, containment or confrontation. Avoidance leads to escaping the threat thus flight or displacement, changing patterns of movement e.g night travels or safer routes or developing information networks & warning systems to alert when there is danger. Containment is living with the threat- paying taxes or protection money, negotiation with the warring parties for safety or submission- e.g ‘marriage’ to the soldiers or commanders. Confrontation is fighting by of self-defence or vigilante groups, or joining either party to the conflict. The concept of community ‘coping strategy’ is contentious in protection since the understanding that people do not ‘cope’ with violence or attack. Protection strategies protect the right to life and personal security shielding people from acts of violence and physical threats; protecting the right to personal liberty e.g from arbitrary detention; as well support movement-related rights and needs like the right to seek safety in another country and return home.
