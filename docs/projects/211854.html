Project Summary

The MSNA conducted by IMC in 5 governorates in Syria highlighted urgent humanitarian needs in sectors including Health, Nutrition, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, Gender-Based Violence, Child Protection, livelihood and WASH. The assessment revealed a dire situation with limited access to essential healthcare services, high malnutrition, psychological distress among population, increased incidents of GBV, and growing concerns related to child protection risks. People faced lack of affordable healthcare, transportation difficulties, limited access to nutrition services, availability of GBV services and awareness gaps on health and protection risks and services. In response, IMC implemented a comprehensive intervention plan to address identified needs. IMC deployed MMTs to provide Primary Health Care services, referrals, nutrition screenings and integrated MHPSS support. IMC also supported both MOH operated and IMC-operated PHCs by providing capacity building, essential trainings, medical supplies, equipment, WASH structure repairs, and solar panels. Specialized GBV and CP programming including comprehensive case management, tailored structured and unstructured Psychosocial Support Services and prevention activities through dedicated facilities. WASH interventions focused on developing user-friendly facilities in PHCs and MMTs locations, with a focus on ensuring accessibility for people with disabilities and gender-sensitive infrastructure. IMC will continue implementing integrated health and nutrition interventions, addressing urgent healthcare and nutritional needs of affected population in targeted areas. These interventions include providing essential primary and secondary health care services, including referrals, mental health consultations, and treatment, nutrition screenings and integrated mental health and psychosocial support. Child protection intervention will be provided using facility-based approach in three centers (in a CFS, community center and family center). These interventions include CP case management, structured PSS using child resilience curriculum, positive parenting, disability & inclusion activities, awareness raising & sensitization, establishment of community-based groups and training for staff and communities. GBV interventions will be provided in three existing facilities (WGSS, community center & family center) and a mobile team. These interventions include GBV case management, structured PSS for women using women rise curriculum, GBV prevention activities using curriculums such as girl shine & EMAP, women empowerment through vocational & life skills, and GBV mitigation through safety audits and dignity kits provision. Also, providing capacity building for GBV and non-GBV staff to maintain a two-way strong internal referral system for specialized SRH services and mental health consultations upholding GBV guiding principles.
A Protection Risk Analysis was conducted by IMC to identify risks to beneficiaries and project implementation. Key risks included loss of access to services due to security concerns, financial losses due to fraud and corruption, disease outbreaks, disruptions in supply chain, and staffing challenges. IMC implemented mitigating measures to address identified risk, including security assessments, anti-fraud mechanisms, health education for disease prevention, procurement contingency planning, and recruitment capacity building. IMC developed a robust (PSEA) strategy to ensure safeguarding of beneficiaries and confidentiality of their data. This strategy included protocols for collecting informed consent from patients, secure data management procedures, and a Community-Based Feedback and Response Mechanism to address complaints and feedback from beneficiaries. IMC committed to providing transparent information about project services, consulting with and engaging beneficiaries throughout the project cycle and maintaining high standards of data protection and privacy.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Furthermore, IMC’s integrated projects aims to address the unique vulnerabilities and challenges faced by different groups within the affected population with a focus most vulnerable groups. These also include children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), who are especially vulnerable in the Syrian health crisis and face elevated risks of maternal and child mortality, malnutrition, reproductive health complications, gender-based violence, and mental health disorders. IMC prioritizes this group, aligning interventions with the targeted sectoral priorities and national and international guidelines. IMC projects ensure comprehensive services, including sexual and reproductive health information and services (including family planning, antenatal and postnatal care), nutrition services, and protection services for women and children. Recognizing the unique challenges faced by persons with disabilities (PWDs), IMC includes them as a priority group in its targeted response. IMC aims to enhance accessibility and inclusivity ensuring that all facilities, including washrooms and toilets (in health facilities, recreational and family centers), specifically designed for PWDs, are accessible. These efforts are dedicated to establishing an environment that caters to diverse needs of PWDs, enhancing their well-being, and encouraging their active participation in community life. Additionally, we are focusing on capacity building of health care providers, solarizing, refurbishing and rehabilitating health facilities, and deploying operational mobile medical teams in rural areas of targeted governorates. Protection activities (GBV and CP programming) specially target vulnerable women and adolescent girls and boys who are exposed to many risks (including domestic violence, early marriage and other) such risks are considered as norms in most cases or as reaction to the ongoing economic situation in others (ex, child labor, school dropouts). In addition, targeting children with disabilities and elderly people also prioritized, particularly in view of the scarcity of facilities and service providers. The MHPSS component focuses on Provision of evidence-based, quality MHPSS services, case management (biopsychosocial assessment, risk assessment and safety planning, care planning, referral, follow up and discharge), as well as individual and group psychological counselling, WRAP Planning and WHO mhGAP and Brief Psychological Interventions such as PM, SH+, EASE and Thinking Healthy. Based on IMC experience, MH case management is one of the main entry points for the identification of protection concerns. Therefore, PSW staff will be trained in CP and GBV CM and clients be served according to their specific needs through internal referrals pathways. PHC doctors undertake assessment, identification, psycho-education and treatment for common mental health conditions, using evidence-based therapies provided by psychotherapists in line with WHO mhGAP-HIG.
