Project Summary

Multi-sectorial emergency response in Eastern Sudan (Kassala/Gedaref) and North Darfur with regard to WASH, FSL, GBV, Shelter/NFIs and Health assistance for refugees. This project mainly targets refugees in East Sudan, specifically influx from Eritrea in Umgargur, Shagarab I, II, III, Abuda camps and influx from Ethiopia in Um Rakuba camp. While in North Darfur the project target South Sudanese Refugees in Al Lait locality. In North Darfur, Kassala and Gedaref the project interlinks with BMZ projects to operationalize the Triple NEXUS.

1 - Needs

With this project WHH undertakes to respond to the needs of refugees (Eritrean, Ethiopian and South Sudanese), in different sectors across 3 states of Sudan. See below detailed description for each State. According to the CRP locality priority list 2021, Al Lait (North Darfur), El Fashaga (Gedaref) and Wad El Helew (Kassala) are #1 priority in terms of needs, while Al Mafaza (Gedaref) and ranks #2. North Darfur: Locality Al Lait In comparison to other localities within North Darfur localities it is very remotely located and a lot of unmet humanitarian needs still exist, due to limited funding opportunities. Despite the efforts already put by various partners to support the local population, there is still a huge gap in access to drinking water, shelter and NFI as well as agricultural inputs that can contribute to saving lives of the SSR. Existence of both the SSR and the IDPs within their host communities is exerting pressure on the existing limited resources such as water points due to competition between the displaced population and the host community members over access to drinking water. The land for crop cultivation is fertile both in Al Lait therefore there is need to support the target group with agricultural inputs for short-term production. Kassala: Localities Wad El Helew and Gedaref: Localities El Fashaga, Al Mafaza. According to UNHCR, almost half of the refugees in the camps live in extreme poverty. Family poverty and lack of livelihoods expose refugee children to protection risks, including child labour, early marriage and dropping out of school. In addition, there is a need for semi-permanent family accommodation, the provision of basic hygiene and household items, especially for new arrivals, and child-friendly and safe housing. In 2022, there will be a continued high demand for drinking water infrastructure and sanitation facilities, such as household latrines, the extension of water pipes and accessible tapping points. In addition, there is a need for support for short-term food production and food use and processing (preservation) to help meet frequent supply shortages. In addition, the project also targets the needs of the Ethiopian refugee influx in the areas of WASH, SFNI and Protection. The aforementioned conditions make the target group particularly vulnerable to diseases, especially in view of the current COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will address the needs of Refugees through a multi-sector response. In the area of WASH the project aims to improve the access to safe and sufficient water supply, through rehabilitation of water sources, such as Hafir, Water pump, etc. The project also aims at improving access to sanitation facilities, through PHAST approach at HHs level and construction of sanitation facilities at community spaces level. Hygiene and COVID-19 awareness-raising is part of this component, but it will be mainstreamed in the project. In the area of FSL, the project aims to provide timely food support and enhance the self-reliance of the target group by providing training in agriculture and livelihood activities, seeds and tools distribution and livestock support. In the area of SNFI, the project aims to provide Refugees with essential SNFI support, including durable solution shelter assistance. Refugees are targeted for protection sensitization which is very relevant to the context since their localities host refugee populations who feature increased protection vulnerability. Under the area of energy, the target population´s access to clean sustainable energy sources (fuel-efficient/solar stoves) is improved. In terms of health, WHH will rehabilitate 4 health facilities, improving access to health services for SSR. The project will be carried out in line with WHH MEAL standards and respective activities such as base/end-line, continuous monitoring and a final evaluation. Specific selection criteria will make sure that most vulnerable groups are addressed taking vulnerability criteria such as age, gender and disability into account.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The intervention area is characterized by resource pressure and degradation of natural resources. Water is a key natural resource which is scarce in all the targeted areas. The project aims to enlarge access to save drinking water through rehabilitation/construction of water sources and reduce the potential for deforestation through promotion of fuel efficient/solar stoves among the target groups. Access to clean energy is further improved by providing solar lighting in public spaces.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHH mainstreams protection into all programs so that activities (i) prioritize safety, dignity and access, in addition to avoiding causing harm, (ii) ensure equality and meaningful access, (iii) include mechanisms for beneficiary accountability, and (iv) emphasize participation and empowerment to meet the proejct minimum requirements. WHH prioritizes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoids causing harm by preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of its interventions that can increase vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. The project will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance and services based on need and without discrimination. WHH considers people with specific needs, ensuring access to assistance and services. Through community-based monitoring, target populations will be able to measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints, thereby supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the right to shelter, food, water, sanitation, etc.WHH will adhere to Sphere Protection Principles to implement activities under this project according the following: (1) Enhance people’s safety, dignity and rights and avoid exposing them to further harm; (2) Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance, according to need and without discrimination; (3) Assist people to recover from the physical and psychological effects of threatened or actual violence, coercion or deliberate deprivation; and (4) Help people claim their rights. These three principles are interdependent and can be carried out at the same time. The principles promote the right to dignity in three ways: • mainstreaming protection risks, activities and related information across all humanitarian programs.• integrating specific protection objectives into assistance projects; and• promoting specific and/or specialized protection activities that address specific protection risks and violations.
