Project Summary

CHADO will carry out about 6500 OPD consultations in the facilities/mobile clinics, facilitate 150 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants, provide 54 quality and timely weekly EWARS/IDSR reports from the 3 focus Counties, facilitate 1000 children receive 3 doses of pentavalent vaccine, carry out 18 community consultative meetings to identify needs of the affected populations.
In addition to the above, CHADO will provide treatment and psychosocial support to 75 mentally ill persons including referrals for specialized psychiatric treatment, manage 3000 uncomplicated diarrhoea cases with ORS and Zinc, manage 5200 uncomplicated malaria cases with ACT, manage 3700 uncomplicated respiratory infection (pneumonia) treated with Amoxicillin.
In community mobilization and sensitization, CHADO will reach 10,000 people with health education and promotion messaging before and during outbreaks.

Needs

Even with the advent of the revitalized peace agreement in late 2018 and the promise of better times to come, the cumulative effects of the conflict lasting half a decade have translated to sustained poverty and persistent humanitarian and protection needs for more than 7 million people. Fashoda and Manyo Counties of Upper Nile State where drivers and multipliers of crisis which have remained present over time include insecurity and violence, local and intercommunal conflicts, ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, economic instability and insecure access to food and livelihoods (South Sudan Draft HNO 2019).South Sudan continue to face significant public health challenges due to conflict. In Upper Nile State, maternal mortality is extremely high (reported at 2,094/100,000 live births compared to the national average at 2,054/100,000). The percentage of women delivering at a health facility is 16.5%. Facilities are mainly run by unskilled staff and deliveries are consequently mainly assisted by unskilled staff. Lack of physical access to health facilities is a huge problem in Fashoda and Manyo as a large part of the 2 Counties is inaccessible during the 7 month rainy season. The infrastructural challenges are immense. Most health facilities do not have the required delivery equipment so privacy and hygiene during delivery are compromised. Other factors hindering access to reproductive health care include: ongoing violent conflict, a high cost of living, widespread illiteracy, a high influx of refugees, and gender inequality.In Fashoda County the 2 PHCC’s and 6 PHCU’s are poorly supported by the CHD. CHADO intends to reach a number of 56,430 people in Fashoda through improved service delivery. While in Manyo has 3 PHCC’s and 2 PHCU’s which will serve about 26,268. In both Fashoda and Manyo, CHADO will 6 complete mobile teams to effectively reach the underserved population in the 2 Counties. In Akobo East (Walgak zone, there is 1 PHCC+ and 4 PHCU’s.In the proposed geographic areas Akobo County (Akobo East, Walgak) of Jonglei State, malaria, acute respiratory infections (ARI) and acute watery and bloody diarrhea, cholera and SAM continue to account for the highest proportion of diseases among internally displaced persons (IDPs). As a result of the disruption in health services due to the intense fighting and the precarious sanitary conditions in the in those, there has since been an increase in morbidity and mortality rates due to traumatic injuries, an increase in water , food and vector borne related diseases in displaced populations and other vulnerable groups. There is also a heightened risk of outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases, including meningitis and measles and cholera. Pregnant women are at greater risk of complications from obstetric emergencies because of the lack of access to health care services. There is also a risk of increased incidents of sexual violence, and an increase in the number of people requiring mental health and psychosocial support.The major causes of morbidity and mortality in those areas are preventable illnesses such as Acute Respiratory Infections, Malaria and Acute Watery Diarrhea and cholera. Among vulnerable populations, health indicators are often more dire. Population displacement has disrupted vital access to health services, increasing vulnerability to ill health for individuals and families. Areas where there are inadequate referral systems for complicated cases such as emergency obstetric care and limited availability of resources from the County Health Department to maintain and expand access to life saving health services.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

CHADO will institutionalize feedback mechanism to allow beneficiaries, girls and boys, women and men including persons with disabilities to voice their successes, as well as complaints and concerns to stakeholders on quality and impact of the project as a means to measure accountability to affected population. The action will launch regular, structured dialogues to create platforms for target groups to engage with local government officials, service providers and CHADO partners. A broad spectrum of the community will participate in decision-making in a way that is inclusive, non-discriminatory and provides direct influence in the humanitarian response.This influence will be achieved through commitment of CHADO in communication approaches that include clear and direct feedback loops between programme design and management and the communities themselves. Protection of the most vulnerable population will further be enhanced because the disaggregated risks/needs of the diversity of the community will be recognized; this will enable a more targeted response. The drivers of the conflict will then be mitigated, thereby reducing the risk.The action will launch regular, structured dialogues to create platforms for target groups to engage with local government officials, service providers and CHADO partners. This will foster a sense of mutual trust and encourage teamwork from planning, implementation and project review. Context specific and culturally sensitive feedback plan or social accountability guidelines will comprise a number of sub-activities such as beneficiary scorecards about the services, complaint collection boxes; displaying activities and budget of this action in visible places, periodic joint review sessions of beneficiaries and the local authorities. Other feedback plans will include: Joint stakeholder field monitoring visits, freedom of information petitions, community monitoring of service delivery and participatory budgeting.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are tailored to contextually fit into the health needs of each age group. All gender and age groups will receive appropriate contextual messages of their age group and gender which are also culturally appropriate. The messages will be tailored to foster understanding of the project by all groups of beneficiaries.At such they will be able to make informed choices or decisions on how they will want CHADO to implement the project. Historical, political and social dynamics within and between gender and age-groups, impact on the crisis and protection risks; Therefore CHADO through a sustained approach will ensure that there is improved interaction/communication by different age groups and gender to reduce protection and tailored messages using proven community approaches such as FGD's and open community forums or meetings.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CHADO has ratified the UN PSEA code of conduct and each staff subscribes to the code of conduct to streamline staff behaviors towards the beneficiaries.Staff members will be held accountable by local, national, and international laws and are aware of the consequences for violating these laws.Sexual exploitation, sexual abuse, corruption, trafficking of adults or children, forced early marriage and other abuse by staff members constitute acts of gross misconduct and will result in disciplinary action, up to and including immediate termination of employment.In accordance with international law, sexual activity with a child (a person under age 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. Mistaken belief in the age of a person is not a defense as stipulated by CHADO policy and other national and international legal frameworks.Staff members are prohibited from having sexual relationships with beneficiaries. These relationships are often based on unequal power dynamics and may undermine the credibility and integrity of humanitarian work.Exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior, is prohibited for all CHADO staff and volunteers including contractors.

Through series of FGD's and community meetings with diverse groups of the communities.Consultations and participation of beneficiaries during needs assessments (including baseline surveys), stakeholder workshops, training, community-centered surveillance and county-level cluster meetings, case study profiling and documentation of lesson learnt will all utilize broad community-based structures.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

CHADO has a complaint and feedback mechanism policy in place which is a participatory approach jointly with community leaders and beneficiaries.CHADO uses community-based complaints mechanisms such as use of local leaders like local chiefs, county authorities, religious leaders, the beneficiaries makes up the complaint mechanism/ feedback mechanism as stipulated in CHADO transparency policy. CHADO feedback and complaints mechanisms defines the purpose and limitations of the mechanisms (e.g. steps involved in processing and responding to complaints, how to handle PSEA reports/complaints), addresses confidentiality and non-retaliation issues, is updated regularly and assigns clear roles and responsibilities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The risk associated by all gender and age groups are taken into highconsideration and if there are concerns, a wide consultation is made using CHADO advocacy tool. More so CHADO always carry out community risk assessment and safety audit to reduce impediment of beneficiaries from receiving assistance. In practical terms, this means identifying who is at risk, how and why at the very outset of a crisis and thereafter, taking into account the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to sexual and other minorities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Safety audit and barriers associated with needs of the beneficiaries has been simulated. Therefore no beneficiary or group will be discriminated. All persons will have unhindered access to services provided. CHADO always ensure that assistance is provided to beneficiaries irrespective of their social status, tribe, age, race, gender, sex, physical and mental state, inter alia. Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Through age appropriate indicators with the total population as the denominator. Access to assistance is one of the main pillars in the design andimplementation of the project. Quality measures will be use to quantify and track access to quality humanitarian assessment. Measurement of access will provide information on beneficiaries registered timely and having appropriate access to assistance. This may include: access to several components of the humanitarian assistance not limited to structural barriers impacting access of the community to health services. CHADO is committed to ensuring that all community members have unhindered access to health services.
