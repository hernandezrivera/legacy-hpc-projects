Project Summary

South Sudan’s conflict has created severe damage to the socio-economic and geopolitical status of the population with 8.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. This situation has been worsened further by endemic inter-communal violence, access constraints, operational impedances, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks (flooding and drought). All these combined shocks have severely impacted on livelihoods, education, WASH, health and general safety nets. According to April to July 2021 IPC reports, 7.24 million people faced acute food insecurity due to climatic events, such as prolonged dry spells, flooding, and persistent macroeconomic deterioration.
The counties of Upper Nile (Fashoda, Ulang, Longechuk and Nasir) have been severely affected by effects of the conflict and floods while Magwi county of Eastern Equatoria has seen influx of spontaneous returnees and inter communal violence. These communities have not been adequately supported.
UNKEA proposes to support the provision of key livelihood interventions to the communities (36,000 households) by implementing activities such as Training & capacity building; Supporting the establishment of kitchen gardens; Implementing local climate-sensitive agricultural and natural resource and disaster risk reduction strategies; Emergency food & livelihood support; Livestock treatment and vaccination; Livelihood support to returnee households; and ensuring all protection & GBV issues are addressed through mainstreaming of activities into the FSL interventions.
The project will target 36,000 (9800 men; 15700 women; 4800 boys; and 5700 girls in need. The target beneficiaries will be distributed as 36000 (Fashoda= 4800; Ulang= 6000; Longechuk= 7000; Nasir= 10200; Magwi= 8000). All the 36000 beneficiaries will receive all the agricultural inputs (crop/Vegetable seeds + tools) while 900 (Fashoda=100; Ulang=120; Longechuk=120; Nasir=200; Magwi=360) will be selected for Cash and voucher Assistance; 20800 (Fashoda=3000; Ulang=4200; Longechuk=4800; Nasir=6400; Magwi=2400) will be selected for fishing kits. 2900 (Fashoda,400; Ulang, 400; Longechuk, 600; Nasir, 800; and Magwi, 700) lead farmers & fish-folks will be trained on various farming & fishing technics
To be able to deliver these interventions across all the counties targeted, UNKEA proposes for a budget of 371,156 US dollars for the period.

1 - Needs

The successive years of civil war in South Sudan left both the society and the economy severely damaged with 8.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. According to April to July 2021 IPC reports, 7.24 million people faced acute food insecurity. The food insecurity in the country is primarily triggered by the conflict, climatic events, such as prolonged dry spells, flooding that led to displacements of the people, and persistent macroeconomic deterioration which led to high commodity prices and depreciation of South Sudanese Pounds (SSP) against Dollars (USD). This has reduced on the households purchasing power as many lost their properties. This situation has been further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic outbreak and its related preventive measures that have threatened human lives and affected every productive entity such as markets and agriculture production leading to minimal harvests and low productivity. In addition, COVID-19 has exacerbated the already deteriorating GBV situation in South Sudan given that resources became scarce and limited access to services such as in Food security and livelihood (FSL), WASH, Health, Environment and Nutrition.According to the recent IPC projection of April-July 2021, Upper Nile state (Fashoda, Ulang, Longechuk and Nasir) has one of the highest overall acute food insecurity situations after Jonglei State with 71%. The population that falls within phase 3 and phase 4 in Upper Nile State is on increase having (phase 3, 47%; and phase 4, 24%). In addition to Nasir as part of Upper Nile state, Fashoda County was massively submerged in waters due to over flooding according to the IRNA reports of December,2021. On the other hand, Eastern Equatoria State is also observed to be moving towards similar situations with population (phase 3, 33% and phase 4, 13%). Again, with over 800,000 people already displaced while others returning, common ailments have increased and further exacerbated the situation as people have been displaced to high grounds. Most of the communities of these counties being agro-pastoralists. In Magwi County, following implementation of revitalized peace, over 260,000 people have returned voluntarily. The returnees were hardly supported to resettle in their villages. Of recent, there has been further exacerbation of the situation due to conflict between nomadic pastoralists from Jonglei who have displaced several communities and destroyed their homes and livelihoods. The displaced are not supported and crowded in schools in Magwi town. There are protection related issues that needs to be addressed even during humanitarian support to the IDPs. Therefore, because of the losses and high influx of returnees in these target locations, many of the people become highly vulnerable and food insecure and have resorted to coping mechanisms such as eating once a day, walk for longer distance in order to access wild fruits/foods, work for others, this further exacerbate crimes within the counties, as they compete for resources, etc.In picture of these situations in these counties, there is need to support the livelihoods of these communities in order for them to be able to adhere to the prevailing effects of the various shocks that they encounter and also support them in ensuring they have the ability of resilience and recovery. UNKEA therefore, proposes to support these communities through the provision of key life-saving food security and livelihoods interventions (agricultural inputs such as crop seeds, vegetable seeds, agric.tools and fishing gears, etc) that targets the most vulnerable households in order for them to be more resilient to the existing situations facing them.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The beneficiary’s identification, selection and registration procedures entail that highly vulnerable beneficiaries such as elderly (65+), disabled persons, chronically-ill, child headed households, and women headed households are highly vulnerable and thus, highly prioritized for such kind of projects modality. This is because they are deemed to lack start-up resources /capital to initiate their own businesses as support to their livelihoods. Moreover, they also, do not have any purchase power (cash) sources to obtain food stuff from the nearby markets. Moreso, they are not capable of supporting the household’s members with other family basic needs such as paying school fees, health and care services, etc. The project therefore, proposes to let such category of beneficiaries be eligible for this kind of project. This is for them as start up and improve their livelihoods and source of household food sustainability.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project proposes for continuity of markets in the rural setting as local resources obtain from both on and off farms require exchanges in terms of valuation yet there are those highly vulnerable households’ beneficiaries who do not afford to obtain even their daily bread due to lack of resources. This category of highly vulnerable beneficiaries will be supported with one-off cash/grants in order to depend their various sources of livelihoods such as initiation/supplement of business, buying household assets like livestock (goats, poultry, etc), buying more foods in their households, for health care, etc. The cash beneficiaries will be selected from the most highly vulnerable group of beneficiaries includes the elderly persons (65+), Child headed households, chronically-ill persons, persons with disability, malnourished children identified from households, etc. Traders have capacity to supply the markets all year round who need to be assisted with grants especially village saving and loans Associations (VSLA) groups in order for markets to stay operational. Also, through cash-for-work, market infrastructures will be constructed such as market stalls as best practices, with establishment of proper child and women safe spaces for protection and gender mainstreaming avenues. Also, with the support and potentiality of clearing roads leading to markets for the rural farmers and fisher-folks, supply of products will be felt for a sustained period leading to improved household’s food security and livelihoods. It can further be reiterated with concerns that as more commodities continue to flood markets and there is value for money, this boost markets to continue to function at regular basis in case of non-seasonal markets, thus, most households become viably food secure.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

As a remedy to efficiently and effectively receive feedbacks and complaints from project stakeholders/ beneficiaries, this FSL project will adopt to the usual two-way communication systems. In this case, Feedback and complaints Committees will be established at Payam levels or distribution centers distinguished into either cash distribution committees and/or Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) respectively. These committees will be trained together with the project staff on their roles as feedback and complaints mechanisms. There will be a set-up of feedbacks and complaints desks at all distribution points to receive complaints and concerns raised during the project implementation. Through these Feedback Committees, information (positive or negative) is directly provided to either the FSL field officer or the FSL Manager. The national-level FSL manager will be focal point person who will interact directly with the beneficiaries.However, the project in collaboration with the community stakeholders will also identify and select trusted and reliable community contact persons in the community to act as liaison officer for the project who presents beneficiaries complaints on behalf of the communities. The community contact liaison persons will follow gender sensitivity including both men and women who will have links with the FSL project staff focal point/contact person both in the ﬁeld and also at the level of headquarter in Juba. The community will choose the feedback and complaints mechanisms they will prefer for their views to be heard and reported. This will also work in line with community cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use, and will include paper-based systems (records), and interpersonal communication through the selected trusted liaison persons. The project M&E system will be responsible to design tools such as relevant feedbacks and complaints utility forms to capture beneficiaries’ feedbacks and complaints through the scheduled beneficiaries’ interviews and PDM analysis. These regular feedback/complaint mechanisms will be incorporated and reported for solutions through out the project implementation time frame. There will be monthly or quarterly dialogue meetings targeting various community stakeholders as an oversight to the overall project implementation process. All complaints shall be handled in a systematic manner ensuring that the complainants are protected and feel safe at all times with confidentiality of sensitive data given by the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This project will adopt independent interview and information transfer system for each category of vulnerable beneficiaries such as those living with disabilities. By considering their various needs, human rights, empowerment, protection and gender equality, beneficiaries such as women, girls, boys, and men who are in the category of people living with disabilities, illiterate people, and chronically-ill persons will be subjected to interviews differently in order to ascertain their insights and personal feelings especially in regards to both sensitive and non-sensitive feedbacks from them and other stakeholders, and will ensure that those selected channels are relevant and inclusive to even the most vulnerable populations. Arrangements to directly meet the persons with disability and chronically-ill persons will be done by paying visits to their various households for their feedbacks and complaints which shall be recorded. The contact persons will include both men and women and these will be linked with the project contact person both in the ﬁeld and also at the organization headquarters. The Feedback and Complaints Mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use, and will include paper-based systems, interpersonal communication through the selected trusted liaison persons including clear translation in case of illiterate beneficiaries. Regular feedback/complaint mechanisms will be incorporated throughout the project life span and beneficiaries are sure to receive rightful feedbacks from the implementing partner (either positive or negative); either monthly or quarterly dialogue meetings will be conducted targeting various categories of vulnerable community members. All complaints shall be handled in a systematic manner ensuring that the complainants are protected and feel safe at all times.UNKEA possess a proper laid down feedback and complaints mechanisms and handling system that will be implemented in all project sites in consultation with project participants who will be encouraged to use it. For feedbacks and complaints on the distributed inputs, trainings, etc. for instance, the project M&E system will design tools especially question guides for Focused Group Discussion (FGDs) to capture beneficiaries’ feedbacks in well-designed feedbacks and complaints forms/counter book or data collection tablets. These feedbacks will be implemented at three levels: First level is by engaging cash distribution committees and/or Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) to coordinate with each identified category of most vulnerable beneficiaries and to report to the responsible project officer in the target location. Secondly, Area Managers who will be independent of any on-going project activities will be tasked to obtain any feedbacks and complaints from such vulnerable beneficiaries and can report directly to the Executive Director for immediate intervention. Otherwise, critical information from the Area Manager is filtered through to the senior program managers (National-Level Manager) without sharing with other project staff (FSL officers and extension workers) for analysis in depth before extension to top management for decision making. Thirdly, feedbacks and complaints desk is set up at all registration and distribution points to receive complaints and concerns raised through the focal point persons who might have been identified by the community as influential. The project staff in collaboration with the beneﬁciaries will jointly identify trusted contact persons in the community to act as liaison person who presents beneficiaries complaints on behalf of the communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project plans to reduce cases of SGBV, including forced marriage, early marriages, rape, defilement, assaults and cases of snake bites that are seen to be rampant due to cultural practices through formation of Community Protection Committees (CPCs) in every target location. The CPCs members including community stakeholders and project beneficiaries will be engaged in regular meetings, trainings and community engagements in order to acquire information on protection concerns that is vital to reduce violence within households as it aims at addressing the already identified community and the partners protection concerns. These committees will help address some of the protection concerns in making good use of the available resources and mobilization including linking the victims of the SGBV to distinct protection referral pathways. They (CPCs) will also act to report as soon any case of violence as possible to the implementing partner (UNKEA) for decision making. The information on the PSEA will be provided to beneficiaries in schools, churches, community gatherings/events, radio talk show in places with radio stations, etc. This protection plan will further help Payam protection committees, project protection officers and local authorities in dividing tasks, roles and responsibilities in addressing the aforementioned protection concerns in the target community. Furthermore, the plan will help to integrate more protection concerns and to upscale protection in future projects. The plan will also address children’s protection action, SGBV and other related protection issues, safety and peaceful working environments through creation of child and women protection/safe spaces in markets and any community gathering for children, women and field staffs. The project plan will make the communities and local authorities understand the availability of the local resources and the role each actor plays in the protection intervention for peaceful co-existence and social cohesion of project stakeholders within communities/households.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project assumes and acknowledge risks anticipated to affect the execution of activities and will provide mitigation actions to support its implementation and safety of project assets and staff. The selected counties of Upper Nile State are only access by water transport, with most roads being poor murram roads only passable during dry season that pose delays in inputs delivery. This will be resolved by effective prepositioning of much cash to inputs transportation by water (hire of boat). Outbreak of violence in intervention areas, inter-clan conflicts due to competition for limited but important resource base. However, security measures will include peaceful dialogue between conflicting communities, observing security procedures, safety & Security Evacuation Plans (SSEPS), National engagement with NGO Forum/Security, local coordination and representation via inter-agency field meetings. Delayed funding will affect project timeline and cycle. UNKEA will maintain open communication with donor and key partners to avail funds for timely activities execution.Economic risks cause inflation, devaluation of SSP against dollars causes widespread food shortages in target areas. There will also be low turnout of beneficiaries for project activities. In this regard, UNKEA will continue to Revise Minimum Expenditure Basket and check on budgetary implications. To safeguard funds, UNKEA will engage a full-time field cashier to safeguard and act as a custodian of field finances/funds. UNKEA will further engage a financial service provider to manage financial flow to field target locations. Regular training and spot checks will be provided as part of internal control measure/system. To avoid discrimination against beneficiaries, there will be strict follow of beneficiary selection criteria and Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism put in place (the AAP Committees).The project also presumes there will be a risk of low absorption capacity and reporting of outcomes to the donors and/or any other collaborating partners. However, UNKEA intends to do Joint Monitoring and Evaluation of project activities by the Donors and service provider (UNKEA).The risk of increase in the spread of diseases such as Corona Virus in the project area causing total lockdown and affect the project implementation will be addressed through adherence to National Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and UN, WHO, and Donor guidelines.Attempts to corruptions and misuse of resources reduced success of the project implementation. Therefore, compliance with internal control systems and regular monitoring and evaluation of project activities will be key to the success of this project.Through the input distribution committees and Accountability to Affected Populations, feedback and complaints handling and management desk whose role extend beyond the day of input distribution to include issues that arise from beneficiary households as a result of items received. The committees are also empowered to address concerns to avoid incidences of domestic violence in benefiting households. They will make sure the crowds are controlled by lining up the beneficiaries while giving a priority to elderly people, persons with disabilities, chronically-ill-persons and child headed family as first to be served. Furthermore, there is a deliberate effort to sensitize the public and the beneficiaries on protection related concerns. This is done during inception meetings, beneficiary mobilization and sensitization, beneficiary registration, and on the distribution day. In addition to above, other specific measures are in place to reduce incidences of violence, coercion or exploitation. The project shall encourage very weak vulnerable persons to be accompanied by physically stronger members of their family/communities. This boost confidence and encourage persons exposed or likely exposed to threats to communicate to the committees and to be supported on time.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Quality, timely and inclusion of protection and psychosocial services is one core areas of UNKEA concerns. It is anticipated that mostly children, women and elderly are the ones at high risks and GBV survivors. Although there still exist prevalence of revenge and targeting killings, cattle raiding by many communities in these project targeted locations, the buy-in of protection measures by armed groups even in volatile conditions are conducive for delivery of humanitarian services to the highly vulnerable beneficiaries for their well-being. It is also anticipated in this proposed project that due to the signed revitalized agreement between the main warring parties in South Sudan, a large number of returnees are coming back to settle in their ancestral/original lands especially through routes of Ethiopia and Uganda where many South Sudanese were displaced to settle in camps where they were displaced from. This is because opportunities were provided for free movement of people to return to their homes. The peaceful engagements between the government and the opposition have paved way to allow unhindered access to project target locations and movement of humanitarian workers and items. Therefore, upon arrival, such arriving groups of returnees need support and protection for social cohesion in the community. In this case, UNKEA has integrated gender and protection within all its project activities to ensure that there is prevention and PSEA is enhanced, and no harm principles are followed strictly and individuals and groups targeted are served with the project activities without interference and difficulty, considering gender sensitivity. Also, the work of the local institutions including police and community leaders is gaining traction. Referral pathways for survivors of SGBV will be clear benched-mark by the project for psychosocial support of the victims of GBV. Through engaging field case workers and protection officers in its protection department, UNKEA will ensure there is timely access to quality case management and emphasize a lobby for proper policies that is inclusive in protecting human roles and dignity. Furthermore, accountability to affected population (AAP) as feedback mechanism when put in place at all times will always ensure that project beneficiaries are served with dignity and through training are usually prevented from practising GBV and sexual exploitation. In this regard, UNKEA has already supported the development of protection plans in its previous projects as means of creating an enabling environment for interventions and intends to upscale in the forthcoming FSL projects. The project will also implement UNKEAs integrated protection approach by mainstreaming protection concerns in all its activities including establishment of child friendly spaces and women protection spaces in market places, churches, schools, etc. Similar initiatives will be rolled out in other project target areas that are proposed for this intervention.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

In Upper Nile State, displacements of its local population have become an obvious phenomenon all year rounds. This is because communities residing there are prone to not only flooding, but also insecurity caused by neighbouring communities due to cattle raiding and revenge/targeting killings especially in areas of Ulang, Longechuk and Nasir counties that has become a predicament in those areas, to mention a few. Flood, with its terrifying impacts on the community’s well-being and displacements need immediate intervention of humanitarian partners. Indeed, through enhancing and encouraging beneficiaries and local community to participate in their communities’ cash-for-work programmes such as digging of secondary dykes, the risk of displacement of inhabitants is reduced. However, UNKEA will first need to do baseline assessments through the local authorities and community leaders on the extent of the floods by engaging its field staff to participate in the assessments, and can also work towards proposed community action plans to implement. UNKEA will nevertheless, embark on training of community leaders and project beneficiaries on Early Warning Systems (EWS) and Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (DRRM) strategies. This will further minimise unnecessary risks and effects of floods on people’s lives, livestock, farm yields and properties in the community. UNKEA will further work collaboratively and in coordination with all the project stakeholders including jointly addressing the communities needs with other partners dealing in related projects. On the other hand, quarterly community/stakeholders dialogue meetings will be initiated to ascertain what, where, when, and how each project partner implement their projects so as to avoid duplication and repeating of projects target areas and beneficiaries previously targeted. According to IPC reports of 2021, floods are on increase, a trend that is anticipated to increase further if interventions is not done by humanitarian partners. Therefore, by engaging many different project partners to operate in different areas affected by flooding and working concurrently, within two-to-three years, displacement trends will lower atleast by 30%, a percentage projection that is vital for community well-being in the current face of changing climate.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

UNKEA will be engaged in providing agricultural inputs to farmers and fisher-folks groups in order to ensure that there is sustainable food security within their households. Demonstration plots will be established as demonstration sites and for community practices, Training of household members will be conducted and others trained as lead farmers to help train other young farmers of group of farmers/women. The old/elderly, persons with disabilities and those who are chronically-ill persons will be subjected to unconditional cash transfer and this can increase their purchasing power and obtain more goods/foods for their households. This can reduce poverty and improve their livelihoods status within the community.
