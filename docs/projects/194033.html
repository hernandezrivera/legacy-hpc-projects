Project Summary

The objectives of the refugee response plan by UNFPA are to address Gender Based Violence prevention and response, addressing the critical life-saving and essential reproductive health needs among the refugees and asylum seekers. This plan will cover integrated response for SRH-GBV prevention and service deliveries, capacity building of local authorities, institutions & service providers, Community Awareness and Community Engagements, Advocacy for SRH-GBV services, and scaling up referral mechanism including the service coordination for at risk women and girls and GBV survivors. The project consists of reproductive health and GBV service delivery and awareness activities. The implementation of activities will be conducted through NGOs and Government partners with the strong technical support from UNFPA, including maintaining the field presence. The project will target individual skills building, strengthening community structures and institutional capacity building of Sudan to respond to the needs of refugees.

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts one of the largest refugee populations in Africa, with 1,144,675 refugees and asylum seekers as of 31 March 2023 segregated 61% of the population is out of camp, while 39% is settled in camps.. Among these vast numbers of refugees and asylum seekers, 73% are women and children and 70% live outside camps. 43% of the population are children under 18 years, 28% are women of reproductive age. Prior to 15th of April, coverage was low due to limited supplies, infrastructure and capacities, coverage with EMONC was around 40%, and CMR services were less than 20%. The situation had catastrophically impacted by the armed clashes, resulting in causalities including amongst pregnant women attending to care, and significant displacement from Khartoum, West Darfur and other affected states. protection risks have emerged as well including increased risks of sexual based violence. Limited safe pathways and access by both people in need and health care providers to seek care, the direct attacks on hospitals and Health care delivery points, Power interruption and water shortages including amongst hospitals, depletion of supplies and unsafe access to stocks, and direct attacks on health care providers; all these factors had serious impact on capacity of health care to deliver and operate. More than 164,000 refugees and refugee returnees have fled Sudan as of 10 May. The deterioration of the security situation in Sudan has exacerbated risks of GBV for women, girls and the vulnerable population including the refugees whose movement and access to livelihood activities, and health and social services has been restricted as a direct result. Lack of access to quality specialized life-saving GBV services remains a challenge. Clinical management of rape (CMR), psychosocial support, legal aid,GBV case management and referral mechanisms is a challenge on the current situation. GBV remains under-reported due to insecurity and fear of stigma as well absence of service. Huge numbers of households in the refugee communities are female-headed(52%) with 1% child-headed households. Sudan remained at high risk of disease outbreaks that have higher impact on pregnant women including Malaria, dengue Fever and Hepatitis E Virus cases. Moreover, the country remains prone to various risks and hazards such as floods, localized armed clashes and inter communal violence which continues to impact the already fragile health system and is reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to the multiple emergencies affecting the country. This project will target 37 localities prioritized for 2023 by the refugee consultation forum (RCF).As the project has focused on integrated SRH-GBV programmes for refugees, UNFPA has estimated the needs for SRH-GBV services for the target groups by using MISP calculator (IAWG-RH). The project will be able to serve 25,757 pregnant women who are in need of critical and lifesaving SRHR services. This intervention, if implemented, should address pregnancy related complications including cesarean section and normal deliveries, prevention and treatment of STIs for both women and men, preventing neonatal complications. Besides, the project will serve approximately 486000primary health consultations for both women, girls, boys and men, including the persons with disabilities, primary focus of consultation will be around SRH. This project targets approximately 3,555 sexual violence cases with CMR services. In addition to that the project will address 50% of GBV survivors who received financial support while seeking other mental health and psychosocial services. Vulnerable hos communities living near to the targeted population will be considered in the response therefore UNFPA will target 20%of the host communities to benefit t from this project of refugees and Asylum seekers to mitigate conflicts between host and refugees.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1. Increased access to and utilization of quality GBV services and improved conducive community structure to prevent GBV in the targeted localities for refugee women and girls, especially GBV survivors. Activity 1.1. Establishment and operation of women centers/Women and girls Safe Space (WGSS) to enable women with increased access to information and services . Activity 1.2: Vocational skills training for women and supporting with start-up capital for the most vulnerable women, and Cash voucher. support. Activity 1.3: Supporting women and girls at reproductive age with dignity kits, Psychosocial support and GBV Case Management Services, including Referral services cost, GBV trafficking prevention and response services. Activity 1.4. Training and capacity building of GBV service providers and social workers, including basic and the refresher training on MHPSS, case management, GBVIE and referral pathway. Activity 1.5. Capacity Building of targeted community based protection network and women's groups, and direct Support to facilitate their work. Activity 1.6. Community sensitization session, public Awareness raising campaign- national and state level, develop IEC materials Activity 1.7. Support GBV mainstreaming and coordination capacity building activities Output 2.Improved access to, and utilization of lifesaving and essential sexual and reproductive health services. Activity 2.1. Timely deployment of temporary and/or mobile clinic services, referral services and health care providers to areas with limited access to life saving and essential SRH services. Activity 2.2 Strengthening the capacity of healthcare providers on provision of essential and lifesaving SRH services, such as EmONC, CMR, Post abortion care, family planning, ANC/PNC, etc. Activity 2.3. Improve the availability of essential and lifesaving SRH supplies including interagency reproductive health kits, personal protective equipment and infection prevention supplies, family planning, hospital equipment and supplies. Activity 2.4 Strengthen existing health facilities and improve their capacities through rehabilitation and refurbishments, hospital equipment and referral services for refugees. Activity 2.5 Engagement of target beneficiaries in demand creation for SRHR services as well as other emerging health issues such as COVID19, and ensuring their active role in establishing community based referral mechanisms for obstetric complication.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

1. Safety audit in a regularly basis with the beneficiaries. 2. Conduct joint assessment in the protection risks. 3. Enable women's social groups to speak on various protection challenges and risks in the refugee sites. The project will also keep community advocacy and consultations that will engage community opinion leaders to enhance deeper understanding of the risks at home and at the community level. The project will engage the community leaders to mitigate the protection needs. 4. Orientation on PSEA to the staff and enabling beneficiary knowing where to report. At the institutional level, the project will build the capacity of the service providers on GBV guiding principles, Do No Harm Approach. 5. GBV and PSEA focal points will be in place for each service delivery point 6. Risk reduction strategy will be integrated into each activities of the programs 7. To mitigate host and refugee conflicts, UNFPA will address the programme following the principle of social cohesion, such as allowing the most vulnerable host communities to access the services. 8. The programme will target based on the gender analysis that reflects the different needs of women, girls, boys and men. At the same time, the project will also address the most vulnerable women and children, such as female- headed households, survivor of child marriage, pregnant women and GBV survivors. To mitigate the protection risks, the project will consult with women and men, young people separately to understand various protection risks while accessing services.
