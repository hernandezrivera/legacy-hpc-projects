Project Summary

CARE/partners (Health Work Committees- HWC and Palestinian Medical Relief Society-PMRS will respond to this situation with a comprehensive intervention that combines elements of direct health service delivery to the identified vulnerable communities, with special focus on women and children, elderly, youth and people with disabilities, reporting and referral of GBV cases, injuries due to settlers and IF violence, and responding to the COVID 19 crisis through community awareness, Hygiene kits, and other preventive measures. CARE will continue working with trained community representatives (focal points) and project staff to report/refer these violations using agreed upon mechanism.
The proposed intervention will be targeting 56 vulnerable communities in Area C, Jordan Valley and H2 areas in the West Bank that were identified by CARE/Partners/ MoH/ and the HNC during the recent HRP 2021. All identified locations have a serious access issues and lack any type of health services. The identified locations will be targeted through 4 mobile health clinics that will be visiting these communities regularly to provide the health services per agreed upon program/plan with the HNC and MoH. The 56 locations are distributed as follow: 15 locations in Bethlehem Area, 19 locations in Hebron Area 6 of them inside H2 areas and the others in area C, 12 locations in north Jordan valley, one location in Ramallah Al Mu'arrajat Centre/ Bedouin communities and 9 Bedouin communities in Jerusalem and middle Jordan valley area.
CARE/partners (PMRS and HWC), in close coordination with MoH centrally and at the governorates’ level, will provide life-saving services in targeted communities in the WB through the use of mobile clinics/teams that offer basic curative and preventive Primary Health Care services (PHC), including women’s healthcare (ante and post-natal care, obstetric care, etc), general practice care (GP), health awareness with focus on communities needs and priorities like COVID 19 control, prevention and referrals, basic lab tests, home visits, children health and psychosocial support, training will conducted to the teams on PSHEA, prevention of GBV, and other related issues. The team will also be provided with emergency kits that enable them to respond to emergency incidents in these communities while working in the clinics to ensure lifesaving before transferring to the hospitals. Additionally, the teams will train selected community focal points (mainly females) on first aid and provide them with 1st Aid kits to better respond to the emergencies in their communities.
The project is designed in fully coordination with other actors mainly Ministry of Health where CARE/partners and MOH teams identified the locations to ensure best utilization of the available resources and avoid overlapping or duplication.

1 - Needs

The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of target communities, based on a number of key sources, including the data and mapping exercise led by the Health and Nutrition cluster needs analysis for most vulnerable locations that are in need of health services, CARE and the two partners were among the group who worked with the HNC to develop and update the matrix of the locations all over West Bank. CARE has conducted internal needs analysis that included several meetings and discussions with targeted community members as well as MoH offices in the respected governorates. The assessments took into considerations all new needs/implications of COVID 19 crisis on the services provided in the identified locations/communities and/or the capacity of the health providers (mainly MoH) to provide PHC in the vulnerable communities. To date, the number of confirmed cases of COVID19 only in the WB according to the Ministry of Health is 36,295 cases and numbers are rising dramatically which means more burden on the health system to continue their health services. CARE has also looked into different relevant documents/studies such as oPt response plan for C-19, C-19 Global HRP, COVID-19 related social stigma, IASC MHPSS Guide for C-19 responders, 9, MHPSS IASC guidelines for C-19, CARE/OCHA joint rapid gender analysis (September 2020) to inform the design. According to all assessments, access to primary health care in the WB is problematic largely due to various restrictions on movement and access imposed by the Israel authorities, including the separation barrier, settlers violence, checkpoints, permits regime and the isolation of East Jerusalem in addition to the weakened (overburdened) health system due to the COVID 19 crisis as well as lack of adequate financial resources, shortages in basic supplies and the structural limitations as a result of the political situation.According to the drafted HNO 2021 and discussions (to be completed by HNC in October!) an estimated 260,000 people, are located in Area C, Hebron H2 and the “Seam Zone”. These communities face severe restrictions in accessing primary healthcare, particularly women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities. without the humanitarian intervention of the mobile health clinics, there is no alternative accessible PHC service. In september,2020, CARE has reviewed its mapping of health services through Focus Group Discussions and meeting with stakeholders including MOH, health care providers, local community representatives taking into consideration the gender needs and age groups in the identified communities in related districts of the WB. The mapping revealed that approximately 44 communities are lacking any type of health. This was confirmed in the HRP 2021 process.Within these communities, poor health outcomes are a result of compound effect of lack of quality care, shortages in medical supplies and lack of health education and awareness. As a result, we see the spread of infectious diseases and complications in chronic diseases such as diabetes as well as complications related to pregnancies and post deliveries, from previous work in these communities, data shows that approximately 50% of the women health were related to Ante and post-natal care, however the remaining 50% were distributed as (25% acute illnesses, 15% gynecological diseases, 10% puberty and post-menopausal related problems)Difficult access to PHC combined with traditional and conservative culture in many of the communities affects women. Inadequate treatment of RH problems is common, often leading to complications. The incidents of GBV, under reported even under the best of circumstances, are here even more. CARE considered the physical access to health services as the only selection criteria of the targeted communities. NO other Services are provided in the proposed locations.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PWD considered as one of the main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project as they were identified by HNC as one of the vulnerable groups whom needs special consideration, for that the project team will map out, with the communities’ representatives and actors all cases of PWD in the targeted communities and will provide the necessary medical services to them. Referral will be also an option for those who need to be referred to advances or specialized services, this will be coordinated with other health providers as needed. Additionally, the social worker, who will be part of the medical team, will work with them on the psychological aspect. PWD will have special attention when they come to the clinics and it is planned to conduct home visits for those who can’t access the clinic. Elderly and bed ridden patients will have special needs as target groups. For the elderly the project will provide the medical treatment and the social worker will work with them on special needs, follow up to the elderly patient could prevent serious medical problems. Home visits will also be planned for those who can’t access the clinic.
