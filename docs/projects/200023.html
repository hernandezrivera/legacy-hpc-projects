Project Summary

The majority of humanitarian needs for WASH in Burundi are caused by recurrent disasters related to torrential rains, strong winds, floods and landslides. These affect thousands of households and cause massive displacement of persons every year, resulting in significant human and material damage, particularly in rural areas.
According to the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM, October 2022), 89 % of the 75,300 displaced persons in Burundi are displaced due to climate related hazards. Additionally, the situation of displacement is prolonged for more than 40% of the 75,300 IDPs who have been displaced for more than one year. On top of the needs of internally displaced persons, in 2023, UNHCR estimates that 75,000 Burundian refugees with high humanitarian needs will return to Burundi, including needs in assistance with WASH as they often come back with no belongings to find their land occupied. A Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by IOM’s DTM between August and September 2022 showed that 45% of person of the move interviewed had no or unimproved sanitation facilities and reported water insufficiency (less than 15 litres per person per day).
In 2023, according to the WASH sector, 146,652 individuals have WASH needs.
IOM's proposed response is in line with the sector 2023 strategy and will aim to enhance equal and safe access for women, girls, men and boys to safe drinking water, hygiene and sanitation in line with sector objectives 1, 2 and 3.
To provide basic access to WASH facilities and promote hygiene practices, IOM will construct 50 water points and 1,000 VIP and Akasuga latrines in areas of high return and natural-hazard affected communes in Burundi. In addition, IOM plans to distribute 10,000 hygiene kits, including dignity items for women. During project implementation, IOM will empower local women by working with community-based organizations (CBOs) on the production of menstrual hygiene management items to have a positive impact on the economy and livelihoods of the beneficiary women beyond project duration. All of IOM interventions will be completed by community-based hygiene sensitization sessions on proper latrine use, on latrine cleaning, waste disposal, handwashing, Cholera prevention, malaria, and other infectious diseases prevention.

All these activities will be carried out in close collaboration with the sector actors. Complaints mechanisms will be put in place to accompany all distribution activities and post-distribution monitoring will be conducted to collect feedback.
