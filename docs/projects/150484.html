Project Summary

The project will target ex-detainee children and young adults (until 25 years old) – a particularly vulnerable and neglected group – their families, as well as children at risk of detention and the wider community.
The project will take place in three locations of East Jerusalem: Shu’fat Refugee Camp, Jabal Al Mukaber and Issawiyeh. The objective is to provide mental health and psychosocial support and protection to ex-detainee boys and girls and their families including mothers and fathers, in order to improve their wellbeing and strengthen their self-help and coping mechanism; facilitate ex detainee children reintegration to school and raise awareness on detention and its consequences.
The direct beneficiaries for this project will be 376 girls, boys, women and men, including 45 CBOs' staff members and volunteers. The indirect beneficiaries estimated: Total population of the targeted communities (around 43,764 people)
The approach is a holistic one, taking into accounts the many facets of community and family cohesion and of a child’s reinsertion. Consequently, the project’s strategy, which interconnects medical, social and legal support with education and livelihoods, involves work with a wide range of actors: educators, children, parents and siblings, medical professionals, legal experts and community leaders. MdM proposes to set up quality services following IASC standards for MHPSS: psychosocial counselors will focus on conducting individual, group and family counseling sessions, and organize various therapeutic and educational activities.
The project entails building a relationship based on trust with the target population including women, men, boys and girls, through the team’s consistent presence in the targeted areas and the provision of quality services.
The coordination with other international organizations also took place for this project, especially with the organizations which may work in some of the locations we are targeting. Both TdH-CH and MdM-CH recognize our common presence in Shu’fat Refugee Camp, Jabal Al Mukaber and Issawiyeh as an opportunity for complementary programmes. In order to prevent overlapping (especially as regards MHPSS) and avoid the risk of missing people in need, both organizations have discussed the necessity of putting in place appropriate coordination mechanisms at all levels. As a medical organization with technical expertise in the field of primary health care, sexual and reproductive health and mental health, MdM-CH will mainly focus on the therapeutical component of its services and activities. Thus, collaboration between the two teams on the ground will guarantee beneficiaries receive a comprehensive support package. Coordination between our 2 organizations will be ensured through regular bilateral meetings and participation in the CP/MHPSS working group. Moreover, as both organizations are based in Switzerland, we exchange and coordinate at HQ-level about operations and advocacy efforts.
Regarding TdH-It and the remedial and educational activities that both organizations propose to provide in Issawiyeh, coordination between our two organizations is indeed paramount and has already been discussed. A scheme whereby MdM-CH will provide (1) educational and remedial activities to children under home arrest, while TdH-It will focus on group educational and remedial activities for released children; (2) vocational training to interested released children, while TdH-It will focus exclusively on educational support, according to each organization’s capacity, is under review.
The risk of overlap with Save the Children is in our view very limited, since Save the Children will focus exclusively on PFA without a specific focus on detention and detention of minors. Moreover, since both MdM-CH and Save the Children have developed strategic partnerships with YMCA, smooth coordination is already ensured and will be further strengthened.
Overall, collaboration with all the aforementioned partners is key and a de facto priority for MdM-CH to guarantee the highest quality of support to people in need and our intervention's long-lasting impact.

Needs

At the end of 2013, MDM-CH carry out a study on ex-detainee children’s needs in Hebron Governorate, which led to the implementation of a 3-year long project with affected children, their families and the community. The experience acquired during these years and the constant monitoring of the situation of child detention has highlighted additional needs in other priority geographical areas. Thus, MdM-CH has conducted an initial needs assessment in the beginning of 2018 in East Jerusalem in collaboration with YMCA following the diagnosis, guidelines and standards of MdM handbook (main tools: literature review, stakeholder analysis, focus group and community discussions/analysis).YMCA was identified as a key partner since the early stages of this assessment offering specialized rehabilitation MHPSS services tailored to survivors of political violence and focusing especially on ex-detainee boys and girls and their families.It is observed and documented that child detention has, regardless of its duration, long-term mental health and psychosocial consequences. Those consequences are often reported during the focus group sessions and experts’ observation: sleeping disorders, enuresis, loss of interest, loss of concentration, anxiety, depression, etc.... Detention also deeply affects the families. Through focus group discussions conducted with the family members, it was observed that the impact of child detention for the parents included several factors: family cohesion, family economic situation, gender of detained child, family type (extended/nuclear), home confinementDuring the needs assessment analysis and evaluation and taking under consideration the mapping appraisal published by the CPWG and UNICEF, the following observations were highlighted as main gaps regarding the protection of vulnerable ex detainee children:• Regarding MHPSS provision of services to children including boys and girls there is a need for specialized, quality, and advance counselling, psychiatric care and quality service delivery on the provision of mental health and psychosocial support according to international measurements standards (such as IASC) is the main current gap on MHPSS • Insufficient follow-up of the detected case in need for MHPSS to the public health system due to difficulty in accessing the available specialized mental health services in Israel facilities and due to severe social stigma or/and lack of communication with the specialists (language barrier in the Israeli primary health care centers)• Tailored response to the specific educational needs and MHPSS needs of ex-detainee children and their families are in need, (set up in place mechanisms that will enable school reintegration)• A need was detected to strengthen the existing cooperation between CBOs and individuals that are providing services for ex-detainee children and their families• Very limited educational services are currently addressing the inclusion of children that have been arrested, to the rehabilitative support (only 8%)• Lack of coordination among mental health actors working in the rehabilitation of ex-detainee children and the educational sector as reintegration support especially through awareness• Lack of communication and strategy between stakeholders for comprehensive child protection case management. Need for a systematize approach is of high priority• There is a need for capacity building and trainings of local CBO’s and NGO’s staff and volunteers on MHPSS in order to enhance the quality of all type of interventions. A child protection approach reinforced by basic mental health is the provision of services should be encouraged and implemented• Help for helpers (health professional volunteers and non-health professional and volunteers) who are daily exposed to high levels of stress and trauma are not available and should be considered a priority for MHPSS interventions, so to ensure staff wellbeing, quality-oriented impact and boost engagement

Activities

- Individual, family and group counseling sessions for the boys, girls, women and men to be provided by the psychosocial counselors and psychologists in the targeted population to the ex-detainee children and families.- Group and community psychosocial activities facilitated by the psychosocial counselors in the targeted population of ex-detainees and their families through the peer to peer groups and mothers’ groups.- Sever cases referred to specialized external services.- Provide remedial education to ex-detainee children.- Provide vocational assessment to ex-detainee children.- Referral of ex-detainee children based on vocational assessment to receive vocational training.- Providing tools for beneficiaries.- Conduct ventilation weekends for children and their families.- Conduct summer camps for the ex-detainee children and their families (1 summer camp per location). ((The main objective of the summer camps is to increase the resilience, coping strategies and mental health and psychosocial well-being of the children and women targeted. Sub objectives include:* strengthening the communication skills between the children themselves and between the children and the counselors, * promoting human rights and specifically children’s rights,* enhancing children's capacity to think skills positively. Topics will include: communication skills and acceptance of others, positive thinking and self-awareness, self-advocacy, knowledge and importance of human rights, children's rights and women’s rights, gender.Activities will include: awareness sessions, group discussions, music therapy, drawing, sports and drama which will help the ex-detainee children and their families better cope with daily challenges. Observation during these activities will allow the counsellors to identify children in need of individual specialized support)).- At least 9 CSOs are supported to meet on a regular basis to discuss community mental health and psychosocial matters and to respond to crises and emergencies in their areas related to child arrests and detention, including through building linkages to other service providers and promoting community-driven initiatives.- 45 CSOs members and volunteers participate in skills training related to psychosocial first aid, team building, leadership, prevention and response to psychosocial and mental health matters.- Develop an awareness package for children about all aspect of detention and information about existing services for detained children.
