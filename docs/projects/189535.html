Project Summary

The project aims to provide integrated, safe, equitable, dignified and disaster-resistant access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services to IDPs, host communities, and returnees living in extreme and catastrophic WASH vulnerability.
To that end, IOM’s interventions will focus on locations identified by the WASH Cluster as key priorities: priority 1 counties (Tonj East, Malakal, Rubkona, Leer and Guit), priority 2 counties (Tonj North and Koch), priority 3 counties (Aweil East) and priority 4 counties (Twic and Tonj South). Through its Rapid Response Team, IOM will also intervene in any other Priority 4 and/or 5 locations, or any other location identified through WASH Cluster allocation. Although not included in the WASH Cluster Targeting Guidance (2022), IOM's HRP proposal also prioritizes Wau, as IOM will remain the main water service provider in Wau’s Naivasha IDP Camp during 2022.
IOM’s HRP project contributes to the 4 WASH Cluster Strategic Objectives, focusing on integrating WASH programming with Nutrition, providing access to basic WASH services in emergencies, whilst mitigating WASH GBV and reducing community’s exposure to environmental hazards. In total, IOM plans to reach 550,000 individuals (132,819 women, 127,049 men, 141,594 girls, 148,538 boys) by deploying a range of activity packages that combine hardware and software interventions, adapted to the context at play and to the needs of the most affected population. To support nutrition gains, IOM will rehabilitate/install WASH infrastructure within nutrition sites and/or at community level, while in locations where WASH services need to be rapidly restored due to sudden emergencies, IOM will rehabilitate/drill new boreholes, rehabilitate/set up/operate surface water treatment systems (SWATs). IOM will also ensure the sustainability of WASH infrastructure through the formation and training of community-based operation and maintenance groups. These hardware interventions will be complemented with hygiene promotion, as well as distribution and demonstration on usage of WASH NFIs and/or Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) to help communities sustain the learnt practices. All activities will contribute towards mitigating GBV risks and to improve accessibility to people with special needs, as demonstrated by IOM’s commitment to allocate 2.9 % of the project budget to GBV risk mitigation mainstreaming Where needed, activities will be adjusted to be climate resilient and/or to build the capacity of communities to respond quicker to natural disasters. Where possible, IOM will also prioritise market-based programming approaches, using local markets to support WASH responses. As the largest direct WASH service provider in camp settings, IOM will also continue servicing conflict-affected populations in Malakal Protection of Civilians (PoCs), Bentiu and Wau’s Naivasha Internal Displacement Camp (IDP), and Bentiu’s IDP sites (A, B and C). IOM will provide WASH services through the operation and maintenance of safe and accessible water supply systems, care and maintenance of accessible and secure sanitation and handwashing facilities, as well as management of solid and liquid waste. IOM will also continue investing in cost-effective and climate-resilient means of ensuring adequate service provision within these settings by setting up, maintaining, and scaling up water supply solar systems and biogas schemes. IOM will support the transition process at these sites working to transfer WASH service provision towards community-based ownership where possible, through a participatory approach, with a focus on women’s empowerment. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), and COVID-19 prevention measures will also be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle.

1 - Needs

South Sudan's remains a protracted crisis context, affected by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and public health challenges, including impact of climate change resulting in extraordinary flooding. More than two-thirds of South Sudan's population (8.9 million people) will need humanitarian assistance in 2022 (an increase of 600,000 individuals in comparison to 2021) – 6 million of which will have inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions are predicted to be the most severe across Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, Lakes, Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states – all areas where IOM plans to intervene through HRP. WASH conditions will continue to be dire due to poor results in 2021. By then, only 39 per cent of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs. In Unity, Warrap, Jonglei and Upper Nile states, 33 per cent of households were found to travel for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water – a problematic issue for people with special needs, and one that increases communities’ exposure to GBV risks. Difficulty in securing enough water was only part of the issue, with extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explaining why 49 per cent of households reportedly continued to rely on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. Sanitation wise, 80 per cent of households did not access a latrine in 2021. Conditions were particularly critical in Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei states, where the percentage of households accessing a latrine was found to be below 10 per cent, with open defecation being the norm. GBV risks continued to limit access, especially of women and girls, with 14 per cent reporting feeling unsafe during water collection and 22 per cent when defecating. The WASH Cluster further predicts that the combination between the poor access to WASH services describe above, combined with elevated levels of food insecurity, will continue to have a detrimental impact on children's health, leading to malnutrition and stunting. An estimated 2 million people will be at risk of acute malnourishment in 2022 in Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Eastern Equatoria – all states where Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent was reported. PoCs camps and IDP sites will continue to be at risk of disease outbreaks. For example, HEV is likely to remain endemic to Bentiu IDP camp, with an ever-increased risk of the virus spreading to host communities/other IDP sites, considering population movement. Several exogenous drivers, such as conflict, climate change, COVID-19, funding gaps, as well as chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructure and service provision, will also continue to hamper access to WASH services; which, combined with the high number of people in need, will only add more pressure to already scarce WASH infrastructure. Due to unprecedent flood levels in 2021 it is also likely that there will be an increase in waterborne diseases and added WASH needs in the most affected/at-risk states (Unity and Jonglei). Additionally, the country is still prone to ethnic and politically motivated conflict coupled with localised violence and disputes over land and cattle, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services while also restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure.

SO 2.2: IOM plans to reach 274,413 women and girls (132,819 women, 141,594 girls) as part of its GBV risk mitigation component, as it is rolled out across all its HRP locations (static and RRT) as part of every activity package. SO 3: IOM plans to drill and/or rehabilitate and/or repair 200 flood-resilient boreholes as well as 2,400 latrine stances rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design in Tonj East, Koch, Guit, Rubkona, Malakal, Aweil East, Twic, Tonj South, Tonj North and any other WSC 4 and 5 counties prioritized for IOM’s Rapid Response Team deployment through WASH Cluster allocation. Progress of indicators above will be measured using a mix of quantitative and qualitative data collection methods, adapted to the needs of each project activity package. In RRT projects, IOM utilizes a mobile data collection software to track activities and progress of the project (Fulcrum). All field teams are equipped with mobile devices to collect relevant program information on hygiene promotion activities, NFI distributions, as well as technical information related to the construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities. In addition, all means of verification (such geotagged pictures of the activities or facilities) are submitted through this application, which allows technical management and supervisors to track the progress and quality of the work remotely and in real time. Post-distribution monitoring reports will be conducted by the WASH Core pipeline, considering materials are sourced from there. For static presence/camp-like settings IOM tracks critical indicators and progress of activities through a weekly WASH indicator matrix and cumulative narrative report, which are submitted by the field teams to WASH management to identify issues in a timely manner. IOM also conducts a yearly Knowledge, Attitude and Practice Survey in Bentiu IDP Camp and Malakal PoC, assessing behavioral changes triggered by its Hygiene Promotion activities, combined with a Latrine Functionality and Latrine Usage assessment. WASH indicators are also reported to the WASH Cluster on a monthly basis (e.g., 5Ws), to measure standards and assess needs. Gender and age disaggregated data is also collected to assist provision of targeted and responsive programming.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IOM's activities are based on a participatory approach that puts the beneficiaries at the center of its interventions, ensuring their proactive involvement at each stage of the project cycle: from the identification of general WASH needs and those of the most vulnerable populations, right through the analysis, strategic planning, implementation, and monitoring, as well as evaluation and learning. This will be done by collecting community feedback through community entry engagement, disaggregated focus-group discussions, surveys, semi-structured interviews, daily interactions and discussions, community meetings, and daily engagement based on the humanitarian principles, as part of activity implementation and monitoring. IOM will also ensure that feedback from women, PSNs and other minority/under-represented groups is considered, by engaging them separately and in a safe way. Such information is analyzed, collated, and discussed with the project team, thus consolidating feedback loops and assisting field teams, WASH officers and WASH Management taking stock of the activities and making adjustments that lead to evidence-based interventions, tailored by the beneficiaries' themselves.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Throughout the project cycle, IOM ensures sub-groups representatives, such as women, girls, men, boys, youth, PSNs, elderly and other minority groups are fully engaged from the start through specific FGDs, targeted conversations, safety audits, gender analysis and localized barrier analysis. Feedback of these different forums is also shared through meetings with sub-groups representatives, responsible for disseminating it through their respective groups. In addition, these sub-groups will be informed about the anonymous complaints and feedback mechanisms that will be implemented so they can share their opinion in a safe and secure way.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IOM will ensure that beneficiaries will be informed about their rights in accessing humanitarian services, that they are protected against exploitation and that there are reporting mechanisms through which complaints can be done in a safe and secure way. To ensure that, IOM's HRP proposed activity package will be guided by IOM's protection mainstreaming guidelines and the IASC centrality of protection (2013), IOM's Institutional Framework Addressing GBV in Crisis, and the IASC CBV guidelines. IOM has also an operational PSEA system enforced through the PSEA Policy (IN/024) and is an active member of the South Sudan PSEA taskforce at national level; co-leading State level groups in Wau, Bentiu and Abeyi. IOM will also ensure that staff and volunteers are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, in line with IOM's Code of Conduct, and that they will be held accountable. The activities are designed based on the Humanitarian Sphere standards and South Sudan's WASH Cluster Protection Mainstreaming tip sheet. This helps ensure that beneficiaries have access to safe, secure, dignified, and sufficient potable water and sanitation facilities. In addition, IOM will provide the UN in South Sudan hotline where complainants are able to report the cases and will train all staff and volunteers on the reporting mechanisms.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In line with the WASH Cluster AAP Checklist, IOM promotes community participation and engagement throughout the project cycle to increase the ability of affected population to give feedback, have access to adequate and timely information and participate in WASH programs’ design, implementation, and evaluation. For example, IOM informs local authorities and community members about the intervention and its duration, humanitarian standards of conduct and complaint-feedback reporting mechanism (CFM) during community entry meetings. Community consultation and safety audits are also always conducted, ensuring separate discussions are held with women, men, girls, boys, PSNs and representatives of other/minority sub-groups (e.g. youth, elderly). These are used to discuss the implementation process, as well as accessibility to infrastructure rehabilitated and/or distribution sites; ensuring site selection is accessible, safe and secure, and that distribution lists are based on community-led selection criteria. Selection of community members is carried out in an equitable and inclusive process to ensure women, men, minority groups and PSNs are represented. To ensure that, prior to the process, IOM ensures community members are fully aware of the recruitment process and that selection criteria and job descriptions are displayed within key community locations. In addition, IOM implements an anonymous, age-appropriate feedback and complaint mechanism, adopted for minority groups and PSNs , where individuals can reach out to the organization to provide feedback on the intervention and quality of the work. In host communities, during initial community meetings, IOM announces the availability of such channel, encouraging beneficiaries to provide feedback/raise complaints linked to IOM’s service provision. To enable collection of such information, IOM hires individuals per location who are trained on CFM and complaints’ recording; whilst also conducting FGDs with women, girls, men, and boys, as well as PSNs’ to better understand the challenges and report to their supervisors for follow up. During WASH NFI distributions, a complaints desk is also made available for beneficiaries. All complaints are logged in an online management database (KOBO) and kept confidential, with information relayed to the appropriate parties depending on the type of feedback/complaint at hands. In the context of PoC/IDP sites WASH service delivery, IOM liaises with respective Camp Management agencies to ensure that transparent information dissemination, and clarity of procedures and complaint and feedback mechanisms through which the affected population can regularly express their concerns are shared with beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To mitigate those risks, IOM will conduct a safety audit with vulnerable groups within the community to inform safety planning and determine the safest location and time of day to conduct distributions or build infrastructure. In addition, IOM will liaise with protection actors in specific locations to facilitate safe referral of cases, help identify the barriers to access services especially for persons with disabilities and other vulnerable groups, and determine ways to improve access in a safe, dignified, and accountable manner. When there is no protection partner on ground, IOM will not raise awareness on issues that require protection response but will form a body of well-respected individuals (such as teachers, religious leaders, elderly) including women representatives to support IOM in ensuring protection concerns are taken into consideration during the entire process of distribution starting from verification/registration up to the distribution. The same body also support to address any complaints coming during the distribution process (e.g. missing token, missing items, etc.). IOM WASH staff are trained in Protection and GBV risk mitigation and safe and ethical referral of protection cases. As per general practice in IOM, ahead of any WASH deployments, IOM GBV and Protection specialists ensure the referral pathways information for specific areas are shared with the team. WASH teams are then able to share these with the recruited volunteers within targeted locations, who can then, in turn, share with their community members, so that GBV survivors are able to seek medical and psychological support as soon as possible. WASH teams responding are also trained on psychological first aid. To address risks in the context of WASH programming in host communities including PSEA, IOM informs the community leaders about the CFM during community entry dialogues. This information is also shared with direct beneficiaries of program activities. To address risks linked to WASH NFI distribution, IOM will inform the beneficiaries about the CFM during the WASH NFIs registration process. After completing the verification and registration process, IOM conducts a safety audit with the women group and PSN group to understand the most secure location and most suitable time (of the day) for distribution. At the same time, when there is no protection partner on ground, IOM forms a body of well-respected individuals including women (such as teachers, religious leaders, elderly) who support IOM in ensuring protection concerns are taken into consideration during the entire process of distribution starting from verification/registration up to the distribution. The same body also supports to address any complaints coming during the distribution process (e.g., missing token, missing items, etc.). In WASH response at community level, IOM liaises with the highest level of governance body (County administration office/payam administration office, County WASH department, RRC representatives etc.) to explain the mission objectives and geographical coverage. This exercise is done to ensure the governance body understands the geographical coverage that IOM has been mandated to target. As a follow up, for WASH NFI distribution processes, IOM conducts the verification and registration exercise in the locality itself rather than bringing the community to a certain location. Led by IOM staff and supported by the locally hired volunteers, IOM maximizes the number of field visits during the verification exercise, where accessibility is possible. At this point, local level community leaders, women leaders, and vulnerable groups are consulted. Selection criteria for selecting the beneficiaries are well explained to the community leaders. One or two days before the exercise, the community is informed through miking, in collaboration with the community leaders.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

IOM’s WASH HRP project includes reducing community members exposure to environmental hazards through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure and the capacity building of community members in disaster risk reduction. The aim is to ensure that communities are better prepared to respond to shocks ahead/when they happen minimizing the likelihood of having to move away due to lack of WASH services that would, otherwise, help them withstand the shock and/or bounce back better. To that end, IOM will deploy a three pronged approach by investing in: i) construction/rehabilitation of flood-resilient water points by elevating the platform, protect the sanitary seal, and ensuring access to people with special needs; ii) construction of flood-resistant sanitation facilities through pit lining to mitigate risk of collapsing, increase the lining above ground level to avoid rainwater filtration, and ensuring access to people with special needs; and iii) capacity building of local communities on maintenance of critical/life-saving WASH infrastructure to increase their capacity to restore the systems’ functionality without having to wait for humanitarian assistance during and after disaster, done by engaging local authorities, community-based organizations, pump mechanics, and IDPs Camp management Committees on WASH infrastructures' operation and management, WASH-related Disaster Risk Reduction activities, including WASH preparedness and response plans for potential shocks (diseases outbreak, floods, displacement). Where possible, this response will also be done with Shelter NFI as a joint response, focusing on disaster risk management and through a Build Back Better approach as part of community empowerment and ownership.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Good access to WASH services is a critical element to achieve the above, as it helps secure nutritional outcomes since unclean water, inadequate sanitation, and poor hygiene practices are linked to undernutrition. As per activities highlighted in previous sections, IOM's HRP WASH program directly contributes to decreasing the prevalence of acute malnutrition, as its focus is on increasing access to safe drinking water and sanitation conditions at community level and at nutrition sites. For example, IOM will restore broken boreholes, by either rehabilitating existing water points and/or installing additional ones in Tonj East, Malakal, Rubkona, Tonj North, Leer and any other WSC locations under priority location 1, 2, 3 and 4 prioritized for IOM’s Rapid Response Team deployment through WASH Cluster allocation. In flood at-risk areas, IOM will complement the above package by investing in the construction/rehabilitation of flood-resilient water and sanitation facilities (e.g. by elevating the platform/protecting the sanitary seal/conducting shock chlorination/increasing the lining above the ground level) and in building the capacity of local communities to restore functionality of flood affected infrastructure by themselves. To further guarantee safe water, IOM works with the community to build fences around the improved boreholes and cattle troughs constructed/rehabilitated to reduce contamination from livestock, whilst conducting water quality testing across the water supply chain. If there is a need to increase free residual chlorine (FRC) at the household level, IOM will train bucket chlorinators positioned at the targeted boreholes to support users with bucket chlorination. Ensuring caretakers have access to continuous safe, equitable and dignified water sources will ensure that children under 5 will also be able to consume safe and treated water while ensuring water availability for hygiene purposes. IOM will complement safe water with hygiene promotion at the community level, focusing on emphasizing disease prevention (HEV, AWD, Cholera, C-19), safe excreta management, and handwashing with soap. Where possible, IOM will also work closely with Nutrition partners operating in locations above by improving water points and sanitation facilities at nutrition sites, providing WASH NFI kits for water treatment and safe handling, as well as storage (buckets/jerrycans), soaps for handwashing practices to SAM and MAM caregivers, and strengthening hygiene messaging on personal hygiene, including babies' feces disposal. Interventions will be done in coordination with other sectors such as Food and Security Livelihood (FSL), Health and Nutrition, to address the humanitarian needs of the most affected population.
