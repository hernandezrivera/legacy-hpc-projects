Project Summary

The project primary goal is to improve the education outputs for boys and girls aged 4-15 years, predominantly in primary schools using humanitarian education cash grants. This would specifically also include interventions designed and carried out to address social and cultural bottlenecks of girls and boys at the household level, to be able to attend school, promote inclusive education activities at the learning institutional level and address underlying socio-economic factors that burden the families of the children. The project will also do distribution of school kits (learning, teaching materials), Capacity building of teacher in Inclusive Education and PC, and Construction of school latrines.
The project target children aged 4 to 15 years in areas affected by humanitarian crises in the Region of the Northwest North, and West of the country. The project target the most vulnerable children including out of school children who have not attended school regularly, children who drop out in the middle of the school-term, children with physical and learning disabilities, girls who are not able to attend due to family socio-cultural priorities and are at high risk to child marriage, and children that are poor where families depend on their children to contribute to the family income and household needs. The project will ensure that; children and their families and caretakers have improved socio-economic conditions at the household level; girls and boys receive education cash grants; children with disabilities gain access to inclusive education. Project will focus on children who are victims of inequality, specifically girls and those living with disabilities in the targeted areas

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The on-going crises in the South-West and North-West regions of Cameroon and the multiple displacements caused by it have affected children education the most. According to need assessments report we carried, it is estimated that more than 600.000 children have been affected by school closures in the SW and NW regions. More than 80% of the schools in these regions are closed with more than 70% destroyed, while students, parents, teachers and other school personnel are subjected to routine violence and intimidation. The targeting of education is putting the future of an entire generation of children at risk. Many parents have sent their children to school in safer towns and neighbouring regions like in the West and Littoral with very limited educational facility to meet the demands. Access to inclusive education for the most vulnerable children is a continuing challenge against the backdrop of a humanitarian crisis and socio-economic development of the SDGs. These are guided and prioritized in the UN humanitarian response and the National Vision 2035. Delivery of timely and accessible, as well as inclusive and appropriate education services are not reaching all children main due to the public institutions’ inability to provide complete school infrastructures, and fund sufficient human resources. For those children living in conditions with protracted humanitarian crisis, multiple burdens on the parents, caretakers, teachers and schools/learning institutions require a strategy that is holistic and community driven to maximize the child’s full potential. According to data from the Regional Education Delegation, of the 95 primary schools that were closed during the 2017-2018 school year, only 26 were reopened in the 2018-2019 school year. The remaining (69) remained closed for security reasons.
