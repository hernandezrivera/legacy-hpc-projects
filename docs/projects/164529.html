Project Summary

The IRC proposes relief and recovery programming to respond to food security needs in northeast Syria, using a mixture of food transfer modalities, including FSL-aligned rapid response of ready-to-eat rations (RTER) and cash transfers for food security assistance (8 rounds of monthly assistance, with cash transfer value equivalent to 75% of the food SMEB for the number of individuals in the HH, in line with FSL guidance). As food assistance alone will not address the humanitarian need for food security in NES. the IRC will also support the restoration of livelihoods and agricultural production through vocational training.

4 - Needs

According to the draft 2020 Syria HNO, 7.9 million people are food insecure, and 1.9 million are at risk of food insecurity. Widespread disruption of income-generating activities, destruction of infrastructure, reduced public sector jobs, repeated shocks, and multiple displacements have contributed to high unemployment and reliance on harmful coping strategies to meet basic needs. Over 50% of Syrians are unemployed (75% among youth), and 83% live below the poverty line. Some 69 percent of Syrians are believed to be living on less than USD 1.90 a day, more than double since 2011. Women’s participation in the labor force is estimated at 16%. Coupled with inflated prices and fluctuating exchange rates, Syrian household purchasing power is severely weakened, limiting the capacity to meet basic needs and leading the majority of households to rely on negative coping strategies. This situation is exacerbated by gender inequality and poverty, where many families have lost their savings and productive assets, remain still in debt and for the most vulnerable- fall into a cycle of harmful coping mechanisms due to limited access to both opportunities and resources. Protection risks are exacerbated by the challenges facing female and child headed households, those without documentation, and other vulnerable groups. There is a lack of economic and livelihood opportunities for women, particularly those who are divorced or widowed, where engagement of women in these activities is perceived as culturally inappropriate or neglectful of domestic responsibilities. As such, women remain with reduced decision making and power within the household and community, and forced into the private sphere- thus continuing the cycle of harmful gender norms.

5 - Implementation of activities

Prior to all food security interventions, the IRC conducts an assessment to determine the food security index. Target areas are selected according to the highest levels of need, the priorities and gap analysis of the FSL WG and CWG and potential linkages with IRC's multi-sector response program. Cash for Food (Raqqa, Deir ez-Zor): IRC conducts HH registration and selection using socioeconomic vulnerability criteria and food security criteria (FCS, HDDS, food-related NCS). Priority is given to female-headed HH, widows, pregnant and lactating women, households with high numbers of dependents, HH with little or no access to income, the elderly, people with disabilities, high usage of NCS, and other vulnerabilities. IRC will provide eligible HH with eight monthly cash transfers to meet basic food needs. In line with FSL/CWG guidance, the cash transfer value is calculated at 75% of the food SMEB value and customized to the HH size. Cash is delivered in SYP via hawala agency, with exchange rate based on the rate that day. The IRC MEAL team conducts beneficiary verification of selected HHs prior to distribution. After each distribution, the MEAL team conducts PDM to assess how the assistance was used, measure its impact and receive beneficiary feedback. RTER: The IRC will distribute RTER to newly displaced IDPs. RTER are sufficient to cover the needs of a household for 6 days with 2,100 KG, in line with SPHERE standards. RTER will be distributed in coordination with the FSL WG in camps and locations without access to functioning markets. Agro vocational training (Deir ez-Zor, Raqqa): IRC will provide mobile vocational training in climate-smart agro and livestock production and cash transfers for agro inputs and livestock production. Targeting criteria include: previous experience, access to sufficient water and land, and, for livestock, 11-20 sheep/goats or 1 cow and appropriate shelter (barn). Cash for agro value is in line with FSS recommendations (180 USD).

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- KRI border closure: Risks of border closures and resulting interruption of life-saving for new IDPs and for the emergency services will be mitigated by keeping contingency ready to eat in stock to respond to new displacement.- Insecurity/loss of access: IRC ensures regular review and updating of contingency plans for its operations inside Syria to mitigate against changes in context. This has recently been updated for NES based on the current context and including multiple scenarios regarding changes in territorial control, continuation or interruption of cross-border access and access to locations of implementation. Humanitarian access staff negotiate with local actors, work closely with program leads to identify and manage risks and engage with local councils to avoid any interference or diversion of aid. IRC has zero tolerance for interference and\or diversion of aid to any parties to the conflict. IRC has a Security Management Plan and Standard Operating Procedures to guide IRC operations and staff to provide lifesaving assistance using the safest possible methods.- Diversion: IRC shares FARM materials and explains to beneficiaries that they are not expected to provide anything to anyone to benefit from the assistance. IRC closely monitors risk using the FARM and PDM, and any evidence of malfeasance is reported to IRC’s Ethics and Compliance Unit to be investigated and addressed in a prompt, comprehensive and safe manner. The IRC’s reporting system provides employees with multiple channels to report concerns. The IRC’s procurement controls also serve to mitigate risk of diversion through supplier due diligence.- Negative market impact: IRC collects market information on the price and availability of key commodities each month to inform transfer amounts and to assess the strength of markets and feeds data into the NES SMEB calculation led by REACH. IRC also conducts rapid market assessments prior to each cash distribution.
