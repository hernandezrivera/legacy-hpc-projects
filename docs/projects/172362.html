Project Summary

The project will achieve its overall objective by coordinating the financing and response actions needed to reduce avoidable mortality and morbidity and reduce the economic, social and security impacts of the pandemic for both migrants and the Libyan population especially prioritized geographical areas (severity scale 3,4 and 5) . The main challenges faced by the population post C-19 are the result of the failure to rapidly mobilize financing for appropriate detection and response efforts. This will result in increased mortality and morbidity, significant economic consequences, and reduced resilience. By focusing on the containment, diagnosis and treatment of patients, this Project seeks to help control the pandemic and limit socioeconomic losses.
In addition, by strengthening the public health subsystem’s capacity to provide health services, the Project will also contribute to enhancing citizens’ access to proper health care. Project activities fall into the categories of biomedical equipment and supplies for ICUs and isolation facilities, laboratory diagnostics, supplies and reagents, PPE for health care workers involved in the case management of C-19 patients, HR and training and capacity building of health personnel involved in defining national protocols for managing the pandemic.
Immediate outcomes will include focusing and strengthening the already established coordination mechanisms since the beginning of pandemic, improving detection through surveillance, taking preventive measures to decrease risk and protect vulnerable groups, and improving the response to the public health crisis and humanitarian needs. The specific objective of this action is to improve the capacities of the Libyan authorities to address C-19 related challenges as well as assist the vulnerable population of Libya, including migrants, IDPs and conflict-affected communities with protection and life-saving support
The overall goal is to prevent the spread of COVID-19 and reduce avoidable morbidity and mortality in Libya. This Action will support the following direct beneficiaries:
• Relevant Libyan authorities from the Ministry of Health (MoH), NCDC, Ministry of Interior, Ministry of Local Government, Ministry of Social Affairs, Ministry of Education.
• Vulnerable populations of Libya, including persons affected by C-19, IDPs, returnees, migrants, refugees, asylum seekers, host communities and conflict-affected populations, especially children, women, and groups with specific needs.
• Direct beneficiaries: At least 643,332 beneficiaries will benefit from this action.
• Indirect beneficiaries: An estimated 4.8 million people reached through RCCE and 1.7million people living in prioritized geographical areas classified as 4 and 5 on the severity scale.

Activities under three main outcomes are operationalized that would be delivered by this Action can be broadly categorized as follows, based on the nine pillars of the C-19 defined in operational planning guidelines to support country preparedness and response document for COVID19 Strategic Preparedness Response Plan .
1. Strong national and subnational coordination is focused and strengthened to deliver strategic, technical, and operational support through existing or public health emergency management mechanisms and partnerships.
2. Awareness raised to prevent the spread of C-19 in the population though a risk communication and community engagement strategy to limit exposure.
3. Enhanced epidemic intelligence through real-time surveillance systems for the rapid detection of suspected cases .

1 - Needs

Poor coordination at different levels is a major issue. Although the draft national plan reflects the key strategies for different transmission scenarios and the responsibilities of the two national committees, it lacks specifics on the strategies and activities under each area of work. The national plan was reviewed by the WHO country and regional offices, which submitted recommendations on the strategies and activities to be incorporated and updated. (As of the time of writing this proposal, WHO’s recommendations as per the latest guidance available and updated as per the current community transmission scenario had not been incorporated into the plan.) Moreover, the links between the scientific committee in the Prime Minister’s office and the technical committees under the NCDC are poor. WHO along with partners such as UNDP is continuing to advocate at the highest levels for the national plan to be updated and endorsed by the government but also to start a health policy dialogue to develop partnerships and capacity at the central/national level to respond to C-19It is also important to note that the scientific committee did not recommend a state of emergency when the transmission scenario in Libya was classified as “clusters of cases”, nor did it activate the PHEOCs in either the western region (in April 2020) or the southern region (in June 2020) to provide coordinated management of the evolving situation. While regional and municipality level mechanisms include components and elements of the national plan, they are not uniformly structured. For example, the regional technical committee in the east is called the Consultative Medical Committee while in the southern region, it is known as the Health Crisis Committee. Sebha Municipality has a Health Crisis Committee but no designated or specialized technical committee. Implementation and coordination of the national plan are poorly coordinated at all three levels (national, regional (district) and municipality). Other important reasons for the suboptimal performance of the technical committees, especially at the regional level, are that members lack public health expertise on C-19, have not been trained on emergency operations and are unable to secure access to the resources required for the response. WHO is actively involved as the lead agency for most of the C-19 pillars and is also a member of the steering and scientific committees. UNFPA, UNICEF and IOM lead the pillars on maintaining essential health services, RCCE and points of entry, respectively. The key challenge related to logistics and supplies is that the MoH in Tripoli has been unable to provide WHO with detailed information on C-19 supplies that are either available, in the pipeline or on waiting lists. (Sharing this information is essential to avoid duplication of efforts.) There has been no forecasting of needs and no meaningful country-wide analysis of local market capacity or the scalability of the national and external response. Data from municipalities are not being aggregated or analysed centrally. The overall C-19 response is hampered by restrictions imposed by the national authorities on the free movement of medical and other humanitarian personnel. Even when exceptions are granted for humanitarian workers, access is slowed by bureaucratic delays. The Medical Supply Office under the MoH has been involved to only a limited extent in the procurement of supplies and equipment for C-19. WHO has recommended to the MoH that a case management strategy be integrated into the NCDC’s national preparedness and response plan. Case management guidelines were extensively revised by the scientific committee with inputs from WHO’s country and regional offices to include a discussion on oxygen therapy in resource limited settings. Initial reports received indicated that although volunteers were working in some isolation sites, most were not staffed.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

CFM in Libya: With the support of the the Government of Luxembourg, the ETS is bridging this gap by establishing Tawasul (“communication” in Arabic) a Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in the form of a single, toll-free, country-wide hotline number that affected populations can call to obtain information on humanitarian assistance programs; submit their feedback; and get referrals to the humanitarian organizations that are best-suited to handle their needs. In turn, the CFM helps humanitarians work more efficiently towards meeting those needs. Through partnerships with 6 UN agencies and 5 Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs), the CFM particularly aims to assist women, children, Internally Displaced People (IDPs), migrants, refugees, asylum seekers and digitally illiterate individuals. Since the outbreak of COVID-19, the ETS-managed call centre now has a dual purpose as it is acting as a nationwide COVID-19 information channel, as requested by the Libyan Ministry of Health’s National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC).
