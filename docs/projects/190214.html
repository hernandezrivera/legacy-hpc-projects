Project Summary

OCHA’s direct beneficiaries are the members of the humanitarian community operating in Ethiopia: UN Agencies, international and national NGOs, government institutions and donor representatives present in Ethiopia and member states through local embassies and consulates.
The humanitarian community in Ethiopia continues to rely heavily on OCHA’s coordination and support function, especially around operational coordination and humanitarian access at national and sub-national levels across the country. OCHA’s work is critical to unlock operational and access challenges, including the regularization of the movement of goods and services. Humanitarian response across Ethiopia has been hampered by the challenging and significant access constraints particularly affecting the Northern Ethiopia response (Tigray, Afar and Amhara), but also in many other parts of the country, including Benishangul Gumuz, Oromia, and Somali regions.
For 2022 and beyond, in addition to conflict dynamics, it is anticipated that disease outbreaks, including a continuation of the COVID-19 pandemic and/or its aftermath, floods, desert locusts infestations, and climatic shocks along with food insecurity and acute malnutrition will continue to affect lives and livelihoods and wear down development gains, further escalating humanitarian needs. The food insecurity and malnutrition situation in the country is also worsening significantly. Disruption of farming activities, below-normal harvests from previous seasons, devaluation of the Ethiopian Birr, and increase in market demands have also caused market prices of basic commodities to soar.
In 2022, OCHA will continue to scale-up its work in all regions of Ethiopia. This will include a focus on ensuring that coordination needs across the country are addressed by strengthening and harmonizing linkages between national and sub-national coordination structures to enable robust humanitarian response and expansion of coordination mechanisms in Northern Ethiopia and across the rest of the country. OCHA will also support improved and consistent communication on the work carried out by humanitarian partners, based on the principles of accountability and transparency and on a robust evidence-based monitoring of the response. OCHA will support collective advocacy around the protection of all affected communities and principled humanitarian response, which also includes resource mobilization strategies that are based on needs and common priorities. OCHA’s pooled funds (EHF and CERF) will be particularly important in providing financing capacity to enable partners to deliver timely, coordinated and principled assistance. Special attention will be paid to ensuring meaningful participation of local actors in funding opportunities. Finally, through its mandate and comparative advantage in the areas of Access and Civil-Military Coordination, OCHA will facilitate and coordinate humanitarian actors’ efforts to establish and maintain humanitarian access and overcome increased access constraints caused by rising insecurity, rhetoric and misperception around aid workers.

1. - Projects activities

1. Effective, accountable and principled coordination of humanitarian assistance:- Provide overall leadership to the two major UN and NGO coordination structures indirectly (EHCT) or directly (ICCG). - Produce regular Humanitarian Bulletins, with detailed context analysis of needs, response, gaps on ongoing crises.- Promote and strengthen engagement with NGOs (national and international).- Coordinate the Humanitarian Programme Cycle process, which includes the HNO, HRP and MYR.2. More credible, comprehensive, and evidence-based situational analysis:- Support the organization and coordination of seasonal assessments, multi-agency/inter-agency assessments, whenever possible. Whenever physical assessments are not feasible due to lack of access, insecurity etc., adopt assessment methods to use remote sensing, early warning information and secondary data.- Use assessment findings in analytical products such as the HNO, humanitarian snapshots.- Strengthen monitoring systems that allow ongoing analysis of changes of the situation and needs.- Manage the Response Planning and Monitoring platform and ongoing data collection from clusters on response provided against targets and operational presence (3Ws).- Implement and manage web-based tools (e.g., HPC.Tools) and platforms (e.g., HR.info) for data management and information/product sharing.- Ensure OCHA information products are available for the humanitarian community.- Improve monitoring and information sharing systems to further support evidence-based decision-making within and outside OCHA through introduction of digital data collection mechanism, improving the reporting system and strengthening analysis.3. An effective, innovative humanitarian financing system that meets the needs of crisis-affected people:- Develop Humanitarian Response Plans that articulate needs and gaps in collaboration with relevant stakeholders and associated with appropriate costing methodology to support robust resource mobilization activities. Provide regular funding updates.- Carry out advocacy for resourcing of the humanitarian response, especially for priority needs across the country, with the HRP as the framework strategy for all resource mobilisation activities, including for the Ethiopia Humanitarian Fund (EHF) and Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF) Allocations.- Manage the EHF and support the CERF allocations as applicable.- Organize regular bi-lateral/joint meetings/briefings with donors to update on humanitarian activities, needs and results.- Provide regular funding gap analysis and monitor it4. Acceptance of the centrality of international humanitarian and human rights law, access and protection that results in meaningful action for affected people, especially internally displaced people:- Provide advocacy (public and private), together with the EHCT and donors, to national and local authorities on humanitarian principles (IDP guiding principles/ humanitarian access etc.), including drafting advocacy points, collective position papers etc. In addition, support advocacy towards the military and other armed forces on the ground, as well as towards local communities (when pertinent).- Support the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG), the Protection Cluster and GBV sub-cluster, to ensure that protection approaches, including prevention of and response to SGBV, is mainstreamed in all humanitarian action.- Promote the Access Monitoring and Framework Tool and an efficient communication between the UN security system and INGOs as per the ‘Saving Lives Together’ framework.- Support IDP-related programming and coordination as needed.- Conduct trainings to humanitarian partners on Humanitarian Principles – Access – and Negotiations as well as on Humanitarian Civil - Military Coordination (CMCoord). Conduct trainings/ awareness sessions targeting Government and security forces on Humanitarian Coordination, Humanitarian Principles, and CMCoord.
