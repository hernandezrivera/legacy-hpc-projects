4 - Needs

Chronic and acute malnutrition is emerging and developing in Syria based on nutrition surveillance 2020 comparing with 2020 ( GAM rate on 2020 was 2.7 % ). reflecting inadequate micronutrient intake, poor feeding practices as reflected by low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life and inadequate complementary food in terms of quality and frequency. The nutrition surveillance system is now consisted of a network of up to 928 health centres, with 1,240,.000 beneficiaries in 2020, further expansion is required to ensure more geographical coverage in rural areas of governorates as well as reactivating new centres in Ar-Raqqa, Hassakeh, Rural Aleppo, Dair Ezzor, Idleb and Daraa.As the GAM rate reported from the nutrition surveillance system, 27455 global malnutrition cases were detected compared to 15121 GAM cases 2019 , this increasing could be due to COVID19 context in Syria. In 2020 a total of 25 stabilization centres are supported to receive complicated cases of severe acute malnutrition with a total number of admissions 1488 on 2020. High severity scale is taken into consideration in Nutrition surveillance, nutrition screening, and stabilization centres implementation especially in NES, Aleppo, north west and south of Syria as priority. These areas require further expansion of nutrition surveillance sites, screening teams and strengthening health worker capacity to detect and manage malnourished cases. Infant and young child feeding practices remain low and suboptimal as indicated from the 2019 SMART survey. support is required to strengthen and maintain the service provision at trained hospitals across the country. IYCF counselling has been initiated in 828 health centre requires expansion.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Support facility and community-based screening for acute and chronic malnutrition and referral via training and anthropometric equipment.2. Improve the treatment of severely acute malnourished children with complications at hospital level through supporting established stabilization centres with training, nutrition therapeutic supplies and equipment.3. Capacity building of health facility staff and community workers on appropriate IYCF practices including implementing the 10 steps to successful breast feeding through BFHI, management of complicated SAM and nutrition surveillance, and early childhood development.4. Support facility and community-based counselling and awareness raising on IYCF and optimal maternal nutrition.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Limited geographical coverage of nutrition surveillance sites and outreach screening for the early detection of malnutrition which increases the risk of undetected cases of acute malnutrition at early stages.2. Limited support of stabilization centres especially in hard-to-reach areas could present a high risk of mortality rate during hospitalization for complicated cases of SAM.3. Reduced rates of exclusive breastfeeding increase the risk of malnutrition in children U5 and morbidity and mortality due to other health implications due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding.
