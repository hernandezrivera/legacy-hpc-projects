Project Summary

The Common Transport Services (CTS) is a free-for-user service that transports key humanitarian supplies on behalf of humanitarian actors. CTS serves as a critical link in the supply chain, enabling products and materials that arrive from regional and international suppliers to move onwards to partners located deep in the field rapidly. IOM has been managing the CTS on behalf of the Logistics Cluster since 2011. During this tie, the project has significantly grown from delivering 2,000 MT of humanitarian cargo in 2011 to a total of 17,773 MT by the end of 2020, making a yearly average of 25000 MT since the beginning, highlighting the increase in the use of road transport as the most cost-efficient mode of transportation as access improves. IOM has continued to maintain flexibility and adaptability to the operating environment in South Sudan. Through CTS, IOM works with more than 80 unique agencies in key operational locations in Jonglei, Unity, and Greater Upper Nile Region, in Humanitarian Logistics Hubs (Malakal, Bentiu), and Rumbek, Bor, Juba, and Wau, which are part of intersectoral severity counties level 4 to 5 and prioritized counties according to the HNO 2021. IOM will maintain heavy vehicles and transport cargo from the main ports of dispatch via air, road, and river and will continue common warehouse management in key operational locations.
Through the effective management of the CTS, IOM aims to support organizations' preparedness measures to mitigate risks associated with increased insecurity, looting/theft of humanitarian supplies, critical access impediments to vulnerable people, and reduce expensive air costs, which ultimately supports the timely response to the needs of the affected populations. With this funding, IOM will directly work with 80 humanitarian organizations located in different areas, enabling them to deliver critical life-saving humanitarian services and assistance items.

1 - Needs

Poor road infrastructure is one of the main challenges for people in need of access to life-saving assistance and services and for humanitarian organizations to reach the people. Access impediments cause delays and add substantial extra costs. The lack of road infrastructure makes the supply chain in South Sudan extremely expensive, not only for humanitarian operations but also for the private sector. According to the HNO 2021, poor roads, and the lack of market infrastructure are some of the reasons South Sudan ranks 185 out of 190 economies. Due to climate variability, South Sudan experienced exceptionally short dry seasons in 2019 and 2020. This has resulted in a shorter window for humanitarian agencies to preposition essential commodities ahead of the rainy season. Access to markets, which has already been limited by poor road infrastructure and transport options, further deteriorates during the long rainy season. The majority of the rural population is far from or unable to reach markets during most of the year. In late 2020, access to many areas affected by flooding remained challenging, with roads washed away and locations continuously inundated with water. As such, major roads are expected to become physically accessible later than usual. This has shortened the humanitarian cargo prepositioning window during the dry season. Violence and insecurity could further impact the populations' access to life-saving assistance and services and the ability of humanitarian organizations to reach populations in need. This could result in humanitarian organizations increasingly relying heavily on costly air transport (HNO 2021). South Sudan is one of the most complex humanitarian operating environments globally, with persistent insecurity, poor infrastructure, and seasonal hazards. Humanitarian needs are dispersed across the country, often in areas challenging to access due to geographical remoteness and lack of infrastructure. Affected populations in hard-to-reach locations face considerable protection risks, with many not provided with consistent humanitarian assistance. It is imperative that support continues for the prepositioning of relief items in the field by road where possible, which remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. The CTS project will ensure cargo is transported from warehouses to airstrip/riverside docks for onward transport to priority locations (in Bentiu, Bor, Juba, Malakal, Rumbek, and Wau) or directly to priority locations through the Beyond Responses, which enables the most time-critical, life-saving frontline multi-cluster activities through a cost-effective and reliable logistics resources provision at ICCG prioritized counties. This project is key in assisting more than 80 humanitarian organizations in South Sudan to deliver life-saving and emergency materials and products to areas where they are most needed across the country through a fleet of 18 trucks, with the addition of common storage facilities where needed. This project ensures beneficiaries can safely access services and resources with dignity, including in hard-to-reach/newly accessible locations. The CTS fleet of trucks ensures the provision of cargo transportation by road, while optimizing cost-effectiveness, and improving the efficiency and timeliness of the wider humanitarian response in South Sudan.In addition to the above, CTS contributes towards making the overall humanitarian response 'as local as possible' through enabling equal and impartial access to assistance and services. In this context, CTS ensures the timely delivery of humanitarian supplies to priority locations served by Logistic Cluster partners, including international and national (I/NGOs) in South Sudan. Therefore, CTS can be considered an enabler towards frontline activities being carried out by I/NGOs where the capacity exists. Any reduction in the scale of CTS will reduce the humanitarian response and put already vulnerable lives in danger.

2 - Project activities

Effective logistics and Supply Chain Management is the backbone of Humanitarian Response operations, enabling delivery of the frontline response. More than 80 agencies representing all relevant clusters are dependent on the CTS for transport, airport/river port clearing, loader management, and warehousing. In response to the logistics challenges faced in critical areas in South Sudan, IOM (in collaboration with the Logistics Cluster) will continue to provide reliable and readily-available assets to transport humanitarian supplies intended to provide relief to affected populations. Through this project, IOM will maintain sufficient technical staffing, office, and mechanical workshop facilities to provide adequate logistics support to partners and counterparts through CTS. This project will also allow the full-time availability of 18 dedicated trucks that will meet the cargo movement capacity necessary to transport relief items to various NGOs and humanitarian agencies serving the critical areas in South Sudan. IOM will continue to position 18 trucks in Bor (3 trucks), Bentiu (4 trucks), Malakal (4 trucks), Rumbek (3 trucks), Juba (2 trucks), and Wau (2 trucks), reflecting operational requirements of humanitarian agencies. The geographic distribution of transport assets is based on priorities and subject to change when necessary. IOM has continued to maintain flexibility and adaptability to the operating environment in South Sudan. IOM will also adjust the service provision to accommodate COVID-19 and flood response preparedness efforts. CTS will encompass six mutually reinforcing activities, as follows: 1. Provide common transport services to the humanitarian community (INGOs, UN) to facilitate the rapid deployment of essential humanitarian cargo to field locations in coordination with humanitarian partners (through the ICWG). 2. Manage a fleet of 18 IOM managed CTS trucks to provide timely overland transport of humanitarian cargo, including air cargo handling, and maintain effective Fleet Management System. 3. Maintain and Improve Humanitarian Logistics Hubs (Malakal and Bentiu), including to increase the storage capacity of humanitarian actors operating in these locations. 4. Provide Common Warehouse Management in Malakal and Bentiu, and other priority locations (Bor, Rumbek). 5. Handle cargo effectively and professionally, through consolidation and loading at hubs, and cargo handling until the final destination. 6. Provide staff to accompany project vehicles, monitor cargo handling, and maintain communications with the control center in Juba.

3 - Project indicators

IOM has developed and maintains a comprehensive trends analysis dashboard, detailing the quantity of cargo transported, allocation of materials transport by cluster, locations materials are transported, truck locations and the number of agencies supported. Weekly updates on truck locations and activities will continue to be produced and shared with the Logistics Cluster and other partners. In coordination with the Logistics Cluster, IOM trucks are positioned in priority locations. Updates on locations and activities are provided to the Logistics Cluster on a weekly basis. The IOM team works regularly with the Logistics Cluster to ensure that any information needed is accessed quickly.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CTS enables products and materials to rapidly move onwards to partners located deep in the field, enabling delivery of life-saving frontline responses. This project will therefore serve to lessen the need for communities to leave their habitual place of residence for the purpose of receiving assistance, as humanitarian and development agencies are better equipped to deliver key relief items across hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan. This will also serve to lessen the exposure of women and girls to sexual violence in relation to access to humanitarian assistance, and more particularly as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services.
