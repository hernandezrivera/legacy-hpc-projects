Project Summary

The UNICEF education project plans to reach a total of 2,904,400 (or 55%) of the children and adults identified in the HNO 2021 under severity scale 3-5 (199 sub-districts).
This proposal suggests access to inclusive and quality education-as-a-basic-service measures for children affected by the Syrian humanitarian crisis. The below activities are designed to help children realize their right to education, as well as to cope, learn and contribute to their communities in a context characterized by multiple risks and crisis: conflict, the global pandemic, and economic shocks. UNICEF prioritizes the following activities in line with 2021 HNO/HRP: (1) scaling up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education; (2) enhancing and monitoring the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 3-17 years) within a protective environment; and (3) strengthening the capacity and the coordination of stakeholders, partners and education personnel to build resilience and effective response. The proposal (including for the selection of damaged schools) focuses on Sub-Districts ranked as Severity Scale 3-5.
UNICEF is committed to Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), and it considers the input of beneficiaries in all phases of the project cycle and makes deliberate efforts to incorporate their ideas, concerns, and feedback throughout. Practically this is done by continuous engagements throughout the project-life cycle to affected populations and their representatives. This is done to ensure that needs of various groups including women, men, girls and boys are known and appropriate response is designed. In addition, UNICEF adheres to the ‘Do No Harm’ principle for the design and implementation of projects.
For the project implementation phase, UNICEF’s accountability strategy is based on five pillars: transparency, community engagement, learning, and evaluation and feedback mechanisms.
GAM is also included in the project.
UNICEF’s package for supporting learning activities has several core elements which have been selected based on their impact on children’s participation, retention and learning-namely:
1- Back to Learning Campaign (BTL
2- Safe, inclusive learning environment
3- Trained, Capable, motivated teachers
4- Availability of learning supplies:
5- Assessment of (learning) outcomes
6- Recreational activities
7- Distance Education Modalities
8- Supporting participation in National School Exams of Grade 9 & 12 children from cross-border and crossline locations.
All the above activities will be inclusive ensuring the integration of children with disabilities through:
• Light rehabilitation and provision of furniture to the learning centres/schools to ensure access of CwD
• Provision of specialized learning materials that support the specific need of different type of disabilities
• Capacity development of teachers and educators to enhance their skills and equip them with the needed competencies to work with CwD
• Awareness session for parents, care givers, and community members on the importance of education
• Support diagnosing services for early intervention
• Provision of educational sessions for CwD
• M&E to ensure monitoring reporting referral mechanisms

4 - Needs

According to WoS 2021 Education Sector Findings, 94% of school-aged children are living in areas with substandard education conditions - an increase to last year’s 86%. Due to population movements, areas with high rates of IDPs experience greater disparity in access to education. There is an overall lack of learning facilities and educational interventions in ‘last resort sites’ and over-burdened communities. Younger children, adolescents; overaged children, girls and children with disabilities face further challenges accessing learning services due to limited availability of to ECE centers, secondary education services and vocational training opportunities, or education facilities that can provide inclusive education for the estimated 15% of school aged children with disabilities. A significant number of boys and girls may be experiencing psychosocial distress, with higher rates found in children not attending school. . Parents are concerned over lack of quality in school, and school violence. The threats that students face attending schools that are unsafe are exacerbated by COVID-19. It is difficult to ensure physical distancing or ventilation in overcrowded schools, together with a limited availability of operational WASH facilities and hygiene measures/practices. Key reasons children were not going to school include schools not being available; unaffordability of transportation, stationary or learning materials; and children expected to be working to support the family household, to get married. Lack of teachers incentives in NWS has resulted in teachers’ absenteeism that cause a critical gap in the protective learning environment that should be provided for children. . 5 - Implementation of activities

In line with Sector Objectives 1 (Availability & Access), 2 (Quality) and 3 (Resilient Systems), UNICEF, will provide technical and supply assistance directly, and through stakeholders (government, implementing partners and contractors). 5 key strategies will be utilized to implement the project: 1) Improve educational services: technical and financial assistance will be provided for integrated-school services to guarantee access to inclusive learning spaces sensitive to gender and CwD, functional gender disaggregated water and sanitation facilities, and the provisioning of adequate furniture. An inter-sectoral comprehensive school approach will foster collaborating with WASH, CP, Social Policy and ADAP. 2) Teachers’ capacities strengthening: Teacher Resource Centers will provide in-service school-based teacher support in the management of multi-age classrooms and the delivery of lifeskills and psycho-social support (PSS). 3) Distance education: To allow children to remain engaged and to grant them access to education when schools are closed or when the OOSC cannot go to school due to the humanitarian situation, such as sporadic conflict or the recent COVID-19 pandemic, UNICEF and partners will continue to support the SLP for delivery through a distance modality. 4) Community mobilization via BTL campaigns: to create demand for education services and sensitize caregivers about the importance of education, door-to-door campaign will be organized. 5) Monitoring, Evaluation and Reporting: UNICEF has a concrete data collection systems the School Integrated Information Management Systems (SIMIS) and the Child Level Monitoring Systems for NFE (CMS). CMS is a child tracking tool to register the beneficiaries details.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Programme related risks: • Hazards: sexual abuse/exploitation and insufficient protection of staff • Mitigation measures: i) promotion of PSEA policies, including training to staff and contractors; ii) promotion of inclusive practices in schools; iii) school policies on abuse/exploitation and insufficient protection of staff; Covid19 Code of Conduct Community related risks, insufficient support including undue influence from local authorities: • Hazards: i) communities/authorities who prevent access to school/curriculum; and ii) checkpoints/barriers which delay delivery of services• Mitigation measures: i) community engagement measures; and ii) protocols with authorities on rights of children. Institutional, reputational risks which results in delay/denial of critical lifesaving services: • Hazards: i) poorly planned projects, low compliance to standards, conditionalities; ii) heavy procedures and approval protocols and (iii) Delays in implementation due to limited access by UNICEF partners. • Mitigation measures: i) follow-up on risk informed planning guidelines; ii) approval agreements.Safety and violence against education facilities and providers:• Hazards: Schools’ attacks and occupation (in the framework of the Resolution 1612)• Mitigation measures: advocacy with parties involved about the neutrality of the schools and of the educational staff

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors against the PSEA principles. The following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken: - UNICEF will adopt the Humanitarian Code of Conduct for Syria cross-border response (2020) and all staff (including contractors and volunteers will sign the CoC- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive a refresh on PSEA training (mandatory for UNICEF staff since 2019)- UNICEF will update internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff are aware of them- Female staff will always be present during distributions at the respective sites- Beneficiaries will be given clear information on distribution (date and content), as well as on complain mechanisms- Beneficiaries will be given counseling/information that aid is free- Contractors and their workers will not have direct contact with beneficiaries- PSEA messaging and awareness raising will be done with beneficiaries through banners, posters and messages

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Safety and violence against education facilities and providers: Hazards: i) arising from UXOs and unstable infrastructures; ii) violence in school, including school attacks and occupation; iii) school health risks arising from COVID19; iv) lack of structures for duly integrating children with disability; v) weather/environmental related risks especially during winter. Mitigation measures: i) learning facilities have standards including safety protocols; ii) school designs consider safety protocols; training provided on UXOs, ERWs with UNMAS; comprehensive measures (training, institutional change, C4D campaigns for mitigating violence in school; psychosocial support activities; promotion of PSEA; implementation of school health protocols regarding COVID19• Further spread of the pandemic results in new school lockdowns both formal and NFEUNICEF will continue to support MoE with the operationalization of the school health protocol (training of education personnel, C4D); distribution of learning materials that can be taken home; development and piloting of new distance learning modalities.• Economic distress and rising rates of poverty will prevent families to allow their children to attend schools or temporary learning centres. Risk of child labour, early marriage, and exclusion of children with disability will be direct consequences.UNICEF and partners will conduct focused and repetitive BTL campaigns in target areas, while also piloting new distance education modalities where education services are being brought to students’ homes rather than requiring students access education services in learning spaces only.• Economic distress and rising rates of poverty exacerbated by rising commodity prices such as fuel/bread)
