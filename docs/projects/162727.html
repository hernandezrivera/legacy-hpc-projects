Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the reduction of morbidity associated with water-borne diseases and preventing livelihood depletion of vulnerable communities through responding to immediate livelihood, water and sanitation and hygiene needs of the target population in Abyei-Muglad locality (outside the Abyei PCA Area), El Nehoud and Wadbanda localities of West Kordofan State. According to the latest IPC analysis, the localities targeted by this project are affected by high levels of food and nutrition insecurity (PHASE 3 and 4). A combination of shocks and stresses have affected the targeted communities including tribal conflict, displacement, loss of productive assets, climate change, degradation of natural resources and lack of basic infrastructure and services. More recently those communities were also affected by the deterioration of the macroeconomic situation which negatively affected their purchasing power further exacerbating their food and nutrition insecurity. This project will target 86,615 direct beneficiaries comprising of (IDPs, returnees and destitute host communities) from Abyei-Muglad locality (outside the Abyei PCA Area), El Nehoud and Wadbanda localities of West Kordofan State. The main objective of the project is to assist vulnerable populations in achieving their own food and nutrition security and improve their livelihoods and general wellbeing through integrating FSL and WASH interventions. Under food security and livelihoods this project will provide Livestock assets protection services including vaccination and voucher-based animal treatment services provided through trained and equipped CAHWs to restore livelihoods and assets of targeted communities. Improving access to food sources and food availability will be provided by increasing access to agricultural tools and improved crop and vegetables seeds (drought-resistant), followed by training on best agronomy practices and post-harvest conservation. These inputs will be utilized in household vegetable gardens, and on staple and cash crop production. This activity enables families at risk or suffering from malnutrition to increase their food intake and their nutritional diversity. Women groups will be formed and supported with training and livelihood start-up kits including goats restocking, milk processing and cheese-making. The project will also support rangeland rehabilitation through community participation and cash for work program. Other vulnerable HHs who cannot participate in the labor work will be reached with the unconditional cash transfer. During VCO needs assessment, it emerged that firewood is the main source of fuel for HHs in the targeted localities. The household dependency on firewood collection leads to deforestation and increase the protection risks for women and girls, therefore VCO proposes training of women on the production of Fuel-Efficient Stoves (FES)/FAO mud stove modality, and this will enable target beneficiaries to switch to the environmentally friendly FES.
Under WASH, the project will establish mini water yards, rehabilitate boreholes and malfunctioning hand pumps; it will also provide sanitation and hygiene services to the communities such as the construction of latrines in schools and conduct of sanitation and hygiene campaigns.

1 - Needs

The needs of the targeted populations were assessed utilizing both primary and secondary data derived from the following sources: 1. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), June-August 2019. 2. The post-harvest assessment carried out by WKS Ministry of agriculture (during October to November 2019) in which VCO is participating, covering Abyei-Muglad locality (outside the Abyei PCA Area), El Nehoud, and Wadbanda localities. 3. VCO own needs assessments carried out during the period of Oct to Nov 2019 in the proposed three targeted localities with participation of WKS Ministry of Agriculture and WKS HAC Office. In this participatory process an estimated 211 individuals, including 110 women, were consulted using a mix of assessment tools and methodologies including household interviews; FGDs, community meetings; key informant interviews; meetings with locality officials; as well as field monitoring visits and direct observations. The main assessment findings concluded the following: humanitarian situation particularly food and nutrition security has worsened due to soaring of food- and also other commodities- prices which affect poor households food consumption and dietary diversity. According to recent IPC analysis Muglad (outside the Abyei PCA Area), El Nehoud, and Wadbanda localities are among affected localities in phase 3 or worse. Water supply infrastructure is inadequate especially, people are forced to use unsafe sources, several water points have been identified as being malfunctioning and in need of rehabilitation /maintenance. Open defecation is practiced and other indicators of poor hygiene and sanitation knowledge were observed in the assessed areas. Furthermore, the assessments demonstrated persisting increase of staple food prices due to low production and a high cost of transportation; this is expected to reduce poor households’ access to food from the markets. Furthermore collection of firewood in the targeted areas contribute a considerable amount to the deforestation and loss of vegetation, most of the households gathering firewood have to currently walk around 12 Km per trip. Firewood collection mostly falls under the responsibility of women exposing them to the risk of physical harassment. To address the needs of the affected populations in the three targeted localities there are needs of an immediate agricultural support services, vaccination and treatment services, support with income generating activities and natural resource management with specific interventions to protect livestock assets through support to community-based animal treatment programs, expand animal vaccination to avoid catastrophic disease outbreaks, awareness raising of farmers and agro-pastoralists to change poor agricultural practices, support extension services to improve crop and animal production performances, build resilience and sustain livelihood systems. Water supply infrastructure is inadequate especially in Muglad (outside the Abyei PCA Area) and Wadbanda Localities, people are forced to use unsafe sources, several water points have been identified as being malfunctioning and in need of rehabilitation /maintenance. Open defecation is practiced and other indicators of poor hygiene and sanitation knowledge were observed in the assessed areas. During these assessments the particular focus was given to the active participation and feedback from women members; female-headed households; pregnant and lactating women; families with under five children and orphans and elderly people, ethnic minorities and vulnerable groups of these targeted communities. This, in particular, enabled VCO, in collaboration with targeted beneficiaries, to analyze the different needs of women/girls, men/boys, ethnic and diverse community groups, elderly and people with special needs, so that we are able to integrate these needs into the planned activities in an efficient and effective manner.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Based on the needs identified and prioritized by the target communities, VCO under this project proposes the following activities: 1. Support government-led strategic livestock vaccination campaigns for 180,000 heads of animals, belonging to 7,030 HHs, against prioritized livestock diseases, Solid Waste Management campaigns will be insured to mitigate the environment. 2. Provision of voucher-based animal health (treatment) services for 1,010 vulnerable HHs, through equipped and trained CAHWs on the Voucher Scheme. 3. Refresher training & provision of vaccination & treatment kits for 30 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) to engage in the provision of voucher-based animal health service delivery, the training will take place before the start of the vaccination campaigns and voucher-based animal treatment system is initiated. 4. In order to improve the nutritional status and income of 1,500 targeted households, the project will distribute crops seeds packages to 1200 HHs and vegetable seeds to 300 women-headed households for planting during the rainy season and home gardening, distribute agriculture hand tool sets to seeds recipients (each set will consist of: wedding and planting hoe and a rake), targeted beneficiaries will be supported with training on good agricultural practices and marketing skills. 5. Carry out 3 trainings to strengthen community extension network for 60 extension workers to fellow farmers in a particular geographic areas. 6. Carry out three pieces of training for Farmer Field Schools for 90 participants in horticulture to assist farmers to improve production of high quality and nutritious vegetables. 7. Installment of solar systems at 6 boreholes to make water available for home vegetable gardening to support vegetable production by targeted women groups and other indirect beneficiaries. 8. Provision of donkeys and donkey carts to 50 HHs to enable farmers to transport their produce from the fields to storage places and from there to the markets. 9. Training and support of 300 women on production of Fuel-Efficient Stoves (FES)/FAO mud stove modality. 10. Provision of pregnant and or lactating goats for 330 destitute and vulnerable HHs along with supplementary feed for a period of two months, to improve the nutritional status of children from vulnerable households in addition to household asset building and possibly income generation; this activity will be coordinated with the Ministry of Animal Resources to provide veterinary checkups and perform necessary tests. 11. Establishment and training of two women Income Generating Groups (50 HHs) in small milk processing and cheese making activities. 12. Rangeland rehabilitation through cash for work (130 HHs per locality a total of 390 cash for work participating HHs) - conditional cash transfer: This activity involves paying individuals cash in exchange for non-skilled work on rehabilitation of communal resources of pastoral and agro-pastoral communities (rangelands). 13. Unconditional cash transfer for households unable to participate in cash for work activity. 120 HHs per locality (360 HHs): the project will provide cash to poor and vulnerable households or individuals who are unable to participate in cash for work activities for various reasons (i.e. disabled, pregnant women, elders) without conditions. 14. Establishment of 2 solar-operated mini water yards: the project will coordinate with WES and community representatives to determine fair locations and which existing water points have a proper yield to support the establishment of the 2 MWYs. 15. Rehabilitation of 30 water sources/points). 16. Recruit and training of water maintenance committees (WMCs) 50% will be women. 17. Construction of 6 latrines at schools: Building conveniently located latrines in areas safe for girls/women, environmentally sound, and in areas that waste will do no harm to water runoff, this activity will be supported with sanitation and hygiene promotion training.

5 - Environment Marker

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The situation on the ground in targeted areas is progressively deteriorating as climate change continues to affect the food security and livelihood situation; communities in the proposed project targeted are highly dependent on fuel wood for energy, climate change may threaten the availability of fuel due to the expansion of agricultural lands into forests (deforestation) and cutting of trees to sell as a source of income; this combined with growing socioeconomic pressures, climate variability and climate change are likely to intensify the desertification in the proposed targeted localities. Additionally Water supply infrastructures were inadequate especially, people are forced to use unsafe sources, several water points have been identified as being malfunctioning and in need of rehabilitation /maintenance. Open defecation is practiced and other indicators of poor hygiene and sanitation knowledge were observed in the assessed areas. Furthermore the restriction of movement of livestock have led to concentration of livestock in the already fragile grazing areas within the targeted localities leading to overgrazing, competition and frictions over natural resources, spread of livestock diseases and losses, high food prices, poor livestock productivity and production and poor market opportunities have left the communities extremely vulnerable to any future shocks. Rain-fed agriculture, agropastoralism with farming and livestock rearing are the main livelihoods means and the main sources of food are the products generated from these activities supplemented by purchasing additional food at local markets. Women are most affected as they shouldered the majority of family management; this situation is clearly observed in targeted localities. VCO proposes an intervention aimed at strengthening community resilience, mitigating environmental degradation and improving food security and WASH status. This will be achieved through integrating FSL and WASH interventions.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During the need assessments the particular focus was given to the active participation and feedback from women members; female-headed households; pregnant and lactating women; families with under five children and orphans and elderly people, ethnic minorities and vulnerable groups of these targeted communities. This, in particular facilitate collaboration with targeted beneficiaries, to analyze the different needs of women/girls, men/boys, ethnic and diverse community groups, elderly and people with special needs which resulted into the planned activities in an efficient and effective manner.Do no harm” principle, conflict sensitive approach and other relevant protection mechanisms have been effectively incorporated in this project. For instance, to deliver services in a none discriminatory manner for all those who are in need, and to ensure any tensions between host communities, returnees and IDPs over access to services is avoided. The bulk of the project activities are planned to be delivered in the dry season, before the critical farming season to ensure communities are not distracted from the core farming activities with potential to harm their productive livelihood engagements. Household heads providing care to vulnerable children will be prioritized for the selection of other vulnerable children. The planned interventions preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, their dignity, and were culturally appropriate and minimize any harmful and unintended negative consequences and they will be provided according to needs and not on the basis of age, sex, gender identity, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance, etc. Affected individuals and communities will play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions that affect them, where effective and easily accessible mechanisms for suggestions and complaints will be put in place and in so doing increasing accountability. The project will insure fostering inclusive participation to decision-making processes, support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empower themselves.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to affected population is a key priority for VCO. In community dialogues on project planning, implementation and monitoring, men, women, girls, boys, the elderly and the disabled will be invited by representation to attend and participate by right, on merit and by affirmative action if necessary to express their opinions and have their voices heard on their needs, implementation process and outcomes of the interventions. The Child Protection Officer contact details (telephone numbers and email address) will be publicly shared to ensure any concerns from the beneficiaries can be channeled for review and consideration. Information received will be treated confidentially to protect identity of the informants. VCO field teams will conduct weekly project management meetings to review and consider any issues emerging that may impact on the project implementation. Minutes of these meetings will be shared weekly by the program management at the HQ office for review and guidance on how to address concerns. For activities involving distribution of inputs, serialized tokens will be designed, printed and issued with full details of the specific beneficiaries during registration process. Register of beneficiaries will be compiled according to beneficiary token serial numbers and the beneficiaries will appear in person and sign list of beneficiaries when receiving inputs to enhance accountability. Distributions of inputs will be conducted in presence of beneficiary trustees such as community leaders and representatives and facilitated by representatives of specific beneficiary groups. Beneficiary distribution lists will be verified and endorsed by beneficiary representatives and filed for records / references. Men, women, boys and girls will be consulted jointly and separately during project review meetings and post distribution monitoring exercises. Men, women, boys and girls are provided with information on how to lodge complains / feedback mechanism. VCO will insure providing accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate continues dialogue process. VCO will actively seek the views of affected populations ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from), this include mechanisms to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse from happening from the outset by defining the conduct expected from VCO staff and collaborators. VCO will design, monitor and evaluate the goals and objectives of project with the involvement of affected populations, feeding learning back on an ongoing basis and reporting on the results of the process.
