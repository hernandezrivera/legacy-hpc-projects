Project Summary

This project targets directly a total of 9,700 beneficiaries broken down as follows (4,000 women, 3,200 girls, 1,500 men and 1,000 boys) in the three counties of Leer, Mayendit and Koch. Coalition for Humanity (CH) will provide assistance to women and girls affected by sexual and gender based violence in the target areas through conducting of a rapid diagnostic assessment of the GBV context to inform an appropriate response. We anticipate this response to include development of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS), provision of GBV case management and psychosocial services to affected persons. Coalition for Humanity (CH) will also provide safe refers for cases for several reasons including WASH provision, livelihood support among others.
In collaboration with our Conflict transformation project by GIZ, CH will enhance participation of women and girls in peace-building and peaceful co-existence through capacity building of local women's groups/women's organisations and women leaders on mediation and peaceful resolution of conflicts. This hopefully will contribute towards long term prevention of conditions that give rise to increased GBV in the target area.
In order to ensure that long term solutions towards combating GBV are achieved, CH will work with government and partners to strengthen implementation of institutional policies, standards, and practices that drive action and accountability on GBV and gender equality in humanitarian service delivery as linked to our commitment to Call to Action on GBV in emergency. This is besides identifying opportunities within the local culture to reduce or if possible altogether eliminate local drivers of gender based violence.

Needs

The conflict in South Sudan has had negative impacts on the entire population with particular adverse impact experienced by women and children. Since the beginning of the current conflict in 2013, millions have been displaced internally with hundreds of thousands (majority of them women and children) crossing the border as refugees. The counties of leer, Mayendit and Koch of former Unity State, have been the epicenters of this conflict. On several occasions, different locations in the central unity especially in Leer, Mayendit and Koch have changed control between government and the opposition forces causing huge displacement and suffering to vulnerable communities especially women, girls and children. The GBV situation in the central Unity counties of Leer, Mayendit and Koch remains dire with high level of rapes, sexual assaults and domestic cultural practices biased against women. Women and girls continue to fall victim at the hands of the warring parties and allied militias. This project will focus in hard to reach areas with priority to payams north of Leer, Mayendit North and South of Koch not previously serviced by other partners. These locations were the epicentres of conflict and accessibility by humanitarian actors has been limited. According to Reach situation report in June 2018, 90% of the populations in leer, Mayendit and Koch have experienced violence, with women and girls being the most affected. The IPC report released in September 2018 classified these target counties in catastrophe phase 5 or emergency phase 4. Lack of food and accessibility to services push women and girls to more dangerous levels, as they become victims in the course of duty to provide for families. The conflict has rendered the legal and judicial system defunct for everyone, causing women and girls to lack an avenue for legal redress in response to the multiple gender based violations. Medical and psychosocial support services have further diminished as the war rages with no support mechanisms for survivors of sexual violence. In 2018, reported incidences of rapes, girl child labour, forced abductions; sexual exploitation, early marriages and bad cultural practices were reported and well documented. According to a GIZ funded Conflict Transformation Project implemented by Coalition for Humanity (CH) in greater Leer, women and girls were particularly affected as they risk their lives everyday going to look for food and firewood. Those who managed to escape from their captives do not speak out or seek help due to fear of stigmatization and lack of enabling environment like supportive services locally. In May and September 2018 Coalition for humanity (CH) in partnership with CARE and DRC-DDG conducted series of assessments in Adok payam of Leer County reaching 19 villages. Among locations reached included Meer and six other islands. Similarly another assessment was conducted in Mayendit County (North) in early October to identify the needs.Coalition for Humanity has worked during the entire year 2018 with IPMT led by CARE reaching to more vulnerable people. These numbers are expected to increase significantly this 2019 with the window of opportunity offered by the recently signed peace (R-ARCISS). This project shall therefore prioritize provision of specialized and integrated services to vulnerable women, girls, men and boys in the selected counties, Payams, Bomas and villages. The project shall integrate and mainstream other programmes such as WASH, ES/NFIs and food security for an holistic outcomes.

Conduct campaign and community mobilization to promote life saving assistance in order to address key GBV needs of the vulnerable women and girls.b. Establishing WGFS in strategic locations where we have a presence in three counties (Leer, Mayendit and Koch) of former Unity State.c. Provide GBV case management services, PSS and create an awareness in those WGFS centersd. Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience a. Strengthen the implementation of institutional policies, standards, and practices that drive action and accountability on GBV and gender equality in humanitarian service delivery as linked to our commitment to Call to Action on GBV in emergency.b. Incorporating community’s cultural norms to eliminate issues fuelling GBV issues.c. Engage women and girls in livelihood opportunities including briquettes making and other livelihoods projects.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Project activities shall be undertaken in consideration of safety of beneficiaries especially the distant taken to reach the service centre such as WGFS. Project activities involving beneficiaries shall be carried during the day giving women and girls ample time to walk back home before dark. Project activities within the designated centres such as WGFS shall be conducted in consideration of prevailing security situation and where possible, local community leaders involved in provision of security especially during distribution days (dignity kits and other PSS materials)

Yes
