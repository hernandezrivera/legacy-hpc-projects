Project Summary

Population in host communities continue to grapple with serious nutrition situation, routine monitoring of the prevalence of acute malnutrition though the nutrition and food security surveillance Findings indicate that Borno State has a GAM rate of 9.1 per cent and a SAM rate of 0.8 percent – SMART Survey, August, 2018.
In the 12 months period GREENCODE will support the capacity strengthen of community structures to support acute malnutrition treatment by organizing community level training for community volunteers, mass MUAC screening, procurement/distribution of locally formulated nutrition supplements, conduct counselling and referral to other INGO’s OTPs/stabilization centers, set-up mother-to-mother support groups, training of community nutrition mobilisers, Hygiene/Sanitation, training of lead mothers, cooking demonstration, training of religious leaders, training of religious leaders on the involvement of IYCF/CMAM. Under this project GREENCODE will be reaching and improving nutrition for 3,000 under 5 children and 300 adults. In addition, adult beneficiaries will receive IYCF¬E interventions through IYCF messages, trainings and mother to mother group sessions and hygiene education to help prevent diseases and acute malnutrition amongst children 0 to 23 months. BSFP will also be implemented in both locations for children 6 to 59 months to help prevent children who are malnourished from deteriorating further and also prevent the others from acute malnutrition, especially those who are malnourished as a result of inadequate dietary intake. The community outreach component, which entails the recruitment of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs), will be implemented on a large scale within the communities in order to identify children with acute malnutrition on time and refer them to the nutrition OTP and stabilization centres. The CNVs will be trained on identification of acute malnutrition within the community through use of MUAC tapes and referral forms. While religious leaders and other key stakeholders will be train for them to understand and engage in the involvement in IYCF/CMAM.
In addition, active case finding in which CNVs will work closely with the community mobilisers in the centres. They will also conduct house-to-house (home) follow up for those in the project to include defaulter tracing and gather information on reasons for absenteeism and defaulting as well as education on key hygiene messages to improve linkages between WASH and nutrition for better health outcomes The project strategy is to provide integrated nutrition interventions for malnourished children and women in both LGA of Borno state.

Needs

Population arriving from the inaccessible areas have particularity high prevalence of acute malnutrition, a SMART survey conducted in Bama in August 2018 indicated a very high prevalence of GAM among the new arrival compared to those that have been in the camp GAM by WHZ – 37.5% (reception site) while 7.8% (rest of camp) . Similar situation is observed in all the 9 reception sites in Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Kalabarge, Kukawa, Mobbar and Ngala, through the screening of new arrivals. Population in host communities continue to grapple with serious nutrition situation, routine monitoring of the prevalence of acute malnutrition though the nutrition and food security surveillance Findings indicate that Borno State has a GAM rate of 9.1 per cent and a SAM rate of 0.8 percent – SMART Survey, August, 2018.Malnutrition is still a public health concern in the North-East Nigeria and a key priority for emergency response. Acute malnutrition is among the leading causes of death weather directly or indirectly for children under the age of five. It is estimated that 1,094,467 children aged 6-59 across the BAY states are undernourished with 367,526 severely malnourished (SAM) while 726,941 with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). There has been a reduction in the prevalence of SAM cases from 439,520 in 2018 to 367,076 in 2019 following sustained scale up of services the same is not the case for MAM where cases increased from 502,536 in 2018 to 723,326 in 2019 . The nutrition situation has further been exacerbated by protection concerns related to new displacement following on going military operations as well as outbreak of diseases such as cholera where 18 LGAS were affected by cholera outbreak in 2018 with 4,250 cases reported .The food security situation also continues to impact on undernutrition, access to food and maintenance of adequate nutrition status are critical determinants of people’s survivals in emergencies, addressing the nutrition needs of infants and young children must be a priority, given their vulnerability. Prevention of undernutrition is just as important as treating acute malnutrition . The first 1000 days, age from conception until two years is particularly important in preventing undernutrition, emphasis must be placed on ensuring appropriate nutritional support to pregnant women and children under 2 years. With 745,271 number estimated to benefit from intervention promoting optimal infant feeding in emergencies and 1,005,804 for VITA supplementation. These interventions are critical to mitigate the long-term impact that nutrition can have on health, cognitive development and physical growth and its long-term impacts such as reduced capacity to learn, increased risk of diseases and irreversible damage to brain. Micronutrient deficiencies are worsened by the disaster when they have already been prevalent in the population, prevence of micro nutrient deficiencies are highest in the North- East stunting is at 52.4 meaning one in very two children below 5 years are shorter for their age. This reflects chronic malnutrition culminating from failure to receive proper nutrition over a prolonged period, these deficiencies must be tackled using population-wide interventions and individual treatment. In the long term, extended micronutrient deficiencies impact on child development, brain development, learning capacities . Vitamin A deficiency can mean a child will be growing up with lower immunity, which can trigger frequent health problems and poor growth.
