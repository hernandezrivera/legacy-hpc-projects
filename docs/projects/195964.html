Project Summary

This project will improve the health service delivery to the vulnerable and affected populations in Kapoeta East, kapoeta South, Ibba and Bor South Counties.
Internal conflict, political instability and natural disasters for instance cycles of severe floods have significantly affected health services delivery and caused massive displacements of communities in and out of South Sudan. During the 2013 through 2016 political instability, health facilities were vandalized and basic essential medical supplies destroyed. In 2022, the country is still recovering from the shocks of the conflict and slowly building resilience towards full recovery.
The journey continue to worsen owing to more challenging and unpredictable situations like the COVID 19 pandemic, other disease outbreaks like measles and the threat of Ebola from the neighboring Uganda.
COVID 19 pandemic specifically hit the health systems hard and other natural disasters like flooding have impacted negatively on the health service delivery exposing populations to serious health risks of contracting communicable diseases and other vector borne diseases like malaria and khala-azar. The threat of increased mortality from COVID 19 disease remains a reality and uptake to the immunization services against the disease remains very low.
Ibba county is also experiencing challenges on health care system and its proximity to the boarder with Uganda exposes the populations to the threat of EVD.
The unavailability of immunization services affected negatively on the vaccine preventable diseases and thus outbreaks of diseases like measles continues to be a big challenge in the country.
Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) will therefore improve the health status of the nomadic communities of Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South County by provision of mobile health services to the vulnerable and affected populations. This will also improve the health status of the nomadic populations as the health services will go to them thus reducing the burden of walking long distances in search of health services. The organization will also support health services delivery in the other two counties of Bor South and Ibba
The mobile health services will ensure that there are out patient consultation to manage common illnesses like malaria, AWD, RTIs and skin infections wound management and provision of other key health services focused towards improving the maternal and child mortality rates . Immunization services will be offered to the communities to ensure that they are not susceptible to vaccine preventable infections. This will include sensitization of the communities on the importance of COVID 19 vaccination as a key way of prevention of the COVID 19 pandemic.
CRSF will offer reproductive health services and health needs of the communities will be fronted thus improving ANC utilization, folic acid supplementation, skilled deliveries in the health facilities, post natal care services, family planning services. The project will offer SGBV services and mental health and psychosocial support to the vulnerable and affected communities creating referral pathways to ensure that the victims of SGBV access swift services.
Immunization services will be offered to the children under five years to ensure that children improve hard immunity reducing vaccine preventable infections in the community. Children will be given vitamin A supplementation and also management of acute watery diarrhea with ORS and Zinc.
There will be screening for acute malnutrition and referrals for children under five years and PLWs in the communities and ensuring that they are able to get nutrition service
The project will target 6,000 vulnerable and affected populations with live saving mobile health interventions. The project will have 2,945 male and 3,055 female with 1,155 children under five (566) boys and 589 girls) and 400 people with disabilities. CRSF has operation presence with ongoing interventions in all 4 counties.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan IPC phase classification analysis for October 2022, placed Kapoeta East County in emergency IPC phase 4, caused by cumulative effects of local conflict, insecurity, poverty and lack of public services. These limited movement of food commodities and disrupting livelihood activities. 10,000 People in Kapoeta South, 34,000 people in Bor South and 3000 People in Ibba County are classified in IPC phase 4 (IPC analysis November 2022) and the situation is expected to worsen at the peak of lean season projected period from April to July 2023 (IPC Nov 2022). Food insecurity, poor dietary diversity, high morbidity and sub optimal IYCF practices continue driving acute malnutrition. Only 38% of the population has access to safe water and only 19% of households access latrines in South Sudan and access to latrines was 10% . Further, the health systems in South Sudan is still unable to unable to respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide essential life-saving health services to vulnerable populations because it continues to heavily rely on international humanitarian assistance. There are re-current issues for instance; Limited health facility functionality, limited human resources for health capacity, and poor coverage including recent closure of health facilities due to decreasing funding for South Sudan, and thus unable to meet health needs of the local and vulnerable population. This has been exacerbated by cycles of floods every year since 2019 to date, frequent sub national violence and conflicts as well as acute food insecurity(HNO, 2022). The health statistics for the specific target counties are not known but at national level, child mortality is 99/ 1000 live births, neonatal mortality is 40/1000 and maternal mortality 789/100,000 live births. This can be attributed to low functionality of health facilities and low utilization of health services in the counties.Malaria is endemic in in the 4 counties. Cholera was an annual occurrence in the greater Kapoeta until an oral cholera vaccination campaign in 2017.There was a measles outbreak in the county in 2020 a subsequent campaign will mitigate future outbreaks but routine immunization coverage remains low at only 45% mostly affecting children. Acute watery diarrhea and acute respiratory tract infections are key causes of morbidity in the Bor south County (IDSR Report October 2022).COVID 19 pandemic and the risk of Ebola from Uganda remains very high this being in greater Kapoeta counties and Ibba due to the proximity with Uganda and the Bor South County is having a major risk of increased waterborne diseases due to the flooding in the area.Access to health services by the nomadic communities in Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East is limited due to the nature of the communities moving from one place to another in search of pasture and water. This therefore calls for concerted efforts to reach the communities with health facilities in the places where they move through mobile health clinics and other outreach services. Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) is an active health cluster member and is proposing to respond to the health concerns of the affected counties. CRSF has presence in all 4 counties The KAP Survey highlighted critical protection risks, including negative coping mechanisms. Community members reported female genital mutilation (FGM), abduction including recruitment to armed groups, unsafe migration to Kenya, medical conditions that lead to family separation, abandonment and neglect by parents and caregivers, children in conflict and contact with the law, psychosocial distress and mental health conditions as the main risks to children’s well-being, high incidences of SGBV, lack of access to school, drug abuse among adolescents, forced and early marriage, child labor, physical abuse and corporal punishment, and lack of basic needs in the Kapoeta Counties and other key protection risks like early marriages and forced marriages in Bor South County.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Since CRSF will establish mobile clinics, the mobile clinic team will work with the community health workers and mobilizers to raise alerts, conduct surveillance, conduct screening and consultations during the weekly visits, follow-upon patients and coordinate referrals for complicated cases. Boma health workers will be recruited from the community, including female and male, to ensure equal gender participation and representation. To assess the quality of health care services especially quality of the mobile clinic services, CRSF will involve beneficiaries in randomized trials and surveys or community mobilization meetings. Local community chiefs will be encouraged to involve the community to empower and protect women children and girls who have need or received assistance. People living with disabilities, elderly and critically ill will be identified through the community leaders, PMC and female FGDs, and health workers will schedule home visits. Their concerns and needs will be included in the assistance, location etc. Those selected for the assistance but unable to come to distribution points will be receiving their assistance at home. CRSF will ensure that beneficiaries will also be involved in the internal end-evaluation. Their opinion about the health response project will be obtained through FGDs and KIIs and project help desk established at each mobile clinic. Findings of the evaluation will also be shared with the community at the end of the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the implementation of this project, CRSF will target vulnerable female headed HH, PLW and acutely malnourished children under the age of 5, and people living with disabilities as assessment subject to show they are disproportionately affected by the different crises in Bor South, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta east. Targets have been established to ensure their inclusion. Furthermore, CRSF will promote women and girls’ participation, to achieve this, CRSF will implement the health care activities in a gender and culturally sensitive way, informed by the long standing involvement in the area and the knowledge and experience That CRSF has with gender relations in the communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) has a PSEA policy in place. CRSF will use its PSEA policy to manage and protect both staff and beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff will be trained on the policy and they will be required to sign the policy to adhere to CRSF policy guideline. Safe and accessible community-based reporting mechanisms will be established guided by CRSF’s organogram, and the investigation channels will be discussed with the community including how to obtain assistance and the possible disciplinary measures will also be explained. The project will adapt PSEA Network information campaigns with linked information, education and communication (IEC) materials. The information campaigns will target county health departments, partners and project staff and the community members. All IEC materials, as a part of the campaign, will be translated into the local language and be distributed widely.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) will engage and involve beneficiaries at different levels of action; from the design phase until end project at evaluation stage. CRSF will engage beneficiaries during assessment where they will be part of the information and data generated as well as taking actions based on the decisions from the collected information. Beneficiaries including women and young girls and boys will form teams involved in project household survey, key informant groups and Focus Group discussion, to identify their priority needs, gaps, and any concerns, preferred mode of assistance and their expectations from the project as well as modes of communication. CRSF has incorporated the findings in its action plan. CRSF will involve the targeted community’s members in the selection criteria and modalities of implementation of the project through their participation in the Project Management Committee (PMC), including women and youth representatives. CRSF will further engage the prioritized beneficiaries in feedback by actively asking feedback information prior to and during implementation of the proposed health care interventions, for instance during their visit to the mobile clinic; for this purpose, information on the feedback and complaint mechanism will be put on banners, and there will be a face-to-face complaint desk. The integrated MYICN and CMAM guidelines provides avenues for working with women and men in the communities to identify, treat and prevent acute cases of malnutrition.This increases community ownership of CRSF health response, CRSF will engage local leaders, women groups and youth groups to increases their participation in case surveillance, so as to prevent eventual outbreaks. Community involvement will be ensured through the community health workers, Boma health workers and mobilizers, and community nutrition volunteers per village/site who will support screening activities, looking for defaulters and making home visits for children who were not gaining weight. They will also be involved in supporting the health mobilizers and nurses in their sensitization activities around basic household-level practices.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Through our programming, CRSF ensures protection mainstreaming in all its intervention, this is aimed at ensuring that negative side effects and conflicts from the intervention are prevented. Therefore, CRSF will ensure that host families will be part of the targeted population of interest for this health care response, and the protection-related issues will be taken into consideration for instance security risks for specific groups; safety, access, dignity issues to be considered in all designs). Potential harmful outcomes for women (increased workload, reduced time for other livelihood opportunities) and children (increased child labor) will be carefully monitored.CRSF anticipates that active participation and involvement of the community’s members and beneficiaries, open dialogues in the planning and roll out of the activities will be very important, to ensure that activities are integrated in the daily workload of the targeted families. In the greater Kapoeta and Bor South in EES and Jonglei States, negative effects of climate change are observed to increase the risk of drought in Kapoeta South and East and floods especially in Bor south. Therefore, CRSF will as advocate to protection of climate sensitive interventions to reduce practices that increase climate change. Transportation of medical supplies and people will be planned carefully, and number of supervisory trips from Juba-based staff to the field will be limited as much as possible. Also, the procurement and logistical processes will focus on using only the absolute necessary packaging material.
