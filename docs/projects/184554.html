Project Summary

Years of armed conflict in Somalia has resulted in widespread contamination of explosive ordnance including explosive remnants of war (ERW), landmines, improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and the proliferation of small arms and light weapons which is still affecting security forces and the civilian population. ERW as well as landmines, remain present in Somalia, affecting the safety and livelihoods of communities. The explosive ordnance continues to affect the civilian population; especially young children in hard-to-reach rural settings and displaced communities. For instance, accidents recorded in 2020 and 2021, affected (killed or injured) 37 and 36 children respectively. To date, there are still 123 Confirmed Hazardous Areas (estimated area size of 60 sq km), with most located mainly along with the Somalia-Ethiopia border areas. There are an additional 74 Suspected Hazardous Areas (estimated area size of 68.5 sq km), and more than 300 Active Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) spots and in South Central Somalia that require further verification. In response to these threats, UNMAS proposes an Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) intervention focusing on promoting safe behaviour among the most vulnerable including children, displaced, and recently returned populations in both rural and urban settings. The project will incorporate the dissemination of a solar-powered Risk Education Talking Device (RETD) which will be able to assist the beneficiaries to listen to audio messages repeatedly. To provide a more sustained awareness of explosive hazards, the project will incorporate messages into other protection-related messages.

Project FieldsExplosive Ordnance Risk Education fostering Durable Solutions in Somalia Outcome 1: Communities experience a reduction of the threat posed by explosive hazards. Activities or Outputs: Output-1: Risk Education is delivered to conflict-affected communities.Activities: • Liaise and coordinate with local authorities and the affected population.• Carry out a procurement process to identify an implementing partner.• Select and hire eight clan diverse EORE teams from the local consortium.• Train 20 locals and provide EORE related equipment/materials. • Monitor & evaluate the training standard delivered to the teams.• Deploy eight teams to conduct EORE in four states (two in each state). • Reach out 50,000 individuals with face-to-face risk education messages.• Monitor the day-to-day activities in line with the EORE standards.• Evaluate the impact of the intervention in terms of change of behaviour on the affected population towards explosive hazards. Visibility:UNMAS, National Stakeholders and implementing partners will enhance visibility efforts throughout project implementation. Security will be considered in public communication materials, and, where needed, the timing or identities will be adjusted.
