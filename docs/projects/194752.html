Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements food security interventions targeting the most vulnerable with a focus on households that are female-headed, or have members who are pregnant and lactating women, women of reproductive age, children under 5, or people with disabilities, to enhance their nutritional status. The agricultural livelihoods of vulnerable smallholder farmers and livestock breeders have been significantly impacted by more than 11 years of armed conflict in Syria. This has been compounded by unprecedented hyperinflation. Furthermore, low income availability, insufficient local food production due to difficulties in accessing high-quality agriculture inputs (imported fertilizers), climate shocks and drought, lack of electricity, and a lack of water for irrigation and livestock has resulted in increasing adoption of negative coping strategies and rising food insecurity and malnutrition. AAH will support vulnerable households with a focus on PwSN (PLW and WRA) to access more nutritious diets through supplementary food assistance providing cash assistance with a MPCT value set by the CWG. AAH will target vulnerable households, supporting them with access to nutritious food and improving their ability to meet their basic needs. AAH will promote and maintain the self-reliance and resilience of affected households by encouraging Climate Smart/Sensitive Approaches through 1) improving the vegetative cover of the pastures by planting drought/saline drought forage plants as AAH will select plants that are drought resistant, are native to the context and contain nutrients beneficial to local livestock; 2) distributing Conservation Agriculture inputs including seeds (high-quality legume (lentil) and barley seeds) and fertilizers, providing Conservation Agriculture capacity building, distributing seed drills machines and improving the water content of soil to help farmers cope with the drought and the erratic rainfall. Slow-release balanced fertilizer and herbicides will be provided to address weed concerns in the adoption phase; 3) providing capacity building on a variety of topics for smallholder farmers and livestock keepers on Climate Smart Agriculture and Livestock Production, how to make compost at household level from food waste and residues, sustainable water management, how to produce hydroponically sprouted barley at household level, and Holistic Management for pastures to improve sustainability; 4) installing rainwater harvesting units in order to improve water availability for the livestock keepers; 5) improving the availability of water for irrigation for the farmers, by rehabilitating irrigation canals and drainage systems. AAH will rehabilitated an estimated 485 sections of irrigation canals in Maskana sub-district of Aleppo governorate as the system is only partially functioning with leakages throughout the system that damage the foundations of the canals and damage has impacted the slope of the canals preventing the correct flow of water; 6) improving the livelihoods of vulnerable female headed households by providing them with vocational training on food processing and preservation and by providing them with grants for establishing their own businesses. Capacity building and training will be provided by AAH technical staff in addition to technical staff from the Arab Center for Studies in Dry Lands and Arid Zone (ACSAD) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform (MAAR) extension officers. All proposed interventions will be aligned with the FSA Sector strategy, recommendations and objectives and CWG recommendations. AAH will ensure complementarity with its WASH, Health and Nutrition interventions.

3 - Needs

Protracted food insecurity in Syria continues as a result of a number of factors, including ongoing insecurity and conflict, devaluation of the Syrian Pound, the high cost of food production and imports, low household income, climate shocks, drought, lack of electricity, and a lack of water for irrigation and livestock. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, 68% of the population. At least 12.1 million are estimated to be food insecure. Unemployment has risen sharply with negative consequences for income and purchasing power. Families are resorting to harmful coping mechanisms to meet their food and nutritional needs. The high cost of inputs continues to limit agricultural input use by farmers and adherence to standard seed and dose rates. This is resulting in reduced yields, crop productivity and food availability. Livestock keepers also suffer from water scarcity and limited access to feed (FSA HNO 2023). AAH’s assessments confirmed that smallholder farmers and livestock breeders require improved access to high-quality agricultural inputs including seeds, fertilizers nutritious animal feed and financial support to restore their agricultural-based activities, in addition to capacity building in context-specific climate-sensitive approaches.

4 - Implementation of activities

AAH will promote and maintain self-reliance and resilience of the households’ agricultural livelihoods by adopting Climate Smart Sensitive Approaches through 1) improving the vegetative cover of the pastures by planting drought/saline drought forage plants; 2) distributing Conservation Agriculture inputs including seeds and fertilizers, providing CA capacity building and distributing seed drills in order to adopt zero/minimum tillage; 3) providing capacity building on Climate Smart Agriculture/Livestock Production, Composting, sustainable water management, hydroponic sprouted barley, and Holistic Management for the pastures; 4) installing rainwater harvesting units in order to improve water availability for the livestock keepers; 5) improving the availability of water for irrigation for the farmers, by rehabilitating the irrigation canals and mitigate any harm of increasing the water table in the gypsum lands by rehabilitating of drainage system also including drainage pumping station and drainage canals; 6) creating job opportunities for vulnerable female headed households by providing them with vocational training on food processing and preservation and by providing them with grants for establishing their own businesses. AAH will support vulnerable households with a focus on PwSN (malnourished PLW and WRA) to access supplementary food assistance and nutritious food through cash assistance.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to SEA. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. AAH prohibits SEA by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; support awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy. All employees and representatives of AAH are trained on Safeguarding, Protection and PSEA and are signatories to AAH’s ethical policies and Code of Conduct, and are therefore obligated not to engage in sexually exploitative behaviour linking sexual favors with the receipt of aid, engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age or be involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH staff and communities AAH works with have access to safe and effective reporting systems. AAH will investigate allegations of SEA in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of substantive devaluation of the Syrian Pound; international bank transfers to Syria are not fluid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: situation closely monitored with communication to donors with options defined. Risk of aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of suppliers, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Risk of data collection hampered. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: transparency, trust building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers. Risk of delayed approvals from authorities to start the intervention. Likelihood: high, Impact: high. Mitigation: advocacy with local authorities; MoU signed with MAAR. Ethical risk. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: provide compulsory training to staff members on organizational ethical policies. Follow the internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a Policy for PSEA with an implementation plan.
