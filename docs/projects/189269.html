Project Summary

South Sudanese continue to face unprecedented levels of food insecurity. It is estimated more than half the population; that is 7.2 million (60%) of its entire population ever since its independence experience food insecurity during the lean season every year. This trend has been worsened for the last three years by widespread flooding, recurrent violence, localized dry spells, indirect effects of COVID-19 on supply chain and production, and high food prices, thus further exacerbating people’s food security situation and livelihoods. Another compounding factor has been the High inflation that is persistent with high basic Commodities prices (HNO 2022). According to the recent IPC findings, at least three-quarters of the population in Mayom, Koch, and Panyijiar Counties are classified in IPC phases 3 and above. In Much of Unity State, WFP and other FSL Partners are currently continuing with food distribution, Food for assets and school feeding programs because most of the counties have been flooded and are highly food insecure (WFP Situation report 298 2022). The dire food insecurity situation, therefore, calls for Livelihoods and food security support through the distribution of Agricultural inputs; fishing, vegetable, Agricultural tools/Implements, and crop kits. This should also be supplemented by training in best agronomic and fishing practices; fish handling, and fish preservation techniques. Additionally, there should be vaccination of livestock, and training in climate change adaptation. Humane Aid for community Organization intends to target 13033 House households (78,200 individuals) with livelihoods support in Mayom, Panyijiar, and Koch Counties in Unity State.

1 - Needs

South Sudanese continue to face unprecedented levels of food insecurity. It is estimated more than half the population; that is 7.2 million (60%) of its entire population ever since its independence experience food insecurity during the lean season every year. This has been worsened for the last three years by widespread flooding, recurrent violence, localized dry spells, indirect effects of COVID-19 on supply chain and production, and high food prices thus exacerbating people’s food security situation and livelihoods. This is even made worse by the High inflation that is persistent with high basic Commodities prices (HNO 2022). According to the recent IPC findings in February 2022, at least three-quarters of the population in Mayom (70,859 individuals), Koch (49,573 individuals), and Panyijiar (42,217 individuals) are classified in IPC 3 and above. In Much of Unity State, WFP and other FSL Partners are currently continuing with food distribution, Food for assets and school feeding programs because most of the counties have been flooded and are highly food insecure (WFP Situation report 298 2022). The dire food insecurity situation therfore calls for Livelihoods and food security support through the distribution of Agricultural inputs; fishing, vegetable, Agricultural tools/Implements, and crop kits. This should also be supplemented by training in best fishing, fish handling, and fish preservation techniques. Additionally, there should be training in climate change adaptation. Humane Aid for community Organization (HACO) intends to target 13,033 Households (78,200 individuals) with livelihoods support activities in the aforementioned Counties.

2 - Project activities

The following Food security and livelihoods activities are meant to improve the fish, crop, livestock yields in order to reduce the food insecurity outlook in Mayom, Koch and Panyijiar Counties in Unity state. The main project activities will be the distribution of high quality Agricultural inputs; fishing, vegetable, Agricultural tools/Implements, and crop kits in order to improve the yield of the farmers. Other activities will be training in best fishing, fish handling, and fish preservation techniques to improve the knowledge of the farmers in fishing methods that are time tested and result in good harvests and Training farmers in best agronomic practices, and climatic change adaptation in order to help famers cope with climatic change and Vaccination of livestock to increase the resilience of the livestock to diseases.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

This is a “do no harm” approach and will contribute to building the resilience of the local communities. CVA will help to support project beneficiaries to access resources they need for livelihoods support which cannot be provided in kind. for instance, they can engage in petty businesses to give their families alternative livelihood support (cash-based). CVA is also a formidable method for market surpport. In this way the resilience of the local communities can be improved. The organization of seed fairs in locations where its feasible can be one avenue of CVA.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The Project sites have good and functional markets where cash-based assistance can be helpful. Cash based assistance is a form of market surpport for instance engaging local famers in the provision of quality seeds through seed fairs. In this way resilience of communities can be increased.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Women, girls, and persons with disabilities will be consulted during project implementation and will be asked for the best distribution sites, the best times for project implementation activities. in this way, their feedback will inform project implementation modalities or processes.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The distribution and AAP committees will comprise members from the affected groups such as representatives from the Womens' groups, persons with disabilities as well as youth groups. this will ensure that the voices of the affected groups are amplified. the leadership of Persons with disabilities, Women and Youth will also be targeted and prioritised for consultations during the project implementation. in this way the views of persons with disabilities, the illiterate and women will be sought and responded to during the entire project cycle.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The proposed project staff and personnel will be trained on the organization's Code of Conduct and PSEA policy and they will be required to sign and commit to adhering to the policy. the beneficiaries will be told that humanitarian assistance is free and they will be given referral contacts should there be cases of SEA.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Project staff will give the beneficiaries information about the project scope, duration, targeting criteria, and limitations. The project will have AAP committees who will be trained on the entire project activities, they will be part and parcel of the project implementation and monitoring. they will be tasked to man the beneficiary complaints and feedback desk and they will be the team that will respond to beneficiary complaints in consultation with the project implementation team. The AAP committees will operate using Suggestion Boxes, help desks, phone numbers and email addresses. Additionally, Focus group discussions, individual interviews, post distribution Monitoring and key informant interviews will be conducted to get the views of the project beneficiaries. AAP Committee focal points and AAP Management Committees and Project managers willCollect Complaints and give Feedback. AAP focal points will disseminate phone numbers, and collect feedback from complaint desks, suggestion boxes and send to field coordinators and H/Q office. Information from feedback desks will be reported daily either through the AAP focal points or sent by email to the field coordinator/H/Q office, and feedback from suggestion boxes will be reported weekly. Complaints from phone calls and email address will be received by Field coordinators and H/Q Program Cordinator and or Protection Manager. AAP focal points will register all complaints according to category; namely programmatic or Accountability and they will be responded to or referred on a weekly basis.
