Project Summary

The primary objective of this project is to catalyze early recovery and bolster the resilience of conflict-affected communities in Northeast Nigeria. This will be achieved through a multi-faceted approach to livelihood stabilization and income recovery, with a particular focus on young and older adults, as well as individuals living with disabilities.
Our strategic interventions encompass a suite of livelihood enhancement activities, including but not limited to, provision of short-cycle business management training, distribution of cash grants, facilitation of access to microfinance opportunities, personalized coaching, and support for various income-generating initiatives.
This 11-month program is meticulously designed to deliver targeted assistance to approximately 4,878 of the most vulnerable individuals among internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and members of host communities across 13 strategically selected areas within the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. By concentrating our efforts in these specific regions, the project aims to address the unique challenges faced by these communities and to foster a sustainable environment for economic recovery and resilience building.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria, spanning over a decade, has significantly degraded public infrastructure and access to basic services, particularly in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states. This situation is exacerbated by the continuous influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs), further straining the already limited services. In 2023, an estimated 8.3 million people, comprising 59% children and 22% women, are in dire need of humanitarian assistance in these regions. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2022 indicate that approximately 2.1 million individuals urgently require early recovery assistance, having experienced profound resilience erosion and diminished livelihood opportunities. Notably, 62% of these are women, disproportionately affected by the ongoing conflict and the socio-economic fallout of the pandemic, with about 1.8% living with disabilities. A breakdown by population groups reveals that 13% are IDPs, 21% returnees, and 65% belong to host communities across the BAY States. As per the 41st round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM – August 2022), there are 2,012,660 IDPs, predominantly women and children. The displacement has disrupted education, with schools being used as IDP camps, leading to reluctance in returning to formal education settings. This has resulted in 78% of households reporting inadequate income for basic needs, and 20% resorting to negative coping strategies like selling productive assets and food borrowing. Even among Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) recipients, more than half still report unmet livelihood needs, especially pronounced in Borno. The strain on resources in host communities due to displacement is escalating tensions, eroding livelihood, and income opportunities. Thirteen out of 61 LGAs in the BAY states are severely affected, with populations forced into negative coping mechanisms due to inadequate and unreliable income. Additionally, erratic climatic conditions, insurgent attacks, and overcrowded settlements are further impairing basic public services like healthcare, sanitation, waste management, and market access. On average, over 30% of people in the BAY states lack access to basic community services. In response to these challenges, the Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) aims to facilitate the transition of IDPs, returnees, and host communities from a humanitarian crisis to a state of sustainable livelihood and public service restoration, thereby strengthening resilience and fostering positive peace in return locations. CNEF's focus is on enhancing both short-term and long-term livelihood opportunities for these vulnerable groups, with particular emphasis on the needs of displaced women and their families. This initiative is critical for rebuilding lives and communities in a context marked by prolonged adversity and disruption.

2 - Activities

Aligned with the identified needs and targeted response areas in the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, our initiative is strategically designed to support Early Recovery Sectors, focusing on specific regions including Mafa, Konduga, Pulka, Dikwa, and Monguno in Borno; Maiha, Madagali, Michika, Mubi North, and Mubi South in Adamawa; and Geidam, Yusufari, and Yunusari in Yobe. Our primary activities align with the first three objectives of the sector's strategic goals, which encompass supporting livelihood stabilization and income recovery, providing emergency employment and Cash-for-Work opportunities, delivering vocational training and business skills development for small-scale business establishment (via start-up or in-kind grants), and enhancing community savings and access to credit through the creation and capacity building of community cooperatives and Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs). Targeting a total of 4,878 individuals across these areas—582 in Borno, 368 in Adamawa, and 3,928 in Yobe—our approach will engage both young and older adults in cash-for-work activities to mitigate negative coping strategies. Community mobilization will involve consultations with community leaders to gain support and insight into the project's implementation. The beneficiary identification and registration process will be conducted in collaboration with the Early Recovery and Livelihood (ER&L) sector and community leaders, adhering to established criteria, with registered participants receiving identification cards to authenticate their program involvement. Training modules will be tailored to the specific vocational interests and skills of the beneficiaries, conducted by experienced trainers in various trade areas. This includes community-level training on saving and loan schemes, promoting the culture of teamwork, savings, and internal lending. The vocational training, lasting six months, aims to ensure the trainees are adequately prepared before receiving seed capital for initial material purchases. Additionally, participants will be encouraged to join the Village and Savings Association (VSLA) for ongoing support and community integration. This project is committed to upholding the highest standards of project management, emphasizing accountability, transparency, and adherence to humanitarian principles. Through these concerted efforts, the project aims to empower individuals and communities in the BAY states, fostering resilience and sustainable livelihoods in the wake of prolonged conflict and disruption.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) is dedicated to making a significant contribution to the Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) Nexus, adopting a development-oriented approach that goes beyond early recovery to fundamentally strengthen resilience. Our focus is on supporting internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities within the conflict-impacted areas of the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. This initiative aligns with our core objective of fostering livelihood stabilization as a means to build sustainable and resilient communities. Collaborating closely with local partners and community leaders, CNEF aims to implement a series of strategic interventions. These include providing vocational training and business skill development programs, facilitating cash-for-work activities, and offering support for the establishment of small-scale businesses. Such initiatives are designed to not only enhance livelihood opportunities but also to drive economic development, reinforce social cohesion, and establish improved community-saving mechanisms. Through these efforts, CNEF is committed to contributing meaningfully to the recovery and development of the BAY states, ensuring that the communities affected by conflict can rebuild and progress towards a future of stability, prosperity, and peace. By integrating the principles of the HDP Nexus into our work, we aim to create lasting positive impacts that resonate beyond immediate recovery, laying the groundwork for long-term development and resilience in these communities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) is committed to integrating protection mainstreaming as a fundamental aspect of its operations, emphasizing the safeguarding of vulnerable communities. This commitment will be actualized through a series of structured strategies, ensuring a safe and responsive environment for all stakeholders involved. 1. Regular Community Engagement: CNEF will maintain ongoing consultations with target communities. These interactions are essential for understanding the specific needs of the communities and ensuring that our interventions are aligned with their expectations and requirements. 2. Staff Development: A significant focus will be on training and capacity building of our staff, particularly in areas of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). This includes in-depth training on effective reporting channels and case management modalities, equipping our team to handle sensitive issues efficiently and ethically. 3. Reporting Mechanisms Promotion: We will proactively promote the use of existing Voluntary Service Overseas (VSO) hotlines and email channels for the reporting of protection concerns, fraud, abuse, or any violations of primary actors' rights. Additionally, we will ensure the visibility of various contacts for reporting and seeking redress in all community meetings, as well as within our own and our partners' offices. 4. Community Feedback Tools: To evaluate the impact and effectiveness of our services, CNEF will employ tools such as community scorecards. This approach will enable us to gauge community appreciation of our work, focusing on the quality, effectiveness, and impartiality of the services provided. Through these comprehensive measures, CNEF aims to foster a culture of safety, responsiveness, and ethical responsibility in all its endeavors. By ensuring active community participation and robust staff training, coupled with accessible reporting mechanisms and feedback tools, we are dedicated to upholding the highest standards of protection and support for those we serve.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Cosmic Nudge Empowerment Foundation (CNEF) is committed to upholding accountability to the populations affected by our projects. To achieve this, we will establish functional and contextually relevant feedback and accountability mechanisms. These systems are designed to empower beneficiaries, providing them with safe and confidential channels to voice their concerns, express dissatisfaction, or offer suggestions regarding project implementation. Our approach includes the implementation of suggestion boxes, strategically positioned within the affected communities to facilitate easy access. Additionally, we will establish a complaint help desk, a toll-free line, and a dedicated WhatsApp channel, offering multiple platforms for beneficiaries to communicate their feedback comfortably and securely. These feedback mechanisms are integral to our project strategy, serving not only to collect input from beneficiaries but also to provide timely and appropriate responses. We place a strong emphasis on maintaining confidentiality, ensuring that the process of giving and receiving feedback upholds the dignity and privacy of all participants. Through these measures, CNEF ensures that beneficiary voices are heard and respected, reinforcing the effectiveness and impact of our programs while adhering to our core values of accountability and transparency. This approach is fundamental in fostering trust and building strong, responsive relationships with the communities we serve.
