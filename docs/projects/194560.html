Project Summary

More than 15.3 million people are in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023, a 3.2 million increase from 2022 (N1).
Syria’s shortage of health infrastructure has negatively affected the availability and quality of health services across Syria, including to the most vulnerable populations. In addition, the health care system continues to face chronic challenges to respond to disease outbreaks including COVID-19 and Cholera / Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD).
Thus, UNOPS proposes to support the strengthening of the healthcare system in Syria by rehabilitation of hospitals and PHCs, provision of solar energy solutions, delivery of related training, and provision of critical medical equipment and supplies to target health facilities in two governorates (Homs and Hama) in Syria.
The objective of this project is to contribute to enhancing the health sector capacity for a timely response to disaster outbreaks. This project is directly linked with the Strategic Objectives 1 (Save Lives) and 3 (Increase resilience and access to services) of the draft Syria Humanitarian Response Plan 2022 and the immediate response priorities of the Health Sector for 2022 in Syria (N2).
The target locations of this project are Homs and Hama governorates, with possible extensions to other governorates based on the needs. These potential target locations are identified based on the analysis of the needs from the Health Sector and Health sub-sector, taking into account the great barriers, lack of availability and high cost of services, and the ranking on the OCHA severity scale. In addition, the priority needs have been identified in close coordination with the Ministry of Health (MoH) and the humanitarian partners. Once the project is launched, UNOPS will conduct gender-sensitive and technical assessments to finalize the selection of the target locations, the exact scope of work, and the specifications and type of the needed equipment.
Through this intervention, it is expected that five public health facilities (1 hospital and 4 PHCs) will be supported.
To fill the gaps in the shortage of electricity in public health facilities, UNOPS will equip the public health facilities with solar energy solutions through the procurement, installation and commissioning of photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems and operations and maintenance training will be delivered to the health facilities staff.
It is expected this project will enable targeted public health facilities to offer enhanced pivotal healthcare services to the most vulnerable population in targeted sub-districts of Homs and Hama governorates.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Harassments (SEAH) risks are present in the direct contact between UNOPS and its contractors’ staff and local community members (beneficiaries) during the rehabilitation works and installation of equipment. An on-site SEAH risk assessment will be conducted before initiating rehabilitation works and installations at all targeted public health facilities and mitigation measures will be included in the project Environmental and Social Management Plans (ESMP). All UNOPS personnel will take a mandatory online PSEA training upon joining the project while contractor personnel working on the project will receive a PSEA training after having signed their contract with UNOPS. The contractor in turn shall train all workers engaged in the project from their side, as well as have them sign a Code of Conduct. Contractors will also be responsible for conducting regular refresher sessions for their workers as well as for putting up PSEA awareness raising posters adapted to the local context on all work sites to facilitate reporting of cases. For this purpose, the posters shall include the referral pathway for reporting cases, i.e. the contact information and uptake channels of the project Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM). In addition, SEAH clauses will be included in all UNOPS personnel and contractor/supplier contracts.
