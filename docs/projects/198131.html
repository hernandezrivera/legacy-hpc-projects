Project Summary

Support 33,512 children affected by insecurity to continue their education by providing school feeding in districts that received most IDPs in Cabo Delgado. The project will provide three take-home rations to targeted children in transitional learning spaces (TLS) and children attending primary schools in host communities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will support 33,512 children affected by insecurity to continue their education by providing take-home rations in learning spaces in Cabo Delgado. The project will target 22,000 children in transitional learning spaces (TLS) and in primary schools in host communities in the District of Mueda and 11,512 in Palma. Targeting has been guided by the number of IDP population in districts and consultations of Provincial Education Directorates. School feeding will contribute to meet the protection and food and nutrition insecurity of displaced and returnee children and support them to pursue their education. WFP also assists children in host communities where already scarce resources have been further stretched by the sharp increase in the number of IDPs. Take-home ration distributions will also be used as a platform to engage with school communities on child protection and to raise awareness about specific protection risks faced by girls, including child marriage.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Provide school feeding to children affected by insecurity to meet their nutritional needs and support in continuing their education. Given the infrastructure limitations in learning spaces in terms of access to water and storage capacity, the intervention will provide school feeding in the take-home rations modality. Monthly distributions of food baskets consisting of 5kg of rice, 1kg of beans, 1 l of fortified oil and 1kg of ionized salt will be carried out in learning spaces, in coordination with the school community. In districts where TLS are being created, these spaces will be targeted. In districts where IDPs are integrated into host communities, school feeding will be channeled to established schools, benefiting IDPs and host communities. Distribute WASH materials to learning spaces such as handwashing facilities, to better equip schools for the safe return to school and to enable the safe monthly distribution of take-home rations. Strengthen capacities of local education services, school staff and school council members on the implementation of take-home rations in the context of an emergency. These teams will also be trained on the protection of children, specially of girls, including linking them to essential child protection referral pathways established in the province. Engage caregivers on child protection and prevention of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) by using take-home rations distributions as a platform to raise awareness of caregivers and school community on these themes.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Measures to ensure children’s protection will be integrated into the training provided to school teams, school councils and District Education Services, and the communication materials distributed in schools, with focus on protective measures for students receiving take-home rations in the monthly distributions. To ensure children are not exposed to any potential risks during distributions and at the same time ensuring their attendance in classes, parents and caregivers are registered and responsible for collecting the food basket on behalf of their children. To ensure proper coverage and avoid exclusion, IDPs' registration also relies on school team for confirmation, including documentation locally provided by area authorities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Prior to programme implementation the school is responsible to identify council members who will be involved in the support of implementation always observing women's participation. These women will also benefit from trainings to unable them to deliver the program's key messages as well as advocate for the programme objective. Furthermore, there is a proper explanation on the registration process, to ensure that assistance is collected by the rightful caregiver.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

WFP and local education services will work with school communities to identify enrolled children with disabilities or those whose parents have disabilities to map barriers they may face to benefit from programme assistance and address them accordingly.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

WFP runs an Environmental and Social Safeguards (ESS) assessment completed with implementing partners and government entities prior to project implementation. The tool maps any risk related to the programme and sets actions to avoid or manage the impact. On the environment side, the tool considers relevant questions related natural resources, Ecosystems and Biodiversity, waste, and pollution.
