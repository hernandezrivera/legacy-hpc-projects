Project Summary

The project aim at improving the protection and respect of fundamental rights for persons affected by crises in the NW region of Cameroon. The project will also make provision for psychosocial support and cash/livelihood assistance to vulnerable IDPs and affected population. The activities include: Conducting protection monitoring in crisis-affected areas; strengthening the civil documentation to conflict-affected people; providing targeted protection assistance to the most vulnerable people (including livelihood); Sensitization on peaceful coexistence between host and IDPs communities and non-discrimination of IDPs; Build capacity of monitors, social and psychosocial workers to monitor human rights violation in standardized, rigorous way for credible, compatible documentation and supports advocacy and direct action effectively; Identify protection issues, monitor events, trend and changes; Collect, gather and analyze information related to physical, material and legal safety, vulnerabilities encountered by displaced and other affected population; Inform humanitarian programing through identification of needs, gaps and trends; Generate a responds from relevant stakeholders through referral and/ or advocacy actions; Identify individual protection cases for referral to specialized agencies (internal or external referrals based on services provided in the different locations); Disseminate information on available services to the displaced and host communities; The project location is in the Northwest region (Bui and boyo divisions) of Cameroon. The project targets 9000 people.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The current crises in the Anglophone region erupted with a strike of teachers and lawyers demanding greater inclusion of English-speaking professionals in the teachings and legal sectors. Civil society groups supported the movement against marginalization and deprivation of Anglophone Cameroonians by the francophone majority and central government. The militarized reaction of the authorities was criticized by the national commission of human rights and freedom (NCHRF) in their report as the disproportionate use of force and abusive reactions by the forces of law and order during manifestations (protests) and acts of torture and inhuman treatments on persons arrested. The report also condemned the detention of minors in prisons. Appeals of dialogue and peaceful resolution achieved no results, instead a spiral of violence was set in motion with the civilian population caught between a separatist movement taking up arms and militarized security forces. Human rights abuse on both sides of the conflict is since causing growing human suffering and humanitarian needs amongst the population with their vulnerable groups most affected. Large scale displacements are the results of raids and torching of entire villages deprivation from basic necessities to survive in remote hiding places, disruption of health and social services and economic breakdown are the direct consequences of the armed conflicts. Freedom of movement is curtailed in a militarized environment with checkpoints, ghost towns and curfews. The rights of education is suspended in large parts of the region by threats and violent acts against schooling, hospitals, medical staffs and patience are attacked in violation of the law that grants special protection. Kidnaping, extortion of money, gender based violence, arbitrary arrests and detention, indiscriminate killing mainly of male youth are on a rise since the onset of the crises. Neither national nor international attempts to stop the violence have succeeded until now. Within this context of armed violence and recurrent human rights violation and abuses by all parties to the conflicts, human rights and faith-based organisations took the initiative to monitor and report such cases in order to contribute to the protection of the civilian’s populations and fight against impunities of the authors. This organisations vulnerability decided to undertake such activities but some staffs need capacity reinforcement in order to carry out monitoring and reporting at the level of international standards. Solid evidence generated from high quality monitoring is the prerequisite for effective advocacy at decision making levels to obtain respect for national and international norms.
