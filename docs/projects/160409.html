Project Summary

Fangak, Ayod (in Jonglei State); Koch and Mayendit Counties (in Unity State) – all bordering The Sudan - have been identified as cantonment sites. Thousands of returnees are expected back home in these counties in 2020, based on high prospects of a revitalized peace in South Sudan. The presence of these cantonment sites in the mentioned counties is a source of vulnerability to the returnee population. Civil population will be exposed to protection risks such as forced and voluntary recruitments into the army; exposure to GBV risks; interference during access to civilian humanitarian services; occupation of civilian infrastructure; availability, exposure and spread of weapon; misuse of humanitarian aid (including deviation, looting and exploitation); violation of housing, land and property right; lack of political will and support - targeted mocks on civilians. As flood-prone areas, the locations almost always experience floods, resulting in human displacements. Besides, man-made disasters are recurrent: tribal attacks and escalation of conflict between warring parties. Historically, the mentioned States have been experiencing massive displacement of people, killing of children, women, the elderly and loss of livestock and property, including burning of houses. In 2019, an estimated 35,000 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) were quarterly displaced in these States. 75,000 people returned from PoCs in Bor, Malakal , Juba, Ethiopia and Sudan . In 2020, thousands more are expected back home in the mentioned States.This project aims to ensure provision of S/NFIs to the most affected population of conflict and disaster, vulnerable categories in the mentioned counties, especially the IDPs and the most affected vulnerable host communities. These are based on the Humanitarian Needs overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plans (HRP) for South Sudan. The project will target a population of 30,000 individuals in Fangak, Ayod Counties of Jonglei State; Koch and Mayendit Counties of Unity State. It targets populations in four locations: Fangak (12, 000); Ayod (6,000); Koch (6,000) and Mayendit (6,000) individuals respectively. Due to the magnitude of the displacement, about 70% of households are expected to be women-headed, with no less than one third widows, plus the housewives whose husbands will be relocating to cantonment sites, seeking for military service or promotions within the army with an intention of earning more money for their households left behind in home villages. IDPs and returnees are (as of 2019) settled in Old Fangak, New Fangak, Ayod, Koch and Mayendit. Several joint assessment and responses conducted from 20th July to 5th Sept 2019 reported IDPs lacking essential NFIs services. Other IDPs had health related issues, especially diarrhea, vomiting, skin diseases, and fever, especially amongst children. IDPs were reportedly sleeping in Kangas and shawls, with about ten or more individuals sharing with one host household. Many children who were attending school were out of school in the locations where IDPs have settled. Children are also at risk of neglect, child labour, gender based violence (GBV), recruitment into armed groups, forced marriage (for girls) and family separation especially after going through the violence when fighting broke out. Over 60% of the most-at-risk IDP populations in these areas will mainly be women and children, with family shelter and NFI needs. The humanitarian imperative is: provide S/NFI to these IDPs and host populations, the basis of this proposal. Upon project approval, ADA will send pipeline requests for the S/NFI supplies to the Logistics Cluster who will share the request with the S/NFI Cluster. The latter will approve or seek clarifications from ADA who will respond to the queries. Once approved, the Logistics Cluster will update the items to be sent to the field and notify ADA accordingly. ADA will liaise with field teams/bases to ensure supplies are received and distributed.

1 - Needs

Unity and Jonglei States are often vulnerable to their exposure both to natural and man-man disaster like flood and displacements from nearby states, tribal attacks and intensity of conflict between warring parties: inter-communal or national. In 2019 serious flooding in the second quarter caused multiple displacements and humanitarian needs in both Unity and Jonglei States. Reports from needs analyses conducted by DRC, IOM, INTERSOS and ADA in Fangak, Ayod, Koch and Mayendit in the third quarter of 2019 revealed that 45,000 IDPs were in dire need of emergency shelter and NFIs. Most of these IDPs expressed their fear for the ongoing hostilities around and within Unity state which made them more vulnerable, as they had no access to food and essential NFIs. In September 2019, a joint needs assessment done by ADA, and Hope Restoration in Mayendit of Unity State reported massive flooding in Mayendit County, leading to mass displacements from affected areas to one small area in the center of the county where people were seen more crowded. In a related incidence, returnees were also noted arriving regularly in Koch County in August 2019 when ADA and IOM mobile team responded in Koch. With the heightened prospects of peace in 2020, more returnees will be moving back home, hence the need to support them with essential S/NFIs. Numerous joint missions conducted by different SNFI cluster partners within IDP populations reflect serious deficiencies in access to basic services, especially in hard to access rural populations. This is exacerbated by low or almost no household income, coupled with unavailability of required household emergency items in the local markets. These are based on the Humanitarian Needs overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plans (HRP) for South Sudan.ADA aims to strengthen resilience copying mechanism of the affected population through training and provision of lifesaving assistance, especially to IDPs that have suffered multiple displacements. In 2020, ADA will target communities with such multiple displacements. It will prioritize female-headed households, people with disabilities, households with children under 5, pregnant and lactating women, elderly people and children with protection concerns including child headed households. Due to the multiple crises in the target location and the limited humanitarian rehabilitation resources, several other essential services continue to remain wanting. For example, boreholes are often in a state of disrepair, as are some health facilities, schools, nutrition centers etc. ADA will, in the course of discharging its S/NFI interventions refer cases related to such to the relevant humanitarian actors through approved humanitarian actor channels. The consistent presence of ADA on the ground would significantly support mobile response partners in coordination, monitoring and lay down the overview context of areas for better service provision by other partners. The provision of training in a number of areas of protection mainstreaming is required to ensure the communities are participating in their own protection and are supporting humanitarian action in their location. The project will also address the needs of the physically challenged or people living with disability and will always involve them in any decision made and inform them about the assistance in advance and will also train them to be aware of most imminent protection challenges. To constantly inform the programming, ADA will use its multi-sector teams to create meaningful synergy and enrich the S/NFI intervention to communities. For example, ADA will deploy an integrated team composed of protection, WASH and NFIs staff to update locational needs across the counties in Unity and Jonglei States. Through this project, ADA aims to address the needs of the conflict affected people, returnees, host communities and IDPs in response to their S/NFIs needs in the four counties.

2 - Activities

In 2020, ADA will maintain its commitment to engaging with affected communities at all phases of the program cycle: assessment, registration, verification, distribution and Post Distribution and Monitoring exercises. ADA being a static partner will continue to engage communities in determining appropriate and needs ¬based response approaches. To maximize efficiency, this project will be carried out in consultation with all stakeholders, communities, local authorities, cluster coordinators, mobile partners. This will ensure solid impact, avoid double count or duplication, and promote sustainability. ADA will work with the planned humanitarian hubs in serving hard-to-reach areas accessible to the hubs. Respective community needs will be shared with humanitarian partners in the hubs with a view of seeking synergy of joint responses to both emergencies and other humanitarian needs by the partners. The hubs will also be strategic locations for both technical support and communication nerve for relay to either the communities or upwards for other national and international actors. The CCCM approach at national and State levels will be used to create better coordination of services amongst different clusters and the affected communities.All interventions will be made on the basis of assessed need. The activities that will make up this project are as follows: • A mobile team always in front line to visit any location to assess needs, do reporting to the line manager, verify and register the legible beneficiaries, do distribution and PDM and report on time as required by the S/NFIs Cluster. The team will train the enumerators from the community, community mobilizers, translators, distribution committees, crowd controllers, and the community leaders to easy the activities in the field. • Mobile and static team will conduct Community Communication Engagement (CCE). ADA team will engage with the community at least 2 weeks on ground per county, constantly bearing in mind the Do No Harm Principle in all community activities. Within the 2 weeks, the team understands the community contexts, needs and protection concerns. They will train and set up committees and feedback mechanisms.• Provide regular accountability to the affected populations (AAP) through establishment of community committees that will be representative in terms of gender, age sets, disability groupings, making sure inclusion of the aged or elderly, people who are physically challenges or those who live with disability, to work with the ADA team during needs assessments, distributions and PDMs to provide relevant, participatory feedbacks to both the community and to the donors.• Select beneficiaries, to oversee the distribution process and respond to complaints from the beneficiaries during the distribution. The intervention seeks to provide blankets, mosquito nets, sleeping mats, and kitchen set (saucepan, spoons, plate, cups and ladle) to the IDPs. Given the average household size of six individuals per household, two blankets, two mosquito nets, two sleeping mats and one kitchen set will be distributed per household. • Train members of distribution committees, community mobilizers, enumerators, community leaders, boys and girls. Trainings will include: types of S/NFIs and how to use them, the quantity of items per households, protection issues, how to report to the concerned people and how to use the supplies in the community. • Set up distribution points and distribution mechanisms, in consultation with the community committees and local authorities to ensure safety of beneficiaries in all project locations- Fangak, Ayod, Mayendit and Koch Counties.• Engage the community committees in all stages of the response (assessment, verification, registration, distribution, monitoring, PDM and reporting) and continually providing post-distribution feedback on methodology effectiveness and impact of the intervention on the lives of the beneficiaries.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, the beneficiaries' rights will be fully and legally recognized in this project. Affected populations must be able to reach emergency assistance when and where needed, and in conditions of safety and dignity. Everyone will be entitled to the full protection of rights available to the population in general. ADA is aware that returnees might occupy land belonging legally to other community members present or absent, thus giving rise to particular vulnerabilities on the part of those conflict affected population. In order to ensure that the displaced and returnees are not deprived of their human rights, the local authority and the response teams are obligated to respect and provide special measures of protection and assistance to IDPs and returnees that correspond to their vulnerabilities in order to ensure that beneficiaries are treated equally with respect to non-displaced citizens. Beneficiaries rights will be legally recognized, respected and promoted through this project design in accordance to the United Nations Deceleration on Human Rights, international human rights treaties. S/NFI due diligent guidelines will be used to mitigate issues related to HLP. Safety audit guidelines as well will be used to manage cases related to PSEA and protection concerns during implementation. In February 2017 South Sudan came up with a framework for returnees; providing special consideration to conflict-affected persons including children, women, widows, persons with disabilities, etc. in the provision of public service, including access to health, HLP and education services and granting the host communities the same protection and humanitarian services. The Cluster and ADA will be accountable to the beneficiaries and the donors to fulfill their mandate to distribute on the basis of need, by ensuring proper assessment and verification of need.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. This project aims to meet the urgent needs of the displaced affected communities. The affected communities are the core target of this project. It will ensure equal access to all the affected populations. All the responses will be conducted through consultation with the affected communities and equal access will incorporate the principle of do no harm. All the affected population will have opportunity to contribute significantly to the implementation of the project. This project targets 30,000 most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in Fangak, Ayod, Koch and Mayendit Counties. The project beneficiaries registration, verification and distribution dates will be made open and announced publicly to all members of the community, to ensure inclusivity and to promote transparency and accountability to the affected persons. The project will connect beneficiaries with other people to share and exchange information, experiences and learning. The project is designed to allow for maximum participation and engagement of all affected people regardless of their gender, religion, race, ethnicity, or political orientation. Prior information will be passed to the community on a potential date for project sensitization and mobilization on the project start, objectives, selection criteria and target beneficiaries, separation of responsibilities, project expectation or outcomes and exit strategy to ensure all vulnerable households and authorities are updated. However, responses on the ground will pay particular attention to the needs of the most vulnerable groups including female and child headed households and people with special needs. During the implementation the project team will engage with targeted beneficiaries to learn how they are assessing their needs

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, ADA jointly works with the government mainly RRC/ROSS through project coordination and joint missions which are planned collectively and aligned to the government priority plans. The project will be linked to longer-term durable solutions efforts for displacement-affected communities (IDPs, refugees, returnees and most affected and vulnerable hosts’ community by providing them with shelter and household items. In this, the government or local authority shall support in allowing the conflict affected people a place to designate as their own. ADA is statically located, hence will participate in all SNFIs coordination meetings to update on activities, challenges, achievements, lessons learned and best practices to leverage. Prior to project inception or entry, project meeting for implementation will be conducted with the local authorities in the project areas, both at the State, County, Payam or Boma levels as safety audits measures for projects links with government plans. The communities in question were consulted prior to the project design phase through needs assessment and existing community feedback mechanisms. The interests, needs, aspirations and future goals were included into the programming and implementation plan. In settings outside PoCs, in this matter ADA will work amicably hand in hand with the local authority to ensure people have a legitimate place to live, without threat of eviction, and that will increase people’s ability to stay longer in a given area, which contributes to the investment and hopes for better future of the conflict affected population. During the beneficiary selection stage, ADA will consult all partners with ongoing related projects in the counties, regarding them as the primary channel for sharing and cross-checking of information on targeted beneficiaries and needs for this project.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this project is aligned to the national plan or/framework for returnees, IDPs and Refugees reintegration and it has incorporated integrated approaches in line with HRP clusters Objectives for 2020. The humanitarian relief programming and coordination is regulated by the Relief and Rehabilitation commission at the national, state and county levels in conjunction with OCHA support. The SNFIs cluster and OCHA does the alignment and coordination with support of cluster state focal points to ensure that project goals, objectives and activities are in line with national objectives and that the implementation criteria does not deviate from planned cluster methodology. The project aligned these to its organizational strategic plans and priorities for Fangak, Ayod, Koch and Mayendit counties. The SNFIs cluster consulted the refugees and returnees lead Agency -UNHCR to ensure the cluster priorities are well aligned to the national returns/refugees framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

ADA being the static partner, will sufficiently contribute to identification of environmental impact, and help in carrying out key messages to mitigate cases related to environmental degradation, e.g. in matters of solid waste disposal within the community. ADA will also pay close attention to the safety and protection concerns and risks within the crises-affected populations during any interventional stage: assessment, verification and registration, distribution and post distribution monitoring. With the communities, potential risks and threats will be identified and mitigation measures developed. The DO NO HARM principle will be upheld in all activities to minimize risks for self, partners and crisis-affected people. Synergies within key community, Government, NGOs, UN, the nearest humanitarian hub managements, private sector concerning local S/NFI environmental and safety concerns will be jointly resolved in respective intervention areas with relevant community sector leads.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. As a community needs-based driven project, shared vision of the S/NFI objectives, processes, monitoring and ownership by the community provide a platform for envisioning its sustainability within the communities. Shared responsibilities with all key community leadership and beneficiaries - men, women, boys, girls, elderly and disabled will promote the needed sense of community ownership and drive to sustain the gains made from the intervention. Self-reliance and resilience-focused trainings will support community efforts to ensure they manage their own S/NFI needs beyond the project period, using own, locally available resources or independently sourcing them.ADA will maintain its presence in the four counties (for other sector interventions) and will be able to monitor and provide further community-based guidance on emerging S/NF needs beyond the project period. Other sector gaps that may be identified will be reported through different cluster forums with the capacity to respond to the specific needs. The one year period, in addition to ADA’s experience in Fangak, Ayod, Koch and Mayendit counties and involvement of other partners, will help the team to map out others service providers, ensuring that all the gaps identified are reported to specific partners for further intervention as may be required. In addition, ADA will apply its funding strategy to solicit for additional emergency donor resources for these locations to ensure that vulnerable people’s need are met. The resilience capacity skills training, aiming on HLP, PSEA, GBV and Protection related issues will provide essential-long-lasting community skills in handling future S/NFI concerns

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ADA will conduct intensive mobilization and sensitization to reach out information to all intended communities to ensure members of the most vulnerable community are included in consultations and/or humanitarian services/assistance. Use various channels of communication including through churches, markets, schools, hospitals, local authorities to ensure all members of the community in all project locations receives or hears the information. The project will establish a community committee comprising of all categories of affected persons including the disable, lactating and breastfeeding mothers, elderly, youths, women and girls to ensure the most vulnerable beneficiaries are identified, selected and registered into the project to access the intended assistance. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas would require discussions on land tenure system with local authorities and sensitization of the affected community to ensure affected persons land rights are not violated or neglected and they have land ownership or title deed rights and have access to land for farming and settlement. ADA project team with support from HLP Agency such as NRC will engage and advocate through the local authorities for beneficiaries rights access to land for settlement and farming or rearing animals. ADA will hold consultations with the displaced women and girls to thoroughly assess their priority needs and provide timely resources and mental support to reduce their vulnerability to exploitation and abuse by those with power or in possession of assets. Besides, ADA will provide better channel of communication on access to information about food, protection/GBV survival supplies, medical services, water, education and shelter.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ADA will use standard s/NFI Cluster Guidelines on handling all the other aspects of S/NFI implementation, including setting up community complaints and feedback mechanisms. These are contained in the Cluster AAP, CCE, Resilience guidelines. ADA will use the Guidelines to gather important positive and possibly negative feedbacks that the community will have in the course of the S/NFI implementation process. ADA will form community committees to oversee the project implementation. Training of youth on protection concerns will follow. Women will participation in all aspects of the project. ADA will strengthen the communication mechanisms within the communities through the formation of community mobilisers who clearly understand their community dynamics. These youths will receive training on key messages on S/NFI best practices. This will ensure messages and information reaches and cover all the affected population. ADA will ensure the protection staffs are included in the joint mission to monitor, evaluate any protection concerns during distribution. The distribution point selection will be done through a series of consultations with the local community, to ensure the principle of “Do No Harm” to the affected population is upheld. During the need analysis and responses, messages on use of items will be demonstrated to the beneficiary to ensure safe and proper use. ADA will ensure distribution sites are safe, the distributions are done and completed early to allow beneficiaries travel home before darkness. During shelter and NFIs distribution, ADA will consult with local authority to understand, whether the beneficiary are entitled to land with special documentation, HLP concerns/issues will be mitigated. The project staff will have a copy and strictly follow the cluster beneficiary selection criteria/guideline to ensure that all “Do No Harm” principles are adhered to, besides HLP :” Key Housing, Land and Property (HLP) issues in Urban areas of South Sudan” – 2017
