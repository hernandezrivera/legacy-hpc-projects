Project Summary

According to South Sudan Technical working group message, food and nutrition security in South Sudan has been persistently acute and on the increase through time, with over 6 million people food insecure at the peak of the lean season. According to the South Sudan Acute Food Insecurity Situation for October - November 2020 and Projections for December 2020 - March 2021 and April - July 2021, the food and nutrition insecurity have been driven by a number of shocks ranging from economic disasters such as the floods of 2020 that affected nearly 1 million people, insecurity, lack of basic social services and infrastructure mainly emanating from years of conflict. In 2020, Jonglei has experienced compounded shocks across most of the counties which include: an intensification of conflict that resulted in loss of life and assets, displacement, disruption and destruction of livelihoods; a second consecutive year of exceptional 40 – 50 year high floods that submerged human settlements, farmlands and pasturelands as well as disrupted markets and delivery of humanitarian assistance to the flood-affected populations. Between December 2020 and March 2021, an estimated 1.55 million people (78.3% of the population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. During this period, the entire State will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, except for Pochalla County that will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Admissions to Tearfund Feeding Centres in all the three Counties (Pochalla, Uror, Twic East) continue to rise even in the postharvest season when typically, malnutrition rates dip as the dry season brings improved access for nutrition and health services as well as humanitarian assistance, and a reduction in morbidity and outbreaks of disease. The high levels of food insecurity seen among children under 5 in the targeted areas can be partially attributed to the scarcity of food and the consequent lack of variety in diet. This is further compounded by the lack of knowledge of minimum dietary diversity required.
Tearfund is the sole organization supporting nutrition interventions in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla. Tearfund is currently responding to the crisis in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla, supporting 26 Feeding Centres and 5 Stabilization Centres. This project will continue to address high rates of GAM through the provision of Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) services, thus alleviating malnutrition and reducing susceptibility to other health issues, including water-borne diseases. As children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women and girls are the most vulnerable to MAM and SAM, these individuals will be the primary target group for this intervention. A total of 6,779 children under 5 are targeted for treatment of SAM (1,354 Pochalla, 1,100 Twic East, 4,325 Uror) and 18,924 children are targeted for treatment of MAM (2,703 Pochalla, 5,451 Twic East, 10,770 Uror). Likewise, a total 15,132 PLWs (3060 Pochalla, 2460 Twic East, 9612 Uror) are targeted for MAM services in 2021.
Tearfund’s MIYCN component will address the issue of malnutrition by delivering key messages on maternal and child feeding and hygienic practices targeting 36, 569 PLWs to reach through individual and group counseling sessions being run by community nutrition workers and IYCF counselors. Furthermore, Tearfund’s wider nutrition programme integrates WASH, GBV and FSL components, including training on kitchen gardening, which increases access to more diverse food items. Without this response, GAM rates would inevitably rise and morbidity and mortality would increase.

1 - Needs

The year of 2020 has been characterized by several emerging and anticipated shocks that have impacted hugely on the nutrition situation of children as well as nutrition service delivery. The COVID-19 pandemic, floods in Jonglei and heightened inter-communal conflict were shocks that tremendously impacted access to nutrition services in the country. COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates, as well as changes in SAM and MAM admission criteria for children have exacerbated access to services alongside other factors such as heightened inter-communal conflict and insecurity, worsening economic crises, flooding, among others. Abnormally heavy rainfall from July to Dec 2020 has led to overflows of the rivers Nile and other rivers as well as inland flooding, mainly in the eastern parts of South Sudan. This has caused large-scale displacement of people, some of whom moved with their cattle. Crop and property damage are widespread in the affected areas. Jonglei has been the worst affected (over 400,000 people affected) according to a South Sudan flooding situation report. About 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 based on the same season historical data of food security and nutrition monitoring system, SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020. According to IPC acute malnutrition situation for Nov 202-March 2021, 82% of the Counties in Jonglei are in IPC Acute Malnutrition phase 4. Compared to the same season of 2019, the situation in 2020 shows a worsening nutrition situation.The high rates of malnutrition in Jonglei state, specifically in Tearfund operational areas, are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the counties. Furthermore, poor access to nutrition services in all the three locations (Pochalla, Twic East and Uror) during the rainy season due to insecurity and poor road infrastructure have significantly affected service delivery in areas with a critical malnutrition situation. According to the Nutrition cluster, the proxy GAM rate in these locations in Jan 2021 are as follows: Pochalla (15.65%), Twic East (13.83%) and Uror (21.76%). As of August 2020, all the people living in villages in Twic East in Jonglei-one of the Counties hardest hit by flooding, had been displaced. The majority of people left for Bor and Juba but a few stayed and sought shelter in Panyagor Town. Following this, Tearfund scaled up Nutrition treatment and emergency prevention services in the County, with increased engagement of community nutrition volunteers in screening for acute malnutrition in children and pregnant women. In 2020 Tearfund had managed 5070 Under five children in OTP, 15,236 in TSFP and 6719 malnourished PLWs in its 27 Nutrition feeding centers in three Counties. Of the total admitted children, 93% were discharged cured in both programs. However, there are still huge gaps in terms of scaling up and expansion, integration with non-nutrition sectors such as FSL, WASH and Health and Maternal, infant and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices that are believed to be addressed in 2021.

2 - Project activities

In 2021, the project will focus on provision of quality preventative nutrition-specific services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and adolescents in Twic East, Pochalla and Uror Counties. Activities will include: 1) Community mobilization, training of CNVs, active case finding, mass screening campaigns, TSFP services, and provision of deworming tablets and vitamin A supplements to <5 yr Children and PLWs 2) MIYCN: PLWs attending the feeding centres and within the community will be targeted for individual and group counselling (mother to mother support groups) on IYCF practices, following the UNICEF recommended approach. The project will also seek to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in the 3 Counties. Activities will include: 1) treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) among children 6-59 months. All children with a MUAC of <11.5 cm will be admitted into the OTP and treated with Ready to Use therapeutic food (RUTF) and routine medicines according to the South Sudan CMAM guideline. 2) Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months, children meeting the criteria (MUAC >=11.5 cm - 12.5 cm) will be admitted in TSFP and treated with Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) and pregnant and lactating mothers who meet the criteria (MUAC <23 cm) will be admitted and treated with super cereal plus (CSB++). 3) Referral of SAM with medical Complications and poor appetite test to SCs. All discharges will be through OTP where they will continue with treatment and follow-up. 4) Provision of quality MIYCN and micronutrient supplementation (Vit A and deworming) services in nutrition sites and community level. Admission and Treatment MAM PLW: Mothers of children less than 6 months or less than 4kgs who are not able to produce enough breast milk will be admitted to the TSFP for PLW. In order to increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, ECD and Protection sectors, the project will entail: 1) Malaria Testing - All children admitted into the OTP programme will be treated for malaria and referred accordingly. 2) Child Friendly Spaces - Tearfund will integrate child friendly spaces into the Feeding Centres, ensuring that Early Childhood Development activities are integrated and involving mothers enrolled in the IYCF programme. 3) Hygiene Promotion - nutrition staff will engage those attending to health facilities in order to deliver targeted health, nutrition and hygiene promotion messages. Tearfund will disseminate hygiene promotion messages, while providing complementary hygiene kits. 4) Tearfund will establish Kitchen gardens in all Feeding centers and conduct food cooking demonstrations for mothers with under five years old children. 5) Tearfund will facilitate GBV safety audits in the nutrition sites. Issues arising from the safety audits will inform the development of targeted actions to respond to safety issues around our nutrition sites and also trigger community dialogues on gender, protection and disability inclusion in target communities. In order to strengthen the health and nutrition systems, the project will: 1) train health and nutrition workers on management of acute malnutrition and maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices (MIYCN). 2) Monitor availability of core pipeline supplies at nutrition sites: Tearfund has an existing agreement with UNICEF and WFP for supply of RUTF and RUSF/CSB++ in three month lots. 3) Conduct nutrition SMART surveys in prioritized counties based on priority lists shared by cluster.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Tearfund will strengthen staff capacity on PSEA and will share with beneficiaries information about protection from sexual exploitation and abuse during all project activities. In targeted locations, Tearfund will work with GBV actors and community based protection actors to spread awareness about the GBV referral pathway including a range of services for survivors. Tearfund will collaborate with protection cluster to provide information that seeks to prevent and respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against women, men, boys and girls, and ensure that a comprehensive complaint mechanism is in place and sagefuarding reports and concerns are investigated. Tearfund will develop Information Education Materials (IEC) and disseminate information to the beneficiaries at the host communities/IDPs/Returnees on the available service providers, locations and services available. The IEC materials will also convey knowledge and skills to young men to adopt gender equitable behavior.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Carrying out need assessment will help us understand Information sharing and feedback mechanisms on Nutrition services in order to adapt information to the literacy levels amongst specific vulnerable groups and avoid excluding vulnerable groups. As part of the program design, risk assessment will be conducted to ensure Tearfund work does not cause harm to vulnerable people. The information tools will be contextualized and explained to generate understanding and be relevant. The project will disaggregate data to match the project affected population or sub-groups where practical actions will be embedded into planning and delivery to ensure inclusivity and needs of all gender groups. The engagement of the vulnerable groups will help us to understand expectations, involve them in the decision making process and enable us to receive timely feedback. The steps will help address the needs of the most vulnerable in reducing inequalities and help everyone benefit equally from the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To support better performance and Nutrition outcomes at the community, Tearfund and partners will ground their response in the needs, priorities and expectations of the beneficiaries through a number of approaches for example, by engaging in dialogue and joint analysis with beneficiaries on feedback and complaint mechanisms to empower the communities in ways to communicate their needs and concerns and provide the space to make decisions that affect them to enable beneficiaries to act on their own independently. At Nutrition centres Tearfund will use notice boards with details about tearfund, contact persons, names and numbers of community representatives in committees, a summary of the project and a map of the project area. Communities will be included in the leadership of response activities in designing distribution processes and community participation in monitoring and evaluating response programs. Complaint help desks will help in providing basic assistance, psychosocial support and counselling to returnees/IDPs/ host communities through the use of community volunteers at the distribution points and community centres and sites so that the communities can provide regular feedback. Additionally, referral pathways will provide the communities with information on the existing service providers, their services and locations and how to access them.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Tearfund will gather data about communications channels in communicating with communities and consider how their rights and entitlements in order to identify feedback mechanisms to be adapted in emergencies. Tearfund will analyse Nutrition information provided by the returnees/IDPs and host communities through the use of field staff and volunteers to triangulate the information analysis for Nutrition service improvement. Tearfund will look at other types of information such as, life saving information, where people can access and support nutrition services, people’s rights and entitlements, sexual exploitation and abuse to the communities, security risks, corruption etc to provide adjustments if necessary throughout the project. Information on people’s rights will take into consideration international humanitarian standards (Sphere, HAP, IASC GBV guidelines, Red cross code of conduct) in the provision of impartial and timely access to services and right to protection. Tearfund will ensure beneficiaries participate in decision-making concerning activities and to lead or support the implementation of activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To propagate the centrality of protection, Tearfund will train staff and stakeholders to integrate sexual gender based violence, child protection and general protection and social inclusion issues in the planning, implementation, monitoring and review of nutrition activities. To ensure women, girls, boys and men are protected from project related protection risks, Tearfund will train nutrition staff in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse including psychological first aid. Staff will receive training on safeguarding and child protection as part of enhancing our accountability to ensure that our programs and operations do no harm. Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse messaging will be integrated in community meetings including OTP and feeding centre activities. Tearfund will forge linkages with protection actors in project sites to promote referrals to survivor centered GBV and child safeguarding services. Tearfund will use the safety audit findings to determine the timing, venue and location of nutrition activities to minimize safety risks that women, girls, boys and men face when they participate in nutrition activities. All nutrition activities will be conducted in locations with safe, accessible, private, and gender-sensitive institutional latrines, handwashing facilities, and bathing facilities to promote greater safety of women and men, girls and boys.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Tearfund will use an inclusive approach in the implementation of the project. The beneficiaries of the project will be the most vulnerable people, those falling in category 3,4 and 5, of the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC). Beneficiaries include Internally Displaced Population (IPD), host communities, refugees, and community vulnerable groups’ representatives (female headed household, households with malnourished children, single mothers, widows, elders, handicapped or chronically ill, IDP, refugee, youth at risk). The emergence of livelihood support will enhance food production which will help to address food insecurity which exacerbates GBV issues encountered and psychosocial stress due to food insecurity.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Tearfund will apply an integrated approach through provision of WASH NFIs for households with SAM cases and information on improved hygiene and sanitation practices for mothers. Additionally, pregnant women will be encouraged to first attend Ante-Natal Care (ANC) where they will receive comprehensive ANC care before they are given food supplements. Lactating women with children less than 6 months with a MUAC less than 23cms will be attended through (Maternal and Child Health (MCH)/post-natal where their children will receive immunization (BCG, Polio and DPT vaccines). Women treated through the programme will be discharged when their MUAC reaches 23cm. To support the adoption of hygiene practices and prevent the spreading of illnesses such as malaria and diarrhoea, there will be provision of hygiene kits including soap upon enrolment in the programme. Nutrition projects will also be integrated with other emergency projects such as food security and livelihood and early child development sectors. Many of the nutrition beneficiaries will also be eligible for inclusion for Tearfund’s FSL interventions, which aim to reduce the incidence of food insecurity and increase food availability and diversity at the household level. Beneficiaries of these projects receive vegetable kits or fishing kits, as well as training, to enable them to increase their access to nutritious and varied diets, which supports the implementation of improved feeding practices, as promoted through the mother to mother support groups. Tearfund is also planning to establish and strengthen existing kitchen gardens in 17 feeding centers where food preparation and demonstration take place. Providing safe access to potable drinking water and sanitation facilities including good hygiene practices, will improve the quality of food being consumed at household level, it as well reduces the prevalence of the acute watery diarrhea case in Children. It gives the mother and caregivers an opportunity to spend more time doing food security and livelihood activities than looking for water. Thus lowers water related diseases, improves nutrition and decreases the IPC level.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will include all groups and gender without total discrimination. Men, women, girls and boys will be included in all training, AAP committee and membership will include all categories of vulnerable people. With the understanding of the project location’s gender roles that put women at the forefront of fending for their families as most men are either in the army or are not participating in any economic activity, therefore is the role of the women to provide food on the table for the children, Tearfund will ensure that more women than men benefit from the project activities including other vulnerable groups.Tearfund approach embedded in the equity and inclusion as well as Accountability to the affected population WASH Cluster approach, Thus consultation and beneficiaries on the design, accessibility and locations for the implementation of the activities. borehole platforms and latrines design include access ramp for the disable.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Tearfund will continue providing TSFP alongside OTP services to children under five and PLWs through 27 feeding centers and five stabilization centres in three Counties. Based on the South Sudan CMAM guideline, Tearfund will continue treating children under 5 years with supplementary Plumpy sup (RUSF) and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) with fortified blended food (CSB++) to improve their nutritional status in all these locations. Tearfund will also rehabilitate feeding centers which were critically affected by the ongoing flooding in the three locations by reinforcing and ensuring that quality programming can continue. Both Nutrition and MIYCN staff will receive basic and refresher training and efforts will be made to equip them with necessary skills and knowledge. Existing groups and volunteers will provide mothers a regular counselling on topics including early initiation of breastfeeding and appropriate feeding practices. This will be conducted both on an individual level at the feeding centres, as well as through mother-to-mother support groups that have been formed and are already actively working. While the individual approach ensures that counselling can be more focussed to specific needs, the group approach will encourage the knowledge gained to be disseminated throughout the community. All children discharged from OTP will be admitted into the TSFP and will remain in the programme for at least one month, after which time they are discharged home. Follow-up visits will be conducted by the Community Nutrition Volunteers and the child’s weight gain is checked through bi-weekly follow-ups at the feeding center by the OTP/community Nutrition mobilizer. If a child has not gained weight for two subsequent visits, the child will be earmarked for intensive home visit support and possibly additional TSFP support.As poor access to WASH services have a high impact on the health of children, resulting in high malnutrition rate, providing safe drinking water to the community, dissemination of safe hygiene and sanitation practices to the mother/care givers will automatically reduce malnutrition and improve the health of children.
