Project Summary

The project will contribute at providing timely, equitable and dignified access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services to 25,000 (women, men, girls and boys) in affected populations in Kapoeta South, Torit and Ikotos counties of Eastern Equatoria State. HCAWFO is responding in Eastern Equatoria State to the ongoing humanitarian crisis since the onset of the conﬂict in the country with support from UNICEF, SNV and NIRAS, Some of the Host community, IDPs and returnees were reached with basic WASH services. The aim of this project is to ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in Kapoeta South, Ikotos and Torit Counties. While addressing the following specific objectives: a) To provide equitable access to quality and inclusive cross-sectoral basic services, including safe water, sanitation and hygiene promotion, education and health is extended to women, children, older persons and persons with disability in priority areas. b) To enhance access to quality, timely and inclusive protection services including medical care, legal support, safety and psychosocial services to children and women at risk, GBV survivors.
HCAWFO's response strategies will include support to WASH Cluster response in counties of critical malnutrition in Kapoeta South, Torit and Ikotos by ensuring provision of a minimum standard of improved basic WASH services in the areas. The activities include; comprehensive rehabilitation of hand pumps, distribution of WASH NFIs, improving latrines, and hygiene promotion campaigns both at institutional and community levels. HCAWFO will ensure that WASH services, speciﬁcally in regards to latrine construction, incorporate the minimum safety and privacy considerations with use of the WASH Cluster latrine checklist, developed with the GBV Sub-Cluster. HCAWFO will work with WASH Cluster and NFI, Logistic and other clusters to ensure adequate supplies are strategically prepositioned in the ﬁeld to respond to new displacement and potential disease outbreaks through the 2020 rainy season. HCAWFO will maintain its active participation in the WASH Cluster meetings and coordination both at the state and National levels where ideas and project updates are shared and monthly WASH reports are submitted. HCAWFO will encourage active participation of the local community including women and children in the implementation of the proposed project. Through Consultation of local authorities at all levels, women, men, boys and girls in the selection of the project Counties within the county based on the identiﬁed needs. Hygiene promoters including hand pump technicians will include both host community and IDPs men, women, boys and girls in the targeted project sites in the county. The local authorities, host community and IDPs men, women, girls and boys will be involved in the design of the community and institutional sanitation facilities putting into consideration the need for privacy for females for the case of latrines and bath rooms’ construction. To this effect, HCAWFO is currently delivering sustainable WASH services in Hai Miaro one of the hot spots for cholera outbreak and flooding areas in Torit since 2016 to date. The key activities being delivered and HCAWFO would like to continue provide hygiene promotion, solid waste management, improving emergency latrines and bathrooms to semi-permanent structures, and improving drainage at the water and sanitation facilities and provision of safe clean drinking water. This project will build on the ongoing activities to ensure sustainability.

1 - Needs

The conﬂict that erupted in 2016 affected communities in Eastern Equatoria State. Kapoeta South, Torit and Ikotos counties have remained consistently below minimum sphere standards in WASH infrastructure and services due to the conﬂicts, leaving vulnerable communities with little resilience to withstand the chronic and acute crises that has affected the country since December 2013. The conﬂicts have forced thousands to ﬂee their homes and seek relative safety in various locations including UNMISS premises in South Sudan and as conﬂict becomes protracted, host communities remain vulnerable with limited coping abilities to withstand the shocks. Among the states directly and badly affected by the crisis included Eastern Equatoria State. Different groups of people left their places at different times since the beginning of the crisis, to present. With the recent signing of revitalized peace agreement between the government forces and the SPLM-IO groups and with the formation of New Government of National Unity in November 2019, it is likely that there will be a continued inﬂux of IDPs/returnee in targeted locations. The displaced persons who are mainly women, children and people with disabilities in these counties remain vulnerable to high level of contracting water and sanitation related sicknesses. Kapoeta South, Torit and Ikotos are some the counties in Eastern Equatoria State that had inadequate WASH services even before the outbreak of the conﬂict in the State, and the situations has worsened with the ongoing conﬂict in the country as most WASH infrastructures have been impaired especially the community hand pumps and sanitation facilities both at the household and public places like health facilities, schools and the local authorities in the area have estimated that only 20% to 25% of the community hand pumps are functional. Poor and bad sanitation and hygiene practices among the IDPs and the host community in these counties poses biggest challenges as nothing is being done to equip the IDPs and the host community with basic knowledge of good hygiene practices. Torit, Kapoeta South and Ikotos are hosting a number of displaced people from other neighboring counties due to the conﬂict, majority of which have been absorbed by the host community. The situation has made the few functional community hand pumps and sanitation facilities being extremely over stressed and most of them are broken down or damaged. This has posed a big threat to health of the both IDPs and the host community members within Kapoeta South, Torit and Ikotos counties. According to the SS 2019 HNO, in 2018, women, men and children were pushed into much deeper crisis due to their inability to receive lifesaving support. Due to the crisis, GBV cases including sexual violence is not reported because of stigma, shame and lack of conﬁdence in the rule of law ensuring justice to the victims, about 2,300 cases of all forms of GBV reported by mid-2018 and yet this represent a small percentage of issues of GBV . Very low per cent of the population have access to a borehole or water yard within 30 minutes’ walking distance and mostly face with protection problems. Majority of the population access water from unprotected sources. More than 90 percent of the population practice open defecation due to lack of sanitation facilities and they lack NFIs. The conﬂict has continued to drive people’s WASH needs, from blocking or destruction of WASH infrastructure to limiting WASH commodities. Insecurity at times prevents humanitarian actors from accessing areas that have high WASH needs.

2 - Activities

WASH activities for IDPs/host communities and returneesa) Identify safety and security risks for women and girls that are relevant to water and sanitation systems to ensure the location, design, and maintenance programmes maximize safety and security of women and girls. b) Mobilize women and men to participate in the location, design and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities. Ensure all users, and particularly women and girls, participate in identifying risky hygiene practices and conditions, and that all users share responsibility to measurably reduce these risks. Ensure that women comprise 50 percent of water and/or sanitation committees.c) Locate water points in areas that are accessible and safe for all, with special attention to the needs of women and children. d) Design communal bathing and washing facilities in consultation with women and girls to ensure that users have privacy and maintain dignity. Determine numbers, location, design, safety, appropriateness and convenience of facilities in consultation with the users, particularly women and adolescent girls (including whether facilities for males and females should be near each other or further apart). e) Design latrines in consultation with women and girls to maximise safety, privacy and dignity. f) Distribute suitable materials for the absorption and disposal of menstrual blood for women and girls who menstruate. Provide individual sanitary packs for all women and girls from at least 13 to 49 years. g) Inform women and men about the maintenance and use of water and sanitation facilities. Women and men should be fully informed of how to repair facilities, and how to make and/or where to find spare parts. Determine timings of information sessions in consultation with the intended users, particularly women, so as not to conflict with their other responsibilities. h) Maintain awareness of involvement of women and men in hygiene promotional activities and ensure continuous sex balance on committees and among hygiene promoters. Ensure that women and men have equitable influence in hygiene promotional activities and that any benefits or incentives are distributed equally among women and men.Water Supply: Design of suitable water supply systems in line with ground water and surface water treatment; Rehabilitation of boreholes and upgrading existence boreholes with a submersible pumps; Continued operation and maintenance of emergency water yards, supplying chlorinated water.Sanitation: Construction of segregated communal latrine facilities and household latrines segregated by male and female cubicles, utilizing casual labour balanced between the IDP population and host community; Development of suitable operation and maintenance mechanisms for communal latrines and household latrines; PHP Volunteers to ensure that water and soap is always available for hand washing in every latrine, latrines are kept clean and covered on a weekly basis; Through a low subsidy approach (subsidy will initially include the distribution of digging tools and plastic sheeting) households will be encouraged to construct household latrines and household bathing units.Public Health Promotion: Public health promotion will be continued by IDPs community volunteers trained; The volunteers will be utilized as part of this project for the on-going dissemination of public health messages which could be achieved; The HCAWFO PHP team will also aim to carry out large scale promotion activities; The public health promotion team is also responsible for development and monitoring the key WASH related risks in the IDP camps; In order to allow IDPs to maintain good personal hygiene there will be monthly distributions of soap (for both hand washing and laundry)
