Project Summary

The COVID-19 contingency fund will strengthen systems and the capacity of government and communities to respond to and recover from the impact of COVID-19 and build resilience to future shocks. This 18-month programme will support 1,000 households, 500 farmers, 400 youths and 40 Savings and Loans Groups (SLGs) in Yobe State. The proposed €1,000,000 programme will build the absorptive and adaptive resilience capacities of 2,900 community members (725 men, 725 women, 725 male youth, and 725 female youth) whilst engaging with communities and local leadership to create the conditions to facilitate systemic change. Mercy Corps will leverage its expertise in fostering resilience in complex environments and strong track record of relief and early recovery programmes in Yobe State, enabling a smooth and rapid start-up to implement the COVID-19 response programme.

1 - Needs

Social Protection Systems A 2016 Mercy Corps study (Borno Strategic Resilience Assessment, 2018) found that government credibility in North East Nigeria was undermined by the lack of state provided basic services, causing grievances that led to community acceptance of violent extremist groups. Transitioning from humanitarian aid to state-led safety net programmes will be of critical importance to building trust and maintaining peace in Yobe, as well as building the capacity of government in agricultural development and youth empowerment to respond to challenges as a result of the impact of COVID-19 in Yobe.Multipurpose Cash Assistance: Mercy Corps will scale up the current cash transfer programme to reach more beneficiaries with conditional cash transfers (households and youth – vocational school graduates) to boost income generation and scale-up business opportunities for households and include those who have lost livelihoods, female-headed households and youth-headed households. The provision of multipurpose grants will help prevent assets’ depletion, meet basic needs, address immediate barriers to individual participation in the local economy, support coping mechanisms, improve market systems over time, and jump-start stalled markets in the wake of the COVID-19 crisis. Target programme participant’s data will be generated from the Yobe State Single Registry through the existing collaboration with YESSO/ State Operation Coordination Unit or SOCU and the Ministry of Budget and Planning.Most of the population rely on cropping and livestock for subsistence and income, with more that 80% engaged in small subsistence farming. Mercy Corps will scale-up its ongoing economic recovery effort by supporting farmers with agricultural inputs (especially improved local seeds, tools and seedlings), locally made food storage bags and distribution of small animals such as goat and sheep. The programme will mainstream smart climate agricultural practices while putting emphasis on locally effective and affordable climate change adaptation technologies and capacity building of vulnerable households and groups to increase their agricultural production and attain food self-sufficiency. Under the BRICC programme, Mercy Corps has established 240 savings groups across 30 communities, and lessons have shown that the concept has been widely accepted by community members. Mercy Corps will scale up the number of savings groups with additional 40 groups to further create access to credit, linkages to financial services and promote community cohesion among members. Mainstream Protection, Nutrition and Risk Communication Appropriate strategies will be designed to ensure that women participate and have a voice in how income is used, specifically for nutrition purposes. In addition, gender balance in decision-making will be promoted to ensure that all household members understand the benefits of growing and purchasing nutritious foods. The programme will explore possibilities of establishing and or supporting networks for women. These networks will assist in designing and disseminating technologies that save women time and labor and in navigating possible behavioral changes. The networks will also be used as avenues to promote women engagement in value chain activities including possibilities of home garden production and start-up of small scale at home food processing. This activity will be linked and integrated with activities in Outcome 3. Mercy Corps will engage community-based protection monitors who will be linked to various government institutions and referral mechanisms. Protection monitors will be critical in ensuring issues affecting different groups and mitigation strategies are monitored and shared. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Appropriate strategies will be designed to ensure that women participate and have a voice in how income is used, specifically for nutrition purposes. In addition, gender balance in decision-making will be promoted to ensure that all household members understand the benefits of growing and purchasing nutritious foods. The programme will explore possibilities of establishing and or supporting networks for women. These networks will assist in designing and disseminating technologies that save women time and labor and in navigating possible behavioral changes. The networks will also be used as avenues to promote women engagement in value chain activities including possibilities of home garden production and start-up of small scale at home food processing. This activity will be linked and integrated with activities in Outcome 3. Mercy Corps will engage community-based protection monitors who will be linked to various government institutions and referral mechanisms. Protection monitors will be critical in ensuring issues affecting different groups and mitigation strategies are monitored and shared. COVID-19 has affected various community social networks and support mechanisms. Cases of security forces brutality have been on the rise and exclusion of certain populations to access services due to misinformation of the public has led to certain populations being targeted. Mercy Corps will roll out community-based protection and reporting mechanisms to promote community accountability to men, women, boys and girls. The issues to be monitored will include cases related to Gender-Based Violence (GBV), social cohesion and child protection for instance, engaging in negative coping mechanisms; access to public health information and medical services (prevention, testing, and treatment); access to other essential services (WASH, shelter, food security and livelihood). Mercy Corps will work with local authorities, community and religious leaders to addressing social discrimination of individuals or groups suspected to be infected by Covid-19 and build responsive approaches to Communicating with Communities. Mercy Corps will work to create awareness on protection risks, including GBV, child abuse, among others. Through this, Mercy Corps will create awareness of available support services and work to strengthen local level referral mechanisms and access to social services for men, women, boys and girls. Mercy Corps will focus on working with communities to carry out community-led activities to end or reduce discrimination and stigmatization, promote safe coping mechanisms, support affected populations and link them to existing services, such as cash assistance for those who lost livelihoods and orphaned children. Mercy Corps will also work with government institutions to strengthen and/or establish safety nets, including identification and registration process for vulnerable groups, especially women and children.
