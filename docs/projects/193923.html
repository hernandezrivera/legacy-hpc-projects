Project Summary

2022 has been the deadliest year since 2006, on a monthly average, for Palestinians residing in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. At least 105 Palestinians, including 26 children, have been killed by Israeli forces.
Child protection concerns are on the increase in both the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. Children living near settlements or Israeli military checkpoints are at constant risk of military and settler violence and intimidation. Save the Children (SC) is proposing an emergency integrated protection and MHPSS response to mitigate the impact of conflict, trauma, and child rights violations including, Gender Based Violence (GBV), on boys’, girls’ and caregivers’ mental health and psychosocial wellbeing, through ensuring access to a continuum of MHPSS and protection services. In partnership with Ma’an Development Centre in Gaza and the Young Men Christian Association (YMCA) in the West Bank, the project will provide holistic support to 5,000 individuals, of which 4,000 are children protracted conflict and violence in Gaza, demolitions and forced evictions in Massafer Yatta, and military detention in the West Bank.
An estimated 5% of targeted beneficiaries are people and children with disabilities, and 50% are women and girls.
SC and its partners will deliver age sensitive , and gender responsive, inclusive services to address the complex vulnerabilities of the impacted communities. These services include comprehensive GBV prevention and risk mitigation , child protection case management and individualized intervention plans, management and individual and group counselling. The services will also include, depending on the needs of the boys, girls, men and women, other structured MHPSS support, recreational activities, referral to specialized mental health and protection services, cash for protection, individual and group counselling for caregivers, including positive parenting activities.
Specifically, iIn Gaza, immediate protection and psychological assistance will be delivered through the Governorate Protection Focal Points (GPFP), the tried-and-tested child protection and psycho-social emergency response system established in Gaza. We SC will use Child Friendly Space/CFSs as an entry-point for MHPSS interventions, including identification and referral of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) children with protection concern and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). In addition, the project will provide child protection case management and PSS support to children affected by violence and their caregivers, including cash for protection. In the West Bank, the project will provide child protection and MHPSS interventions, including cash for protection to eligible households, to children and their caregivers affected by occupation violence. Through these interventions, the project aims to enhance the wellbeing, resilience, and recovery of girls and boys in the West Bank and Gaza, as well as their caregivers, who have experienced occupation violence, displacement and rights violations. The project will draw on SC’s evidenced based approaches and expertise including structured home-based MHPSS intervention for children affected by conflict, group MHPSS programmes, positive parenting sessions and SC’s child resilience programme. The project will strengthen parental care and support and build upon the capacity of local organizations with extensive experience delivering protection services in the target communities.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in the oPt has continued to rapidly deteriorate in 2022 due to COVID-19, escalation of hostilities in both Gaza and West Bank and the increase in energy and food prices due to the Ukraine war. The protracted protection crisis in the oPt means that around 1.8 million Palestinians across the oPt 64 per cent in the Gaza Strip and 36 per cent in the West Bank, experience or are at risk of conflict, violence and displacement, including forcible transfer. Women and children constitute 49% and 37% of the affected people respectively. The protracted conflict, economic stagnation and restricted access to basic services and resources, coupled with high unemployment and poverty rates, is increasing the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance. In addition, and since October 2021, we have witnessed the continued shrieking of the humanitarian and civil space in the oPt, with the designation and raiding of 7 NGOs, many playing a critical role in delivering critical humanitarian assistance but also monitoring and verifying human rights violations. Humanitarian staff are harassed, intimidated, and detained during humanitarian responses in hot spot areas. One of the best examples is the Massafer Yatta and Ras al Tin areas. Protection related incidents are on the rise; there have been more demolitions of schools and houses, forced displacement, search and arrest operations and more killings of Palestinians than in 2021, including children. Every year, approximately 500–700 Palestinian children come into contact with the Israeli military detention system. In study conducted by SC on the Impact of Military Detention on children’s wellbeing, surveyed children reported they had endured a distressing or violent arrest or detention in most cases at night, a coercive interrogation environment, physical and emotional abuse in detention and a denial of essential services, including legal counsel. In education incidents alone, the mid-year 2022 report revealed a significant increase in the incidents targeting schools and students compared to the first halves of 2021 and 2020, with 115 incidents, affecting around 7,600 students, of which 2,767 were girls. Women and girls in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) are facing increasing and disproportionate impacts of the worsening humanitarian and security situation, which is resulting in an increase of civilian casualties and protection risks, deprivation of freedom of movement and forced displacement. oPt protection analysis conducted by the protection cluster highlights that women and girls feel unsafe due to the high levels of GBV, and adolescent girls in particular are at high risk of early marriage and school dropout. The Multi- Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022, also showed that the on their way to school is where women and girls feel the most insecure in Gaza and around checkpoints and settlements in the West Bank. Particularly, women and girls with disabilities, Bedouin women, internally displaced women and women and girls living in refugee camps are particularly at risk of violence. Gender Based Violence (GBV) continues to be a key protection concern in Palestine given existing gender and social norms that normalize violence, violence that increases during escalations and continues to be under reported. A 2019 survey by the Palestinian Centre Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) found that 27% of women who are currently married experienced at least one form of violence from their husbands within a 12-month period. Psychological violence was the highest at 52%, economic 41%, social 33%, physical 17%, and sexual 7%. Despite 40% of survivors reporting being aware of the existence of services, only 5% sought formal help. The situation in Nablus, Jenin and East Jerusalem is of particular concern for children and families; Israeli forces have imposed extensive movement restrictions in these areas as of September 2022, limiting access of many to health care, education, and livelihoods. In the midst of the olive harvest, we are also seeing an increase in the severity and frequency of settler violence. According to OCHA September report, the monthly average of settler violence against Palestinians is at its highest since 2005 with a 34% increase in number of incidents compared to 2021 and an 83% increase since 2020. Settler expansion is on the rise as well as punitive demolitions and coercive measures, particularly in Area C and notably in Massaffer Yatta, where the risk of forcible transfer is at its highest. The result of these incidents has severely deteriorated boys, girls, men and women mental health and psychosocial well-being, as well as their protection from violence, neglect or abuse. The MSNA 2022 (May) revealed that around 40% of households in Gaza and 11.9% in West Bank report that at least one household member shows signs of psychological distress or trauma. The % is higher in targeted areas such as Rafah or Khan Younis in Gaza, Hebron, Nablus, East Jerusalem or in general, in area C of the West Bank. However, these figures are likely higher now with the deterioration of the situation in Jenin, Nablus and EJ. SC “Trapped” report similarly revealed that children in Gaza are experiencing higher levels of emotional distress in 2022 than back in 2018 (on average an increase from 55% to 80%). Overall and across the territories, there continues to be a lack of specialized mental health services. Further to this, service providers, particularly in Gaza, are overstretched and underfunded which limits their ability to provide timely, quality and specialized services to boys, girls, men and women, particularly during escalations, when there is a need to quickly scale up. The proposed action seeks to address critical protection needs in localities which are prioritized by the protection cluster which includes Rafah, Khanyounis, Gaza, Middle and North Area of Gaza and Jenin and Nablus of WB.

2 - Activities

1)Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) and MHPSS home based for 420 emergency incidents(210 Girls): emergency assessment visits will be conducted for affected HH in response to emergency incidents, develop rapid detailed MHPSS needs assessment, and based on the results, identified children will be engaged in structured interventions. OCHA alert system in both Gaza and WB and partners outreach work -including referrals from protection committees, CPNs and other organizations- will be a key entry point to identify and establish contact with children and caregivers who face emergency incidents. Project staffs will be supported to get the necessary knowledge on barrier free approaches and on non-discrimination and how to communicate with courtesy and dignity, support and include persons with disabilities equally. 2)Child protection case management for 600 children(200 Girls), including referral to multi-sectoral services: SGBV survivors and other vulnerable children , including children with disabilities, children involved in harmful work, out-of-school children, child-headed households, child mothers, children living with chronically ill parents/caregivers, adolescent girls, and children at risk of abuse and exploitation will directly benefit from individualized case management support. Case workers assigned for each individual case will be responsible in developing holistic action plans for children and their families including (a) referrals to appropriate child protection and multispectral support services and (b) actions to be undertaken by the child and their family. Boys and girls will equally benefit from this intervention.SC will ensure children receiving case management support will have access to both male and female counsellors. Case workers will set up their meetings with beneficiaries in safe, appropriate, child-friendly meeting places that support confidentiality and privacy when meeting with children and families. SC will support its partners to be able to maintain an acceptable ratio of caseworker to children(1: 25). Partners will be also supported to employ interagency case workers supervision and coaching package so as to enhance the quality of child protection service. SC will support partners in developing, updating and utilizing data protection and information-sharing protocols (ISPs) and procedures for case coordination and case conferences.3)Provision of cash for protection to 300 (Girls)vulnerable GBV and child protection cases to mitigate further violence and harm: Complimentary to the case management activity, SC will provide cash to eligible boys and girls (through their caregivers) in Gaza and WB who are at a higher risk of child labor, child marriage, school dropout or GBV. Beneficiaries will be prioritized against the vulnerability criteria and in coordination with the MOSD and partner CBOs. The process includes checking the eligibility of beneficiaries using CWG tool /UVAT, organizing orientation; pre-distribution assessments; cash distribution and follow up, and monitoring.4)Provide individual and group counselling to 1500 (750 750 Girls) ex-detainee and conflict affected girls and boys experiencing higher levels of distress, and/or more complex behavioral and emotional difficulties including through referal: Children identified through schools, CBOs and SC child/youth resilience activities and recreational activities, CPN referral or Family Centres, will be assessed on the severity of the incident and will participate psychosocial support sessions accordingly. These sessions will account for the gender, age and disability of the children and in some cases, the group work will be separated by genders so as to give space for both gender to discuss their issues without fear. SC recognizes that work on mental health and psychosocial support has the potential to cause harm because it deals with highly sensitive issues. As such, SC and its partners will try to reduce the risk of harm in various ways, such as: by participating in coordination groups to learn from others and/or developing an understanding of, and consistently reflecting on, universal human rights, power relations between our staffs and emergency-affected people, and the value of participatory approaches. 5)Provide structured community-based mental health and psycho-social support to 1500 caregivers: Support will be provided to mothers, fathers and caregivers to equip them with skills on how to protect and support their children. The counseling sessions will be a chance for detection of SGBV cases and refer them to service providers. 6)Conduct recreational and performing arts activities for 3000 (1500 Boys) children & caregivers: Leveraging the power of performing arts (theater, music or art) under the HEART approach, we will facilitate session that help children and caregivers to socialize in an accommodating, creative and fun atmosphere, alleviate stress, and improve their well-being. 7)Provide self-care and stress management for 30(15 Female) child protection practitioners: Child protection practitioners and front-liners from the different actors are faced with many challenges and obstacles while handling complicated cases and heavy workload during the project life. Thus, they will be targeted for short term stress management program which gives them the opportunity to overcome any burnouts. 8)Design and implement child-led advocacy initiatives to address protection concerns: By organizing workshops children will be equipped with skills and knowledge on children’s rights and responsibilities, child participation, leadership and negotiation skills. This skillset will support the children to design and implement initiatives that map and address child protection risks and concerns at home, school and community. 9)Conduct career and vocational assessments to 100(20 girls) ex-detainees; Provision of Career Guidance and Counselling and Remedial Education

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

SC is committed to being accountable to communities by sharing appropriate and timely information, by supporting them to influence processes and decisions that affect them, by enabling them to provide feedback and share complaints about our work. Over the years we have developed strong practices on engaging and responding to feedback from children and communities safely and appropriately to the local context. Accountability mechanisms and feedback channels will be in place and disseminated, including feedback boxes placed at partner locations, a dedicated hotline number and email address, as well as direct communication with field staff to ensure that beneficiaries have safe and quality access to our feedback and complaint mechanisms and feel comfortable sharing feedback. Existing CPCs will also help beneficiaries and community members raise any suggestions or feedback. Due to the nature of our work with children, we have stringent policies and procedures around child and adult safeguarding, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), ethics, data protection and other essential principles. We are committed to ensuring that all adults and children with whom the organization works and has contact with- directly or indirectly- are safeguarded to the maximum possible extent. This commitment is implemented through our organizational Child Safeguarding (CSG) Policy, which applies equally to all children irrespective of their gender, disability, ethnicity, sexuality, marital status, or religion at all stages of the project, including recruitment, training, implementation, monitoring. Any child protection or CSG concerns brought to the attention of SC during the project will be dealt with in line with the policy and its protocols. SC team will train providers in SC Child Safeguarding, SC Gender Policy and SC financial policies with the focus on preventing fraud and corruption. SC PSEA mechanism is integrated into our broader feedback and complains mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project is designed and will be implemented following human rights and protection principles and standards that promote safety, respect and dignity of beneficiaries and meaningful access to project services. The interventions under this project will prevent and minimize unintended negative effects, which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. This will be done through putting protection and safeguarding policies in place and introducing accessible complaint mechanisms to all beneficiaries. The project will provide meaningful access to much-needed cash support for all the beneficiaries based on assessed needs and without discrimination. SC will ensure that vulnerable women, children (girls and boys, including girls and boys with disabilities), youth, persons with reduced mobility, including elderly and persons with specific needs, as well as target communities access services without discrimination. SC is also a member of the Protection Cluster and the CP/MHPSS AoR, and will closely coordinate with them in the implementation of this project, as well as with the Cash Working Group (CWG). All activities will be implemented according to the (IASC) Guidelines for Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Settings and MHPSS in emergencies, an agreed minimum standard by Save the Children and the sector globally, as well as the Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS). At the start of the project, SC will deliver a workshop to staff and partners on these standards.SC ensures the ‘Do No Harm” principle is considered in all our interventions. In this project, all the activities will be developed and designed to reduce the risk of negative impact on the lives of the direct and indirect beneficiaries. Protection will be mainstreamed in all project interventions. For example, safe mechanisms for food e-voucher disbursement will be used, identification will be technically sound and transparent, and staff and volunteers will be trained on child safeguarding, rights of girls and persons with disability, and sexual harassment. This will reduce the risk of sexual abuse and exploitation of women and children by staff and volunteers. Additionally, SC will provide any needed protection, referral, and case management support to project beneficiaries through ongoing protection programming. In respect to child safeguarding and PSEA, and as described above, SC ensures that all concerns, allegations, and incidents are reported, recorded and investigated according to our local country procedures and hierarchy of reporting within 24 hours. Moreover, SC pays special attention to the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Our complaints and feedback mechanisms are robust and designed to manage PSEA related reports and we do regular awareness training with our staff, partners and community on abuse and the duty to report. SCI will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will conduct strong community engagement through existing CPCs formed under other projects to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences, and transparency for preventing tensions amongst community members.
