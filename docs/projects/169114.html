Project Summary

Global Communities (GC) proposes this project to support the immediate shelter needs of 117 vulnerable households (HH) (approximately 468 individuals) affected by the Beirut Port Blast. GC will meet immediate shelter needs through disbursing cash for rent for displaced families and repairing and rehabilitating residential units. Through this project, GC will provide immediate shelter assistance to those most in need while linking efforts to an anticipated longer term recovery program.
Since 1997, GC, known as CHF International in Lebanon, has worked to improve the economic outcomes, community development, and shelter for people in need through funding provided by the United States Agency for International Development (USAID), the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) and others. Vitas Lebanon, a microfinance institution and subsidiary of GC, provides loans for housing and enterprise development. It is backed by the Development Finance Corporation, which has guaranteed nearly $205 million of loans to people in Lebanon since 2007. Drawing on their combined expertise, GC will work with Vitas Lebanon to provide cash for rent, and facilitate Level 1 and 2 shelter repairs to restore residences to minimum humanitarian standards.
GC will provide cash for rent for 34 HHs (approximately 136 individuals) for three months. This approach leverages Vitas’ established cash transfer infrastructure used for loan disbursements to immediately mobilize funds to quickly reach people in need. GC is actively coordinating with the Task Force on Emergency Cash Assistance (TF-ECA) for the Beirut Blast Response and will continue to do so in disbursing cash for rent.

GC will also target 27 HHs (approximately 108 individuals) with Level 1 shelter repairs and 56 HHs (approximately 224 individuals) with Level 2 shelter rehabilitation, to be completed by November 12, 2020. Where appropriate, GC will contract Vitas-supported construction businesses for repairs to support the economic recovery of small businesses. Targeting for both interventions will be based on technical eligibility criteria, with additional vulnerability criteria used to prioritize beneficiaries and ensure aid reaches those most in need. All staff will be trained in humanitarian and protection principles, and GC will provide information on ongoing and planned activities to relevant sector working groups and government units to ensure a coordinated response.
To leverage Vitas’ established relationships with affected communities, the project intends to target areas with primarily Level 2 damage for shelter rehabilitation and cash for rent for families who need to vacate their homes temporarily; this includes Remeil, Bachoura, Achrafieh, and Saifeh. For Level 1 shelter repairs, we will target Bourj Hammoud and Sinn El-Fil. GC is committed to addressing humanitarian needs wherever they may be, and will work with the Shelter Sector Working Group (SSWG) to fill gaps where identified, including within specific zones or in other areas. Vitas has served clients in all parts of the affected area and maintains the capacity to reach them through this response as needed. Moreover, GC is willing to participate in or lead any consortia required to best coordinate and reach those in need.
GC and Vitas have also raised funds from individual donors to support shelter recovery through their Li Beirut campaign, which will be used as complementary funding for this project. GC HAS SECURED $10,000 FOR MINOR SHELTER REPAIR TO DATE, AND HAS ACCESS TO ADDITIONAL COMPLEMENTARY FUNDING SHOULD THIS PROJECT BE FULLY FUNDED. GC is working to raise funds through private donors and individual giving, with the aim of developing a complementary grant and loan scheme that can support a range of individuals based on their needs and available resources. Vitas is also in discussions with the World Bank on implementing a city-wide survey to gauge the economic impact of the disaster.

1 - Needs

According to the most recent data available (as of August 13) from the ongoing Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) coordinated by the Lebanese Red Cross (LRC), 44% of the 4,223 respondents surveyed to date listed shelter or shelter repairs as their top priority post-explosion. Another 14% reported cash as their biggest need, without specifying a purpose. Additionally, 83% of respondents reported having no savings while 42% reported having no monthly HH income, which indicates that repairs may be beyond their reach with their existing means. One third of respondents reported having no savings nor no monthly income. Approximately 42% of respondents own their residence, while 52% rent. The remaining 6% reported that they were being hosted by others or did not respond. For the 58% who are not owners, potential disputes over responsibility for repairs and rehabilitation are likely to slow the ability to make any needed repairs. Many people have sought temporary shelter with family and friends, but this may add a significant burden to hosts due to the ongoing economic and COVID-19 crises. Additionally, as injured people are discharged from hospitals, more people may find themselves in need of temporary shelter (ACAPS Secondary Data Review), and may or may not have means to pay for shelter repairs or alternative housing arrangements. According to the SSWG, an estimated 29,771 apartments, inhabited by 89,313 individuals, sustained Level 1 damage that can be addressed through minor repairs. Approximately 1,144 buildings, inhabited by 30,660 individuals, suffered major structural damage (Level 3 damage) and are considered uninhabitable; this number may increase as technical assessments continue. Approximately 17,100 apartments, inhabited by an estimated 51,300 individuals, sustained Level 2 damage, and may or may not be safely habitable. Minor shelter repairs addressing Level 1 damage, and shelter rehabilitation to rectify Level 2 damage in homes that remain structurally sound and habitable, will maximize available housing stock by ensuring that blast-affected residents can repair habitable units and improve security, privacy, and dignity, ensuring that people can comfortably remain in their homes, or return quickly to their homes. Cash for rent can be used to meet immediate needs of individuals whose homes sustained Level 2 or Level 3 damages, while they temporarily rent other accommodation and identify longer-term housing solutions. Nearly 400 current Vitas clients live within 3 kilometers of the blast, primarily in the Bourj Hammoud (33%), Sinn El-Fil (9%), and Marzaa (28%) districts. While shelters in Bourj Hammoud and Sinn El-Fil have been damaged, food can still be purchased locally (ACAPS), indicating that with minor shelter repairs people are able to stay in their residences. While Marzaa is outside the current immediate zone for shelter priorities, GC will consider it as a target area for future economic recovery interventions. Historically Vitas’ clients have lived throughout the city, and GC and Vitas are poised to continue to gather needs information and respond city-wide. Remeil and Saifeh, proposed by GC for cash for rent, largely fall between the “structure destroyed area estimate” and “blast vacuum area estimate,” indicating they will likely have significant Level 2 damage (MapAction). Similarly, Bachoura and Achrafieh, while mostly beyond the blast vacuum area, are also likely to have many residents who require financial support for temporary housing due to a higher percentage of socio-economically vulnerable HHs (MapAction). According to a Strategy& survey, 92% of respondents’ businesses suffered damage from the blast, with 50% needing at least a year’s income for repairs. Needs assessments for clients living within a five kilometer radius from the blast site are ongoing and will be shared in the relevant sector working groups and used to shape programming for intermediate and longer-term support.

2 - Activities

GC’s project contributes to the SSWG logframe through 1) conditional cash for rent for displaced people; and 2) Level 1 and 2 shelter repairs. GC will leverage Vitas’ existing relationship with the Greater Beirut community and remains ready to serve alternative locations as requested. Activity 1: Cash for rent will target 34 HHs displaced from Remeil, Saifeh, Bachoura, and Achrafieh, and will improve beneficiaries’ access to safe and livable residential units. GC will provide an average of $200 per month for three months while beneficiaries repair their homes or look for other suitable housing arrangements, based on verified need for temporary relocation and vulnerability, in line with Cash for Rent Temporary Technical Committee (TTC) guidance. GC will draw on Vitas’ established cash transfer mechanisms with Cash United, Liban Post, and Sardar Bank to immediately disburse cash to beneficiaries. We will coordinate with the cash for rent TTC and TF-ECA during implementation.Beneficiaries will have already identified temporary housing with only the rent as a barrier. GC will prioritize the most vulnerable HHs based on criteria detailed below. GC will consider extensions if appropriate. GC will coordinate with UN Habitat and others assessing structural damage to support those who are forced to urgently relocate, primarily reaching those who are forced to move from residences with Level 2 and Level 3 damage. Activity 2: GC will work with local contractors to implement Level 1 and 2 shelter repairs for 83 HHs in Remeil, Saifeh, Bachoura, Achrafieh, Bourj Hammoud and Sinn El-Fil. Level 1 repairs will address up to $1500 worth of light, non-structural damage in inhabitable residences, per SSWG standards, while Level 2 rehabilitation will address up to $4500 of damage. We will coordinate shelter works with the TTC for repair of residential shelters. GC will identify contractors through an open, transparent, competitive procurement process, prioritizing those that are existing Vitas clients where appropriate to support the economic recovery of small businesses. GC Engineers will develop bills of quantity (BoQs) for each repair, and supervise the work of the contractors to ensure that repairs are done in accordance with the BoQ and meet quality standards. Prioritization will be based on vulnerability criteria detailed below. Cross-Cutting Approaches: GC will draw on the MSNA to identify needs, and follow up to identify beneficiaries through a tailored eligibility assessment to ensure appropriate targeting. Vulnerability factors used to prioritize beneficiaries include: lack of income or access to savings; high dependency ratio within the HH; and HHs with particularly vulnerable members, such as the elderly, disabled, chronically ill, children under age 5, or pregnant and lactating women.While proposed methodologies align with humanitarian principles and are based on GC’s prior experience in Lebanon and the Middle East, eligibility and prioritization criteria may be adapted as response efforts are standardized through coordination with the SSWG and TTCs. GC will provide anonymized program and needs data to the SSWG to aid coordination, identify gaps, and facilitate referrals.Staff will be trained on Psychological First Aid and protection principles to safeguard recipients and ensure that all activities incorporate protection considerations. Beneficiaries will receive information on how to register complaints as a measure for quality control and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Any SEA allegations, or those of fraud, waste, or abuse of resources, will immediately be elevated to GC’s Office of Ethics for appropriate investigation and action.GC will aim to maximize the use of technology to support rapid assessment, repair, and monitoring of shelter works, including assessment applications that link repair works to local contractors, costing data, and loan calculations, and provide real-time progress monitoring data.
