Needs

The humanitarian context of the WB, including EJ, remains directly tied to the impact of the Israeli occupation, which marked its 51st year in June 2018. Protracted socio-economic & protection difficulties continue. Representing one-third of the West Bank population including East Jerusalem , many of the 836,550 Palestine refugees registered with UNRWA experience challenges marked by continued violence & forced displacement, administrative & punitive demolitions; & restrictions on access to = services & markets, particularly for those living or owing in Area C & in the Seam Zone. In addition, the stagnant economy & lack of employment opportunities erodes the purchasing power of Palestine refugees thus classifying them as one of the poorest segments of Palestinian society. While 18.7 per cent of the Palestinians in the West Bank were unemployed during the first half of 2018, Palestine refugees’ unemployment rate reached 19 per cent, rising to 23.4 per cent for those living in camps. Food insecurity also remains higher among refugees (15.1 per cent) & especially for refugees residing in camps (25 per cent) in comparison with non-refugees (12 per cent). In addition, refugees continue to experience high level of poverty which also contributes to their food insecurity levels. Females are furthermore considered vulnerable as their participation in the labour force are at the low level of 26 per cent in in the first half of 2018 (17 per cent for male), as a result to several factors including availability of job opportunity & cultural norms inhibiting women’s participation on the labour market, further contributing to their vulnerability. Female headed households are thus especially vulnerable to poverty, furthermore affecting the food insecurity. In 2018, UNRWA has faced a critical funding shortfall under its Emergency interventions which led to the discontinuation of most of its food insecurity interventions. This has resulted in 90,000 refugees (48,000 which reside in camps) at risk of deepening poverty. This is also against a backdrop of the deterioration in the West Bank which has resulted in an increase of unemployment levels, particularly within the refugee community amongst other factors including regional insecurity, an increasing coercive environment by the occupying power and donor fatigue for Palestine support. Since UNRWA started discontinuing its emergency program, it has witnessed a significant increase in the number of emergency beneficiaries applying to enroll in its main relief programing, the E-Card program. The reassessment of beneficiaries’ poverty status revealed a significant number of refugees that are living in abject poverty, less than 2 dollars a day. This forced UNRWA to reconsider its approach in halting emergency services & decide to integrate this load into its E-Card program. This decision was instigated by the dire financial situation of those families which will force them to resort to negative coping mechanisms that would have detrimental effect on their lives if they were left without assistance.

Activities

In 2019, UNRWA will seek to provide unconditional & non-restricted cash assistance to a minimum of 2,543 abject poor refugee households (although the number of needy households are bigger than this) in the WB & its 19 refugee camps through its E-Card modality. This modality provides cash to extremely poor refugees through bank debit cards dedicated for this purpose. Beneficiaries are selected after UNRWA’s social workers visit the households & assess their consumption. The poverty level of the households is then assessed using the (PMTF). In addition to consumption, this formula takes into consideration aspects of vulnerability, such as presence of chronic diseases, housing characteristics & accessibility to utilities, number of elderly & disabled (multi-disabilities) within a family, & female-headed households. Households with those aspects are given an advantage on other families & are prioritized for assistance. Families found to be extremely poor & vulnerable are then enrolled into the program. Heads of households receive their debit cards, & they start collecting basic assistance of NIS125 per person each quarter. Social workers maintain contact with beneficiaries as they work in the field & beneficiaries know them. Households consider the social worker as one of the major sources of information they have. Since its introduction of the e-card modality, the Agency has been developing & improving it. It created a troubleshooting system & a hotline to assist beneficiaries. Based on their feedback, UNRWA modified its processes & signed a contract with the Bank of Palestine as the new service provider. This has improved the efficiency of the existing program. Furthermore, the Agency created a number of monitoring tools to monitor the implementation & targets reached through the program. Through the monitoring of the program during the last years, the program was found to be contributing to food security & protecting people from slipping further into poverty. Beneficiaries reported that the new modality improved their dignity & choice . The majority of respondents have been finding obtaining cash through this modality as easy while more than 90 per cent agree that the e-card gives flexibility on how to receive & use the money, & when to spend it . The cash provided is mainly prioritized to obtain food over non-food items, & female members of a household largely decide what to buy with the cash. The project will go through two main phases: Preparation Phase: The identified beneficiaries by end of 2018 will be informed of their enrollment to the program. This will be done through the call center operated by UNRWA & the social workers. In January & February, around 20 workshops will be conducted in the WB in which social workers will give the new beneficiaries an orientation on the program. Arrangement will be put in place for the service provider, Bank of Palestine, to accommodate the new beneficiaries. The bank will issue the new e-cards & distribute them to beneficiaries in its branches, according to the lists provided to it by UNRWA, by the end of January 2019. Implementation && Monitoring Phase: By the end of February 2019, the first payment will be sent to beneficiaries’ accounts for Q1 2019. UNRWA will generate lists of beneficiaries on a quarterly basis && send them to the bank. In its turn, the bank will send the payment to listed beneficiaries to withdraw the cash from the designated shops or bank branches. Social workers will monitor the progress during monitoring visits as well as regular ones. They will address any concerns beneficiaries might have && assist beneficiaries in using the system, if needed. Special attention will be given to vulnerable groups, particularly elderly & persons with disabilities. Staff operating the troubleshooting system will monitor complaints, resolve them & report on trends to management to address any issues that might arise.

Indicators

Outcome: abject poor refugee households living in West Bank (including the 19 camps) are receiving cash assistance to cover their basic food needs & restore their coping capacities. Indicators & targets: • 2,543 abject poor refugee households in the West Bank (in rural, urban & in the 19 camps) receive cash assistance through the e-card modality. • 18,182 abject poor refugee individuals in the West Bank (in rural, urban & in the 19 camps) receive cash assistance through the e-card modality. • US$2,545,480 in total value is disbursed as cash to abject poor refugee households. Monitoring & evaluationIn 2019; UNRWA will ensure monitoring of progress & results at different levels through its Relief & Social Services (RSSP) staff (social workers & monitors). This will be done through the field visit.UNRWA will monitor first-hand the program’s overall progress, on-spot visits will be conducted through RSSP & UNRWA Monitoring & Evaluation unit. UNRWA will monitor around communication & outreach, cash withdrawal, utilize of cash, technical issues with withdrawing cash as well as access to bank branches Furthermore, UNRWA has an additional internal monitoring tool which is the free toll hotline, in which UNRWA can receive complains or feedback from its beneficiaries. The hotline is also a tool to get feedback from beneficiaries about the program, any issues with the bank & the utilization of the cash.
