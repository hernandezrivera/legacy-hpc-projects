Project Summary

In 2019, Concern aims to build on its 2018 response by continuing with existing activities and focus on Raqqa city and country side, while increasing the provision of assistance to recently conflicted areas along the Euphrates River in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate. This will be done in coordination with other food security partners in North East Syria. Concern aims to provide family food rations and food vouchers to the most vulnerable Syrian families in Northern Syria in order to meet their food security needs while markets and livelihoods are being restored.
In 2018, Concern food security activities had a specific focus in areas along the Euphrates. In Menbij, Concern successfully distributed 8 months of food voucher support to 209 households and between 3 to 8 months support to 2,957 households. Moving south to At-Thawrah city, Concern remains the only INGO in a position to deliver food security support in the city, providing regular 30-day food ration support to the accommodation centres and Mansoura Camps. Within the city itself, Concern has distributed 5 rounds of support in the 3rd Quarter neighbourhood. In Raqqa Concern was one of the first INGOs to distribute food rations within the city and was the first INGO to distribute food vouchers, regularly distributing vouchers to over 6,000 households per month. By the end of 2018, Concern was providing food voucher support to 6 of the city’s neighbourhoods (Baath, Furat, Hisham Ibn Abd Al Malek, Yamuk, Qadessiyeh and Hettin) and will expand into a seventh in 2019 (Tas-heeh). Concern has committed to reaching families for eight rounds of voucher support in the city. At the time of writing Concern has distributed four rounds of support to 1,433 households in Zone 1 and 3 rounds of support to 1,956 households in Zone 2 and 595 households in Zone 5. Concern opened a sub-office in Kisreh Deir-ez-Zor in the first quarter of 2018. This has allowed Concern to programme extensively along the east line of the Euphrates, distributing 38,000 food baskets to 36,302 households and instituting a pilot food voucher project in Kisreh for 47 households.
The nature of context and the reduction in conflict in North East Syria necessitates a further shift in the modality rationale away from immediate emergency assistance through food rations to a more sustained support through repeat food voucher distributions. Food ration support will continue to be distributed to newly displaced IDPs (within 72 hours of displacement) as it provides a ready and immediate source of food. Rations will also be distributed to households in hard to reach areas, settlements and collective centers where there are few market opportunities and where the draw effects should be limited. Food vouchers are however the preferable modality in 2019 for providing sustained and regular food security assistance, with an aim of 8 months of voucher support. Vouchers also give households the freedom to select their own food items and for Concern they are preferable from an operational and cost efficiency perspective

Needs

In 2019, the Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview estimated that there were 1,978,000 food insecure persons in Governorates in which Concern programmes, this includes 257,968 individuals in Raqqa and 352,165 individuals in Deir ez Zor, areas which saw significant new displacement in later 2017 and 2018. Districts within these governorates reported some of the highest levels of food security in Syria with 132,379 food insecure individuals reported in Raqqa, 106,260 individuals in Menbij and 107,655 individuals in Deir ez Zor. Concern has undertaken comprehensive household-level assessments, taking into consideration a range of vulnerability indicators including the food consumption score and livelihood coping strategy categories. These show continued food insecurity in Ath-Thawrah, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor. A recent assessment in Kisreh, Der-ez-Zor has shown that food insecurity with an average food consumption score of 17.7 of those assessed, while a recent assessment of 820 households in Raqqa in 2018 show an average food consumption score of 24.5. There are still large gaps in coverage amongst INGOs, particularly among long-term IDP communities (in urban Manbij, Tabqa and in rural informal camps) and recent returnees in Raqqa city. Raqqa city has seen thousands of residents return every month and recent surveys by REACH shows that despite scaled up food security assistance in Raqqa, there are still large unmet needs as while food is available and prices stable, the ability of residents to purchase food continues to decline due to the absence of employment opportunities and the exhaustion of household assets and reserves, forcing many to adopt negative coping strategies.The nature of context the reduction in conflict in NES necessitates a further shift in the modality rationale away from immediate emergency assistance through food rations to a more sustained support through repeat food voucher distributions and limited agriculture support. Food ration support will continue to be distributed to newly displaced IDPs (within 72 hours of displacement) as it provides a ready and immediate source of food. Rations will also be distributed to households in hard to reach areas, settlements and collective centers where there are few market opportunities and where the draw effects should be limited. Results from FFP post distribution monitoring of 272 beneficiaries in January indicate that 99% of households are satisfied with the modality. Note that while 99% where satisfied with the quality, 47.8% did say that they would like additional products in the food basket, most wanted sugar and tea which has no positive calorific content so will not be included in the ration.

Implementation of activities

The project will consist of three main modalities of food security assistance - dry food rations and ready-to-eat rations (regional procurement and in-kind distribution) and food vouchers. Concern will provide at least one month food assistance through dry food rations or 5-day ready-to-eat rations (for newly arrived IDPs without access to cooking provision), and a minimum of eight months assistance through food vouchersBeneficiaries for the rations will be selected through the emergency assessment tool. Demographic data in addition to needs, gaps, and field staff observations are used to select households. Rations are generally distributed to IDPs after they first arrive in a location and in areas where it is not possible to set up a food voucher activity, and RTERs for those who do not immediately have access to cooking facility. In 2019, due to the transition from emergency to medium-term recovery, it is expected that the majority of the food security assistance from this project will be provided through food vouchers rather than through food rations. Food rations will be reserved for sudden spikes in displacement and new emergencies. Approximately 24,000 30-day food rations will be distributed during the complete project period but with narrower selection criteria to ensure they are reserved for the most vulnerable households, or for any sudden spikes in displacement. A smaller portion (8,400 in total/average of 700 per month for 12 months) of RTERs will also be reserved for households that are suddenly displaced. RTERs will meet 100% of the recommended daily allowance (2100 kcal/100% of required calorific needs) for five days are in line with cluster specifications.Food voucher participants will be selected through the household assessment tool which includes a built-in vulnerability criterion. Concern will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community members using Concerns extensively tested household assessment tool; 100% of households receiving assistance will be assessed. This has been most successfully used in Raqqa City, where Concern assessed all households in its targeted neighbourhoods. The criteria for selection is based on assessment questions relating to household composition, vulnerability and food security. On average, each month 4,000 vouchers will be distributed over a period of 12 months reaching a total of 36,000 households. While rations are the preferred modality for households in need of immediate food assistance and who have difficulty accessing local markets, food vouchers are the preferable modality for providing sustained and regular food security assistance; Concern where possible aims to provide up to 8 months’ voucher support. Until recently food vouchers have only been used in large urban areas such as Raqqa, Tabqa and Menbij cities, but at the end of 2018/start of 2019 pilot distributions have shown the viability of vouchers in smaller towns (Kisreh), informal camps (Alkhomea Camps) and in rural areas (Raqqa countryside). These distributions have shown both the flexibility of Concerns voucher mechanism and the strength of local markets in Syria. Concern aims to expand voucher activities in these types of areas in Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor in 2019.Prior to rolling out voucher activities in an area, Concern conducts detailed market assessments to ensure areas have sufficient numbers of vendors and that each vendor selected for the voucher project has sufficient stock of food items. Concern then monitors the market in the project area, in which the M&E team collect data on Concern and non-Concern vendors and on shopping and non-shopping days.Concerns food security activities are linked with other sectors including livelihood, shelter and NFI. For example, in 2019 Concern aims to pair food security support with livelihood support to IDPs and vulnerable host community with access to small piece of agricultural land or gardens, through the provision of Agricultural vouchers and kits.

Protection Risk Analysis

IEDs UXOs are DiscoveredProbability: Moderate / Severity: Moderate - CatastrophicThere remain numerous IEDs and UXOs in Northern Syria that pose a significant risk to civilians and NGO staff. Concern and Bahar staff, as well as program participants, will receive mine awareness training with specific focus on high-risk activities such as rehabilitating irrigation channels, tilling soil, or conducting other activities involving digging. Concern will consult with local authorities and with mine clearance organizations before starting work. Those Concern staff who have been trained to give mine risk awareness trainings are responsible to share this training with all program staff and project participants. Concern maintains regular coordination on de-mining and mine risks. Mine removal authorities will be called to deal with any suspicious devices. Attack Targets NGO StaffProbability: Unlikely / Severity: Severe - CatastrophicConcern is careful to maintain their neutrality and independence and take a conflict-sensitive approach in order to avoid being targeted by any party to the conflict. This is especially important in North East Syria where any NGO implementing activities may be perceived to be aligned with the Self-Administration who are currently controlling the program target areas. The cross-border security group – in which Concern is a member – provides a forum for Concern staff at different levels in the organizational structure to analyze security risks and to update the security management plan to reflect evolving risk. Security groups are used for tracking staff movements within Syria and for dissemination of information about security incidents to all staff. Should Concern receive credible evidence that its staff, volunteers, or beneficiaries will be targeted, all stakeholders will be informed and all program activities will be immediately suspended until Concern determines the risk has adequately diminished. Local Officials Pose a Threat to Concern’s IndependenceProbability: Moderate / Severity: MajorIn the Kurdish-controlled regions in which this program will operate, the Democratic Union Party (PYD) maintains political dominance and does not represent all of the political and ethnic groups in the area. PYD could use humanitarian assistance as a political tool, e.g., intervening in selection of beneficiaries or staff.Local authorities may present candidates whom they wish considered for work with Concern, but as the recruitment process is competitive and transparent they have grown to understand Concern will only select qualified candidates and those which also support ambitions for diversity and gender balance. Concern avoids interference from local authorities in the creation of beneficiary lists by conducting 100% household assessments with Concern staff to select project participants. Households who are not selected through this household assessment process are to contact Concern through our Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) via phone, WhatsApp, email, or feedback boxes at office locations. Additionally, MEAL personnel systemically visit samples of targeted beneficiaries in order to verify the household meets the selection criteria. Exploitation of beneficiaries by Concern StaffProbability: Moderate / Severity: ModeratePeople can contact senior management in Concern through a CRM. This mechanism is advertised widely in Concern project areas and is accessible through the mechanisms outlined above. All feedback received will be analysed by Concern's Community Response (CRM) Assistants, and shared with the appropriate staff members for follow up, action and closure. Sensitive complaints which may be about harassment, fraud and misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and other violations of Concern's Programme Participant Protection Policy (P4), are shared immediately with the Country Director (CD), who forms a committee for investigation as appropriate.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk- There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS or FSA groups supported by Turkey, especially in border districts such as Manbij and Tabqa. - Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioration in the security situation, in Der Ez Zor as a result of any of IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict; movement of GoS troops into the area. . - Closure of cross border supply routes into NES Assumptions- The security situation will continue to improve in Der Ez Zor, enabling Concern to establish a field office in Basira in the first quarter of 2019 and to expand activities from the West line to the North Line and East Line. - The self-administration/SDC retains control over key locations west and south of the Euphrates, specifically Manbij and Tabqa. - Concern will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly- That the border between KRI and Turkey and KRI and NES remains open to commodity traiffic Mitigating MeasuresConcern has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict - particularly in the wake of the likely US troop withdrawl in early-mid 2019. Concern's response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localised insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations, Concern would likely suspend operations in these areas and shift its resources to its nearest other functional field offices to assist those newly dispaced and affected by these events. If international staff had to be scaled down or pulled out completely (due to border access issues, loss of emergency evacuation options), Concern would remotely manage staff and the project from its support office in Dohuk and using its remote-management team based in Turkey.-Concern has also been expanding it's network of local suppliers inside Syria and has offices in Ras Al Ain and Manbij in order to reach suppliers based in Qamishli city and Aleppo Governorate respectively. Concern has also increasingly transitioned its emergency support (winterization, NFIs, food vouchers) from in-kind distributions to voucher/cash modalities which circumvent issues of cross-border goods transportation while also promoting local markets and providing beneficiaries with greater freedom of choice over the items they chose to purchase.
