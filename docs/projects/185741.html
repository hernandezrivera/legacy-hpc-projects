Project Summary

The project is designed keeping in consideration the importance of continuing WASH services in the Rohingya and Host community. To facilitate programme participants across age and gender, MHM kit distribution for vulnerable Rohingya women will be implemented throughout the project. Operation and maintenance of bathing cubicles, handwashing statins as well as repair/maintenance of communal handwashing points with pedal system is a priority considering COVID-19 prevention and protection practices. For better sanitation service FSM De-sludge Management in camps, dumping station upgradation and (O&M), solid waste management (drainage cleaning, waste collection, sorting and dumping for composting for organic fertilizer and recycling), activity will be operated ensuring environment safety and cleanliness. Additionally, community awareness raising, and behavioural changes will be assured through hygiene promotion outreach sessions.

1 - Identified Needs

The recent findings have found, dirty and dysfunctional latrines as well as insufficient sanitation facilities were a common concern across all camps and host communities. In August 2021, heavier than usual seasonal rains caused widespread flooding to the host community, resulting in a request for support from DPHE to assist in the assessment and the response. Approximately 23% of the HC population was affected by the floods, affecting both water and sanitation facilities. Most households in the host communities (88%) access water from tubewells/handpumps, but only 77% of households are reporting having enough water for their domestic need (only 74% in Teknaf where groundwater is scarce) [J-MSNA 2021] In camps current Hygiene knowledge and access to soap remains high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge on hygiene habits. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females through stigma, taboo and restrictive practices but also from a lack of MHM kit individuality due to common practices of one size fits all or variations of inter sector kit and insufficient coordination. It also requires wide scale of Hygienic Practice monitoring, staff capacity building and meaningful community engagement [J-MSNA 2021]For sanitation functionality has an average of 92% and is a continual process for partners. Despite additional latrine installation during 2021, improvements in existing facilities with inclusion and privacy features and regular facility monitoring by the Area Focal Agencies and partners show number of latrines has stayed constant with 2020 figures, highlighting the continued lack of operational space for WASH infrastructure. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in private spaces, with 86% of households reporting private bathing space inside or attached to their shelter for but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry spaces (79%) and dishwashing places (77%), very often those spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 11% not connected to any form of drainage. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces. Pilot programmes have been instigated to install women friendly spaces and WASH blocks but are challenged by space and the lack of direct water supplies to each facility. [J-MSNA 2021]A continual concern regarding sanitation is the quality of the faecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements or scale-up. Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51% of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area and 16% reported disposing of waste direct to open space. On the other hand, 74.5% of households are satisfied with the household level segregation and waste collection practices (CAID Need Assessment 2021)
