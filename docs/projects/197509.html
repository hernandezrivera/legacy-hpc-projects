Project Summary

This project is to respond to the need for life-saving shelter and NFI assistance in Aleppo and Idleb, targeting IDP sites and host communities to increase social cohesion and provide equal access to humanitarian aid.
The project has four components as follows:
* In communities/villages:
1)- Individual Shelter Rehabilitation” (HH level). (Target: 1,500 HHs / 7,500 individuals / 800$ per shelter unit/HH house) – to “rehabilitation of collective centers”.
2)- Individual Shelter Rehabilitation” Light repair for partially-damaged individual houses, valued at (600-1,000 USD, average of 800 USD) for 1,500 HHs (7,500 ind).
* Assistance is determined as a technical assessment of each house/unfinished building, and BINAA will follow SNFI Cluster Guidance to ensure the scope of work matches the standards, dignity, privacy, and protection.
Shelter repair beneficiaries will be IDP and HC HHs living in an inadequate shelter that does not meet minimum SPHERE minimum standards for square meters of covered living space per person and has inadequate privacy or protection, by targeting houses that have light to average damage but remain structurally sound, the works include doors and windows repair or replacement, partition installation, sealing cracks and holes in walls, and WASH facilities repairs to increase privacy and enhance gender-sensitive response.
Furthermore, for HHs with PwDs, BINAA will follow Washington Tool and do additional work to respond to PwDs' needs, including disability-friendly entrances and toilet facilities, a ramp with a handrail for using wheelchairs, and seating provisions for toilets.
* In Camps/IDP Sites:
3)- Targeting 800 HHs (4,000 ind) by replacing 800 un-secured, un-protected, non-privacy, and un-dignified tents with 800 dignified shelter units.
The shelter design is classified as dignified, more sustainable, and made of sandwich panels for walls and roofs, PVC internal doors, and windows. Each unit is equipped with a toilet, bathroom, and kitchen, in addition to the final flooring layer through the CFW program.
Moreover, BINAA circulated individual discussions using Consultation Forms, surveyed 16 IDP sites, and introduced 11 shelter solutions "as per SNFI-Dignified Shelter Guideline".
That 97% of residents had stated acceptance to such the module for:
- Local availability of skills and materials
- 25 m2 Inter-area
- 5 m2 Covered living space per person (match SPHERE)
- Low-cost design using shared-walls partitions without any effect on privacy
- Min 10-year lifespan
- High protection against weather, fire, dust, wind, rain, snow, and insects
- High privacy and security
- Easy removal and re-use units by local skills
The interlinked SNFI, WASH, and ERL activities are developed with the Clusters to ensure the best integration.
SNFI:
1. Replacing 800 tents with Dignified Shelters solution
2. Constructing 1bath, 1WC, and 1kitchen for each unit
3. Constructing special facilities for PwDs
4. Road graveling in camp
5. Camp lighting
6. Constructing a rain drainage system, 7. Paving people's pathways with stones, and 8. Paving special ramps and entrances for PwDs, all are integrated with the ERL CFW Program.
WASH:
1- Extending and maintaining the Sewage and Water Networks to connect the Units' WASH facilities
2- Installing a 500-liter tank for each unit
3- Construct a high water tank, 80 m3 in the camp
4- Equipping the existing borewell
5- Providing 200 Trash containers (100-liter)
6- Supporting operation costs and WASH services (septic tank desludging and waste removal) for 3-months
7- Hygiene awareness
ERL:
Integrate 250 CFW workers in:
1. Flooring works for the units
2. Paving the rain drainage system with stones
3. Paving the pathways with stones
4. Paving special ramps and pathways for PwDs
* In camps and communities:
4)- Provide to the accommodated 6,800 households (34,000 ind), an NFI assistance (Winter-Labelled CASH) valued at 150 USD, in one go, in Dec, as per the SNFI Cluster Guide.

3 - Needs

HNO-22, 13.8M are estimated to require humanitarian and protection assistance such as health, WASH, education, nutrition, critical protection services, and inadequate shelter conditions. With 36% of the population relying on unsafe water supply, at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and 50% of the sewerage systems are not functional, and about 29% of garbage is inappropriately disposed of.BINAA has conducted a rapid MSRNA need assessment and CFW Market Assessment in 16 camps and 7 communities. The MSNA results indicate:- All respondents declared that their tents are expired and should be replaced by a better solution of dignified shelter.- The main source of water for camps HHs is still water trucking 88% while only 2% depend on water networks.- For HHs, more than 75% are facing poor economic conditions (monthly income may not reach 500 TL) to secure their basic needs (food, health, shelter), while most of them 65% depend on receiving aid.- Most IDPs in urban and rural areas live in poor host communities that need support, where the IDPs are facing shelter/accommodation issues, this forced IDPs to live in places that don't meet the minimum standards of housing requirements or preserve either security or dignity of family members.- The participants also stated that MPG including cash for rent or cash grants to support shelter reconstruction/rehabilitation is the most prioritized intervention to protect IDPs and secure their privacy and safety.
