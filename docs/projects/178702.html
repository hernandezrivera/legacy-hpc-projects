Project Summary

30 field assessments will be conducted by the FSL technical and MEAL teams through discussion with Key Informants or Focus Group discussions. Focus on locations with high vulnerability (defined by OCHA vulnerability scale). Ideally, common beneficiaries and/or locations will be selected to achieve an integrated approach. Beneficiaries selected by Beneficiary Selection Committees according to AAH needs and vulnerability selection criteria. A validation survey of a random sample will be conducted. The action focuses on households with woman head of household. The Action will support farmers and herders to develop sustainable agricultural enterprises to become independent of external aid and become more resilient. Farmers will receive agricultural inputs (either through direct provision or a conditional voucher). 2,500 crop farmers will receive 150kg of seed wheat, 100kg of chickpea and/or 80kg of broad bean seed. The lands will be divided into two 5 donum sections. A crop rotation approach will be implemented with alternate seasonal planting of cereal and legume on the two sections in successive years. 2,500 livestock herders who have access to 10 donums of land for cultivation will receive 150kg of seed barley, the harvest from which will either be directly fed to the farmers own animals as an alternative to purchased fodder or sold to generate household income. Beneficiaries will receive technical and capacity building training through a farmer field school approach to efficiently use the provided agricultural inputs. Linkages between Income Generating Activities and the local market will be investigated to provide household producers with information and support to ensure improved access to the market leading to improved household incomes and financial security; this will include a market study of the optimal economic agricultural sectors. Cooperation will be promoted between farmers and the MAAR extension service through publication of relevant reports and brochures. Post-distribution monitoring surveys, baseline/endline surveys and a session on lessons learned to identify the best practices for future interventions will be conducted.

4 - Needs

There are 13.4 million people in Syria in need of humanitarian assistance. 12.4M people are estimated as food insecure (1.3M are considered severely food insecure) . The WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in February 2021 was SYP 136,398. The prolonged nature of the crisis has strained the ability of the population to cope; negative coping strategies are on the rise. Households have responded by reducing the number of meals per day and not consuming expensive food categories (e.g.: meat). As hostilities have reduced in many areas, particularly those that have shifted control, the demands from the population relating to livelihood opportunities and basic services have increased as they seek to rebuild their lives. AAH conducted 30 needs assessments in 8 governorates at sub-district level during 2020-21. The assessment methodology involves collection of information from Key Informants in targeted sub-districts to complete a questionnaire (sections on context, livelihood activities, food security and production status); the assessment undertaken through a gender perspective. Households have indicated there is limited access to agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, tools irrigation equipment) and market support to establish or maintain sustainable agricultural livelihoods. Access to fuel for tractors or irrigation pumps is either not available or unaffordable. The Ministry of Agricultural and Agrarian Reform has established a support scheme to provide subsidized fuel and fertilizer, but the quantities distributed are insufficient to meet the needs of farmers. Livestock farmers with limited financial resources are unable to provide optimal diets to their animals. Extension services are limited.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented by the AAH_FSL 6-member team composed of international and national staff based in the Damascus hub office, with regional offices in Aleppo and Hassakeh. AAH has partnered with ACSAD, which is responsible for ensuring access to project locations, implementation of activities, and developing and presenting agricultural extension material to farmers in cooperation with MAAR. AAH is responsible for project design, development of technical specifications, monitoring and reporting to the donor. A tri-partite agreement has been signed between AAH, ACSAD and MAAR. AAH has a dedicated MEAL department with staff in the hub and regional offices. A comprehensive Monitoring & Evaluation plan will be designed to support collection and tracking of sex and age disaggregated data in line with the proposed Monitoring Framework. Technical and MEAL staff conduct regular monitoring field visits at all stages of the project cycle to confirm beneficiary selection criteria have been maintained, aid distribution has proceeded correctly (a Post-Distribution Monitoring report prepared) and at project completion, outputs are measured against defined indicators to measure the impact of the intervention through baseline/endline surveys. All monitoring information is used to capture good practices and inform future project implementation monitoring procedures. The MEAL team collect quantitative testimonies from the perspective of beneficiaries; this material can be used evidence-based advocacy messaging. A feedback mechanism is already in place where recipients of aid or other stakeholders of the Action can voice their complaints or other comments. A telephone number and contact information is included in the package.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Prioritise safety, dignity, and “Do No Harm” principle: AAH implements a transparent beneficiary selection procedure to target households with high levels of vulnerability. AAH negotiates with ACSAD and MAAR to locate the point of delivery close to beneficiaries’ home. The time and location of aid distribution is widely publicized to allow beneficiaries time to arrange for transportation of the aid. Distribution points are managed to ensure effective crowd control with gender considerations. No location is selected at risk of UXO. Distributed aid is sourced from reputable suppliers with government approved quality controls. Meaningful access: AAH ensures special arrangements for people with protection concerns (e.g. PWDs, the elderly) for a representative of the beneficiary to collect the aid on their behalf. AAH ensures protection of the rights of the marginalized or those facing discrimination by identification of these groups during assessment and beneficiary selection. Accountability, Participation and Empowerment: AAH ensures key informants represent all groups in the community during the assessment phase to understand their needs and methodology of assistance; identifies measures to help people avoid resorting to negative coping strategies; establishes a complaints feedback mechanism with a widely available hotline accessible for all stakeholders. PSEA: Ensure procedures are put in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH follows internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a PSEA Policy with an implementation plan.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect, and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. Sexual exploitation and abuse can do great harm to people affecting integrity and trust; AAH prohibits such exploitation by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of the PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; undertake awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy to the prescribed channels. All employees and representatives of AAH will not engage in sexually exploitative behaviour or linking sexual favour with the receipt of aid; engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age; or being involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH will investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of spread of COVID-19 at distribution sites, staff/partner sickness. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: social distancing; safe hand-washing area; PPE distribution; COVID-19 protection guidance implemented. Risk of unstable security situation; access to targeted locations prevented; additional administrative procedures on DINGOs, lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: use local staff and SARC. Advocacy, coordination with local authorities/Ministries, to monitor situation, negotiate access. Adhere to SOPs/security systems, Conflict-Sensitivity approach. Risk of substantive devaluation of the Syrian pound; international bank transfers to Syria are not fluid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: situation closely monitored with communication to donors with options defined. Risk of aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Risk of data collection hampered. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: transparency, trust-building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers. Ethical risk. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: provide compulsory trainings staff member on organizational ethical policies. Follow the internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a Policy for PSEA with an implementation plan.
