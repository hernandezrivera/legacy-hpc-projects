Project Summary

The project aims providing critical life-saving WASH services through emergency WASH services and further establishing WASH infrastructures for the most vulnerable 12800 persons (5325 women, 4915men,1280 boys and 1280 girls) in Baidoa and Burhakaba Districts Bay Region Somalia.The project targets the most vulnerable populations including IDPs, returnees and marginalized host community members (women, men, boys and girls) including those with disabilities and the elderly that are discriminated from accessing essential basic services. The plight is exacerbated by combined impacts of Covid-19 pandemic, devastating 2019 Deyr and 2020 Gu flooding, past successive drought seasons and intermittent conflicts has significantly compromised existing limited WASH infrastructures and services and exposed them to risks of outbreak of AWD/cholera and other sanitation and hygiene related diseases. (FSNAU and FEWS NET May, 2020 report). Furthermore, their plight is compounded by anticipated La Niña conditions associated with high likelihood of consecutive below-average rainfall seasons, projected high risk of drought conditions by early 2021. (July/August 2020 FSNAU post-Gu assessment report).
The project will employ comprehensive consultation and inclusion of people with disability to meet their distinct WASH needs and ensure they fully utilize the services without any form of discriminations since they are exposed to increased risks of exploitation, abuse and discrimination by when seeking assistance. Moreover, they are often denied access to water and sanitation services since their distinct needs are never put into consideration when designing WASH programs. Their comprehensive consultation and and inclusion will ensure that they have water points and sanitation facilities designed to accommodate them. while they will be fully involved in the project right from the project design face as well as during implementation including monitoring till completion and lessons learnt, success stories and best practices clearly captured. In addition, women, men boys and girls will be involved in the project just like the people with disabilities and their distinct needs addressed by the project. The project will be implemented continuously for 12 months.

Project Activities

1. Drilling of 2 new boreholes in Baidoa and Burhakaba Districts Bay Region, Somalia for the targeted 10500 persons/beneficiaries (6368 women, 4032 men, 1050 boys and 1050 girls)2. Rehabilitation of 2 boreholes in Burhakaba Districts Bay Region, targeting 9600 persons/beneficiaries (3994 women, 3686 men, 960 boys and 960 girls)3. Upgrade of 10 shallow wells in Burhakaba District targeting 4000 persons/beneficiaries (1664 women, 1536 men,400 boys and 400 girls) 4. Training of 60 water committee members (30men and 20women) on management and maintenance of newly established and rehabilitated water systems5. Water Distribution by Voucher for 5 weeks in 17 drought affected remote villages in Baidoa District targeting the worst disaster/floods and drought affected and conflict displaced 2000HHs/12000 persons/beneficiaries (4992 women, 4608 men, 1200 boys and 1200 girls)6. Construction of 80 gender sensitive new latrines acceptable for the disabled (for men and women)fitted with hand washing facilities and lockable doors and are lighted at night in IDP camps in Baidoa for the targeted 480 HHs/2880 persons/beneficiaries (1198 women, 1106 men, 288 boys and 288 girls) 7. Distribution of 2500 Hygiene kits in Baidoa and Burhakaba Districts to villages with cases ofAWD/Cholera and other water borne diseases 8. Conducting emergency hygiene promotion in targeted remote villages prone to AWD/Cholera outbreak and other water borne diseases as well as in schools, nutrition and health centers in the project locations in villages and IDP camps through house house visits, demonstration, as well using IEC materials translated in Somali Language for the targeted beneficiaries 8500 persons (3536 women, 3264 men, 850 boys and 850 girls)9. Chlorination of Water sources (10 shallow wells and 4 boreholes) in the project locations targeting 12000 persons/beneficiaries (4992 women, 4608 men, 1200 boys and 1200 girls)

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and service: Mitigation Measure : Enhancing security at the project sites by hiring security staff/ additional guards as well as ensuring that WASH services are located at place within WASH cluster sphere standards and that beneficiaries don't queue at water for more than 15minutes. This will limit risk associated with long distances by women and girls that are vulnerable to GBV incidences.2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation Measure: Ensuring all staff sign PSEA code of conduct and adhering with it as well as conducting awareness on SEA and the reporting procedures that doesnt expose the victims to the perpetrators.3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.)Mitigation measure: Conducting awareness on protection assistance for survivors of protection incidences4.
