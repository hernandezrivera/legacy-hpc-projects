Project Summary

The 2021 WASH response aims to provide continuity of life-saving support for the most vulnerable host and internally displaced persons (IDP) communities affected by undernutrition, WASH related disease outbreaks, COVID-19 and climate/human induced disasters but will be adapted to shift the focus on activities addressing the direct consequences of COVID-19 and floods. While many shocks continue to have an impact on WASH, acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) and the COVID-19 crisis remain a current threat in Somalia and the situation is unlikely to change for the better as the 2020 Deyr season (October/November) is predicted to be below average to average and La-Nina is expected with a likelihood of drought. Somalia will therefore most likely be faced with rising water scarcity, which requires greater investments in emergency water services and restoration of basic water supply services. The WASH response will contribute to prevention of a further increase in AWD, as well as raise awareness to prevent the spread of COVID19 through risk communication and community engagement, and support compliance to recommended measures through hygiene promotion, hygiene kits distribution and increased access to water and soap. To further improve the contribution of WASH to the achievement of health outcomes, the proposed WASH response will invest in improvements in hand hygiene behaviour and access to WASH facilities in health care facilities and other public institutions. The response will pay particular attention to the different WASH needs of vulnerable groups among the targeted IDPs and non-IDPs including the elderly, persons living with disabilities, women, girls and boys and closely engage them through targeted FGDs, KII and extensive consultations to plan and agree on age and disability-friendly WASH infrastructure designs and installation locations. ACF will support the establishment and training of committees to oversee the operation and management of communal WASH infrastructure. ACF will work with the communities targeted to ensure that the committees are representative of all groups within the community paying particular attention to gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability and tenancy agreements to ensure that the unique needs of each group are put into consideration. The response will target a total of 60,185 (17,033 Men, 16,490 Women, 13,331 Boys and 13,331 Girls) in Bakool, Lower Shabelle, Nugaal, Banadir, Hiran and Sool regions.

Project Activities

The proposed project will target the most vulnerable crises affected households without access to basic WASH services. ACF will provide both emergency and sustainable WASH services in the targeted locations prioritizing cholera/AWD prone areas, communities persistently affected by drought/floods, communities with high malnutrition caseloads and IDP settlements with low WASH coverage.Emergency water supply will target the most vulnerable crises affected households including people living with disability, female headed households and pregnant and lactating women without access to a sufficient amount of potable water and this will run for a period not exceeding two months. To support the speedy recovery of the WASH market environment in the crisis-affected areas, ACF will use her experience in the use of cash and vouchers in WASH to provide drinking water using village water vouchers. The targeted households will be supported to carry out household water treatment and safe storage and provided with containers for transport and storage of water. Further, ACF will carry out bucket chlorination at strategic water points in the targeted communities, monitor the quality of water by testing samples collected at different points in the water supply chain and at the same time invest in rehabilitation and upgrading of strategic water points to ensure basic water access for the targeted populations post the emergency water trucking phase. ACF will construct ramps and aprons at water points to improve access for persons living with disability. To ensure proper operation and maintenance of the new and rehabilitated water points, ACF will support communities to establish gender balanced water management committees. Furthermore women will be involved identification of the strategic water points for rehabilitation.ACF will build the capacity of the committees through joint training with Ministry of Energy, Water and Natural Resources staff and private sector players (suppliers of water equipment and spare parts). As part of disaster risk reduction, the aprons of the water points will raised to protect the facilities from floods and drainage of wastewater included in the design to maintain the water point surroundings free of contaminants. For communities with poor or no access to latrines, ACF will rehabilitate the existing latrines and construct new ones to ensure basic and dignified sanitation access for targeted communities. Communal latrines and bathrooms in health facilities, schools and IDP settlements will be gender segregated and 10% of the latrines will be will be suitably adapted to make them age and disability friendly. The latrines will be constructed within 50 meters distance with a walk way to enable access with assistive devices such as wheelchair. This will include constructing ramps and handles to provide the support. The cash for latrines and Community Led Total Sanitation approaches will be used as sustainable solutions in non-IDP and IDP sites more likely to become permanent settlements, land tenure agreements allowing. ACF will scale up the capacity of local masons to fabricate re-usable dome shaped concrete latrine slabs. To ensure proper use of the newly installed and the existing WASH infrastructure, ACF will recruit and train community hygiene volunteers from the targeted communities to carry out robust hygiene promotion targeting behaviors identified through Barrier Analysis studies. To further contribute to sustainability of the hygiene promotion initiatives, ACF will utilize the increasing capacity of the government departments and conduct joint training for hygiene promoters with public health staff from MoH at district level. ACF will ensure PWDs are consulted and participate in all project phases. PWD representatives will be included as part of water management committees (WMC) and ACF will train the WMC members on disability inclusion to improve their knowledge.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will apply a mix of cash, voucher and in kind programming. In-kind distributions will continue as the dominant delivery model for items not available in the immediate market or are not available in the quality required. Such items include menstrual hygiene materials and water treatment tablets. Cash and vouchers will be prioritized for all other WASH kit items as market assessments in the project areas have confirmed market functionality and availability of the items at prices and of the quality demanded. The CTP approach will enable ACF to contribute to the promotion of a people centered approach as well as promote economic recovery in the target areas and by working directly with the market, ACF will reinforce the existing economic relationships between the targteted beneficiaries and the WASH market players. By strenthening this relationship, the proposed project will contribute to the sustainability of WASH services in the targeted areas.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

ACF has conducted systematic market assessments and participated too in the recent JMMI survey by REACH. The information collected over time has confirmed the availability of WASH goods on the market in addition to confirming re-stocking capacities of the retailers and wholesalers in the intervention areas. As such, ACF has a good understanding of the local WASH market infrastructure and services and how the targeted population interact with themduring both ordinary times and times of crises. ACF will use the acquired market information to inform pthe proposed project preparedness planning to ensure that should shocks occur in the project area, ACF will have capacity to immediately provide lifesaving assistance to the affected populations.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: ACF have strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a strong whistleblowing policy to receive serious complaints. ACF already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and serious complaints are raised to ACF regional office to ensure accountable management. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: ACF will identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and ensure they are also targeted. ACF will identify central locations for delivery of NFI distribution to ensure beneficiaries do not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access them.Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: ACF will engage District local authorities and water management committees’ structures including opinion leaders at the kick planning stage and during implementation to allow them participate in project implementation decisions.

Please provide further explanation

ACF designs its WASH interventions in line with Somalia WASH cluster and SPHERE standard which are aligned to government quality standards to ensure that local capacity and government structures such as Ministry of Water are engaged and incorporated in the programme design. ACF will engage community hygiene volunteers (CHVs) recognized by government authorities to conduct hygiene promotion and awareness. CHVs will also be trained to ensure that they have adequate skills in hygiene promotion for use at the local level even after end of the project. Through community engagement people affected by conflicts and natural disasters are allowed are allowed to get directly involved in the project such as through water management committees. Subsequently ACF will partner with government in the delivery of services and offer capacity building to ensure that joint program implementation strategies are developed and linked to community actions. This will ensure that displaced people are integrated into the interventions and are able to access the services just like the host communities.
