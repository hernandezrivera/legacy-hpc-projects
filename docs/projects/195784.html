Project Summary

CRS South Sudan proposes to support the most impacted communities in Duk, Ayod, and Kapoeta North Counties through the drilling of 15 new and rehabilitation of 45 existing communal boreholes utilizing flood-resistant designs. 45 water point user committees (WPUC) and 10 pump mechanic groups will be trained to manage and maintain the water schemes. The project will also construct water troughs at all water facilities to provide access to drinking water for small ruminant animals, reducing the required amount of time and distance travelled to access water. The project will also integrate these WASH activities with other livelihood interventions to promote multiple water uses. By engaging vegetable producers to grow crops and fruit seedlings near water facilities, enhancing the food and nutrition security of affected communities. These activities will complement and amplify the impact of CRS’ USAID-funded, Pathways to Resilience (P2R) program in Duk and Kapoeta that is implementing resilience building and livelihood activities, including food security and nutrition, in both counties, as well as CRS' health programming in Ayod. Increased access to clean water for personal and domestic use, will enhance the uptake and sustain the use of promoted hygiene and sanitation best practices, contributing to improved nutrition outcomes in the target communities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has experienced three civil wars and decades of multi-level, violent conflict between the government and its opposition as well as sub-national anti-government insurgencies, inter-communal conflict over resources, and rivalries among ethnic groups. This violence, paired with recurring climatic shocks, has traumatized and displaced populations; disrupted agricultural production and adaptive capacity; eroded trade, markets, and basic services; exacerbated food and nutrition insecurity; and increased household dependency on humanitarian aid. Recurring climatic events including drought, flood, livestock disease, and crop pests have also negatively affected production. The target counties are all predicted to be in IPC 4 (food security emergency) in lean season 2023. The target counties are historically under-developed and seriously affected by insecurity and intercommunal conflict. The routine revenge killings and cattle raiding displaces communities, forcing them to concentrate in smaller locations with limited access to clean water and power, hygienic sanitation facilities, etc. Climatic shocks such as unseasonal rains and dry spells as well as crop and animal diseases and pests have reduced food availability. This has contributed to Critical (IPC AMN 4) acute malnutrition situations in these counties. The latest Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) Severity Classification report found that WASH conditions in these counties were at Phase 4 (Emergency) levels. Most communities have limited access to clean water, relying on seasonal streams, unprotected wells, and water ponds for their WASH needs. The number of protected water schemes available are in short supply due to poor surface and groundwater source potentials. In addition, these protected water schemes are often overburdened, nonfunctional, or contaminated. The high reliance on unimproved, open water sources compounds the risk of waterborne and diarrheal diseases in the region. The low access to quantity and quality water also contributes to low rates of hand-washing and other hygiene practices, impacting the health and nutrition of many, especially children. The high unmet community needs for access to clean water has significantly contributed to the emergency conditions in both counties.

2 - Project activities

CRS South Sudan proposes to support the most impacted communities in Budi and Kapoeta North Counties through the drilling of 14 new and rehabilitation of 30 existing communal boreholes. 44 water point user committees (WPUC) and 10 pump mechanic groups will be trained to manage and maintain the water schemes. The project will also construct water troughs at all water facilities to provide access to drinking water for small ruminant animals, reducing the required amount of time and distance travelled to access water. The project will also integrate these WASH activities with other livelihood interventions to promote multiple water uses. By engaging vegetable producers to grow crops and fruit seedlings near water facilities, enhancing the food and nutrition security of affected communities. These activities will complement and amplify the impact of CRS’ USAID-funded, Pathways to Resilience (P2R) program that is implementing resilience building and livelihood activities, including food security and nutrition, in both counties. Increased access to clean water for personal and domestic use, will enhance the uptake and sustain the use of promoted hygiene and sanitation best practices, contributing to improved nutrition outcomes in the target communities. While the geophysical and hydrological characteristics of the terrain makes borehole drilling a complicated process, CRS South Sudan has its own drilling rigs and an experienced drilling crew to meet the task. Prior to the drilling of the boreholes, a geographical survey will be conducted to assess potential water source locations and maximize success rates in securing productive boreholes. Water quality tests will also be conducted at each borehole site to ensure safety for human consumption and use. In areas where borehole drilling is needed and there are no existing boreholes for rehabilitation, P2R will install surface water treatment (SWAT) plans.CRS has worked in Jonglei for over 10 years, and in Kapoeta North County since 2020, addressing a range of humanitarian needs, including food assistance, livelihoods, nutrition, health, social cohesion, and conflict mitigation. This project will complement P2R’s existing resiliency building, food security, and nutrition programming, coordinating with local actors implementing WASH activities in the counties. This includes the WASH and Nutrition Clusters, State and County authorities, and other UN and USAID implementing partners in the region. The proposed project will integrate WASH interventions with P2R’s existing food security and nutrition activities. This will help to enhance acceptance and adoption of promoted sanitation and hygiene practices, reducing the rate of malnutrition due to waterborne diseases. In addition, layering the WASH interventions with P2R’s existing gender, livestock, and social cohesion programming will facilitate greater impact. The participation of women in WPUCs as committee members, pump mechanics, and caretakers will improve their agency, empowering them to take on more leadership roles, participate in decision making at the community level, and improve their access to information and income. CRS programming and operations can sustain strong systems for accountability and program quality. The agency has personnel experienced in WASH emergency implementation and a well-established procurement department to purchase and deliver the required supplies and equipment during the intended time frame. The project will adhere to a “Do No Harm” approach while considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls. In addition, CRS will ensure staff, office space, and vehicles are shared across programs to maximize cost effectiveness.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CRS will work through these coordination fora, as well as directly with other actors on the ground especially WFP and its partners to avoid duplication and make every effort to ensure targeting and adaptive management aimed at maximizing reach to at-risk communities. The project will also adhere to a “Do No Harm” approach while considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls. CRS mitigates risk through strong community engagement and acceptance, long-term relationships with trusted partners, well-developed program design and monitoring systems, high-quality regular audit coverage, and strict internal controls and operating procedures. CRS updates its risk analysis matrix regularly and maintains a risk register for every project. CRS in South Sudan is supported by regional and global experts in security, compliance, PSEA, finance, HR, supply chain and logistics. CRS screens all potential employees, partners and vendors.The CRS Field Safety and Security Plan details procedures to ensure the safety and security of personnel, programs, and assets. The South Sudan Country Representative is responsible for safety and security and makes critical assessments of partners. Locations of outreach services are informed by security assessments. CRS has comprehensive internal controls to prevent misuse or diversion of assets including strict segregation of operational duties. CRS has an established robust supply chain. CRS’ Sub-Recipient Financial Monitoring Policy requires in-person review of subrecipients’ accounting standards, Internal Control Improvement Plans, and regular monitoring per risk-profile. Project staff, MOH staff, volunteers and community leaders will be trained on safeguarding and Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. CRS will implement robust community feedback and complaint mechanisms. CRS’ Code of Conduct and Ethics reinforces the highest standards of integrity; CRS staff must report violations or concerns via confidential reporting mechanisms. CRS investigates allegations of misconduct, protects good-faith reporters, and monitors partners’ compliance with comprehensive safeguarding minimum standards. CRS and HFO will comply with global, national and local COVID-19 prevention measures, including promotion of vaccination.
