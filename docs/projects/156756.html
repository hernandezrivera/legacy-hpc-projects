Project Summary

To alleviate the impact of this crisis on animal source food supply and on the population itself, it was deemed crucial that there be a rapid restoration of the most essential livestock needs for the people in Jiba, Beir Ajam, and Jibata Al Khashab. The recipients will be mostly women, who are traditionally responsible for livestock in rural Syria and who now make up more than 60 percent of the country’s agricultural workforce, “This is often their only source of income to feed their families,”. The distribution of the cattle cows will be conducted in a systematic way, with clear beneficiary selection criteria emphasizing competence and access to feed the animals. The beneficiary should agree to submit a regular data collection and follow-up of the offspring.
MAIN DRIVING FACTORS • Some 60% of the population is comprised of returnees • Livestock prices are likely to increase • 90 percent of households already spending over 75 percent of their income on food.
Quneitra has faced high levels of conflict and displacement. Despite the de-escalation agreement introducing some improvements in security, the area is still at risk of confrontation and expected to remain tense, potentially impacting the supply of commodities from other sources. A proportion of 20% the population are IDPs from surrounding areas many of whom have been displaced for a year or more. About 80% are living in rural areas. Due to the amount of humanitarian support arriving in Quneitra, the current food security situation has been prevented from deteriorating further. Yet despite the levels of assistance, the great majority of households still spend more than 75% of their income on food purchases. The main source of income is agricultural activities (crop production and livestock) followed by skilled labor and humanitarian assistance. The main source of food in Quneitra district is through market purchases. As of last year, some 9 percent received assistance through livelihood-based activities, by provision of livestock (one cow per household with several months’ fodder portion 3.5 ton) in addition to vaccinations and medication. This project, however, aims to support restoration of livelihoods for the most vulnerable population in three areas in Quneitra because they are farmers who depend on livestock for their living. Women and children consist 60% of the society and are fully dependent on livestock breeding for their living. The project will assist 300 households (100 from each town) based on strict selection criteria based on the community and partner agencies feedback. Focus is to provide one cow per family to FHH, farmers under dire need for support, elderly, people with special needs, families with children at age of schooling to ensure their return to study rather than work to support their living. The project will procure 300 cows of the Friesian line with good productivity and adapted local breed, in excellent health condition with veterinary license. Each at 4 years of age and will produce between 10-20 Kilos of milk per day based on its food and care. Cow with the second or third pregnancy, best option, the quantity of the milk is known.

Needs

The crisis has caused at least $16 billion in damage to local agriculture, leading to extensive crop and livestock losses as well as the destruction of greenhouses, irrigation systems, tractors and other assets. (FAO Reports 2018). Rescate field visits to the areas in Quneitra showed the deteriorated standards of living of the farmers in the areas. There is dire need for support to limit the dependency of the population on assistance, which is not received in all the areas. The populations in the selected areas based on the life modality as being strictly farmers covering their living needs from farming, livestock breeding and market purchases and exchanges. The project will target 300 households (+1500 individuals) who are in dire need for support, such as FHH, elderly farmers, families with persons with disabilities, or families with children at schooling age who are obliged to work to support their families. • Livestock prices are likely to increase due to high feed prices and no available pasture until March, which will further affect the population’s purchasing power. • Coping strategies are likely to further deteriorate with the expected seasonal increase of food commodity prices, linked to an increased cost of fuel and transportation in winter. Further loss and depletion of assets through coping strategies to maintain food consumption levels. The project will enhance women empowerment for directly address their need for livestock since they are the main animal breeders in the rural areas. The project will help FHH to meet their families’ needs and support their children to go back to schools. To ensure capacity to care for the cows, one-month fodder will be supplied with each cow to each household after which they can depend on their own income to feed the cow. Communities were identified nationally based on their vulnerability in coordination with the local NGO. Based on community meetings, farmers were identified at the respective pilot sites, A work plan with timeline and budget was developed which outlined the various activities to be implemented. Targeting methodology: Small and marginal owner-farmers: - all returnees who a) live in a rural area at or below subsistence level; b)- are dedicated full- or part-time to agriculture, the families' main source of income is agriculture c) landless laborers; d) are over-dependent for work and livelihood on others with more power and means of production. The poor household women can belong to any of these categories. Beneficiaries Selection Criteria: 1. Female heads of households, breadwinners for their families whose farms are located in vulnerable zones as it relates to events hazards including destruction; 2. Livestock Producers in the past and small farmers practicing farming who lost their livestock to the events; 3. Farmers who demonstrated capacity to produce with the objective of generating reasonable income and utilize the good farming practices in a sustainable manner; 4. Farmer who are willing to provide production and market data to the donor and who are willing to building a dairy cattle house which ensures that the beneficiaries are willing to put resources into the project, the obligations should be within the resource means of the target population; 5. Producers who demonstrate ability and willingness collectively to be part of a farmers’ group; and 6. Farmer willingness to collaborate with other sector partners.

Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented directly by Rescate and in close collaboration with the ministry of agriculture and agrarian reform; where the inputs for this project i.e. milking cows will be obtained. The other actors that Rescate will collaborate with are the Trust and LNGO Association, in addition to local community leaders. Rescate will recruit the project staff including veterinarians’ project manager. This technical team will be led by project Manager and supported by Procurement Officer, M&E Officer among other supporting staff. Rescate will constitute a project management committee comprising local counterpart, agriculture department and the local authority. This committee will support the project implementation process and to ensure that the beneficiaries have a voice in the project implementation process. Together with the stakeholders ONG-Rescate will set criteria for selecting beneficiaries among the returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host households: The beneficiary selection criteria are HHs which are: Not able to plant/have no food stocks; Female headed HHs (e.g. widows) ; Child-headed households, Pregnant/ lactating women headed HHs; That are labour poor (e.g. elderly, malnourished children, persons with disabilities; Not possessing/ have very few livelihood assets e.g. livestock and did not receive livelihoods support (provision of food production inputs).These beneficiaries will be selected in consultation with local leaders and target community members as well the local Agriculture Units in the targeted locations. There will be respect for peoples’ privacy and confidentiality and on any sensitive aspects of targeting and beneficiary selection; Rescate will use proxy methods and/or secondary information where possible.The impact of this response will be that vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families will enjoy a better quality of life, improved food/income security. Expected outcomes of the proposed project is that 300 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community families will benefit from access to milk outputs and products, in addition to the extra income from newborn calves. Although the objective of this project is to address immediate humanitarian needs of people affected by the crisis in Quneitra, the project activities are designed in a way that they will address longer term rehabilitation and resilience of beneficiaries. For example, the breeding cows and milk outputs of the intervention are primarily designed to address immediate acute food insecurity. However, it is milking cows will enable individuals and households to meet additional needs, highlighted through our recent needs assessments. For example, by increasing beneficiaries’ food security we will increase their resilience in response to any future breakdown. ONG-Rescate staff will conduct regular project follow up and planned post-monitoring distribution exercises to assess progress against targets and get also to get feedback from the beneficiaries on the benefits of the intervention. Rescate will share a simplified version of its monitoring framework with key stakeholders (local counterpart, local leaders and beneficiary representatives – men and women and where necessary. Post distribution monitoring will be done during implementation and simple community based reviews and lesson learnt.Meetings will be conducted at least twice in the life time of the project. Distribution will be supervised by volunteers who will provide protection during the distribution and the distribution will take place during day time from 11: 00 am – 2: PM.Exit Strategy: Given the context of the targeted area there is no indication that the current reconciliation will be restored within the year. Moreover, the current rainfall in the area has exceeded the national average which guarantee abundance of grazing areas; Nevertheless, Rescate will distribute a quantity of 100 tons fodder sufficient for several months for each cow in case of bottleneck.

Protection Risk Analysis

Since Rescate is well aware that resources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension, therefore; 1. Rescate will obtain the approval of the concerned authorities on the intervention and implementation of the project. Coordination is mainly with the Governorate office. 2. Rescate had ensured that a comprehensive needs analysis and the response formulation is informed to the concerned community and authorities, it will be openly documented and made transparent.3. Rescate have used the existing community mechanisms to identify needs and arrived at a consensus as of who should be assisted. The mechanisms will be communicated with the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the local authorities and communities concerned.4. Rescate will be keeping the local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress to efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’5. Rescate will ensure that the process of receiving feedback and of responses to it is put in place by establishing a claim mechanism at the Project Management level reachable to all beneficiaries. 6. Rescate will set up and have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities), in consultation with concerned communities. 7. Rescate will ensure that Do No Harm is established and abide to by all staff, partners, and contractors.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Rescate is planning to intervene with livestock distribution in Quneitra 3 towns, namely Jiba, Beir Ajam, and Jibata Al Khashab where the severity level is 5 (2019 HNO). The project objective is providing life-saving and life-sustaining support, and to alleviate suffering and increase access to humanitarian response for vulnerable people and those with specific needs. The intervention will consider support for women and girls amongst others and will contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving community living. Support the resilience of affected local communities, households and individuals within the humanitarian response by protecting and restoring livelihoods and enabling access to essential services and rehabilitation of socio-economic infrastructures.
