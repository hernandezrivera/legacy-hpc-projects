Project Summary

Somalia is prone to epidemic disease outbreaks due to protracted war that has led to total breakdown of health systems. Displacement of people in camps where access to safe water and proper sanitation is limited predisposes these population groups to repeated disease outbreaks especially cholera,measles and Malaria. Early detection and notification of the epidemic disease alerts supports real time response leading to reduction in morbidity and mortality attributed to these diseases.

Needs

Somalia is home to over 5.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. The protracted conflict has led to displacement of over 2 million people in camps where access to safe water and proper sanitation is limited. In 2017 severe drought led to severe food insecurity that led to increased number of children with severe mal nutrition. The country also experiences floods twice a year in the Months of April to June and October to December which lead to flash floods. Displacement of people due to conflict,malnutrition and contamination of water sources are some of the contributing factors to repeated outbreaks of diseases especially cholera and vector borne diseases. In 2007,Somalia recorded one the biggest cholera outbreaks in the past decade where over 78,000 cases including 1000 deaths were reported from 77 districts of 18 regions. This outbreak was attributed to severe water shortage as a result of floods. In the same year over 60,000 cases of measles were reported in all regions as a result of reduced immunity among children experiencing severe food shortage. In December 2017,a new cholera outbreak was reported in Beletweyne Hiraan regions following contamination of water sources leading to cholera outbreak in 4 states located in the basin of rivers Shabelle and Jubba. As of September 2018, over 6000 cases and 42 deaths have been reported. The severity of cholera outbreaks has reduced over time due to implementation of cholera response activities including Oral cholera vaccination,strengthening of water sanitation and hygiene as well as training health workers to manage cholera cases using standard guidelines and protocols. In order to sustain reduction in morbidity and mortality attributed to water and vector borne diseases,there is need to strengthen these interventions in all regions at risk of cholera in Somalia.

Activities

In order to address the needs mentions about,WHO will expand the already established Early Warning Alert and Response network (EWARN) from 484 health facilities to 800 health facilities in all regions. EWARN has been useful to support real time detection and reporting of alerts of epidemic prone diseases in difficult to reach areas. WHO will also establish and train 56 Rapid Response Teams (RR) in 56 high risk districts to support the verification and investigation of alerts of epidemic prone disease. A total of 500 health workers located in EWARN sentinel facilities will be trained in the use of the mobile application to report alerts of epidemic prone diseases,Ministry of Health will be supported to establish the event based surveillance system as recommended by the International Health regulations which will triage and verify alerts of health events from all regions of the country,WHO will support the 3 reference laboratories located in Hargeisa,Garowe and Mogadishu with reagents and equipment to analyse biological samples for infection health hazards, the lab personnel will also be supported with funds for the shipment of samples from all districts to the reference laboratories,Rapid Response teams will be facilitated to conduct field visits to conduct outbreak investigation and initiate response activities at the reporting sites to contain the spread of any outbreak

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The number of displaced populations especially in the south central regions continue to increase as a result of insecurity,severe food shortage and limited access to health care. The increasing number of displaced population leads to increased demand for health services. The insecurity also causes limited access to the affected populations which puts them at risk of high incidence of epidemics. The number f established health facilities as well as qualified health workers working to support displaced population is always limited and health workers turn over is very high leading to delays in the implementation of health services as well as reduction in the quality of services implemented

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In conflicts Women and children are more affected by the effects of disaster especially war. Women usually experience Gender Based Violence including Rape while children are always at high risk of Malnutrition putting them at a higher risk of morbidity and mortality attributed to epidemic prone diseases

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WHO has an established polio network with qualified health workers in all villages and districts. These will be engaged to support the implementation of health activities in hard to reach areas. With support from the health cluster,local non government organisations that have physical presence in selected districts will be engaged and supported to implement health activities in these areas. The MoH has been supported to establish region based surveillance officers who are lead persons for the investigation and verification of health events in all regions. These will be supported to implement similar activities
