Project Summary

The DRC mobile response team aims to support the most vulnerable and underserved populations in Upper Nile and other hard to reach communities in other parts of the country affected by conflict through the provision of multi-sector survival kit, non-food items, and emergency shelter support. All responses are led by a protection centred need assessments that will determine the exact nature of the response. Through this project, DRC will fund a mobile team based in Malakal and Juba with rapid response capability to cover various areas of the Upper Nile State and hard to reach locations prioritized by the cluster and SNFI state focal points. The Juba-based mobile team will quickly deploy to locations of Upper Nile and other prioritized hard to reach locations where land access is limited for the Malakal based mobile team in Upper Nile and rest of the country, respectively. By having mobile teams in Malakal and Juba, DRC will be best placed to support new displacements and spontaneous returns across the Upper Nile and other hard to reach locations throughout South Sudan. Additionally, DRC will continue to have a static presence in Malakal PoC, providing robust emergency shelters and NFI distributions. Reconfiguration of PoC will take place in early 2019, requiring shelter construction for Sector 3 of the PoC and shelter upgrades for approximately 75% of Sectors 1 and 2.
Furthermore, following a thorough market analysis, the static Malakal team will pilot an outreach Cash for Shelter program in the west bank of Malakal and Fashoda County in areas where local resources and markets are functioning.
All S/NFI materials (with the exception of the Cash for Shelter activity) will be provided by the cluster pipeline and will not be budgeted under the project. These items will include survival kit, NFI kits, and emergency shelter items distributed by the cluster. The beneficiary calculation for the mobile emergency response teams is in line with the USD $13 per beneficiary targeted by the cluster, including household members (i.e. one S/NFI kit directly benefitting all members of a household), while Cash for Shelter is $25. The response missions will be followed by a rapid monitoring exercise during the response mission in hard to reach locations where access is limited to redeploy team for evaluation and post-distribution monitoring in locations that are easily accessible to the redeployment of a team. The PDM and the rapid monitoring exercise will highlight any risks by the S/NFI intervention as well as lessons for considerations for future responses. Beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC and cluster interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to make a complaint. A formal feedback mechanism will be fed through DRC’s hotline whereby complaints or queries may be logged and responded to remotely.

Needs

The revitalization of the South Sudanese peace process and freshly signed agreements by the parties to the conflict promise to offer new opportunities in 2019 for the country and its conflict-affected women, men, girls and boys. Yet, after half a decade of the most recent conflict, sustained violence and destruction of lives and livelihoods, about two thirds of the people in country remain in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection – the same proportion as in 2018. People’s protection needs remain severe, while violence and violations of international humanitarian law (IHL) persist as a reality of daily life for many South Sudanese – in particular for women and children. Close to 2.5 million people have fled the country and sought refuge in the region, while nearly 2 million people are estimated to remain internally displaced. Together, this represents close to one in three South Sudanese. The conflict has caused about 382,000 excess deaths, approximately half from violence. Conflict-related sexual violence is a defining feature of the crisis. Sexual violence represents 2 per cent of all reported cases of gender-based violence. In first 9 months of 2018, there were some 800 known survivors, including very young children. Some 1,70,0006,523 women, men and children will not have adequate shelter and NFI conditions in 2019. The number of people in need represents a 11 per cent decrease from the previous year. Needs remain high particularly in Jonglei (Ayod, Nyirol, Uror and Akob), Unity (Rubkona, Koch, Leer and Panyjar), Western Bahr el Ghazel (Wau and Jur river) and Central Equatoria (Juba and Yei). Amongst the IDP population, women and children will remain especially vulnerable both due to their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. A small number of host community members do not have sufficient ES/NFIs as they have used their already limited resources to provide for the displaced. People’s ES/NFI needs are primarily linked to conflict-induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. People outside of camp-like settings have better access to shelter materials, their overall ES/NFI needs are traditionally broader due to the absence of functional and accessible markets and lack of communal connections or coping mechanisms from other resources. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacement. Any new outbreaks of violence are expected to prevent people from moving out of camp-like settings in large numbers. One noticeable trend since the conflict broke out in 2013 is how multi-displaced people tend to move along distinguishable routes, in which case they have grown accustomed to the environment and receiving humanitarian services at certain points along the way. By understanding where host communities have provided support and where other services are available, the need for NFIs may decrease. At the same time, the number of returnees from neighbouring countries is expected to increase in 2019, based on 2018 trends. Returnees’ needs include training in utilizing natural resources and participating in the local economy. Additionally, returnees need support in understanding their housing, land, and property rights in cases where their land has been given away or reused. High rates of poverty, recurrent inter-tribal disputes for instance in Kajo Keji, Pibor and other areas; coupled with extended displacement has led to loss of ES/ NFIs and also diminished ability to access basic household items.

Activities

Activity 1: Conduct 12 S/NFI analysis in newly affected areas highlighting key recommendation for response interventions or any associated risks and mitigation measures. The semi-static S/NFI team in Malakal or mobile team in Juba will be deployed to prioritized areas for assessment/ analysis for a period of one week. The assessments/ Analysis aim to identify the S/NFI needs and identify the best way of delivering SNFI assistance considering the protection risks associated with such assistance and a key consideration of DO NO HARM. The mobile team will use the new cluster analysis tool. Based on the findings, a response mission will be planned if necessary. DRC during the project period aims at conducting 12 assessments/ analysis that guides the response. Activity 2: Provide access to emergency shelter (In- Kind). DRC will distribute life-saving shelter to most vulnerable, IDP and returnee individuals/households in frontline areas based on the analysis report identifying the needs and the protection and do no harm considerations. The S/NFI response missions will be conducted over a period of one to two weeks, and the same team who conducted the analysis would be deployed to the location. Response interventions include distribution of emergency shelter kit based on the cluster standard and recommendation. DRC will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its interventions. DRC aims to reach 8,400 Individuals through the emergency shelter provision. Activity 3: Provide access to life-saving NFIs (in-kind). Distribution of life-sustaining NFI items to most vulnerable IDP and returnee individuals/households in frontline areas based on the analysis report identifying the needs and the protection and do no harm considerations. The S/NFI response missions will be conducted over a period of one to two weeks, and the same team who conducted the analysis would be deployed to the location. Response interventions include distribution of emergency NFI kit based on the cluster standard and recommendation. DRC will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its interventions. DRC aims to reach 8,400 Individuals through the emergency NFI intervention. Activity 4: Shelter upgrade to most vulnerable returnees through Cash for Shelter program. Through the static program in Malakal POC, DRC will pilot an outreach Cash for Shelter program in the West Bank of Malakal and Fashoda County in areas where local resources and markets are functioning. The protection team will do the profiling of the PSNs with critical conditions with risk of exposure due to the current shelter conditions and directly refer identified beneficiaries to the S/NFI team for shelter assistance. DRC will conduct a thorough market analysis to determine the modality of intervention either through direct assistance or use of vouchers. DRC will encourage the use of local resources including local poles and grasses through a supply linked with local traders. Through this activity, DRC aims to reach 200 HHs (1,200 Individuals). The shelter construction activity will be conducted using local skilled labour through CfW activities.Activity 5: Monitoring of quality service provision (Remote or field monitoring). For each mission DRC will deploy a quick monitoring tool to monitor the quality of service provision. Rapid response monitoring will be conducted in the days following distributions and will be factored into mission planning and daily activities. DRC aims to conduct 3 PDM in areas that are accessible for redeployment of a monitoring team. The PDM will highlight any risks exacerbated by DRC’s NFI/Shelter interventions. DRC will use in independent team from the MEL department to conduct the PDMs. Activity 6: Provision of shelter upgrades in Malakal PoC. In Malakal PoC, DRC will provide shelter upgrades for approximately 75% of households in Sectors 1 and 2 of the PoC. A total of 5,200 HH (13,200 Individuals) will benefit from the shelter upgrade assistance.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

Each response missions will be guided by the analysis report that comes out from the community consultation. DRC will use the cluster analysis tool in each location of response and will produce an analysis report. Where possible DRC will follow the cluster recommended number of community consultations to ensure information is gathered from a different margin of the communities. DRC will use the list of recommended analysis and consultation questions that will serve as a guide to ensure that appropriate information is obtained to inform post crises shelter and NFI response. DRC will deploy the HHs questionnaire and Focus group discussion with the different margin of the communities to gather consultative and representative information. HHs questionnaire will be gathered from a minimum of 90 representatives HHs to bring a qualitative data to guide response. Furthermore, DRC will conduct Focus group discussion to gather qualitative information including suggestions and opinions of the affected community from a different margin of the affected populations. Though there is no standard guideline on deciding how many Focused Group Discussion DRC will Ideally gather information from 6 FGDs in areas where the SNFI assessment is planned (1 woman and 1 man with the affected group and 1 woman and 1 man with the host families and 2 with other segments including vulnerable groups based on age, gender and diversity). The FGD will ideally participate 6-12 participants. The participants comprising different segments of the proposed community, including separately from men and women, Host communities and IDPs/Returnees. In cases where the time frame is limited the number of FGDs can be reduced to 3 to 4, ensuring participation from different segments or semi-structured discussion can be held with a small group of people (3-5). Ideally, DRC aims to gather consultative information from 900 (360 Women, 65 Men, 225 Boys, 225 Girls) Individuals representing 6 affected communities. The information will be summarized and then a debrief consultation will be held with the community representative to re-verify and validate the information gathered through the HHs consultation and the FGDs with the community representatives.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

DRC has put in place global organisational policies and procedures to safeguard against harmful behaviour; DRC is in full agreement with the UN zero tolerance policy relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Violations constitute acts of serious misconduct and are grounds for disciplinary measures up to and including dismissal, as described in sections 3.7 and 3.9 of DRC’s Code of Conduct (CoC). DRC implements awareness raising in project sites on PSEA, provides training to staff on PSEA and CoC, and has complaints and feedback mechanisms in place in all operational areas. Moreover, DRC also commits to organisational diversity and gender equity through adherence to the DRC Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) Vision and Standards policy.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritizing, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC is a certified member of the Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) and as such fulfils the HAP benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. Fundamental to all of DRC's activities is adherence to Age and Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) by engaging the various community members and existing institutions and CBOs including women groups, youth groups, and elderly groups. Through both formal and informal interactions with representative groups and other community members, members of the community are empowered to inform the type of activities and services provided by DRC and ensure their implementation is relevant, effective, and responsive to the needs of displaced populations.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

In all project sites, DRC operates complaints desks to enable residents to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline number that beneficiaries can call to register complaints or requests.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community members will be engaged in the project implementation from the assessment phase through the response and evaluation phases, whereby they will be encouraged to identify gaps and needs, confirm response strategies, and they will be highly involved in the identification of beneficiaries, and the information dissemination strategies. To ensure two-way dialogue, DRC will also implement a two-way feedback and accountability mechanism. This will include community focus groups whilst on the ground, but also use of DRC's national complaints and feedback hotline based in juba which will enable affected populations to communicate with DRC by phone even once the mobile team has left the area.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With a mandate for protection, DRC mainstreams protection into all sectors of the DRCs programs in South Sudan and will do so for the proposed activity in accordance with the Global Protection Cluster guidelines. Furthermore, DRC’s Mobile response is designed with a protection lead approaches whereby service provisions are seen to reduce protection risks associated with access to services and enhance the safety and dignity of affected populations.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is designed to minimize negative effects which could increase beneficiaries’ vulnerability to risks. DRC adheres to AGDM standards and will ensure that beneficiaries have assistance linked to their needs, without any barriers. Once risk factors have been identified through the integrated protection and NFI assessments, DRC will ensure the recommended response is conducted in adherence with the protection recommendations.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project addresses inequalities and protection threats by conducting thorough protection risk assessments throughout the project cycle, which inform protection programming and allow for immediate responses to identified inequalities and protection threats. DRC will mainstream protection into its SNFI response activities to ensure affected and at-risk communities and individuals are identified and assisted in a safe manner. Following the Sphere standards and the IASC Guidance on assessments, DRC will ensure ethical principles are strictly adhered to. These considerations include do no harm for survivors (for example by ensuring all staff members are trained on PFA), ethical, survivor-centred response, and best interest of the individual.

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

All SNFI team will be trained in protection mainstreaming including in protection principles, do no harm, meaningful access, safety, dignity, accountability, and participation in the day to day activities. All SNFI interventions will also adhere to the GBV principles which include: safety, confidentiality, survivor-centred approach, respect, non-discrimination, and do no harm. DRC will manage an accountability system to ensure that affected populations receive information, provide feedback, and complaints. The DRC hotline will compliment this accountability. Sensitization to communities on the functionality of DRC hotline contributes to the accountability. In these areas of poor coverage, DRC will work with communities to ensure they are provided with avenues of reporting, through interviews, follow-ups, or the presence of MEL specialists to ensure DRC remains accountable to targeted populations.
