Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 420,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2023. In a context of rising inflation and additional economic hardship caused by COVID-19, Palestine refugees are more and more affected by food insecurity and therefore increasingly depend on UNRWA assistance to meet their minimum food needs. The Agency will continue to monitor the availability of food on the markets and will retain operational flexibility to adjust to a changing context as appropriate. The composition of the food basket was developed based on WFP guidelines and in view of meeting 50% of daily nutritional needs for 4 months.
UNRWA's original plan is to distribute 3 rounds of in-kind food assistance, each round covering a period of 4 months. However, this depends on the availability of funds. Until January 2023, UNRWA received or confirmed pledges are only sufficient to cover part of the first round of 2023. The Agency will continue to mobilize resources to ensure funding for the food rounds are secured. In 2023, the number of people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance including for food needs is 420,000 Palestine refugees to strengthen their resilience and prevent further deterioration into acute food insecurity. As per its emergency planning, UNRWA aims to provide food assistance as well as multipurpose cash assistance, and protection interventions at both household and community levels, as well as the coordination of these activities across the different Sectors framework. The in-kind food assistance program remains one of the most important forms of UNRWA emergency support to stabilize the food security status for Palestine refugees in Syria with the MPC based on complementarity approach considering that MPC transfer value will not cover the full needs of the beneficiaries and the potential unavailability of fund. , as it helps them meet part of their basic nutritional and survival requirements (~50% of kcal/person/day) which will increase resilience, and reduce humanitarian needs and the spiraling food insecurity levels.

3 - Needs

Syria hosts a significant number of Palestinian refugees, and the majority of them continue to require assistance. Of the 438,000 Palestinian refugees in Syria, 60% have been displaced at least once. In addition to their refugee status, Palestinian remain equally affected by the broader macro-economic, and social conditions affecting Syria as a country. There is a growing lack of food or money to buy food. As per HNO 2023, the continued deterioration of the food and nutrition situation and the increased reliance and adoption of negative coping strategies has impacted the resilience of households (HHs). As many as 77% of HHs reported buying less expensive and less preferred foods resulting in poorer diets (72% with low and medium diets).According to an UNRWA post distribution monitoring (PDM) survey conducted in Aug. 2022, only 43% of assessed households demonstrated acceptable food consumption patterns – indicative of access to adequate quantity and quality of food; 40% were borderline and 17% showed poor food consumption patterns. The PDM findings also showed that 36% of assessed HHs were practicing some form of coping strategies to respond to dwindling food stocks: of them, 1% showed minimum coping strategies, 17% stress level coping strategies and 18% crisis-level coping strategies. The majority of coping strategies used were reliance on cheap, less expensive food (35%), borrowing or relying on help from friends (31%), and reducing the number of meals eaten per day (19%).

4 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA will provide in-kind food assistance to 420,000 PalRefs through three distribution rounds. Each round will cover 4 months of food assistance. Each food ration for one person will be composed of the following items: W/Flour (10 kg), Rice (3kg), S.F. oil (3 lt), Sugar (1 kg), Chick Peas (3 kg), Lentils (3 kg), Milk (0.4 kg), Bulgar weat (3 kg). The composition of the food basket was developed based on WFP guidelines. Wheat flour was included in the basket in 2021 for the first time, following WFP and Agency-wide guidelines. Distribution will be reorganized to adjust to the important quantities of wheat that will be distributed and ensure a smooth distribution process without overcrowding. Procurement will be either internationally or locally depending on price competitiveness’s and timely availability. UNRWA will distribute the food baskets through its distribution centres in targeted governorates. All precautions measures will be taken to limit COVID-19 contamination risks during the distributions. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has a dedicated Area Support Team and a SFO based Programme Support Team. Monitoring will be guaranteed through regular field visits and feedback from implementing staff based in the field and their respective programme. The UNRWA PSO has a Monitoring and Evaluation system in place that closely follows up on and provides feedback and donor reports for all projects.MPC transfer value was reduced to ensure complementarity with this intervention

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1 Beneficiaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from distribution points (Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium) • Mitigating measures: 1. In order to minimize problems in accessing distribution points, flexibility allowed within the families as to who collects the assistance. A delegation system allows individuals to nominate a person to collect the assistance on their behalf. 3. Focus on community engagement and participation enhanced, and feedback responded to. RISK 2 Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, particularly at locations with high numbers of beneficiaries (Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium) • Mitigating measures: 1. Vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and PwDs prioritized in order to reduce their waiting time. 2. Training provided on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion, ensuring principles such as safety and dignity and access are incorporated. 3. Protection audits conducted on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve services and optimize compliance to the main protection principles. RISK 3 Explosive remnants of war (ERW) risk for Palestine refugees when accessing UNWRA services (Likelihood: low, Impact: high) • Mitigating measures: 1. Awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children (awareness sessions and activities, awareness material, social media campaigns).

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RISK 1 UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. RISK 2: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. RISK 3: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
