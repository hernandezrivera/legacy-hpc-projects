Project Summary

Purpose:
To survey and clear priority cluster munition strikes as well as UXO spot tasks reported by the community in border regions in Southern South Sudan (especially in Juba, Magwi, and Torit counties) to provide safe access and land in areas with high rates of returns; building resilience by releasing land back to the communities than can be used for agriculture, grazing, and settlement.
As knowledge regarding contamination of explosive remnants of war (ERW) in South Sudan is not complete, returnees and South Sudanese refugees and IDPs play a crucial role in identifying additional areas where clearance is needed. The Juba-Nimule highway is a high-priority area for MAG to re-focus efforts as contamination straddles return routes and the highway is of key importance for traffic and transport from Uganda to South Sudan.
The project will also educate displaced persons, returnees, and host community members about the dangers of and safe behaviors to adopt when encountering explosive hazards, with an emphasis on reaching men and boys who are most at risk of death or injury. In addition, the project will train community focal points who can provide safety briefings to returnees, while also increasing the number of women in leadership positions in the community.
MAG is working to ensure that the voices of women and girls, people living with disabilities and other marginalized groups are heard during community sessions and that all members of the community are able to attend and contribute. Strategies include gender balanced female and male community liaison and clearance team staff, the recruitment of women as volunteer Community Focal Points and work with women’s groups. Ensuring that marginalized groups are listened to during these sessions is particularly important as information gathered can be used to inform future prioritization of sites for clearance.
Target:
• Focus on clearing a minimum of 300 EOD spot tasks as well as clearance of small high-impact task sites, destroying 100% of hazards identified, benefitting a minimum of 2000 people
• Over 12 months MAG will clear 150,000 m2 of cluster munition strikes in Central and Eastern Equatoria, directly benefitting 400 people
•Deliver lifesaving EORE to over 8000 vulnerable people, 50% of beneficiaries must be either returnees or IDPs
•Train 100 (W:50, M:50) community focal points
•In coordination with UNMAS and UNMISS, address immediate hazards impeding humanitarian access to target locations, and will reduce safety risks for displaced and transient women, girls, boys and men who, typically, are more exposed to explosive ordnance risks in an emergency.
Summary of activities:
- 1 hybrid mixed gender-balanced emergency multi-task (MTT) and community liaison (CL) team focusing on clearance of spot task and explosive ordnance risk education
- 1 gender-balanced mobile surge team that can respond to high impact clearance requests

1 - Needs

The people of South Sudan are still living in danger from the decades long conflict between the SPLA and the SAF. During the long fight for the eventual independence of South Sudan, both sides used anti-personnel and anti-tank landmines and the SAF dropped cluster bombs throughout the area. In addition, fighting from both the war with the Khartoum government and subsequent civil conflict within South Sudan left behind other explosive remnants of war (ERW) such as air-dropped bombs, mortars and small arms ammunition. These bombs and explosive hazards, left behind from conflict, continue to kill, maim, and disrupt lives and livelihoods long after the conflict has ended. In 2021 alone, there have been 7 reported deaths from mines and ERW, as well as 29 casualties (IMSMA monthly report, December 2021 sent via email from UNMAS), although this number is likely underreported due to the lack of transportation, medical and communications infrastructure in the country. In total, there have been a recorded 5,167 injuries and deaths from mines and ERW, according to IMSMA, as of December 2021. Because of this threat to physical safety, explosive hazards also reduce the ability of community members to access land, services such as schools and clinics, and natural resources such as natural water sources, wood for charcoal, and land for grazing. Over half of all recorded hazardous areas (amounting to a total of 18 million square metres) are in Central and Eastern Equatoria, as of December 2021. Seven of the ten most contaminated counties by number of open hazardous areas (HA) were in Central and Eastern Equatoria: Juba, Magwi, Terekeka, Torit, Yei, Morobo and Lainya.The most sever contamination is primarily along the border counties, areas where people displaced either internally or externally are expected to return to. Given their prevalence in the Equatoria region, this project will focus on clearance of cluster bomb strikes. Explosive hazards affect all members of the community: women, girls, boys, and men. However, men and boys are at an elevated risk of being harmed by mines and explosive remnants of war (ERW), making up 74% of known accidents in the IMSMA database between the year 2000 and June 2019 (data provided by UNMAS; casualties nation-wide: 74% male, 16% female, 10% not specified or unknown). In addition, men often are more difficult to reach with risk education: in many communities, young men may be away from their villages with their cattle, in other communities they may be focused on farming activities and may be reluctant to take time away from their farming activities to attend risk education sessions. Community liaison teams, upon deployment to the community, conduct focus group discussions with community leaders, youth, and women (and possibly other groups, e.g. cattle keepers, if relevant to the local context) to determine what types of contamination different members of the community encounter. As knowledge regarding contamination in South Sudan is not complete, returnees and South Sudanese refugees in Uganda play a crucial role in identifying additional areas where clearance is needed. Given the overall food insecurity in-country, freeing high priority areas with high agricultural potential (the Equatoria region is considered to be the breadbasket of South Sudan) as well as high rates of return is key to address and improve the humanitarian situation in South Sudan.

2 - Project activities

MAG proposes to deploy:- 1 hybrid mixed gender-balanced emergency multi-task (MTT) and community liaison (CL) team focusing on clearance of spot task and EORE only- 1 gender-balanced mobile surge team that can respond to high impact clearance requests prioritizing the Equatoria border regions (due to the high agricultural potential) and more specifically return routes from Uganda and Kenya due to expected high number of returnees in the coming months. The Juba-Nimule highway is a high-priority area for MAG to re-focus efforts as contamination straddles return routes and the highway is of key importance for traffic and transport from Uganda to South Sudan. As knowledge regarding contamination in South Sudan is not complete, returnees and South Sudanese refugees in Uganda play a crucial role in identifying additional areas where clearance is needed. Having worked closely with NGOs in border regions, MAG will continue strengthening its links with refugee-led organizations as well as engage in public sensitization campaigns , strengthening digitalization via the use of radios. • Focus on clearing a minimum of 300 EOD spot tasks as well as clearance of small high-impact task sites, destroying 100% of hazards identified, benefitting a minimum of 2000 people• Deliver lifesaving EORE to over 8000 vulnerable people, 50% of beneficiaries must be either returnees or IDPs• Train 100 (W:50, M;50) community focal points In recognition that emergencies will likely continue to arise in coming years, MAG will maintain the ability to rapidly deploy clearance and / or EORE capacities to emerging crises. - In coordination with UNMAS and UNMISS, address immediate hazards impeding humanitarian access to target locations, and will reduce safety risks for displaced and transient women, girls, boys and men who, typically, are more exposed to explosive ordnance risks in an emergency. • Over 24 months MAG will clear 150,000 m2 of cluster munition strikes in Central and Eastern Equatoria, directly benefitting 400 people

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Community feedback will be used to determine locations where teams are deployed, prioritizing land for agriculture and other livelihoods activities as well as risk reduction along movement corridors.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?
7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

As of March 2021, MAG is co-chairing the Mine Action Sub-Cluster together with UNMAS and will thus further strengthen its work within the protection cluster and other partners. In the past, it MAG remains an active participant of the UN’s protection cluster and the mine action sub-cluster, and has also participated in meetings of the Housing, Land, and Property Rights Technical Working Group in 2019. MAG’s technical capacity is imperative to improving access and providing an understanding among aid workers deployed in South Sudan on how to recognize and avoid threats from explosive remnants of war. MAG’s teams operating in population centres provide EORE to humanitarian agency staff in the field.Via its teams on the ground MAG will share information either at state level or at Juba level.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

MAG has a well-established suggestions and complaints mechanism, with various options for feedback provision. We have easily seen and accessible locked boxes in each location, where both staff and local community members can put all their feedback anonymously. We also train our community liaison teams to be gender-inclusive focal points for the feedback in case community members wish to raise any concerns. We also advertise contact details and easily understood (in local languages and pictures) reporting chain in case anyone would want to raise concerns via phone or internet. All submitted concerns are always responded to within a defined delay, following MAG policies on safeguarding.
