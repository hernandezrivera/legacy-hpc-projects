Project Summary

Between 2021 and 2022, Gaza has seen the most serious escalation of hostilities since 2014 and 15 years of blockade caused significant damage and loss of life, increasing aid dependency and exacerbating poverty and food insecurity .MSNA 2022 indicated that 65% of HHs in Gaza are suffering from moderate or severe food insecurity.
The project is in line with HRP’s strategic objective 3 “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks” through provision access to food for the food insecure HHs
The project will target vulnerable households in East and
North Gaza to meet their basic food needs. These communities are
seriously struggling to meet their basic needs, from food to health, as a result of the
extensive destruction of their livelihood assets. This is aggravating the chronic shelter,
infrastructure and energy deficits in these areas, which is leading to increased levels of
food insecurity across the strip.
SCI will provide food assistance to 600 food insecure households (around 3360
individuals) to enable them to meet their basic needs and enhance their access to
sufficient food intake. SCI will receive a preliminary list of beneficiaries from the Ministry
of Social Development (MoSD). SCI will conduct socioeconomic assessments in order to
verify the information provided by MoSD, and will then crosscheck the final list with the
Food Security Sector (FSS) to avoid duplication and overlapping in its areas of
intervention.
SCI, will conduct the socio-economic assessments and sensitization sessions, will provide e-voucher food assistance
for a period of six months. Monthly top-ups will be calculated based on the number of
household members (57 ILS for each family member) The food assistance value is expected to cover 60 % of
the household’s food needs. The list of redeemable food items will be based on the list of
food items in the FSS’ basket, in addition to fresh fruits, vegetables, fresh meat and eggs.
SC will select the beneficiaries from MoSD nominated list of beneficiaries , SCI will prioritize female-headed households (at least 25% of the overall target) and households with low income and larger household size, especially with members with a
disability, children under the age of five, pregnant or lactating women, and child-headed
households. SC will use its existing valid framework agreement with the vendors to support the

intervention. program. For the new areas, SC will select and contract new vendors to
support the program for each new geographical area where the project will be
implemented. The selection of vendors will include an evaluation of their capacity to
supply the required goods in quantity and quality on time the compliance with safety
conditions.

1 - Needs

The economy in Gaza continues to deteriorate as a result of the ongoing 15 year blockade, cyclical violence and destruction, restrictions on economic activity, limited market access and poor governance. With an employment rate of 47%, of which 64% are women - unemployment rates in Gaza are amongst the highest in the world (PCBS 2021). The Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022 indicated that 81% of households in Gaza reported challenges in meeting their basic needs, with 65% of households suffering from moderate or severe food insecurity and over 50% reported aid assistance as their primary source of income. The MSNA reported that around 89% of households employ stress, crisis, or emergency livelihood coping strategies such as buying food on credit, borrowing money for food, rationing, and reducing food consumption. Female-headed households are among the most at risk of poverty and food insecurity given gender and social norms; on average and according to the MSNA, they spend 30% less on food than their male counterparts. They also face additional barriers in owning land (owned generally by men) or working outside their homes, as this is not deemed appropriate. This limits their ability to earn an income and meet their basic needs and of their families. People with disabilities heading households are another group of concern as they face additional barriers in terms of movement or accessing jobs. In October, 2022, SCI conducted socioeconomic needs assessments for food assistance with 1,652 households across Gaza, in coordination with the MoSD. Results showed that the monthly average income for surveyed households was 390 Israeli Shekel (ILS), which is far lower than the Minimum Expenditure Basket of 1,717 ILS advised by the Cash Working Group. Between March and August 2022, SC conducted a second socio-economic assessments with 260 HHs in Gaza, which were nominated by the MoSD to benefit from food assistance. The results showed that the monthly average income for surveyed HHs was 493 NIS, which is far lower than the threshold borrowed from the Cash Working Group through the cost of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (1717 NIS). Also, the results showed that the average household expenditure for food represents 87% of HH income. The assessment highlighted that 91% of the surveyed households use negative coping mechanisms such as, taking debits and loans from vendors, relatives, and friends and reducing food consumption, which increases exposure to health risks. Results also showed that more than 9% of households had a food consumption score (FCS) of less than 21 (poor) and that 25% of households had a FCS of less than 35 (borderline). Food insecurity in female-headed households was higher with more than 70% of female-headed households .recording an FCS of poor or borderline. The results were confirmed by FGD conducted by Beit Lahia Development Association in September 2022 with 23 vulnerable women-headed HHs. The participants stated that access to food is the main concern for their families and they are applying negative coping mechanisms such as reducing the consumption of foodIn 2022, We World ( WW-GVC) in coordination with SCI, conducted a MSNA in 11 communities in North and East Gaza to identify livelihood risks and explore ways to support youth employment. The assessment was conducted through focus group discussions (FGD), in coordination with SCI’s Community Protection Committees (CPC). The FGDs indicated an increase in the poverty rate in these communities due to the destruction of livelihood assets in the most recent escalations in August 2022. They stated that the blockade, shortages in water, and the energy deficit has led to a decline in agricultural and commercial small business revenues and household income. Results also indicated that poor public services and the lack of medicines in public clinics, the lack of drinking water, and electricity outages increase the financial burden on poor families and thus reduce the food share of their already low income. Increased food insecurity has also led to an increase in the number of malnutrition cases reported among the children; the community nutrition screening for children conducted via an ongoing SCI project in four municipalities in Rafah and Khan Younis in 2021-2022 revealed an 8.5% increase in malnourished amongst children in 2022.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Selection of beneficiaries: SCI will request a list of nominated beneficiaries from MoSDA based on the essential selection criteria including food insecure HHs, HHs with low income and large family size. SCI will receive a preliminary list of beneficiaries from the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) based on the essential selection criteria which include food insecure HHs, HHs with low income . SCI will conduct a socioeconomic assessment to verify the information provided by MoSD using the CWG’s Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT), The selection will prioritize must vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, children under the age of five, and dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, malnourished and disabled children, SCI will coordinate with the existing CPCs and community-based organizations (CBO) to ensure reach to most vulnerable HHs . Based on scoring system, SC will develop a list of potential beneficiaries and will then crosscheck the list with the FSS to avoid duplication and overlapping in its areas of intervention. Activity 2: Conduct Sensitization and information sharing workshops for beneficiaries and vendors: SC will conduct sensitization sessions with the selected beneficiaries (men, women, and adolescents) to inform them about the project objective, scope, selection criteria and duration. The sessions will also cover our safeguarding mechanisms, including PSEA, as well as the feedback and accountability mechanisms. Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials will be provided, including guidance on using e-vouchers, vendors’ addresses and SCI’s hotline number. SCI will also discuss the list of allowed items (food categories) and make the needed amendments to the list based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries to best meet their specific needs. SCI will also agree with beneficiaries on the redemption plan and discuss their suggestions to address any challenges they may face. Additionally, SCI will conduct sensitization sessions with vendors, providing vendors with training on the distribution process, including instructions on filling out the voucher distribution list, keeping records, and steps to avoid corruption and fraud. SCI will ensure that vendors are aware of and are able to comply with the SCI’s child safeguarding, fraud and bribery, and sexual harassment policies. Activity 3: Provide food assistance to 600 food insecure vulnerable households: SCI will provide e-voucher food assistance to 600 food insecure vulnerable households (3360 individuals) for a period of six months. Each household will receive a monthly top-up that will be calculated based on the number of household members (57 ILS for each family member) Based on CWG update , August 2022, the household size of six individuals will receive an average monthly amount of approximately $100, based on the rate in local currency and information from the Cash Working Group MEB yearly update (around 60% from the updated Food Basket value. Each food -voucher will be charged with the assistance amount at the time of debit card distribution. To keep a detailed record of all vouchers, vendors will be required to collect the signed receipt (machine generated) following each transaction, which will be later shared with SCI. SCIwill ensure the quality of implementation by close monitoring and supervision of the e-voucher distribution and the collection of items by beneficiaries in each round of distribution.. Monitoring E-Voucher use (Post Distribution Monitoring-PDM): The post-distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted to understand if the e-food vouchers were effective in meeting household member’s food needs, including boys, girls, men and women with and without disabilities. Questions will be asked to understand if the mechanism was appropriate, if vouchers were not exchanged for cash or if the selection of food items was done by both men and women of the household. The team will ensure the regular follow-ups and monitoring to the platform, which enables monitoring and the ability to track the items with high redemption to ensure balance efficient use of the vouchers.

3 - Indicators

In line with FSS objective 2: Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs the intervention will measure the following indicators: - # of beneficiaries receiving food vouchers (600HHs) 3360 individuals - % of targeted households with enhanced food consumption score (FCS) (80% of targeted households) - # of targeted households protected from harmful coping mechanisms ( 420 HHs). The MEAL system of SC considers monitoring as a live function for systematically tracking the implementation of actions that form part of a project/program resulting in effective, performance-based project/program management. It provides basic tools to collect information to assess a project’s efficiency and effectiveness. SC has developed a comprehensive list of Quality Benchmarks (QBMs) and continuously updates these. QBMs monitoring visits will be conducted to project activities on a monthly basis and reported to program management for their consideration and action. In addition, SC MEAL has a set of minimum standards and standard processes determine minimum requirements to be adhered to by project staff throughout the whole implementation cycle, from initial assessment and selection to post-project activities and review. SC team will train beneficiary partners in SC Child Safeguarding, SC Gender Policy and SC financial policies with the focus on preventing fraud and corruption. To ensure smooth implementation and manage expectations, SC organizes sessions for beneficiaries to inform them about the selection methodology and targeting process’ the project duration. SC field team will monitor the redemption process in each distribution point to ensure that quality of food items and prices are in accordance with the signed agreements as well as to provide the needed support to beneficiaries.The field team will provide daily update to the program officer including challenges and updates on venders’ and beneficiaries’ compliance to SC policies and program requirements.At the beginning of project, as a part of beneficiaries selection process, SC will conduct socio-economic assessment for each HHs. The assessment will reveal the coping mechanism for each HH and its impact on the family’s life.SC will ensure the quality of implementation by close monitoring and supervision of the voucher distribution and the collection of items by beneficiaries for each monthly round of distribution. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) activities will be completed after the distribution to collect feedback from the targeted families, both children and parents, about the process and how the acquired items have impacted their situation. The post-distribution monitoring will include follow-up on the use of the materials to ensure vouchers were not exchanged with others for cash. In addition, PDM will assess the improvement of coping mechanisms against the socio-economic results for each HH conducted at the beginning of the project. The MEAL team will develop monthly report on the results of PDM. SC will ensure that corrective measures resulting as a result of these monitoring activities are implemented where and when needed.As part of the vendor on-boarding process, SC will conduct sensitization sessions with the staff of the selected vendors covering SC’s key requirements and policies. Through on-the spot monitoring visits to the vendors, SC MEAL will be looking at the effectiveness of the voucher redemption process, checking the quality, quantity, and pricing of the food commodities, and if the vendors’ businesses has been impacted. On a regular basis, SC will provide a short summary of challenges encountered (e.g. beneficiary did not collect a voucher) or issues raised through the monitoring system (e.g. harassment on site) that can be factored into future planning, to mitigate such identified issues, during subsequent rounds of voucher distribution.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

SCI is committed to being accountable to communities by sharing appropriate and timely information and supporting them to influence processes and decisions that affect them by enabling them to provide feedback and share complaints about our work. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be in place and disseminated at the start of the project, including SCI’s toll-free hotline (1800-724-726), complaint boxes and community sessions. SCI pays special attention to ensure that beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any concerns and make use of their preferred communication channel. Therefore, the sensitizations sessions will be utilized to raise the awareness about the rights and responsibilities of the beneficiaries, as well as about the feedback and complaint mechanisms (FCM) and how we identify and respond to feedback. SCI will also train stakeholders and teams in SCI Child Safeguarding, Gender and financial policies with a focus on preventing fraud and corruption. A specific session on PSEA, with a focus on identification and reporting, will be delivered to partners and providers, ensuring these incidents are also reported through the cluster system. SCI PSEA mechanism is integrated into our broader Feedback Complaint Mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

SCI’s risk analysis ensures that all the activity risks will be identified failure access cash assistance or restrictions to reaching distribution areas due to security risks. The relevant mitigation measures will be put in place to enable SCI to follow a conflict-sensitive approach that ensure the timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will ensure strong community engagement to ensure we do no harm; that activities are culturally sensitive and that both men and women are equally engaged in project activities. Community consultations through CPCs will include discussions on targeting criteria to ensure a transparent selection process, which has been one of the key feedback received by affected populations. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated through field visits, cluster consultations and local stakeholder discussions. SC will select multiple merchants to ensure competition, do no harm to the local market, ensure better access to supplies for beneficiaries, and maximize the positive impact of vouchers on the market SCI will maintain its neutrality and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SCI will continuously monitor context and risks and adapt activities and their schedules to ensure access and reduce risks. It will analyze the potential interaction between the planned activity, the changing context, and potential implications for the relations between stakeholders and communities. SCI`s Social Tensions Tracking and Mapping system (STTM) will locate geographic areas with communities vulnerable to social tensions. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The selection criteria will ensure that all beneficiaries have equal access to the proposed assistance based on a clear targeting criteria. SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services for them are limited. Gender sensitivity will be integrated into project activities and will considered as a minimum standard to address the unique needs of vulnerable groups, specifically women and persons with disability and to ensure that both men and women are actively participating in the project and sharing feedback. SC will ensure gender mainstreaming in the assessments and interventions and in the structure of its teams. The selection criteria will focus on female-headed households (at least 25% of the target). It will also prioritize the needs of pregnant/lactating women, children with malnutrition, children with disability and women-headed households. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI’s policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities in communities to assist in their access to food security services. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to services.
