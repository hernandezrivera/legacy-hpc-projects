Project Summary

The project aims at addressing of food insecurity and building resilience of the communities in Maroodi jeex pastoral districts.
The project targets an estimated 6500 households, particularly women, children the elderly and the poorest of the poor families, living in Salahlay , Farawrine , and Baligubadle districts in Somaluland . Assistance will help affected households to meet their immediate and long term food and cash needs, as well as provide appropriate capacity building to the target communities.

Needs

The impact of recurrent drought-related crises in Hawd ecological zones of Maroodijeex region in Somaliland is rapidly escalating, with more and more people being affected each time a drought occurs. Drought is becoming more frequent, allowing less time for recovery in between droughts, and increasing the vulnerability of local populations. The current ability of pastoralists to respond to drought is limited not only due to the increasing frequency of drought, but also due to increasing population, a dwindling resource base, conflict, changes in access to land and water, as well as the impact of other shocks such as flooding and disease The recurrent crisis is not solely due to the poor or failed rains over the years. Rather it is the result of the gradual erosion of community resilience and traditional coping strategies by successive shocks and limited development investments by the Government of Somaliland. The 2017/18 drought affected 150,000 people in Somaliland, an estimated 50-60% of livestock (Shoats, Camels and Cattle) died. Many households and communities had only started to recover when 2017/2018 post-election brought of high inflation, high food and fuel prices and SAGER related flooding.According to the 2017-2018 short rain assessment, most parts of Salahley, Baligubadle, Farweyne and Balimataan were classified in the stressed phase due to the positive impacts of the above average short rains and on-going interventions though some areas were still in the crisis phase due to Recurrent drought and poor food access.At present there are acute shortages of food, water and pasture for grazing, GAM rates in some districts are high, above the 15% emergency threshold and the deterioration in the body condition of livestock has reduced production and increased mortality rates due to diseases and starvation and led to decline in their market value. The high food prices have affected the capacity of the very poor to buy food and to access basic services.

Activities

1- sensitization and 21 Project Community committee establishments. 2- Build the capacity of 21 community committee on conflict resolution and natural resource management, and relevant skills. 3-Distribution of drought resistance Seeds & Fodder for 600 Households.4- 900 farming households supported with hand tools for farming5- Establishment of 3 greenhouses in the three target districts.6- Provision of tractor hours for 900HH agro pastoralist affected by climate change resulted droughts 7- Environmental protection, soil and water conservation on cash for work, ( building 19200LM, soil bands)Cash for work: Cash for work will be used to construct or rehabilitate community infrastructure, and help to ensure that targeted households have access to the cash needed to purchase food and other basic items and services. Activities undertaken using cash for work include building soil bands in agricultural land and grazing land to reduce water run-off and gully formation. 8- District veterinary office (DVOs) trained and facilitated to conduct periodic disease surveillance and plan for appropriate response measures in areas with incidences.9- Food distribution for 300 vulnerable households for three months.10- Training of local Government staff, Community Animal Health Workers, Leaders and pastoralists: with community managed district risk reduction (CMDRR) 11- 150 women groups trained on alternative livelihood options, and income generation such as poultry farming and beekeeping, skins and hides etc, to diversify household source of income12- Poultry distribution for 150 women groups. Distribution of 900 chicken to 150 women led households to enhance their livelihoods and children nutrition

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will work in close collaboration with the line ministries with special reference to the Ministry of Agriculture, Ministry of Livestock, and Ministry of Social Service. These ministries will provide services such registration of groups and capacity building on better farming methods, specific business technical and management skills. Their inputs will mainly be on technical backstopping and linkages to front-line extension workers; this will provide benchmark for sustainability. The project will also provide capacity building to the target communities to ensure or build community resilience and bring long-time or durable solution to recurrent droughts and climate change impacts.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The proposed activities will directly benefit 6500 households in three target districts of Maroodi-jeh region, special emphasis will be placed on project activities benefiting most vulnerable households in the community. More detailed beneficiary numbers by gender, district and sectors will be identified during the first month of the project after communities have been selected. The beneficiaries will be identified through a participatory planning meeting with community development committees in the project areas. A selection criteria will be established by BVO through consultation of individuals who hold key responsibilities in the villages, local provincial administration, Other considerations will include vulnerability, skills and potentiality to initiate and maintain business, trust and honesty, gender and age, ethnic reflection, owning farm in the district, vulnerability of the household, type of intervention the person is engaged or capable of doing and acceptability to sign contractual loan agreement (interest free) among others. The beneficiaries will be verified and validated in a public meeting. The direct beneficiaries will be individuals households affected by several years of cyclic droughts, internal conflicts and insecurity and consequently suffer from chronic livelihood vulnerability. The project will target those vulnerable families who have no other options to start or expand their business activities. BVO will have an open dialogue and consultation with them and together will agree on participatory tools for project monitoring and procedures of loan repayment. They will decide on the timing and duration of the training events so as not to inconvenience clients businesses.The resident population of the target districts will indirectly benefit from improved food security support services. It is hoped that the interventions will reach wider populations than those directly benefiting from the programme.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Prior to implementation, BVO will facilitate the community to come up with their own action plan and prioritize the projects activities by establishing project community committee. The community being the major stake holder will be given the chance to prioritize its needs. Other stake holders and partners will also be contacted to share their experiences and avoid duplication. The beneficiaries will be selected through a well-structured, mutually agreed, and inclusive and gender sensitive criteria. They will then be sensitized and made aware of the project importance, involvement and ownership for sustainability. The beneficiaries will include classification of the vulnerable groups including women, men and youth, age will also be respected and whole project approach will be gender sensitive approach where no one is left behind because of clan or gender.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

North Somalia, (known as somaliland) is stable for last two decades, especially the project area has no history of armed conflicts, so that there is no risk of armed conflict expected during the implementation of the project and if there is any BVO will mitigate with the collaboration of local authorities, project community committees and community elders who are part and parcel of project implementation and planning.
