Project Summary

The purpose of the proposed interventions is aimed to enhance the national capacity to respond to the continuing health needs for the affected displaced people, returnees, and host communities in north east Syria, challenged with protection concerns. VIYAN will provide an integrated package of services spanning public primary health sector including strengthening mental health and psychosocial support component. The supporting modality for the public PHCs will cover infrastructural rehabilitation, facilities operationalization, comprehensive and standardized PHC services provision both diagnostic and treatment procedures, strengthening the inter-sectorial and external referral system as part of strengthen the national capacity by incentiving drivers, maintenance for the vehicles as well as covering the operational cost for 24/7 work to the designated hospitals (Raqqa national hospital and Sur hospital) with case-by-case follow up by VIYAN medical staff, promoting the knowledge of the targeted populations by conducting continuous awareness activities in areas of need, carrying out a capacity building component throughout the project lifetime covering topics such as (PM+, PFA, EWARS, first aid, IPC practices, SRH and medical case management) for the national healthcare workers. Services provided under the proposed interventions will address specific needs of women, men, girls, and boys, including older persons and persons with disabilities. All phases of the intervention will rely on close coordination and consultation with affected populations, as well as key stakeholders, including national and sub-national clusters, Directorate of Health, and relevant line ministries. The target beneficiaries will mainly be IDPs out-of-camps, returnees, and host populations in areas of high severity of needs that continue to experience inadequate public basic services, overwhelming existing resources and are yet to experience relatively stable security as well as areas out-of-coverage and hard-to-reach areas.

3 - Needs

The fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. With more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths, COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systems due to low levels of COVID-19 vaccination, lack of adherence to preventive measures, and emerging variants which, together, strain attempts to stabilize and restart services affected by the pandemic, including gaps in routine childhood immunization services. It is critical to ensure investments in health service delivery are not lost: shortages in resources risk contraction of health access at time when the COVID-19 pandemic is on-going, other communicable diseases are on the rise, treatment access barriers persist, household level vulnerability is increasing, and worries about insufficient income to pay for urgent health needs rank 3rd among household concerns after sufficient income for food and electricity access. Essential inclusive health services including trauma, referral, and physical rehabilitation must be maintained at every level of the health system and ensured to avoid negative outcomes in population mortality and morbidity.

4 - Implementation of activities

A1: Provision of quality primary healthcare including NCD care, MHPSS, trauma care and referrals in DEZ and Raqqa, through supporting four public PHCCs (Al-Akeirshi & Al-Shaheeda Media in Raqqa – Al-Hssein & Al-Jazrat in DEZ). Assistance will be through the supply of essential medicines, equipment, medical supplies, incentives to PHC staff, PPEs, minor rehabilitation works, purchasing of essential furniture and ensuring a stable power and water supply as well as supporting the referral system. VIYAN will ensure that PHCCs are staffed (12 Doctors, 10 M-Nurses, 10 F-Nurses, 6 APHs, 3 PHs, 6 lab technicians, 8 Psychologists, 16 CHWs, 4 Data Entries, 8 Service Staff, 8 Guards, 6 Ambulance drivers) A2: Contribute to the improvement of health services through capacity building and supportive supervision of health personnel and CHWs, in which they will be trained on mhGAP, PM+, PFA, COVID19 case management and IPC. The proposed activities will be delivered through well-established coordination and reporting mechanisms including regular attendance of NES HWG meetings, MHPSS sub-working group & health authorities’ meetings, reporting on 4Ws, Activity Info and EWARN. The project will be monitored and evaluated by MEAL manager Erbil and MEAL officer NES who will ensure data collection forms are designed and completed using quantitative & qualitative tools. A3: Rehabilitation of the targeted PHCCs with the aim to restore the national minimum building requirements.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISKS: 1. Duplication or mis coordination in activities 2. Limited availability of medicines, delayed deliveries at boarder points 3. Fraud and aid deviation 4. Accountability to affected populations 5. Deterioration of security conditions in the areas where the project operates. ASSUMPTIONS: 1. Coordination network among partners is good 2. Local market is stable 3. Antifraud policy in place and being implemented 4. Local community members are well involved in the project design and implementation 5. Security conditions remain stable and predicated. MITIGATION: 1. improve networking with NES HWG and LHA to avoid duplications 2. conduct medical quality assessment both in KRI and NES 3. Actively participate in the UN & Health Cluster coordination meetings and strengthening policies on antifraud and misappropriation of humanitarian aid usage as well as staff are sanitized and signed code of conduct 4. conduct key informant interviews with IDP, host community populations, project staff and LHA officials about the project (all gender & age) in addition to conduct patient satisfaction surveys in the target locations, VIYAN will hire local staff providers for service delivery, which allows for a better understanding of the local operational context 5. Arrange provision of larger quantities of medical supply in to overcome protracted interruption of services as well as constantly monitor the security situation through our security focal point sources.
