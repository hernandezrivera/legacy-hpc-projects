Project Summary

SARD is committed to assist affected people most in need in northwest Syria, despite unpredictability and the risks of working in areas with security threats. The response plan with a budget of 4,100,000 USD will address the needs of more than 60,000 people over the course of 12 months, providing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance.
The action contributes to improve shelters and provide life-sustaining assistance with Non-Food Items (NFI) for selected most vulnerable persons including IDPs who have been recently displaced or displaced multiple times.
Implemented with a protection lens, and addressing gender and disabled-specific needs, the shelter response is aimed at providing housing options in emergency and life-sustaining situations. The activities include: emergency shelter in camp settings, temporary shelter solutions in open fields, life-saving shelter upgrade, and life-sustaining house rehabilitation through a self-help approach. The modality of intervention will be adapted to the urgency, situation, needs, and the HLP situation of the beneficiaries. SARD is committed to promoting social cohesion including shelter programming based on a community approach. The shelter activities are detailed as follows:
- Upgrade of IDP sites/camps including infrastructure improvements, tent insulation, and tent replacement to cope with flood season. Upgrade of IDP sites/camps will also include the shift to dignified shelters where SARD will replace the tents with better shelter solutions which will provide safe, secure, and longer-term accommodation to the beneficiaries. SARD’s team in coordination with CCCM, shelter clusters, and local authorities proceed with the assessment and the selection of the most vulnerable IDP sites/camps camp.
- Emergency shelter option with distribution of kit or quick-fixing by SARD’s team
- Collective shelter upgrade and unfinished building repair by contractors under SARD’s technical supervision
- House rehabilitation through provision of cash grants under SARD’s supervision
The shelter response proposed in this action is integrated with WaSH and basic shelter-related NFI support adapted to the needs of the assessed, eligible families. The modality of NFI interventions will range from in-kind to cash/voucher according to the market accessibility and availability, family capacity, and needs. Door-to-door distribution is the preferred option to ensure specific needs and security are addressed to girls, women, the elderly, and people with special needs.

4 - Needs

SARD’s needs assessment conducted in November and December 2020 showed that:- 45% of interviewed households live in private houses and almost 1 in 4 (24%) in open fields. However, preliminary findings show variations based on households’ displacement status with the majority of host households (87%) living in private houses compared to only 34% of displaced households. In addition, 31% of IDPs live in open fields and 12% in informal camps.- Host households tend to live with one other household (a total of 2 households living together), while IDPs tend to live with two other households (a total of 3 households living together) and with up to 80 households in open field settlements.- 52% of interviewed households reside in partially damaged houses, 25% in lightly damaged buildings and 21% in completely destroyed houses.- Cash remains the most preferred aid modality for shelter support, as reported by 54% of households, in particular for IDPs. SARD conducted an assessment in April 2021 where 32 IDP sites/camps were assessed (7,872 HH - 43,490 individuals) across Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The Data collected in close coordination with the Camps’ Management Committees showed that:-12% of the households are headed by females-87% of the camps were flooded during the last flooding incidents between December 2020 and January 2021-84% of the camps had no infrastructure for rainwater drainage-78% of the camps had no sewage system and relied only on septic tanks-75% of the tents were not insulated (no slab under the tent)-63% of the tents (shelters) were damaged

5 - Implementation of activities

To address the shelter needs as described above, shelter assistance will be provided ranging from emergency interventions with shelter kits and core NFIs to house rehabilitation, shelter upgrade, and community projects. SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have access to life-saving assistance through emergency shelter kits and quick-fixing of collective shelters/unfinished buildings. The action provides also life-sustaining shelter support to most vulnerable families including house rehabilitation through cash grants (self-help modality). In addition to this, SARD will be upgrading IDP sites/camps on both the site infrastructure level and the shelter unit level. Activities will take place as follows: assessment of the security, locations, tenders, and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response (cash, voucher, or in-kind). Identification and targeting of beneficiaries, registration, preparation of BoQs, distributions/implementation, monitoring, and post- monitoring, and close coordination with relevant clusters, including sharing the 4Ws matrix and identifying gaps. The selection will be based on eligibility criteria and scoring mechanism, taking into consideration HLP documentation. Bill of Quantities will be prepared for shelter upgrades in close consultation with the beneficiaries. For self-help modality (cash grants), MoUs will be signed with selected families and followed by cash grant dispersion. Following the action, a review and satisfaction survey are conducted by the M&E team to verify implementation and measure the impact of the action. Implemented with a protection lens, the shelter response is implemented in line with SARD PSEA policy.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

SARD mainstreams protection principles throughout its projects, ensuring that assistance is provided safely in secured locations, responding to people's needs, and reinforcing their dignity. Protection is mainstreamed throughout the response by promoting meaningful access, safety, and dignity with "do no harm" as a key principle. SARD will refer any protection issues and vulnerable cases to other protection actors through its referral system. SARD utilizes protection mainstreaming indicators in all project aspects, aiming at measuring the impact of the protection mainstreaming throughout the entire programming/SARD follows the below principles:1. Prioritisation of safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program team constantly monitors the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. All SARD teams follow Covid-19 prevention measures which are streamlined throughout programming and include: wearing masks and gloves and maintaining social distancing, as well as ensure awareness sessions around Covid-19.2. Accountability: SARD has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities. Currently, due to Covid-19 prevention measures, beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can register any feedback/complaints via SARD’s WhatsApp number.3. Participation and Empowerment: Beneficiaries and other key stakeholders are actively involved in project design through regular needs assessments and mobilization sessions. Their contribution and voice has weight at every step of the program.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

I. Safety and Security Risks The volatile and often unpredictable nature of the Syrian conflict continues to impose significant risk on humanitarian staff and beneficiaries' safety. Risks could include but not limited to a sudden escalation in hostilities, airstrikes, armed clashes, and kidnapping. The likelihood of increased conflict hostilities is considered moderate in northern Aleppo compared to opposition-controlled areas of Idleb and western Aleppo. The impact of this risk is assessed as high in both areas. SARD has developed and adopted tools to ensure a sufficient level of standard of care to safeguard employees and beneficiaries from a reasonably foreseeable risk of harm is maintained.II. Operations and Program-related Risks The high-risk environment of northwest Syria continues to impose significant risk on the continuation of humanitarian activities. Risks to humanitarian programs implementation in the context of northwest Syria include but not limited to partial or complete closure of border crossings with Turkey; disruption of the supply chain; fluctuating exchange rate; and community acceptance. The likelihood and the impact of operations and program-related risks in areas of northern Aleppo, Idleb, and western Aleppo assessed as high. SARD has developed and adopted tools to ensure its readiness to cope and adjust with sudden unexpected risk as they arise. III. Financial and Reputation Risks The absence of a fully functioning banking system in northwest Syria is a key challenge is how to facilitate fund to northwest Syria with the at most due diligence approach. SARD has developed and adopted tools to minimize the risk and ensure the incorporation of due diligence in its financial transactions.
