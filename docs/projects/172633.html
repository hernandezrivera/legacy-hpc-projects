Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at strengthening the protection environment of conflict and displacement-affected Libyans and non Libyans in Tripolitania area.
Proposed services include protection monitoring, aiming at providing timely protection information and evidence to on-going and future interventions, critical information sharing and awareness raising, individual protection assistance (IPA), group and individual PSS, and case management services. These will target internally displaced persons and returnees, as well as migrants, asylum seekers and refugees in need of humanitarian assistance through a rights-based and community-based approach.
A combination of modalities, including cash and in-kind assistance, individual and collective services, direct and indirect delivery methods will be used to provide tailored and needs-based protection services to the groups of concern.

1 - Needs

Through its current Protection programming, DRC has been able to assess several trends of needs, and gaps in protection services. Highlighted needs are evidence-based and reflected in Libya Response HNO, the Protection Sector HRP and strategy. Cross-cutting needs:- Gaps in meeting basic needs, resulting in resorting to harmful coping mechanisms. Gaps in meeting basic needs are largely exacerbated by COVID-19 with mobility restrictions and reduced overall income opportunities. - Critical gaps in self-efficacy due to limited employment opportunities, protracted displacement, combined with reliance on humanitarian aid and lack of awareness on governmental/humanitarian registration and services availability.- Conflict-affected and displaced Libyans remain highly exposed to risks of HLP rights violations, including evictions for IDPs, secondary occupation, lack of compensation processes, damage and destruction of houses and properties for returnees. - Migrants and refugees similarly face obstacles in ability to secure adequate shelter forced into temporary/sub-standards solutions by living with community-members. Women are particularly in need for shelter solutions. - Additionally, migrant and refugee women are more exposed to risks of sexual violence. This highlights the needs for strong advocacy on migrants and refugees’ shelter needs in urban areas, key prevention and awareness messaging, and quick, tailored, emergency protection response to prevent or mitigate protection incidents. Libyans:- Conflict-affected Libyans, regardless of displacement status, have an estimated overall wellbeing estimated to be weak. Yet mental health and psychosocial wellbeing are not at the forefront of Libyans’ priorities, nor are these widely acceptable topics of discussion within families. Psychosocial support and awareness on psychosocial wellbeing to counter stigma are highly needed. This includes in family settings, where often women and girls are isolated and at risk of forced/early marriage and domestic violence.- Safety and security: many Libyan IDPs feel unsafe to return to their area of origin or their houses have been damaged too severely to inhabit. Additionally, presence of ERWs and UXOs remain scattered across Libya, highlighting the need for timely awareness and response. Non-Libyans:- Migrants and refugees remain at high risk of exposure to abuse and exploitation due to the lack of overall and legal protection. Lack of understanding of cultural practices, as well as legal procedures result in additional fees for services being requested and at times confiscation of civil documentation. - Lack of durable solutions: the misunderstanding of voluntary return and resettlement procedures and criteria results in willingly remaining without key civil documents such as birth certificates and passports due to the misconception that possession of these will result in a negative decision on UNHCR/IOM’s part to accept the individual’s application.- Non-Libyans, especially of East and West African descent are more likely to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination. Where there are no specific trends and interactions are based on individual experiences and interactions, especially in the context of COVID19 reports of racial slurs, denial to services, and - Arbitrary arrest (including at sea) and detention: Libya remains a port of departure for migrants and refugees in attempts to cross the Mediterranean to find protection and/or better opportunities in Europe, resorting to smuggling and human trafficking networks. This is exposing migrants and refugees to important human rights violations, violence, deprivation, and coercion. - Relatedly, overall safety and security of migrants and refugees is noted as a continuous concern. Robbery and theft are often reported, and migrant and refugee women and girls are particularly at risk of sexual violence.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.2 - Increased tensions and conflicts in the community due to perceived or actual unequal distribution of assistance: this risk is identified particularly high for migrants and refugees living in areas also hosting Libyan communities, and for IDPs and returnees with regards to host communities. Prevention and mitigation: DRC will strengthen its outreach activities and information sharing with all groups in each area of intervention. This includes information on assistance selection criteria, visibility on DRC-CFM (helpline), and DRC protection services. All DRC activities will be delivered through a community-based approach which will be supported by a network of Community Protection Volunteers. CPVs will be members of the supported communities, strengthening empowerment and participation throughout the project, as well as early warning (report of tension) and awareness raising and mediation. 4.13 - Exposure of women and girls to violence (incl. verbal and psychological): this risk was identified to be particularly high for IDP, returnee, migrant, and refugee women and girls. According to DRC protection monitoring findings, in targeted locations women and girls are particularly exposed to discrimination, violence, coercion and rights violations due to hostile and unsafe living environment and as a result of exposure to conflict and displacement. They also face increased barriers to identify and secure a source of income socio-cultural norms and practices. Prevention and mitigation: DRC works to mitigate this risk through inclusive programming, identification of specific needs (based on age and gender), tailored referrals, individually tailored protection assistance; and key outreach messaging. 4.15 - Barriers for beneficiaries to report sexual exploitation and/or abuse in a safe and confidential manner. This risk was identified to apply to all groups, for different reasons. Drivers include the important social and physical barriers in accessing existing reporting mechanisms, such as the general lack of awareness and confidence in agencies' existing SEA reporting mechanisms from targeted populations, language barriers, lack of access to phones, social stigma, lack of knowledge on rights and on how to claim them, etc. To contribute mitigating this risk, DRC will work to ensure specific messaging, the dissemination of all relevant information (including contacts and ways of reporting) in accessible communication formats, the overall meaningful access to reporting mechanism, including the DRC-CFM and WFP-lead CFM, as well as all other initiatives at local level. 4.16 - Forced eviction of persons targeted by the project. This risk has been identified as particularly high for IDPs, but may also apply to returnees, migrants, and refugees. Given the type of accommodation utilized by some groups (mainly IDPs) and the evolution of the context and the displacement of the front line outside Tripoli in June 2020, mass evictions are anticipated to rise in number in 2021 as unfinished-building, informal sites land-owners, and the state reclaims occupied properties. Prevention and mitigation of evictions require a multi-sector and multi-agency response and coordination. To contribute to this effort, DRC engages at different levels with other agencies, the protection and the shelter clusters, and OCHA to facilitate early warning, rapid needs assessments, and provides protection information to form evidence and support high and local levels advocacy efforts on eviction prevention and response.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

DRC is currently in discussion with WFP on signing the CFM agreement, to formalize the established referral pathway between the CFM and DRC teams. In parallel, DRC has put in place a strong internal complaint and feedback mechanism accessible in all areas of intervention, allowing for the participation of the targeted group in the services and assistance delivery throughout the project. The DRC CFM is managed by the MEAL department and includes a helpline of which the number is systematically communicated to targeted populations. DRC registers and addresses all requests formulated, and regularly reviews and highlight learnings from received feedback throughout the project cycle. Learnings inform current and future programming and allow for increased engagement with the supported groups. In addition, for this particular project, and specific "MEAL plan" is developed, to allow the increased participation of beneficiaries and other groups into the design, implementation, and review of proposed protection services. These include regular focus group discussions, various key informants interviews pre and during the project, as well as satisfaction surveys, post distribution monitoring, and project evaluation activities after service provision. DRC has also established a strong code of conduct feedback mechanism, mainstreamed and accessible through the MEAL-lead CFM for populations of concern.
