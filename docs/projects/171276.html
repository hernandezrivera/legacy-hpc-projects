Project Summary

According to the HNO for 2021, GBV continues to be an issue of great concern in Federal Members States (Galmudug, South West State, Jubaland, HirShabelle) Puntland and Somaliland worsened by multiple displacements and forced evictions due to flooding and armed conflicts. Women and adolescent girls from 18 – 50 years old (including those living with disabilities) are subjected to Intimate Partner Violence, rape, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment and abuse in Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle, Gedo, Snaag, Awdal, Baidoa, Bay, Mudug and, Banadir. A CCCM assessment in Dinsor in Bay region and a joint Humanitarian Gap Analysis (July 2020) in Middle Shabelle region (Balcad and Jowhar) in IDP camps found that distant water points, markets, health facilities/schools, poor lightning, lack of doors on toilets and lack of disaggregation of sanitary facilities are some of major factors that increase GBV exposure. Also, a July 2020 “GBV/FGM COVID19 assessment indicate that 38 percent respondents affirm increase in GBV incidents; while 35 percent inform that GBV incidents are the same. The number of GBV survivors call to GBV hotlines increased by 283% in FMS and 767%in Somaliland. Similarly, GBVIMS 2020 data show increase in intimate partner violence (55%); Physical Assault and rape at 61% and 12% respectively in contract with Physical Assault and rape at 56% and 14% of 2019. Also, draft January – August, 2020 MARA report increasing incidence of GBV among adolescent girls. Out of a total of 189 GBV incidents involving 214 survivors; 196 were girls with 16 women and 2boys). Low reporting of GBV cases persist due to stigmatization, victim blaming, shame and interference by clan and religious leaders, slow access to justice and weak or lack of legal framework for the protection of women and girls from GBV.
A Safety Audit assessment conducted by IMC in April 2020 in Baidao, Jowhar and Mogadishu indicated Gender Based Violence remains a daily occurrence with poor access to gender segregated and lockable latrine and bath facilities. IDPs settlements of Bay, Middle Shabelle, Banadir and Mudug, face some of the most serious gender-related problems in the whole country. The particularly low awareness of the extremely high rates of violence, low reporting rates and effective limited of treatment facilities as well as insufficient infrastructure, poor WASH facilities, limited access to markets, water points, and health facilities often equate to inadequate protection capacities.
Through this project, IMC aims to support vulnerable populations in drought-affected areas in Banadir (Mogadishu), Bay (Baidao), Middle Shabelle (Jowhar)Mudug regions (Galkayo), who are exposed to Gender Based Violence (GBV). This includes child survivors of violence. The project tackles both GBV prevention and response through a combination of activities which rely on IMC’s combined experience in the sector together with long-term presence in the geographical areas of intervention.
IMC will prioritize the right of persons with disabilities to adore access, an equal basis. We will enhance the physical environment, transportation, information, communications, and quality services will benefit the persons with disability. Persons with a disability will attain meaningfully participate in all our services and information. Their rights will promote, and IMC will address disability-related concerns in compliance with the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.

Mitigation Measures

IMC will safeguard there is passable integration and connections with Health/WASH/Nutrition services in the targeted areas to minimize distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services. • Marginalized minorities, single/child headed households and Women/children/PLWs with disabilities will be consulted on the Protection risks they face and activities that would make their communities safer for them. During distribution of dignity kits, beneficiaries with disabilities & other special needs, will be identified and given priority. • Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from marginalized groups know how to access timely, confidential Protection response services. Messages will be disseminated through household visits, community gatherings and videos at heath facilities, in consideration of literacy, disability and mobility levels. Protection teams will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, socio-demographic groups, disability). IMC will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Protection specific awareness messaging will involve both girls, boys, women and men and will be timed to fit around women and men’s other work schedules. IMC will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying Protection risk mitigation solutions and the decision-making processes so as to reduce GBV stigma, promote survivor centered care and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. 3b. All IMC Protection staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. • All IMC Protection staff in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to avoid and report Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. • IMC will provide solar powered lamps as part of dignity kit distribution to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls in high risk areas. • Working in coordination with WASH and Nutrition sectors, IMC will put in place measures to prevent physical/sexual violence arising from distributions and poor WASH infrastructure in camp settings. • Through establishment of community risk mitigation mechanisms, IMC will advocate for community watch and buddy systems particularly for activities that have heightened GBV risk such as fetching firewood or water. • Protection staff will be trained on provision of safe, survivor centered case management, including provision of PSS and safe data storage. • In areas where IMC has Women and Girls safe Spaces, teams will refer beneficiaries to the WGSS group PSS and basic life skills training. The location and the timing of WGSS activities will endeavor to achieve safe proximity to the beneficiaries a well as keeping to hours that allow women and girls to return to their homes before nightfall. • All IMC health centers will have confidential and private spaces, with at least one trained female health worker on duty at all times to ensure 24/7 access to clinical management of rape and GBV services. • IMC will roll out a safe-word based referral protocol at all implementation sites to ensure direct referral of survivors from Health to the GBV teams, without the Survivor needing to repeat her/his story potentially exposing her/him to further traumatic maintains an emergency GBV response number that allows confidential reporting of GBV cases and ambulance callout when necessary. • Through a Somalia wide complaint and feedback line, IMC teams will receive and make timely corrective responses.
