Project Summary

The crisis affected the health situation as many health facilities are damaged or partially damaged and the functional ones are many times hard to reach. Women and girls are particularly vulnerable and exposed to GBV from a multiplicity of ways ranging from “…direct exposure to hostilities, displacement, conditions in sites/collective shelters, protracted displacement and returns to destroyed and impoverished areas.”3 Besides, many areas including Rural Aleppo and Rural Damascus are unserved raising the probability of waterborne diseases and infections. Accordingly, the project will establish 2 Primary Health Center, 1 in Rural Aleppo, 1 in Rural Damascus that will provide PHC services and reproductive health in addition to MHPSS individual sessions as well as the project will support two dialysis clinics one in Damascus Dwelaa and the other in Rural Damascus East Ghouta.
Each Primary Health Center clinic will contain 5 doctors, 1 MHPSS specialist, and 5 nurses. The health staff will receive health training on health-related topics. In the implementation phase, it will be a high level in coordination between the clinics in each center (the reproductive health and the consultation one) for example if a pregnant woman who is being treated in reproductive health clinic suffer from another disease it will be referred directly to the second clinic. Regarding mental health, there will be PSS specialists in each center. EPDC will ensure the proper application of CRM mechanism as it will be informed with the targeted community along with hotline, WhatsApp number, Facebook page, complaints box and
Primary Health Centre will be delivered the medical supported to 800 (7200/year) patients per month and 200 patients will be benefited from medication and 60 from medical tests according to their need. and 50 patients will be benefited from surgeries fees including reproductive health. All medical activities will be conducted according to WHO guidelines and standard operation process. Meanwhile 3,000 people will be targeted with health awareness campaign on healthy living- IEC materials printed and hygiene kits distributed during campaigns. Finally, 600 women will be provided with health devices and health awareness sessions on non-communicable diseases. Sessions will cover the following topics: permanent illness as diabetes, gout, osteoporosis, sexually transmitted diseases, pre-marital tests, renal insufficiency, urinary infections, reproductive health, breast cancer, early detection, self-examination, and nutrition.
The dialysis centers will continue to provide lifesaving dialysis services to 44 patients, 27 in Dwelaa and 17 in East Ghouta.
In Dwelaa the center will run 3 dialysis machines providing 9 sessions a day for 26 days in a month.
In East Ghouta the center will run 2 dialysis machines providing 9 sessions a day for 26 days in a month. The machines in both centers will be serviced/ maintained as needed to ensure safety, quality and durability.

4 - Needs

Due to the inflation more than 90% of Syrian population live below poverty. Prices of goods increased 236% (HNO 2021) and the people are prioritizing securing food for their children to get necessary health assistance or cleaning items to protect against C19. The number of the centers which provide with the service of purifying the blood for kidney failure patients are very few, and limited, besides that, there are lots of centres which are damaged, in additions to, a large number of public hospitals which used to provide with this service, but they were closed because of the crisis. Each Dialysis session conducted at a private centre costs about 70,000 SYP, which considered expensive to limited wages in Syria. Considering the importance of the dialysis sessions to renal disorder patients in conflict affected areas where access to public hospitals means waiting for months on the waiting lists to get this lifesaving service, and that the number of patients is big needing service in Erbin and Dwelaa, EPDC needs support in securing the needed medicines and running cost especially that EPDC has the equipment and staff to conduct the hemo sessions. Arran is populated by 14000 people and the PHC there was damaged by the crisis and went unfunctional and the nearest medical point is 25 km. The PHC needs rehabilitation and proper equipment to respond to the needs of the area especially with the current shortage of fuel and spread of C19.Maaraba was heavily affected by the crisis and hosted about 150K people from different governorates. There is no hospital in the area and only one health dispensary which can receive only 50 people. The area needs a PHC to respond to the health needs giving the large number of populations.

All EPDC staff and beneficiaries use adequate PPE, practice social distancing and project staff will be trained on COVID-19 preventive measures.- Maintain strict controls and application of Anti-Fraud policy and procurement and finance procedures.- Purchase available brands in local market.
