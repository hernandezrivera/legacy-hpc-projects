Project Summary

While every child has a right to education, access to safe schools and provision of quality education in government schools have been a standing challenge to most South Sudanese children. This has been influenced by different factors including cost of school fees and registration as well as education supplies, access to safe water and latrines with changing rooms has affected girls’ attendance in schools’ low incentive for teachers and limited number of in-service trainings for teachers largely impact the access and provision of quality education. According to the HNO 2022 an estimated 3.6 million people are in need of education services in South Sudan.
The proposed activities aim to ensure girls and boys can be enrolled to school and access safe and quality education while responding to strategic goal 2 and 3 and Education cluster goal 1,2 and 3 in Aweil East and Aweil South of the Northern Bahr el Ghazal as highlighted as second priority by education cluster. The project will reach a total of 5600 (3055G, 1874B, 312F and 359M). The project will ensure all groups are reached including girls, boys, men, and women as well as children with special needs.
To improve access and inclusion students will be provided with scholastic materials and recreational materials which will support retention of adolescent and pedagogical and protection trainings will be provided to teachers to improve their teaching practices and creating safe spaced for the students. Improvement of WASH practices in school through latrines construction with safe water points and provision of hygiene material which not only will respond to hygiene practices in schools but also the prevention against COVID-19. The education administrator, school management committee and Parents teachers’ associations will be capacitated in school leadership and management and psychosocial social support as well as coaching technique training to school inspectors which will support in supervision and guidance for both educators and learners. During this HRP period, IRC will continue implementing the 15 months existing education project funded by LEGO Foundation ($1.4M), which is aims to contribute to improving children’s wellbeing and resilience through Learning through Play (LtP) approaches in Aweil East and Aweil South.

1 - Needs

The intervention will be operating in two areas which are classified as severity 5 and second priority of the education cluster in Aweil East and South, responding to the children in need of education service while addressing the key gaps highlighted in the Education Needs Assessment (ENA) 2022. There is an estimate of 2.4 million children out of school, either with poor attendance or dropout completely and among the top reasons highlighted by different stakeholders is inability to pay school fees, including children themselves, of which 33% of boys and 27% of girls assessed responded to not attending schools due to lack of school fees and the indirect cost of supporting attendance in school. The attendance of students in schools is not only influenced by whether school fees are paid but also by whether learners have scholastic materials to support their learning process. According to ENA 2022, only 15% of headteachers reported that all children in the school had both a pen or pencil and their own notebook/exercise book, while just 24% of headteachers reported almost all their teachers had a full set of textbooks for the subjects they teach.The average number of learners per functional latrine in Northern Bahr el Khazal is 120.5:1 and out of the schools which have a functioning latrine/toilet, over half of them do not have latrines which are accessible for children with disabilities (CwDs). Over three quarters of schools (76.9%) reported not having bathrooms or washrooms available for female learners, and, of the secondary schools sampled, the majority (80.8%) reported not having bathrooms or washrooms while access to water is important for menstrual hygiene management, particularly for female learners. Given that only 41% of households in South Sudan have basic drinking water and only 2.8% of households have safe drinking water, providing safe water in schools can help to increase school enrollment. In order for schools and learning centers to provide high-quality education, there needs to be teachers who are well-trained as well as school management committees (SMCs) and educational administrators who can do their jobs well. According to ENA 2022 findings, only 22.4% of the engaged teachers in the assessment have been trained in pedagogical training, 34.2% for child protection in emergencies, and 34.5% in psychosocial support (PSS). The IRC will respond to the needs as suggested in the ENA 2022 report by strengthening protection in schools as well as referral pathways as 40% of 388 headteachers who responded reported there being no functional referral mechanism in their school or community and less than a quarter reported there being a general protection referral mechanism and less than 20% reported there being a functional Gender-Based Violence referral mechanism. The education response will focus on 73 schools, which are currently identified Counties of Aweil East and Aweil South for the IRC led learning through play project named Resilience Expansion and Active Learning (REAL)in crisis project which aims to improve holistic learning outcomes and wellbeing of children affected by COVID-19.

2 - Project activities

IRC in collaboration with the National, State and County MOEs will provide access to quality basic education in safe and protective learning environment for 4,929 (3,055G, 1,874B) children in Northern Bahr el Ghazal . The proposed activities will respond to the education cluster's goals 1, 2 and 3 focusing on increasing equitable access to safe schools, improvement of inclusive educations services and strengthening the education system. The project will construct 4 blocks of latrines in schools for both girls and boys reaching 1260 (720G, 1240 B) children while ensuring every block include a child with disability(CwD) room as well as menstrual hygiene menstruation (MHM) room for girls blocks and Urinal for boys’ blocks. To ensure the constructed latrines are safe to use, the project will construct safe water points and distribute hygiene material which will support cleanness at schools and also support children on the prevention of COVID-19 and other communicable diseases which are associated with WASH practices. Furthermore, to support children attending schools as it has been highlighted in HNO 2022 and ENA 2022 on the need of improving attendance and collection of data in schools children to ensure there not attending schools the project will the attendance of children in schools through the intervention in the REAL project for emphasis of play based interventions in schools which will attract and sat as a motivation for students attendance in schools and reach 2649 adolescent girls and boys in schools. that children are not attending schools due to lack of school fees and scholastic materials, the project will support 2500 students (adolescent girls and boys) as a motivation to keep them in schools. The projdect will further provide scholastic materials to 450 students and 65 teachers to facilitate teaching and learning activities. The IRC will facilitate recreational activities in schools by providing material to 270 students in schools.To strengthen safety and quality education provision the project will provide pedagogical training to 278 teachers. The training will encompass teaching methodologies that will include the IRC Healing Classrooms Programme and Learning Through Play intervention which equips teachers with special techniques that will help them engage distressed children and create a secure and nurturing learning environment, use of play in classes as well as formulation of Teachers Learning Cycles(TLCs) as they will support the professional development practice among the teachers themselves based on the observed gaps as identified by head teachers and school quality assurers(Inspector). Protection training for 278 teachers and learners across all three counties will be conducted to ensure they are capacitated with basic Child Protection aspects including referral pathways to support case reporting for potential support from relevant protection interventions (CP, GBV, Health or Nutrition) hence creating safe environment in schools.To ensure school are well managed and smoothly support teaching and learning processes, the IRC will train 50 School Management Committees (CMCs), administrators, inspectors, and PTA on educational leadership and management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) as well as capacitating 50 inspectors on effective coaching techniques

3 - Project indicators

Success will be measured using the IRC’s IMPACT standards which identifies a set of monitoring actions across the project life cycle. The project will dedicate funds to recruit monitoring and evaluation staff that will be charged with setting up project M&E systems and check points to track project implementation progress and to document learning. The project will be implemented in line with the overall strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Education Cluster strategic objectives to ensure that activities align with the overall prioritized needs of affected populations. The indicators tracked will be in line with the Education Cluster priorities and will be disaggregated by age, sex, nationality, residency, and disability. This will further inform access and utilization of project assistance by vulnerable groups in the community. Data will be collected in line with EMIS requirements and using standardized tools used by the education cluster and reported on a monthly basis. Regular review meetings will be held at the project locations where project staff and partners will review data on all indicators and use the data to make critical decisions about how to best deliver on the project outcomes and make necessary adjustments. Project cycle meetings will be held on a monthly basis to discuss performance across service delivery sites, share experiences and learnings and make high-level evidence-based decisions geared towards strengthening the project delivery mechanisms and success pathways.The project has been designed to support children access safe learning centers while receiving quality education aligning with education cluster priorities. A total of 7 output indicators will be used to support measure the success of the project. Data resulting from the project implementation will be routinely collected, analyzed, and compared to the targets set during the project design. Data will be collected using IRC internal tools which will respond to cluster indicators and support EMIS data as required. The collection of data will be reported in quarterly basis on the progress of its achievement and the data collected will be segregated in number of boys, girls, women, and men including those with disability who will benefit from the project, schools, and counties. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Experience and research has shown that if beneficiaries do not know what has become of their feedback, then they will lose trust in the feedback process. At the IRC, we respond to beneficiaries’ feedback by asking them how we should respond, selecting the appropriate channels, identifying the timing for the response, planning and allocating resources, recording and keeping track of client feedback, and monitoring and reviewing our feedback mechanisms. Complaints and feedback will be analyzed and presented during weekly field site meetings and monthly project cycle meetings where practical actions to address them will be put in place with clear timelines and responsibility allocated. These actions will then be followed up in subsequent meetings to ensure they are implemented and to measure the results. Key actions to adapt project assistance will include, but not limited to, making programmatic changes on assistance delivery, changing policy, provision of goods and services, providing information on available assistance, providing linkages with other projects, providing external referrals and including the needs of clients in project funding proposals.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IRC will collect, analyze and use feedback from beneficiaries regardless of their age, nationality, gender, residency status, education status, disability status and other diversity factors. IRC will use the Washington Group Short Set of Questions to disaggregate feedback and complaints by disability status. The IRC’s feedback channels will disaggregate feedback and complaints by gender, age, nationality, residency status and disability status to enable us to understand the needs and perspectives of different clients, including the most vulnerable members in the communities we serve. We will pay special attention to the most vulnerable and gather their feedback through key informant interviews, focus group discussions and community meetings. The community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements include persons with special needs to ensure their needs are into account.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The IRC safeguarding policy focuses in ensuring both children and adults are protected against any for of harm including exploitation and abuse in all perimeters i.e., Emotional, physical, mental, and verbal while working with IRC direct or IRC associates. Client will be informed on the safeguarding & PSEA information in every interaction with the program team as well as safeguarding champions in every field location topping up all safeguarding messages and reporting channels through IEC materials accessed by all clients and staff accessing IRC premises.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be informed on the scope of the project, timelines, key decision gates and community accountability mechanisms and systems during project opening meetings, project review and coordination meetings, stakeholder and community forums, information boards and using IEC materials. IRC has established community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements who will inform community members on the available client feedback and complaints mechanisms that they can use to provide feedback about project assistance. The community-led feedback management committees will also follow up with IRC on actions taken on the feedback and complaints provided to ensure that the feedback and complaints received from clients are included in adaptive program management. As a client-centered organization, the IRC has a well-structured client responsiveness and accountability system in place where clients’ complaints and feedback are collected, analyzed, and used to inform program decision making. We will use both the proactive and reactive feedback channels to solicit client feedback that will guide project implementation and strategies for improvement. This feedback will be used to inform decision-making and adapt interventions to achieve better outcomes for beneficiaries in line with their priority needs and preferences.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

IRC will continue to strictly adhere to the safeguarding and protection principles throughout the project period across the proposed locations. Safeguarding and PSEA messages will continue being provided to clients in various interactions and coordination forums and ensure the schools targeted are being informed on the reporting mechanism. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Schools will also be informed about the focal point in the IRC to handle any complaint report and feedback. IRC will explain its procedures, decisions from the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries to allow women, girls, men, and boys to hold IRC accountable. To ensure the participation of all beneficiaries throughout the project cycle the program will set clear targets disaggregated by gender, age and vulnerability status including disability so that anyone will not be left out in the program responses.
