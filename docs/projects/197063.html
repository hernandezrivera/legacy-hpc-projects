3 - Needs

Over four million people continue to be in need in NWS. With 2.8 million people still living in displacement, there continues to be a high need for life-saving humanitarian assistance to affected households as well as communities in addressing short- and long-term needs. The economic situation in NWS remains dire, and 90 percent of the population is in poverty. Key drivers of food insecurity in the region include low agricultural output, ongoing hostilities, and disruption to global food markets linked to the war in Ukraine. Over half of IDPs (58%) report borderline or poor food consumption. Moreover, the price of the standard monthly food basket in Syria is currently among the highest ever reported (USD 111.51). Food is consistently ranked among the top three priorities of IDPs and returnees in NWS.

4 - Implementation of activities

• Selection of locations based on needs and gaps in food severity ranking and FSL Cluster’s gaps analysis.• Registration of beneficiaries by IPs.Verification of 20% of beneficiaries by IHH M&E through commissioned TPM. • Initial assessment of FCS and CSI scores. • CfW: Identify activities through community assessments and consultations.• contract food vendor in Syria approved by IHH Program and Compliance. • Distribution of E-vouchers to beneficiaries; beneficiaries procure food items from vendors within the month. • Conduct distribution monitoring by IHH TPM in 100% of distribution sites as well as exit interviews. • Conduct a satisfaction survey (PDM) end of the project to measure the change in FCS and RCS

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Beneficiaries can be exposed to violence, theft, or other kinds of abuse at or after leaving the distribution sites.2. Pressure from the local authorities on who should or should not receive assistance. 3. The selection of beneficiaries can lead to tensions among the communities. 4. Risk of Covid-19 spreading at distribution sites, or of distributions being canceled due to Covid-19. Mitigation measures: 1. Set the point of delivery close to a beneficiary’s home or provide transport to ensure safety. 4. Raise awareness with implementing partners about the importance of principled humanitarian assistance, and reporting any incidents of interference. 5. Explain the selection and vulnerability criteria clearly to the communities via the IHH AAP. 6. Enforce all required hygiene measures including social distancing, sanitation, PPE, spread distributions over a longer period with fewer beneficiaries per day, or distribute door-to-door.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: - Abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries by IP staff during beneficiary selection and distribution processes. - Lack of staff awareness and code of conduct on PSEA. - Lack of availability of any information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries. - Lack of female IP staff in the field. Mitigation/prevention measures: - Code of conduct includes PSEA language. - All IP staff are trained on PSEA.- Internal SEA reporting mechanisms for staff and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization.- PSEA awareness-raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities are conducted and shared during the activities and reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available.- All field activities are supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks.- Beneficiaries regularly receive information regarding the free and unconditional nature of humanitarian aid and know about and have access to complain mechanisms and hotlines.
