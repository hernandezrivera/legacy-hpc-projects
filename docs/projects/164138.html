Project Summary

International Medical Corps have addressed the importance of livelihood and vocational skills needs of internally displaced and host Syrians in alleviating mental stress, GBV and exploitation by offering a variety of training courses of varying lengths. The proposed program aims at improving the livelihoods and resilience of the targeted population beneficiaries (450 Syrian youth and adults) by increasing their skills with the view of improving their livelihood opportunities through vocational training (VT).
The choice of training will be based on each beneficiary background, interests and future aspirations.
Beneficiaries will be identified and selected as follows:
- Internal referrals with other IMC programs (specifically MHPSS, Protection and Health activities)
- External referrals from other organizations such as Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) and other NGOs.
Main criteria for selection will be:
 internally displaced person;
 female heads of household;
 youth who have dropped out from schools
 members of low-income/large families
 survivors of gender-based violence
 people experiencing mental health challenges.
The courses will be delivered in officially certified institutes registered with the General Association of Syrian craftsmen. All successful participants will therefore receive a certificate that is recognized by Syrian employers upon completion of the program. Based on the specifics of the course, participants will receive start-up kits to support them in establishing their income generating activity. Besides building the technical capacity of the participants, the VT courses are also designed to help the beneficiaries mitigate the negative effects of the crisis on their mental and psychosocial well-being. In addition to contribute to improve beneficiaries’ livelihoods, the program also aims at reducing their vulnerabilities by engaging them in a team of peers and therefore helping them make new friends; the increase of knowledge and skills will enable participants to improve their self-esteem, become more self-reliant. In this aspect, the VT has proved very effective in improving the beneficiaries’ social lives. For youth out of school, who might be at risk of exploitation, illegal migration, armed recruitment or being engaged in illegal or other risky activities, VT also represent an opportunity for them to provide an income for their family.

4 - Needs

Preliminary findings from assessments conducted during the second half of 2020 to inform HNO 2020, indicates that approximately 9.9 million are in need of assistance within the early recovery sector. Severe disruption to basic services and damages to infrastructures have caused a fast decline and worsening of the living conditions; loss and depletion of livelihoods, coupled with increased cost of living, have further contributed to the deterioration in the quality of life and eroded resilience of the Syrian population. Multiple and protracted displacement combined with the damage to education infrastructure has contributed to high levels of unemployment which is mostly affecting young people. Findings shared by the early recovery cluster indicates that vocational training related needs are most severe in the major cities, including Damascus. The lack of economic and educational opportunities and the resulting inability to meet families' basic needs continue to be major contributors to vulnerability, especially among youth, femaleheaded households and people living with disabilities. It is often women who struggle the most to access limited livelihood opportunities, and they are particularly affected when they are heads of household. During October 2019, IMC MHPSS team conducted different focused group discussions with the beneficiaries from its community center in Jaramana, Rural Damascus. The results indicated the main challenges faced by the population in Jaramana is mainly of financial nature, combined with displacement and disintegration of families, breaking of social protection mechanisms, depletion of family assets and reduced livelihoods opportunities. Those challenges, combined with reduced availability of services, translate in negative coping mechanisms and heavily affect the living conditions of most of the population (host, returnees and displaced communities).

5 - Implementation of activities

1 Selection of beneficiaries: Prospective trainees will be identified based on recommendations from shelters, NGOs, MHPSS/Protection teams working in IMC facilities and previous beneficiaries. To ensure that courses support those most in need, applicants will be screened by IMC Livelihoods & VT team. During interviews with candidates, IMC VT team assesses their qualifications, their IDP status, and other personal information. The interview is an opportunity for candidates to inform IMC of their interests in specific courses, and for IMC to assess their ability to fully benefit from the program. Applicants will need to meet one of the following criteria to enroll in this programme: being IDPs; breadwinners; having dropped out of school; lost their livelihoods; being orphans; having disabilities; having faced any form of violence; having faced MH challenges associated with stress, GBV, major depression, exploitation; facing poverty or destitution.2 Market survey: IMC team will conduct market surveys to assess what are the most required jobs, skills, vocations and services.3 Planning and implementation of training courses: based on the assessment IMC will proceed with contracting the service providers based on the courses required. These might include but are not limited to the following subjects: sewing, accounting, E-Marketing, photography, graphic design, programming, hairdressing, beauty care, culinary, food processing, carpentry, decoration.4 Distribution of start-up kits: Based on the specifics of the course, participants will receive kits to support them in establishing their income generating activity or to start their own practices.5 Beneficiaries satisfaction surveys: in addition to satisfaction surveys and assessments conducted at different stage of the course by IMC VT team, IMC MEAL team also contacts beneficiaries in order to assess their satisfaction and evaluate the impact of their participation to the program.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: girls may be prevented from attending the courses if travelling alone is risky Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation measures: Arranging shifts for girls in the day time when travelling is not a problem and choosing safe locations for the training's. Risk 2: Women and girls participating in the program may be exposed to problems from their husband if the courses included men Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation measures: Arranging separate courses for male and other for female, also will be the trainers are of the same gender as the trainees. Risk 3: PSEA and safeguarding related risks Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) IMC policies and procedures in place, including effective, safe and confidential reporting mechanisms; 2) All staff provided with training and refresher training on PSEA and safeguarding issues.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK Risk 1. Increase of prices do not allow to cover all target beneficiaries or to provide startup kits as per plan. The risk is also associated with limited number of accredited institutions providing vocational training courses in the targeted area . Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: 1) Regular market assessments and monitoring of price for the items included in the startup kits; 2) Market surveys to identify any additional institute providing similar courses in the areas of intervention; Risk 2– Sanctions affecting/delaying bank transfers to Syria Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: 1) Maintain bank accounts with at least two different Syrian banks; 2) Continue to advocate through INGOs forum on waiving sanctions for humanitarian funds transfer into Syria; 3) Maintain information sharing with other INGOs to identify alternative solutions Assumptions: Training institutions continue to operate and are able to provide training as required to all selected beneficiaries Adequate financial resources.
