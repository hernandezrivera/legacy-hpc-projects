Project Summary

Within SAMS efforts in integrating the protection service into HFs, SAMS plans under this proposal to continue providing (GP, GBV, CP and MA) services in its supported health facilities. SAMS aims to operate integrated protection services into 20 health facilities in northern Syria. Through this project, SAMS will work on achieving the following objectives: Objective1: Improve protection of population affected by the crisis through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearers Objective2: Improve access to quality and life-saving GBV response services for GBV survivors and women and girls at risk and put measures in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV Objective 3: Minimize the impact of explosive ordnance for communities most at risk.
Each protection safe space at the supported HFs will have PSS workers, GBV case Managers, and Case Workers, they will provide in center and outreach (GBV and GP) services including Identification of cases; GBV case management; GBV prevention and awareness-raising (utilizing GBV awareness toolkit), SAMS will work on the inclusion of males in GBV programs, through ensuring their access to all services, including awareness raising and case management, without discrimination or causing any harm utilizing the best practices and lessons learned; non-GBV and non-CP case management; PSS and referrals to specialized services (CMR, HLP, Health, mental health, CVA, livelihoods, etc.), outreach emergency protection services in coordination with the emergency protection response team. As for the MA component, it will be provided in 4 health facilities. The VA team in each facility will consist of: Physiotherapists, PT assistants, and PSS workers, they will provide specialized services (PT and rehabilitation in addition to MHPSS services) for the survivors of explosive hazard accidents (both in center and outreach mobile services), Moreover, SAMS will refer the survivor to other service providers. SAMS will follow all the precautionary measures as per SAMS & Protection Cluster Recommendations and Best experiences and practices in implementing GBV programs in the time of pandemics. Furthermore, awareness against COVID 19 or Cholera will be included in all community messages and awareness activities.
SAMS will conduct routine monthly visits to the supported facilities to monitor the performance of provided services through the utilized GBV M&E toolkit (individual interviews, spot check, observations, etc.), MEAL team will carry out the community based KAP survey to measure the impact of provided protection services on BNFs and targeted communities. Besides, SAMS will monitor the project technically through the technical team in Gaziantep (GBV specialist, MHPSS and Physical rehabilitation technical staff), finally, for the VA services, SAMS will use the Functional Independence Measure and WHO DAS.
SAMS will ensure the inclusion of people with disabilities through direct visits and through assessing the beneficiaries' needs and tailoring the responses on a case-by-case basis, SAMS is going also to offer transportation service to the most vulnerable groups specifically women and adolescents’ girls to access the services, through mapping the communities to identify the targeted beneficiaries who will benefit from the transportation. SAMS will consider the people with mobility disabilities, through alterations and adjustments to health care facilities’ buildings such as establishing ramps at the entrance and handicapped toilets, secure clear floor space on both sides of the entry door to permit entry and exit in addition to removing architectural barriers, SAMS will ensure the availability of some important equipment such equipment includes wheelchairs, A handle or support rail is often needed along one side of the table for stability during a transfer and during the examination, in addition to gurneys and stretchers.

3 - Needs

The continuous deterioration of the protection situation will result in further neglect of persons with disabilities and the elderly. Child protection risks are to worsen 96% children are labor HNO2023, with rights violations (child recruitment, detention, neglect, exploitation, sexual violence, abduction, attacks on schools and hospitals, and denial of humanitarian access) continuously on the rise (HNO 2023 draft) Latest SAMS KAP survey for SGBV and PSS needs in NWS showed that emotional-psychological abuse still was the most reported type followed by physical abuse, economic violence, and sexual abuse. Based also on HNO 2023 draft, an estimated 2 of 3 survivors will sustain lifelong impairment and will need long-term assistance. Approximately one-third of all populated communities in Syria are estimated to be contaminated where based on 2022 MSNA, 73% of heads of households interviewed reported that at least one household member has exhibited signs of psychological distress. The deteriorating economy and widespread poverty, lack and loss of livelihoods, destruction and loss of housing and property, protracted and multiple cycles of displacement, substandard living conditions (even for people in areas of relative stability), family separation, and the breakdown of family or community support structures have depleted the coping abilities of individuals and communities and generated high levels of psychological distress. As a result, people resort to harmful coping mechanisms.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: lack of sufficient support/supervision for specialized staff; limited specialized mental health professionals inside Syria. Possibility: high, Impact: high. Mitigation: SAMS initiated recently a program that supports mental health education, in the meantime, until the graduation of the first batch of the PSWs and other specialized personnel, SAMS will depend on the available capacities utilizing the referral pathways and will build the capacities of the available staff in collaboration with the MHPSS TWG and HC.Risk: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm. Possibility: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: SAMS GBV specialist will ensure proper implementation of the SoPs and updating the services mapping on regular basis, including only those that can provide survivor-centered services. Risk: Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services. Possibility: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: Very limited staff will work on the cases, assure that our confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place.The local population may become reliant on victim assistance and medical capacity provided by humanitarian organizations. Possibility: Low. Impact: Low. Mitigation: As part of SAMS exit strategy, SAMS build the capacities of the local community in order to hand over the services to them.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. Possibility: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: The availability of a clear complaint mechanism within the service centers and the definition of it during the definition of the beneficiaries of the services of the Center and explain the mechanism of its flag "complaints box and mechanisms" follows the complaints section directly to the private line and mail moderator complaints section and brochures and the mechanism of regular complaints, in addition to the availability ofperiodic training for service providers and ensure supervision of effective technical service.SAMS displays various posters to enhance the awareness of both the health facility staff and the beneficiaries regarding (PSEA), the services provided, the patients’ rights and responsibilities, and the (CRM). The CRM poster includes the complaints/feedback channels (Phone hotline number, WhatsApp, Viber, and email), and SAMS installed a designated ‘Questions/Comments/Concerns’ box. Moreover, SAMS developed a short animation video displaying patients’ rights and SAMS’ CRM channels, the video is published via SAMS social media channels. SAMS will include also SEA network Whatsapp number as well in facilities. CP risk:Assumption2: Community welcome visits outreach teams for sites. Risk: Due to its conservative nature, the community do not welcome home visits conducted by the PSS workers.Mitigation: outreach teams include females from the same Community.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / GoS. Mitigation measures: (ATC), Information security SOP; Relocating the activities locations; Inform the partner civil authorities (HD and Civil Local Authorities), of the suspension and the reasons, in order to play their role in negotiation and advocacy, and apply the agreements of neutralizing humanitarian facilities.Risk: UNSC XB non-renewal. Impact: High. Mitigation: SAMS has multiple channels of funding and has cross-border access to pass commodities to Syria with its wide local coordination with local authorities. Alongside contingency plans from OCHA and UN in place prior to implementation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries. Mitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the training regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Theft. Mitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records.Risk: Financial risks (Cash Transfer/ Financial Diversion). Mitigation: SAMS utilizes official channels, authorized by the Government of Turkey, to transfer money from Turkey to Syria.
