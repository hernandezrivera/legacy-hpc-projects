Project Summary

The intervention under Sector Objective 1 aims to target vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs) (displaced, evacuated, or returnees) and host community in Northwest Syria (NWS). The overall objective of this project is to ensure that vulnerable children and their families, who have been forced to flee inside Syria are able to meet their basic needs, and are protected. Save the Children (SC) will scale up the acute response to continuous displacement as result of the active conflict in NWS. Through the rapid response mechanisms, SC and partners will deliver critical services through different modalities (cash or in-kind). Note that based on the needs, Multi-purpose cash grant (MPGs) and/or Ready to Eat Rations (RTERs) could be distributed in camps and different IDP sites hence this is to be also consulted with Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster. The MPGs 1,400 (FMoFA) and 2,100 (Danida), alongside the RTERs: 3,500 one-off is distributed to recent and most economically vulnerable IDPs HHs who are in need of urgent assistance, and 1,500 HHs receive one-time food assistance in the form of RTERs. Specific activities include: monitoring displacements and returns; coordinate food assistance needs with the Food Security Cluster; conducting a rapid market assessment to ensure no negative unintended consequences with a cash distribution; conduct rapid needs assessment to determine appropriate mechanism; registering beneficiaries; refer Child Protection (CP) cases to appropriate services; distribute relevant and appropriate support (e.g. MPG, or RTER) to beneficiaries; conducting Post-Distribution Monitoring; and training staff and community members on child safeguarding, safe identification and referral; CP Case Management supervision and coaching; identifying and refer most vulnerable children to CP team and other services.
The activities under Sector Objective 2, instead, will address the needs of the most vulnerable families (mainly those with children under 5) by improving their household (HH) level food security as foundation for improved livelihoods conditions and protection. SC will support targeted families by creating livelihood opportunities and improving agriculture and livestock production, while at the same time improving water management, irrigation and other agricultural infrastructure in close collaboration with local institutions wherever possible. By improving production of crops and livestock by-products, it is anticipated that this will increase availability of these goods in the local market, particularly in terms of nutritional food types (e.g. dairy products from livestock, and vegetables). In addition, improvements to household income for the targeted agro-pastoralists is expected to support local traders by increasing the purchasing power of targeted households for essential goods as well as increasing the demand for livelihoods inputs in the future. Activities include the provision of agricultural inputs for Winter and Summer Cultivations to 1,300 HHs, complemented by irrigation infrastructure support for 700HHs, the provision of inputs for Kitchen Gardens to 300HHs, and the provision of fodder support to 600 herder HHs. All these activities are complemented by training and/or extension services. Furthermore, in order to address protection mainstreaming aspects, we will conduct also assessment exercises to ensure that negative coping mechanism into which farmers are engaging are addressed (e.g. over pumping; use of wastewater mixed with clear water for farming purposes). SC will also be looking at preparedness for natural shocks such as floods, droughts and other seasonal fluctuations by working with farmers and vulnerable HHs to ensure they are considering these shocks into the preparation of their infrastructures and farming inputs.

Needs

As per the latest estimates cited by the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), there are currently 6.1 million people internally displaced inside Syria. In addition to movements agreed through area-level reconciliation processes, the offensives have led directly to significant displacement and life-threatening living conditions for the affected communities. NWS is facing a potential escalation, with estimates of up to 700,000 people will be displaced in any initial offensive (Turkey Health Cluster Bulletin, August 2018), adding pressure on an already over-stretched humanitarian response within the area. These households (HHs) are expected to move toward the Turkish border, particularly to Salqin, Dana, Afrin and A’zaz, where there are already camps hosting thousands of displaced populations (REACH, September 2018). The availability of food in markets has generally improved compared to the previous years. Main constraints are linked to the high exchange rate of Syrian Pounds to US Dollar; steep transport costs; and low demand due to high unemployment rates and limited purchasing power. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80% of an unskilled laborers’ monthly salary and 50-80% of a public service employee’s monthly salary. A review of local minimum rates for unskilled work carried out in different locations of Aleppo and Idleb Gov. in 2018, where 20 days of unskilled labour at minimum market rates would pay $90-100, below the SMEB. As per ESCWA, an estimated 83.4% of Syrians live below the upper (moderate) poverty line which is an increase from 28% in 2010. The Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) which takes into account health, education and basic needs related indicators showed that the national poverty rate in Syria is at 38.9%. The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in the 2017-2018 winter season in Syria. Low rainfall in the initial months of the winter were followed by floods in spring. While the overall rainfall was in line with normal years, the rainfall distribution was not appropriate for good crop growth/yield. The result was lower yield/donnum compared with the previous season, or the full loss of the harvest. The low rainfall led to less growth, less plant elongation, and general stress on the plants that resulted in dwarf or smaller plants. According to FAO HNO’s analysis 2019, unprecedented livestock asset losses are estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies, with main challenges being the high cost of fodder and the insufficient veterinary services. This was confirmed by SC-funded FSL project in 2018, although the impact was exacerbated by population displacement. In May 2018 (13th-19th), SC conducted a Rapid Needs and Market Assessment (RNA) in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates; the majority of the target beneficiaries had been displaced at least twice in the last five years. The RNA used a survey method to determine the needs and to assess the feasibility of cash as a modality. The findings showed that over 67% lived in rented accommodation, and that 79% did not have food stocks available at household (HH) level. It was also highlighted that shelter needs increase in the winter season, with temperatures ranging between 3-7 degrees.The winter season also coincides with the lean season. In SC's experience in NWS, MPGs are an effective way to provide targeted support to meet basic immediate needs, whilst preserving dignity. During a MPGs learning workshop conducted by SC and partner staff (for a project in Idleb in 2017), feedback from a beneficiary was that “MPG support gave people freedom and flexibility in selecting and buying their life basic needs”. An MPG programme Evaluation (SC, March 2018) revealed that “100% of beneficiaries said the assistance provided helped them meet their short-term/immediate needs and the majority, 80% said they had improved access to food as a result of the cash grants”.

Implementation of activities

On Obj. 1, SC monitors the context vs. the indicators. With Local Councils (LCs), community leaders, and referrals from CP and CM outreach teams, SC identifies economically vulnerable new arrivals. The selection criterion focuses on those deemed the most economically vulnerable – i.e. HHs with pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), and children under 5. SC through partners distributes cash assistance to newly displaced HHs. SC coordinates with the Cash-Based Response Technical Working Group (CBR-TWG) to monitor market functionality; coverage; response plans; and amount calculations. Idlib markets are currently functioning, with relatively stable prices; sufficient vendors; and availability. MPGs support market functionality; beneficiaries directly purchase items required from vendors. SC continues to work with the CBR-TWG to monitor market functionality, specifically prior to distributions. An alert on displacement/emergency is shared to capture location; expected numbers; and nature of response. After a security review, partners conduct a Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) to determine market conditions (incl. functionality/availability of supplies); appropriateness of MPGs; and priority needs. SC coordinates with the FSL and Shelter/NFI Clusters on response to distribute MPGs/RTERs. After the RNA, the response modality is decided. When responding, partners orientate stakeholders on the criteria and sign a MoU. Using the criteria, LCs register beneficiaries, with support from partners. Beneficiaries are HHs (not individuals). Security permitting, in order to reduce margin of error, partners conduct a random verification (50%). In the case of <95% of the sample meeting the criteria, partner reviews with LC, and conducts 100% verification. To prevent errors, partners have a feedback mechanism for non-beneficiaries to raise concerns directly; consult with neutral third parties to verify anyone who has not been included in the original list; and referrals from other SC/partner programmes. Final lists will be signed and approved by all parties. Once approved, no changes can be made without written approval from partners and LC with written justification. This information is submitted to SC with the final lists. In line with FSL Cluster recommendations, SC provides RTERs in order to meet immediate kilocalorie daily requirements (2100). On Obj. 2, SC distributes agricultural inputs to restart or expand agricultural production for HH consumption needs and income, and training on improving practices (provided through extension agent services and farmers field schools), such as the importance of rehab of spillways for water drainage. Services cover: irrigation infrastructure to improve efficiency of use of scarce resources (water); rehab of infrastructure to access water more efficiently (canals; wells) or to use water more efficiently (drip irrigation. Distribution of inputs for Kitchen Gardens (KG) aims to provide an increase in vegetable availability; improve food diversity and reduce HH expenditure. Based on SC’s experience, HHs are likely to produce more than needed at HH level and sell/process part of the harvest, thus creating additional income. KGs provide an opportunity to involve women through the provision of inputs, trainings, and provide a launchpad in forming small home-based businesses. It offers an opportunity to promote diverse diet and integrate Nutrition, through prioritizing PLWs and children under 2. KGs provide HHs with tools for harvest on their homestead (close proximity is crucial during insecurity). Fodder provision to protect livestock assets (goats; sheep) is planned for 3 months per year, during times of highest scarcity, supporting 5 goats/sheep per HH, and training for improving practices. The modality is planned to be vouchers; it will be adjusted if needed. When possible, infrastructure rehab that complements and enhances the needs above (e.g. canals) will be linked up with CfW activities (reported in ERL HRP).

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: The potential community tension due to service delivery locations/types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served/can not access or if information offered is not consistent between communities. Mitigating Measures: Ensure partners actively engage with communities, as part of their standard engagement approach clearly communicate to the local population the reasons for prioritizing the selected areas. Continuously ensure service mapping, consultation with the community on program interventions and changes, regular communication with different communities on the availability of services including host communities and returnee populations. Monitoring: SC will continuously monitor the reaction of communities to services provided, and mitigate any disputes by reaching out through FGD's, communities and local leaders. Risk: Resistance from local authorities to accessing and implementing programming. Mitigating measures: SC's partners have established relationships with relevant local authorities and stakeholders, such as local councils, representatives of community groups, community stakeholders, gatekeepers and religious leaders. The stakeholder will be, involved throughout program design and implementation. Monitoring: Regular meetings with relevant authorities and stakeholders. Risk: Barriers to access for marginalized groups Mitigating measures: Specific measures are taken to ensure input flow from marginalized groups; women and girls, children with disabilities and the elderly. Feedback can be submitted through Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (incl different media) and is used to inform planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluating activities. Mitigation measures are brainstormed with the community to ensure that men and women (people with disabilities) have equal access to SC activities. Additionally, as needed specific measures are taken to ensure activities are age and gender sensitive. Monitoring: FGDs, feedback mechanism Risk: SCI or partner staff violating SCI CSG rules harms children; damages the reputation of the organization; and can lead to loss of funding and prosecution. Mitigating measures: All SC staff, volunteers and representatives sign the Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct, and mitigating actions and put in place, all staff are trained on the Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct, as well as on reporting mechanisms; Partners have received CSG training via Hurras and training from SC on how to investigate cases related to CGS. Partners have their own reporting mechanism as well as CSG focal points in the field and main CSG focal point in Turkey. Risk: Affected populations and SC staff and volunteers, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to SC staff and volunteers partners on SC’s Anti-Harassment Policy and Code of Conduct. SC will also continue to provide trainings on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through SC’s PSEA focal points. Overall Monitoring: SC upholds the importance of setting up various channels of communication and complaint to mitigate the risks or solve the issues in a rapid and most culturally sensitive way. As some of the activities are harboring high risks for both beneficiaries and humanitarian workers (including risk of sexual exploitation or harassment), SC and partners’ staff will be trained on various topics on potential risks and how to mitigate them and provided with confidential complaint mechanisms to report in case of an event. Throughout the activities, SC’s partners will be conducting regular consultations with communities and FGDs, and will be promoting different communication channels to provide their feedback and complaints such as complaint boxes, phone lines, email and one on one meetings. Lastly, SC carries out monitoring through TPM and remote participation to FGD's

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Safety & Security (S&S) SC’s partners engage with relevant stakeholders and receive timely updates. SC works on a common response to unacceptable conditions. Airstrikes SC supports partners to immediately report security incidents to be analysed and to develop and implement comprehensive safety plans. Loss of access SC and partners conduct thorough security assessments and maintain updated contingency plans. If necessary, SC will suspend programs and work to resume activities. If this is not viable, SC would assess possibilities for relocation. Only in a worst-case scenario would project termination be considered. Work permits Measures are in place to mitigate the Gov. of Turkey (GoT) legislative environment hindering SC’s ability to operate, I.e. using a third-party HR company to overcome work permit challenges. Diversion of resources to terrorist/ proscribed groups In 2018, interviews were conducted to identify areas for increased risk mitigation; an action plan was developed. SC launched an initiative with OCHA to provide external training on international humanitarian law and negotiation with armed actors. Partners have their own systems of anti-terrorism vetting checks for all contracted suppliers and SC’s Syria office vets against the third-party database for procurements $750+. Shelling Whilst the most frequent attacks of shelling take place in southern rural Idlib and northern rural Hama, no area is immune. Escalation of military activity at Turkey/Syria border SC program locations and contingency plans are under constant review and the Hibernation, Relocation and Evacuation plan is shared with staff. Offices and guesthouses are located away from military bases and have the necessary S&S infrastructure. Attacks by armed groups Security assessments are conducted on potential project sites. Proximity to military locations is monitored and a back-up vehicle is used in high-risk areas. Staff transit must be pre-approved and take place during daylight. Improvised Explosive Devices SC coordinates closely with Tetra Tech, new project locations are cleared by the Mines Advisory Group and follow up trainings in Mine Risk Education are planned for staff. Road accidents SC drivers are tested; daily vehicle checks; all vehicles are equipped with tracking devices and satellite phones; mandatory use of seatbelts. Disruption to cash transfer operations In NWS, NGOs have seen temporary suspension of Bab Al Hawa and have faced challenges in money transfer due to GoT restrictions.
