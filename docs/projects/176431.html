Project Summary

Project goal:
This overall goal of this project is to reduce excess to morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases among the vulnerable population of returnees/IDP and host communities in Morobo, and Terekeka Counties through setting up mobile and static health clinics package providing essential emergency critical lifesaving healthcare services.
Project location
This project will be implemented among affected vulnerable communities in Gemeza, tindilo, and rijang payams of Terekea County while in morobo county the project is targeting Morobo town, Bura, Isebi/Awuro, Panyana, and Wudabi which currently accessible
Summary of Project Activities:
The project will focus on provision of essential emergency critical lifesaving healthcare services through mobile clinic teams amongst the displaced population within the host communities, Ono aid will recruit qualified staff locally and deployed to the sites: including Clinical Officers, Registered Nurses, Midwives CHWs, Community Mobilizers, Laboratory Assistants dispensers Home Health Promoters and supporting staff, the mobile health clinic teams will be involved conducting OPD consultations including diagnosis, treatment and referral of common communicable, manage cases of SGBV (Clinical Management of rape) and referrals of cases for further management, weekly IDRS/EWARs reports submission and non-communicable diseases. Midwives will be responsible for maternal and child health care including ANC, skilled deliveries and PNC services in the community. Nurses will provide basic nursing care, taking patient’s vital signs, dressing of wounds, establishing Oral Rehydration Corner and carry out vaccinations of children. The Lab Assistants will carry out malaria tests using RDTs in the current setting. The Dispenser’s role is to issue drugs to patients as prescribed by the Clinical officers. The Home Health Promoters assigned to households will provide health education sessions and follow up with patients on treatment, and defaulter tracing. They will work a long side with community mobilizers to raise awareness on health-seeking behavior, hygiene promotion, and defaulter tracing. Community mobilizers will mobilize communities prior to mobile clinic days to create demand for health care including EPI, ANC and OPD activities during mobile clinic days. The cleaners will ensure adequate cleanliness of the area, fetching water for the team and manage waste collection.

2 - Project activities

1- Support through mobile emergency clinics teams: the mobile health clinic teams will be involved conducting OPD consultations including diagnosis, treatment of common communicable, manage cases of SGBV (Clinical Management of rape) referrals of cases for further management, Nurses will provide basic nursing care, taking patient’s vital signs, dressing of wounds, establishing Oral Rehydration Corner and carry out vaccinations of children. The Lab Assistants will carry out malaria tests using RDTs in the current setting. The Dispenser’s role is to issue drugs to patients as prescribed by the Clinical officers. 2- Conduct Epidemic disease surveillance, this will be done through general Health education and mobilization for routine, immunization, screening of under 5 for malnutrition with MUAC and lactating women for malnutrition, including rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women as well as Screening for Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy, vaccination needs to all under 5 and TB Screening, the mobile health approach is employed to achieve rapid response to fragile health stations, communities under ( IPC 4) of this project areas to reach affected patients or provide referrals where necessary, this will reduces access to morbidity and mortality, in hard to reach . 3- Communicable Diseases ONO AID will work to prevent, detect and respond to communicable diseases including case management. In addition, the services carried out through the mobile clinics’ sites. National MoH treatment guidelines will be used for case management. RDT Para-checks for malaria and syphilis will be done at mobile outreach sites. Weekly reporting on data collected to Health Cluster at both State and National level on common morbidities like Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Measles and COVID-19 reported through EWARS and IDSR.4- Emergency measles vaccination for children 6 month to 15 years; During the mobile emergency health clinic consultations, children will be screened for measles vaccine and other antigens as well; vaccine will be obtained from health facilities. the activities for immunization will be done twice a week every Mondays and Thursdays until the project period.5- Reproductive Health. ONO AID will provide maternal and child health care activities guided by MISP and BPHNS of the National Ministry of Health. At each mobile clinic site, a midwife will provide ANC, skilled community deliveries, post-natal care within 7 days, family planning, STI/HIV prevention and management, and GBV prevention & management. ONO AID will ensure that, other commodities like rape kits for management of rape cases, family planning commodities, Mama Kits are distributed, the midwife will conduct counselling on the birth preparedness plan and do screening of pregnant women for malnutrition during the ANC services and those found malnourished referred for nutritional services at the respective targeted Payams . 6- Immunization ono aid will provide consultations where children are screened for measles vaccine and other antigens as well as vaccinated the vaccines were obtained from WHO/UNICEF Core pipeline 7- Community Health ONO AID mobile health teams will provide health education sessions at all mobile clinic sites established and, in the communities, as well. Community Health workers will provide at the established mobile clinic sites while the Home Health Promoters will provide in the communities and household level twice in a week every Mondays and Fridays. Community mobilizers in their respective locations will mobilize community members to utilize health care services and for adequate turn up for efficient service up take. necessary topics covered will be diseases prevention and health promotion, ANC services, awareness rising on GBV issues, COVID-19 preventive measures, and personal hygiene

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Upon the approval of the project ONO AID will engage all stakeholders involved in the project right from project inception to the closure. In order to ensure a sense of ownership. Consultative community meetings held with the community members and other stakeholders to inform them about the project. These stakeholders include County Health Department, community leaders, women’s groups, youth groups and religious leaders. Project beneficiaries will be encouraged to express their views, concerns and feedback regarding project implementation through regular biweekly meetings. Their expectations as part of the routine monitoring while ONO AID will provide regular feedback regarding key issues identified by these affected people and how they dealt with by the organization. Community leaders including IDP leaders used as a source of communication between ONO AID and the affected populations. Persons wishing to provide feedback are allowed through suggestion box provision at the established mobile clinic sites in all the targeted Payams of Reggo, Gemeza, Nyori, Tombek and Terekeka and a location to voice their feedback in person. This information will be displayed publically, alongside the IEC materials used by the health promotion teams, and at the established mobile clinics sites. ONO AID has a Complaints Handling and Feedback Mechanism policy document that spells out how the rights of beneficiaries and ensures active consultation of beneficiaries from the project inception to phase out. The beneficiary feedbacks handled with confidentiality and shared only with relevant stakeholders without compromising quality of project implementation and without exposing the beneficiaries to protection risks. There will be active consultation with the beneficiaries before, during and at project close out; their rights to accept or refuse certain project components considered.

ONO AID will involve the community leaders of IDPs and host communities by introducing the project and in the staff recruitment like community mobilizers, community health workers (CHWs) and home health promoters (HHPs) to support the implementation and enhance sustainability, project acceptability, improvement in household income, and boost the local markets in the affected communities. More so, Community leaders will be involving in the project meetings from inception and at the end to inform them about the starting and closure of the project activities.
