Project Summary

The proposed intervention links and contributes to the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Sector strategic objectives for the upcoming 2024 humanitarian cycle by
1) improving the food security status of food insecure, conflict affected people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance;
2) supporting the self-reliance of conflict affected households through protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives; and
3) improving conflict affected communities’ capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods through the rehabilitation/building of productive infrastructure as well as supporting services.
The programme in 2024 targets conflict affected displaced populations, returnees and host communities to meet their basic needs and essential basic services in West Darfur, Red Sea West Kordofan, and South Kordofan States.
this intervention will also contribute to the FSL sector sub-objectives by addressing life threatening and life sustaining needs.
In 2024, Concern aims at reaching (target: 2000 MPCA, 2000 Food assistance, 18,000 Input kits, 3,500 Livestock Vaccinations and 1,050 livelihoods/Agric trainings shelter kits) across West Darfur, Red Sea, South and West Kordofan through a integrated, multi-funded interventions. In-kind food assistance will provide immediate, life-saving support where cash programming is not an option, , cash, and voucher assistance will be the preferred option in geographic locations where transfer system exists and can be utilized by humanitarian response, agricultural and livestock interventions for both farmers and pastoralist (including animal vaccination campaigns will be promoted in locations where the conflict did not affect the local agriculture system and both agriculture and livelihoods activities can be implemented.).
Vulnerability targeting will follow selection criteria preferring integrated approaches, and the most vulnerable people, including the newly displaced and protracted displaced, refugees and host communities will be prioritized, with a special focus on women and girls.
Concern Worldwide will mainstream such as S/GBV risks mitigation, conflict-sensitive targeting to minimize exploitation that are usually associated with low access to food, cash provision, lack of privacy, and overcrowding. In order to reduce these risks, flexible programming such as joint PDM for service deliver monitoring, involving females in monitoring processes etc. will be applied. Based on Concern's current present in West Darfur, West and South Kordofan and Red Sea states, response will mobilize local networks, available social structures especially in places where IDPs settled with local/host community population to minimize the social, community level conflicts over limited resources.
Concern Worldwide response plan follows the cash working group and cluster agreed unit values, although local prices and availability of resources will be considered, and may play an impact during roll out of the action.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. To ensure appropriate protection mainstreaming, Concern will apply international and internal program quality monitoring standard procedures to target most vulnerable persons of concern with a focus on women, children, elderly, and persons living with disabilities. Protection principles such as CRM tools which will be complemented by international guidelines, Sphere standards to ensure accountability to, and participation of, affected population. Principles of protection mainstreaming will be exercised, including meaningful access through consultation and involvement of communities about the best way to deliver FSL services and consideration for vulnerable groups, and accountability mechanisms. Protection / (S) GBV risks faced by women and girls will be core focus of capacity building for staff and volunteers to ensure they are able to detect cases of abuse, recognize and refer to protection-mandated entities. A comprehensive protection referral mapping will be conducted for each FSL activity to ensure right referral in our operation locations. Information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a project field team, and the Safeguarding and Accountability Coordinator will work with the CRM officers in each of the field offices to link all activities with safeguarding principles within all operation activities. . Understanding of "Do No Harm" approach will be mainstreamed through training sessions and sensitization across project activities, and will be integrated in all our distribution (in-kind and cash programming alike). Post-Distribution Monitoring will be conducted, max. 2 weeks after each distribution (in-kind, cash), will feed protection information for the programme team CRM system will use protection related information gathering tools that will feed the improvement of the service deliver. Concern Worldwide has a dedicated Safeguarding and Accountability Coordinator who will provide support in the rolling out the protection and safeguarding related programme mechanisms for the FSL intervention.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Complaints and feedback will be collected by staff (CRM officer) and will be shared with programme team (except sensitive complains) and each complain will be responded. If the complain will be sensitive, the CRM office will work with the Safeguarding and Accountability Coordinator in the organization to address the issue in a separate protocol system to protect the individual. The programme team will use the feedback to adjust e.g. the contents of the Input kits, MPCA amounts, improve quality of the contents etc. After distribution of services, particularly the Cash and Input kits, there will be a PDM (post-distribution monitoring) for each activity to visit the beneficiaries (in community or HH level), and listen the feedback of the services, in-kind, cash-based support. Feedback from the project participants will help not only to address the particular complaint, feedback, but also to improve the quality of programme. Information (non-confidential) will be shared with cluster members to further improve the quality of services as well as adjust the programme to the eve-changing context of Sudan. During the PDM, sensitive information will be gathered following safeguarding and protection protocols, and response/referral of these cases will be separately managed.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by two central documents, notably the organisation’s Programme Participant Protection Policy and Code of Conduct; and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Standard in Accountability and Quality Management. Concern’s Programme Participant Protection Policy sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries –children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist e.g. gender based violence due to lack of safety nets, food, stealing cash from beneficiaries ect.; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities such as harassments while walking to Cash distribution site, iii) the risks our programme may create such as distributing Inputs kits only for displaced people not to consider other vulnerable groups. As certified member of HAP, Concern is committed to implementing the HAP Standard related to different aspects of beneficiaries’ accountability. Three of these aspects are particularly relevant to conflict sensitivity and protection: beneficiaries’ access to information (e.g. how beneficiary selection procedure conducted); beneficiaries’ participation in all the stages of the programme (e.g. from objective of an intervention, selection procedure, implementation till evaluation and reporting); and establishing a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for each community/place where the grant will be implemented. In 2022, Concern Sudan’s Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) was updated and aligned with country specific needs and priorities, and communities’ expectations, resulted the adjustment of the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Concern will incorporate beneficiaries’ feedback through two ways: 1) PDM after (max.) one month by the accountability team mainly FGDs, and HH visits, and 2) interviews with other stakeholders, programme participants contributed to the activity e.g. Input kit distribution, MPCA activities. Feedback and complaints feedback will use Satisfaction and Consultation form. Based on data received from these two sources, Concern will regularly evaluate and re-design the CRM system to improve accessibility, responsiveness, efficiency, and protection of the participants. Sensitive complaints (GBV risk, SGBV risk) will be managed through a separate CRM system to ensure safeguarding of the individual. The Accountability and Safeguarding Coordinator will handle these cases through a separate, data protection system. CWW has done a protection services mapping in 2022 that is currently under update. This referral system will be used to provide support and response to S/GBV survivals, as per standard protocol.
