Project Summary

Under the proposed Action ACTED aims to deliver critical emergency shelter and non-food item assistance to vulnerable and conflict-affected IDPs in Borno State. In alignment with core objectives of the Shelter and Non-Food Item Sector, the project will address immediate shelter and NFI needs faced by new arrivals, while ensuring equitable and safe access to life-saving assistance for vulnerable and newly internally displaced persons (IDPs). In order to provide immediate relief to IDPs, ACTED proposes to build on its long-standing experience as a Shelter and NFI implementing partner in Borno State, through engagement of its dedicated Shelter and NFI Team, in order to provision emergency aid in coordination with the Sectoral response. The Action will strengthen the overall humanitarian response to address urgent needs faced by new arrivals, by individuals living in protracted displacement without access to shelter repair, and IDPs living in vulnerable makeshift shelters, by delivering Bama type emergency shelters, and non-food item assistance. The Action aims to contribute towards the significant decrease of protection risks faced by displaced families that lack access to basic and essential shelter and household needs by reducing exposure resulting from inadequate privacy and protection from the elements, and reducing correlated negative coping strategy adoption rates.
ACTED anticipates that the Action will directly benefit a total of 16,000 beneficiaries, based on a five-person household average, within Dikwa, Mafa, Maiduguri, and Monguno LGAs.

1 - Needs

Based rapid assessments conducted by ACTED in Dikwa in July and August 2019, an estimated 7,000 IDPs are in need of emergency shelters assistance following flooding. The damage caused by floods throughout this season has increased the threat of sanitation and health crisis, as on 31 July, ACTED CCCM reported 269 damaged latrines due to heavy rain, with the Joint Assessment on 14 August placing this number at over 500. Chronic over-congestion rates continue to affect living standards in camps, compounded by high makeshift shelter rates. ACTED’s CCCM have recorded shelter conditions, and found high makeshift prevalence rates in Dikwa camps: in Shuwari 60%, in Kamchejin 70%, Modu Kasa 90% and in Mohamed Kyarimi 100%. Since January 2019, ACTED’s CCCM Team have consistently collected reports from beneficiary communities on critical levels of NFI needs, debilitating the ability to safely store food, water, and maintain household hygiene. In late June 2019 in Monguno, ACTED conducted FGDs with food security beneficiaries across 9 camps, in which participants reported loss of food rations due to rain and floods due to lack of access to NFIs for safe storage, and damaged shelters allowing water entry. Further, IOM DTM Round 28 indicates that in 3 out of 12 camps in Monguno, there are IDPs without any access to shelter, while in 8 out of 12 camps over 50% of IDPs are living in makeshift shelters. Self-made shelters have high vulnerability rates, making them gravely susceptible to flood and storm damage, which carry further risks of loss to personal properties, food rations, and increasing susceptibility of exposure to unsanitary environments. Throughout 2019, IDPs residing in Mafa have been underserved as humanitarian actors minimize their presence in the location. In October 2018, there were 23 humanitarian partners. Yet, in October 2019, only 8 partners were present. ACTED remains operational in the camp as the Shelter actor but the shelter needs following the recent rainy season have significantly increased, leaving vulnerable households in urgent need of emergency shelter assistance. The rainy season of 2019 has resulted in 948 households identified as needing critical shelter assistance due to partially damaged or completely destroyed shelters following severe flooding at the end of August 2019. To further note, the immense shelter needs within Mafa, in October 2019, ACTED’s CCCM carried out site profiling that indicates that only 55% of shelters are able to meet SPHERE standards, with 1,400 makeshift shelters present. Critical levels of shelter and NFI needs continue to debilitate the safety, security and wellbeing of IDPs across Borno State, requiring urgent emergency assistance.

2 - Activities

ACTED will procure, pre-position, and construct 1,600 Bama model emergency shelters in order to deliver critical emergency shelter assistance to displaced families. Shelter assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable IDP families, providing immediate relief to women, girls, boys and men by reducing exposure to increased risks of physical and verbal harassment and abuse when sleeping outside, and/or to individuals living in inadequate and vulnerable makeshift shelters. In addition, ACTED will procure and distribute 1,600 improved NFI kits, ensuring that the most vulnerable households receive urgent non-food item assistance. Beneficiary selection for both emergency shelter and non-food item assistance will be conducted based on vulnerability criteria, and in collaboration with CCCM Teams, community leaders, protection partners, and other implementing partners. ACTED will coordinate with NEMA/SEMA, site facilitators, IOM and partners present on the ground to identify land for the construction of Shelters, taking camp planning, infrastructure, roads, service points, flood risk areas, and international standards for shelter spacing into account. ACTED’s Shelter Team will develop site plans for the construction of the shelters, ensuring that camp planning is integrated into the intervention, ensuring adherence to the Do No Harm principle. All shelter construction activities will be closely supervised by ACTED’s technical Shelter Team, providing consistent management of the construction process, ensuring shelters are built in line with technical specifications for quality assurance. The Bama emergency shelter is designed for peri-urban and rural areas, with more difficult and unstable access and associated higher logistics cost, but where people are expected to remain for some time. The reinforced Bama shelter, approved by the SNFI Sector, provides more durability and sustainability against environmental stress, in conformity with international acceptable standards and suitability for family size in North-East Nigeria. The Bama shelter is 3.5m x 4.6m in size and consists of a robust timber frame secured by tie-wire bracing to increase the resistance to strong winds. It has a lockable door and windows for improved ventilation. It is clad in plastic sheets secured to the frame by timber battens. The entire shelter can be constructed within one day and has an intended life-span of 6 months. The NFI kits proposed for distribution under this project will be the Sector NFI Improved Kit, designed and approved by the SNFI Sector, and based on feedback provided by target communities and implementing humanitarian partners. Items included in the kit: 3 blankets, 2 foldable mattresses, 2 20-L jerry cans, 2 10-L jerry can, 7 pieces of bathing soap, 7 pieces of laundry soap, 150 NaDCC water disinfection tablets, 1 solar lamp, 2 10-L cooking pots, 2 7 L- cooking pots, 6 plates, 4 trays, 2 kitchen knives, 4 serving spoons, 2 kettles, 1 10-L basin, 3 treated mosquito nets, 1 package of reusable sanitary pads, 3 sleeping mats, 2 woven bag, and 2 20-L buckets. ACTED’s independent AMEU will conduct a post distribution monitoring exercise to assess beneficiary perceptions of selection criteria, distribution processes, the ability of kit contents to satisfy household needs, and ACTED staff conduct. The information collection process will be conducted by enumerators, recruited locally, under the oversight of ACTED’s AME Officer. Enumerators will receive training on appropriate data collection methods and tools, providing capacity building and economic empowerment through employment opportunities. Information collected will be analyzed and applied to future interventions, enabling the integration of beneficiary interests in future similar intervention processes.
