Project Summary

INTERSOS has been implementing lifesaving health activities within Borno state in covering 4 LGAs: Bama, Magumeri, Dikwa and Ngala since 2017 by providing free primary health care services to vulnerable populations both IDPs and host community. In Bama LGA, INTERSOS is currently providing free primary health care services (General OPD consultations, rapid diagnosis of common morbidities such as malaria and cholera, SRH services and referral of patients in need of secondary/ specialized health care services) at the GSSSS IDP camp and OTP at the Bama MCH facility, and is the only health actor in Magumeri LGA.
Through this project, INTERSOS intention is to strengthen the existing health system by providing support to the MCH health facility (in Bama LGA), and Magumeri General Hospital and its catchment PHCs in the remote hard-to-reach (H2R) parts of Gajiganna. INTERSOS will support MOH to ensure the continuation of health services by deployment of heath staff, provide performance-based stipends and capacity building / training, essential medical equipment and supplies, support in reproductive health services including BeMoc and SGBV services for survivors of sexual assault. The project will facilitate the referral of patients in need of secondary / specialized health care facilities to Maiduguri. INTRESOS will also engage in community mobilization and engagement through the CHV to provide health education, active case finding and referral of patients to the health facility. In addition, INTERSOS will mainstream protection, GBV, inclusion and COVID-19 risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) throughout the stages and activities of the project. Through the NHF funding, INTERSOS proposes to support increasing and the local health systems capacity for readiness and response for cholera, malaria and other epidemic prone diseases like hepatitis E, meningitis and measles. This includes procurement and pre-positioning of cholera kits, essential drugs and supplies in the supported facilities. The interventions seek to reinforce community dialogue and engagements in all health activities to systematically collect and provide answers to questions coming from the community and facilitate community-led response planning. In addition, the intervention will strengthen capacity of the existing CHWs to be able to detect and report suspected cases in support of the EDPT strategy of disease prevention. For a sustainable approach that will promote easy takeover by the MOH, INTERSOS will recruit only professional health positions, while the government will provide CHEWs and other support but INTERSOS will pay a performance- based incentive.
By addressing those unfunded gaps (through this proposal to NHF) in a well-coordinated rights-based approach that engages the target communities, government (SMOH, the Emergency Operations Centre [EOC] etc.,), UN agencies and the existing coordination structures: - a complete package of quality lifesaving and life-sustaining health services will be provided the most vulnerable populations in Bama GSSSS IDP camp and the host community. All services will be provided in accordance with guidelines approved by the health sector, SMoH, NCDC, WHO, SPHERE and other international standards, and in accordance with humanitarian principles -- free from all forms discrimination, based on prioritized need, avoiding duplication and with a focus on specific vulnerabilities, including pregnant and lactating women (PLW), persons with disabilities, elderly, chronically ill, IDPs and IDPs.
In order to provide an integrated multi-sector services, INTERSOS will implement the proposed activities in the same geographical areas and populations targeted by the nutrition, WASH and Protection proposals.

1 - Needs

After more than ten years of conflict, the humanitarian crisis in the BAY states remains one of the most severe in the world today. A total of 7.9 million people – more than one in two people in the three crisis-affected states are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020. The majority of the PIN are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis. It is estimated that one in four of the affected population is under 5 years of age. Women and children are the face of the protracted crisis, as they constitute up to 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the new displacements. The gender and age dimensions of the conflict triggered displacements is having a significant impact on health and wellbeing of women and girls especially with respect to crude mortality, morbidity and psychosocial trauma from fragmentation of households. The health sector targets 2020 PIN estimates for Magumeri stands at 86,119 accessible and 114,340 inaccessible people. Corresponding figures for Bama are 94,308 and 77,905 people respectively. Up to 59% of the accessible population in Magumeri live in remote H2R areas with limited access to quality health care. The 2019 MSNA indicate that out of 235 HHs that participated in the survey in Bama GSSS camp and host community, 45% reported illness from its member, dominated by symptoms as fever (57%), headache (47%), diarrhea (32%) and cough (24%). These HHs continue to face challenges of accessing quality healthcare due to structural barriers created by the on-going crisis. These include shortage of qualified health workers (doctors, nurses and midwives), essentials medical drugs, supplies and equipment. Only 15% of all births were in health facilities, while majority of the births occurred at home and unassisted by professional health workers thereby predisposing women and their newborns to deaths caused by delivery related complications and/or infections. Bama was mapped by SMOH as among 6 high risk LGAs for COVID-19, yet the capacity to cope with, and address the possible impact of disease outbreaks remains very limited, can easily be overwhelmed leading to high case mortalities. The IDPs, returnees and host communities continue to depend on humanitarian aid for basic healthcare. The assessment revealed further that even though at least 91% of HHs that received humanitarian assistance were from INGOs, the gap was still wide, as only 59% of HHs affirmed they had received some sort of assistance in the last 6 months. To this end, absence of humanitarian aid will escalate morbidities and mortalities from preventable diseases like malaria, measles, diarrhea and other childhood illnesses. The hostile security situation characterized by indiscriminate attacks continues to hamper efforts by humanitarian actors. The conflict triggered displacements have resulted in disrupted healthcare, social life, livelihoods and traditional food security safety nets, which together with overcrowding and congestion in the IDP camps exacerbate the risks outbreak and high mortalities from of communicable diseases like cholera and malaria, more-so among U5 and PLW due to sub-optimal access to health care services as well as compromised child feeding and care environment. The WHO estimates that more than half of the deaths recorded in Borno State are due to seasonal outbreaks of malaria during the rainy season. Malaria co-infection is also a leading cause of mortality exceeding 20 per cent among severely acute malnourished children (HNO 2020). The prevailing crisis is expected to worsen as a result of the unprecedented COVID-19 pandemic, particularly due to increased unprecedented stressors on the already highly fragile health system. As the challenge of COVID-19 increases, the State is already drawing resources and personnel from Nigeria’s health systems that are already suffering from a shortage of qualified personnel, a lack of stable financing and a reliable supply of essential drugs and equipment.

2 - Activities

• Increase health workforce through deployment of qualified healthcare workers to support MOH chews in the supported health facilitiesSupporting the provision of essential medicines, medical supplies and equipment: INTERSOS will collaborate with UNICEF for timely procurement of essential medical commodities, including equipment, medicine, consumables and supplies so as to PHC services at the health facilities. These include antimalarials, RDT kits, cholera kits, PEP kits, NCD kits, and other drugs, supplies and equipment required for lifesaving and life-sustaining healthcare for the vulnerable patients.• Strengthening the provision of SRH services: INTERSOS will continue to support the SRH services at MCH facility. Through MOH driven participatory discussions and awareness raising among women of reproductive age, INTERSOS aims to stimulate rights-based demand for RH services aimed at curbing the high maternal and child mortalities. Through this NHF funded project, RH services will be provided in line with the Minimum Initial service Package (MISP). To this end, PLW have access to free and quality, ANC, Basic Obstetric and Neonatal care, and PNC services. In addition, the project will support building capacity clinical management of rape (CMR) services to the survivors and ensure all the health facility staff are trained on CMR and on the internal and external referral to GBV services, including appropriate psycho-social support services. The MCH services will be provided in collaboration with the protection/GBV/PSS staff.• Enhancing readiness and response to epidemic prone diseases: INTERSOS proposes to support increasing and the local health systems capacity for readiness and response for cholera, malaria and other epidemic prone diseases like hepatitis E, meningitis and measles. This includes procurement and pre-positioning of cholera kits, essential drugs and supplies in the supported PHC and MCH facilities.• Strengthening community-based surveillance and reporting: INTERSOS- OFDA established robust network of 26 CHVs in Bama and 116 CHVs in Magumeri/Gajiganna will continue to be engaged at the frontline for early detection and reporting of suspected cases in their respective communities. The CHVs will refer suspected cases to health facilities, as well as notify INTERSOS health workers- who will verify the cases and notify the DSNO. COVID-19 surveillance will be integrated existing community-based surveillance system.• Training of health workers: INTERSOS will collaborate with SMOH, health sector, WHO and other health actors in Bama and Magumeri to organize capacity building sessions for 50 health workers and support staff working in the supported healthcare facilities. Training sessions will be based on the identified needs and continued on job coaching and mentorship on IMCI, ANC, PNC, BEmONC, and SGBV case management, CMR and COVID 19 mainstreaming, with focus on RCCE.• Monthly health education and awareness sessions: INTERSOS staff will work with the CHVs and community leaders to organize monthly awareness and health education sessions that will also provide platform for receiving feedback directly from the communities.• Bi-weekly community health education and meetings: The CHVs will be supported to organize monthly health education sessions with community local leaders and members in their communities. INTERSOS will mainstream inclusion, gender, protection and COVID-19 RCCE in all community engagement activities.• Printing and use of IEC and visibility materials: In order to assure accuracy and uniformity of the messages, the CHVs will be provided with standard IEC materials that have been designed by WHO and the SMOH. The IEC materials are available in dominant dialects, but INTERSOS will engage with WHO/MoH to allow addition of UNOCHA visibility on all IEC materials that will be printed with NHF funding. Messages on IEC materials will include pictures on alert signs and measures for preventing transmission.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project team will continue to assess the situation and respond to immediate needs among vulnerable groups, especially PLW, in the areas of intervention. INTERSOS has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, INTERSOS protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. Furthermore, all INTERSOS staff is bounded to INTERSOS Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all INTERSOS operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, transparency. Moreover, protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures:- Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities;- Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services;- Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

INTERSOS activities are based on the humanitarian principles of solidarity, justice, human dignity, equality of rights and opportunities, respect for diversity and coexistence, paying special attention to the most vulnerable people, including children, women, persons with disability (PWDs), older persons, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities in hard-to-reach (H2R) areas, and in a way that is free from all forms of indiscrimination. As a front-line humanitarian aid organization, INTERSOS embraces participatory community-led approaches, that involve target beneficiaries, Project Management Committees (PMCs) and key local leaders and influential persons to understand the project targeting criteria as well as expected contributions from the community, for instance social capital and indigenous knowledge. Community entry messaging include information like source of funding (donor), selection criteria, targeting criteria and expected humanitarian support among others. As part of the CHS commitments, INTERSOS establishes functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. A compliant help desks are set up in INTERSOS supported safe space (funded by OFDA) where people can report their complaints or give feedback about the project.Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including: face to face with field staff, WhatsApp, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. This information is sent to the INTERSOS office in Maiduguri, where it is added to the Complaints Response Mechanism Register and referred to the most relevant person.In addition to the aforementioned set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls, is at the base of the project design, implementation and Monitoring.
