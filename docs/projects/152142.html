Project Summary

Acute poverty, Food insecurity, Adverse unemployment, and high levels of gender-based violence are key challenges that IDPs, returnees and host communities face in both formal and informal IDP camps in North East Nigeria especially in DIKWA LGA in Borno State.

In order to combat these challenges and address critical needs of the IDPs, returnees and surrounding host communities, ADRA Nigeria seeks to foster income generation, promote self-worth and dignity, resilience, sustainable and durable solutions through restoration of livelihoods in the affected communities by establishing micro and macro livelihoods and early recovery activities like micro-gardening, enterprise development, productive asset development and financial literacy among others that would enhance and improve the livelihoods of the affected communities.
Through ADRA’s livelihoods experts; the project will equip women, youths and other targeted categories in and around the Gajibo, Magata/Sheffri,Mallam, Mudu/Kaza,Muliye/Jemuri,Ngudoram,Sogoma/Afuge,Ufaye/Gulile in DIKWA LGA Returnees, IDP in host communities and remaining population with marketable vocational skills and knowledge with which they will be able to be employed or start-up their own businesses within secured places in the LGA.
The project will carry out training activities in diversified livelihoods aspects that would include but limited to; 3T-automobile repairs, information technology (IT) short courses, beautification and saloon-cosmetology, carpentry and masonry, tailoring, cap making, bakery and pastry, poultry, weaving and hand craft, small-scale business-enterprise development, financial management skills training, book keeping and eventually form Village Savings and lending Associations (VSLAs) among the beneficiaries to foster sustainability as a durable solution.
ADRA will incorporate Cash based initiatives (CBI) like cash for work (CFW) in all planned interventions to the returnees and IDPs in host communities as one way of boosting their markets functionalities and also ensuring that there is a drift from dependency on aid. ADRA’s key targets will include; three thousand (3,000) individuals from different households with major focus on Women, the Youths; both girls and boys in the community, People living with disabilities (PLWDs), Elderly, single mothers and the chronically ill persons. The target distribution will be; 45% women, 40% youths and 20% men whereas the other interest groups have been captured among the indicated percentages above. This is because the above highlighted categories of people constitute more than an estimated 85% of the affected population. In addition, ADRA will consider crosscutting issues like age, gender diversity and mainstreaming (AGDM) in all the livelihoods and recovery activities especially during beneficiary selection to avoid any GBV cases as a result of assistance provided.

Needs

Through the activities of this project, ADRA will address the following needs:(1) Low levels of understanding about S/GBV (wrong perceptions, norms, and practices) among IDPs men and women about gender related issues but more so among men which affect the dignity and rights of girls and women;(2) Coping mechanism of the harsh situation by women and girls in formal and informal camps and host communities include begging, conditional pregnancy to increase family size to qualify for more cash or food assistance ( this is because beneficiaries with bigger family sizes are entitled bigger amounts of money, food distributed and other form of assistance based on per capita modality of assistance) , engaging in high risk sexual behaviour (all Unanimous)(3) High GBV levels especially in the formal and informal camps compared to IDPs in host communities.(4) High poverty levels and hence inability to meet basic needs(5) Lack of income, as it is observed that between 70% - 88% of the IDPs are not earning any income(6) Low income among women, in that about 55% more IDPs male earn an income than female(7) Violence linked to stress, because IDPs in all informal and formal camps link incidences of violence to stress over lack of employment.(8) Hopelessness among IDPs men that have been in this situation for longer since 2009 to date, which resort to elicit manners and abuse of drugs and violent behaviours targeting women.(9) Violence against women, since more women confess express experiencing more violence than men while more men engage in physical violence against their partners or abandon them.(10) Limited opportunities for IDPs women to learn new skills that can enable them enhance their earning compared to their male counterparts due to limited mobility due to culture and capability. Thus, equipping such women with the enterprise and employable skills would revert the situation above.
