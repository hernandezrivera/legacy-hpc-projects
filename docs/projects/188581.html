Project Summary

This proposal aims to target the the most vulnerable families of returnees, host communities and displaced through provision of summarization NFIs in Deir Ez Zor city and its countryside, Abu Kamal city and its country side and in Al Mayadin town and its country side, with special focus on vulnerable groups, youth, women headed households and people with disabilities, who have lost their livelihood means and sources, and who do not have any stable income to properly survive due to lack of work opportunities, thereby increasing their reliance on host communities support to survive, and as they do not have access to humanitarian lifesaving assistance and services provided by the limited number of humanitarian actors present in the area of intervention and its operational capacity which is not commensurate to meet the scale and scope of the crisis and its impacts on affected families in Deir Ez Zor governorate. And due to these negative impacts of the crisis on all sectors, combined with deteriorating underlying factors such as water, sanitation and heath care services, electricity supply and food security, BICA, through the medical teams and the assessment teams and its volunteers has conducted several home visits to majority of these families at their residence areas where the initial assessment at the targeted geographic areas estimates, there are more than 6,400 of families of 38,400 people are in urgent needs of humanitarian assistances.
As the targeted families are now experiencing the worst summer, the proposal recommends providing summer NFIs to alleviate the suffering of these vulnerable groups in summer as the climate of DZ is characterized by severe dry weather and high temperature in summer which reaches more than 45, and lasts more than six months, and as the project proposal was initially approved last year by the NFI sector, it is expected the distribution operation of the assistance items to the targeted beneficiaries will be launched by April 2022.
Moreover, As of the Electricity Company, the Governor office, SARC and DoSA as well local communities, Deir Ez Zor city and its countryside currently suffering of shortage of the electricity supply, in which the rate of feeding of electricity supply does not exceed 2% of the total of electricity produced at Syria level. This situation has affected negatively on living conditions and wellbeing of the poor, sick, people with disabilities. And despite these summarization and essential core NFIs are available at the local markets but these families unable to get it for its high prices, as a result to the depreciation of the Syrian pound against the US$ where the exchange rate of the US$ against Syrian pound has exceeded SP 3,500 in the local blanket markets, leading to more declining of purchasing power of the crisis affected families, in the face of skyrocketing prices of the basic living commodities including NFIs.
This project proposal has been designed in consultation and coordination with the humanitarian actors in the targeted areas, concerned line ministries especially DoSAL and SARC, UN agency especially with HCR, Local and international NGOs, NFIs sector, as well, communities and beneficiaries where effective beneficiary and community participation is practically indispensable to render a project successful where beneficiaries were involved in information sharing, consultation, decision making, and initiating action in the program activities. To reach this level of coordination, the assessment teams conducted several meeting with the representatives of beneficiaries and targeted communities, then their views are taken into account and consequently is explicitly incorporated in their objectives, and approach and methodology, related to their urgent needs, plan of action and the response to their NFIs needs.

4 - Needs

The assessment team conducted field trips to targeted intervention locations in DZ countryside, which identified by HNO 2022 and paid visits to the residence of potential beneficiaries; poor, female headed families, PWD and families who lost their livelihoods, The assessment showed that there are 6,400 HHs/38,400 persons out of population, meet the criteria and in need to the following total summer items of NFIs:1. Solar Lamp: 6,400 ones (a lamp/HH). 2. Rechargeable Fan: 6,400 pieces (a fan/HH). These items to be distributed to intervention areas: - DZ city and its Eastern (Khasham, Hatla, Marat, Mathloum,Al Huseinieh and Al Tabieh) and western rural area (Al Bghelieh, Ayyash, Al Shmetieh, Al Msaraband Al Tebni) : 900 solar lamps and 900 rechargeable fans benefitting 900 HHs (5,400 people).- Abu Kamal town and its rural area (Jalaa, Sabkha, Tothiyeh, Qataa, Abbas and Salhiyeh): 2,600 solar lamps & 2,600 fans benefitting 2,600 HHs of 15,600 people. - Al Mayadin town and rural area (Sbekhan, Mehkan, Al Ashara): 2,900 solar lamps & 2,900 fans benefiting 2,900 HHs of 17,400.Needs assessment sources: assessment teams of the Association and secondary sources (SARC, DoSA, local municipalities).

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by a distribution committee composed for this purpose, which comprises association field staff, and representatives of qualified beneficiaries including women, local community and municipality, DoSAL and SARC in close coordination with HCR. The distribution operations will adhere to the action plan and timelines as of the PCA. The association approach to implement the project activities has been as the following: 1. Carrying out the required field assessments by qualified trained staff in close coordination with other partners including those who are to be responsible for the response especially logistics specialists, in order to identify the targeted beneficiaries and the needs which assessed against a knowledge of what is required. 2. Purchase the assistance items: When local purchase is available, the association logistics specialists conduct market assessment and produce reports on the availability of the items in the local markets, prices which kept secretly as a reference to avoid cheating by suppliers , specifications, quality and quantities including the entire logistical arrangements, then the association management drawing up open tender requests as of required specification, quantity, quality and with clear understanding on 4W logistical arrangements. Supplier, who offered the best prices combined with the required conditions, and provided that prices should not exceed the confidential prices, will be awarded the contract.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

BICA considers protection risks through assessments and secondary sources. To reduce protection risks, BICA applies its operational guidelines. (1) BICA recognizes that Population of Concern have the right to expect that those who provide assistance measure their interventions by the best standards and practices of Humanitarian action. (2) Protection of beneficiaries and do-no-harm principle will be considered when planning and assuming program decisions; (3) BICA follows humanitarian standards its responses and vulnerability criteria for selection of beneficiaries, appointing community committees, project implementation, considering that the project results a continuation of current programming, possible risks that might be encountered in project implementation have been identified based on context analysis, daily monitoring and observations. The analysis takes into consideration several stakeholders that might influence the context, access as well as the planning and implementation of activities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

BICA is committed to mainstreaming the protection considerations at all stages of the project cycle. The project is deigned to make the concerns and experiences of women, girls, boys and men an integral dimension of it. BICA field team will select community volunteers from both genders and rely on their feedback and inputs throughout the implementation period. Men and women will also be separately consulted with regards to the day-to-day logistical considerations, such as regarding the most appropriate locations for gender-segregated distributions points. As a result, women and men have an equal the right and opportunity to participate in decision-making. The risks of PSEA will be identified through publicly discuss and address ongoing violence where men and women will be given a chance to express their different concerns, experiences, needs and capabilities through gender-age including persons with disabilities and survivors of GBV as identified. The assessment phase will highlight different gender- age specific gaps, such as unequal access assistance and services for men and women, in addition to likely protection risks for women, boys and girls. And as prevention measures that: 1. Employees sign code of conduct related to sexual exploitation. 2. Training of employees on procedures related to sexual and material exploitation. 3. Posters that include the complaints numbers. 4. Assigning a specialized team to follow up and verify complaints in order to respond immediately.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risk: The security situation in targeted areas is unstable and restrictions on access to beneficiaries. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: BICA coordinates with authorities to maintain access to target area. Financial challenges risk: Substantive devaluation of USD against the Syrian pound. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, BICA will analyze timely the scope of the challenges, create proper solutions of options, and communicate it with HCR and NFIs sector. Fraud risk: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruption Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures updated and followed. Ethical risk: related to any kind of abuse (Sexual, Power, etc...) Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: provide trainings to staff member on organizational ethical policies. COVID 19 risk: Vulnerable households including persons with disabilities are mostly affected and in need of support. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Support the beneficiaries to implement the health protocol.
