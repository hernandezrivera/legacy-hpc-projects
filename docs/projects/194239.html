Project Summary

In 2022, it has been estimated that 8.9 million people (more than two thirds of South Sudan’s population) are in need in South Sudan among which 24% are women, 53% are children and 15% are people with disabilities. A trend that increased since 2021, with an added 600.000 people with humanitarian needs. South Sudanese people are suffering the direct consequences of years of conflict and the deteriorating humanitarian conditions, worsened by endemic violence, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene, as well as health services. The continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in a large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. South Sudan hosts nearly 338,000 refugees, with the majority from Sudan. More than half of these refugees or 53% are hosted in Upper Nile State while 38% are in Unity State and the rest spread out in Central and Western Equatoria, and Jonglei states. These displacements place an additional burden on host communities, as IDPs place additional pressure on limited host community food stocks, which can result in tensions.
Through this project INTERSOS plans to respond through an integrated CP-education project in 7 locations that is Ayod and Pibor in Jonglei State, Ezo and Yambio in Western Equatoria, Leer and Rubkona in Unity state and Malakal in Upper Nile State. These locations are selected based CP and Education cluster prioritization for HRP 2022 (CP: severity level 4 & 3 and Education: priority 1 & 2). INTERSOS is currently implementing projects in the selected locations therefore, INTERSOS is aware of the dire needs for the child protection and education services in these locations. The proposed activity aims to address the immediate needs of children, unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children , in need of child protection services and without conducive learning environment due to lack of learning spaces, wash facilities and learning materials.

1 - Needs

In 2022, it is estimated that 3.6 million people, of which 97% are children, and about 139.000 refugee children, need education services. Necessitated by measures to limit COVID-19 diffusion, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic challenges exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, an estimated 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children. According to the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) findings only 2.3% of headteachers surveyed reported that all children enrolled attend school almost every day. The top reason for inconsistent attendance reported by learners was families not being able to pay fees, direct (e.g. exam fees) or indirect (e.g. uniform costs), associated with attending school. Lack of teaching and learning supplies remain one of the key challenges whereby only 15% of headteachers reported that all children in the school had both a pen or pencil and their own notebook/exercise book while just 24% of headteachers reported almost all of their teachers have a full set of textbooks for the subjects they teach. Slightly over half (50.4%) of surveyed schools reported that their female learners have not received any dignity kits. The report also found out that 40.9% of surveyed schools classrooms were not accessible for Children with Disabilities (CwDs) while 59.5% reported the latrines were inaccessible for CwDs. A third of schools reported not having access to a functioning safe water source and while 19.3% of schools have no functional latrines at all. Furthermore, due to the massive floods and constant conflict, around 1.095 schools over the country suffered many damages. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities as well as the destruction or occupation of facilities during conflict were reported to limit access of children to schools. Major protection concerns reported include grave violations, such as killing/targeting of civilians, grave violations against children, GBV/CRSV, restriction of freedom of movement, forced recruitment, abductions, constraints on safe and equitable access to services, housing, land, and property. While the country is facing multiple shocks and risks have been in existence in various communities, new patterns are on the rise, such as risky population movements among IDPs, unaccompanied minors, and adolescents in search of work, food, education, and better opportunities. They are at risk of family separation, physical and sexual abuse, violence, and vulnerability to recruitment by armed groups, among others. These children are often exposed to hazardous or unpaid labor and detention and lack access to immediate and long-term child protection services, such as identification and prompt referral to appropriate services. It is necessary to strengthen integrated responses and services to address the diverse needs of children, joint analysis, needs assessment, and monitoring through collaboration with other sectors, capacity building, localization, and community engagement. The situation is particularly dire in the state of Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile and Western Equatoria. In many counties of these region, the situation has reached a catastrophic level due to the consequences of inter-communal fighting and floods that left a significant part of the area under water. This, consequently led to the separation of children from their primary caregivers/ parents, a series of inter-communal abductions and constant sexual assaults, mainly targeting girls. Additionally, the massive displacements led families to find shelter in public places such as schools and churches.

2 - Project activities

INTERSOS plans to implement activities of an integrated CP-education project in 7 locations that is Ayod and Pibor in Jonglei State, Ezo and Yambio in Western Equatoria, Leer and Rubkona in Unity state and Malakal in Upper Nile State. These locations are selected based on the needs and the prioritization of location for HRP 2022 by the clusters. 1. Proposed CP activities:1.1 Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services including through mobile teams;a) Provision of focused and non-focused PSS activities at CFS and schools to enhance psychosocial wellbeing of girls, boys, women and men through recreational activities, livelihood services, and parenting skills. INTERSOS team will use play as tool to address emotional distresses using physical (football, running), constructional (clay, shapes), role play (drama, debates), and instructional (ball games). b) Peer-to-peer sessions facilitated by adolescent girls and boys aimed at understanding and learning how to deal with emotions and feelings c) Conduct parenting sessions with caregivers and parents on parenting skills, PSS and positive discipline d) Provision of focused specialized PSS services through referrals to specialized MHPSS services.1.2 Strengthen Case Management approach including cash-based assistance for UASC, GBV survivors and CAAFAGa. Identification and registration, documentation, referrals, and family tracing and reunification services for UASCs.b. Identification of vulnerable children including orphans, children with disabilities, children suffering form GBV related concerns and child labor.c. Provision of materials assistance for the most vulnerable UASC and other vulnerable children to reduce their immediate protection risks. d. Provision of interim care for the unaccompanied children. Foster families will be identified and trained on foster care for unaccompanied children and children with no appropriate care, positive discipline, PFA and parenting skills. e. Conduct family tracing and reunification of UASC.f. Establishment of CP referral pathways in all locations of intervention1.3 Establishment of Community Based Child Protection Networks, capacity building and outreaches;1.4 Community engagement through outreach activities with adolescents and caregivers. The communities will be engaged through awareness raising sessions, and FGD;1.5 Capacity building of frontline staff and community volunteer;2. Education activities:This project will focus on increasing equitable access to safe and protective education services for all gender and for children living with disability. In order to achieve the project objectives, INTERSOS will carry out the following activities that are in line with the cluster’s objective as follow:1. Improve equitable access to safe and protective education service1.1 Enrollment of children in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools/ learning centers1.2 Construction/rehabilitation of TLS and classrooms to provide safe learning environments for children.1.3 Establish gender segregated latrines and water points1.4 Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soaps, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers. 2: Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers'.2.1 Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary and secondary school education2.2 Conduct training of teachers on EiE, pedagogical and child-centered methodologies 3. Strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community3.1 Train SMCSs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS), PFA and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/climate change3.2 IEC Materials3.3 Promote enrollment through “Back to Learning” campaigns.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IEC material will be provided in all schools informing learners, teachers and school staff about the feedback mechanisms established. The messaging will be tailored by age to ensure child friendliness. In each school a CRM committee will be established composed by school management staff, teachers, parents and child representatives since the age of 7. Additionally, complaint boxes will be established in each school supported. Finally, the help desk staff will be trained to receive complaints. On a monthly basis, each CRM will provide a matrix with all feedback receive through all the mechanisms.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use several channels to ensure proper communication with beneficiaries. First, the project will include a strong awareness and mobilization component where community mobilizers will oversee reaching all the community using information sessions and FGD to present the project, the selection criteria and the activities in general. Additionally, the project will work closely with local authorities to ensure they also promote the project within the community. To ensure beneficiary perception of the project is gathered on a regular basis, satisfaction quality survey questions will be included in the PDM exercises done after every distribution. INTERSOS will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place throughout the implementation period, aiming at safeguarding proper accountability and program quality, For complaint and feedback on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), a CRM committee in each location will be established by ensuring all groups of the society - and particularly children, women and people with disabilities - have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained about standard procedure for treating complaints and feedbacks. Additionally, complaint box will be established at every distribution point and training venue, and the CRM will also be in charge of collecting feedback provided by this mean.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Each CRM committee will have one representative of the most vulnerable groups of the community including among others PWD, elderly, youth, and women. The CRM committee mechanisms was set up to ensure most vulnerable members had access to the feedback and complain mechanisms after other feedback mechanisms, including complaint boxes showed their inadaptation to the context precisely by the important number of illiterate individuals. It is expected that through this inclusive mechanism feedback of the most vulnerable will be collected on a regular basis.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, program staff will held information sessions with community members, PTA, school management committee and community protection committees on the ground and add to a pre-established database all feedback received from community members and the beneficiaries will the measures and addressed. The database will be shared with INTERSOS coordination that will do a strict follow up of the measures taken during field visits. For the PSEA and CP complaints, complaint will be collected through different channel including complaint boxes and PSEA focal points and will be share with the PSEA referent at coordination level. Each complaint and concern will be assessed and investigate if needed and feedback will be provided to the person that raised the complaint.
