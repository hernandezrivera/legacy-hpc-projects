Project Summary

SKUS, as a women-headed organization, is committed to improving meaningful access to mental health & SRH services for all & comprehensive care to GBV survivors practicing the survivor center approach. The proposed intervention aims to ensure comprehensive gender mainstreaming of GBV through existing mental health, SRH, strong referral creating camp focal, changing attitudes about disability, and promoting respect for diversity in terms of guiding principles. Advocacy, awareness, and considering the GBV issues in all phases of our project are the GBV risk mitigation strategy.
SKUS proposes a mainstreaming approach to gender-based violence and sexual and reproductive health that will reach 16,925 people living in Camps 7, 18, & 24 in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, where we are planning to construct the total 03 service center. The project is adapted to meet the differing needs of women, men, adolescent girls, & boys, especially with PWD ensuring making all the facilities physically accessible & ensuring the human & sexual health rights, & providing a rights-based approach to health & social services for sexual & gender diverse population with respect & dignity. A total of 80% female(child, adolescent & adult), including PWD & GDP with GBV mitigation services, resilience-building & empowerment programming, which will include Case Management, Structured Psychosocial support services (PSS), Individual health as safe motherhood (ANC, PNC), Prevention & response to STI/STD, RTI, CMR, FP, Adolescent & Adult RH awareness, Skill-building of the women and girls, capacity strengthening of the community volunteer, service providers, and stakeholder, etc. that will be conducted by SKUS specialized team of a psychologist, case managers, community mobilizer with clinical supervision & case review meeting. For the other technical advanced support, SKUS will collaborate with the GBVSS & existing organizations working on the same modality in the camp.
Additionally, through community mobilization, a total of 20% of women, men, boys, & girls, including PWD, will be reached with proven strategies like building links with organizations of persons with disabilities & other national and local actors to address harmful social norms that enhance GBV among the population & uncertain disasters or pandemics like COVID-19 & contingency plan for DRR regarding with PERU task force & aligned with PERU Team leader in a different camp. SKUS will mainstream GBV interventions in EPRP through specific capacity building for others partners & the community, maintain stock of dignity kits, disseminate COVID-19 safety messages, & address the usage of dignity kits with management from an environmental aspect. SKUS will share COVID safety materials & other distributions to both communities to enhance social cohesiveness & add local staff members to the team. Prevention & mitigation strategies, mechanisms, & initiatives to support community-based interventions aligned with IASC principles and guidelines will be strengthened through other WG & Sub-sector support. To ensure the safeguarding of beneficiaries, SKUS will continue to participate regularly & pro-actively in the GBVSS & related task teams, especially GBV Case Management & GBVIMS Task Team regarding any desire to establish GBV Case Management services & additionally collaborate with the PSEA network to reinforce Sexual Exploitation & Abuse reporting & response mechanisms.
SKUS will reinforce AAP principles & beneficiary feedback on services provided by a decentralized visual community-based complaint mechanism in all the service points, register complaints, suggestions and encourage SEA reporting at the community level. In addition, SKUS will have a resident legal officer who will provide legal counseling to GBV survivors. SKUS has an excellent communication and advocacy team closely connected with local stakeholders and the community to implement their project smoothly.

1 - Identified Needs

GBV is a global problem that transcends cultural, geographical, religious, social, & economic boundaries. It is a human rights violation that jeopardizes the victims' health, safety, & dignity. GBV is a threat to women & girls in the Rohingya community (RC) & nearby host communities (HC) in Cox's Bazar. Community & evidence-based psychosocial assistance are crucial in coping with GBV occurrences among Rohingya refugees & host communities to promote & enhance mental health. The SRH rights are the same for everyone, including PWDs & GDPs. Basic relaxation techniques were recognized as prevalent requirements across target populations, as were reinforcement of positive coping mechanisms such as prayers, spending time with trustworthy people, & engaging in constructive activities. According to SKUS Women Survey November 2020 report, 56% of RC women & 54% of HC endure violence regarding they don’t have any other choice & they have a fear of losing their children. 85% of HC women & 90% of RC women said they needed a husband or son or another male kinsman to protect them. 74% of women from both communities have heard but don't fully understand that sexual harassment can be more than just physical touch & rape. 43% of HC women & 52% of RC women said that if they know that their husband has a disease they can get during sexual intercourse, they can’t refuse to have sex or ask their husband to use a condom. Domestic Violence is still a significant issue in both communities, being one of the primary reasons for divorce. Three primary reasons why divorce happens within the RC were identified as Domestic Violence by the spouse (72% of RC women), Domestic violence by in-laws (59% of RC women), & Dowry (54% or RC women). Where for the HC, it was the Death of the husband (43% of HC women), Domestic Violence by the spouse (22% of HC women), & Domestic Violence by in-laws (18% of HC women) (SKUS Women Survey-2020). Still, 40% of RC women plan on giving birth in their own house/ house of relatives, compared to only 30% of the HC women choosing to do the same. Only 2% RC & 5% HC identify condoms as a family planning device. UNHCR estimates that 8,96,879 Rohingya refugee women, men, girls, & boys are housed in Cox's Bazar District as of September 2021, with 51.52 % & 51.8% of children (UNHCR fact sheet, Sep' 21). Intimate partner violence (IPV) has been recognized as the most dominating type of sexual & gender-based violence recorded in Cox's Bazar, according to the inter-agency Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBV IMS) quarterly reports. Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) continues to account for a significant portion of all reported instances. In Q2 2021, the average number of recorded IPV occurrences was (84%). According to a data contextualization session performed in July 2021, IPV may still be underreported since some survivors may not be aware that IPV counts as GBV. According to FGDs, KIIs, & other secondary sources, child sexual abuse (1.1 %) is underreported. GBV events are reported by just 3.1 % males & 3.8 % children.Update on JRP Implementation in 2021, In August & September, only 12,875 Rohingya & Bangladeshi girls & boys received age- & gender-sensitive organized & sustained mental health & psychosocial support services. The difference between now & 2021 is 88.8%. According to the aim, just 23% of women & girls had access to MHM supplies at the end of September. Individuals (representing service providers & authorities) were not trained on protection in 70.6 %, regardless of gender. Only 9,601 people in the camps & the targeted HC received structured PSS services in August & September. Where the gap from then to 2021 is still 41%. In August & September, 37% of reported incidents of sexual violence were sent to a doctor & got treatment within 72 hours (monthly indicator where the still gap of 63%). The needs of this project plan were determined with the aid of the discussion, as mentioned earlier.
