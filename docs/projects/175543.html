Project Summary

This 12-month project plans to complement 2021 FSLC objectives; CO1: Food assistance, CO2: Emergency food production; and CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs in Ayod, Nyirol, Uror, Duk, Fangak, and Renk (IPC 3 & 4) target Counties, in a way that prevents COVID-19 spread. The project is designed to support 7,503 vulnerable HHs (45,018 individuals) composed of IDPs, Host communities and Returnees, PWDs, and minorities through life-saving food assistance, own food production, livelihood support, and livestock health services. 13,955 men, 22,058 women, 4,052 boys and 4,953 girls are targeted.
Implementation for food assistance (CO1) will be through general food distribution in Ayod and Duk counties targeting 1906 and 2185 households respectively.
The implementation of CO2 to enhance food production will be through in-kind distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits to 11,124 households (Ayod 2,000, Nyirol 1,553, Uror 1,761, Duk 2,300, Fangak 2,019 and Renk 1,492), livestock support; treatment and vaccination campaigns to 5,561 HHs in Ayod (1,612), Uror(2,100) and Nyirol (1,850) counties targeting a total of 163,000 livestock heads through 105 trained Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (35 per county), and livestock restocking program in Ayod, Nyirol, and Fangak for 500 households in each of the three counties.
In light of resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs aids (CO3), this will be realized through training of farmers (750), fisher folks(360), 20 VSLA groups (20 member each), and through local procurement of seeds from through seed fairs. The trainings will cover good and climate-smart agronomic practices, countering post harvest losses, sustainable income generation activities, value addition with special emphasis on minimising spread of COVID-19. Seed Fairs (SFs) for Crop Seed procurement will be done from 1,500 seed producer groups/traders in Renk county where seeds will be procured locally from these farmers and distributed to the community farming purposes.
Beneficiary targeting will be through the AAP committee with mainstreaming of gender, persons with disabilities, minority and marginalised groups. This project will benefit the most vulnerable; Child-headed, Elderly and Female-headed households under IPC 3,4, and 5 suffering from food insecurities and malnutrition. Protection will be mainstreamed in order to reduce risks and exposure to Gender-Based Violence and ensure the Do No Harm principle is followed during beneficiary targeting and provision of support. There will always be timely, routine and well-established feedback mechanism channels from the beneficiaries throughout the project cycle.
Sustainability plan of the project will be achieved through community engagement in the design and implementation of the project in collaboration with County Agricultural Department, ROSS office, and other local authorities. Awareness and risk mitigation in view of COVID-19 will be raised through training and engaging community members on specific techniques for contextualized approaches. Project staff will support assessments initiated by the FSL cluster and Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries and will monitor crop disease, pest outbreaks, Fall Armyworm (FAW), and desert locust and make referrals to FSL cluster and MAF. Complementary programming will be advanced in the counties, working closely with nutrition and health partners. The project will receive pipeline supplies from FAO whereas the requested funding will be used to provide front-line services.

1 - Needs

October 2020 – July 2021 IPC findings estimate combined populations of 140,983 people with Emergency food needs and facing Crisis (IPC phases 4 and 3) in Ayod, Nyirol, Uror, Duk and Fangak Counties (Jonglei state), and Renk (Upper Nile State) respectively; one of the worst in South Sudan. High SAM and GAM rates in the counties have been attributed to deteriorating food security, high morbidity, and extremely poor diets along with underlying issues of suboptimal infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM), and PLW’s cases. In Jonglei, the food security and nutrition monitoring system (FSNMS R25) assessment reported a GAM rate of 29.4%, MAM rate of 22.3%, and SAM of 7.1% for children under 5 years while pregnant and lactating women rate of malnutrition was 39.9%, all above WHO emergency thresholds. Ayod, for instance, has GAM rate of 16.2%; SAM rate of 3.6%, MAM rate 12.6%, and Prevalence of malnutrition PLW is 34.2%. Duk County was assessed to have severe levels of malnutrition. (FSNMS R25). The situation has worsened in the era of COVID 19 pandemic and floods thereby pushing more people to IPC 3+ than the previous years (HNO 2021). CFSAM and FAO/WFP (2020) noted significant decrease of most trade routes activities and safety. Livelihoods disruptions have been recurrently driven by protracted conflict and displacement, missed planting seasons, lack of quality seeds and inputs, destruction and coverage of agricultural field by flood water, livestock and crop diseases, lack of veterinary and agricultural extension services, post harvest losses and lack of markets to sell their produce. There has been a public health concern on zoonoses particularly on Rift Valley fever due to the floods. Settlements within the target counties are using extreme negative coping strategies such as child marriages, child labour, intensification of cattle rustling and conflicts, reduction of meals taken per day, only children eating with devastating effects on pregnant and lactating women and children under the age of five years. Jonglei State (84 percent) has the largest proportion of female-headed households, with just 16 percent of households headed by males. Female-headed households also have a higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male-headed households), a possible indication of gender-based vulnerabilities. Significant decrease in the number of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor passed from 20% to 30% to merely 6% to 8% in Jonglei, and the Upper Nile States. 99.6 percent of female-headed households were involved in decision-making about food and other resources in the household. If the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the start of 2021, and in the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of emergency as large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity. Through this project, CMD proposes to protect and safeguard lives in crisis phases by carrying out general food distribution in the proposed target areas where food stores and reserves have been depleted due to natural disasters and conflicts. The provision of crop, vegetable and fishing kits will not only promote food production but also diversify the food varieties and nutrients within the diet. Provision of livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns will help minimize the decimation of livestock due to disease outbreaks intensified by floods and inaccessible veterinary services thereby countering erosion of their livelihood sources. Provision of livestock through restocking program will help in the recovery phase of communities who have lost their livestock. In order to build the resilient capacities of the target population, there will be numerous trainings with regard to farming, fishing, VSLAs and seed fairs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will Include GBV mitigation as part of staff training & conduct. The project will Identify and minimize the risk of harm to children and adults from staff.In order to provide beneficiaries with information about PSEA, all training will incorporate elements of PSEA, indicating the engagement levels of the project staffs with the community, reporting avenues available, their rights to benefit without being compromised and more. The beneficiaries will be reminded that CMD staff interactions with children or with adult beneficiaries, or with personal data about such persons, will comply with the attached CMD’s Safeguarding Behaviour Protocols.- Any incidents of harm or risk of harm to children or to adult beneficiaries will be reported immediately through channels that will be explained and demonstrated to the community.- Beneficiaries will be reminded of all issues related to PSEA including reported cases being treated with utmost confidentiality; investigated and action taken.All actions taken will be discussed with the complainant after a specified time (feedback).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In order to enable vulnerable underrepresented groups are served and feedback collected for action, the project will set regular community meetings to share information and solicit feedback from minority beneficiaries about the support they receive and how it can be customized to fit into their specific needs, to generate useful information for quick decision-making). The Project will ensure feedback from the minority groups, through: - Surveys and FGDs with specific age groups, women, men, and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In collaboration with the AAP committee, the project will use monthly focus group discussions and community meetings and participatory activities to share information and solicit feedback from beneficiaries about the support they receive, to generate useful information for quick decision-making. The Project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, through: - Surveys and FGDs with specific age groups, women, men, and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis. Quarterly focus group discussions will be conducted with children and communities to assess protection knowledge, attitudes, and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with the participatory assessment. Furthermore, CMD plans to establish feedback/complaints desks to collect daily complaints/feedbacks from the target beneficiary/community. Every group ranging from gender, majority and minority groups, persons with disability, youth to leaders will have a particular feedback/complaint desk/contact person to report to. The information will be routinely collected, analysed and feedback given in timely manner.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All feedback regarding project-related matters will provide self-reflection and providing actionable plans to ensure the project is adjusted to suit recommendations provided during consultation sessions. Feedback obtained from project activities will be consolidated and analyzed at the state and Juba level offices and feedback into the program unit used to realign activities to planned standard.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Throughout the project, CMD will establish complaints/feedback help desks/help persons disaggregated by gender, people with specific needs, debilitated, old and marginalised groups to gather daily and routine complaints. If the concerns can be addressed on the ground level, the feedbacks will be given in realtime, if it requires some bureaucratic procedures, the referral persons/powers will be engaged confidentially to attain amicable solutions. Moreover, there will be constant awareness campaigns to the public on PSEA and also capacity building capacitybuilding to enable different actors (e.g help desks/contact people and referral persons) to understand their roles and meet their responsibilities with regard to protection. CMD staffs involved in the project will also undergo several PSEA refresher courses to spearhead in the PSEA and other protection related issues.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The project is designed to support vulnerable HHs through life-saving food assistance, own food production, livelihood support, and livestock health services. Implementation will follow a combination of Seed Fairs (SFs) for Crop Seed and Tools, and distribution of Emergency Kits (Crop, Vegetable, and Fishing). Targeted households will receive seeds (direct distribution and seed fairs), vegetable seed kit, and crop kit while HHs in Renk County will benefit through seed fairs using vouchers to procure 10kg to 13kg of seeds with traders redeeming them for cash. Increased production of food at the household level and increased knowledge on post-production will help the communities be more food secure while selling or exchanging as a means to ensure diversification.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Protection will be mainstreamed in order to reduce risks and exposure to Gender-Based Violence and ensure the Do No Harm principle is followed during beneficiary targeting and provision of support.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4
