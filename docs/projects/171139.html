Project Summary

The ongoing security situation in Palestine remains volatile and unpredictable. In Gaza, new rounds of military escalations and exchanges of fire result in the increasing presence of remnant explosive hazards (ERW). The ongoing escalations continue to leave behind thousands of items of ERW. These residual outbreaks of armed clashes further increase ERW contamination in Gaza and are becoming more frequent and exacerbate a security environment already on the brink of another full-scale conflict.
UNMAS aligns with its Country Programme Strategy in contributing to two outcomes: 1. The UN, and its humanitarian partners in Gaza, maintain critical functions, uninhibited by explosive hazards. UNMAS is the only provider of specialized explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) capacity that is permitted to operate in Gaza by the Gazan and Israeli authorities. UNMAS continued presence has been requested by the Palestinian and Israeli authorities, the UN Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator, OCHA, UNRWA and UNDP. UNMAS continues to provide ERW mitigation measures to UN and humanitarian partner sites to ensure that vulnerable communities are protected and prepared against the threat of explosive hazards from past, present and future escalations in conflict.
UNMAS Palestine maintains an emergency EOD presence in Gaza to remove and destroy ERW posing a threat to Gaza communities. This allows UNMAS to be ready for a rapid response during potential conflict and to maintain an Emergency Response Preparedness Plan (ERPP) to ensure access for the Humanitarian response in any future Gaza conflict.
Outcome 2: Conflict affected people know how to mitigate the risk of explosive ordnance. An individual’s risk of exposure varies on factors that include where they live and work and the information they have at hand. ERW risk awareness is critical to mitigating the threat posed to civilians. UNMAS Palestine has identified two types of educational activities that are required in the specific context of Gaza. After several years of community engagement with beneficiary stakeholders and local partners they have developed targeted curricula which consider the needs of different gender and age groups as well as persons with disabilities: (i) risk education provides information for civilians to protect themselves from existing ERW threats, (ii) Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) is a novel form of risk education providing resilience training to prepare for future escalations, and is critical to avoid civilian casualties. To address COVID-19 restrictions, remote delivery methodologies have been developed as well as dual messaging addressing ERW and pandemic mitigation. Informal street session with social distancing measures have also been utilised.
In the West Bank, UNMAS works with the National Mine Action Authority, Palestinian Mine Action Centre (PMAC) to enhance their capacity to provide risk education to vulnerable communities in the West Bank and to address the needs of victims of ERW accidents. There are 16 minefields and over 46 identified areas with recorded ERW contamination that pose a
risk to shepherds, farmers, women and children. Lack of resources has impacted PMAC ability to coordinate EORE in the West Bank and provide age, gender and disability appropriate risk education where needed. Victim Assistance (VA), is the pillar of mine action that meets the short, medium and long-term needs of women, girls, boys and men who have been injured by explosive ordnance and addresses affected families and communities. UNMAS works to support PMAC in developing their capacity in addressing this requirement. UNMAS has consulted, via a number of workshops, in 2019, to develop a project that will assist PMAC to develop and implement an EORE strategy in the West Bank, that is inclusive of the varied needs of women, men and persons with disabilities; and training of training to women volunteers for EORE, with a special focus on children in the West Bank.

1 - Needs

Existing ERW contamination poses a threat to communities in both Gaza and the West Bank. The current level of ERW contamination is impossible to determine; however, on average since 2017, there are still approximately 100 items of ERW cleared every month in Gaza and ERW accidents continue to occur. The most recent escalation in 2020 resulted in contamination of a school in Gaza by an unexploded missile. By mid 2020, the recorded number of ERW accidents in Gaza was 63, with over 180 casualties. A trend of decrease and then increase in accidents following military escalations can be seen, indicating an ongoing ERW threat to the local population. Almost 80% of the casualties have been men and boys, especially as young boys regularly play with suspicious objects posing a risk. 150 known and accessible Deep Buried Bombs (DBB) were cleared by UNMAS, the latest being in 2019 which posed a threat to the local population as it was under a main road. There remain DBB cases that still pose a threat to the safety of communities in Gaza and will need to be cleared. While the exact number of cases will only be known as and when bombs are discovered, it is estimated that there are many items of uncleared unexploded ordnance, clearance of which would support vulnerable communities to become more resilient to the impact of ERW’s. UNMAS provides an emergency EOD response to allow the United Nations and its humanitarian partners in Gaza to maintain critical functions, uninhibited by explosive hazards. An EOD response involves UNMAS clearing UN premises that have suspected or confirmed ERW contamination after military engagements. UNMAS manages sites while coordinating with the EOD police to remove and dispose of any explosive items to enable the site to re-open and continue implementing humanitarian activities. An individual’s risk of exposure varies on factors like where they live, work and the existing knowledge on how to protect and prepare themselves against ERW threat. UN/humanitarian staff, construction workers, and other at-risk populations including children, persons with disabilities, boys and men are particularly vulnerable. All require targeted knowledge to safely respond to ERW contamination. Community engagement with female groups and local CSOs have allowed women to be particularly targeted in CPP sessions as they have been identified as, requiring skills to prepare and protect themselves and their families against future ERW contamination and conflict. There is concern amongst mine action partners of an increase in desensitization to the risks of ERW amongst the Gaza population leading to accidents. There exists a gap in the capacity of the Gazan authorities to respond appropriately to ERW contamination. UNMAS provides different types of EORE to mitigate the risk of explosive ordnance, including: coordinated community sessions, training for professionals including UN and humanitarian staff, street sessions for beneficiaries not otherwise reached, especially children and reconstruction sessions for construction workers on sites and are provided on request. UNMAS also delivers Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) resilience training to empower females to understand ERW risks, how to prepare homes for bombardment or evacuate persons with disabilities safely. Since 2019, UNMAS reached 19,141 persons via 1,148 EORE sessions (5,198 children) and a further 264 CPP sessions have been delivered to 6,694 beneficiaries (11,832 women). Since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, UNMAS has suspended normal EORE activities and started specialised street sessions covering ERW and COVID-19 safety messaging. To date, 10,149 beneficiaries (4,053 children) have been reached via 3,596 mixed sessions. Remote methods of delivery are being developed to allow continuation of delivery in case of future movement restrictions. In the West Bank, the PMAC has mapped 46 areas that have recorded ERW contamination. These areas are either military training sites, which occupy 20% of the West Bank, or hotspot areas that see demonstrations on a regular basis. Community engagement through EORE sessions have identified that ERW contamination poses risk to farmers and shepherds while performing their livelihood activities and impedes their access to the land. It also impacts the lives of the Bedouin communities who often live in these areas, including women who are harmed while handling ERW to safeguard their children and community. To assess the extent of the impact of ERW in the West Bank, UNMAS is conducting a household survey of West Bank communities to enable PMAC to prioritize their EORE activities. Due to ERW contamination, the PMAC are providing vital EORE to the communities in villages and residential areas around the minefields and areas highly contaminated by ERW. Children are especially at risk in the communities, as ERW contamination exists around the schools in the areas. UNMAS is working with UNICEF, UNRWA, and CSOs working in child protection to develop CPP modules to address the impact of the use of chemical irritants and other so-called riot control agents, in the vicinity of schools. There exists a lack of resources limiting the capacity for the PMAC to deliver age and gender appropriate EORE in the most-needed areas. Gender balanced teams do not exist and some of the most vulnerable communities are not reached. UNMAS is addressing this imbalance as part of the consultation process with national authorities, UN agencies, and CSOs in the development of the EORE strategy for Palestine. UNMAS is also working with UNICEF, UNRWA, and CSOs working in child protection to develop CPP modules to address the impact of the use of chemical irritants and other riot control agents, in the vicinity of schools.

2 - Activities

To implement its project and contribute to the two outcomes, UNMAS will undertake the following activities taking into account the COVID-19 restrictions in the West Bank and Gaza. UNMAS has tailored much of its risk education messaging to include integrated EORE/COVID awareness in coordination with UNICEF and WHO to ensure the messaging is appropriate, relevant and up to date. All messaging is being converted into remote alternatives to ensure that remote learning, when needed, is available, accessible to all and social distancing guidelines can be followed by UNMAS staff and by community based organization partners. After 2 years of feedback and surveys from the risk messaging and CPP sessions, the modules are now customised to the requirements of the participants, for example the information can be adapted to suit the varying needs of women, men and persons with disabilities. UNICEF is a member of the Mine Action Working Group for Gaza which enables UNMAS to consult with other mine action partners to incorporate child protection principles. UNMAS has specific risk education messaging tailored to children that uses comic book characters, risk educations games, role playing, music and dance as well and positive reinforcement in a simpler and child friendly manner that have been developed by risk education experts globally. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback surveys are administered by mixed-gender teams to reflect the participant pool and ensure our beneficiaries do not feel pressured when answering questions or voicing their opinions and the questions used are drafted to be gender neutral. The UNMAS EORE Advisor regularly attends the sessions to ensure a consistency in quality is maintained and encourage beneficiaries to share any additional feedback. The information gathered is collated for internal reporting, for example when reporting to donors and analysed to identify areas for improvement in our services to ensure our activities remain relevant and beneficial, including informing ways in which we can make our future interventions more inclusive, gender sensitive and appropriate for children. For any complaints, targeted populations are provided with an UNMAS contact number that they can call anonymously, to ensure they feel at ease and are able to freely participate in the feedback process safely regardless of their circumstances such as persons with any disabilities or COVID-19 related restrictions. All logged complaints are dealt with confidentially by a trained focal point within UNMAS. In regards to PSEAH, UNMAS follows the guidelines set by UNOPS, which involves all personnel attending PSEAH training and adhering to a code of conduct, and is also an active member of the oPt PSEAH network. Our EORE implementing partners will be provided ethics, gender and integrity training to ensure all personnel engage with beneficiaires appropriately. UNMAS uses the PSEAH complaints mechanism set up by the oPt PSEAH network and a confidential hotline managed by an independent service provider on behalf of UNOPS and accessible free worldwide, alongside the UNMAS complaints number, is also an available port for PSEAH complaints. The complaints logged with UNOPS are handled by the Internal Audit and Investigations Group.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

One of the aims of the project is to deliver the service of ERW clearance in Gaza which is essential in restoring normality to Gaza, a place that is under constant threat of war. UNMAS ensures through pre- and post survey on clearance and risk education is carried out to effectively take the principle of DNH into consideration in relation to beneficiaries by improving accountability towards beneficiaries, building relations with beneficiaries, and understanding the contexts and community dynamics in which the projects are taking take place both in Gaza and in the West Bank. UNMAS consults and involves communities from the identification phase of a project through surveys, community consultations and workshops with staff, partners and other relevant actors. The project design has considered the safety and the dignity of beneficiaries as a first priority, given that the target group is very broad and covers men, women, boys and girls directly affected by ERW contamination. All staff involved in the project, including partner organizations’ staff, are or will be trained on protection and gender mainstreaming as well as on PSEAH to promote best practices and to ensure that no harm results from UNMAS intervention. UNMAS interventions have also all been planned in a gender sensitive manner, and collection of disaggregated data collection according to the protection principles is maintained. Child protection principles have been integrated in project planning, and UNMAS ensures that the different needs of children are being met in targeted risk education sessions. In terms of COVID-related prevention and no harm: UNMAS ensures that its offices are safe for the staff members to return to, and adhere to all minimum precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and good hygiene measures. All staff members are provided with weekly updates on the best practices for prevention of infection and on the changing restrictions. All UNMAS activities have been adapted and implemented during the COVID-19 restrictions. Remote messaging to beneficiaries will be activated as soon as face to face is no longer advisable, shorter and more informal street sessions have been devised and regular need assessments will be conducted to identify any new emerging need.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through its activities UNMAS works to ensure that equal access is available to all those in need. In its focus on the technical side of ERW clearance, and the provision of risk education and CPP, UNMAS creates an atmosphere of safety that enables people to move around and rebuild their lives. UNMAS risk education is tailored to different groups, taking into consideration the needs of the groups. For example, CPP sessions are provided at times that stay at home mothers are available, and delivered in locations where child care can be made available. Sessions tailored to children ensure that children who have other child care duties can attend with their siblings, and are directly after school or a time that is convenient. UNMAS also ensures that access for PWD is made available through its CBO at women and family centres. Throughout the project cycle, persons with disabilities will be mainstreamed in line with the IASC guidelines. UNMAS and its partners will ensure that persons with disabilities are able to access the UNMAS interventions on the same terms as other members of the population. During the preparation phase of the activities, the accessibility of persons with disabilities to the activities will be assessed and adjusted to promote the participation of persons with disabilities. UNMAS intervention indirectly touches on human rights in Gaza by enabling people to go about their normal lives, go to work and schools without the threat of explosions. In addition, age and gender disaggregation is systematically included in all reporting and data capture. UNMAS will continue monitoring data related to victims of ERW and other explosive hazards, disaggregated by gender/age/location, to ensure early identification of potential/actual new trends in terms of at risk groups and victim profiles, and timely gender sensitive recommendations for preventive action. UNMAS will also continue to work with partners to engage men and boys particularly, who in Gaza are most at risk of being injured/killed by ERW. At the same time, UNMAS will ensure that women continue to be identified and encouraged to actively attend CPP sessions and ERW briefings in order to further reduce the low number of female casualties caused by ERW in Gaza and to better relay awareness messages amongst families.
