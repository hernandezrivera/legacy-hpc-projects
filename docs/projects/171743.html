Project Summary

The continuation of protracted crises in Somalia, now compounded by the effects of Covid-19, Floods and droughts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene. Approximately, 4.6 million Somalis are still in need due to insufficient access to portable water and inadequate sanitation services of the affected population in many districts, thus negatively affecting the country’s public health and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities. This spike in numbers compared to 2020 is mainly exacerbated by the situation developed as a result of Covid-19 and its rapid transmission. Lack of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD), leading to malnutrition in the country and contributing to increasing the existing stunting rate of 25.3% in children below 5 years of age. It is also a cause of relapse (ADD) when treated patients and children returning to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited, resulting in limited sustainability of health programs. Lack of access to water and adequate sanitation in schools is also a factor contributing to poor school attendance. In historically drought-affected districts, lack of access to water for humans and for livestock remain one of the leading causes of displacement, conflict and diseases. According to JMCNA, 53% of the IDPs face challenges in terms of access to WASH services in Somalia (53% as highest pertaining to access to soap and handwashing facilities.
According to JMCNA, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by the prevalent poor WASH conditions in Somalia. The methodology used for the PIN calculationestimated that 4.6 million people are in catastrophic (10%) severe (9%) and extreme (18%) need of humanitarian WASH assistance. The 10% put under the catastrophic severity is mainly due to lack of access to water both in terms of quality and accompanies by lack of access to functional sanitation. In comparison to the last year’s revision of HNO after Covid-19 situation, the overall WASH needs have not reduced. This is mainly due to the availability of limited resources to respond to the needs exacerbated by the Pandemic and the ongoing complex humanitarian crises in the country. Following this year’s trend, it is anticipated that over 1 million people could be in acute life-saving needs along the Shabelle and Juba river basins in case of above normal rainfall performance during the Gu’ and Deyr 2021 rainy seasons. The population in need of adequate WASH services in 2021 includes approximately 1.16 million IDPs 3.36 million host communities, 53,906 Returnees and 28,002 Refugees. Among the people in need, 2.9M are children, 0.7M are women and 0.7M are men. Among those groups, the cluster also recognises that 15% are persons with disabilities and 4% are elderly persons who will require specific attention. Persons with disabilities face additional challenges and barriers. Barriers include environmental barriers, which inhibitive access to WASH facilities, as well as social barriers, leading to discrimination within communities and further aggravating the risk of exclusion to WASH services. Physical barriers are often due to the design and distance to water and sanitation facilities, which makes it difficult for persons with disabilities to have equal access to WASH services. The lack of consultation and engagement with persons with disabilities in WASH designs, limits the opportunity for an inclusive response and contributes further to the marginalization of this vulnerable group.
In an effort to respond needs of WASH in Hiiraan,Banadir and Gedo Region, Direct Aid will implement WASH activities and DA will reached with continued, equitable, safe, sufficient and appropriate basic water supply in targeting both IDPs and non-IDP locations for potable and domestic use of water at household level in Gedo and Hiiran districts of Beledweyne and Baardheere .

Project Activities

Activity 1: Construction/Rehabilitation and protection of 10 wells, in order improve access for 2500 women, 1600 men, 3700 girls and 1200 boys. To improve the quality of water,DA will chlorinate and monitor residual chlorine levels (0.2mg/l -0.5 mg/l) of water at the rehabilitated water points. DA will also provide emergency household water treatment chemicals/or devices (including bucket chlorination). • Activity 2: Construction/Rehabilitation of 10 water Kiosks in 6 IDP camps in Tuula Aamiin, Camp Ajuuraan,Camp Alla-Aamiin and Bacaadweyn IDP camps in Belethawa and 6 in Mogadishu 3 Dayniile and 3 Kahda districts to improve access to safe water. Where absent, DA is proposing installation of running water at 6 target health facilities and 6 schools by connecting the nearest strategic borehole. This activity will target 6000 beneﬁcairies including boys,girls,men and women • Activity 3: Improve environmental sanitation through construction of 10 solid waste disposal sites in selected IDP settlements in Beledwyne,Mogadishu and Belethawa and other environmental cleaning and management activities including drainage and/or removal of stagnant black/grey water ponds in Mogadishu. • Activity 4: Improve sanitation coverage through construction of 100 latrines in IDPs (30 Belethawa,20 Beledweyne and 50 Mogaishu) gender-segregated communal latrines. The latrines will be designed to allow future desludging access, ready accessibility and hand washing facilities. DA will consult all anticipated users, particularly vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, the disabled and elderly), on siting, design, & appropriateness of the latrines to ensure the facilities allow for rapid, private, safe (well-lit and lockable) and secure access for all users. • Activity 5: Rehabilitation of 60 sex disaggregated latrines in 10 schools (20 Belethawa and 40 Mogadishu as per SPHERE and WASH cluster standards. • Activity 6: Establishment, training and ongoing supportive supervision of Solid Waste Management Volunteers (50 male & 10 females) 15 for each IDP camp including host community members. (Targeted IDP camps, are Tuula-Aamiin,Camp ajuran,Camp Alla Aamin and Bacaadweyn IDP CHPs, WASH committees and waste management volunteers will coordinate with local authorities to lead community members in clean-up campaigns aimed at addressing any accumulated solid waste or stagnant water that serve as a breeding ground for disease vectors. • Activity 7: Establishment, training and ongoing supportive supervision of 20 gender balanced WASH committees (10 male & 10 female • Activity 8: Conduct hygiene promotion for 9,000 beneﬁciaries through house to house visits (average 8 -10 households per week/per CHP), IEC materials, FM radio messaging and during participatory community outreach activities, • Activity 9: Provision of health/education to improve WASH in institutions, including DA operated cholera treatment centers, nutrition facilities and primary health care facilities in Banadir and Gedo Region. • Activity 10: Distribution of 1500 Hygiene kits with accompanying messaging on.DA will regularly assess needs for hygiene supplies (aqua tabs, 20L water storage containers, soap, & menstrual hygiene items) among the target population; and address these through distribution of items that will help ensure men, women, and children have the materials to act on the hygiene education.Activity 11: Conduct emergency water trucking with temporary distribution systems for 2 months in Belethawa IDPs with beneficiaries 3200 from 533 HHs
