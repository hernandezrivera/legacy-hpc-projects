Project Summary

This project aim to ensure access to education and psycho social support, and reduce protection vulnerabilities by providing emergency education opportunities for crisis affected boys and girls to return to normalcy with minimal disruption to learning.

Needs

The presence of explosive remnants of war and conflict related debris poses an additional hazard for children on their way to and from school and in the school environment, Over 400,000 children in the area along the contact line are in constant danger of injury and death, with landmines, ERW and UXOS being the leading cause of child casualties in 2017. In 2018 the tendency remains unchanged 6 February, 2018, three children (3, 9 and 14 y.o.) were injured after an unknown explosive device they found on the street detonated in their hands in Krasnohorivka (Donetsk, GCA); late September 2018 as a result of mine explosion 3 children (two boys 13 y.o. and one boy 12 y.o.) died, and 1 boy (10 y.o.) is hardly injured near Horlivka Donetsk NGCA.) The presence of military installations near some schools is a concern. In several settlements, children are in danger while attending school because the military installations are potential targets. According to Education Cluster, Needs Survey in Government Controlled Areas of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts 66% or a total of 606 education facilities reported that they have conflict related or non-conflict related safety and security concerns in the vicinity of the education facility area/site or on the children's commute to school According to the Education Cluster attacks on schools continued on a regular basis in 2017 and 2018. Since the beginning of 2017, at least 55 educational facilities (as of August 2018) were damaged due to continuous hostilities, affecting the education and wellbeing of thousands of children and teachers. Access to qulity education was heavily impacted with the consecquences of more than 4 years of conflict. 113education facilities need conflict-related rehabilitation out of which 77 education facilities are located within 20km of the contact line. Furthermore, based on the results of Education Cluster, Needs Survey in 2018 84% or a total of 771education facilities reported that they need rehabilitation for some ofthe infrastructural components. There is also significant issue with access to preschool education: as an illustration, in 2018 more than 2,000 children reportedly are on the waiting list for kindergartens in Luhanska oblast NGCA and some kindergartens in both oblasts are significantly overcrowded. Children, learners, teachers and staff struggle with the lasting trauma, fear and stress of living through years of armed conflict, and for those closest to the ‘contact line’, the impact continues to accrue. Most schools (54 per cent) in September 2018 reported that they observe impact of the conflict on their student’s ability to learn and/or well-being. Many schools do not have a full time psychologist, and teachers themselves are also in need of psychosocial support and guidance on self-care. Since the conflict began, many schools are lacking teaching materials, and some are perceived as unsafe for children. Children in the NGCA are of particular concern, as protracted conflict is putting a generation of school graduates at risk of diminishing opportunities to continue higher education outside the NGCA and to enter the job market. SCI Ukraine acknowledges that humanitarian crisis have different impacts on women, girls, boys and men and access to services and resources is different for each as they face different risks and may experience different vulnerabilities, relationships, roles and responsibilities. For example, it is understood that women make up the majority of the teaching force within GCA and NGCA schools, so boys do not have proper male role model. The presence of armed groups close to schools is a high risk for girls that might get harassed and abused by armed groups. SCI Ukraine will continue to assess the needs on the ground to ensure that the proposed activities continue to be appropriate to the different needs of boys and girls.

Activities & outcomes

Activities • Schools and pre schools infrastructure rehabilitation and winterisation• Capacity building for teachers on identified priority topics (Incisive education, Gender awareness, Child Safeguarding, Conflict Sensitive Education, Education in Emergencies) • Provision of psychosocial support to conflict –affected children and youth (i.e. HEART, Resiliency and recreational activities, Life Skills for Youth etc.)• Provision of learning mkits to conflict –affected children• Provision of teaching and learning materials to conflict –affected schools • Mine risk awareness • Advocate for the government to sign up to/implement the Safe Schools Declaration • Implementation of the concept of Schools as Zones of Peace • Capacity development of local authorities (conflict‑sensitive education, referral mechanisms , reporting on attacks on education, inclusion)• As the Co‑Lead of the Education Cluster, support the coordination mechanism in both GCA and NGCA including the Coordinator, IM, etc. Outcome Indicators • % of targeted girls/boys who have access to formal education • % of male/female teachers with increased knowledge and skills • % of targeted schools with active School Management Committees• # of policies and regulations adopted by the government of Ukraine to protect schools and learners • % of male/female teachers and education authorities increase knowledge in EiE basic practices• % of targeted boys/girls, school staff increase feeling of safety in school. •

Indicators & targets

# of ECCD units established/refurbished (target: 20) • # of schools rehabilitated and/or provided with winterization (target: 20) • # of schools participating in Schools as Zones of Peace (Emergency repair, security measures, risks and resource mapping, Schools Safety Plan, Code of Conduct in school etc.) (target: 20) • # of boys and girls have access to equal opportunities to quality education (target: 8040 boys and 8040 girls) • # Boys and girls increase their knowledge in Mine risk awareness (target: 5000 girls and 5000 boys) • # of male/female teachers and local education authorities trained on identified priority topics (target: 100 males, 500 females) • # of girls/boys provided with learning materials (backpacks and stationery) (target: 5,000 girls/5,000 boys) # of schools provided with teaching materials (furniture, stationery, methodological materials etc.) (target: 40) 70 % of listed activities are planned to be implemented in NGCA and 30% in GCA Donetsk and Luhansk
