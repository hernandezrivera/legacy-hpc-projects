Project Summary

The project seeks to provide services in the Child protection sub-cluster to achieve the HRP objectives (SO 5) " Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action "in Idleb governorate- Harim subdistrict.
The project designed to include a child-friendly space that provides them with a wide range of activities that operate in the center.
All project activities will be in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action(CPMS) and other standards and SOPs such as UNICEF alternative care SOP, CP Case management SOP, UNICEF Guidance for Inclusion of Children with Disabilities in Child Protection Interventions.
The project includes the preparedness of a child-friendly safe space to provide the children with:
- Psychosocial support activities and recreational activities targeting 450 children.
- Provide psychosocial support activities and recreational activities in camps through a mobile team for 900 children.
- Provide the staff with training about self-care, CPi & CPM training, referral path training, Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for non -Humanitarian actors and Psychological First Aid (PFA) specific for children.
- Provide non-formal education in child-friendly safe spaces.
- Provide awareness-raising sessions for parents/caregivers.
- Conduct positive parenting training sessions.
The M&E team will conduct visits to the child-friendly safe space periodically and verify the implemented activities as well as conduct of base–end line assessments.
Finally, the project team will take into consideration data protection and confidentiality at all stages, information sharing protocols, and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries. MASRRAT has been in the protection sector for eight years.
MASRRAT will commit to sending the 4w report at a specific time monthly.
This project has not been funded yet.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO 2023, more than six million children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation, and need critical child protection services. The ongoing hostilities, protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services continue to have a significant effect on the physical, mental and social well-being of children and caregivers. Dana subdistrict is considered a high-severity area with a range of 4. Child protection risks are equally likely to worsen in 2023 in the regain, with rights violations (child recruitment, detention, neglect, exploitation, sexual violence, abduction, attacks on schools and hospitals, and denial of humanitarian access) continuously on the rise. Based on MASRRAT needs assessment, all the respondents confirmed that education is the most important need to ensure the safety of children in the area and 79% of them said that the specialized services for child protection / gender-based violence and 74% said that the basic needs such as clothes and heating are the most important needs. In addition, more than half (53%) of the respondents said that all the children in the community have access to humanitarian aid and the rest of the children cannot reach the aid due to their disabilities, the lack of transportation, poverty, and displacement and that confirms that the humanitarian interventions should target those children directly.

4 - Implementation of activities

At the beginning of the project, MASSRAT team will sign coordination MOUs with the relevant LCs to facilitate the implementation of the activities. As well, the team will announce the center by distributing brochures and advertisements in public places in the community, in addition to visiting the local councils and informing them about the activities of the center. After that, the center manager with specialists will start providing psychosocial support services and activities during the project life-cycle as the following:- Psychosocial support activities and recreational activities 450 children with ages (6-9),(9-12),(12-17) children.- Provide psychosocial support activities and recreational activities in camps through a mobile team for 900 children in ages (6-9), (9-12),(and 12-17) children and focus on children engaged in child labor/ school dropouts.- Provide the staff with training about self-care, CPi & CPM training, referral path training, Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for non -Humanitarian actors, and Psychological First Aid (PFA) specific for children.- Provide non-formal education in child-friendly safe spaces for (350) children.- Provide awareness-raising sessions for (100) parents/caregivers.- Conduct positive parenting training sessions.MASRRAT M&E will monitor the implementation of activities by:Conduct random weekly visits, pre-posttest for the conducted training, monitoring spot-check, and End assessment to evaluate the impact

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.Protection risk: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialised child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1 : Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Mitigating measures: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Risk 2: Lack of understanding of employees about PSEA issues .MASRRAT understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident, and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about MASRRAT position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children. Risk3: Fear of beneficiaries to send complaints.MASRRAT mobile teams will encourage the beneficiaries to claim their rights and will follow up on the issues with the project management in Turkey. Furthermore, the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate to their need and without any discrimination.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption:1) Existing a High number of children in the region.2) Local qualified staff required for project activities are available and adequate.3) Availability of materials on the market and their natural prices.4)The relationship between the displaced and the host community is good and stable. Risk and mitigation:MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions according to the following Potential risks: 1- Interference in schools’ selection processes,Mitigation: at the beginning of the project, the team will conduct the community mobilization process and will explain the intervention and mechanism of selection of the schools according to the needs.2- High prices of materials and changes in exchange rates, Mitigation: freeze the prices of the contract with the vendors.3- lack of some materials and tools. Mitigation: Import from other regions inside Syria or from Turkey4- Deterioration of the security situation in the project’s areas. Mitigation: Postpone the implementation process until the stabilization of the security situation.5- Sexual exploitation and abuse to the beneficiaries/staff:Mitigation Measure: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on the code of conduct and code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any SEA complaints.6- Transition of Cholera infection to a community member, if any.Mitigation Measure(s): MASRRAT will follow up on the recommendation and measures of WHO and clusters on dealing with it.
