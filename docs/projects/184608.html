Project Summary

The key focus will be to prevent and respond to separation of children from their families by strengthening community based child protection mechanism for prevention of separation and build capacity of protection service providers and stakeholders to facilitate appropriate referrals and strengthen coordination in service delivery to ensure quality comprehensive services to unaccompanied and separated children (UASC).
The project will support 3500 Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) through comprehensive in family tracing and reunification in line with minimum standards and inter-agency (IA) information and management systems (IMS), initiate appropriate alternative care arrangements & train 500 foster families (All women) to provide interim care & temporary foster care support targeting 80% of UASC identified children. 500 foster families will be provided with food and Nutritional support to ease the economic burden on the families. Community sensitization /outreach services will be conducted targeting caregivers on prevention of family separation, child recruitment, risk education, targeting education and community members. In addition, the project will seek to increase the capacity of target communities to identify, report, and refer children with urgent child protection concerns as well as to provide psychosocial support services in the long term. Activities will focus on the establishing community-based child protection mechanisms training and mentoring them and raising awareness about existing referral pathways.
The project will further enhance and improve the coordination and support of the existing services which are being provided by other local and international NGOs working in the area. Specialized services and useful information about service providers will be provided to all beneficiaries that meet the vulnerability criteria, including both IDP and host communities.
NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. The safety and dignity of children will be prioritized by ensuring confidentiality, prioritizing the best interests of the child and respecting the wishes of parents/caregivers, where they do not endanger the child further. All measures to respect and adhere to the Do No Harm approach will be taken during the process of beneficiary selection, service provision and data collection and management.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1.1: Case management of Unaccompanied Minors (UAM) through Identification, documentation, these will facilitate family tracing and ensure that children receive appropriate care and assistance until reunification or until alternative solutions are arranged. This will target of 3500 UASC. The Unaccompanied Minors (UAM) will be Identified and documented through mobile teams of Child Protection Workers and Managers, and identify emergency alternative care arrangements, and provide tracing and reunifying services through best interest determination. Activity 1.1.2: Through the case management NoFYL will establish referral mechanisms and ensure all children affected by any form of violence are referred to appropriate services. Activity 1.1.3: Provide family tracing and reunification, alternative family care and arrangements including community-based support to identified UASC. UASC will be referred to NoFYL’s CP Case Workers through inter-agency emergency response teams, in which NoFYL will participate, alongside other agencies. 25% of the identified Unaccompanied Minors (UAM) are expected to be reunified with their families. Activity 1.1.4: Facilitation of caregivers support sessions for 2500 parents on how to deal with children in distress and how to manage their own stress. The sessions will also be on toxic stress, which has been customized for children facing separation by a caseworker who is embedded into the mobile response team. Activity 1.1.5: Provisional of mental health and psycho social support/ basic emotional support. This includes provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA)/PSS/counseling and emergency referrals to external services for newly displaced boys and girls, through mobile teams of GBV/CP Outreach Workers. MHPSS will also be provided through mobile community based approach (CFS) providing psychosocial support, recreational activities and non-formal education activities to the affected children and their families.Activity 1.1.6: Capacity building of working group members and other NGO staff, service providers (e.g. social workers), community focal points and local authorities particularly those supporting emergency response teams on CPiE, child safeguarding, IDTR, Case management, SCA and PFA trainings. Activity 1.1.7: Develop, obtain, print and distribute through mobile teams of Outreach Workers, IEC materials on grave violations against children/CPiE, mine/IED risk, IDTR for UASC, referral pathways and available services in the local language.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1- Preventing risks of exclusion based on disability, gender and age. The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of women, girls, boys and men will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with female beneficiaries when undertaking protection assessments through NoFYL female staff accessing them and identifying their needs. 2- Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas: Mitigation measure being the following; 1- Assess the ownership of land if government owned or private before establishing any facility on the land. 2- Engage local authorities on the allocation of public land with lease of over 5 years to ensure long term sustainability of projects 3- On private land ensure agreement is signed with the land owners for a lease of over 5 years to establish community structures like community centres in an IDP camps. 4 - Engage directly with the owners of the land rather than gate keepers to understand land tenure processes. 5 - Validate with the regional authorities tenure documents produced by private land owners to ensure claimed land owners are known before proceeding in any engagement.
