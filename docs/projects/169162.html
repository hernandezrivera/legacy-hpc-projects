Project Summary

Following the devastating explosions in the Beirut Port on 4 August 2020, UNHCR reached out to its wide network of Outreach Volunteers and community groups to help identify immediate shelter, protection and other needs of persons affected by the blast, and engaged with humanitarian partners on the ground to support assessments and provide immediate humanitarian relief. In parallel, refugees living in the impacted areas were proactively informed through SMS about existing services, while a dedicated blast hotline was set up to expand the capacity of the existing hotlines and national call centre to provide relevant information to persons calling in distress, and ensure their prompt referral to counselling and specialized services. Since the start, UNHCR’s response has adopted a whole-of-population approach, targeting the most affected and vulnerable Lebanese, refugee and migrant households in Beirut and displaced outside the city. While UNHCR is contributing with its specific sectoral expertise in protection and shelter, and experience in these areas gained over the years in the refugee response, our interventions within the blast response are targeting people with protection needs and vulnerabilities without distinction to nationality or status; this is important from an equity, peaceful coexistence and social stability perspective.
The project covers four mutually reinforcing components aimed at enhancing the resilience and empowerment of victims of the blast. It places individuals at the centre of planning, implementation and evaluation, as actors of the response, in charge of their own future.
A central component of the project is to work with existing community structures and networks, and expand well-established channels of communication with communities to identify individuals and households facing protection problems and facilitate their active participation in deciding on the support and interventions needed.
Another component of the project builds on existing legal aid programs and aims at helping people who have lost their IDs, residency permits and civil documents, which are important to access rights and services, to obtain new documents promptly. UNHCR’s longstanding collaboration with the General Directorate of Personal Status within the Ministry of Interior and Municipalities to facilitate refugees’ access to civil documents will be leveraged to enable speedy document recovery.
Some individuals impacted by the blast are in particular need of tailored, specialized services, including case management, rehabilitation services, and emergency cash assistance (ECA) to address one or several protection concerns or risks. The third component of this project thus aims to respond to particular protection needs of persons with specific needs impacted by the blast. Older persons without family support, persons with disabilities, single female headed households and LGBTI individuals, especially in poorer urban settings are particularly targeted as they are more likely to lack access to adequate services, livelihoods, shelter and legal residency (in the case of refugees and migrant workers), and risk SGBV. LGBTI persons face housing discrimination due to their gender or sexual identity, particularly transgender persons. Pre-existing economic vulnerabilities of the targeted populations expose them to a higher risk of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) than other population groups.
The fourth key component of the project aims to address mental health issues and trauma that can prevent the survivors from recovering and moving on with their lives. Psychological ﬁrst aid, psychosocial support and, when required, specialized trauma or psychiatric services and tailored assistance will be part of all stages of the response to help lay the ground for emotional recovery and resilience against further shocks. UNHCR will work with its existing solid network of partners to provide tailored MHPSS to people impacted by the blast.

1 - Needs

People in Beirut have been deeply affected by the blast, physically, emotionally and materially. At the same time, many volunteers have emerged from within the community, be they Lebanese, refugees, migrant workers, to help affected househols. A number of these existed before the explosion within UNHCR’s community-based protection activities. Communities are best placed to locate and refer those affected rapidly. Affected households themselves have more insight to ﬂag their needs and help calibrate the response. Most importantly, participating in the relief, helping their own families and others, is also part of the psychological recovery of the individuals who fell victims to the blast, giving them a sense of control over the situation and their future. The right to safety and physical integrity has been seriously affected by the blast. Safety concerns continue after the blast as some buildings have become uninhabitable or children play in areas where debris or toxic fumes remain a danger. Some families have been separated during the blast or due to lost lives and remaining members may ﬁnd themselves without protection and at risk of SGBV or exploitation. Affected individuals who do not have a support network need to be assisted with alternative safe accommodation. A number of, notably, older persons are also reluctant to leave their homes for fear of looting and losing their belongings. Individuals and families living closest to the blast have lost all their possessions including their IDs, residency permits and civil documents that are important to access services, education, justice, etc. They will need support in receiving new documents promptly. Structural damages to homes will generate housing, land and property issues that many among the most vulnerable communities are not aware of, or lack supporting documentation in order to claim compensation or restoration. Protection and shelter actors will work closely together to ensure that support to rehabilitation of homes does not result in landlords evicting tenants who lack lease agreements.People living in the affected neighborhoods have gone through multiple traumas. The fear of the blast, destruction of belongings and the loss of loved ones and its impact has generated feeling of insecurity, loss of self-conﬁdence and trauma for many. If unaddressed, mental health issues will aggravate and prevent the victims from recovering. Psychological ﬁrst aid, psychosocial support and, when required, other specialized services need to accompany the response at all stages, from the emergency to the recovery phases.Communication will be sensitive and adapted to age, gender and other characteristics to avoid aid discrimination. In addition, the content and delivery of aid needs to take into account cultural, age and gender sensitivities. To ensure that all persons affected by the blast, including refugees and other marginalized communities receive the protection and assistance they need through the collective efforts and capacities, UNHCR will coordinate its response with of all stakeholders involved in the emergency. Close coordination will be ensured with IOM and ILO to address the specific needs of migrant workers. UNHCR’s legal services will complement legal aid provided by NRC and UNDP on HLP and lost documents. Physical therapy and rehabilitation of injured persons and those with disabilities will be coordinated between Humanity and Inclusion, Sesobel, FDA and other partners extending specialized services to persons with disabilities to avoid duplication and maximize complementarities. To ensure everyone is able access aid without discrimination, UNHCR will, throughout its response, promote access to clear, transparent and understandable information, adapting communication to age, gender and other characteristics and taking into account cultural, age and gender sensitivities. Do No Harm principles, conﬂict-sensitivity and PSEA will be integrated throughout the response

2 - Activities

1. Community engagement, mobilization & outreach. People in need are identiﬁed and referred to services by trained Outreach Volunteers, community groups as well as UNHCR and partner staff crisscrossing the impacted areas on foot or in mobile units, and through ongoing protection monitoring and assessments by the Lebanese Red Cross and other partner organizations. A dedicated blast hotline has been set up to increase the capacity of existing hotlines to provide information to any person affected by the blast and refer him/her to specialized services. 2. Legal aid to secure basic documents. People living closest to the blast have lost all their possessions including IDs, property documents, residency permits and civil documents that are important to access health services, education, justice, and claim compensation, amongst others. Tailored legal aid is provided to help people obtain new documents promptly.3. Individual case support. Some persons impacted by the blast are in a particularly vulnerable situation and in need of tailored support. This can be older persons or persons with disabilities or other specific needs who have lost family members in the blast and families trying to cope with trauma while struggling to ﬁnd shelter and food. To prevent, reduce or mitigate exposure to protection risks, or limit the effects of rights violations, Emergency Cash Assistance as well as counselling is provided following an individual assessment, to capacitate these families to cover medical needs, burials, recovery of necessary belongings and other immediate survival need.4. Mental Health & Psychological Support (MHPSS) & Psychological First Aid (PFA). People in the affected neighborhoods have endured multiple traumas and struggle with the pains of loss, feelings of insecurity and shattered dignity. Psychological ﬁrst aid (PFA) is integrated as part of the response at all stages, from emergency to recovery, to comfort and help people to address basic needs, connect to information, services and social support and to protect people from further harm. Psychosocial support (PSS) is provided to help survivors recover and rebuild their self-conﬁdence and resilience. Frontline staff are trained in PFA to receive and appropriately respond to calls from persons in distress, and refer individuals in need to more specialized MPHSS services when needed.
