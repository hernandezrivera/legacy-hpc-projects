Project Summary

ZOA plans to implement Water, Sanitation and Hygiene project in Greater Jonglei area. Currently ZOA is implementing a WASH project in 17 Islands in Duk county. Previously under the South Sudan Joint Response project ZOA had implemented WASH project in Akobo, Pibor, Gumuruk, Lekwangole, Verthet, Bor South and Fangak Counties. The WASH need in the above mentioned County is very large due to the inflow of IDPs from other close by areas where there is security disturbances and the return of the people to their place of origin from the refugee camps in Uganda. There are not many INGOs who are addressing the WASH needs in the above mentioned Counties and its only ZOA that is currently working in the 17 Duk Islands which is a hot spot for cholera outbreaks.

Needs

In South Sudan 5.3 million people and 304,560 refugees are in need of access to WASH services (South Sudan HRP 2018). As malnutrition and food insecurity levels increase, communities, especially children under age 5 become more prone to WASH related diseases. The cholera epidemic that started in 2017 is spreading to new and previously unaffected areas placing further pressure on already vulnerable communities WASH systems. The ongoing conflict has led to the destruction of most water infrastructure in affected areas. In urban areas increasing fuel prices or unavailability of fuel has put an additional strain on existing water system and substantially increase the cost of water forcing people to resort to use unsafe drinking water. Malaria remained the major health concern in Jonglei in the first quarter of 2018, particularly along the Bor, Duk corridor. This is particularly concerning in light of continued need for mosquito needs and the approach of the rainy season. In January, malaria was reportedly the main cause of 46% of the death in this area which gradually declined in the dry season. The high prevalence of waterborne diseases was reported as most common health problem due to inadequate access to latrines in counties in Western Jonglei along the River Nile. Moreover, access to safe drinking water remained a particular concern in Ayod and Fangak, where at least a third of assessed settlements reported primarily using unprotected water sources in each month of the first quarter, except March, when 21% of assessed settlements reported primarily using unprotected water sources in Fangak. Given previous outbreaks of cholera in Ayod and Fangak, these inadequate WASH conditions represent a risk factor in the event of future outbreaks.( REACH situation overview Jan to March 2018). Insecurity and an uptick in clashes between government and opposition forces continue to impede humanitarian efforts in many areas of the country. During the month of May, several incidents including the unnecessary stoppage and restriction of humanitarian vehicles; the abduction of aid workers; violent clashes between opposition and government forces; and criminal activity, affected local communities and aid efforts in Central and Western Equatoria, Upper Nile and Jonglei Accessibility was further constrained by the arrival of the rainy season that has rendered many locations in need unreachable and has limited the movement of many communities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

There are barriers to access both natural and man made in the mentioned project locations. In Pibor area during the rainy season its difficult to travel by land so to certain areas the boat is used. But during age-set fighting in Pibor area and insecurity in Fangak,Duk and Ayod it is beyond the control of the project. The project plans to address these barriers by building the capacities of both individuals and communities to keep themselves safe: e.g. designated safe spaces, community plans, linkages with protection-sensitive institutions, awareness of rights and responsibilities, and the ability to communicate with their peers and with ZOA.
