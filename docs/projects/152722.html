Project Summary

Continued fighting and insecurity has resulted in displacement, livelihood disruption, dire food insecurity, and malnutrition, contributing to high dropout rates in education. Children and youth have been made increasingly vulnerable as they have discontinued their academic activities to search for food and safety, with increased exposure to protection risk such as sexual violence, child labor, early marriage, and recruitment into armed groups.
The project will implement an integral education intervention with key activities to address the unique needs of those affected by the conflict, such as emergency school meals and life-skills messages and referrals of GBVs survivors for both girls and women. The project will target 15,842 beneficiaries (Male 9,550 female 6,292) of whom 8,750 children (Male 4,850 female 3,900) will directly benefit from the school meals during the project period. A total of 10 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs) will be established or rehabilitated with environmentally friendly materials, along with 10 gender-segregated latrines and WASH facilities. Teaching and recreational supplies will be distributed and psycho-social support will be provided. Teachers will be trained in using the Skills for Life Kit.
IDPs and host communities will receive orientation in peacebuilding and trauma healing activities, how to share limited resources, child protection, and GBV prevention and response in emergencies. Schools and communities will be sensitized concern sanitation, the prevention of disease transmission, and HIV/AIDS. Campaigns for supporting girls’ enrollment will be organized for IDPs and host communities. All schools will be supported to become safe and inclusive spaces where children, parents, and the community can receive improved psychological awareness for other possible crises.

Needs

Continued fighting and insecurity has resulted in displacement, livelihood disruption, dire food insecurity, and malnutrition, contributing to high dropout rates in education. Children and youth have been made increasingly vulnerable as they have discontinued their academic activities to search for food and safety, with increased exposure to protection risk such as sexual violence, child labor, early marriage, and recruitment into armed groups. The fighting and destruction of the conflict in these areas has been well-documented and, unfortunately, education facilities were also looted and destroyed. This is especially true in hardest to reach locations of Koch County (Boaw, Pakur, Ngony Bieh) and Guit County (Niemi, Kueregeng). In these affected communities, the number of children attending school has reduced as a result. No significant efforts are underway to establish rural schools and improve the facilities in terms of infrastructure. WRSS is currently assisting in Koch, Guit, and Fangak Counties for multi-sectoral response to humanitarian needs. This response includes education, but the projects are limited, the needs are significant, and the context continues to change, offering additional opportunities for response. With years of static presence in these locations and ongoing similar programming, WRSS has a unique understanding of the local contexts and needs of the communities, particularly towards education. In Ibba County, only 2 out 18 schools is up-to primary eight as the rest went to Primary Four, and pupils have to go to either Yambio or Maridi for upper primary. Currently, there are only six primary schools and 1 ECD operational, meaning that factors such as lack of teachers, low teacher salaries, poverty, early pregnancies and the inability to pay schools fees, orphans with no means to support themselves, and or forced or organized marriages that complete with home responsibilities at tender ages. Other family issues are of concern; the rate of child abuses in Ibba County is 44% higher than the state average. Mvolo County of Western Equatoria has continued to experience a dare situation since April 2017 and hosted about 28,847 IDPs in Mvolo and Yeri Payam, which has exposed children to various conflicts, including lack of food, increased malnutrition rate, and affected their education, engagement, increasing vulnerabilities among women and children. There is an already difficult education situation, with low rates of enrollment, the limited participation of girls in schooling, poor school infrastructure in which schools are destroyed and burned for example Kata Primary School was looted, and textbooks were burned as these factors increase the current wave of violence and displacement. School-age children, teachers, and Parent/Teacher Associations are identified to be main direct beneficiaries. This is because of the necessity to have capable teachers and parents follow up after capacity development to provide quality services and improve enrollment.

Activities

This project will significantly contribute to the planned Education Cluster activities: 1. Establish and rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms; 10 TLS will be established or rehabilitated with environmentally friendly materials. 10 Gender segregated latrines and WASH facilities for children and youths will be constructed. On-site kitchens will be built to provide shelter for the preparation of meals.2. Provide school-aged children with fortified school meals/take home ration; the provision in-school meals (Lunch in school) and take-home rations as transfers of food resources to entire families conditional upon school enrollment and regular attendance of children. The parents will be involved to supervise the food measurement, food preparation, storage, handling kitchen dishes, washing hands before eating, collect firewood, fetch water, prepare and cook school meals and build schools infrastructures and also provide condiments of other food stuffs to improve the quality or palatability of school meal. Parents will be encouraged to develop and carryout advocacy initiatives on issues related to Emergency School Feeding. Children will receive meals during school hours and get value transfers as incentives for daily attendance and encouraged parents to send their children to attend schools. Cooks will be hired at each school to prepare food to children. Food items that are available at the locations will be locally procured or more food will be procured from Juba. School gardens will be set up to provide children with skills on vegetable production and could produce vegetables used to improve the nutritional content and palatability of the school meals.3. Equip teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience for emergency-affected children. Teachers will be trained in using the Skills for Life Kit and trained on psycho-social support. Additionally, teachers will be trained on EiE, basic pedagogy, and teaching methodologies. Teaching and recreational supplies will be distributed. 4. Equip SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management governance; Training on school management will be carried out in Koch, Guit and Fangak; this will include School Management Committees, Parent Teachers Associations and Board of Governors of primary schools in order to promote and improve the quality of school management. IDPs and host communities will receive orientation in peacebuilding and trauma healing activities, how to share limited resources, child protection and GBV prevention and response in emergencies. With poor pupil enrollment, attendance, and retention in the school system, parents will be provided with adult literacy classes to improve children's enrollment as resulted from high illiteracy rate towards the importance of children's education.The project will also implement the below activities;• Procurement and distribution of food supplies (Sorghum, Lentils, Cooking Oil, and Salt) for 15 supported schools in Koch, Guit & Fangak.• Train 100 teachers (M: 80 F: 20) on school nutrition programme, psychosocial support, life-skills awareness, commodity management, storage, record keeping, data collection and school supervision and monitoring.• Train 140 PTA (M: 40 F: 100) and 15 cooks on emergency preparedness, roles & responsibilities, food preparation, storage and commodity management and developing school action plans.• Train 300 vulnerable girls on menstrual hygiene Management (MHM) and form 12 school health clubs in 15 schools.• Form 6 school farmer clubs and train 30 members, teachers and learners on vegetable growing and seed demonstration.• Form and train 12 school health clubs of 300 members across 3 locations; Koch 120, Guit 80 and Fangak 100 on basic WASH, health and nutrition guidelines.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

WR South Sudan’s ability to achieve our aims, in complex and insecure environments like theGreater Upper Nile Region, is linked to our service quality and commitment to ethics. Given thepotential for desperation among the beneficiaries, and the power dynamic of those bringing anddistributing relief items over the beneficiaries, World Relief (WR) will take a firm, zero tolerancestance towards any staff or volunteers taking advantage of beneficiaries financially, sexually, orphysically, or in any way attempting to divert resources or use the system for their personal gainin addition to their wages. The Country Director and Human Resource Manager educate all staffon the Code of Conduct content, which includes the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse(PSEA), which each staff member receives and signs upon acceptance of employment. A copyof the signed Code of Conduct stays with the employee and another copy is kept on record.Already employed staff benefit from a series of HR trainings organized annually to deepenunderstanding on a number of important HR and protection issues. Line managers explain thecontent if an employee, beneficiary or local authority have any questions or issues regarding theCode of Conduct or PSEA. In order to manage cases of a breach of conduct if they arise, World Relief will identify protectionFocal Points at the implementation site who will receive complaints from beneficiaries or thecommunity. Breaches of the code of conduct will be referred immediately to senior managementin Juba (CD and HR Manager) for action. In order to ensure beneficiaries and local communitiesare able to report complaints, a complaint mechanism will be developed and disseminatedthrough community volunteers in the project.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

With years of static presence in the target locations and existing long-term programming, World Relief in a unique position to implement the proposed project because of the resulting relationships with the communities, beneficiaries, and volunteers. Interactions and dialogues with local staff concerning similar past projects and the needs of their communities have helped to shape the proposed project. Local staff, as members of the community, have unique insight to help shape humanitarian programming. WR's recruitment strategy that prioritizes local staff first and then other national staff and building their capacity has resulted in 97% of employees being nationals, even in senior positions, such as Program Director. WR Staff regularly interact with beneficiaries through normal programming activities in these locations. Likewise, beneficiaries are regularly consulted through monitoring and evaluation activities in existing programming, where they provide their opinions and feedback as well as expressing their needs and those of the larger community. These formal consultations have taken the form of discussion groups, interviews, and surveys, in addition to informal consultations. This feedback has been used in planning the proposed project. Feedback is also regularly obtained through consultation with project volunteers. WR has an extensive network of volunteers in each location, such as Community Nutrition Volunteers, Lead Mothers/Fathers, Community Health Promoters, Water Use Committee Members, Parent Teacher Association Members, and/or Lead Farmers. As community members with specific commitments to humanitarian activities, these volunteers are able to provide learning that is used to tailor the project to the needs of their communities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With years of static presence in these communities and multiple existing long-term programs in the target locations, WRSS is familiar with the protection risks in the population and regularly analyzes and tailors its programming. Existing programming addresses the needs of those with protection concerns. WRSS has established two layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community level volunteers working on health and nutrition. The volunteers will be involved in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-based committees, which are comprised of members of both genders, elderly, youth, people with disabilities, and of minority groups where relevant. The community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meeting with the volunteers and community-based committees. WRSS sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meeting at community level and discuss progresses, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. Feedback is discussed with the management team in Juba and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs. WRSS also interacts with specific protection actors concerning their findings in the target locations to make sure programming addresses the needs of all community members. Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the largest number of beneficiaries.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With similar programming in these locations, WRSS Staff are aware of the barriers to access that different population groups could face during the project. Staff will work with volunteers, local leaders, and other community representatives to ensure that all eligible beneficiaries are able to access and benefit from the proposed services and activities. These consultations will be very important during beneficiary selection/targeting. One of the most significant barriers to access in these locations is logistical, either through distance or terrain or due to restrictions on movement due to the conflict. WRSS will mitigate these barriers by attempting to reach beneficiaries as close to their own communities as possible.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

This project will measure access of beneficiaries to key activities. Known population estimates will be used in setting numerical targets and in the targeting of beneficiaries by geographical location. Known population statistics will be compared against the number of beneficiaries accessing the project’s activities. Access of beneficiaries will also be measured by beneficiary category, with monitoring disaggregated (as appropriate) by gender, age, disability status, vulnerability status, etc. Where the monitoring identifies insufficient access by a beneficiary group, project staff will adjust the project to ensure their inclusion.
