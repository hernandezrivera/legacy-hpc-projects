Project Summary

South Sudan has experienced above-emergency levels of food insecurity, and even famine at times, since civil conflict broke out in 2013. Protracted insecurity and economic crisis has driven more than 4 million away from their homes, with 2 million internally displaced and 2.2. million residing in neighbouring countries as refugees. The 2021 IPC analysis indicates more 6.35 million facing severe food insecurity. Despite favourable climatic and geophysical conditions for large-scale cultivation, more than 80% of the country is dependent on subsistence agriculture. Moreover, hyperinflation combined with limited livelihoods alternatives has led to extremely eroded household purchasing power. These conditions combined with combined with a lack of physical and water infrastructure as well as poor hygiene and sanitation practices has led to a high disease burden and emergency rates of malnutrition. SSGID’s food security and livelihoods strategy is centred on long-term reduction in the burden of undernutrition. Therefore, in direct response to the crisis in Warrap state, the organization will increase access to food and dietary diversity levels by providing opportunities for beneficiaries to improve their agricultural knowledge/practices and food preparation practices. Additionally, access to sources of food will be improved through the improvement of community infrastructure, diversification of livelihood sources including access to credit/loan for increased supply of nutritious foods. To improve the efficacy of nutrition and health programs, our FSL intervention will be fully integrated with local nutrition and health programs, targeting households with children and pregnant and lactating women facing acute malnutrition. Moreover, the program will be linked with local WASH interventions (e.g. hygiene promotion) to promote prevention of disease and, in turn, positive nutrition outcomes.

1 - Needs

Despite efforts of partners, the food and nutrition security situations remain critical in states such as Warrap. Local food production is hampered by periodic flooding, prolonged dry spell and pest infestation including army worms. Extremely poor infrastructure and hyperinflation limits market functionality. A severe lack of basic health and WASH services drives a high disease burden, which contributes to persistently emergency level rates of acute malnutrition. According to IPC projection for January 2021, 42% (520,000) of the population of Warrap state will remain classified as in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or above. SSGID latest SMART surveys conducted in Tonj East and Gogrial West counties show GAM rates of 15.5% and 20.1%, respectively. This trend will be difficult to reverse in the coming years, hence requiring concerted efforts of humanitarian and development partners within HRP framework. The food insecurity in Warrap state is also due to limited income-earning opportunities, high morbidity and limited access to services. Limited access to farming inputs due to the lack of purchasing power has negatively impacted the capacity of the farming population to adequately produce staple food for household consumption. Vulnerable households in the regions resort to negative coping strategies due to the lack of social support systems including access to credit/loan at the community level. Therefore, in direct response to this reality, the organization will increase access to food and dietary diversity levels by providing opportunities for beneficiaries to improve their agricultural knowledge/practices and food preparation practices. Additionally, access to sources of food will be improved through the improvement of community infrastructure, diversification of livelihood sources including access to credit/loan for increased supply of nutritious foods.

2 - Project activities

Nutrition-sensitive cultivation of arable land using cash: The cultivation of arable land will last for 3 months during which participating households are expected to work for 15 days in each month. In return, participants will be paid cash through a third-party financial service provider. Each participant will be issued a voucher/electronic card that will be used for the cash transfer. SSGID will advocate for the cultivation of nutritious food products to contribute to a sustainable reduction in the burden of undernutrition. Construction of dykes and access roads through cash: Participants of the program will work on a dyke, multipurpose pond, or an access road that will be identified by the community through a participatory process. Participants will be required to work for 15 days in each month Cash will be transferred to participants based on the number of days worked in each month. It is intended that the cash will be used to supplement participating households’ dietary needs for the period of the lean season. Construction of demonstration plots around nutrition centers: Demonstration plots for vegetables will be established around feeding centers to teach farmers and mothers at the feeding centers to teach mothers vegetable cultivation methods. SSGID staff from the FSL department will conduct cooking demonstrations using vegetables produced from the demo plots. Demo plots for crops including sorghum and groundnuts will be established at lead farmer’s sites. The plots will be used for learning best agricultural practices but the ownership of farm will revert to the lead farmer providing the demo plot. Formation of Village Saving and Loan Associations: Members of the cultivation groups (farmers) will be organized into Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs). Farmers who are already involved in farming and earning some income from the cash for work will be encouraged to join the associations, although other community members who are interested will also be encouraged to join the group as it will be a voluntary association. Capacity building for nutrition sensitivity in agriculture: Demonstration plots established around the feeding centers will serve as learning centers for both farmers and mothers coming to the centre. Mother/farmers will be trained and supported to grow nutritious vegetables/crops and taught preparation skills. The project will support producers to have nutritious produce available all year round. Support for value chain addition: SSGID will support groundnut farmers’ groups with appropriate groundnut oil extraction machines. Groups around existing markets who produce surplus will be trained on marketing skills prior to receiving the equipment. Seed fairs (seeds and tools): Vulnerable households who qualify for cultivation support will be given seeds and tools through input trade fair or direct distribution. The seeds will be sourced locally from vendors in the project locations. In locations where appropriate tools are available, vendors will be supported to supply the needed quantities. Farmers will be issued vouchers to procure the needed tools. SSGID will also seek support for in-kind donation of tools and or seeds that will be distributed directly to farmers. Early market recovery: SSGID will use the existing market survey data collected in Tonj East and Gogrial West Counties to provide support to vendors in terms of training on supply chain, storage and awarding of contracts (vouchers) for the provision of farming inputs including tools and seeds.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SSGID will deploy three gender protection officers who will be responsible for gender mainstreaming during implementation. The gender officers have received training on PSEA and are well versed with GBV sub-cluster standard operation procedures while dealing with GBV cases. The GBV officers will be responsible for conducting GBV awareness and communication during project activities. They will also conduct training to project implementation committee and locally hired enumerators on PSEA mitigation, prevention, and reporting. SSGID is a member of GBV referral and will be able to refer any cases of PSEA to relevant referral pathway in order for survivors to access services. All new SSGID staffs, locally hired enumerators and project implementation committee members will receive training on organisation’s PSEA policy. They will then be required to sign the policy prior to be included as part of implementation team. SSGID PSEA policy clearly stipulates that the organisation has zero tolerance to PSEA. The policy is clear on steps to be taken if any staff or individuals working for and with SSGID is found to have contravened the policy.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SSGID will implement the project the project implementation committee. In each county, a PIC comprising of 14 members (7 females) will be formed and oriented on implementation modality, project monitoring and evaluation. The PIC will have four positions exclusively reserved for women, girls and individuals living in disability. This will ensure increased participation of females and disabled persons not only in decision making but also direct implementation of the project. SSGID will position feedback and complaint desks and suggestion boxes in locations that are safe and accessible to women, girls and persons living with disability. A male and female enumerator will be seconded in the project to receive complaints as well as provide feedback. Having both genders will encourage women and girls to make complaints since they will be dealing with individuals of the same gender. To take care of the needs of those who cannot read and write, a public announcement will be made on the feedback and complaint mechanism including on how beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can access the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information on feedback and complaint mechanism will be provided during inception meetings with community leaders, beneficiary’s representatives, local authorities, and other relevant stakeholders. All trainees including PIC and local enumerators will also be provided with information on CFM. Further, posters bearing a toll-free number and banners will be displayed prominently at registration and selection of beneficiaries’ sites, distribution points, during meetings and training. Further, public announcement will be made by gender protection Officers and project staff on avenues beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can use to submit complaints and receive feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SSGID interventions are right based and informed by need to ensure dignity, privacy and comfort of people in need when accessing humanitarian assistance. As such SSGID will embed accessible, effective, and safe feedback and complaint mechanism throughout the implementation process. During registration and selection of beneficiaries, SSGID will avail feedback and complaint desk in every beneficiaries’ selection and distribution sites. SSGID will also provide lockable suggestion boxes where complaints can be submitted. Desks and suggestion desks will be manned by a trained enumerator who will ensure all complaints received are submitted to SSGID protection and Safeguarding officer, who in turn will feed the complaints to a complaint and feedback file for redress. Also, SSGID will provide and display toll free phone numbers which beneficiaries can use to submit their complains and receive feedback. All received complaints must be addressed within two weeks with feedback provided in safe and protective way to ensure security and privacy of complainants.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

SSGID will create awareness on the availability of protection referral services where they exist. Survivals will also be supported to pursue the appropriate means of support. The best interest of the victim/survivor will always take prominence. The organization liaisons with local protection actors (IRC, ARC, WVI) to identify available services and contact information. We will work with local authorities including Payam Administrators, Boma Administrators and other local government officials in ensuring that land selected for project activities are void of conflict including personal ownership. We have experience in negotiation land acquisition in Tonj East and Gogrial West Counties. Where applicable, we and the Project Management Committee of the area will negotiate the acquisition of land, in line with applicable laws. To mitigate the interference of external stakeholders in the needs-based beneficiary selection process, a beneficiary selection committee will be formed and trained on selection criteria and procedures. The selection process will involve local authorities and representatives of women’s, youth and minority groups, charged with the role of producing the provisional beneficiary listing. This process will culminate in a town-hall validation procedure. All beneficiaries registered will be validated in a public and transparent process involving beneficiaries and other stakeholders. We will also work with external stakeholders with the potential to negatively impact the process to understand the criteria for the selection of beneficiaries and the probable ramifications for their interference in a transparent process. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements.
