Project Summary

UNFPA, in collaboration and coordination with key stakeholders will endeavor to increase and scale up efforts to meet the immediate lifesaving needs of 140,700 women and girls among the 300,000 who have been left homeless due to the Beirut Port explosion, including nearly 81,000 women of reproductive age and 24,000 adolescent girls. Given the devastation wrought on maternity wards and primary health care centers, an estimated 3,938 women who are currently pregnant will also be in need of antenatal, Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (EmONC), and postnatal services.
To ensure continuity of lifesaving services, including EmONC and the Minimum Initial Services Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health in emergencies, UNFPA is recruiting and deploying additional surge personnel, including midwives, to health facilities. Furthermore, the agency is scaling up SRH service provision through existing and new implementing partners and will avail more health personnel and medical supplies and ensure wider services beyond the immediate SRH needs. This includes supporting women and girls to meet their menstrual hygiene needs, and in light of COVID-19, their overall sanitary and hygiene needs and promoting access to SRH services to specific target groups such as LGBTQI, PLHIV and people with disabilities. UNFPA is also supporting provision of PPE to health care providers and humanitarian relief providers in an effort to stem the risks of COVID-19 infection throughout the response.
UNFPA is also coordinating with the National Mental Health Program (NMHP) to develop and roll out an integrated approach to mental health in the SRH care setting. UNFPA partnered with the NMHP for the mental health national action related to Beirut blast. UNFPA collaborated with the NMHP to integrate a section on mental health in the assessment of the Primary health facilities and is currently supporting the development of joint IEC material (short TVC and tip sheets) on mental health and pregnancy. Also UNFPA is partnering with the NMHP to ensure mainstreaming of Mental health in SRH and GBV interventions.
To help reduce the burden on the public health system, UNFPA will provide cash and voucher assistance to women who may need to travel farther to access health facilities or private delivery facilities as well to cover diagnostic services that are not subsidized by a third party. Cash and voucher assistance (CVA) are a tool, or modality, to address the economic barriers to access SRH and GBV services. Women and girls affected by the Beirut blasts and COVID-19, may face increased sexual and reproductive health (SRH) risks because of increased costs, travel cost or waiting time to access SRH care and services. Noting that the health system is overwhelmed in its response to the COVID-19 pandemic compounding an already dire economic situation resulting in more than 50% of the Lebanese living below poverty.Hence the CVA will be used to cover transport, medical testing, ultrasound, medications and other medical supply costs through unconditional but labelled cash transfers/vouchers which will help increase positive health-seeking behaviors in women and girls. This is a critical intervention to prevent complications of pregnancy, unintended pregnancies, unsafe abortion and sexually transmitted infections, including HIV. This will also be a tool to increase access to integrated GBV services as well. CVA systems are used in response to a crisis and are meant to be short-term and limited to the crisis at hand. It is meant to strengthen access to and support of lifesaving SRH services.
The UNFPA plan of action will ensure:
- Increased access to sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR) services and information
-Increased integration of SRHR, mental health and gender-based violence (GBV) services and mainstreaming of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) services throughout all projects
-Mainstreaming mental health in SRH services

1 - Needs

During conflicts, natural disasters and public health emergencies, sexual and reproductive health needs are often overlooked- with staggering consequences. Pregnant women risk life-threatening complications without access to delivery and emergency obstetric care services. Women and girls may lose access to family planning services, exposing them to unintended pregnancy in perilous conditions. Women and girls also become more vulnerable to sexual violence, exploitation and sexually transmitted infections, including HIV.The Beirut Port explosion has created yet another large-scale crisis in a country that is in the midst of a serious economic crisis as well as the COVID-19 pandemic. Given the scale of devastation wrought by the incident, the burden on the health care system in Beirut- including continuity of care related to sexual and reproductive health services- is expected to be severe, with primary health care centers working at or beyond capacity and consistently reporting shortages in essential medicines and medical supplies, including personal protective equipment (PPE) for staff. Direct targeted beneficiaries105,000 women of reproductive age (15-49), girls, adolescent, pregnant and lactating women; youth and young people; LGBTQI; people living with disabilities, people living with HIV; health care workers and other vulnerable groups. Indirect targeted beneficiaries50,000 Men, boys, and the elderly will have access to sexual and reproductive health services, medical commodities and medicines in primary health care centers. They will also benefit from health and social behavior change messages and linkages to care through mass media, social media and outreach activities.
