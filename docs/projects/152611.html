Project Summary

The project seeks to enhance access to quality, inclusive education in Western Equatoria and Unity States targeting conflict affected adolescents, children and youth aged between 3 and 18 years. Teachers, Community members representing PTAs and local education officials will also benefit from the project as primary project participants. Mercy Corps with Unicef funding is a currently implementing EiE project in the two States covering 92 schools (25 in WES & 67 in Unity State) While complementing already on-going activities, the new project to be implemented during 2019 school year (February - December 2019) will increase the number of supported schools to 123 (49 in WES and 74 in Unity State). At least 24 additional schools to be supported by Mercy Corps will be from Mundri West, an area that is not covered under the current Unicef education PCA. However, Mercy Corps is already implementing an OFDA funded WASH program in Mundri West and has already established contact with local officials. By taking the new education project to Mundri West Mercy Corps does not only respond to local needs but the organisation also remains compliant within its strategy addressing community needs holistically through integrated programming. As part of WASH project, in August 2018, Mercy Corps distributed hygiene kits to schools. In Unity State Panyijiar County which is classified as an IPC 4 area, the project will implement school feeding activities in 10 schools including minor repairs of school kitchens and storage facilities. In Mundri West, where insecurity has resulted in the destruction of school infrastructure, the project will repair (10) and establish (10) TLS as well as construction of (10) gender sensitive school sanitary facilities. Moreover, in both States, Mercy Corps will jointly facilitate psychosocial support for teachers and community members while providing essential teaching-learning and recreational materials to enable conflicted affected children to access quality education. The project will target children aged between 3 and 18 from IDPs, returnees and host communities. Teachers and PTAs will also be linked to child protection and GBV services to enable them to provide requisite support to children traumatized by conflict.

Needs

The education cluster estimates that some 2.8 million boys and girls aged between 3 and 18 will be out of school due to conflict and crises country wide. This translates to 67 per cent increase in the number of girls and boys in need of education in 2019 from 2018. Additionally, more than 40, 000 teachers and members of school management committees will require humanitarian assistance to be able to deliver quality and inclusive pre-primary, primary and secondary educational services. Poorly qualified teachers with low levels of motivation due to inconsistent payment of teachers incentives, lack of teaching-learning materials further aggravates the situation. Demotivated teachers are constantly looking for jobs elsewhere, Job search makes them lose concentration thereby deliver poor quality lessons. Hunger as a result of food shortages and lack of school feeding programs in areas such as Unity State have resulted in disproportionately large school drop outs with boys and girls opting to spend their time at home or in cattle camps engaging in livelihoods activities. In Panyijiar girls and boys dropping out of school due to hunger are from both host and IDP families who arrived in Nyal over the past 3 months from Leer and Mayeindit. In 2018 anecdotal evidence from Mercy Corps facilitated community feedback meetings showed that nearly 2,500 boys and girls whose families were displaced from Leer and Mayendit arrived in Panyijiar especially Nyal. Some of the IDP families successfully enrolled their children in schools whilst others could not. A recent assessment (Oct 2018) by the education cluster 50 percent of the schools assessed reported that that in the latest academic year 3 - 4 weeks of learning were lost due conflict induced disruptions, Insecurity was also cited as a major reason why some 21 schools across the country were none functional. Building and furniture were destroyed and school supplies looted in most of the schools that suffered these attacks and children and teachers were left with psychological trauma.

Activities

Mundri West is one of the areas that has been severely affected by conflict and economic crisis, there has been deterioration of both physical and social infrastructure necessary to support education. Mercy Corps in collaboration with the local education authorities will conduct back to learning campaigns mobilizing parents to enroll girls and boys in school. In addition, the project will engage community mobilisers who will conduct door to door campaigns identifying school aged girls and boys out of school and encouraging their parents to enroll them in schools. The project will prioritize schools with damaged learning spaces that are in urgent need of rehabilitation as well as those with high pupil : classroom ratio for construction of new learning spaces; construct gender sensitive sanitation facilities including Comfee Corners for menstrual hygiene management in schools with upper primary classes and conduct menstrual hygiene education as part of school clubs activities for adolescent girls. Volunteer female teachers to act as club mentors will be trained on the use of hygiene kits and supported to provide girls's training in their schools. Mercy Corps will collaborate with the State Ministry of Education and jointly train teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner centered methodologies. Initial training will last for 3 days while refresher courses will be over 2 days. To strengthen the capacity of PTAs and education authorities in psycho-social support, record keeping, school management and monitoring the project will encourage volunteer parents to actively participate in school management committees, and train them in essential skills which will enable them to fulfill their oversight responsibilities. Initial PTA training will be for 3 days with refresher courses conducted over one day's period. PTAs will also be encouraged to participate in monthly review meetings whose primary purpose will be for monitoring implementation of school development plans. The project will coordinate with Unicef and request teaching-learning supplies including ECD kits for distribution to boys and girls in 24 selected schools. Three phases of supplies distribution will be done during this project i.e once a term. In addition to Unicef recreational kit, material support to children's clubs after concluding school based consultations determining specific needs of each club. Cases of child protection and SGBV especially of girls will be referred to the relevant cluster partners Further to these interventions the project will implement school feeding in Unity State, Panyijiar County in both Nyal and Ganyliel in twenty schools targeting 4,461 children. Mercy Corps implemented a similar project in the same area in 2017 under SSHF and in Rubkona County in 2018 with support from WFP. Applying lessons learnt from these two, the project will integrate with FSL and WASH teams in activity implementation. FSL team will conduct demo cooking classes training volunteer parents, boys and girls on nutritious cooking methods and the importance of dietary diversity. The team will also facilitate establishment of school gardens and young farmer's clubs in six schools in the county. The project will provide tools and other materials required for demo cooking classes. Moreover, the project will repair school kitchens and storage facilities in selected schools; provide cooking utensils and water storage containers; procure food commodities from Juba and local markets where feasible, mobilize volunteer parents & train PTA members, cooks on food handling including measuring daily portions and hygienic preparation of daily school meals. With support from WASH teams, conduct hygiene awareness sessions with an emphasis on the importance of hand washing and disposal of waste; Mercy Corps will coordinate activity implementation with Welthungerhilfe (WHH) who are also piloting school feeding in partnership with WFP in Panyijiar County.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project provides information to beneficiaries through community dialogue sessions conducted on a monthly basis. In addition to this, the project engages on a regular basis with local education officials for joint planning and monitoring sessions. Written project updates are also shared with the relevant line Ministries.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages on IEC materials are context-specific and tailored for different gender and age groups. For instance, training and community feedback sessions are conducted in Nuer and Moru for Unity State and WES respectively. Where necessary women, men, boys and girls are put in separate discussion groups during community dialogue sessions.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project collaborates with the protection and GBV clusters and the nature of EiE is to provide boys and girls with safe learning environments. Through collaboration with the child protection cluster, the project will train teachers, community members and PTAs in psychosocial support where issues of protection will be addressed. Trained adults will be able to provide children affected by stress and trauma due to conflict with necessary support. Referral pathways will be mapped and made available to teachers and parents to enable them to assist children who need specialised services. Teachers will learn the importance of delivering lifesaving messages and information to children and the project will support this through providing information flyers. A special emphasis on code of conduct will be placed in teachers training as well as referrals of protection cases to ensure that all children and especially girls are safe and protected from SGBV.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Further to consulting community leaders and key informants such as County Education Directors, the project conducted focus group discussions as part of broad community dialogue sessions. Participants in these, included the youth, women and men as well as girls and boys who represented children’s voices in the consultation meetings. The project plan includes community dialogue sessions in which these consultations will continue throughout the project cycle and they will form part of the monitoring plan of this project. General community members as well as the leadership will be encouraged to use these dialogue sessions as a vehicle for providing project feedback including treating this sessions as part of the project complaint mechanism.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Through regularly conducted community engagement meetings, the project allows participants and beneficiaries to give their feedback while complaining about issue that need attention. Furthermore, there are suggestion boxes placed at individual Mercy Corps field offices for use by community members in the event that they prefer to remain anonymous in their feedback. The M&E teams also conduct impromptu monitoring visits to participating schools and communities in addition to post distribution monitoring activities. Mercy Corps whistle blowing mechanism provides an opportunity for other staff members to provide feedback and complaints to Senior Management Teams.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project plan includes monthly community engagement meetings. Individuals such as head teachers, community mobilisers, PTA members, cooks and local education officials directly participating this this project will be requested to participate regularly. Furthermore, interested parents and other community members will also be notified of the monthly meetings which will be open to the public. In addition to daily feeding and school attendance discussions, separate dialogue sessions will be held on quarterly basis with boys and girls benefiting from the project. Information collected through these session will be analysed alongside other monitoring data collected.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on protection risk analysis as centrality of protection is key in providing education in emergencies. The different protection needs of boys and girls were analysed and incorporated in the project design that links children especially girls to protection and GBV clusters due to their disproportionate vulnerability to SGBV. Likewise, boys needs to be addressed by the protection cluster include their increased risk to be forcibly recruited into militia and other armed groups. Both boys and girls are exposed to psychological trauma due to crises and displacement. Therefore, in cognizant of this risk, the project has created strong linkages with the child protection cluster partners for referrals. In addition to the risks faced by girls and boys, risk analysis considered the needs of men and women who work directly with children in EiE activities hence inclusion of training in prevention of GBV and PSS for PTAs, community members including cooks and education officials. While this training is aimed at improving the capacity of adults to provide support to children, it will also be useful the adults themselves as they also face similar risks in conflict situations.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project promotes inclusive access to education and implements large scale as well as localized mobilization strategies. Through regular close monitoring, community feedback and complaints mechanism exclusion cases are minimized and address. Mercy Corps being an active member of the education cluster, participates in cluster meetings at both the national and subnational levels and highlights cases of exclusion to State Ministries and authorities and follows up on action taken to address such issues. For example, Mercy Corps is recently followed up on a case where children were not allowed to attend classes because they did not have school uniforms. The subnational education cluster took this case as an advocacy issue with the State Ministry of education afterwards children affected were allowed to attend classes without further discrimination.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Creating safe learning spaces is central to this project. Education in emergency seeks to reduce inequalities and deprivation and empowers communities, boys and girls to be assertive in speaking up against neglect. School peace clubs and community engagement meetings will create a platform where protection threats are openly discussed and addressed.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project activities include psychosocial support, SGBV, life-skills training. These activities promote well being of children in cases where teachers and students have been victims of trauma. They also enable teachers to convey life saving messages and life enhancing skills and information including practical steps to take in cases of separation from family members, displacement and upon citing of explosive devises.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project measures number of beneficiaries accessing services in relation to set targets. In addition to quantitative information, the project through observations and community discussions will be able to establish qualitative information as it relates to access.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Creation of safe learning spaces is of importance in the EiE projects. The project monitoring system assesses and measured if physical infrastructure provided meets safety standards. Other non physical safety indicators such as location and time of activities are also measured and reported on.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The monitoring system measures if beneficiaries are treated with dignity by analyzing comments provided through community feedback mechanisms as well as through observations. Dignity is also a measure of respect and since respect is reciprocal, the way Mercy Corps is treated in the community will signify if this project and other organisational interventions are upholding the dignity of beneficiaries.
