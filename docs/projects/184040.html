Project Summary

Gedaref: ACTED has designed this proposal by taking into account the FSL prominent needs detected in Tunaydbah and Um Rakuba. In detail, the project consists of two major components. Within the first one, ACTED is planning to target 3,000 Ethiopian HHs (1,500 per camp) with no able-bodied members through unconditional direct cash distribution or cash distribution through a partner bank. The measure is meant to supplement the food rations provided by WFP for the most vulnerable, who have less chances to earn labour income and use to buy additional food. To increase the resilience among the refugees in the camp, the proposal will also target the able-bodied youths, in order to build their capacity and eventually contribute to diversify their livelihood opportunities through the conduction of vocational trainings. The list of programs will be designed as per youths’ demand and interest in different vocational trades. ACTED will propose at least 20 different trades to the selected group of youth, and based on their prioritization ACTED will organize training for the top 5 trades.
Khartoum: The Action consists of three major components. ACTED will cover FSL needs through provision of MPCA to most vulnerable families. It will conduct an in-depth assessment on site to identify the most vulnerable population among South Sudanese refugees. Under FSL, the proposal targets also the able-bodied youths to build their capacity and eventually contribute to diversify their livelihood opportunities by improving their technical capacity to engage with labour market through provision of vocational training. ACTED will act in coordination with humanitarian partners operating in target areas to set-up a system of legal support for refugees helping them joining the formal labour market. ACTED will cover critical shelter needs given that 73% of refugees do not benefit of any protection from elements. The SNFI component was also designed in relation to the flood-proneness of the open areas. Indeed, floods affected over 4K people of Khartoum state in 2021, and this proposal includes strategic pre-positioning of NFI kits to be delivered in case of need throughout 2022. The last project’s component will address major WASH gaps in Sharg An Neel and Um Beda. ACTED will restore access to safe water supply through rehabilitation of 3 water pumps, while providing further quality control and water safety. ACTED will construct 100 Communal Latrines, targeting health facilities, schools, market areas or in settlements, meanwhile rehabilitating additional 50 latrines, reducing open defecation risk and contamination of water sources and water-borne diseases. ACTED will distribute 2000 HKs alongside with 800 dignity kits.
Blue Nile: The Action aims to cover emergency WASH, FSL and NFI gaps at Blue Nile State entry points, where currently over 5,000 individuals are suspected to seek refuge from Ethiopia. The project will ensure the access to half food rations as per WFP’s standard basket, the provision of NFI as per ES/SNFI cluster standard composition, the distribution of soap and menstrual hygiene kits as well as an improved access to WASH services, thus contributing to restore the dignity of the population who have fled their home country. Hence, the life-saving assistance is conceived for the new arrivals before their relocation to Camp 6. For this reason, food and hygiene items distribution rounds will be activated upon UNHCR alerts on new arrivals at the entry points. The outputs and the activities of this proposal are strictly related to the critical and multi-sectoral needs faced by the refugee population, which have been described more in detail in the section below.

1 - Needs

Gedaref: As per September 2021 BaNVA report, in Gedaref refugees experienced higher food vulnerability compared to their host communities. High levels of food expenditure were recorded. In Gedaref, Reduced coping strategy index (rCSI) is aligned with the majority of the states, reporting a low rCSI index of 0-14 which indicates a moderate vulnerability. As per Sudan’s Nutrition cluster, malnutrition rate in both camps increased significantly since the arrival of refugees. Although WFP has been providing monthly food rations in Um Rakuba and Tunaydbah in partnership with ACTED, 89% of the surveyed people stated they had a serious problem with food in terms of quantity, quality or possibility to cook it, 63% continue to perceive the access to food as the highest priority, and up to 95% of the HHs admitted they had to sell their assets or other items to buy food in the last 30 days, as per 2021 September survey published by a partner implementing Cash Programs in Um Rakuba. Indeed, several humanitarian partners are currently running various cash for work Programs in the camps, but none of them is targeting families with able-bodied members, thus having consequences on their access to sufficient quantities and nutritious food. Khartoum: Khartoum is among the main refugee-hosting states of Sudan, with 305,964 individuals equal to 27.5% of the state population. 63% of refugees are South Sudanese. More than half of total refugees are living in open areas of Jebel Awlia (31K), Sharg An Neel (69K) Um Bada (20K) and Um Durman (15K), where the bulk of people is from South Sudan. Ten years after South Sudan’s independence, amid economic challenges of post-Sudanese revolution, many people stay in informal settlements, with uncertain legal status and far more susceptible to financial shocks. According to latest IPC (April 2021-February 2022), 250K individuals experienced food shortages in Khartoum. In detail, refugee HHs who spent at least 51% of their income on food represented 45% of total. A critical level of vulnerability was reported also in SNF, where only 6% of individuals in-camp or camp-like situation are living at least under a permanent shelter without a hard fixed roof. Remaining percentage is either living in tent (36% of total) or in emergency shelter (37%). Focusing on WASH, 23% of refugee having unusable latrines or no latrines at all, and their access to water is not sufficient or requires to travel more than 5 hours. HHs facing severe WASH vulnerability have no access to handwashing tools and soap. Blue Nile: Since November 2020, more than 7,534 refugees have entered BNS in south-east Sudan from Benishangul- Gumuz regional state in Western Ethiopia, of which 2,101 have been relocated to Camp 6 as of 31st July 2021, while 1,918 were settled in Macheleng, 167 in Menza, 133 in Yabacher, 70 in Magano, 2,537 in Dem Saad and 608 in Shamfoz, according to the figures shared by UNHCR in the population update of 31st July 2021. In this scenario, ACTED intends to integrate the inter-agency multi-sectoral life-saving assistance provided for the population currently staying at the entry points of Wad Al Mahi, which have been mostly inaccessible during the 2021 rainy season. As per BaNVA September 2021 Report, 34% of refugees experienced a high food vulnerability level. Percentage of out-of-camp families facing high levels of food vulnerability reached 52%, while percentage of families living in-camp and facing the same food needs has been below the 15%. SNFI vulnerability is even higher, considering that 50% were found in a severe status. Vulnerability in WASH sector is even worse. Considering the access to sanitation facilities, enough water to meet the water needs, the time to reach the nearest water source, the availability of handwashing tools and the access to waste disposal facilities, the 89% of refugees faced a level of vulnerability which varied from high (26% of the total population) to severe (63%).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Program envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups direct and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase:- Before each intervention, ACTED conducts an assessment of security context and protection risks, followed by regular monitoring of situation throughout project’s lifespan.- The whole community will be sensitised on project’s scope and activities and fully informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (single women, PLWS, children, elders, PwDs, HHs with no source of income, HHs with PwDs, HHs with more children under 5 years old). Program Implementation Phase:- ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during community sensitization and trainings. - MHM component is included. - Distances to fetch water, waiting time, safety during traveling will be addressed through rehabilitations and ad hoc protection measures. - First aid kits and protective wear are also provided to ACTED’s staff, beneficiaries and daily workers, in line with the type of activities. Accountability:- ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM at Area office and Field office levels, where beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff permanently deployed in the field, ACTED will also conduct regular spot checks. ACTED will evaluate if the complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the ongoing Action and inform the future projects’ design. A more detailed description of the CRM is provided under the AAP section of this proposalHuman Resources - ACTED has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. ACTED also complies with The Code of Conduct for The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and the UNHCR Code of Conduct. This CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to the coordination through a standardized communication pattern and actions will be taken by the coordination.- ACTED will set-up a gender-balanced Program team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusionary mechanisms. In all activities, ACTED will promote an equal gender participation.
