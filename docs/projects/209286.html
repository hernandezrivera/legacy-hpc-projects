Project Summary

In collaboration with the State Ministry of Health, the project implemented by ALIMA has for objective to reduce the morbidity and mortality for vulnerable host and displaced populations, including women and children, affected by the crisis in Kadugli and Reif Arshargi localities in South Kordofan and in two IDP camps in in El Fasher locality in North Darfur State.
In North Darfur, ALIMA aims to increase access to basic primary healthcare and nutrition services by supporting a full PHC package in the two IDP Camps and support case management for SAM with medical complications and improve quality of care at the Stabilization Center.
ALIMA will support the implementation of life-saving curative and preventive interventions and improvement of services provided through minor rehabilitation of health facilities, training of healthcare workers, curative consultations, BE/CEmONC, management of severe and moderate acutely malnourished under-five children, provision of essential medical supplies and support to emergency referrals. The capacities of the of the SMoH Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) will be strengthened to deliver minimum healthcare services to population affected by disease outbreaks and population displacement due to the armed conflict. Security will be closely monitored through ALIMA/UNDSS security monitoring apparatus, staff and community information; where necessary prepositioning of stock and/or providing nutrition food to patient to cover for a month.
In South Kordofan, ALIMA aims at improving access to free quality life-saving curative and preventive health and nutrition care with focus on maternal and child health in 2 PHCs, mobile clinics for hard-to-reach populations and support to the Kadugli Teaching Hospital in the Kadugli and Reif Arshargi Localities in South Kordofan state. ALIMA will support the implementation of life-saving curative and preventive interventions through OTP, TSFP and SC facilities, community-based MUAC screening for under five children, and recruitment and training of CHWs and MUAC Mother. In addition, training of healthcare workers, minor rehabilitation of health facilities, provision of essential medicines and supporting referrals of complicated SAM children to ALIMA supported SC. Work in active collaboration with MoH in MoH facilities with the combination of ALIMA/MoH staff. Mobile clinics will be used to access hard to reach areas. The team will deliver primary health care for children under 5 and pregnant women, including nutritional screening. They will also ensure the provision of a comprehensive package to ensure the management of sexual violence victims.
ALIMA will improve access to quality life-saving secondary health care for pregnant and lactating women and survivors of sexual violence at the maternity in Kadugli Teaching Hospital. ALIMA will support the maternity ward in Kadugli Hospital to deliver comprehensive emergency obstetric and neonatal care. ALIMA will strengthen the skills of midwives and other auxiliary nurses through on the job-training to improve patient care and global management of the maternity ward.
The nutrition activities will be integrated in the existing primary healthcare facilities and mobile clinics; this will in turn enhance malnutrition management capacity for the healthcare workers and hence, continuity even when ALIMA shall have withdrawn their support from the facilities

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To make the community aware of the services, community health workers (CHW) will be chosen from each community to conduct awareness raising activities about sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence, health promotion, personal and environmental hygiene, and referrals from the community to the health facility. Considering the lack of sensitization and level of stigmatisation against survivors of GBV, this community-level intervention is critical to increase the number of survivors reaching life-saving health and psychological care services. This approach aims at building the confidence of communities to reduce stigma and to foster disclosure by survivors and encourage them to reach to available GBV services. Survivor-centered GBV case-management, such as clinical management of rape and MHPSS services will be offered at the ALIMA supported facilities to the most vulnerable patients. ALIMA has a leading role in the SGBV Referral Pathways Taskforce to strengthen community sensitization and support the coordination of referral mechanisms to link survivors with timely, appropriate medical and mental health care as well as protection services.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

To ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, ALIMA will engage communities in enhanced service delivery and the establishment of a community-structured feedback mechanism. The conversations will take place during project meetings, which will include sensitizing the community to the benefits of providing feedback on services received as well as walking them through the current communication and feedback technique ALIMA is examining. The objective is to get feedback on the current system and tweak it to fit the community structure, allowing clients to respond with ease and confidence. Throughout the year, ALIMA will continue to strengthen and improve community capacity and confidence to have a say not only on the method in which services are delivered but also to freely discuss any concerns they encounter in reporting problems they encounter through such sites.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The projects will have a feedback and complaints handling mechanism that allows the local population to provide feedback. In addition, an e-mail address is made available and communicated to all project staff. These feedback and complaint mechanisms are inclusive. Feedback and complaints are stored in a secure database. In terms of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse ALIMA is committed to prevent any sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers. ALIMA's staff recognizes the problem related to the imbalance of power in the humanitarian field and the potential exploitation that this could cause. Sexual exploitation and abuse are condemned by law and international standards and ALIMA condemns such behavior. They are absolutely contrary to the humanitarian principles and ethics to which ALIMA and its employees adhere. Measures are in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse: Any employee involved in a case of sexual exploitation or abuse will be subject to immediate termination and ALIMA may refer the matter to the national authorities or the authorities of the country of origin of the employee being prosecuted. Sexual activity with persons under the age of 18 is strictly prohibited, even in the event of differing local laws regarding age of consent or age of majority. Mistaken belief in a child's age will not be taken as a defense. Any exchange of goods or services, including assistance due to recipients, for sexual services or other sexual or exploitative behavior will not be tolerated. All ALIMA representatives and managers are responsible for creating and maintaining a safe environment that protects against sexual exploitation and abuse and are encouraged to pay attention to promoting the safety of women and girls and any other vulnerable population. ALIMA staff are urged to report any inappropriate behavior they may witness. Several complaint mechanisms have been defined. One of them is to send an alert to this newly created email address: alert@alima.ngo. All of ALIMA's employees are key elements in the enforcement of this policy. We are a humanitarian organization and therefore have a great responsibility, individually and collectively, to prevent abuses or violence from being committed in our name or through the positions that are assigned to us by our organization At the field level, the HR/Admin Manager is in charge of enforcing the policy, while at the country level the HR coordination, the Deputy Head of Mission as well as the Head of mission have the task to enforce the policy.
