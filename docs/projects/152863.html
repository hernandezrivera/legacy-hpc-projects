Project Summary

The project will save lives of children 6-59 months and PLW suffering from SAM and MAM with focus on un-served payams in Fangak and Nyirol counties. This objective will be achieved by: 1. Delivering timely quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition for most vulnerable and at risk targeting 42% of SAM and 40% of MAM in girls and boys 6-59 months and 25% of PLW. 2. Increasing access to MIYCN programs preventing under-nutrition among the most vulnerable and at risk, reaching 30% of PLW in high burden areas. 3. Increasing integration of Nutrition, Health and WASH services, and improved access to FSL and Protection activities in counties with GAM ≥15% and IPC ≥ 3. 4. Enhancing nutrition monitoring, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response decision making. Using static, mobile outreach and RRM/ICRM responses, the project will scale-up services from 8 to 11 OTP-TSFP sites in 4 payams in Fangak (Manajang, Paguir, New Fangak and Mareang) and 2 payams in Nyirol (Chuil and Pultruk) all currently accessible. Focus will be on reaching IDPs in locations not served. The response strategies will be (1) carry-out case-finding screening using the MUAC method (including malaria testing for SAM patients) from static units and through outreaches to PHCUs and admitting under 5 SAM and MAM patients and PLW MAM patients to SC, OTP and TSFP services using expanded criteria where needed (2) conduct mobile outreaches and RRM/ICRM to insecure and inaccessible locations beyond the reach of static services (3) deliver MIYCN services to PLW and care-givers of children U23 months through counselling MtoM support groups, including vitamin A supplements and de-worming (4) fully integrate nutrition, health and WASH services, and partially integrate FSL, Education and Protection activities. SAM with medical complications and requiring malaria treatment will be referred to PHCCs. Insecurity creates risks for PLW and U5s. CMA and communities will implement protection practices to reduce risk of SGBV. Key targets: 1. Needs

The emergency this project will address is life threatening acute malnutrition (GAM ≥15%). The needed services include SC, SAM and MAM services using expanded criteria where needed, MIYCN services and strengthened capacity to monitor the nutrition situation. The project will use a combination of responses - RRM/ICRM, static/mobile, survival kits ensuring timely delivery of supplies. Conflict has caused population displacement, breakdown of facilities and interruption of services. Recent data shows 26,200 IDPs in Fangak and 53,590 in Nyirol (Health Cluster PIN 31 Oct 18). Children suffering from SAM are nine times more likely to die than their healthy peers. Malnutrition in pregnant women causes low birth weight babies, miscarriages, pre-mature deliveries. FAO warns that food security situation remains dire showing a case load 20% higher than 2017. Fangak is a concern due to conflict causing market disruptions, damage of productive assets and displacement (GIEWS Country Brief, FAO 25 Sep 2018). In a fact sheet dated July 2018, a REACH study showed 61-80% of settlements reported inadequate access to food in both Fangak and Nyirol. Both counties are rated as IPC 4 Emergency for the period January to March 2019, and well above the ‘Critical’ threshold (GAM WHZ ≥15%) (SS IPC Key Messages Sep 2018 – Mar 2019, pg 5). The same report (pg. 6) shows the IPC 4-Critical for acute malnutrition for Sep to Dec 2018. SCI Mar 2018 SMART survey in Nyirol showed U5 GAM rate of 25.7% (SAM 6.6, MAM 19.1) and in Fangak, a ACF May 2018 SMART survey showed U5 GAM rate of 16.1% (SAM 2.5, MAM 13.6) (WFP SMART Survey Matrix 2018). CMA’s data for the period Jan - Sep 2018 showed: Nyirol of 14,160 U5 children screened, 9% were SAM, 15% were MAM, of 4,025 PLW screened 26% were MAM; Fangak of 33,017 U5 children screened, 5% were SAM, 10% were MAM, of 8,252 PLW screened 19% were MAM. This data shows the critical need of target populations are for SAM and MAM services and MYICN for prevention of chronic malnutrition. Estimates for Fangak show the caseloads are 5,986 for U5 SAM, 15,070 for U5 MAM and 12,248 for PLW MAM. For Nyirol, estimated caseloads are 3,011 for U5 SAM, 12,132 for U5 MAM and 4,474 for PLW MAM (Nutrition Cluster Case Load Projections 2019). This data indicates the 2019 caseloads are 95% higher than 2018 for U5 children. In Nyirol the PLW population requiring MIYCN support is 13,048, and for Fangak it is 12,881. Based on Cluster estimates, the caseload in the payams reached by this project in Fangak will be 2004 for U5 SAM, 4805 for U5 MAM and 2441 for PLW MAM, and in Nyirol 597 for U5 SAM, 1363 for U5 MAM and 314 for PLW MAM. High GAM rates for children 6 – 23 months is made worse by worms, Vit A deficiency and diarrhea caused in-part by poor feeding and WASH practices. The REACH assessment of July 2018 showed 38% of Fangak settlements reported consumption of wild foods that may make people sick and 39% of Nyirol settlements reported the same. Nutrition responses that link with FSL, and are integrated with health and WASH are essential. To reach a large population of children, education services can deliver health, nutrition and WASH messages widely. At 65%, the rate of sexual or physical violence against women in South Sudan is double the global average and among the highest in the world (Women, Girls Face Staggering Rates of Violence, Global Women’s Institute, Dec 2107). The violence has heightened the need for care for the victims of SGBV along with facility and community-based protection. Women, adolescent girls and children are most at risk. This reality coupled with conflict, insecurity and flooding causes access restrictions to nutrition services. Special case-finding outreaches and protection measures are needed to ensure PLW and children have access to services.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Through quarterly meetings (more frequent if required), the project keeps the community well informed about the assistance being provided, their rights and entitlement as well as detailed plans for outreaches and other implementation concern. Further, community meeting are used to seek the community’s support to identify vulnerable households for action by project personnel

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the regular meetings with MtoM Support Groups and in counselling individual mothers on infant feeding practices and nutrition, message are very tailored to specific target group needs. When men are part of the target group for nutrition education, messages are adapted to their gender roles and responsibilities for child and pregnant mother nutrition.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project addresses protection from sexual abuse on two levels: (1) in the community as the threat of abuse and exploitation is a barrier to accessing nutrition services and (2) at the facility level in terms of safety of the facility as patients access services. In the community, the project seeks to reach those with access issues through outreaches and by promoting community-based protection approaches to ensure all have equal access. At the facility, the time-of-day of service delivery is planned in consultation with patients to ensure it is appropriate for PLW and children and does not expose them to risk of sexual abuse, and the conduct of personnel are closely monitored to ensure there is no coercion or sexual exploitation of patients.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Project community consultations go well beyond community leaders and the usual constituencies. The project reaches women in focus groups and on an individual basis as key informants. Where MtoM Support Groups have been established, these group are clearly very important in broadening consultation as they provide vital information on household and gender related realities that inform project plans and strategies

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Each nutrition site supported under this project will have a feedback and complaint mechanism established as a pillar of project approach to the accountability to affected populations. Again, in the complaints and feedback mechanism, the project will consult with users on an individual, key informant and focus group basis as well as through community meetings to establish a viable, transparent and effective mechanism for delivering feedback and complaints and to seek input to finding resolution to complaints

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Both the nature and quality of community engagement are important elements of project monitoring and evaluation. Community engagement is monitored as part of the ongoing process of project implementation (the regular community meetings, meetings of MtoM Support Groups), and also through interviews with key informants, focus group discussions and gathering the perspectives of community leaders

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

At each location, the project has a strong awareness of protection risks of vulnerable populations (elderly, PLW, adolescent girls, boys and girls especially for IDPs) due to experience gained through both nutrition and health programming. However a formal protection risk assessment as presented in the SS Protection Mainstreaming Toolkit (final edition) Dec 2015, has not yet been undertaken. Resources have been allocated to undertake protection risk assessments at each project site in 2019. The assessments, risk mapping and analysis will inform ongoing programme planning beginning 2019.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project personnel have a wealth of knowledge on barriers to access nutrition services based on experience at the field level in both health and nutrition programming. IDPs, PLW and children all face these barriers. More will be learned through completing the protection risk mapping and assessment, which will help inform location specific protection plans applying the protection mainstreaming toolkit. Presently, to mitigate these access barriers, CMA conducts a robust outreach approach to serve populations facing access barriers, and promotes community-based protection approaches to enable vulnerable people to access nutrition services.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project activities do address inequalities and protection threats mainly through a robust outreach approach to serve populations facing access barriers, and by promoting community-based protection approaches to enable vulnerable people to access nutrition services. Completion of the protection risk assessment and analysis will better inform project strategy and plans. Further, at the facility (employee) level, the protection focal person is responsible to ensure that all personnel conduct their duties in an ethical manner without threat of coercion, violence or mistreatment of patients in anyway.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project measures beneficiary access to nutrition services through facility-based and community based safety audits/assessment including community consultations, focus group discussions and gathering knowledge from key informants. Through this approach, both the reasons for access constraints and measures that need to be put in place to mitigate access constraints are learned.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project will conduct facility-based and community-based safety audits/assessment to give clear measure of safety issues. Presently without a completed formal protection risk analysis, the project relies on local leaders, focus group sessions and responses of key informants for knowledge on safety issues faced by nutrition patients as they access services. MtoM Support Groups have been critical in the identification of safety issues. This same group of local representatives also help to mitigate and resolve recognised safety issues of beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

As with beneficiary safety, issues of dignity of beneficiaries is also monitored through consultations with local leaders, focus groups and responses of key informants especially MtoM Support Groups. These community-based structures have been very valuable resources for recognising dignity issues, especially for the ultra-vulnerable mentally handicapped, orphaned children, women headed households etc. At the employee level, CMA defines clearly how personnel are to conduct themselves in delivery of services, and any reported mistreatment or misconduct of project personnel that shows a lack of respect for patients is dealt with quickly. The project will conduct safety audits/assessments at facility level and community level which will provide more insight into dignity issues.
