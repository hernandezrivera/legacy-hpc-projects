Project Summary

Under the leadership of the Education Cluster, UNICEF will pilot an innovative initiative, connecting 200 teenagers (50% girls) to secondary schools located in anglophone countries. The crisis affected children will be formally enrolled in these schools at the cost of UNICEF and resume their education on the remote basis, supported by ICT equipment and remote coaching.
The choice of curriculum content will be guided by a joint (low profile) reflection between UNICEF and the Ministry of Secondary Education of Cameroon, that will inform on the minimum competencies and subjects required for secondary school students of various grades.
UNICEF will interact with Natcoms (National Committees for UNICEF), for the mobilization of secondary schools willing to enrol (adopt) one or more conflict affected children from the NW and SW of Cameroon, in the spirit of cooperation and humanitarian assistance. At the end of the remote formal learning experience, the Ministry of Secondary Education will validate the certification provided by the foreign secondary school.

Besoins / Needs

The target population are 200 out of school secondary students of the North West and South West. The NW and SW crisis has left more than 1.2 million school-age children (51% girls) in need of humanitarian assistance in NW and SW, Adamawa (Mayo Banyo), Littoral, West, and Centre (Yaounde). Approximately 900,000 of them need urgently access to education service. An estimated 59,000 adolescents are displaced within the 2 regions (NW and SW). The main needs for children of the NW and SW are linked to the risks associated with attending formal education, the unavailability of formal education offer in areas of insecurity, the general lack of protective learning environment. The project targets 200 former students with access to distance formal education in schools located in anglophone countries (tbd).
