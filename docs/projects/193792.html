Project Summary

The occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) is a chronic protection crisis characterized by 55 years of Israeli military occupation, the blockade and siege of Gaza, and the internal Palestinian political divide between the Palestinian Authority (PA) and Hamas. In Gaza, scarcity of services and restrictions on freedom of movement creates a fragile humanitarian situation that negatively impacts infrastructure, economic recovery, and employment opportunities. Electricity shortages disrupt daily activities, livelihoods, and access to essential services. The May 2021 escalation and the COVID-19 pandemic deteriorated the already severe crisis, and the outlook for people in Gaza is grim. While for the West Bank, the tension between Israeli forces and Palestinians at checkpoints and hotspots, the acts of violence perpetuated by settlers toward Palestinians, and the demolition of houses, worsens the humanitarian situation and leads to daily physical and psychological trauma among the population, which has seen a consistent increase of needs in terms of availability of services, localization in marginalized and temporary sealed locations. In Gaza, the project will work towards increasing access to basic and specialized services for vulnerable persons with disabilities by providing multidisciplinary rehabilitation services; through outreach and center-based services, including; physiotherapy, occupational therapy, wound dressing, speech therapy, and psychological support services, in addition to the provision of assistive devices and wound dressing materials for people in need to improve their mobility participation and functional independence. Moreover, the proposed project will support the coverage of transportation fees for people with disabilities to access mainstream services, including medical, protection, MHPSS, and shelters through the local referral network in the five governorates. That shall come with special consideration to the COVID19 situation by providing family hygiene kits for affected persons with disabilities to avoid contamination and exposure to the virus. The package of services will be based on pre-identification and assessment of individuals' needs by the combination of well-trained and skilled multi-disciplinary rehabilitation professionals. Nonetheless, the project will work on a holistic approach to ensure proper and better access to other needs beyond project deliverables for the targeted beneficiaries through the coordination mechanism of local referral networks, particularly by continuing the support of the rehabilitation task force. This will ensure standardizing of rehabilitation intervention and information sharing among actors and preparedness through the capacity building of rehabilitation professionals on early rehabilitation during emergencies. Moreover, the project aims to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities, especially persons with disabilities in the West Bank, through enhanced emergency preparedness to mitigate the impact of current and future crises. HI will support one local partner in West Bank (BASR) by establishing three emergency stocks in most hot areas in West Bank in Jenin, Nablus, and south-Hebron to respond to the early rehabilitation needs of persons with injuries and persons with disabilities affected by emergencies. The stocks will be managed by BASR, and HI will support them financially and technically to ensure proper management of the stocks and release and its effective use during emergencies by building the capacity of the partner on stock management. Additionally, HI will train a pool of community volunteers with rehabilitation backgrounds on basic humanitarian topics, including; evacuation, Psychological First Aid, First Aid, GBV, and safeguarding policies, in addition to early rehabilitation and inclusive health service provision to persons with disabilities.

1 - Needs

The Multi Sectorial Needs Assessment (MSNA) showed that 21.0% of interviewed households ( ) in Gaza count at least one member with physical and/or cognitive difficulty, and 9.3% of HHs were assessed to have at least one child (age 5 - 17) having a disability Among 96.1% of households assessed to have a member of the household with a disability that reported a healthcare need, 99.7% said a barrier to accessing healthcare. While in West Bank, 9.7% of interviewed households were assessed to include at least a member of their household having a disability, and 4.0% of households were assessed to have at least one child (age 5 - 17) having a disability. Of that 81.4% of households assessed to have a member of the household with a disability that reported a healthcare need, 95.6% reported a barrier to accessing healthcare. According to the same assessment, the most commonly reported barriers to care are the cost of services being too high, unavailability of services, and lack of geographical distribution of services (centralization), which increase the financial burden of facilities to cover the cost of transportation fees, and lastly the lack of inclusiveness of services to persons with disabilities needs. According to Humanity & Inclusion (HI), the main barriers to access to rehabilitation services are gaps in identifying rehabilitation needs, scarce availability of rehabilitation services, and quality of the service provided. Affordability is also recognized as an additional barrier and the availability of assistive devices. The demand for assistive products increases during emergencies as new injuries arise, and many devices are destroyed or left behind when persons with disabilities have to flee to safer shelters. Another challenge is the lack of maintenance shops for assistive products (HI, Rehabilitation Factsheets, October 2022). In December 2021, HI conducted a needs assessment in Gaza targeting 682 participants (406 Males and 276 Females). The barriers identified are linked to access to information; 61% of participants don't know about the available rehabilitation services in their locality/ governorate, and accessibility, 49.27% of respondents reported that rehabilitation services are accessible and affordable. 51.32% of the participants also agreed that they could access comprehensive rehabilitation services through a home-based program "outreach", and 56.45% of the participants agreed that they could access comprehensive rehabilitation services through a center-based program. However, the availability of rehabilitation services is limited to the availability of external funds. The gaps in access to rehabilitation services affect functional independence and limit their participation and inclusion to overcome these barriers; collaborative efforts from the key actors in rehabilitation, including persons with disabilities, service providers, and authorities, are required. Shocks and Emergencies: oPt is at risk of small, medium, and large-scale emergencies related primarily to military escalations and severe weather. In the Gaza Strip, between 5 and 7 August 2022, Israeli forces conducted a “Breaking Dawn” military operation in multiple locations in Gaza. While hostilities broke down, within the space of 3 days 360 Palestinians, including at least 151 children and 58 women (Gaza Ministry of Health), have been reported injured (OCHA, August 2022). While in West Bank and East Jerusalem, daily tension between Palestinians and Israeli forces at checkpoints, demolition of houses, excessive use of force and acts of violence of settlers toward Palestinians lead to daily physical and psychological trauma among Palestinians, requiring health and rehabilitation intervention. According to OCHA (September 2022), 175 Palestinians, including at least 29 children, were injured by Israeli forces across the West Bank, of whom 17 were hit by live ammunition. In August 2022, HI conducted a consultation with MoH and key rehabilitation actors in the West Bank, including; Care, PRCS, BASR, and MDM-F, to assess the level of preparedness of actors to respond to urgent and early rehabilitation needs of persons with disabilities living in the most vulnerable communities; all the actors stated that early rehabilitation needs of persons with injuries and disabilities are not tackled within their preparedness effort and agreed that it is an area to invest in to ensure a response to the needs of persons with injuries and persons with disabilities, to compensate for lack of functioning or to prevent or slow down any deterioration in functioning following an injury, illness or disability.Additionally, according to HNO 2023, around 94,000 persons need emergency and trauma care, including rehabilitation services in areas A, B, C, H2 n West Bank, and East Jerusalem. The needs identified include; emergency stocks or supplies and equipment to respond to the rapid needs of the most affected population, triage training, trained staff on first aid and psychological aid, and improvement of data collection and renovation of health facilities.

2 - Activities

The project is centered on two pillars, corresponding to two outcomes. The first outcome aims at ensuring access to accessible, quality, and coordinated rehabilitation services for the most vulnerable persons with disabilities in the Gaza Strip. Output 1.1: Multidisciplinary rehabilitation and psychological services are provided to the most vulnerable people with disabilities through outreach and center-based in Gaza Strip. The activities proposed to address output one are. Activity 1.1.1: Identification and assessment of rehabilitation needs and the socio-economic situation of persons with disabilities and definition of an individual intervention plan in the Gaza Strip. Activity 1.1.2: Provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation services using center-based, home-based to 1,250 people with disabilities who have limited access to services in Gaza and the West Bank. Activity 1.1.3: Purchase and donation of 650 assistive devices for the targeted beneficiaries as per their identified needs in Gaza. Activity 1.1.4: Purchase and donation of nursing disposables (400 kits) to support wound dressing services for persons with disabilities in Gaza. Activity 1.1.5: Purchase and donation of hygiene kits (400 kits) for the most vulnerable families of persons with disabilities in Gaza. Activity 1.1.6: External referral for other mainstream service providers to respond to the holistic needs of persons with disabilities through the local referral networks. Activity 1.1.7 Technical Support for partner’s mobile teams through coaching, monitoring field visits, and evaluating their interventions in the Gaza Strip. Output 1.2: Rehabilitation actors strengthened their coordination and capacity to support the continuum of rehabilitation care and rehabilitation preparedness efforts in the Gaza Strip. The activities proposed to address output two are. Activity 1.2.1: Facilitating bi-monthly coordination meetings of the rehabilitation task force and supporting the update of the annual action plan. Activity 1.2.2: Provide technical training to rehabilitation professionals on early rehabilitation in crisis and emergency guidelines. Activity 1.2.3: Follow up the development of action plans with RTF members to adopt the early rehabilitation guideline. The project's second outcome aims to address the needs of most vulnerable people, including persons with disabilities and injuries, in West Bank through disaster risk management and early rehabilitation. Output 2.1: Community emergency volunteers demonstrated capacity to respond to early rehabilitation and humanitarian needs of the most affected population in WB, especially persons with disabilities. The activities proposed to address output 2.1 are the following. Activity 2.1.1: Identification of emergency volunteers at most vulnerable communities in West Bank. Activity 2.1.2: Training 200 emergency volunteers on basic humanitarian topics and early rehabilitation. Activity 2.1.2: Conduct refresher training and simulation exercises to test the level of preparedness of volunteers. Output 2.2: Improved emergency infrastructure in West Bank to respond to the needs of the most vulnerable communities, including persons with disabilities and persons with injuries in emergencies. The activities proposed to address output 2.2 are as follows: Activity 2.2.1: Identify and map emergency stocks. Activity 2.2.2: Install maintenance and security work for the identified stocks. Activity 2.2.3: Purchase and procurement of AD and NFIs based on minimum standards set by the shelter cluster. Activity 2.2.4: Provision of training to partners on stock management and follow-up. Activity 2.2.5: Coaching and follow-up for partner on stock management. Activity 2.2.6 establish a referral mechanism across emergency actors in major hotspots of Nablus, Jenin and South-Hebron.

3 - Indicators

HI adopts a monitoring system that will ensure proper tracking of the progress and captured any challenges in the implementation in order to deliver the requested support on time. The assigned Project Manager will be in charge of the M&E system with close support from the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) unit. This system is composed of: I) a monitoring tool (PMBox) developed at the project’s inception phase and regularly monitored during the project’s implementation period. This tool tracks the progress of different activities, achievements and indicators; it also allows for identifying the challenges and delays within the project activities and identifying potential solutions. II) A project review meeting is scheduled and conducted every four months according to HI standards. During this meeting, all the departments and the project team discuss the progress of the project’s activities and the achievement of results. Additionally, recommendations for improvements are identified and followed to ensure the quality and timely implementation of activities. III) Technical monitoring can be provided by the Regional MEAL and Technical Units based in Jordan, as required. IV) Field support visits conducted by HI project team to partners and beneficiaries, on a regular basis, to monitor and support the activities’ implementation and outcome achievement. The project monitoring will use and apply the following tools: Post Intervention Monitoring (PIM), lines, and satisfaction surveys. The M&E of the project is designed in accordance with HI Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation Policy, which is derived from HI institutional policies on Child Protection (CP), Protection Against Sexual Exploitation Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH), Prevention of and Fight Against Bribery (PFAB), Code of Conduct (CoC), and Safety and Security (SaS). The M&E system complies with IASC guidelines on the inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action through the application of Common Humanitarian Standards (CHS) Commitments 4 and 5, SPHERE Standards, and Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP).
