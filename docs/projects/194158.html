Project Summary

WHO project aims to decrease avoidable under five mortality and morbidity related to Severe acute malnutrition through improved access and quality of inpatient care for the Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications in 165 stabilization centers. The project includes curative, preventive and promotive measures integratedly. The activities will address the clinical life-saving interventions in Stabilization centers targeting the children with SAM and the mothers/caretakers through IYCF counseling inside the SCs, strengthening baby friendly hospital initiatives, growth monitoring and promotion interventions, raising awareness of mothers/care takers to lower relapse and prevent SAM cases. The projects’ activities and costing factored in 41 SCs supported by NGOs. The SCs Supported by NGOs will require only medical kits (1st and 2nd line medicines) and technical support. Main activities under this HRP project are include to: Training of the health and nutrition staff of 124 SCs (all except the ones Supported by NGOs, however the NGOs supported staff can be included based on the request of NGOs) to include IPD SAM , ToT, initial training (7 days) and refresher Training (3 days), provision of guidelines, Job Aids and IEC materials to all participants from the 124 SCs (excluding 41 NGOs supported SCs). WHO will procure and distribute to 165 SCs; medicines (SAM inpatient Kits – first and secondary level) and medical supplies based on the needs identified together with MOH. To improve the quality of care and recovery rate in SCs, WHO nutrition and public health officers present in all targeted states will conduct jointly with SMOH and NGOs regular supportive supervisions in all targeted 165 SCs including provision of on the job training, coaching, and to improve adherence to the national treatment protocols. Routine nutrition surveillance to know the trends, identifying hot spot areas, effective planning of activities will be enhanced through training, provision of recording and reporting tools in 18 targeted states. The system is built on the existing information system for better integration into HFs reporting mechanism making sure the sustainability. WHO nutrition team will provide technical and operational support to the MOH at federal and state levels to regularly analysis the nutrition data (using the agreed performance indicators) disaggregated by sex and age. The information will be disseminated to relevant stakeholders through nutrition cluster platform. Effective coordination of WHO and partners will promote an improved referral system between the SCs, TSFP, OTP, and community. The project activities also include small repairs of 12 Stabilization Centers (SCs) to ensure better environment for work and improved hygiene in the SCs. The total budget for this project is $39,074,840 out of which 7.3% contributing to the resilience activities which is not included in the total HRP budget.

1 - Needs

An already dire situation in Sudan has been compounded by the escalation of violence since mid-April 2023. Currently, there are around 103,598 under-five children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with medical complication that need inpatient specialized care at stabilization centres. The World Health Organization (WHO) is supporting the Sudanese health authorities and partners, including non-governmental organizations (NGOs) to ensure these lifesaving nutrition services are available and accessible to vulnerable children and mothers. The pre-existing situation was not optimal with high prevalence of global acute malnutrition, The national prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was recorded at 13.6 percent places Sudan at the very high level for public health significance according to the WHO criteria. Food insecurity levels continue to increase in Sudan driven by the worsening macroeconomic situation, poor harvest, and conflict. 11.7 million population (24% of the analyzed population) facing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above meaning Crisis or worse) who need urgent action, with an increase of 2 million population compared to last year. (Latest IPC- APRIL 2022 - FEBRUARY 2023)The projected worsening of the food insecurity and other drivers including high levels of poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake, low dietary diversity at the household level, Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affecting children’s growth and development from an early age are likely to contribute to further deterioration of nutrition situation, which is already at high level with each 3rd under five child is suffering from chronic malnutrition and over 3 million children below age of 5 years are suffering from acute malnutrition. Sub-optimal IYCF practices continue to remain low. While exclusive breast-feeding prevalence among children under-six months in Sudan is over 62 per cent age-appropriate dietary diversity is very low at 25.4 per cent. Anaemia prevalence in children aged 6-59 months estimated at 48 per cent is among the highest falling into severe levels as per WHO classification.The pre crises figures of malnutrition revelated 550,400 children aged 6-59 months as severely acute malnourished, while SAM cases are above 611,000 among children 0-59 months children. Some 2.2 million children are moderately malnourished (MAM) among 6-59 months, while 2.44 million MAM cases are among children 0-59 months. In addition, over 67,000 refugees (66 per cent children under-five and 34 per cent PLW) will need life-saving nutrition assistance. The projections for 2023 HRP shows that about 91,730 children are suffering of SAM with medical complications who needed in-patient care. Without specialized care, the risk of death for these children is extremely high. The revised caseload project is 103,598 for SAM children with medical complications with a target of 42,302 for the remaining 7 months of the year 2023.. Despite some progress in regard with access to, and the coverage of nutrition services over the last assessment for the SC situation in Sudan result (WHO & MOH 2022) revealed gaps in access to SAM inpatient care services, low capacity, high turnover of the staff, quality of the services, availability of free of charge medicines, IYCF counseling for the mothers, and the quality of reporting and monitoring of SCs. The geographical coverage of SAM treatment services remains low, especially for the inpatient treatment.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The following activities are planned under HRP 2023: The WHO responses will be integrated of live saving treatment, capacity building and some lifesaving preventive activities, with top priority given to treatment of severe inpatient. About US$ 39.1 million is required to support implementation and scale up of life-saving preventive and treatment nutrition responses in 2023 targeting to reach about 70% of the total severe acute malnutrition with medical complications during 7 months. About 93% (US$36.2M) is earmarked to support core humanitarian nutrition responses while approximately 7% of the total budget (US$ 2.9M) is needed to support lifesaving preventive nutrition interventions contributing to resilience. The implementation mode will be identified after assessing the situation and it will be mainly though national authorities, partners, and direct implementation. The activities under the HRP 2023 are briefly stated below:a. Provision of technical support for the federal, state and localities for the coordination and management of SAM inpatient care ensured by the WHO Khartoum and field team in all states. b. Procurement and distribution of 2,031 WHO SAM kits (6 different modules containing first and secondary medicines) for 165 SCs, and equipment (medical and kitchen) for SCs to meet the urgent needs during 2023. The budgetary calculation excluded the costs of therapeutic milk and RUTF for 165 SCs provided by UNICEF. c. Repairs of 12 SCs to improve the infection prevention, and optimization of the work environment d. Establishment of a Centers of Excellence and Professional training for SAM inpatient care in one state initially. e. Capacity building for the health and nutrition staff working for the stabilization centers. Support the IYCF counseling for the mothers and caretaker in all targeted SCs g. Support early detection of malnutrition through Growth Monitoring and promotion at health facility for 0-23mo children g. Joint supportive supervisions with MOH and nutrition partners to improve the adherence to the treatment protocols. h. Enhance nutrition surveillance in all states through conducting trainings provision of recording & reporting tools, analysis of dis-aggregated data for dissemination to identify the trends and challenges. i. Strengthen the linkages with communities through social mobilization in coordination with other UN agencies and NGOs, improve referral and treatment. j. Contribute to the development of Sudan Multi-sectoral nutrition plans with related sectors (include Regional and HQs support. k. Support provision of printing of materials including guidelines, job aids, IEC materials… etc. l. provision of operation cost for 52 SCs. Rehabilitation/small repairs of Stabilization Centers (Cs).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental protection of biological and chemical contamination is part of accountability to affected population. Working with patients who mostly suffer from communicable diseases creates real risks for staff, other patients, care takers, environment and thus the communities. WHO will support and reinforce strict adherence to Universal Precautions (as per WHO/MOH guidelines) through provision of necessary materials such as safety boxes, self-degradable disinfectants, and personal protection equipment. In collaboration with WASH and Health partners support for availability and use of incinerators, pits for sharps, hand washing facilities in each SCs/HFs will be provided to ensure the protection of the staff, patients, caretakers, environment, and communities. During each supervisory visit the environmental protection will be checked, and the staff will be given orientation during the on job training. The training will include message on environmental protection from biological contamination as part of safe waste disposal and improved community level sanitation. Expired medicines will be destroyed (incineration) as per WHO guidelines and in the presence of health authorities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO has a protection mainstreaming policy that all staff in the field have been trained in, at field level WHO partners with organizations whose programming is wholly protection focused to ensure that beneficiary protection needs are properly addressed. Equally, there is a country child protection focal person who gives the child protection focal points remote support, this ensures WHO is equipped to capture and refer appropriately the protection issues to the best-placed protection partners. World Health Organization during the design phase, implementing SAM inpatient programs and activities, protection implies implementing projects in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the SAM children receiving treatment from malnutrition with medical complications. The principles of protection mainstreaming will be incorporated during the Monitoring and Evaluation. Assessment of the project incorporates the key elements of Protection Mainstreaming, which include Avoiding-causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity, Ensuring meaningful access, Practicing accountability and Promoting participation and empowerment of the stabilization center staff and beneficiaries. A confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system is in place and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations. Feedback on the complaints should be regularly be provided to the beneficiaries
