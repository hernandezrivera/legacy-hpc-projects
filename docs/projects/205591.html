Project Summary

The South Sudan Education cluster prioritisation 2024 (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023), estimates that about 3.3 million children are in need of urgent educational service provision in 2024 and that 244,233 of these children are located in the 8 Counties from the three states of Lake (Yirol West, Awerial), Upper Nile State( Melut, Panyikang, Fashoda) and Jonglei State (Fangak, Pigi and Nyirol). South Sudan education sector plan objective is to provide equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality education; Improve educational management; and Promote quality non-formal and formal post primary TVET. The realization of this objective is impeded by poor infrastructures, insecurity, impact of climate change that results to many displacements of the communities, influx of refugees and returnees following the conflict in Sudan and communal fighting that displaced many people in western Ethiopia to South Sudan eastern state of Jonglei (Fangak, Pigi and Nyirol,) and Upper Nile state ( Melut, Panyikang and Fashoda) and Lake State( Yirol, Awerial). The most pressing education needs as requested by all schools to operate were: recruitment of additional teachers, provision of WASH facilities, school feeding programme, teachers’ salaries/incentives, teaching and learning supplies, security for schools, rehabilitation of school infrastructures and training of teachers.
To meet the Education Cluster HRP 2024 Objectives of: 1) ensuring ''access to safe, protective and inclusive education services to emergency-affected children and youth, that are appropriate to their age, learning level and needs’’, CINA will establish/rehabilitate 17 learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards; Establish change rooms for girls; Establish/rehabilitate gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE and sphere standards. CINA will increase enrolment of ALP children, Functional Literacy children, Life Skills for Children and Youth, Pre-Primary children, Primary children, Secondary children, Skills for Life and TVET. CINA will conduct distribution of dignity kits to adolescent girls; Awareness raising on key issues; Distribution of hygiene kits to school boys and girls and assistive devices to children with disabilities; CINA will establish reporting (toll free lines, suggestion boxes, designated focal-points etc.) mechanisms; Conduct training of teachers and staff on child safeguarding and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanism; Provision of school meal to children; Establish school gardens at school; Training of teachers on PSS and Provision of PSS to learners.
2)To ensure that ‘’children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle’’, CINA will distribute ECD Kits to school children and Recreation Kits to children; Train teachers on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skill and support volunteer teachers with financial incentives. 3) To ensure that ‘’Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response’’, CINA will conduct training for SMC/PTA members and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
CINA has qualified personnel, governance and organizational structures and is currently implementing GESS besides child protection, MHPSS, GBV, EORE, and FSL programs in Lakes (Yirol West, Yirol East and Awerial), Jonglei (all the counties), GPAA (all counties) and Upper Nile (all counties). CINA will maximize its education capacity to accelerate learning for the crises affected out of school children to increase enrolment and retention while maintaining the quality of education, safeguarding and protection of learners. To ensure success, education services will be coordinated at state and national levels, optimizing nexus efforts with humanitarian-peace-development actors for improved learning outcomes for children.

1. Needs

South Sudan has faced enormous setbacks as a result of the December 2013 and July 2016 civil wars. This prolonged conflict exacerbated by social and political instability, climate-related shocks; such as severe flooding and economic depreciation has led to slow progress in the humanitarian situation in the Country. Intercommunal conflicts as a result of revenge killings and cattle raiding even continue to worsen the situation. The conflicts and the natural hazards have devastating impact on education, both in terms of psychological impact on learners, teachers and communities and in physical dilapidation of the learning system and it infrastructure. The situation is made worst by the returns of South Sudanese who had remained in Sudan or fled to Sudan due to the 2013 and 2016 conflicts and Sudanese refugees and other national fled to South Sudan following the eruption of the fighting in Sudan in April 2023. According to IOM displacement tracking matric published in September 2023, there are a total of 2,027,331 IDPs (1,472,827 are settled among host communities and 554,504 and settled in IPD camp) with Canal/Pigi hosting 104, 779 IDPS, Fangak hosting 60,496 IDPs, Awerial 56,034 IDPs and Nyirol hosting 44,600 IDPs while other Counties like Melut is hosting between 30, 000 to 60,00 IDPs and Fashoda, Panyikang, Yirol West are hosting between 20,000 to 30,000 IDPs. The occupation of the schools by IDPs and flooding has affected the learning spaces in the schools, as most of the classrooms and WASH facilities have been damaged, learning materials destroyed. The economic depreciation pushed most of the trained teachers away from the classroom to seek for better opportunities leaving the schools with untrained volunteer teachers who are themselves traumatised due to the conflict and economic hardship. According to the South Sudan Education strategy 2023-2025, 2.8 million children are out of school, 23% of schools have classes under trees, 77% of schools reported a lack of separate toilets or washrooms for girls and boys, 60% of classrooms and 50% of latrines are not accessible for persons with disabilities, less than 25% of the children are reported having a general protection referral mechanism and only 34% of head teachers have been provided with PSS training The situation is worst for girls as there are fewer female teachers in the schools as twice as many girls than boys do not have the opportunity to obtain a secondary education, and the proportion of girls who are not in secondary school in rural areas is twice as high as their peers in urban areas leaving the girls in the schools with very few or no role models leaving the adolescent girls without role models to guide them on menstrual hygiene causing some of them to drop out of school due early pregnancies. The lack of trained teachers has double effects on children with disabilities as schools are unable to provide inclusive learning environment. While all citizens of South Sudan have the right to education regardless of disability or gender as enshrined in Article 29 of the Transitional Constitution, people with disabilities (PWD) have not been able to realise this human right. School attendance for PWD is 22%, the majority of whom are male, with a dropout rate of 48% (Disability Assessment report 2017). With limited resources, education efforts are often focused on formal primary education, ignoring those who previously missed out due to the conflict. Children who have missed years of education often have particular needs not met by standard primary education systems.

2. Project activities

CINA will target 13,477 beneficiaries; 13,078 ( 6,918F 6,158M) learners ages 3-17 years, 339 (61F 278M) adult teachers and 62 (11F 51M) School Management committee/Parents teachers Association (SMC/PTA) and School Officers with different activities. The selected Counties have returnees, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host community members including persons with disabilities, youth (boys and girls) to enable them attend learning in a protective, conducive and inclusive learning environment. CINA will support the school children and out of school children and youth to enrol in schools by providing inclusive temporary learning spaces and gender segregated inclusive latrines following the INNE Minimum standards, provide learning materials, train teachers on Psychosocial support, EiE, teaching pedagogy, mind risk education. CINA will integrate the gains from this project and integrate some of the Girls Education South Sudan (GESS) Project activities into the schools. These returnees and internally displaced female learners in primary five and above will benefit from the cash transfers and the schools will benefit from the capitation grants being provided by the GESS project. This would increase the enrolment and sustainability of the school girls to continue learning and transition to the next level of learning. CINA has already started holding orientation sessions with organisations of persons with disabilities and organisations supporting persons with disabilities to strengthen partnerships and coordination which will help in referral pathways to secure the necessary support for persons with disabilities who will be identified through this proposal.
