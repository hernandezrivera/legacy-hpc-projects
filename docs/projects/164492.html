Project Summary

Muzun for Humanitarian & Development was founded in 2015 by professional humanitarian workers engaged in the Syria response. We come from the communities affected by the conflict. We have set up this organization in order to support fellow Syrians with relief and non-relief assistance to maintain their under-pressured resilience and help them cope with the challenging conflict environment. In our work we focus on the most vulnerable including persons with special needs, children and women. As a grassroots organization, our work is community-led and therefore owned and sustainable and we are a new member in protection cluster and we hope to be very active member because We have direct contact with the beneficiaries, we know their needs in an excellent manner, and we are working with the camp management sector to provide complete information on displacement movements and the evaluation of camps and shelters since 2017.
The project designed to provide integrated services for women and girls to meet all their protection needs
(Psychosocial support and empowerment). Which is based on the
following:
1.Promoting the existing community protection mechanisms through upgrading the provided services in tow Safe Spaces for women and girl’s community centers managed by Muzun in Harim in Idleb, and in Jarablus in Aleppo. • Investing in the positive impact achieved through the previous activities in the Tow centers, which made the local community more accepting and supporting for protection activities.
2.Upgrading the services and activities that Muzun is conducting (PSS + GBV referral System +Protection monitoring +PSEA +MA)
3.Increasing opportunities of meaningful access for all vulnerable men, women boys and girls, throughout the following: - Enhance the services already provided for all beneficiaries including W&GWDs. - Enhance the CFS (Child-friendly Space) for children of the women beneficiaries in each center
4.Provide awareness raising sessions for men, women boys and girls with disabilities including survivors of explosive hazards incident.
5. Reduce the risk and the impact of ERWs by providing RE sessions in the communities and for the non-mine action specialized humanitarian worker, including establishing a community focal points networks in the two locations targeting mainly women and girls. All these new additions and enhancements will be for the sake of reaching the most vulnerable, neglected and marginalized categories of beneficiaries (Survivors with disabilities-Widows-vulnerable adolescent girls) and giving more access for mothers with multiple young children. Which means more accountability for vulnerable women and girls’ beneficiaries
6.Center-based activities will include: - Provide quality GBV services referral to most vulnerable, at risk, and survivors’ women and girls. Group PSS sessions and individual consultations, Empowerment and awareness activities for adolescent girls (Reproductive Health awareness, Life Skills sessions, RE, community messages). - Staff training (a reminding training) on different protection topics. PSS for children of the women beneficiaries in the CFSs allocated inside the centers. - Emergency response to new IDPs arrivals (PFA, receiving and orienting activities, Protection monitoring, GBV cases referral, sharing information.
7.Outreach activities will include conduct parenting skills' and GBV awareness sessions, Muzun proposed to implement the project in Jarablus District in Aleppo & Harim District in Idleb through targeting 14500 beneficiaries with different ages and vulnerable cases PWD (23 % men , 25 % women , 26 % Boys and 27% girls ) taking into consideration Gender and age diversity. And we have plan to targeting 315 PWD,,40% host communities and 60% IDPs, women and youth girls 52% (7540 Beneficiaries ) Muzun Staff in each center there is (4 CP staff , 4 MA staff ,5 GBV Staff , 6 GP, 1 media ,1 HR,1 logistic ,1 Center Manager, 1Team Leader,2 Guards and 1 child attendant

4 - Needs

according to the HNO 2019.The Protection Monitoring Report (on Idleb and Aleppo Displacement, Sep 2019), reported that IDPs are experiencing high needs for all basic all needs and services, As many families fled on short notice and were ill-prepared for the financial and physical demands of displacement, they are struggling to cope with their new circumstances, resulting in increased protection risks including: physical, domestic and sexual violence, family separation, verbal abuse, shelling/clashes, , explosive hazards, child labor, kidnapping, abduction, restricted freedom of movement, , etc., particularly for women, girls and boys.In addition, as per the GBV emergency assessment conducted in May-August 2019, 69% of the interviewed Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) reported that there has been an increase in security incidents affecting women and girls since the onset of the emergency in NWS. Further, the most reported types of violence during the current displacement were the domestic violence and being asked for marriage within the family. GBV specialized services are available in only 45% of the assessed communities. Additionally, two of the most reported barriers in accessing GBV services for women and girls were fear of being identified as a survivor and distance to service center. Muzun is mainly working in shelter/NFI cluster in addition to CCCM in this area, there is a huge need for integration the GBV prevention activities with other humanitarian clusters activities to ensure that other humanitarian activities will deliver to the women, girls, and PWDs with dignity and respect, the humanitarian workers should train on basic concepts of GBV and PFA to make sure that they have enough skills to refer any GBV survivors to available services in case of emergency response

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

-The inability to meet the needs of all women,girls,PWDs ,men and boys in the region that we work in. Risk Level : Average / Medial Mitigation Measure : Prioritizing the women according to their needs and provide them the services they need.- Fear of the inability to keep the conﬁdential and secret documents safe due to the emergency security incidents. Risk Level: High. Mitigation Measure: Archiving all the documents on conﬁdential electronic system and destroying the paper documents periodically as soon as they are completed and do-exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for speciﬁc persons/groups. So, Muzun will ensure that Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services.in addition to establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place.- doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality. So, Muzun will work on ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context also services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proﬁciency in training module-Sexual exploitation and abuse by project personnel. Likelihood: low; impact: medium. Mitigation Measures: All staff, volunteers and associated personnel should sign the policy on PSEA and child safeguarding, receive induction on PSEA and safeguarding children and are aware of reporting Procedures. All staff and volunteers to receive regular trainings/refreshers on PSEA and child safeguarding. The organization has developed a policy on PSEA and child safeguarding and communicated it to all staff in local languages.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Interference and aid diversion by Armed Opposition Groups. Likelihood: low; impact: high; Mitigation measures: share activity plans with safety and security department to get clearance and advice. Train staff for such scenarios, proper protocols and how to report similar incidents immediately. Ensure beneficiaries selection is conducted by Muzun staff in an impartial and independent manner. Ensure Accountability System (e.g. complaint and feedback mechanisms) is active and accessible to all beneficiaries. Monitor the project activities implementation and report any significant issues.
