Project Summary

The lack of access to safe water is a dire reality for many vulnerable communities in Lebanon, leading to devastating consequences for their health and well-being. To address this issue, DRC Lebanon has launched a project aimed at providing sustainable access to safe water for local communities in Bekaa, Akkar, Baalback, and North.
The initiative involves the installation of solar systems in four boreholes that are already equipped with an electrical system and a deep water pump. The solarization of these systems ensures that water is continuously available from a reliable source to a network/water tank, enabling a consistent stream of water supply even during electricity cuts in the areas. This project also has a go-green element by reducing dependency on fuel and promoting environmental sustainability, ultimately also leading to decreased costs of running the systems.
In compliance with Sphere standards, the project aims to fulfill the community's average water demand per capita per day, which is 15L, through the sustainable operation of the borehole. The water source will be regularly tested to ensure its quality, and a filtration/chlorination system will be installed, powered by the solar system, if necessary. This will help improve the health and well-being of these communities and reduce the prevalence of waterborne diseases that have been rampant in the mentioned areas.
Furthermore, DRC Lebanon will provide training to municipality members on how to operate and maintain the system efficiently, and manuals will be given for future reference. Additionally, backup solar panels will be provided to the municipality in case of any misuse or damage, ensuring proper maintenance and sustainability of the system even after DRC's exit.
In summary, this project aims to provide sustainable access to safe water for communities in Lebanon, promoting health and well-being while reducing the prevalence of waterborne diseases, with a focus on environmental sustainability and community empowerment through training and support.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

To identify the project location, a site visit and assessment of the land and location will be conducted. The assessment will include verification of land ownership and the existence of a MOU between the municipality and Water Establishments to ensure that the project can be implemented in compliance with relevant regulations and laws.A borehole test will be conducted to determine the water flow rate, particularly in cases where there is no available data on the submersible pump and water flow. Water testing will also be conducted to check for quality, ensuring that the water is safe for use.A detailed technical assessment will be conducted, along with technical specifications and logistical preparation to ensure the smooth implementation of the project.The tender for the project will be launched after site visits and pre-explanatory meetings with potential bidders. Following this, the bidder evaluation and selection process will be conducted, and the site handover process will begin, followed by regular monitoring to ensure the project's successful implementation.Once the project has been completed, a testing and commissioning process will be undertaken, and related staff and end-users will be trained on the operation and maintenance of the project. This will be followed by a post-evaluation MEAL verification of both the quality and satisfaction of the project's implementation to ensure that the project is meeting the needs of the community and operating as intended.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Gender, diversity and inclusion has been integrated into the design and are in line with the IA Standing Committee GMT. DRC will disaggregate all beneficiary data collected by sex and age. DRC will protect the identity and data of its beneficiaries as per IA GBV data protection protocols. DRC maintains a high level of confidentiality, ensuring services are provided discreetly, and never identifying the survivor. DRC ensures that all staff are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for PSEA, and the internal reporting mechanisms for SEA cases, through the mandatory trainings and the signature of the code of conduct. DRC will work on enhancing its awareness raising activities with communities to disseminate information on SEA key messages, available reporting channels and how the overall procedure functions, from the point of receiving the complaint, to investigation, resolution and feedback to communities on the outcomes of complaints and actions taken.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

DRC maintains a set of robust accountability and feedback mechanisms committed to participation of affected communities. DRC’s community feedback mechanism (CFM) ensures voices of beneficiaries are included, valued and heard. It systematically captures records, tracks, and follows up on the feedback it receives to improve elements of a response. Any person or community affected by DRC, or the DRC’s partners’ actions, decisions or policies can submit feedback and complaints through the CFM system, including through physical Feedback Boxes (FB), a hotline and email. Information on the availability and purpose of these channels is consistently disseminated by DRC staff through business cards. An information poster is installed next to each FB explaining its purpose. DRC staff are trained to explain the existence and importance of these channels. DRC ensures to close the feedback loops through communicating a response back to beneficiaries about the actions taken to address it.
